# Event 13 Natural Disasters Super-Event Text Research

Scope: text research only for three non-terminal abnormal Evolution III super-events tied to Event 13 Natural Disasters.

Role note for all three: these should read as catastrophic escalation and regional order breakdown, not world-end finality.

Selected quote: `Though the waters thereof roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with the swelling thereof.`

Button or remark candidate:
- `The maps are obsolete.`
  Note: original non-quote remark

Description direction:
- Present a cascading rupture sequence that outruns local response.
- Emphasize shattered rail belts, tunnel collapse, dam strain, broken ports, and coasts hit by withdrawal and return of the sea.
- Keep it non-terminal. States still exist, but whole regions are cut apart.

Why it fits:
- Psalm 46:3 covers both the quake and the sea response without sounding like a final apocalypse.
- The title is short, physical, and specific to a fault-wave catastrophe.
- The remark matches a super-event button tone of stunned administrative realism.

Backup options:
- Quote backup: Isaiah 24:19
- Quote backup: Pliny, *Letters* 6.20
- Remark backup: `Count the bridges still standing.`

Selected quote: `The meteors, midnight flambeaus of the sky, How after them they draw long trails of flame`

Button or remark candidate:
- `Keep clear of the windows.`
  Note: original non-quote remark

Description direction:
- Frame it as repeated impacts and airbursts over hours or days.
- Emphasize crater fields, rail cuts, urban fires, overloaded shelters, and late strikes after the first panic.
- Keep the tone on attritional skyfall and overwhelmed civil defense, not extinction.

Why it fits:
- Lucretius gives an exact image of streaking fire without pushing the event into end-times prophecy.
- The title is plain, memorable, and usable for both meteor shower and clustered impact variants.
- The remark sounds like an immediate civilian instruction delivered after normal safety language has already failed.

Backup options:
- Quote backup: Lucretius `How stars and constellations drop to earth, Seest not?`
- Quote backup: Matthew 24:29, only if a harsher omen tone is desired
- Remark backup: `The shelters were built for weather.`

## Implementation Recommendation

- Slot these as catastrophic escalation super-events, not defeat aftermath and not world-end.
- Best text package balance:
  - Great Rupture Wave: `The Great Rupture` + Psalm 46:3 + `The maps are obsolete.`
  - Massive Eruption: `The Mountain Unsealed` + Psalm 104:32 + `The ash will choose the harvest.`
  - Meteor Showers or Skyfall: `Stones from Heaven` + Lucretius Book II + `Keep clear of the windows.`
- Copyright risk is low in the selected set. All selected quotes are public domain or from public-domain translations.
- Avoid Matthew 24:29 unless the main agent explicitly wants a heavier apocalyptic register.
