## Selected package

### The False Revelation reveal

- Title: **The False Revelation**.
- Button: **“All that glisters is not gold.”**
- Button source: William Shakespeare, *The Merchant of Venice*, Act 2, scene 7; [Folger source](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/the-merchant-of-venice/read/2/7/).
- Main quote: **“There are more things in heaven and earth, Horatio, / Than are dreamt of in your philosophy.”**
- Quote source: William Shakespeare, *Hamlet*, Act 1, scene 5; [Folger source](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/hamlet/read/1/5/).
- Quote date: *Hamlet* is generally dated to 1599–1601.
- Attribution confidence: High for both selected references.
- Rights: Underlying Shakespeare texts are public domain; use only the short lines, not modern editorial matter.
- Fit: The title rejects the movement's divine claim without identifying the entity, the button warns against spectacular appearances, and the quote preserves uncertainty about an impossible presence.

### Defeat aftermath

- Title: **The Broken Claim**.
- Button: **“We will remember them.”**
- Button source: Laurence Binyon, “For the Fallen,” first published in *The Times* on 21 September 1914; [Representative Poetry Online source](https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/content/fallen).
- Main quote: **“Those who cannot remember the past are condemned to repeat it.”**
- Quote source: George Santayana, *The Life of Reason*, “Reason in Common Sense,” 1905; [Project Gutenberg source](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15000/pg15000-images.html).
- Attribution confidence: High for both selected references.
- Rights: Binyon's poem is public domain; Santayana's 1905 text is public domain in the United States as reproduced by Project Gutenberg, with ordinary territory-specific review still advised.
- Fit: The title marks the collapse of false authority without proving what the entity was, the button acknowledges the dead and displaced, and the quote turns memory into a condition of safe reconstruction.

## Rejected and backup candidates

### Reveal

- Backup quote: **“Fanaticism consists in redoubling your effort when you have forgotten your aim.”** George Santayana, *The Life of Reason* (1905), [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15000/pg15000-images.html). High confidence and public-domain source; less specific to the entity's uncertain appearance than the selected *Hamlet* line.
- Backup quote: **“For there shall arise false Christs, and false prophets, and shall shew great signs and wonders.”** Matthew 24:24, King James Version, [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+24%3A24&version=KJV). High wording confidence; not selected because its overt scriptural framing is less neutral and KJV rights can vary by jurisdiction.
- Backup remark: **“Who will guard the guards themselves?”** Juvenal, *Satires*, Satire VI, [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A2007.01.0093%3Abook%3D2%3Apoem%3D6). Translation and textual-history caveats make it weaker.
- Rejected remark: **“The emperor has no clothes.”** Andersen allusion, [Hans Christian Andersen Centre](https://andersen.sdu.dk/vaerk/hersholt/TheEmperorsNewClothes_e.html?oph=1). Too comic for the terminal reveal and not the exact wording of the cited translation.
- Rejected title: **The False Prophet**. It overfocuses on a human prophet and narrows the event's ambiguity.
- Rejected title: **The Unveiling**. It is generic and implies settled knowledge.

### Defeat aftermath

- Backup quote: **“Any man's death diminishes me, because I am involved in mankind.”** John Donne, *Devotions upon Emergent Occasions*, Meditation XVII (1624), [Adrian Brinkerhoff Poetry Foundation](https://www.brinkerhoffpoetry.org/poems/no-man-is-an-island-from-meditation-xvii). High confidence and public domain; stronger on shared loss than on vigilance.
- Backup quote: **“The evil that men do lives after them; / The good is oft interrèd with their bones.”** Shakespeare, *Julius Caesar*, Act 3, scene 2, [Folger source](https://www.folger.edu/blogs/shakespeare-and-beyond/famous-quotes-from-julius-caesar/). Public domain; too fatalistic for the preferred reconstruction emphasis.
- Backup remark: **“No man is an island.”** John Donne, *Meditation XVII*, [Adrian Brinkerhoff Poetry Foundation](https://www.brinkerhoffpoetry.org/poems/no-man-is-an-island-from-meditation-xvii). Public domain; abstract and somewhat redundant with the Donne quote option.
- Rejected remark: **“The rest is silence.”** Shakespeare, *Hamlet*, Act 5, scene 2, [Folger source](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/hamlet/read/5/2/). Public domain but too final for a world with splinter cells and reconstruction.
- Copyright-risk alternatives: **“The past is never dead. It's not even past.”** William Faulkner, *Requiem for a Nun* (1951), [University of Virginia source](https://faulkner.iath.virginia.edu/shanti/content/requiem-nun?page=23); and **“Not everything that is faced can be changed; but nothing can be changed until it is faced.”** James Baldwin, “As Much Truth As One Can Bear” (1962), [source review](https://barrypopik.com/blog/not_everything_that_is_faced_can_be_changed). Both are attributed, but both remain copyrighted and are unnecessary for the final package.
- Rejected title: **The Revelation Ends**. It implies permanent destruction and removes uncertainty.
- Rejected title: **Humanity Prevails**. It is generic and too triumphal.
