## Actionable findings

1. **Completed tests still have a pre-result report.** `localisation/english/023_soviet_nukes_l_english.yml:26-30`, keys `chaosx.nr23.101.d` and `chaosx.nr23.102.d`: `.101` says the site and plans “determine whether” a test succeeds, has a partial yield, fails, or becomes an accident. The source has already chosen the outcome before scheduling `.101`: `common/scripted_effects/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_event_effects.txt:800-849` records success, failure, partial yield, or accident and sends accidents to `.102`, other outcomes to `.101`. Thus `.101` needs outcome-specific text for success, partial yield, and failure; `.102` needs an accident-specific material consequence or site context. The phrase “ended badly” is vague, and “the device remains accounted for” cannot be certified from the popup alone because the shared action/ledger receipt governs consumption. **Recommended disposition: unresolved; event owner should select distinct report descriptions from recorded outcome flags or a verified scripted-localisation selector, then render each branch.** Preserve the difference between a failed non-detonating test and a partial-yield detonation.

2. **The ultimatum popup lists responses but gives little consequence or location.** YAML `chaosx.nr23.120.d` at line 39 names `[ROOT.GetName]` and `[GetSovietNuclearBombsDemandName]`, which is an improvement and must remain. It then lists nine possible replies and ends with the implementation-facing claim that the reply does not start war by itself. `events/023_soviet_nukes.txt:257-353` shows the actual choices: partial settlement requires a demand above minimum (`:268-275`), delay can occur only once (`:278-282`), foreign support has its own predicate (`:305-314`), evacuation requires the selected state to be owned or controlled by the recipient (`:315-331`), retaliation requires atomic technologies (`:333-343`), and a raid requires war with the coercion actor (`:344-353`). The response resolver records different consequences at `common/scripted_effects/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_event_effects.txt:1413-1469`. **Recommended disposition: unresolved; shorten the common description to the concrete demand and response window, and put concise option-specific blocked reasons and consequences on the affected choices.** A partial reply is only an offer until Soviet acceptance; evacuation orders the selected state and reduces Soviet credibility; preparation for retaliation is not a launch. Do not promise those outcomes as settled in the button labels.

3. **Selected-site requirement is mechanically correct but still hides the player's chosen depot.** The postpatch `sov_nuclear_bombs_breakaway_secure_custody` and `sov_nuclear_bombs_breakaway_request_return` are state-target decisions at `common/decisions/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_decisions.txt:1067-1083,1128-1143`. Their requirements use `FROM` with `sov_nuclear_bombs_event_state_is_valid_breakaway_custody_site`/`...return_site`, and the former really accepts an intact transferred site owned **or** controlled by the actor (`common/scripted_triggers/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_event_triggers.txt:280-291`). YAML `sov_nuclear_bombs_breakaway_custody_requirements_tt` at line 177 therefore matches the core ownership predicate, unlike the earlier random-site handoff. But it says only “Select an intact transferred site,” and `sov_nuclear_bombs_breakaway_custody_requirements_tt`, `sov_nuclear_bombs_breakaway_secure_custody_desc` (line 314), and the later technical/command/delivery descriptions (lines 316-320) never name the selected depot. The tooltip also contains the file's one sentence semicolon, against the project prose rule. **Recommended disposition: accepted source fix for the selector, unresolved text/context display and style repair.** Add a state name in a scope-verified map-decision tooltip or concise selected-site summary, with a safe no-site fallback. Verify the `FROM` scope in the actual rendered decision rather than assuming `[FROM.GetName]` resolves there.

4. **Doctrine reform eligibility is repaired; its tradeoff still needs exact display.** YAML `sov_nuclear_bombs_doctrine_reform_requirements_tt:194` now correctly says investigated failure, accident, confirmed enemy use, capital threat, or war after Evolution I. This matches `common/scripted_triggers/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_event_triggers.txt:28-46`. The decision at `common/decisions/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_decisions.txt:91-114` starts a timed reform and the options at `events/023_soviet_nukes.txt:152-188` select four different doctrines. YAML `chaosx.nr23.103.d:21` says only “long reorganization” and “disrupt the arsenal,” while `sov_nuclear_bombs_reform_custody_doctrine_desc:193` says Readiness and Command Integrity fall without showing their extent or the period. The four option labels at lines 22-25 give an institution but no visible institution-specific tradeoff. **Recommended disposition: unresolved.** Let the decision or option tooltips state the actual duration and Readiness/Integrity effects using source constants where supported; keep the popup prose brief. Do not add a second dashboard paragraph.

5. **Decision requirement coverage remains uneven.** The complex `available` predicates of `sov_nuclear_bombs_issue_public_ultimatum`, `sov_nuclear_bombs_stage_wartime_demonstration`, and `sov_nuclear_bombs_prepare_limited_strike` at `common/decisions/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_decisions.txt:489-522` have no `custom_trigger_tooltip`, although selected target, backing device, test route/site, or an unreserved device can block the action. Retain the distinction at the consequential choice and use the remaining passages for the specific depot action or people involved.
