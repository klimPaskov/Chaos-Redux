# Event 23 super-event text research handoff

## Scope and exact super-event role

This package is for the first confirmed multi-major nuclear exchange: one nuclear major's confirmed detonation is answered by, or occurs during an active exchange with, another nuclear major.

The presentation is nonterminal and must not claim Fallout, Final Silence, world_end, or the end of civilization.

The text should therefore mark an irreversible escalation, shortened response time, failed containment, and retaliatory danger without implying that the shared terminal route has already begun.

## Selected recommendation

### Main quote

> “The first blow or series of first blows may be the last.”

- Author or speaker: General Leslie R. Groves, Commanding General of the Manhattan Engineer District.
- Source work: “Our Army of the Future—As Influenced by Atomic Weapons,” a memorandum by the Commanding General, Manhattan Engineer District, Washington, 2 January 1946, reproduced in *Foreign Relations of the United States, 1946, General; the United Nations, Volume I*, document 600.
- Source URL: [Office of the Historian, FRUS document 600](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1946v01/d600).
- Exact-source location: Item 10 on the FRUS page, printed page 1200.
- Attribution confidence: High.
- Wording status: The sentence is reproduced exactly as printed in the official historical record; do not replace “first blow” with “first strike,” change “may” to “will,” or otherwise normalize the wording.
- Rights status: The Office of the Historian states that information in the FRUS series is public domain and may be copied and distributed without permission; see the [FRUS public-domain FAQ](https://history.state.gov/about/faq/what-is-frus). Retain the source attribution in the research documentation even though the selected 12-word excerpt is from a public-domain U.S. Government historical record.
- Fit: The line directly evokes the exchange threshold, the compressed retaliation window, and the possibility that one confirmed detonation can trigger a chain of further blows. It is severe without declaring Fallout or a terminal world state.

### Button reaction

`The exchange has begun.`

- This is an original plain reaction, not a quotation or cultural reference, so it needs no external attribution and has no identified copyright risk.
- It states the exact confirmed threshold in a short player-facing form and avoids the generic “We must act” construction.
- It is preferable to forcing a cultural allusion into the button when the super-event already has a strong period quote.

## Quote candidates considered

### Candidate A — selected: Leslie R. Groves, 1946

> “The first blow or series of first blows may be the last.”

- Source: “Our Army of the Future—As Influenced by Atomic Weapons,” FRUS 1946, General; the United Nations, Volume I, document 600, item 10, printed page 1200.
- URL: [Office of the Historian, FRUS document 600](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1946v01/d600).
- Fit: Best match for the first confirmed major-to-major exchange because it focuses on the first blow, the next response, and the danger of rapid escalation.
- Confidence and rights: High attribution confidence; public-domain status is documented by the FRUS series FAQ linked above.

### Candidate B — strongest explicit-nuclear backup: Chester W. Nimitz, 1946

> “No plan of peacetime control will surely prevent the use of atomic weapons in a prolonged war.”

- Author or speaker: Fleet Admiral Chester W. Nimitz.
- Source work: Letter from the Chief of Naval Operations to the United States Representative on the Atomic Energy Commission, Bernard Baruch, Washington, 11 June 1946, Department of State Atomic Energy Files, reproduced in FRUS 1946, General; the United Nations, Volume I.
- URL: [Office of the Historian, Nimitz letter](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1946v01/d451).
- Fit: This is the clearest direct statement about nuclear control failing once war is prolonged, making it the best backup if the parent wants the quote itself to name atomic weapons.
- Attribution confidence: High; the official page identifies Nimitz, recipient, date, and document context.
- Rights status: FRUS information is public domain under the [Office of the Historian FAQ](https://history.state.gov/about/faq/what-is-frus).
- Tradeoff: At 17 words, it is still compact, but its policy-memo tone is less immediate and less evocative than the selected Groves sentence.

### Candidate C — deterrence and moral choice: Bernard Baruch, 1946

> “We are here to make a choice between the quick and the dead.”

- Author or speaker: Bernard M. Baruch.
- Source work: Opening of the “World Control of Atomic Energy” address to the United Nations Atomic Energy Commission, 14 June 1946, commonly known as the Baruch Plan speech.
- Exact-wording source: [The Baruch Plan historical transcript](https://www.atomicarchive.com/resources/documents/postwar/baruch-plan.html).
- Archival cross-check: [DPLA/National Archives recording metadata](https://dp.la/item/e98a5dfeb70281c9b0bbf4656d73ca84) identifies the speech, speaker, and date, and marks the U.S. archival recording “No Copyright—United States.”
- Context source: [State Department history of the Acheson-Lilienthal and Baruch Plans](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/baruch-plans).
- Fit: Strong for the nuclear-age choice between restraint and destruction, but it does not itself identify an exchange and can read like a generalized life-or-death slogan.
- Attribution confidence: High for the speech wording, speaker, and date.
- Rights status: The NARA/DPLA recording metadata gives a U.S. no-copyright determination, but the transcript host does not provide a separate universal rights determination for the text; keep the excerpt short and preserve attribution.

### Candidate D — exchange consequence: U.S. Acting Secretary of State memorandum, 1945

> “In other words, if the invention is developed and used destructively there will be no victor and there may be no civilization remaining.”

- Author or speaker: A memorandum by the Acting Secretary of State to President Truman; the FRUS page does not identify the individual Acting Secretary in its heading, so individual attribution should not be guessed.
- Source work: “U.S. Policy Regarding Secrecy of Scientific Knowledge About Atomic Bomb and Atomic Energy,” Washington, 25 September 1945, reproduced in FRUS 1945, General; Political and Economic Matters, Volume II, document 17.
- URL: [Office of the Historian, FRUS document 17](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945v02/d17).
- Fit: It directly describes the consequence of destructive atomic use, but “there may be no civilization remaining” leans too close to a terminal Fallout reading for this nonterminal super-event.
- Attribution confidence: High for the wording and document, medium for a named-person attribution because the source heading identifies only the office.
- Rights status: FRUS information is public domain under the [Office of the Historian FAQ](https://history.state.gov/about/faq/what-is-frus).
- Tradeoff: The full sentence is 23 words and is less suitable for the first-exchange threshold than the selected Groves line.

### Candidate E — first-use period context: Harry S. Truman, 1945

> “With this bomb we have now added a new and revolutionary increase in destruction to supplement the growing power of our armed forces.”

- Author or speaker: President Harry S. Truman.
- Source work: White House press release, “Statement by the President of the United States,” 6 August 1945, reproduced in FRUS 1945, The Conference of Berlin, Volume II, document 1315.
- URL: [Office of the Historian, Truman statement](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945Berlinv02/d1315).
- Fit: It is an authentic period statement about the weapon’s destructive escalation, but its victory-oriented wartime rhetoric suits a first-use announcement more than a grim exchange after deterrence has failed.
- Attribution confidence: High.
- Rights status: FRUS information is public domain under the [Office of the Historian FAQ](https://history.state.gov/about/faq/what-is-frus).
- Decision: Rejected for this role because it risks sounding celebratory and does not express reciprocal exchange or loss of command control.

## Button and cultural-remark candidates considered

### Selected plain reaction

`The exchange has begun.`

- Status: Original text, not a direct reference.
- Fit: It confirms the exact threshold, remains severe and understated, and does not imply that Fallout is already active.
- Rights: No external rights issue identified.

### Cultural backup A — William Butler Yeats, 1920

> “Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold;”

- Source work: “The Second Coming,” first published in 1920.
- Source URLs: [University of Virginia Literature in Context text](https://anthology.lib.virginia.edu/work/Yeats/yeats-second-coming) and [Poetry Foundation text](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/43290/the-second-coming).
- Fit: The line can allude to failing command, communications, and political order, but the poem’s surrounding apocalyptic imagery risks making the nonterminal exchange event sound like Fallout.
- Rights status: The poem’s 1920 publication makes the quoted line public domain in the United States in 2026; the University of Virginia page identifies the 1920 source text. Confirm local distribution-law requirements if the package is distributed outside the United States.
- Decision: Acceptable sourced backup only; the plain reaction is safer and more exact for this event role.

### Cultural backup B — Julius Caesar as reported by Suetonius

> “The die is cast.”

- Attributed speaker: Julius Caesar, as reported by Suetonius.
- Source work: *The Twelve Caesars*, “The Deified Julius,” chapter XXXII, written in the early second century CE; the wording is an English translation of the Latin “Iacta alea est.”
- Source URL: [Fordham Internet History Sourcebooks Project, Suetonius](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/suetonius-julius.asp).
- Fit: It conveys irreversible commitment after the first exchange, but it is a generic military allusion and the surviving attribution is literary and indirect rather than a contemporary record.
- Rights status: The Fordham page identifies its sourcebook texts as public-domain or copy-permitted; the ancient work is public domain, while translation wording should remain tied to the cited edition.
- Decision: Backup only, not preferred over the original button reaction.

### Cultural backup C — Baruch speech fragment

> “We are here to make a choice between the quick and the dead.”

- This is the same verified Baruch source documented under Quote Candidate C above.
- It could work as a button because it is short, stark, and nuclear-age specific, but it is better used as a quote candidate than as a reaction after the exchange has already begun.
- Rights note: Use the short excerpt with the NARA/DPLA archival rights caveat documented above.

## Modern-work copyright note

No modern song, film, book, or game line is recommended for the final package.

The recognizable Oppenheimer recollection “We knew the world would not be the same.” is real and short, but the readily available wording is carried by a 1965 broadcast/interview transcript and secondary media hosts, not a period 1936–45 source; it also describes the Trinity test rather than a reciprocal major-power exchange.

If the parent later considers a modern cultural reference, keep it to a very short fragment or title-like allusion and verify the exact source separately; the selected Groves quote and original button avoid that copyright and tone risk.

- Recommended `.q`: `The first blow or series of first blows may be the last.`
- Recommended attribution: `General Leslie R. Groves, 1946` or the repository’s established equivalent for source attribution.
- Recommended `.a`: `The exchange has begun.`

- Backup sourced remark: `Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold;` — William Butler Yeats, “The Second Coming,” 1920, only if the parent accepts its collapse/near-terminal implication.
