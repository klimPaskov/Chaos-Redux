The U.S. Army's [*The Chemical Warfare Service: From Laboratory to Field*](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo94181/pdf/GOVPUB-D114-PURL-gpo94181.pdf), Chapter III, p. 63, says Edgewood's restored mustard plant ran for only two weeks in 1937 and large-scale output began in 1940. Page 52 dates a brief phosgene run in 1937, a replacement design in 1939, and the new plant's opening in July 1941. The Army's [*Organizing for War*](https://history.army.mil/portals/143/Images/Publications/catalog/10-1.pdf), “The Years Between the Wars,” p. 37, records a 1937–39 pilot shell-filling plant, which is not proof of continuous agent production. Therefore a 1939 U.S. mustard or phosgene production-line grant would overstate the evidence. Its existing reserve and research distinction is retained without a new line or free lot grant. Britain's 1939 civilian issue and one-factory respirator line are separately supported by [Hansard](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1939/feb/02/air-raid-precautions) and documented in the accepted British correction. The [Showa Denko identity audit](../2026-09-27_national_mio_source_audit.md) records its 1 June 1939 formation; the date gate is in `common/military_industrial_organization/organizations/cbrn_national_organizations.txt`.
