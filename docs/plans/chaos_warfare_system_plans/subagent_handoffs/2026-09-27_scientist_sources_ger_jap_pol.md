The numeric tier is an abstraction, not a historical measurement. Confidence below concerns the person-specific evidence for field and date; exact tier boundaries remain a design judgment. The vanilla traits are project modifiers rather than archival attributes. The custom inhumane trait has a more specific claim: `localisation/english/chaosx_traits_l_english.yml:4-9` describes the holder as known for inhumane experiments on living subjects, often causing death or severe suffering. The character definitions attach this trait statically, and its trait definitions use `available = { always = yes }` in `common/scientist_traits/chaosx_00_traits.txt:18-47`.

The prior startup handoff records dates and specializations at `docs/plans/chaos_warfare_system_plans/subagent_handoffs/2026-09-20_historical_cbrn_startup_roster_revision.md:105-124,175-194,247-266`. ## Firm chronology versus unresolved proposals

The Koizumi and Hirt findings are firm at the historical-evidence level. Koizumi’s documented CW research began decades before the 1936 start, so his current BW field is contradicted by his recorded expertise. Hirt’s first documented Lost inmate experiment is 1942-11-25, while the current gate opens in 1941; the existing date therefore precedes the source-supported CBRN chemical-research role. The proposed CW 3 tier for Koizumi and proposed replacement date for Hirt remain gameplay recommendations for parent acceptance.

The proposed removal of Rascher and Mengele from the CBRN scientist roster is unresolved and must receive parent review; this report does not enact either removal. Ambros's proposed date remains unresolved. Kuhn's earlier date proposal has been resolved by the accepted 1940 availability floor documented below. The static inhumane trait is unresolved for Rascher, Mengele, Hirt, Bickenbach, Rose, and Mrugowsky; the existing character fields do not date-condition that trait, and this report does not remove, replace, or split any character. Gutzeit’s year-only evidence and the suitability of other generic vanilla traits also retain source-confidence limits as noted above.

## Kuhn availability follow-up

**Disposition: implemented.** The user-approved 67-scientist calibration authorized individual date corrections where source review supported them. This follow-up changes only `GER_richard_kuhn` availability and preserves the 67-character roster.

The ETH-Bibliothek profile distinguishes defensive work beginning after the outbreak of war, Wehrmacht-directed investigation of vitamin inhibitors for chemical-weapons use from 1940, and poison-gas research from late 1942. Its 1940 wording dates that chemical-weapon suitability work by year only. The approved 1940-or-later scientist-access boundary therefore opens on 1 January 1940 as a gameplay floor, not a claimed appointment day. The Nobel Prize facts page records his award for carotenoids and vitamins, supporting broad expertise while the existing `scientist_trait_genius` remains a gameplay mapping. The approved CW 4 skill, genius trait, and portrait remain unchanged.

`history/general/chaosx_startup_character_recruitment.txt` no longer recruits Kuhn statically. The existing `chaosx_startup_recruit_dated_ger_cbrn_characters` helper in `common/scripted_effects/chaosx_startup_history_effects.txt` now recruits him when `date > 1939.12.31` and he is not already present. That helper runs in the Germany-only `on_daily_GER` callback and from the `on_startup` call in `common/on_actions/chaosx_on_actions.txt`. On 1 January 1936 and the installed 1939 bookmark date, 14 August 1939, the date condition is false. On a 1 January 1940 start the startup call satisfies it; a campaign that continues from an earlier date satisfies it when the bounded Germany callback reaches 1940. This is source-logic evidence, not an engine-run result.

The availability change does not grant chemical use authorization, policy, research completion, or an advisor appointment. The existing CBRN policy decisions in `common/decisions/cbrn_doctrine_decisions.txt` continue to control retaliation-only, battlefield, and unrestricted authorization independently.
