## Country findings

### GER

**Documented facts.** The Max Planck Institute for the History of Science's Kaiser-Wilhelm-Gesellschaft project dates Schrader's discovery of tabun to late 1936 and identification of sarin to 1938 ([KWG im Nationalsozialismus project page](https://www.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/KWG/projects.htm)). A scholarly site-history chapter describes German military testing and pilot work beginning in 1939, with large-scale tabun production starting later ([Springer, “The Reconstruction of Production and Storage Sites for Chemical Warfare Agents and Weapons…”](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-51664-6_16)). Those facts support program maturity and research-date distinctions; they do not establish an August 1939 operational inventory.

Dräger's company history records the civilian Volksgasmaske as a government order introduced in 1937 and intended for civilian use ([Dräger company history](https://www.draeger.com/de_de/About-Draeger/History)). A 1938 Reichsluftschutzbund advertising/distribution instruction is catalogued by the Karlsruhe City Archive ([LEO-BW, Stadtarchiv Karlsruhe item](https://www.leo-bw.de/en/detail/-/Detail/details/DOKUMENT/stka_findmittel/8_StS_08_127/%22Die%20Volksgasmaske%22%20Arbeitsanweisung%20f%C3%BCr%20Werbung%20und%20Vertrieb%20Hrsg%20Reichsluftschutzbund%20Landesgruppe%20W%C3%BCrttemberg-Baden)). This establishes a civilian protective-equipment procurement and outreach program before the 1939 bookmark, but neither source gives national issue totals by August 1939.

**Current code and calibration.** The late-1936 date gate for Schrader and the 1939 `tabun` technology are already a sourced distinction. The current source does not add tabun lots, and `sarin` is explicitly a locked special-project technology (`allow = { always = no }`, `is_special_project_tech = yes`) at `common/technologies/chaosx_technologies.txt:699-723`; a 1938 identification does not prove that project was completed. Keep the existing offensive setup. A 1939 pilot/testing program alone is not a basis to create an operational starting stock or an arbitrary factory line.

Treat the documented civilian mask program as distinct from issued coverage. No standalone procurement/readiness flag is recommended because no gameplay or UI consumer has been identified. A numerical civilian grant needs a German issue/stock record dated by the August start, plus the count and geographic distribution. The existing zero measured coverage should not be described as proof that Germany had no civilian mask program.

### FRA

**Documented facts.** The Service historique de la Défense (SHD) catalogues an inventory file specifically on civilian protective equipment manufacture by multiple establishments in 1938–39 (GR/8/N/118, [SHD archive record](https://www.servicehistorique.sga.defense.gouv.fr/en/node/1140998)). Its administrative finding aid separately lists a “Fabrication et livraison de masques à gaz, commandes à l’industrie privée” dossier dated 31 May 1938–3 November 1939 (fonds 6 N 340, [SHD inventory PDF](https://www.servicehistorique.sga.defense.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/notices_files/SHDGR_INV_1N_6N_1920_1940_T1.pdf)). The inventory establishes actual production/delivery activity and private-industry orders during the period that includes the August 1939 bookmark; it does not state a count of plants or factory-equivalent capacity.

The CHRD states that municipalities distributed masks to civilians in spring 1939 and that French manufacturers could not satisfy all demand ([CHRD collection/history entry](https://www.chrd.lyon.fr/musee/collections/masque-gaz-et-son-etui)). The contemporaneous International Review of the Red Cross article *Mesures de défense passive* appeared in January 1939 (vol. 21, issue 241, pp. 20–27); its pages 23–24 describe a prospective five-million-mask target expected to be ready at the start of 1939, not a delivery receipt ([ICRC article PDF](https://international-review.icrc.org/sites/default/files/S1026881200035029a.pdf), [Cambridge issue record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-review-of-the-red-cross/article/abs/mesures-de-defense-passive/C590F358F178E79909F90E795553C3F0)). A later official Senate statement on 15 February 1951 reports twenty million civilian masks distributed over 1938–42, a broad cumulative period that cannot establish what was produced or held by August 1939 ([Conseil de la République, 15 February 1951, printed p. 501](https://www.senat.fr/comptes-rendus-seances/4eme/pdf/1951/02/S19510215_0483_0517.pdf)).

**Implementation review and calibration.** The FRA branch now requests one `gas_mask_equipment_1` factory only after 31 December 1938, inside the existing FRA `exists = yes` scope and after the basic-mask technology grant. This is the correct date, owner, and equipment identity. It allocates one existing military factory through the production queue; it creates no industrial building, free stock, civilian coverage, or status flag. The one-factory request and `efficiency = 100` are gameplay settings. Historical records support active production and distribution during 1938–39, but do not establish one physical plant or its exact output/efficiency.

The retained parsed-source result at `docs/testing/2026-10-09_france_1939_protection/source_test_results.json` reports 12 passing cases covering the two bookmark dates, the year boundary, FRA existence, once-only startup, unchanged ENG production/receipts, and preservation of the remaining startup AST. This is source-fixture evidence, not a live production-queue test. The reference evidence reports the syntax validator's `valid = null` and unchecked production argument block; installed documentation gives the supported Country-scope `add_equipment_production` structure, but actual factory allocation, efficiency handling, and output remain native behavior and unproved.

Do not turn the ICRC forecast or the 1938–42 Senate total into starting stock or an exact line-capacity claim. Numeric civilian issue remains zero as directed; a civilian equipment transaction still requires the missing August 1939 delivery/issue counts and geographic coverage. No standalone issue-status flag is needed.

### ITA

**Documented facts.** Italy's Ministry of Defence history states that the Military Chemical Service was established on 1 July 1923; from 1934 army corps had chemical companies, and a chemical detachment was transformed into a Chemical Regiment two years later ([Italian Ministry of Defence, “La Storia”](https://www.difesa.it/smd/entimi/scuolanbc/la-storia/34082.html)). The Central State Archives guide records administrative autonomy for the Military Chemical Service under the 1935 measure, converted in 1936, and a 21 March 1938 ministry reorganization that included the Directorate for the Military Chemical Service ([Archivio Centrale dello Stato, Ministry of War guide](https://search.acs.beniculturali.it/OpacACS/guida/IT-ACS-AS0001-0001214)). That is a concrete institutional change after the January 1936 bookmark.

The Foreign Relations of the United States series preserves a military-advisor report on Italian chemical use during the Ethiopian campaign, forwarded to the Secretary of State in a memorandum dated 21 July 1936 ([FRUS 1936, document 85](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1936v03/d85)). This proves campaign use during 1936, but it postdates the 1 January 1936 bookmark and does not by itself prove use or issue by that start date. The accepted Italian 100% military issue band remains the user's gameplay calibration; this review does not reclassify it as a sourced inventory claim. A pre-1 January record would be needed to assert historical use by the 1936 bookmark.

For civilian protection, the Pirelli Historical Foundation catalogues a confidential 17 April 1936 draft convention with UNPA concerning sale of masks to the civilian population ([Pirelli Historical Archive record](https://www.fondazionepirelli.org/archivio-storico/documenti/detail/IT-PIRELLI-MISC0001-0000012744/34-34-u-n-p-34-materiale-protezione-popolazione-civile-34.html)). The State Archive of Udine inventory lists a 31 May 1939 Civil Mobilization Office circular titled “Civil population — gas masks,” within a file series concerning distribution to civilians and company personnel ([State Archive of Udine, UNPA inventory, p. 41](https://archiviodistatoudine.cultura.gov.it/fileadmin/risorse/img_patrimonio/inventari/unpa_inventario.pdf)). These records establish planning and a dated instruction, but the public inventory does not show deliveries completed or masks issued by the August start, nor any amount.

**Current code and calibration.** ITA's chemical technologies, 480 chlorine lots, 360 mustard lots, the accepted commander traits, and one state-158 facility are present at both bookmarks. Montecatini is available as the current country-restricted MIO, but no military production queue is assigned. No CBRN scientist is in the Italian roster. The 1938 Directorate is a documented historical distinction, but no current gameplay or UI consumer justifies adding a standalone flag for it.

**Disposition and evidence gap.** Make no extra Italy setup change from this source set. Do not turn the Directorate into an unused status flag, extra building, stockpile, factory line, or named scientist. For a civilian equipment grant, require a dated delivery/issue return completed by August 1939 and the issue totals/localities; the May circular alone is not proof that masks were handed out. For an offensive-stock increase, require a dated army depot inventory or procurement/receipt record after the Ethiopian campaign. For a named Italian advisor/scientist, require a service roster or appointment that identifies the person and CBRN role by the 1939 start.
