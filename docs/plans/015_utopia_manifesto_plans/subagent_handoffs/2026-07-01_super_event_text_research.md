# Event 015 super-event text research handoff

Scope used: `utopia_manifesto_super_event_prompt.md`, Event 015 core and AI-assets specs, asset prompt, and the quote or remark guidance in `chaos-redux-super-events`. No gameplay, localisation, `.gfx`, or audio files touched.

## 1. New Utopia peaceful proclamation

### Selected final title
`New Utopia`

Why: safest final choice. It is already the route's researched cosmetic identity in the Event 015 spec, reads cleanly on a super-event card, and avoids drifting into unsourced poetic wording.

### Title candidates considered
- `New Utopia` - strongest. Specific, public, already aligned with the spec.
- `Utopian Commonwealth` - workable, but more generic and less memorable.
- `The Commonwealth Proclaimed` - readable, but too invented for a research-gated final pick.

### Main quote candidates
1. `if care is taken to keep the public stores full no private man can want anything`
Author: Thomas More
Source: *Utopia*
Year: 1516
URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2130/2130-h/2130-h.htm
Fit: directly names public stores and abundance without sounding triumphalist.
Copyright: underlying work is public domain.

2. `Neither was there any among them that lacked`
Source: Acts 4:34, King James Version
Year: 1611 translation
URL: https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+4%3A34&version=KJV
Fit: concise and strongly tied to common provision.
Copyright: KJV is public domain.

3. `many things in the commonwealth of Utopia that I rather wish, than hope, to see followed in our governments`
Author: Thomas More
Source: *Utopia*
Year: 1516
URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2130/2130-h/2130-h.htm
Fit: excellent for uncertainty and durability, but weaker than candidate 1 on the common-stores angle.
Copyright: public domain.

### Selected main quote
`if care is taken to keep the public stores full no private man can want anything`

Attribution: Thomas More, *Utopia* (1516)
Confidence: high
Copyright note: public domain source text

### Button remark candidates
- `Rather wish, than hope.` - Thomas More, *Utopia*, 1516. URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2130/2130-h/2130-h.htm
- `According as he had need.` - Acts 4:35 KJV. URL: https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+4%3A34-36&version=KJV
- `No town desires to enlarge its bounds.` - usable only if the main agent wants a More line and re-verifies the exact chosen translation before localisation.

### Selected button remark
`Rather wish, than hope.`

Why: restrained, literary, and slightly uncertain, which fits a peaceful proclamation that still may not last.

### Implementation cautions
- If More is used for both quote and remark, keep both lines from the same translation family. Do not mix Robinson-style archaism with the Gutenberg modernized text in one package.
- Do not escalate the title into something apocalyptic or messianic. The route is a visible commonwealth announcement, not the end of history.

## 2. Marked Bounds State dark route

### Selected final title
`Marked Bounds`

Why: strongest researched fit. It is already the route's established identity in the spec, short, severe, and centered on the doctrine becoming public.

### Title candidates considered
- `Marked Bounds` - strongest. Route-specific and already legible.
- `The Marked Bounds Doctrine` - acceptable, but slightly long and more bureaucratic than a super-event title wants.
- `Needful Land` - thematically relevant, but too abstract once the doctrine has already become public and coercive.

### Main quote candidates
1. `they account it a very just cause of war, when one people suffers a part of their soil to lie idle and uncultivated`
Author: Thomas More
Source: *Utopia*
Year: 1516
URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2130/2130-h/2130-h.htm
Fit: near-perfect for the route because it is the exact clause the dark path literalizes.
Copyright: public domain.

2. `Justice being taken away, then, what are kingdoms but great robberies?`
Author: Augustine of Hippo
Source: *The City of God*, Book IV, Chapter 4
Approx. year: early 5th century
URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45304/45304-h/45304-h.htm
Fit: excellent moral condemnation of a state that turns public doctrine into organized seizure.
Copyright: public domain.

3. `Remove not the ancient landmark, which thy fathers have set.`
Source: Proverbs 22:28, King James Version
Year: 1611 translation
URL: https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+22%3A28&version=KJV
Fit: sharp boundary imagery, but it reads more like a warning from outside the regime than the regime's own doctrine.
Copyright: KJV is public domain.

### Selected main quote
`they account it a very just cause of war, when one people suffers a part of their soil to lie idle and uncultivated`

Attribution: Thomas More, *Utopia* (1516)
Confidence: high
Copyright note: public domain source text

### Button remark candidates
- `Remove not the ancient landmark.` - Proverbs 22:28 KJV. URL: https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+22%3A28&version=KJV
- `A very just cause?` - fragment from Thomas More, *Utopia*. URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2130/2130-h/2130-h.htm
- `Great robberies.` - fragment from Augustine, *The City of God*. URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45304/45304-h/45304-h.htm

### Selected button remark
`Remove not the ancient landmark.`

Why: terse, grim, boundary-specific, and it reads like the judgment already gathering around the regime.

### Implementation cautions
- The selected More quote works only if the route is clearly framed as the manifesto's land clause turned coercive. If the event text softens the route into mere strategic expansion, this quote stops fitting.
- `Remove not the ancient landmark.` should be attributed as scripture, not as a generic proverb.
- Avoid using both Augustine and Proverbs in the same final package unless the description text stays secular and concrete. Otherwise the whole card may drift into sermon language.
