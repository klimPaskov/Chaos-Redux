## 5. Current defects that this addendum must close

### 5.1 Constituent agency

`africa_world_accept_constituent_consent` and `africa_world_record_constituent_refusal` are both package-actor decisions.

That lets one player author both sides of a constitutional dispute.

The package actor must issue an invitation, while the constituent country must answer through an event with accept, negotiate, or refuse options and an independent AI evaluation.

### 5.2 Withdrawal

`africa_world_accept_constituent_withdrawal` immediately records departure.

There is no constituent-owned notice, negotiation window, command or basing settlement, debt settlement, or contested exit.

Withdrawal must become a timed two-actor procedure with peaceful, renegotiated, coerced, and war outcomes.

### 5.3 Sovereign completion

`africa_world_complete_sovereign_package` currently marks the package complete without calling the full heartland and constituent proof.

Every capstone must require a selected route institution, all required shared lanes, a resolved founding crisis, a ratified withdrawal law, sufficient voluntary membership, no pending constituent response, and heartland proof.

### 5.4 Quorum

`constant:africa_world_roster.minimum_constituents` is currently one.

Because the actor is included in the foundation, one can mean a polity with no external voluntary constituent.

The final proof must require at least two voluntary external constituents when three or more eligible external constituents existed at founding.

When fewer than three eligible external constituents existed, the sparse-continent rule must require every eligible external constituent to be resolved and at least one to be a voluntary member.

Coerced constituents may add capacity and territory but must not satisfy the voluntary quorum.

### 5.5 Successor and breakup

`africa_world_commit_package_successor` records a successor but does not transfer the package role, focus package, constituent arrays, route identity, or political ledger.

It must either perform a complete successor handoff to another existing eligible sovereign or end in a complete breakup.

`africa_world_record_package_breakup` currently subtracts `constant:africa_world_roster.breakup_authority_loss`, while that constant is negative.

That operation appears to increase authority and must be corrected during implementation.

### 5.6 Shared focus lanes

The shared lane prerequisite in each external tree places multiple focus IDs inside one `prerequisite = { ... }` block.

That syntax is an OR gate under the offline `National focus modding` reference, so the current shared lanes are reachable and there is no mutual-exclusion deadlock.

Preserve that same-block OR structure.

The shared lane must call a route-specific helper and produce route-specific law, command, representation, or settlement state.

North America, South America, and Oceania already have some route-specific shared-lane helpers.

The Middle East, Europe, and Asia need equivalent helpers, while the existing helpers for the other three packages need proof and event hooks.

### 5.7 Sponsorship, union, war, and terminal state

Sponsorship is currently one material obligation.

Union is currently an immediate partner flag and host cosmetic identity.

Continental war is currently a pairwise government-toppling path.

Terminal proof is mostly a settled counter and bare resolution flags.

Each needs a bounded lifecycle that respects constituent votes, route compatibility, alliances, defeat, successor state, cleanup, and AI.

## 7. Constituent agency and ratification protocol

### 7.1 Invitation

Keep the existing `africa_world_constituent_countries` array.

Replace the package-owned acceptance and refusal decisions with one package-owned targeted decision named `africa_world_invite_constituent_to_compact`.

It costs `constant:africa_world_package_protocol.invitation_pp_cost`, proposed as 25 political power.

It can target only a pending, independent, living, same-continent registered constituent that is not already answering another package.

The decision saves the package actor as a short-lived event target and fires `africa_world_package.1` on the constituent.

`africa_world_package.1` offers three choices.

| Choice | Constituent result | Package result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accept | Set consented and voluntary member status | Increment voluntary count and authority, then fire `.2` to the package actor |
| Negotiate | Set counterterms pending | Add one pending response and fire `.3` to the package actor |
| Refuse | Set refused status | Remove the outstanding invitation, reduce legitimacy, and fire `.4` to the package actor |

The package actor cannot select the constituent's answer.

### 7.2 Counterterms

`africa_world_package.3` lets the package actor accept the counterterms, revise the offer, or close the invitation.

Accepting costs `constant:africa_world_package_protocol.counterterm_pp_cost`, proposed as 35 political power, and grants consent with a route-sensitive safeguard flag on the constituent.

Revising fires `africa_world_package.5` on the constituent and gives it a final accept or refuse choice.

Closing records refusal without coercion.

The safeguard flag must affect later withdrawal and union votes.

Examples are `africa_world_constituent_local_command_guarantee`, `africa_world_constituent_resource_veto`, `africa_world_constituent_language_guarantee`, and `africa_world_constituent_basing_restriction`.

Use only the safeguard relevant to the active package route.

### 7.3 Withdrawal notice

Add a constituent-owned decision named `africa_world_submit_constituent_withdrawal_notice`.

It appears only to a living, independent constituent member whose package actor still exists.

It costs `constant:africa_world_package_protocol.withdrawal_notice_pp_cost`, proposed as 25 political power.

It sets a notice flag, increments the package pending-response count, activates `africa_world_constituent_withdrawal_window`, and fires `africa_world_package.6` to the package actor.

The proposed withdrawal window is 60 days.

The package actor chooses one of three answers in `.6`.

| Answer | Cost | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accept an orderly exit | 25 political power | Fire `.7`, settle faction, command, basing, guarantee, and sponsorship ties, then record voluntary withdrawal |
| Offer constitutional safeguards | 35 political power | Fire `.8` on the constituent and keep the window open |
| Contest the notice | 75 political power and an authority loss | Fire `.9` on the constituent and open a coercion or limited-war branch |

The constituent answers a safeguard offer through `.10` or `.11`.

Timeout event `.12` resolves an unanswered notice as peaceful withdrawal unless an active war, subject relationship, or valid contested-exit flag requires a settlement event.

### 7.4 Coercion

Keep `africa_world_coerce_constituent_compliance`, but make it available only after a constituent has refused a real invitation or rejected a real withdrawal settlement.

Coercion must set a public coerced status, reduce legitimacy and authority, add a grievance memory, and block that constituent from satisfying voluntary quorum.

It must not silently convert refusal into consent.

When the package actor and constituent are already at war, the coercion decision must defer to the registered continental-war settlement.

### 7.5 Ratification proof

Replace the count-only proof with `africa_world_package_ratification_is_proven`.

It must require all of the following.

- `africa_world_package_heartland_is_proven = yes`.
- Exactly one grounded route institution is complete.
- The distinctive founding crisis has a success or compromise result.
- Every required route-specific shared lane is complete.
- `africa_world_package_withdrawal_law_ratified` is set.
- No constituent is pending an invitation, counterterm, or withdrawal response.
- At least two voluntary external constituents are active when the frozen eligible external count is three or more.
- Every eligible external constituent is resolved and at least one is voluntary when the frozen eligible external count is below three.
- Authority, legitimacy, and capacity each meet a proposed minimum of 50.
- No successor, exile, breakup, or active continental-war review is unresolved.

`africa_world_complete_sovereign_package` must call this proof before setting sovereign completion or incrementing any host counter.

## 10. Four sponsorship modes

The Event 012 host chooses one sponsorship mode before the candidate answers.

The selected candidate receives `africa_world_package.20`.

The candidate can accept, negotiate safeguards, or refuse.

Accepted sponsorship does not replace the candidate's constituent congress.

It modifies the opening conditions and creates a continuing obligation.

## 11. Two-continent union lifecycle

A two-continent union remains a relationship between existing countries.

It does not create a new tag and does not annex the partner.

The host may use the already delivered union cosmetic identity after ratification.

The partner remains an autonomous union partner with its own country, focus tree, constituent ledger, and route history.

### 11.1 Union phases

Use `africa_world_union_status` values proposed, ballots, ratified, active, strained, dissolving, and dissolved.

The convention can begin only when both actors are sovereign, neither is in successor or breakup review, both have withdrawal laws, neither has a pending constituent response, and they are not at war.

The package actor answers through `africa_world_package.700`.

If both actors agree, each actor fires ballots to its voluntary constituents through `.701`.

Each side must meet its own voluntary quorum.

Coerced members do not count toward ratification.

### 11.2 Constitution

`africa_world_open_union_convention` selects the partner and opens the proposal.

`africa_world_ratify_union_constitution` requires both actor approvals and both constituent quorum proofs.

The constitution records these terms without a new GUI.

- Union name and adjective come from the existing union cosmetic tag ladder.
- The Event 012 host remains the cosmetic host.
- The partner remains an autonomous country.
- A distributed government flag records that no constituent capital is automatically erased.
- Existing factions are preserved when compatible.
- If one actor leads a faction and the other is factionless, a separate invitation response is required.
- If both lead incompatible factions, ratification is blocked until a diplomatic settlement resolves leadership.
- Mutual defence, resource coordination, and constituent withdrawal remain separate ratified clauses.
- Both focus trees remain loaded.
- Neither actor's route history is replaced.

### 11.3 Union dissolution

Peaceful dissolution drops the host union cosmetic tag, preserves both sovereign actors, closes mutual obligations, restores independent faction state where valid, and records the union as dissolved.

Contested dissolution enters the continental-war protocol.

No union flag may count as a terminal resolution until ratification is complete.

## 21. Research basis

The European route draws on the 1930 Briand memorandum's attempt to combine political cooperation with a common market and customs union, while recognizing resistance from national sovereignty and the economic crisis.

Source: European Parliament, `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/100books/en/detail/14/memorandum-on-the-organization-of-a-regime-of-european-federal-union?info=en`.

The European route also draws on the Pan-Europe movement's interwar conferences and its collision with nationalist and fascist repression.

Source: European Parliament, `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/100books/en/detail/18/pan-europe?info=en`.

The North American route uses the Pan-American conference system, the Montevideo principle of equal state rights, and the Good Neighbor commitment to non-intervention as concrete pressure against automatic continental hierarchy.

Sources: Organization of American States, `https://www.oas.org/en/about/our_history.asp`, and United States Office of the Historian, `https://history.state.gov/milestones/1921-1936/good-neighbor`.

The South American route uses the 1915 Argentina, Brazil, and Chile peace and mediation treaty as a historical basis for a regional balance mechanism.

Source: United States Office of the Historian, `https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1915/d35`.

The Middle Eastern route uses the League mandate framework, Iraq's 1932 independence, and the 1937 Saadabad Pact's consultation, non-interference, and non-aggression language.

Sources: United Nations, `https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-176218/`, and United States Office of the Historian, `https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1937v01/d820`.

The Asian Relations Conference material is later than 1936 and is inspiration only.

Its continent-wide representation, public and group sessions, and effort to avoid unresolved political controversies support the distributed-centres design without being treated as a preexisting 1936 institution.

Sources: Nehru Archive, `https://nehruarchive.in/documents/invitation-to-inter-asian-relations-conference-7-september-1946-l5w27q` and `https://nehruarchive.in/documents/asian-relations-organisation-2-april-1947-p449e`.

The 1947 Canberra agreement is also later inspiration only.

Because the original institution was created by metropolitan powers, the Oceanian package should invert that precedent through constituent and island consent.

Source: Pacific Community, `https://www.spc.int/fr/actualite/actus-web/2022/03/quand-tout-a-commence-la-communaute-du-pacifique-a-75-ans`.

The existing Event 012 research package remains the primary source for Pan-Africanism, Ethiopian diplomacy, colonial administration, restoration limits, regional institutions, and the warning that historical statehood does not equal automatic consent.
