# Africa Super-Event Text Research — Final Handoff

Date: 2026-07-30

Status: final text recommendation for the four approved Event 12 super-event roles. This note supersedes the 2026-07-29 text note for source-edition and wording status only. It remains a research handoff; no localisation, event script, GUI, GFX, audio, spreadsheet, or gameplay files were edited.

## Scope and decision rule

The four roles were treated as distinct public-history moments: Africa is one, the Scramble response, Continental wars, and The World. Candidates were compared for exact wording, traceable attribution, source edition, date, fit with the role brief, route neutrality, and copyright or permissions risk. The selected direct references are intentionally short. The four selected button lines are original Chaos Redux text, not historical quotations, and must not receive a historical attribution.

The parent implementation owner should freeze the named source edition in the final source ledger before placing any direct quote in localisation. A later scan, transcription, or Project Gutenberg page is evidence for the wording, not automatically a blanket licence for every edition or jurisdiction.

## 1. Africa is one

### Role fit

This role is the continent becoming a public political subject. The title and quote describe institution-building and entry into the record rather than a single army, dynasty, or imposed colonial boundary. They remain usable for the approved federation, elected republic, council of crowns, people’s congress, command order, confederation, and Covenant routes when the dynamic description names the actual route.

### Title candidates

| Candidate | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| `THE CONTINENT TAKES ITS SEAT` | Selected. It gives the completion a public and institutional action while leaving the constitution route-neutral. |
| `AFRICA ANSWERS FOR ITSELF` | Backup. Strong reversal of the outside gaze, but too close to the selected button line and less institutionally specific as a title. |
| `THE CONTINENTAL ORDER` | Rejected. Clear but generic, with no sense of the first public assumption of a seat. |

### Main quote candidates

Attribution and wording confidence are high when frozen to the cited scan. The underlying early publication is public-domain by age in the United States; later scans and jurisdictional rules still need separate recording. | Selected. It names the act of building a continental political body and does not require every route to be peaceful. |
| `Unite we must.` | Kwame Nkrumah, OAU summit speech, May 1963, in an [official African Union speech collection](https://au.int/sites/default/files/speeches/38523-sp-oau_summit_may_1963_speeches.pdf). ### Selected package and sources

- Main quote: `building up for themselves a great nation in Africa.`
- Speaker and source work: Marcus Garvey, “Africa for the Africans,” *Negro World*, 22 April 1922. The fragment is the objective clause describing what the Universal Negro Improvement Association advocates.
- Source URLs: [PBS provenance and transcription](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/garvey-his-own-words/), [proofread scan page containing the exact sentence](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Marcus_Garvey_-_Philosophy_and_Opinions_of_Marcus_Garvey_%282009_printing%29.pdf/42), and [University of Ghana metadata for the 1923 collection](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/items/2d5e716a-d723-48cd-8227-3c769cf99b1a).
- Attribution confidence: high. Wording confidence: high when the Wikisource scan/transcription is used as the frozen wording witness and the 1922 article is retained as the historical provenance.
- Copyright note: the 1922 publication and 1923 collection are old enough for strong United States public-domain confidence. The later scan/transcription has separate site or edition terms, and wider-jurisdiction treatment should be recorded rather than assumed.
- Recommended original button remark: `The continent will answer for itself.`

### Cultural and sensitivity note

Garvey’s line is historically relevant to Black self-determination and continental institution-building, but Garvey’s wider Black-nationalist and separatist rhetoric is politically specific. The parent should explicitly approve using this line across coercive and high-chaos outcomes; it must not be presented as a universal endorsement of one ideology.

The dynamic description should name the final public country or institution, the actual constitution or institution, the settlement method used for the last regions, and the unresolved integration burden. Military control alone must not be described as completed integration.

## 2. Scramble response

### Role fit

This role is the external response to a sovereign African actor, not a replay of colonial triumph. The selected legal fragment exposes the old language of outside claim-making so the title and button line can reverse its direction.

### Title candidates

| Candidate | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| `THE MAPMAKERS RETURN` | Selected. It makes the outside powers’ response legible while implying that old mapmaking has returned against a unified African subject. |
| `THE OLD CLAIMS COME DUE` | Backup. It communicates inherited claims but sounds more like a debt event than a recognition crisis. |
| `THE SCRAMBLE BREAKS` | Rejected. It is clear but risks making a high-stakes colonial reversal sound playful. |

### Main quote candidates

| Candidate | Source, confidence, and rights | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `to enable them, if need be, to make good any claims of their own.` | General Act of the Conference at Berlin, Chapter VI, Article 34, signed 26 February 1885. The exact wording is now checked against Edward Hertslet, R. W. Brant, and H. L. Sherwood, *The Map of Africa by Treaty*, 3rd ed., London, 1909, vol. II, Article XXXIV, printed p. 484, with a second English transcription as corroboration. Attribution and wording confidence are high for this frozen edition. | Selected. The fragment makes external claim-making visible and lets the event reverse its point of view. |
| `It is us today. It is you tomorrow.` | Haile Selassie, League of Nations address, 30 June 1936, with [FDR Library event provenance](https://www.fdrlibrary.marist.edu/daybyday/event/june-1936-2/). Attribution and date confidence are high, but the speech was delivered in Amharic through an interpreter and English versions vary; rights and exact wording remain unresolved. | Backup only. Powerful warning, but not a stable paste-ready source. |
| `to protect existing rights` | General Act of the Conference at Berlin, Article 35, in the same [Hertslet 1909 OCR](https://archive.org/stream/mapofafricabytre02hert/mapofafricabytre02hert_djvu.txt). Attribution is high, but the fragment is euphemistic without context and less pointed than Article 34. | Rejected for the main quote. |

### Selected package and sources

- Main quote: `to enable them, if need be, to make good any claims of their own.`
- Source work and date: General Act of the Conference at Berlin, Chapter VI, Article 34, signed 26 February 1885.
- Frozen wording witness: Hertslet, Edward, R. W. Brant, and H. L. Sherwood, *The Map of Africa by Treaty*, 3rd ed., London, Printed for H.M.S.O. by Harrison and Sons, 1909, vol. II, Article XXXIV, printed p. 484. [Internet Archive volume metadata](https://archive.org/details/mapofafricabytre02hert) and [full OCR with Article XXXIV](https://archive.org/stream/mapofafricabytre02hert/mapofafricabytre02hert_djvu.txt).
- Corroborating and contextual sources: [English treaty transcription](https://www.internationalwaterlaw.org/documents/regionaldocs/1885GeneralActBerlinConference.pdf) and [German Federal Foreign Office archive context](https://archiv.diplo.de/arc-en/the-political-archive/general-act-2684414).
- Attribution confidence: high. Wording confidence: high for the Hertslet 1909 edition; the earlier web-only translator blocker is closed for wording purposes. Hertslet’s title page says the volume is not an official publication, so the final ledger should identify it as the edition witness rather than claim it is the treaty original.
- Copyright note: the 1885 act and 1909 edition are public-domain by age with strong confidence, and the Internet Archive record reports no known restrictions. Keep the edition and jurisdiction caveat in the ledger.
- Recommended original button remark: `Africa answers through its own institutions.`

### Cultural and route fit

The German Federal Foreign Office context records that African representatives were not consulted and that the act intensified colonial exploitation and oppression. The selected fragment therefore exposes a colonial legal mechanism; it must never be presented as a celebration of the Berlin Conference.

The dynamic description should name the unified African actor, each outside responder, the response class such as recognition, sanctions, ultimatum, expedition planning, or open war, and Africa’s answer. Do not use an unnamed rival, a generic continent label, or a neutral adventure framing.

## 3. Continental wars

### Role fit

This role is a conflict between political projects at continental scale. The selected line keeps the focus on war aims and policy rather than burning-globe spectacle, and it remains compatible with diplomatic, ideological, economic, dynastic, and territorial routes.

### Title candidates

| Candidate | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| `CONTINENTS UNDER ARMS` | Selected. It states the scale without implying that the world is empty or already ended. |
| `THE ARGUMENT REACHES THE FRONT` | Backup. It connects policy and war but is too close to the selected button line. |
| `THE WAR OF THE GREAT POWERS` | Rejected. It assumes a specific geopolitical vocabulary and can obscure a non-state continental actor. |

### Main quote candidates

| Candidate | Source, confidence, and rights | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `War is a mere continuation of policy by other means.` | Carl von Clausewitz, *On War*, Book I, Chapter I, J. J. Graham translation, first published 1874 and represented by the 1909 London reprint. Project Gutenberg identifies the translation and edition history. Attribution and wording confidence are high; rights confidence is high by age for the translation. | Selected. It ties the front to political stakes and does not prescribe one ideology. |
| `War is cruelty, and you cannot refine it;` | William T. Sherman, letter to Atlanta citizens, 12 September 1864, preserved in his [Project Gutenberg memoir](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2617/pg2617-images.html). Attribution and wording confidence are high in the public-domain memoir. | Backup when a human-cost-forward tone is wanted, but the Atlanta and US Civil War context is narrower. |
| `the strong do what they can and the weak suffer what they must.` | Thucydides, *History of the Peloponnesian War*, Melian Dialogue, Richard Crawley [Project Gutenberg translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/7142/pg7142-images.html). Attribution and wording confidence are high. | Rejected because its deterministic coercive frame risks making suffering sound inevitable. |

### Selected package and sources

- Main quote: `War is a mere continuation of policy by other means.`
- Speaker and source work: Carl von Clausewitz, *On War*, Book I, Chapter I, J. J. Graham translation, 1874; the cited page is the 1909 London reprint represented in the Project Gutenberg text.
- Source URL: [Project Gutenberg, *On War*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1946/1946-h/1946-h.htm).
- Attribution confidence: high. Wording confidence: high for the Graham translation. Copyright note: the translation is public-domain by age; retain the source edition in the package ledger rather than relying only on the hosting site’s licence.
- Recommended original button remark: `The arguments have reached the front.`

### Cultural and route fit

The dynamic description must name both continent-scale rivals, the conflict family or war aim, the front or corridor, the alliance method, and the human stakes. If a rival, war aim, or front is unavailable, block the super-event text rather than falling back to a generic apocalypse line.

## 4. The World

### Role fit

This role is terminal political exhaustion and inheritance, not extinction. The selected line warns against empty triumph while leaving surviving institutions and peoples visible.

### Title candidates

| Candidate | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| `ONE WORLD REMAINS` | Selected. It signals final political exhaustion while leaving the surviving order and living peoples visible. |
| `THE LAST BORDER` | Backup. Strong and concise, but it can imply a geographic border event rather than the surviving political order. |
| `THE FINAL SETTLEMENT` | Rejected. It is explicit but too administrative for the terminal role. |

### Main quote candidates

| Candidate | Source, confidence, and rights | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Nothing beside remains.` | Percy Bysshe Shelley, “Ozymandias,” first published in *The Examiner*, January 1818; exact text checked against the Hutchinson 1914 edition. Attribution and wording confidence are high; rights confidence is high by age. | Selected. It is short, final, and cautionary without claiming planetary or human extinction. |
| `In the Parliament of man, the Federation of the world.` | Alfred, Lord Tennyson, “Locksley Hall,” [*Early Poems* Project Gutenberg edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8601/8601-h/8601-h.htm). Attribution and age confidence are high. | Rejected for the main quote because it privileges a peaceful federation and fails for submission, victory, and Covenant routes. Route-specific backup only. |
| `Covenants, without the Sword, are but Words.` | Thomas Hobbes, *Leviathan*, Chapter XVII, [Project Gutenberg edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3207/3207-h/3207-h.htm). Attribution and age confidence are high. | Rejected for the main quote because it privileges a coercive settlement and undermines negotiated routes. |

### Selected package and sources

- Main quote: `Nothing beside remains.`
- Speaker and source work: Percy Bysshe Shelley, “Ozymandias,” first published in *The Examiner*, January 1818.
- Source URL: [Hutchinson 1914 edition of “Ozymandias”](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Complete_Poetical_Works_of_Percy_Bysshe_Shelley_%28ed._Hutchinson%2C_1914%29/Ozymandias). A [University of Virginia first-printing transcription](https://anthology.lib.virginia.edu/work/ShelleyP/shelley-ozymandias) is useful as independent provenance, while the Hutchinson page is the frozen wording witness.
- Attribution confidence: high. Wording confidence: high for the cited edition. Copyright note: the poem and 1914 edition are public-domain by age; cite the underlying poem and edition rather than treating a later transcription page as the only rights basis.
- Recommended original button remark: `The last border is an archive.`

### Cultural and route fit

The dynamic description must state the surviving public actor, last rival, resolution method such as union, federation, submission, negotiated settlement, victory, or Covenant, final institution, inheritance burden, and human continuity. It must not imply extinction, a globe explosion, or a generic map wipe. The sentence “Their peoples remain” or equivalent continuity wording belongs in the dynamic description when the implementation brief requires it, not in the quote attribution.

## Button and cultural-remark research

The four selected button lines are original Chaos Redux-authored text. They have no external author, source work, or direct-copyright dependency:

| Role | Selected original line | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Africa is one | `The continent will answer for itself.` | A concise declaration of political agency; it does not choose a constitution or ideology. |
| Scramble response | `Africa answers through its own institutions.` | Gives the African actor public agency through the institutions named by the dynamic description. |
| Continental wars | `The arguments have reached the front.` | Connects Clausewitz’s policy frame to the active front and preserves human stakes. |
| The World | `The last border is an archive.` | Makes the terminal moment about memory, record, and inheritance rather than extinction. |

Considered sourced backups:

- `Unite we must.` — Kwame Nkrumah, OAU summit speech, May 1963. The line is verified in the [official African Union speech PDF](https://au.int/sites/default/files/speeches/38523-sp-oau_summit_may_1963_speeches.pdf), with [archive metadata](https://archives.au.int/handle/123456789/10452). Attribution confidence is high, but the [African Union legal notice](https://au.int/en/legal_notice) leaves permission unresolved. Keep it non-shipping unless permission is documented.
- `It is us today. It is you tomorrow.` — Haile Selassie, League of Nations address, 30 June 1936. The [FDR Library event record](https://www.fdrlibrary.marist.edu/daybyday/event/june-1936-2/) verifies the speech and date, but the address was delivered in Amharic through an interpreter and English versions vary. Keep it as a research backup, not paste-ready text.
- `To promote the unity and solidarity of the African States.` — OAU Charter, 25 May 1963. The [African Union charter PDF](https://au.int/web/sites/default/files/treaties/7759-file-oau_charter_1963.pdf) and [UN Treaty Collection metadata](https://treaties.un.org/pages/showDetails.aspx?objid=08000002801314cf) provide provenance. Rights and exact reuse conditions are not resolved; it is less vivid than the original button lines.
- `The old order changeth.` — Alfred, Lord Tennyson, “The Passing of Arthur,” in the [Project Gutenberg *Early Poems* edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8601/8601-h/8601-h.htm). Public-domain and traceable, but less final than the selected World button and better as a route-specific transition backup.

No modern song, film, game, or recent book dialogue is used. No recognizable modern copyrighted line should be substituted for the selected originals without a new rights review.

## Dynamic-description contract

- Use the direct public country, coalition, institution, and ruler names supplied by the current runtime state. Do not use “an unnamed rival,” a generic continent label, or invented actor text.
- Africa is one: include the final actor, constitution or institution, settlement method for the last regions, and unresolved integration cost. Do not call military control “integration” without the required political state.
- Scramble response: include the outside actor or coalition, response class, and Africa’s answer. Response classes may be recognition, sanctions, ultimatum, expedition planning, or open war only when the runtime state supplies that fact.
- Continental wars: include both rival continental actors, war aim or conflict family, front or corridor, alliance method, and human stakes. Do not replace missing facts with an apocalypse cliché.
- The World: include the final actor, last rival, resolution method, final institution, inheritance burden, and surviving peoples. Never imply extinction or a generic world wipe.
- Block firing when a required actor, institution, rival, war aim, response state, or resolution method is unavailable. No generic fallback is authorized by this research handoff.

## Language and sensitivity safeguard

All four selected packages are English. Do not add a source-language line, translation, profanity, slogan, or cultural allusion without a direct source and appropriate native-speaker review.

The two user-mandated fictional Afaan Oromoo strings remain restricted to fictional ruler, regnal, court, or title flavour and must never appear in a super-event title, quote, attribution, description, button, identifier, filename, tag, sprite, audio cue, or asset name:

- `qaama saalaa koo xuuxaa`
- `haadha kee waliin wal qunnamtii saalaa raawwadhe`

Their exact idiomatic meaning, register, and spelling still require native-speaker review elsewhere. This does not block the English super-event text package because these strings are explicitly excluded from all four super-event surfaces.
