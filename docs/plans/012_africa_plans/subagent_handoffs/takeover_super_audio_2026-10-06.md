The official Clinton Presidential Library page describes the 1994 state dinner as a diplomatic ceremony for the newly elected South African President and says national anthems were played before introductions.

The South African Government describes Nkosi Sikelel’ iAfrika as a church hymn that became an anthem at political meetings and was sung as an act of defiance during apartheid.

Those sources make this recording a direct musical image of foreign recognition of African political authority, while its source is a named hymn and formal state-band performance rather than a generic tribal loop.

The Wikimedia Commons file page supplies the audio-only FLAC used here, separates composition, performance, and recording rights, identifies the composition as public domain, marks the federal-government performance and recording public domain in the United States, and reports the duration, metadata, and source SHA-1.

The official NARA permissions page explains that federal government works are uncopyrighted in the United States, but it also notes that NARA records may contain rights in other countries and that NARA does not license its records.
