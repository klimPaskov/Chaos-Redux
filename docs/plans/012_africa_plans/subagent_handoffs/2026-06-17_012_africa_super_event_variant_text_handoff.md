## Detailed notes by blocked role

### 1. Forest Parliament reveal

- Best fit found remains Job 12:7.
- This is the cleanest source-backed line for nonhuman testimony entering formal politics without turning the route into a joke.
- The button fragment `Ask now the beasts.` is short, public domain, and UI-safe.
- Title direction should stay parliamentary or congress-based. `The Forest Parliament` is the strongest plain-language candidate.
- Blocker status: not source-blocked; still acceptance-blocked because no final slot/media package is accepted in current docs.

### 2. World Root Mandate

- Job 12:10 remains the strongest candidate because it explicitly binds all living things and mankind in one line.
- It fits the route better than Whitman because the route is juridical/covenantal, not only visionary.
- The button fragment `The breath of all mankind.` is strong, short, and solemn.
- Blocker status: not source-blocked; still blocked on whether this becomes a dedicated super-event versus only a mission/focus milestone.

### 3. Bestiary Clause reveal

- Quote support is strong: Job 12:7 or Job 12:10 both work.
- The weak point is the button.
- `The animals have papers.` is memorable but is not a quotation and can undercut solemnity if paired with uncanny ceremonial art.
- Safest parent-ready package is:
  - title direction: `The Bestiary Clause`
  - quote: Job 12:7
  - button: `Ask now the beasts.`
- Blocker status: title/quote are effectively ready; remark tone remains the real blocker.

### 4. Parliament of Root and Fang escalation

- The route wants a mixed human/nonhuman constitutional escalation, not a comedic creature reveal.
- Job 12:10 remains the best quote.
- `Root and fang.` is a workable original button, but it is not source-backed and should be treated as an original allusive line, not a quotation.
- If parent wants every visible line source-backed, switch the button to `the breath of all mankind.` from Job 12:10 instead.
- Blocker status: source-backed quote exists; button-source policy choice remains open.

### 5. archive-world union terminal

- This is the least secure remaining variant.
- The strongest thematic line is still the Comte-attributed line, but current evidence is through public-domain secondary witnesses rather than a verified primary French edition.
- Safest quote fallback is Shakespeare: `What’s past is prologue.`
- Safest button remains the original line `The record remains.`
- Blocker status: attribution confidence, plus no accepted dedicated variant slot or media package in current docs.
