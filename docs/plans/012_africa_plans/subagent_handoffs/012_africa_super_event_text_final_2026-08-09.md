This is the first completed continent-wide political settlement. The title announces public institutional standing rather than a map colour or a single conquering army. The dynamic description must name the actual constitution, public institution, final actor, settlement method for the last regions, and one truthful integration burden. Territorial occupation alone is not enough.

### Role fit

This is the first globally visible outside response to a sovereign unified African actor. The title and quote reverse the old colonial claim-making frame. The dynamic description must name the outside actor or coalition, response class, public demand or action, and Africa's answer. It must never present the old Scramble as a neutral adventure.

| --- | --- |

| `THE SCRAMBLE BREAKS` | Rejected. The word `scramble` risks a playful tone for a high-stakes crisis. |

### Selected quote package

- Exact quote: `to enable them, if need be, to make good any claims of their own.`
- Source work and date: General Act of the Conference at Berlin, Chapter VI, Article XXXIV, signed 26 February 1885.
- Frozen wording witness: Edward Hertslet, R. W. Brant, and H. L. Sherwood, *The Map of Africa by Treaty*, 3rd edition, London, 1909, volume II, Article XXXIV, printed page 484. The title page explicitly says the compilation is not an official publication, so cite it as the edition witness rather than as the treaty's official publication.
- Source URLs: [Hertslet volume metadata](https://archive.org/details/mapofafricabytre02hert), [Hertslet OCR at Article XXXIV](https://archive.org/stream/mapofafricabytre02hert/mapofafricabytre02hert_djvu.txt), [corroborating treaty transcription](https://www.internationalwaterlaw.org/documents/regionaldocs/1885GeneralActBerlinConference.pdf), and [Federal Foreign Office historical context](https://archiv.diplo.de/arc-en/the-political-archive/general-act-2684414).
- Attribution confidence: high. Wording confidence: high for the Hertslet 1909 edition.
- Copyright note: the 1885 act and 1909 edition are public domain by age. The Internet Archive record reports no known copyright restrictions. The quote is a short fragment from a historical legal document.
- Context note: the Federal Foreign Office states that African representatives were not consulted and that Articles 34 and 35 established effective occupation. The quote must be used critically as the language of the outside claim system, never as a celebration of colonial rule.

## Contradictions and reconciliation

1. The 2026-07-16 research handoff called all four packages candidates and not wire-ready. The 2026-07-30 text handoff called them final text recommendations. This 2026-08-09 note supersedes both for text wording and approval state and upgrades all four to approved text packages.
2. Older Africa notes sometimes copy the PBS ellipsis wording `building up... a great nation in Africa.` The exact selected fragment is the complete 1923 collection wording `building up for themselves a great nation in Africa.` Freeze the 1923 witness and do not splice PBS's editorial ellipsis into localisation.
3. Older Scramble notes treated the English Article XXXIV translator as unresolved. The Hertslet 1909 edition closes the wording question. It is an edition witness, not an official treaty publication, and must be identified that way.
4. The Clausewitz phrase is printed as a Section 24 heading in the cited Graham translation. That is intentional. Preserve the heading wording exactly rather than silently replacing it with the nearby sentence `War is only a continuation of State policy by other means.`
5. The button lines were previously grouped under cultural remarks, but the selected lines are original. They must not be given historical attribution. Historical lines remain explicit, non-shipping backups with their own rights notes.
6. Slots `101`-`104` and audio IDs `58`-`61` are documented allocations from the earlier registry scan, not permanent engine reservations. A parent-owned live scan is still required before implementation. This is an implementation gate, not a text-package blocker.
