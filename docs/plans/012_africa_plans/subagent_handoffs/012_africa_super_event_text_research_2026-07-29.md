# Africa Super-Event Text Research Handoff

Date: 2026-07-29

Status: research complete for the four approved Event 12 super-event roles. This note is text research only. No localisation, event script, GUI, GFX, audio, spreadsheet, or gameplay files were edited.

## Scope and method

The four roles in the approved brief were treated as separate public-history moments: Africa is one, Scramble response, Continental wars, and The World. Candidate text was compared for exact wording, attribution, source traceability, date, fit with the route-neutral visual variants, and rights risk. Public-domain or historically primary material was preferred. Modern copyrighted lyrics, film dialogue, and game dialogue were not used.

The selected direct references are deliberately short. The source ledger below distinguishes the underlying historical or literary work from a later transcription, translation, scan, or institutional web page. A quote must be frozen to one cited edition before it is placed in localisation. The original button or cultural-remark lines are Chaos Redux-authored text and do not require external attribution.

### Selected package

- Main quote: `building up for themselves a great nation in Africa.`
- Speaker and work: Marcus Garvey, “Africa for the Africans,” *Negro World*, 22 April 1922. The fragment is taken from the sentence describing the Universal Negro Improvement Association’s objective.
- Source URLs: [PBS transcription and provenance](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/garvey-his-own-words/) and [proofread scan page for the exact wording](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Marcus_Garvey_-_Philosophy_and_Opinions_of_Marcus_Garvey_%282009_printing%29.pdf/42).
- Attribution confidence: high. Wording confidence: high if the cited scan is frozen. Rights confidence: medium-high for the underlying 1922 publication in the United States, with jurisdictional variation because Garvey died in 1940. The Wikisource page is a later transcription or scan and should not be treated as a blanket licence for every edition.
- Recommended original remark or button line: `The continent will answer for itself.`

### Cultural and route fit

The title and quote frame the moment as political construction and public entry, not as a military annexation. The dynamic description should name the final public country or institution, the settlement method used for the last regions, and the unresolved integration burden. It may mention a federal congress, elected republic, council of crowns, people’s congress, command order, confederation, or Covenant only when that is the actual route state. Military control alone should not be described as completed integration.

### Backup recommendation

Use `Unite we must.` as a non-shipping backup or as a button candidate only after African Union permission is resolved. A safer rights-neutral replacement is the original line `The continent has entered the record.`

### Selected package

- Main quote: `to enable them, if need be, to make good any claims of their own.`
- Source work: General Act of the Conference at Berlin, Chapter VI, Article 34, signed 26 February 1885.
- Source URLs: [Article 34 English transcription](https://www.internationalwaterlaw.org/documents/regionaldocs/1885GeneralActBerlinConference.pdf) and [German Federal Foreign Office historical context](https://archiv.diplo.de/arc-en/the-political-archive/general-act-2684414).
- Attribution confidence: high. Wording confidence: medium until the parent freezes a named English treaty edition or scan. Rights confidence: high for the 1885 underlying act by age, but the particular translation or transcription should be cited separately.
- Recommended original remark or button line: `Africa answers through its own institutions.`

### Cultural and route fit

This package reverses the direction of the 1885 claim language. The dynamic description should name the unified African actor and constitution, each outside responder, and the response class such as recognition, sanctions, ultimatum, expedition planning, or war. The old scramble must be presented as an external crisis over a sovereign African subject, not as a neutral adventure or a repeat of colonial triumph.

### Backup recommendation

`It will be you tomorrow.` is a short button backup derived from Selassie’s 1936 warning, but it remains rights and translation unresolved. If the parent wants a fully original line, use `The old claims have found a subject.`

The quote supports diplomatic, ideological, economic, dynastic, and territorial war aims without assuming a single ideology.

### Backup recommendation

- Source URL: [1914 Hutchinson edition of “Ozymandias”](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Complete_Poetical_Works_of_Percy_Bysshe_Shelley_%28ed._Hutchinson%2C_1914%29/Ozymandias).

### Cultural and route fit

The quote gives the terminal moment a warning against empty triumph. The dynamic description must state the surviving public actor, last rival, resolution method such as union, federation, submission, negotiated settlement, victory, or Covenant, final institution, inheritance burden, and human continuity. It must not imply extinction, a globe explosion, or a generic map wipe.

### Backup recommendation

`The old order changeth.` from Tennyson’s *The Passing of Arthur* is a public-domain transition button, but it is less final. `Covenants, without the Sword, are but Words.` is a route-specific coercive backup only and should not be reused across peaceful endings.

## Cross-role language and sensitivity gate

- All proposed selected text is English. Do not add Afaan Oromoo or another source-language string until a qualified native-speaker review and a direct source are available.
- Direct public country names and public institution names should remain in dynamic descriptions. Avoid caricature, exoticising labels, and invented royal or tribal stereotypes.
- Titles, quotes, and original remarks are route-neutral across decorated-kings, plain-background, and other approved visual variants. The dynamic description carries the actual constitution, host, capital, settlement method, and response class.
- The Garvey line has a real historical association with Black nationalism and separatist rhetoric. It is historically relevant to continental self-determination, but the parent should approve its use across coercive and high-chaos outcomes rather than presenting it as a universal endorsement.
- The Berlin Article 34 line is colonial legal language. Its purpose is to expose and reverse external claim-making, not to celebrate the Berlin Conference. The Federal Foreign Office context confirms that African representatives were not consulted and that the act intensified colonial exploitation and oppression.

Keep all direct quotations exactly as written above, including punctuation and capitalisation chosen by the frozen edition.
