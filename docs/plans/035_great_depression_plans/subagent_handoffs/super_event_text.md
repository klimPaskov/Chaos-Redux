## Selected main quote

Exact text: “The means of exchange are frozen in the currents of trade.”

Speaker: Franklin D. Roosevelt.

Source work: First Inaugural Address, delivered March 4, 1933.

Primary source: [FDR Presidential Library reading copy of the First Inaugural Address](https://www.fdrlibrary.org/documents/356632/390886/document003.pdf/ee286f5b-ce6b-4784-9b1f-176686e60959)

Source metadata: [FDR Presidential Library, Four Presidential Inaugurations](https://www.fdrlibrary.org/inaugurations)

Corroborating transcript: [American Presidency Project, Franklin D. Roosevelt First Inaugural Address](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/inaugural-address-8)

Attribution confidence: High.

Fit: The sentence directly names frozen exchange and trade, matching the worldwide contraction of orders, payments, ports, and construction while remaining compatible with a recoverable crisis.

Rights note: This is an 11-word excerpt from a 1933 historical U.S. presidential address and has low text-excerpt copyright risk. The archive scan and any speech recording are separate assets and were not cleared by this text recommendation. The U.S. Copyright Office discussion of government works is a reference point, not a blanket rights determination for every presidential drafting or publication artifact. Use the excerpt with attribution and do not reuse the scan or recording.

## Selected button or reaction

Exact UI text: “A bad attack of economic pessimism.”

Speaker: John Maynard Keynes.

Source work: “Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren,” first published in The Nation & Athenaeum in 1930.

Scholarly source: [Cambridge University Press, Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/collected-writings-of-john-maynard-keynes/economic-possibilities-for-our-grandchildren-1930/230A7959F87C44AC6E83E406DF126B97)

Text and rights source: [History of Economic Thought, Keynes 1930 essay](https://www.hetwebsite.net/het/texts/keynes/keynes1930grandchildren.htm)

Attribution confidence: High.

Fit: The short fragment is a dry period understatement that acknowledges the crisis without turning the button into a joke or a terminal pronouncement.

Rights note: The phrase is a six-word excerpt. The History of Economic Thought page says the essay is believed to be public domain and allows noncommercial use of its electronic version, while Cambridge supplies scholarly metadata. This is low to moderate text-excerpt risk rather than a formal jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction clearance. Use only the short fragment with attribution.
