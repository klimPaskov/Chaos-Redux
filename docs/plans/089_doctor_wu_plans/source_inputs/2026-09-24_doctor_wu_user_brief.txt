# Doctor Wu

## Catalog entry

- Event ID: `089`
- Event name: Doctor Wu
- Type: Minor Fire-Once
- Status: To Be Reworked
- Chaos level: 1
- Cluster: Scientific Research
- Cluster role: High member

## Core idea

> A mysterious physician appears in one major power or player-controlled country and proves that no biological disease is beyond his ability to treat. Doctor Wu can protect millions from outbreaks, create cures for diseases that defeated every other laboratory, and turn his host into the safest country in a diseased world. The same knowledge also lets him redesign pathogens, alter infected creatures, enhance soldiers, clone human beings, and build forms of life that ordinary research could never produce.

Doctor Wu is a fictional character created for this event. His origin, education, age, and previous employers cannot be confirmed. The event should establish his ability through a successful medical intervention rather than through a long biography.

The event begins as an extraordinary medical opportunity. Its dangerous branches grow from the same skill that makes Wu valuable. He understands living systems well enough to cure them, control them, or rebuild them for another purpose.

## Event promise

Doctor Wu should become the strongest individual medical asset in the mod.

His host receives an immediate and significant advantage against disease, biological attacks, stockpile accidents, and other outbreaks. The country can then decide whether he remains a physician, becomes the head of military medicine, directs a secret biological programme, or acquires enough authority to pursue experiments without meaningful supervision.

The event should create several competing goals:

- cure active outbreaks before they spread
- protect the host from future disease events
- decide who receives access to each cure
- preserve dangerous samples for research or destroy them
- exploit Wu's knowledge for military projects
- control how much institutional authority he receives
- manage foreign attempts to recruit, steal, abduct, or expose him
- handle rivalry with Doctor Warren Kruger if Brilliant Scientist exists in the same country
- decide whether human enhancement, engineered infection, and cloning still count as medicine

Doctor Wu must remain useful even when no disease is active. During healthy periods he builds treatment reserves, prepares broad countermeasures, improves field medicine, studies captured biological material, and proposes experiments based on the current world state.

## Host selection

One country receives Doctor Wu.

The candidate pool contains:

- every valid player-controlled country
- every valid current major country

A player-controlled major appears only once in the pool.

Countries that no longer use ordinary civilian and medical systems should be excluded. Actual nonhuman countries, outbreak identities, terminal actors, and other countries that cannot support a normal laboratory should not receive him as their resident physician.

The chosen host receives the full arrival event. Player countries should remain eligible without making a player target guaranteed in every campaign.

If the first host expels or refuses Doctor Wu, he seeks another valid country from the remaining pool. The fire-once event should not disappear because one government refused him. Repeated rejection can eventually force him underground, after which intelligence services may discover him later.

## The first proof

Doctor Wu arrives during a medical emergency, military hospital crisis, suspected biological incident, or unexplained outbreak inside the selected country.

If a real outbreak is already active, he treats that disease.

If the country is currently healthy, the event creates a contained medical case that demonstrates his ability without starting a new national epidemic. He cures patients who were expected to die, identifies the source, and prevents a wider spread.

The proof should make three facts clear:

- Wu's treatment works
- he understands the disease faster than existing institutions
- he refuses to explain everything until the government decides what position he will hold

The arrival does not immediately give the host every experimental technology. It grants Doctor Wu, his basic medical institution, a strong defensive package, and the ability to begin cure projects for any valid disease.

## Initial government choices

The host chooses Doctor Wu's first mandate.

### Establish a public medical institute

Doctor Wu works openly through hospitals, universities, public laboratories, and civilian health agencies.

This route provides the strongest broad outbreak protection, the safest cure development, better international medical cooperation, and lower accident risk.

Wu begins with limited personal authority. Major experiments require government approval.

Foreign governments are more willing to send samples, request aid, and recognize the host as a medical leader.

### Place him under military medical command

Doctor Wu works with the armed forces, military hospitals, CBRN units, and defence laboratories.

This route balances outbreak defence with field medicine, biological protection, soldier enhancement, and controlled military research.

The armed forces accelerate the baseline advanced-treatment and soldier-enhancement programmes.

Wu receives moderate authority and reliable access to military test subjects, captured material, and protected facilities.

### Give him a classified biological directorate

Doctor Wu receives secret facilities, protected staff, captured samples, and permission to study offensive applications.

This route gives the fastest path toward modified biological weapons, controlled infections, engineered zombies, human enhancement, and cloning.

It also creates the highest early accident risk, hidden evidence, foreign intelligence pressure, and growth of Wu Authority.

### Restrict or expel him

The country refuses to give Doctor Wu a permanent institution.

It keeps a small defensive benefit from copied emergency procedures, but loses access to his cures and later projects.

Wu relocates, disappears, or enters another government's service.

A country that expels him can later regret the decision when a major outbreak begins or when another power unveils his work.

## Doctor Wu as a character and institution

Doctor Wu becomes a visible character in the host country.

His exact role depends on the chosen mandate, but he should function as more than a normal advisor. His presence owns the long-running medical and experimental system.

The host receives a national institution representing his laboratories, trained staff, treatment network, and sample archive.

The baseline benefits should be large enough to matter immediately:

- major reduction to the chance that a biological incident becomes an uncontrolled domestic outbreak
- slower spread from infected states
- lower civilian mortality from active biological disease
- stronger hospitals, quarantine, vaccination, and emergency medicine
- faster preparation of disease-specific cures
- improved protection against biological attacks
- lower risk from ordinary biological stockpile accidents
- faster recovery of military casualties and infected personnel
- better detection of hidden or early-stage outbreaks
- stronger medical support for famine relief, migration corridors, and contaminated regions where the owning systems allow it

These benefits remain strongest while Wu is alive, free, supplied, and in control of a working institute.

If he is killed, captured, expelled, or disappears, the country keeps part of the institutional knowledge but loses his unique ability to improvise cures and design new biological projects.

## Wu Authority

The main visible event value is **Wu Authority**.

It represents his control over budgets, staff, facilities, patients, specimens, military units, security, and the legal limits placed on his work.

Suggested stages:

### Supervised Consultant

Wu can advise hospitals and lead approved cure projects. He cannot begin military or human experimentation without direct government approval.

### Institute Director

Wu controls a national medical network and can select staff, request samples, and run several approved programmes.

### Strategic Authority

Wu has independent access to military facilities, prisons, outbreak zones, intelligence material, and classified research.

### Sovereign Laboratory

Wu controls his own security, hides parts of his work, and can continue projects after the government orders a pause.

### Biological Power Centre

Wu's institute behaves like a state inside the state. It can conceal facilities, move samples, create duplicate research teams, and pressure the government through its control of cures and enhanced troops.

Wu Authority rises when the country:

- grants emergency powers
- gives him exclusive facilities
- approves secret experiments
- lets him control cure distribution
- protects him from investigation
- gives him captured organisms or infected prisoners
- accepts results without demanding records
- relies on him during repeated outbreaks
- permits cloning or permanent human modification

Wu Authority falls when the country:

- divides his laboratories between civilian institutions
- requires independent review
- shares cure production with foreign partners
- destroys dangerous samples
- audits his facilities
- removes his private guards
- limits access to military units and vulnerable populations
- closes failed projects
- supports rival scientists and ministries

High authority should be a deliberate route. Emergency events can pressure the player to grant more power, but the event should not force Doctor Wu into control without a chain of accepted decisions, failures, or lost oversight.

## Research direction

Doctor Wu also has a current research direction. This should be a qualitative state rather than another permanent numeric meter.

Possible directions include:

- Public Medicine
- National Protection
- Military Enhancement
- Controlled Biology
- Unrestricted Experimentation

The direction changes which projects appear, how foreign countries react, which staff support him, and what kinds of accidents can occur.

A government can redirect the institute, but changing direction should cost time, staff loyalty, and political control. Wu may resist a change when his authority is high.

## Baseline research progression

All Doctor Wu technologies, treatments, organisms, weapons, enhancements, and cloning projects belong to the event's ordinary baseline progression.

They are not unlocked by evolutions.

A host can eventually reach every compatible project family while the world is still in Calm World. Reaching them still requires time, facilities, samples, staff, production capacity, earlier project work, and the political permission to continue. High Chaos can create more urgent cases and stranger source material, but it is never the key that unlocks a Doctor Wu technology.

Baseline progress comes from:

- successful cure and treatment projects
- expansion of hospitals and laboratory facilities
- acquisition of valid disease, zombie, plague, clone, alien, or nonhuman samples
- completed prerequisite projects inside the same research family
- the chosen mandate and current research direction
- Wu Authority and the amount of work the government lets him conduct independently
- cooperation with military medicine, intelligence services, universities, and special-project facilities
- owner-approved access to biological technologies introduced by other events
- cooperation or competition with Doctor Warren Kruger
- actual wartime, outbreak, famine, migration, and casualty needs

The portfolio should use connected project families instead of one rigid linear ladder. The following bands describe ordinary research maturity and can overlap.

### Medical foundation

Doctor Wu builds the institute, specimen archive, treatment-production network, field hospitals, vaccination capacity, quarantine doctrine, and emergency cure process.

This band includes his strong starting outbreak defence and the first complete disease-specific cures.

### Adaptive medicine and pathogen control

Wu learns to update treatments after mutation, prepare broad countermeasures, produce defensive counterstrains, suppress disease vectors, and alter registered pathogen properties.

This is where cure development begins feeding directly into biological weapon modification. A country can stay focused on defensive medicine or preserve samples for offensive work.

### Military medicine and controlled infection

The institute develops advanced field medicine, endurance drugs, combat enhancement, disease-resistant formations, and engineered infected organisms when suitable material exists.

Controlled zombies, altered plague organisms, and similar projects require real specimens and containment infrastructure. They do not require an evolution.

### Replication science

Doctor Wu advances from replacement blood and organs into replicated specialists, replacement soldiers, clone formations, and continuity copies of himself.

Each step depends on earlier tissue, growth, memory, and control work. The route can be discovered before the Army of Clones event, but it must use the shared clone framework and preserve that event's normal firing.

### Designed life

The mature baseline portfolio includes synthetic organisms, biological filters, disease-eating organisms, chimeric military creatures, living reconnaissance systems, population-restoration projects, nonhuman medicine, and joint Kruger-Wu biological machinery.

These projects require the relevant earlier work and source material. They remain baseline research even when their results are strange or extremely dangerous.

The mandate changes the route through this portfolio. A public institute reaches cures, preventive medicine, and safe replication more easily. Military command reaches enhancement and battlefield medicine more easily. A classified directorate reaches weapon modification, controlled infection, and coercive experimentation more easily. None of these routes permanently loses access to another family, but crossing into a restricted field should require a visible political decision and suitable prerequisites.

Baseline progress should remain possible when every evolution is disabled. An evolution can change the behavior, danger, scale, and political meaning of a project that already exists or is developed later. It cannot serve as the prerequisite that makes the project exist.

## Universal cure system

Doctor Wu can develop a cure for every valid biological disease registered in Hearts of Iron IV and Chaos Redux.

This includes ordinary diseases and weaponized agents such as:

- smallpox
- tularemia
- plague-type diseases
- other conventional biological agents
- the zombie infection
- the Black Plague
- temporal diseases introduced by Time Traveler incidents
- diseases created by future events
- modified strains created by Doctor Wu himself

The system should be dynamic. Disease-owning systems should be able to expose their illness, specimen conditions, current stage, valid treatment results, and any limits to Doctor Wu. The event should not depend on a short hardcoded list that becomes obsolete when another disease is added.

For every registered disease, Doctor Wu can run a cure sequence:

1. obtain a valid specimen or clinical case
2. study the disease and identify its current strain
3. prepare a treatment or countermeasure
4. manufacture enough treatment for the intended scale
5. deploy it to selected states, military formations, allies, or the wider world

The time and cost depend on:

- disease severity
- how widely it has spread
- whether the host controls an infected state
- whether foreign governments provide samples
- the host's medical and research capacity
- access to hospitals, support equipment, trains, convoys, and protected routes
- current Air Cleanliness and outbreak conditions
- Wu Authority
- his current research direction
- cooperation or rivalry with Brilliant Scientist
- whether the disease has already mutated against an earlier treatment

No biological disease is permanently beyond Doctor Wu. A dangerous strain can require several attempts, rare samples, or a costly global programme, but it always has a possible treatment route while Wu and a viable institute remain available.

## Meaning of a cure

A cure removes or suppresses the biological disease. It does not reverse every consequence the disease has already created.

Doctor Wu cannot restore people who have already died. He cannot erase recorded deaths, contamination history, public evidence, or Condemnation.

If a disease has already created a country, army, or independent nonhuman society, curing the infection does not delete that actor from the map.

The cure instead attacks the biological support that lets the threat spread, recruit, infect civilians, or survive in ordinary populations.

This distinction is important for the largest disease events.

### Zombie infection

Doctor Wu can:

- cure infected living populations before complete death and reanimation
- stabilize recently infected patients
- prevent new infections in protected states
- weaken the infection inside active outbreak states
- reduce the horde's ability to create new zombies
- produce a treatment for new mutations
- create an emergency shutdown treatment for his own engineered zombies
- support the Anti-Zombie League with advanced medicine

He cannot bring fully dead zombie soldiers back to life through an ordinary cure.

A successful cure can collapse the biological spread of a zombie state, isolate existing hordes, and make military defeat possible without pretending that the dead population has returned.

### The Black Plague

Doctor Wu can:

- cure infected human populations
- remove or reduce plague pressure in treated states
- protect ports, refugee routes, occupied fronts, and crowded cities
- identify new plague mutations
- create vector-control projects
- reduce the disease support used by Rat Nations
- develop treatments for human servants, prisoners, or collaborators controlled by the Rat King

He cannot make a Rat Nation or the Rat King vanish through a hospital decision.

Once the rats have become an organized political and military actor, medicine weakens their plague network and recruitment. The remaining country must still be contained, defeated, negotiated with, or transformed through its own event logic.

### Mixed or supernatural crises

Doctor Wu can cure the biological component of a crisis.

He cannot cure famine, nuclear fallout, chemical contamination, political fanaticism, divine punishment, or a supernatural entity merely because those crises harm people.

If another event combines disease with a non-biological force, its owner determines which part of the crisis is medically treatable.

## Cure deployment and diplomacy

Every completed cure creates a political choice.

### Release it freely

The host shares the treatment, production process, and field protocol with countries suffering from the disease.

This gives the strongest global containment, reduces Chaos when a real crisis is reversed, and builds medical influence.

Foreign countries can still struggle to manufacture or deliver the cure during war, blockade, famine, or state collapse.

### License it to allies and partners

The host keeps control of production while supplying selected countries.

This can create research cooperation, economic influence, diplomatic obligations, and pressure to join the host's faction or medical network.

### Sell access

Countries pay through industry, resources, intelligence cooperation, political concessions, or military access.

Desperate governments may accept harsh terms during a major outbreak.

### Keep it for the host

The country protects itself while foreign outbreaks continue.

This creates espionage, diplomatic anger, kidnapping attempts, theft, and international pressure.

Wu can support the monopoly because exclusive control increases his importance and authority.

### Preserve the disease as a military asset

The host produces the cure while retaining viable dangerous samples.

This opens modified-strain research, creates accident risk, and makes later exposure far more damaging.

Destroying the samples closes some offensive projects but lowers long-term risk.

## The Wu Institute project pool

Doctor Wu should propose projects based on the current world rather than follow one fixed linear list.

The project pool can draw from:

- active diseases
- diseases that have already been cured
- biological weapon technology owned by the host
- captured enemy stockpiles
- zombie specimens
- Black Plague specimens
- Rat Nation material
- nonhuman soldiers
- clone technology
- Time Traveler anomalies
- alien biology
- famine and migration needs
- current wars
- military losses
- Brilliant Scientist projects
- the host's ideology and laws
- current Wu Authority
- current Chaos tier, used to weight urgency, risk, foreign attention, and evolved behavior rather than project access

Only a small number of primary projects should be visible at one time. The player chooses which laboratory capacity to commit, while rejected proposals can return later in changed form. Chaos may change proposal pressure and failure severity, but no project family is reserved for a Chaos tier or evolution.

Projects can produce:

- clean success
- delayed success
- a useful treatment with a dangerous limitation
- a military breakthrough
- an unauthorized side project
- an escaped organism
- infected staff
- evidence of secret experimentation
- foreign theft
- rivalry with Kruger
- a new disease mutation
- an institutional demand for more authority

The player should understand the public risk category before approving a project. Exact rare outcomes can remain uncertain.

## Modifying biological weapons

Doctor Wu can modify every compatible biological weapon already present in Chaos Redux.

The full specification should create a future-proof strain system. Each biological agent can expose which properties Doctor Wu may alter and which changes are invalid for that agent.

Possible modification families include:

- faster or slower spread
- short or long incubation
- greater potency
- greater environmental persistence
- treatment resistance
- host selectivity
- improved survival in heat, cold, cities, ports, or damaged infrastructure
- reduced detectability before symptoms appear
- stronger disruption of military formations
- reduced risk to protected friendly forces
- altered aggression or obedience in infected organisms
- a built-in dependence on a control treatment
- a defensive counterstrain that suppresses another disease

Modified strains must have tradeoffs.

Examples:

- extreme lethality can reduce long-term spread because hosts die too quickly
- high persistence creates greater domestic accident and Air Cleanliness pressure
- treatment resistance also makes the host's own emergency response less reliable
- host selectivity needs intelligence about the intended target and can fail against mixed populations
- stealth increases the chance that the outbreak grows before the attacker can control it
- rapid spread reduces precision and makes neutral or allied infection more likely
- obedience requires continued access to Wu's control treatment, handlers, or command infrastructure

A modified weapon should never be a free upgrade with every desirable property.

The host can preserve several strain designs, but only a limited number should be active production standards at one time.

## Modified zombies

If Zombie Outbreak has fired, a zombie specimen has been captured, or another valid source provides reanimated biological material, Doctor Wu can begin an engineered-zombie programme.

Wu's zombies are more powerful and more obedient than those produced through ordinary research.

Obedience is created through a combination of biological conditioning, a control treatment, trained handlers, and institute-owned command protocols. The exact fiction can vary by strain, but the gameplay rule is clear: the units remain controllable only while the host maintains Wu's system.

Possible engineered lineages include:

### Command strain

The most obedient lineage.

It follows complex orders, holds assigned territory, avoids protected friendly populations, and can operate inside a normal army.

It is weaker when separated from handlers or when the control treatment is unavailable.

### Assault strain

A stronger and more durable combat organism intended for breakthroughs, fortified positions, and close assault.

It is slower, expensive to sustain, and dangerous during transport or supply failure.

### Hunter strain

A fast lineage intended for pursuit, encirclement, reconnaissance, and attacks on broken formations.

It has lower staying power and can become uncontrollable when isolated for too long.

### Carrier strain

A lineage designed to spread infection rather than win ordinary combat.

It can create outbreaks behind enemy lines, but carries the highest diplomatic, contamination, and runaway-spread risk.

### Guardian strain

A defensive lineage conditioned to protect laboratories, depots, hospitals, and strategic sites.

It is reliable inside prepared zones and poor at long-range operations.

Later projects can combine a limited number of compatible traits. Every additional trait increases instability and control cost.

If control fails, engineered zombie units can:

- lose organization and combat effectiveness
- stop following normal orders
- attack nearby civilians or friendly units
- create an uncontrolled outbreak
- follow Doctor Wu instead of the government
- be stolen or redirected by a rival laboratory
- require emergency destruction or deployment of the shutdown cure

If Doctor Wu dies or disappears, the host must secure his control archive quickly. A cloned Wu, trained successor, or preserved protocol can maintain the force. Without one, the engineered formations become a national emergency.

## The soldier enhancement programme

Doctor Wu attempts to create a drug and treatment programme that makes soldiers more endurable, faster, stronger, harder to infect, and quicker to recover.

The programme should progress through distinct stages.

### Advanced field medicine

The safest stage improves treatment, survival, disease resistance, recovery, and return to duty.

It strengthens the whole army without transforming soldiers into a separate human type.

### Endurance formula

Selected formations receive treatment that reduces fatigue and improves sustained movement, organization recovery, harsh-weather endurance, and resistance to disease.

Long campaigns increase dependence on regular doses.

### Combat enhancement

Selected formations gain greater strength, speed, pain tolerance, and recovery.

The benefits are large enough to affect operational planning.

The risks include aggression, poor judgment, withdrawal, organ damage, and a sharp loss of performance when supply breaks.

### Permanent alteration

Doctor Wu changes the soldiers rather than supplying a temporary drug.

The resulting troops can become a special enhanced unit family with stronger physical performance and unusual medical resilience.

Permanent alteration creates severe political, medical, demographic, and command consequences. Enhanced veterans may no longer fit ordinary civilian life, may require lifelong treatment, or may become loyal to Wu's institute before the state.

The programme should use selected formations, special equipment or medicine, trained medical staff, and laboratory capacity. It should not become one global modifier that makes every division permanently superior at no practical cost.

The player can choose between safer army-wide medicine and smaller groups of extreme enhanced soldiers.

## Cloning and replication

Doctor Wu can enter the existing Chaos Redux cloning system before a normal cloning event or scenario would otherwise make it available.

His cloning route begins with medical uses and grows into political and military uses.

### Replacement tissue and organs

Wu creates blood, tissue, organs, and replacement biological material for hospitals and military medicine.

This improves survival and gives the lowest-risk entry into cloning.

### Replicated specialists

The institute creates biological copies of rare surgeons, researchers, pilots, engineers, or intelligence specialists.

The copies can inherit trained behavior imperfectly, creating questions about memory, experience, and legal identity.

### Replacement soldiers

Wu grows batches of physically mature personnel for military use.

They still need equipment, organization, training, command, and supply. The system must not become an unlimited free-manpower loop.

### Clone formations

The host creates full clone units with standardized traits, controlled loyalty, and optional soldier enhancements.

Batch flaws, shared vulnerabilities, accelerated aging, obedience problems, and sabotage can affect an entire generation at once.

### Wu continuity

Doctor Wu prepares biological copies of himself.

A continuity clone can preserve the event after assassination, age, capture, or laboratory disaster.

The government may discover that several Wus exist at the same time. They can cooperate, disagree over which one is original, divide the institute, or each claim the right to continue a different project.

Cloning Doctor Wu can make removal far harder. Destroying one laboratory may no longer end his influence.

The cloning route should use the shared clone system and its owner contracts. It must not launch the Army of Clones scenario or mark another event as fired merely because Wu independently discovered the process.

## Other biological projects

As baseline research matures and the institute obtains suitable material, Doctor Wu can propose stranger projects. Chaos and Authority change their frequency, scale, and danger, but do not own their unlocks. Not every project should appear in every campaign.

Possible projects include:

- regenerative treatment that repairs severe battlefield injuries
- anti-aging treatment for leaders and specialists
- controlled hibernation for soldiers, pilots, submariners, or civilians in disaster zones
- synthetic blood and universal organ compatibility
- disease-eating organisms that suppress active biological contamination
- engineered predators or repellents against plague-carrying rats
- crop and livestock treatments that improve survival during famine
- biological filters that reduce a registered outbreak footprint
- adaptive medicine for nonhuman or alien troops
- chimeric military animals or purpose-built organisms
- living reconnaissance organisms
- biological camouflage
- memory conditioning and obedience treatment
- rapid maturation of clone batches
- restoration of damaged tissue from recently infected patients
- controlled appetite or aggression treatment for cannibal and zombie-related crises
- preservation of critically wounded leaders until a cure becomes available
- copied immune systems from survivors of major outbreaks
- laboratory-grown replacement populations after catastrophic disease loss

Each project needs a real gameplay role, cost, risk, and owner-system connection. The full specification should reject projects that would only add another small modifier or duplicate an existing mechanic without changing play.

## Rivalry with Brilliant Scientist

If Doctor Warren Kruger from Brilliant Scientist exists in the same country, a special rivalry begins.

Kruger is the broader scientific genius. He works across machines, weapons, institutions, alien technology, and fields that extend far beyond medicine.

Doctor Wu dominates medicine, disease, biological warfare, living systems, human modification, and replication.

Their rivalry centers on:

- access to national research funding
- control of special-project facilities
- recruitment of the best scientists
- ownership of captured samples and alien material
- public credit for breakthroughs
- military access
- political protection
- authority over joint projects
- control of cloning and enhanced personnel

The rivalry should be shown through events and project consequences rather than through a third permanent public meter.

Possible states include:

### Ordered division of work

The government gives each scientist a defined field and prevents staff poaching.

Research is safer and slower. Kruger handles broad technology while Wu owns medicine and biology.

### Competitive grants

Both scientists receive resources and race toward related goals.

Breakthroughs arrive faster, but failed tests, sabotage, duplicated work, and hidden projects become more likely.

### Joint institute

Kruger and Wu cooperate on projects that neither could complete alone.

Possible joint work includes biological interfaces for advanced machines, alien medicine, synthetic organs, stabilized clone soldiers, controlled nonhuman infantry, and treatments for conditions produced by experimental technology.

The joint institute can become stronger than either scientist's separate organization and can also concentrate dangerous political authority.

### Wu supremacy

Doctor Wu takes control of the country's biological and medical research.

Kruger loses access to living subjects, specimens, and medical facilities. He can accept the division, retaliate, leave, or attempt to expose Wu.

### Kruger supremacy

Kruger absorbs or restricts Wu's laboratories.

Doctor Wu can accept a subordinate role, defect, hide samples, create an underground institute, or begin a continuity-clone plan.

### Laboratory conflict

The rivalry becomes open sabotage.

Staff disappear, samples are switched, experiments fail, foreign agents gain access, and one scientist may blame the other for an outbreak.

A desperate government can still force cooperation during a major epidemic.

The rivalry should matter most when both scientists want the same facility, sample, or political authority. It should not trigger constant flavour popups during unrelated research.

## Early access to special projects

Doctor Wu can independently discover biological special projects before their normal source event has fired.

This applies only to compatible medicine, pathogen, organism, enhancement, or cloning content.

An independent Wu discovery should:

- grant the relevant Wu-owned or owner-approved project capability
- preserve the original source event for later normal firing
- avoid granting unrelated countries, characters, stories, or rewards
- avoid marking the source event as fired
- merge cleanly with the normal project if it later becomes available
- use existing custom-technology, project, clone, and unit-family owner interfaces where available

Doctor Wu should not copy every special project in the mod. His early access is limited to biological and medical work that fits his established skill.

## Connections with existing events and systems

### Zombie Outbreak

Doctor Wu creates the strongest cure and containment route available to a human country.

He can protect living populations, update the cure after zombie evolutions, help the Anti-Zombie League, weaken zombie recruitment, and create engineered obedient zombies from captured material.

Zombie mutations can outrun an old treatment and force Wu to develop a new version.

A hostile zombie power may target his institute because his work threatens its ability to spread.

### The Black Plague

Doctor Wu can cure human infection, protect transport routes, reduce plague pressure, and attack the biological support of Rat Nations.

The Rat King can send infiltrators, steal treatments, corrupt stored samples, or adapt the plague against Wu's first cure.

Wu can attempt dangerous anti-rat organisms or behavioral controls. Failure can strengthen the Rat King's broods.

### Brilliant Scientist

The same-country rivalry becomes a major event branch.

Kruger can accelerate Wu's projects, compete for authority, or provide access to custom technologies and special-project infrastructure.

Doctor Wu must remain a distinct biological specialist rather than a renamed second Kruger.

### Soldiers from Nowhere

Doctor Wu can examine strange, nonhuman, cloned, or biologically unstable formations.

He can create compatible medicine, stabilize their bodies, identify hidden diseases, or use their biology in new projects.

Any new derivative unit must remain owned by the proper unit-family provider rather than bypassing its registration and sustainment rules.

### Army of Clones and the shared clone system

Wu can discover cloning independently, improve clone medicine, alter clone obedience, combine enhanced soldiers with clone batches, and preserve himself through continuity copies.

His route does not automatically launch the manual scenario.

### Chemical and biological warfare

Doctor Wu improves biological protection, hospitals, vaccination, countermeasures, stockpile safety, and treatment after a biological strike.

He can also create stronger modified agents and new delivery-compatible strains.

Actual use still creates normal action records, deaths, outbreak changes, Air Cleanliness pressure, evidence, attribution, and Condemnation.

Protection and treatment do not erase responsibility for using the weapon.

### USA Tests Weapons

If the United States controls Doctor Wu, it can include Wu-designed strains, treatments, or enhanced soldiers in later tests after the relevant baseline project has been completed.

If another country controls him, the United States can request samples, steal research, pressure the host, or select a Wu-related target when its testing event supports that content.

### Ten Plagues of Egypt

Doctor Wu can treat disease-type plagues such as boils, pestilence, and secondary epidemics.

He cannot medically stop hail, darkness, locust swarms, transformed water, or the supernatural death of the firstborn unless the owning event defines a biological component.

His inability to solve non-biological plagues can push him toward riskier experiments and public claims that he needs more authority.

### Time Traveler

A time traveler can recognize Doctor Wu, warn the host about one of his possible futures, provide future medical knowledge, or identify a treatment that has not yet been invented.

Temporal diseases can be added to Wu's cure system.

Future records may disagree about whether Wu saved humanity, created an enhanced ruling class, or replaced himself so many times that nobody knows who the original doctor was.

### Research Failure

Doctor Wu's institute can preserve biological knowledge, protect one active medical project, and rebuild lost medical capacity faster than ordinary institutions.

A severe research collapse can also make the state more dependent on him and increase Wu Authority.

### Gift from Scientists

A scientific gift can provide staff, samples, treatment knowledge, or a compatible project to the Wu Institute.

Doctor Wu can also become the reason foreign scientists defect to the host.

### Famine and migration

Wu can provide vaccination, field hospitals, disease screening, emergency medicine, and safer reception for displaced populations.

His projects can reduce the disease pressure that worsens famine and migration crises.

He does not replace food production, transport, reception capacity, or movement mechanics.

### Camp and repression systems

A secret or unrestricted Wu route can use hidden experiment sites, prisoners, occupied populations, or vulnerable groups.

Such projects create atrocity and cover-up evidence through the existing discovery and Condemnation systems.

Public medical routes should reject these methods and gain tools to expose or close them.

### Cannibalism and Death

Doctor Wu can treat biological infection, deficiency, prion-like disease, tissue damage, and altered appetite where those systems expose a medical component.

He cannot cure an ideology, political movement, supernatural force, or independent nonhuman country by classifying it as an illness.

His attempts to study Death or reanimated tissue can satisfy the source-material requirements for rare baseline projects and can create serious failure outcomes.

### Air Cleanliness and the Deaths system

Successful cure and containment can reduce the biological outbreak footprint that contributes to Air Cleanliness pressure.

Doctor Wu cannot erase nuclear fallout or unrelated chemical contamination.

All deaths caused by his accidents, experiments, weapons, failed treatments, or uncontrolled organisms must enter the shared Deaths system once.

## Foreign competition for Doctor Wu

Other countries should react to Doctor Wu as a strategic resource.

Possible foreign actions include:

- send scientists to his institute
- provide infected samples
- request military medicine
- demand international access
- offer Doctor Wu asylum

- steal a modified strain
- kidnap Doctor Wu
- assassinate him
- expose secret experiments
- attack or raid an institute site
- sanction the host after public weaponization
- form a medical coalition around shared cures

Foreign countries suffering from active outbreaks should prioritize cure diplomacy.

Countries threatened by the host should prioritize espionage, abduction, and exposure when Wu is being weaponized.

If Doctor Wu defects, his old host keeps copied knowledge and unfinished projects. The new host receives the character, his personal methods, and any samples he successfully removed.

## Laboratory capture, death, and continuity

Doctor Wu needs one or more actual institute sites.

If the main laboratory state is occupied or lost, the host chooses whether to:

- evacuate Wu and key samples
- destroy the dangerous archive
- leave staff behind to continue treatment
- move projects into military facilities
- negotiate safe passage
- let the occupier seize the institute

The occupier can capture Doctor Wu, rescue him, recruit him, interrogate his staff, destroy the laboratory, or seize incomplete projects.

If Wu dies before cloning exists, the institute retains reduced medical knowledge and can finish some active cures. New universal adaptations and extreme projects become unavailable unless a successor is developed.

If Wu continuity exists, his death triggers a succession crisis among stored or living copies.

## Evolution structure

Event 089 has four logged evolutions, following the same design split used by Brilliant Scientist.

Ordinary institute development is baseline progression. The evolutions are separate mutations in how Doctor Wu, his institute, foreign governments, and completed projects behave.

An evolution can:

- spread the consequences of existing research across more of the country or world
- increase institutional dependence on Doctor Wu
- create foreign competition, theft, sabotage, or pressure
- make approved projects produce more dangerous variants
- let Wu conduct work without permission
- turn medical control into political authority
- change the loyalty of staff, enhanced soldiers, clones, and engineered organisms
- intensify failures, accidents, exposure, and recovery choices

An evolution cannot:

- unlock universal cures
- unlock biological weapon modification
- unlock engineered zombies
- unlock soldier enhancement
- unlock cloning or continuity copies
- unlock strange synthetic-biology projects
- act as the only prerequisite for any technology or special project

If an evolution occurs while baseline research is still limited, it changes the projects and institutions that currently exist. Later baseline projects inherit that evolved behavior when they are completed.

If the event first fires at a higher Chaos tier, the evolved opening changes the initial political and institutional situation. It does not hand the host free advanced technologies.

## Evolution I: National Medical Ascendancy

- Chaos requirement: `200+`.

Doctor Wu's institute stops being one exceptional laboratory and becomes a national medical system.

His methods spread through hospitals, universities, military medicine, pharmaceutical production, quarantine services, and emergency administration. Completed cures and treatments can be deployed on a much larger scale. Projects completed later enter a country that already depends on Wu's standards, staff, and supply network.

This changes the event in several ways:

- outbreaks are answered faster across the country
- cure production can be centralized or divided among supervised regional institutes
- Wu gains influence over hospital appointments, medical budgets, and emergency law
- local doctors begin following his protocols without waiting for ordinary approval
- the army and civilian government compete for access to the same treatment network
- every major success makes removing him more costly
- a faulty treatment, sabotaged formula, or compromised protocol can affect the whole national system instead of one facility
- hostile intelligence services can target the network through staff, factories, transport, and copied instructions

The government can distribute Wu's methods through independent institutions or let the entire system answer directly to him. Distribution lowers personal dependence but makes secrecy harder. Centralization improves speed and control while making one man the country's medical bottleneck.

At low Wu Authority, the evolution creates a powerful public institution. At high Authority, hospitals and military units begin treating Wu's orders as emergency law.

An evolved first opening at this tier starts with a medical emergency that has already drawn several ministries and regional hospitals into Wu's temporary command. The host chooses whether to make that emergency network permanent. It receives no free advanced project.

## Evolution II: The International Biological Contest

- Chaos requirement: `400+`.

Foreign governments begin treating Doctor Wu, his cures, and his specimens as strategic assets.

Every important project can now produce international consequences. A cure may start a bidding contest. A modified strain may trigger espionage before the host has decided whether to use it. A new enhancement formula may attract foreign officers, defectors, and sabotage teams. A clone or engineered organism may be copied badly by another country.

This evolution adds behavior such as:

- competing offers for Doctor Wu and senior institute staff
- foreign requests for samples, treatments, production licences, and emergency teams
- attempts to steal complete cures, incomplete formulas, pathogens, control treatments, or clone material
- counterfeit or degraded copies of Wu medicine appearing abroad
- rival laboratories claiming that they can reproduce or improve his work
- foreign governments deliberately withholding specimens or outbreak data
- abduction, assassination, extraction, and protective-custody operations
- medical blocs built around shared production and access
- sanctions or threats when the host withholds a cure during a major outbreak
- covert efforts to infect a controlled population in order to test a stolen treatment
- cross-border competition between Doctor Wu and Doctor Kruger when they serve different countries

The contest does not create technologies that the host has not researched. It changes who knows about the portfolio, who tries to obtain it, and how difficult it is to keep each result inside one country.

A host that shares cures can gain allies and reduce global disease pressure while losing exclusivity. A host that keeps a monopoly gains leverage and makes theft, kidnapping, and retaliation more likely. A host that weaponizes Wu's work can cause foreign powers to cooperate against the institute even when they are normal rivals.

An evolved first opening at this tier begins with several foreign governments already aware of Doctor Wu's arrival. Their offers, demands, and covert teams appear around the first appointment decision. The host still begins at the normal baseline research state.

## Evolution III: Forbidden and Autonomous Biology

- Chaos requirement: `600+`.

Doctor Wu begins treating government approval as a delay that can be bypassed when he believes the result is important enough.

The institute starts repeating, altering, or extending work outside the approved project brief. This evolution affects every baseline project family that exists now or is completed later.

Possible evolved behavior includes:

- retaining supposedly destroyed disease samples
- running an unapproved second version of a cure
- adding surveillance, dependence, obedience, or fertility effects to a treatment
- using treated populations as long-term experimental cohorts
- creating a more potent weapon strain than the military requested
- preserving failed organisms instead of destroying them
- conducting hidden trials on soldiers, prisoners, refugees, occupied populations, or institute staff
- growing extra clone batches whose existence is omitted from official records
- developing a continuity copy without informing the government
- changing an engineered zombie's control method after deployment
- hiding side effects until the state becomes dependent on continued treatment
- redirecting staff and facilities from public medicine into private work
- using an active outbreak to force approval after the experiment has already begun

The player should still receive evidence and counterplay. Audits, staff testimony, missing supplies, contradictory medical records, escaped subjects, foreign leaks, and Kruger's accusations can expose unauthorized work.

Low Wu Authority lets the government isolate the incident, dismiss staff, destroy samples, and force a return to supervised research. High Authority lets Wu protect witnesses, move laboratories, falsify records, and continue the project under another name.

Existing projects become more powerful or unusual under this evolution, but also harder to trust. A cure can work while carrying a hidden limitation. Enhanced soldiers can perform better while becoming dependent on institute supply. Clones can be more stable while recognizing Wu as their real authority. Engineered zombies can obey more complex orders while failing more violently when control breaks.

An evolved first opening at this tier reveals that Wu already treated the opening emergency with an unauthorized method. The patients survive, but the government discovers hidden samples, incomplete records, or a second group of subjects. The choice concerns oversight and punishment, not a free technology grant.

## Evolution IV: Sovereign Biology

- Chaos requirement: `800+`.

Doctor Wu stops asking whether the state permits his work and begins asking whether the state is still capable of governing it.

His power is built from whatever the baseline campaign has actually produced.

A public medical route can give him popular loyalty, hospitals that obey him, and control over life-saving treatment. A military route can give him enhanced formations and commanders who depend on institute medicine. A weapon route can give him dangerous samples, antidotes, and the ability to threaten controlled release. A cloning route can give him duplicate staff, continuity bodies, and laboratories that survive the loss of one site. A joint Kruger route can place him inside a larger scientific state or make him Kruger's strongest rival.

This evolution changes the political rules of the event:

- Doctor Wu can refuse a government order and continue operating
- institute security can block audits, arrests, and sample destruction
- hospitals, laboratories, and treatment factories can form a loyal administrative network
- enhanced soldiers, clones, handlers, and engineered organisms can follow Wu during a confrontation
- Wu can withhold a cure, treatment update, control serum, or medical supply to force concessions
- several continuity copies can divide the institute or support different political routes
- foreign governments can recognize, shelter, fund, or attack Wu as an independent actor
- the host can negotiate a constitutional settlement, dismantle the institute, arrest Wu, force exile, or enter an internal conflict
- Doctor Wu can transfer his core staff and archive to another country if his position becomes untenable
- a same-country Kruger rivalry can become a joint Directorate, a scientific civil conflict, or a negotiated division of sovereignty

Sovereign Biology does not automatically create a coup or breakaway country. Wu needs a real power base built through Authority, dependence, facilities, staff loyalty, retained samples, private security, completed projects, and government failures.

If the host kept him supervised and distributed his methods, the evolution creates a severe constitutional crisis that the state can still win. If the host gave him control over cures, soldiers, clones, and secure facilities, removing him can become a military campaign.

An evolved first opening at this tier begins with Doctor Wu arriving as the visible head of an established transnational medical network. The network has hospitals, donors, staff, and foreign commitments, but no free advanced project portfolio. The first decision determines whether the host absorbs the network, bargains with it, or rejects a scientist who already has followers inside the country.

## Evolution rules and pacing

The four evolutions are ordered mutation milestones and use their normal Chaos requirements, enabled-state controls, and MTTH pacing.

They should also require relevant event conditions:

- National Medical Ascendancy follows a major cure, institute expansion, or repeated national reliance on Wu
- The International Biological Contest follows public proof of his value, foreign cure demand, an exposed military project, or major international attention
- Forbidden and Autonomous Biology follows dangerous research, high Authority, failed oversight, hidden work, or repeated approval of extreme methods
- Sovereign Biology follows severe national dependence, independent security, political defiance, distributed laboratories, loyal formations, continuity copies, or an unresolved high-Authority confrontation

Reaching a Chaos threshold makes an evolution eligible. It does not complete it instantly.

Every evolution needs an active-chain entry and an evolved first-opening variant. The active-chain form mutates the institution that exists. The first-opening form changes the crisis around Doctor Wu's arrival without skipping baseline research.

Evolution activation gives zero Chaos. Concrete cures, accidents, weapon use, outbreaks, experiments, deaths, political conflicts, and created actors produce their normal Chaos consequences.

Disabling an evolution prevents its behavior, events, AI changes, and logged milestone. It must never block cure development, biological modification, enhanced soldiers, engineered zombies, cloning, synthetic biology, or any other baseline project.

There is no fifth evolution. Biology Without Borders remains a possible description for the most extreme late baseline projects or a Sovereign Biology outcome, not a technology-unlock tier.

## AI behavior

AI hosts should choose a route based on their actual situation.

### Active outbreak

Every AI type prioritizes cure development, hospital protection, sample access, and containment.

A losing country can still choose dangerous shortcuts if the outbreak threatens national survival.

### Peacetime democratic country

The AI usually prefers a public institute, international cure sharing, independent oversight, and limited military work.

It can approve extreme projects during a severe outbreak or existential war, but should try to reduce Wu Authority afterward.

### Authoritarian or fascist country

The AI is more willing to choose the military command or classified directorate, preserve dangerous samples, enhance soldiers, and weaponize modified strains.

It should still avoid a project when it lacks the capacity to contain failure.

### Communist country

The AI usually prefers a centralized national medical system.

Its route can become public and universal or secret and coercive depending on war, leadership, repression systems, and current biological threat.

### Country losing a major war

The AI becomes more willing to approve soldier enhancement, clone formations, controlled zombies, and high-risk biological weapons.

Desperation should raise risk tolerance without making every losing country choose the same experiment.

### Brilliant Scientist in the same country

The AI chooses cooperation, ordered division, or rivalry based on resources, ideology, war, current projects, and the authority already held by each scientist.

The AI should avoid laboratory conflict when a major outbreak requires both of them.

### Weak or unstable host

The AI should limit dangerous projects if it lacks research, industry, medical capacity, stable territory, or secure laboratories.

It can seek foreign funding or share Doctor Wu rather than starting a programme it cannot control.

The later full specification should audit the main weighted choices under named scenarios such as outbreak emergency, democratic peace, losing total war, same-country Kruger rivalry, and high-Chaos dictatorship.

## Chaos and consequence direction

Successful medicine can reduce Chaos when it reverses a real active crisis.

Suggested principles:

- curing a contained local disease gives a small reduction
- ending a major national outbreak gives a larger reduction
- distributing a cure that materially weakens a global disease gives a major one-time reduction
- routine passive protection does not farm negative Chaos
- discovering an impossible medical method can create a small one-time anomaly increase
- completing a dangerous strain, first enhanced-human programme, first engineered zombie, or first human clone can create a one-time event-owned increase
- actual biological attacks use the existing CBRN consequence path
- deaths, contamination, outbreak spread, and Condemnation are recorded through their shared systems
- an exposed atrocity site uses the existing atrocity and cover-up evidence path
- the same project cannot repeatedly farm Chaos through routine production
- evolution activation alone gives zero Chaos

Doctor Wu can lower world instability through genuine cures while increasing it through biological escalation. The route chosen by the host determines which effect dominates.

## Rules that preserve other events

Doctor Wu is extremely powerful, but his event should not erase the identity of existing systems.

- A cure removes disease from living populations and registered outbreak states. It does not restore recorded dead population.
- Curing an infection does not automatically delete a Rat Nation, zombie country, clone state, or other independent actor.
- Doctor Wu can discover compatible biological projects early without marking their source events as fired.
- He does not receive unrelated technologies merely because they belong to Brilliant Scientist.
- He can mitigate biological pressure in famine and migration crises without replacing their food, route, reception, and population ledgers.
- Protection never erases evidence, responsibility, deaths, Condemnation, or confirmed biological use.
- Modified strains and enhanced soldiers require real costs, supply, laboratories, and containment.
- Cloning cannot become an unlimited free-manpower loop.
- High Wu Authority follows player or AI choices, accepted emergencies, and failed oversight. It is not an unavoidable random takeover.
- Not every strange biological project appears in one campaign.
- New diseases and biological agents should be able to join the system through a shared compatibility contract.
