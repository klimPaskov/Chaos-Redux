## Substantive design checks

**Knowledge and material.** The archive, manufacture, stock, operation, and delivered care are distinct. Retooling cannot change a collected consignment. Capture of an archive cannot fabricate physical stock. Ordinary equipment's existing licensing and lend-lease exclusions are retained.

**Capacity.** One initial modified standard and the optional second annex share a country-wide ceiling across continuity copies and regional institutes. Research uses the existing lanes. Existing cures and aftercare are not terminated by closing a modified standard, and care cannot be used to manufacture extra offensive material.

**Choice quality.** Alternative tempo, burden, matching, recipient, context, control, and defensive profiles have distinct roles and limits. The paired list is short and fixed. The design rejects unrestricted all-traits combinations and blocks unsupported owner facts instead of supplying a generic result.

**Native integration.** Profile identity must follow an actual native reservation. The current archetype and fixed-agent interfaces do not establish the proposed finer distinction. The exact owner extension remains a prerequisite for implementation, with no second arsenal, staging system, stock balance, native debit, or refund.

**Care and people.** Matching services retain their state, provider, and case identity. General treatment remains useful and adaptation remains reachable. A recall treats people as existing people with care obligations. It cannot create negative deaths, free manpower, loyalty, or a second first settlement.

**Food and reception.** Unsettled living cohorts remain in custody with real demand. New recipients cannot bypass Part 8's shared receiving capacity or missing food-safety proof. Medical success does not create housing, food, or a safe route.

**Presentation.** The part supplies working labels and direction, not pasteable localisation. Ordinary phases keep their value and action limits. A changed profile alone does not justify a new body model or an unapproved asset family.
