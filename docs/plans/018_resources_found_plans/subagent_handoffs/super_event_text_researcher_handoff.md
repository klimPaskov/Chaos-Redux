## Final selections

### Slot 82

- title: `THE OTH-KESH HOST RISES`
- quotation: `As for the earth, out of it comes bread. Underneath it is turned up as it were by fire.`
- attribution: `Job 28:5, World English Bible`
- button: `The field has a sovereign.`
- purpose: announce the birth of a hostile state from the extraction field without presenting it as a world end

### Slot 83

- title: `THE DEEP WAR CROSSES THE SEAS`
- quotation: `the firmament is embroiled with the deep.`
- attribution: `Aeschylus, Prometheus Bound, trans. Theodore Alois Buckley`
- button: `Every deep has found a road.`
- purpose: announce simultaneous major emergence fronts beyond the Host's first continent

### Slot 84

- title: `THE LAST DEPTH IS SEALED`
- quotation: `In peace sons bury their fathers, in war fathers bury their sons.`
- attribution: `Croesus in Herodotus, Histories 1.87, trans. A. D. Godley`
- button: `The living will rebuild above it.`
- purpose: record a costly peace with burial, reconstruction, shared surveys, and retained underground-war knowledge

All recommended titles, descriptions, and buttons are original authored text. The quotations and attribution lines are sourced. The research file labels that boundary explicitly.

## Main findings

- The three events need distinct public meaning. Emergence is state formation, world end is loss of continental containment, and defeat is reconstruction after a globally significant war.
- Job 28 is the best emergence anchor because its immediate context is mining, valuable material, shafts, hidden things, and a dangerous depth.
- The final clause of *Prometheus Bound* gives the world-end slot a compact image of the ordered world above becoming entangled with the deep below.
- Herodotus gives the defeat slot a complete sentence about the generational reversal caused by war. It keeps victory subordinate to the dead.
- Pliny's mining indictment, Milton's rising beast, biblical abyss imagery, Hesiod's cosmic battle, Poe's thousand thrones, Wells's lesson of pity, and an official USGS mine-safety warning were retained as documented alternatives.
- Existing active super-event quotations in slots 67 through 77 were checked for direct phrase reuse. The final quotations do not duplicate them.

## Source and license confidence

- Slot 82 uses the World English Bible British Edition, whose linked page explicitly declares the edition public domain.
- Slot 83 uses a Project Gutenberg transcription marked public domain in the United States. The translator, Theodore Alois Buckley, died in 1856.
- Slot 84 uses the A. D. Godley translation on Perseus. The source page licenses its display text under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 United States and provides the 1920 publication record. Godley died in 1925, so the underlying translation is also beyond life-plus-70 terms.

Direct source URLs, work dates, contexts, rights notes, and translation caveats are in the research file.

## Implementation cautions

- The slot 83 source clause begins with a lower-case letter in the primary transcription. The package preserves it exactly. Capitalizing it would be an editorial normalization that should be recorded as such.
- Historical source language is interpretive framing. It must not become Oth-Kesh religion, species lore, or a claim that Vhorruk is a figure from an existing tradition.
- Keep trigger thresholds out of the public description. The supplied descriptions state only visible conditions.
- Keep slot 84 conditional. Its text assumes a long, costly, widely experienced war and is unsuitable for a local or early defeat.
- The final titles in this handoff replace working labels only for the text package. The parent agent owns any specification promotion and all live wiring.
