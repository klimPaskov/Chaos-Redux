## Recommendation and bounded scope

Implement the European regional-matrix row `The River Ration League` as the next manually reviewed dormant Fallout chain after Ashline Firebreak.

The chain is a seven-block regional incident in which one European successor controls at least two viable districts along the Danube corridor, but barge crews, granary councils, bridge guards, and flood engineers disagree over who may move food and clean water.

The four choices are the matrix-promised joint barge law, upstream priority, armed customs, and flood authority.

The chain creates durable river-compact trust and border-tension memories that a later river federation or barge-war arc may consume.

It does not create a country, formable, war, decision category, focus route, scripted GUI, bilateral treaty system, recurring on action, achievement, or super-event.

Those additions would exceed one reviewed ordinary tranche and would duplicate systems whose scheduler payload contracts are not yet accepted.

## Opening conflict and government awareness

Recovered barges can move grain and filtered water again, but the old river rules no longer have an uncontested sovereign.

Upstream storehouses want priority.

Downstream ration councils demand scheduled allotments.

Bridge militias want tolls and searches.

Pump and flood crews argue that navigation will fail unless one authority controls dredging, levees, warnings, and emergency closures.

## Research basis and regional connections

The fictional league draws on real European river commissions without asserting institutional survival.

The Danube Commission's historical account records international navigation commissions from the 1856 Treaty of Paris and an unrestricted, equal-navigation regime under the 1921 Definitive Statute.

The later 1948 convention is outside some possible Fallout timelines, but its separation of free navigation, maintenance duties, hydraulic works, and special river administration is useful structural inspiration only.

Modern Danube flood planning demonstrates that flood management joins prevention, protection, preparedness, warnings, mapped risk, and coordination across a shared basin.

Research references:

- [Danube Commission historical background](https://www.danubecommission.org/uploads/doc/2018/DC_A5_leaflet_final_web.pdf)
- [Danube Commission navigation convention](https://www.danubecommission.org/dc/en/danube-commission/convention-regarding-the-regime-of-navigation-on-the-danube/)
- [ICPDR flood risk management](https://www.icpdr.org/tasks-topics/tasks/flood-risk-management)
