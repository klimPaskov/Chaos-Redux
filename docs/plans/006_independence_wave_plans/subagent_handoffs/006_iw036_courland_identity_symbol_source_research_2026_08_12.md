## Symbol findings

### 1930 Kurzeme regional coat of arms (design reference only)

The Latvian National Encyclopedia records the 1930 Kurzeme arms as a silver field with a red lion turned in the opposite direction (`red lion contourné`). The entry states that artist Kārlis Krauze designed the regional arms under Rihards Zariņš and that regulations signed by the Latvian prime minister approved the district arms on 26 April 1930. The lion was derived from the historical Duchy of Courland and Semigallia arms, but the 1930 regional device is a Latvian historical-land symbol rather than proof of an independent 1936 state.

Source: [Latvian National Encyclopedia, “Latviešu vēsturiskās zemes. Kurzemes ģerbonis”](https://enciklopedija.lv/skirklis/181350-latvie%C5%A1u-v%C4%93stur%C4%81s-zemes-Kurzemes-%C4%A3erbonis).

The article identifies the archival image as “Kurzemes ģerbonis. 1930. gads. Zīmējuma autors Kārlis Krauze. Avots: LNA Latvijas Valsts Vēstures arhīvs.” The embedded image URL is [LNA-referenced 1930 drawing](https://enciklopedija.lv/api/image/original?name=7be7c2358535-96fa9908-2697-47ea-9bbb-c983fca215a6.jpg); the alternate multimedia image is [the article’s second image record](https://enciklopedija.lv/api/image/original?name=965d488b7ea1-a757b94a-293c-42e9-86a7-751e8a4b9a5c.jpg).

The National Encyclopedia rights notice says that text, images, and third-party material remain subject to their respective rights, that photos and illustrations require permission from the author or rightsholder, and that transformations must not make the source unidentifiable. See [National Encyclopedia copyright, data protection, and use notice](https://enciklopedija.lv/autortiesibas-datu-aizsardziba-un-izmantosana). Therefore the article and archival scan are **research/design references, not cleared runtime art**. Do not copy, crop, recolour, or ship the scan without a separate rights decision.

An official legal text confirms the same geometry: Section 4 of the modern law describes Kurzeme as “a red lion turned in the opposite direction on a silver field.” See [Law on the Coat of Arms of Vidzeme, Latgale, Kurzeme, and Zemgale](https://likumi.lv/ta/en/en/id/246381-law-on-the-coat-of-arms-of-vidzeme-latgale-kurzeme-and-zemgale). The law was adopted in 2012 and its translation carries State Language Centre copyright, so it is a geometry corroboration rather than a 1936 legal source or an art licence. Its Annex 3 image is [IMAGE005.JPG](https://likumi.lv/wwwraksti/VVC_TULKOJUMI/2012//BILDES/LAW%20ON%20THE%20COAT%20OF%20ARMS%20OF%20VIDZEME,%20LATGALE,%20KURZEME,%20AND%20ZEMGALE_FILES/IMAGE005.JPG); do not treat that modern annex as a historical runtime asset.

**Use recommendation:** The red lion on silver is admissible as a cited motif constraint for a newly generated, flat, civic flag only after the parent chooses the provisional-civic route and the asset worker produces a fresh ImageGen master plus normal/medium/small TGA variants and a manifest. It is not admissible as a direct source image and does not by itself clear BJX for code.

### Red-over-white Courland flag (traditional-duchy route only)

The historical red-over-white horizontal flag is attributed to the Duchy of Courland and Semigallia from the early modern period, conventionally 1561–1795. The secondary [Flag of Courland summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Courland) describes the red-over-white design, the 1561 adoption and 1795 relinquishment, and distinguishes civil, naval, and governorate variants. This is useful corroboration, not a licence or proof of 1936 civic status.

The strongest archival lead is the Library of Congress scan of Carington Bowles’s 1783 chart, [Bowle’s universal display of the naval flags of all nations in the world](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2003680671/), handle [http://hdl.loc.gov/loc.pnp/pga.00276](http://hdl.loc.gov/loc.pnp/pga.00276). The chart was published under an Act dated 19 May 1783 and the Library record gives the rights advisory **“No known restrictions on publication.”** Available derivatives include [150px JPEG](https://cdn.loc.gov/service/pnp/pga/00200/00276_150px.jpg), [right JPEG](https://cdn.loc.gov/service/pnp/pga/00200/00276r.jpg), [verso JPEG](https://cdn.loc.gov/service/pnp/pga/00200/00276v.jpg), and [TIFF master](https://cdn.loc.gov/master/pnp/pga/00200/00276u.tif). The Library also cautions that it generally does not own rights and cannot grant or deny permission, so retain the LOC record and its rights advisory in any future asset manifest.

The [Flags of the World Courland page](https://www.fotw.info/flags/lv-cour.html) (last modified 2023-06-03) cites Karlis Dzirkalis’s 1936 *Latvijas karoga vesture*, Norie/Hobbs (1848), and an eighteenth-century chart. It is a secondary research aid and has its own site/image-rights disclaimer; do not ship its images.

The following Wikimedia Commons reconstructions are useful only for geometry comparison, not as period originals: [state flag reconstruction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_Courland_(state).svg), [civil flag reconstruction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_Courland_(civil).svg), and [naval reconstruction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_Courland_(Naval).svg). They are marked PD-self by the uploader, but they reconstruct historical designs and the naval file is route-specific. The [Commons scan of the 1783 naval-flags chart](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Naval_flags_of_Courland_1783.png) is a Public Domain Mark copy of the LOC source; use the LOC record as the controlling archive.

**Use recommendation:** Red-over-white may be used only if the event explicitly enters a traditional-duchy/restoration route. It must be described as a historically attested duchy flag family, not as a neutral 1936 Kurzeme civic flag. Do not combine it with modern regional arms or present it as an automatic BJX flag ladder without a route owner and date/function note.

## Historical and institutional caveats

- Kurzeme was a historical region within interwar Latvia in 1936; the evidence above does not establish an independent Courland government in that year.
- The 1930 arms date is period-appropriate for a regional motif, but the device was approved as Latvian district/historical-land heraldry. It should not be relabelled as a sovereign state emblem.
- The 2012 Latvian law is useful for geometry only and must not be back-projected as the 1936 legal instrument.
- The 1561–1795 duchy flag and its quartered/dynastic arms belong to a restoration route. The 1918 German-sponsored Courland/Landesrat episode and the 1795–1918 Russian Courland Governorate flag are not neutral 1936 baselines.
- Current Kurzeme Planning Region branding is modern and is not evidence for an independent Event 006 polity.
- The Latvian national flag and full Latvian state arms cannot be used as neutral Courland symbols. The red lion component is a regional/historical-land motif and also appears in Latvian state heraldry.
