The current Commons record uses `{{Cc-zero-project}} {{Pd-old-70}}` and reports public-domain metadata, but those labels do not establish an affirmative CC0 dedication or a U.S. sound-recording clearance.

The linked Public Domain Project record labels the item `PD USA 2020` and `PD INT 2020`, but its licensing prose explains the country-of-origin and life-plus-70 or 50-years-after-publication basis rather than supplying a recording-rights waiver that resolves the U.S. pre-1972 sound-recording issue or every worldwide jurisdiction.

The U.S. Copyright Office’s current Title 17 §1401 text covers unauthorized use of sound recordings fixed before 15 February 1972 and keeps the prohibition through the 95-year publication term plus an applicable transition period, with a final statutory ceiling of 15 February 2067.

As an arithmetic inference from that statute, a 1949 first publication falls in the 1947–1956 tier, reaches the end of the 95-year period in 2044, and has a further 15-year transition through the end of 2059 unless a separate rights basis applies.

The CC0 and Public Domain Mark deeds also make clear that a public-domain label or mark is not a substitute for checking jurisdiction-specific rights and does not itself warrant the status of a work.

Disposition: retain the London Brass Players recording as the accepted original selection but keep it blocked, undownloaded, unprocessed, and unwired.

### Rights analysis

The `Cc-zero-project` template currently describes the collection and Wikimedia CH cooperation; it is not itself a CC0 legal-code grant from the recording rights owner.

The `PD-old-70` template explicitly requires a separate United States public-domain tag explaining why a work is public domain in the United States, and the London file’s raw description does not provide such a separate recording-specific U.S. basis.

The Public Domain Project record is useful provenance for the source transfer and catalog metadata, but its `PD USA 2020` and `PD INT 2020` labels conflict with the absence of a rights-holder waiver and do not explain how a 1949 non-U.S. sound recording clears the U.S. pre-1972 rules.

The Public Domain Mark deed says that a work identified as free of known restrictions in one jurisdiction may not be free of known restrictions in all jurisdictions and that the identifier supplies no warranty of status.

The U.S. statute therefore prevents an age-only conclusion that this exact recording is free to redistribute in the United States in 2026.

Worldwide redistribution is likewise unverified because the current source record provides no affirmative rights-holder license, CC0 dedication, or jurisdiction-complete waiver for the recording itself.

Required unblock: obtain a written permission or waiver from the actual recording rights holder that expressly covers redistribution in the United States and worldwide, or obtain explicit parent/user approval to reopen the accepted cue selection and choose a separately rights-complete recording.
