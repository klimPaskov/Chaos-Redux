## Licensing and redistribution assessment

### Recording

The Commons file description states that the Croatian Armed Forces Symphonic Wind Orchestra placed the recording online for free download and applies `{{cc-zero}}`.

The current file page identifies the license as **Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal Public Domain Dedication**.

The linked CC0 deed states that the rights holder waives copyright and related/neighbouring rights worldwide to the extent legally possible, including copying, modifying, distributing, performing, and commercial use, without permission or attribution requirements.

Recording-license confidence is **high for the file-level grant** because the page carries an explicit CC0 dedication rather than a country-specific public-domain template.

Residual authority confidence is **medium-high rather than absolute** because Commons identifies the uploader as the source of the file description and does not expose a signed Ministry of Defence release; the description nevertheless attributes the free-download publication to the named Croatian Armed Forces orchestra.

### Composition

Muhvić died in 1942, and the biographical source identifies him as the composer of *Mačekova koračnica*.

The available sources do not document the exact composition or publication date of this march.

The life/death evidence supports public-domain status in many life-plus-term jurisdictions, but this handoff does not assert a universal composition-rights conclusion for every possible redistribution country because the reviewed source does not separately establish the term in each jurisdiction.

The CC0 evidence is strongest for the uploaded recording itself; the parent should retain this composition-date and jurisdiction caveat in the final legal review if the mod is distributed worldwide.

No attribution is legally required by CC0.

Recommended courtesy attribution if the cue is later approved: `Ivo Muhvić, Mačekova koračnica; Croatian Armed Forces Symphonic Wind Orchestra; source Wikimedia Commons, CC0 1.0; edited excerpt.`

## Historical and tonal fit

The Croatian biography identifies Muhvić as a military bandmaster and conductor, records his 1936 recording of the Croatian anthem in the Croatian National and University Library, and notes that he taught military-orchestra instrumentation at the Zagreb Music Academy from 1936 to 1941.

The same source identifies him as the composer of *Mačekova koračnica*, likely written for the needs of Croatian Protection (`Hrvatska zaštita`), and notes that the march is performed by the Croatian Army Orchestra today.

This gives the cue a direct 1936-centered military-band and national-institution connection even though the exact composition date of the march is not documented.

The instrumentation is a ceremonial symphonic wind/military band, with a public-proclamation energy suitable for the league formation and treaty-order reveal.

Compared with the accepted Clarke cue, it is less organ-like and more period-local, and it avoids the overtly United States-specific signal of the Sousa alternatives.

The main tonal risk is cultural specificity: the title invokes Stjepan Radić-era Croatian Peasant Party leader Vladko Maček, so the march may read as Croatian or partisan rather than pan-league unless the event's independence-wave identity intentionally embraces that regional voice.

Human listening remains required for the final cadence, perceived political valence, and whether the 110-second excerpt sustains the intended reveal pacing.
