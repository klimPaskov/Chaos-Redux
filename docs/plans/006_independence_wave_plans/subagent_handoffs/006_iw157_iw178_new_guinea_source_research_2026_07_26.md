## IW-157 West Papua (`WPG`, state 669)

### Candidate A: Yapen/Serui, Yapen Islands, Cenderawasih Bay

This is the strongest named territorial lead if state `669` demonstrably contains the Yapen/Serui district or a dedicated current-map substate.
Serui and the Yapen Islands are a distinct local geography and are not interchangeable with Biak, Arfak, Marind, Dani, or a pan-Papuan label.
Silas Ayari Donrai Papare (1918–1978) was born in Serui, worked as a nurse in the Sorong hospital in 1936, returned to Serui in 1940, and organized local resistance and intelligence around Cenderawasih Bay during 1942–1944.
Those facts give a real male local figure with adult wartime activity, but his overt political leadership is primarily post-war and the surviving permissive portraits are from 1950 or later.

Portrait source leads:

- [Silas Papare, interviewed in 1950](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Silas_Papare_interviewed_in_1950.jpg) is a period-adjacent newspaper image dated 1950, attributed to *Pandji Negara* (18 March 1950), with a `PD-IDOld-Art30` claim on the Commons file page.
- [Silas Papare in *Kami Perkenalkan*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Silas_Papare,_Kami_Perkenalkan_(1954),_p154.jpg) is a 1954 Indonesian Ministry of Information biographical publication image with a `PD-IDGov` claim.
- [Silas Papare, 1955 parliamentary volume](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Silas_Papare,_Hasil_Rakjat_Memilih_Tokoh-tokoh_Parlemen_(Hasil_Pemilihan_Umum_Pertama_-_1955)_di_Republik_Indonesia,_p419.jpg) is a government parliamentary portrait dated 1956, sourced to the People's Representative Council, with a `PD-IDGov` claim.
- [Official Indonesian biography repository](http://repositori.kemdikbud.go.id/7607/) is the Ministry of Education and Culture source packet for Marthen Indey and Silas Papare; it is useful for identity and role confirmation, but its image-reuse terms must be checked separately.

Disposition: **needs_user_review / not admitted**.
The 1950 source is the best identity-master lead, but the portrait date is after the 1936–1945 WWII visual band and the Indonesian old-art rights template needs territorial confirmation before conversion.
Admission would require a map check proving Yapen/Serui containment in state `669`, a dated release gate that makes Papare an adult local actor, an unchanged attributed master with a verified rights basis, and an independently reviewed community-specific symbol.

### Candidate B: Biak–Numfor / Supiori, Biak cultural area

Biak–Numfor and Supiori are a defensible named community and district distinction for western New Guinea, but they cannot stand in for all of West Papua.
Petrus Kafiar (birth year reported as 1864 or circa 1873; died 1926) was born on Supiori, trained through the Mansinam mission, returned to his home village in 1908, and became a pioneer teacher-preacher among Biak communities.
His institutional role is real and locally specific, but he died before the Event 006 period and is not a suitable default wartime officeholder without a legacy-institution route.

Portrait/source leads:

- [Petrus Kafiar source photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Petrus-kafiar.dok-2.jpg) describes an image believed to date from his Depok Seminary study period circa 1892–1896; the author is unknown and the page carries a `PD-IDOld-Art30` claim.
- [Nationaal Archief staged Kafiar scene, 1955](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Scène_uit_het_openluchtspel_100_jaar_Zending_in_Nieuw-Guinea._De_zendeling_geeft_de_gekochte_slaaf,_Petrus_Kafiar,_Europese_kleding,_Bestanddeelnr_144-0882.tif) is a public-domain (`CC-zero`) Dutch New Guinea government photograph, but it is a staged play and cannot serve as a real-person identity master.
- [Second Nationaal Archief staged Kafiar scene, 1955](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tijdens_de_herdenking_van_100_jaar_Zending_in_Nieuw-Guinea_werd_het_openluchtspel_Petrus_Kafiar_opgevoerd,_Bestanddeelnr_144-1003.tif) is likewise `CC-zero` archival context only, not a portrait.

Disposition: **blocked** for the current country-leader package.
The identity source is unattributed and rights are not independently cleared, the subject died in 1926, and the available public-domain archival scenes are reenactments.
Do not use a generated face, a reenactor, the staged scene, or a broad Biak/Morning-Star identity as a substitute.

### IW-157 symbol/flag evidence

- [Flag of the Koreri movement](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_the_Koreri_movement.svg) is a community-specific reconstruction of a Biak–Numfor Koreri resistance flag, explicitly sourced on its file page to Freerk Ch. Kamma, *Koreri: Messianic Movements in the Biak-Numfor Culture Area* (1972), and released by the redraw author under CC0.
- The Koreri movement itself is documented as a Biak-centered anti-occupation movement beginning in 1939 in the [Silas Papare historical account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silas_Papare), with the cited Indonesian government research packet linked above.
- The Koreri SVG is a 2025 reconstruction, not a contemporary flag photograph or an official period flag record; it may guide a later source-locked flat design only after primary archival corroboration and cultural review.
- The [Morning Star flag](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Morning_Star_flag.svg) is a post-1961 broad West Papuan independence symbol and is not a valid automatic symbol for a named Yapen, Biak, or other district package.

Symbol disposition: **needs_user_review / not admitted**.
No directly attested, period-specific Yapen/Serui flag was found in this bounded pass.
The Koreri lead is useful because it is community-specific, but the package must not present a reconstruction as an authentic historic flag without additional primary evidence.

### IW-157 admission gate

Admit only if the main agent confirms that state `669` contains the selected Yapen/Serui or Biak–Numfor district rather than relying on the broad `West Papua` state name.
Require one named local institution or district authority, one sourced real male leader with a rights-clear unchanged master and an appropriate release-date gate, and a symbol whose source describes the exact community rather than all Papuans.
Until those receipts exist, keep `WPG` unbound and preserve its carrier content and origin guards.

## IW-178 Papua (`PNG`, state 523)

### Candidate A: Dogura/Gedulalara, Milne Bay District

Dogura/Gedulalara is a named village and Milne Bay District anchor on the Papua side and is distinct from Port Moresby, Gulf, Oro, Tolai, and highland identities.
Sir John Douglas Guise (1914–1991) was born in Gedulalara near Dogura in Milne Bay District, worked at Samarai before the war, served with the Australian New Guinea Administrative Unit as a Signals clerk during the war, joined the police in 1946, and later represented East Papua in the Legislative Council.
The [Pacific Islander Biography entry](http://pib.anu.edu.au/biography/guise-sir-john-15980) and its [Australian Dictionary of Biography article](https://adb.anu.edu.au/biography/guise-sir-john-15980/text35290) are authoritative institutional sources for the district, dates, and role progression.

Disposition: **needs_user_review / not admitted**.
Guise is an adult period-fit district figure, but this pass did not locate an unchanged archival male portrait with a permissive rights statement, and the sources identify a district/village rather than a specific named indigenous community.
The safe route is a Dogura/Milne Bay institutional package using authentic Anglican mission or District Native Administration material if an archival image and rights basis can be obtained; do not invent a Motu, Tolai, or pan-Papuan identity for Guise.

### Candidate B: Kerema / Gulf District

Albert Maori Kiki (1931–1993) was born in the Kerema district on the Papuan coast and became a major politician only from the late 1960s.
He is a useful later-period named Gulf candidate but is too young for a 1936 opening and no rights-clear period portrait was established in this pass.

Disposition: **blocked for a WWII-era opening** unless a later release-date gate and an independent portrait source are explicitly accepted.

### Candidate C: Motu–Koitabu / Hanuabada, Port Moresby District

Motu and Koitabu are named coastal peoples associated with Hanuabada and Port Moresby and are not a generic `Papua` identity.
The [Motu historical overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motu_people) records the Hiri trade cycle and the lakatoi trading canoe, with early ethnographic documentation by C. G. Seligman.
Seligman's [*The Melanesians of British New Guinea* (1910) scan](https://archive.org/details/melanesiansofbri00seli) is a public-domain source candidate for Hiri/lakatoi geometry and period visual context.

Disposition: **institutional-symbol lead only**.
The Hiri/lakatoi motif can inform a later flat civic design, but it is not an attested historic national flag and no named adult male Hanuabada/Motu–Koitabu leader with a rights-clear wartime portrait was established here.
Do not use a modern Hiri Moale festival photograph, PNG national flag, or generic Melanesian emblem as a substitute.

### IW-178 symbol/flag evidence

The only defensible symbol lead from this pass is the Hiri/lakatoi trading complex for a Motu–Koitabu package, sourced to the 1910 public-domain ethnography above.
It must be treated as a sourced motif for a new civic design, not as proof that a historic Motu–Koitabu flag existed.
No period flag for a named Papua district was found with a sufficiently clear provenance and rights statement.

### IW-178 admission gate

Admit only after the main agent proves that the selected Dogura/Milne Bay, Kerema/Gulf, or Motu–Koitabu/Hanuabada district is contained by state `523` and does not consume a broad Papua replacement.
Require a named local institution plus a rights-clear sourced male leader or authentic archival institutional image, and a symbol source tied to that exact community.
Until then keep `PNG` unbound and preserve the vanilla USA/Japan/Indonesia interactions required by the accepted origin note.
