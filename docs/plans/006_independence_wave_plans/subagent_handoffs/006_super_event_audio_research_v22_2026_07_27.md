## Decision summary

No rights-cleared brass-and-organ recording that preserves the accepted composition and ceremonial tone was found. Two same-composition user recordings are legally usable in the United States under the source pages' stated terms, but both are organ/harpsichord performances and therefore require an explicit user choice before they can replace the accepted brass-and-organ cue. `6001` remains blocked and unselected.

The accepted `6001` source is `A Trumpet Voluntary`, London Brass Players, Harry Mortimer (trumpet), Reginald Foort (organ), conducted by George Weldon, recorded 8 November 1948 and first released in 1949. Its source page is [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:CC0-CH_-_London_Brass_Players_-_A_Trumpet_Voluntary_-_Jeremiah_Clarke_-_Columbia-dx1536-cax10357.flac), with direct file URL `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/75/CC0-CH_-_London_Brass_Players_-_A_Trumpet_Voluntary_-_Jeremiah_Clarke_-_Columbia-dx1536-cax10357.flac`. Commons reports 167.573 seconds. The composition is public domain, but the page does not provide a verified United States public-domain tag or rights-holder permission for this pre-1972 recording; the current [Commons United States copyright guidance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/United_States&oldid=1229600415) states that recordings first published from 1947 through 1956 are protected for 110 years after first publication, which also leaves this 1949 recording non-clear through at least 2059 absent a separate permission basis. The accepted brief therefore correctly keeps it rights-blocked. Do not download or process it under this handoff.

A 1927 recording therefore is not a safe United States redistribution candidate during this research pass (conservatively, not before 2028). It is the closest tonal match, but remains rejected until a current US-rights basis or permission is supplied.
