## Candidates and disposition

### Casimir d’Almeida — conditional named-person candidate, not promoted

The Albert-Kahn collection identifies Casimir d’Almeida with spelling variants `Casimir d’Alméida` and `Casimir Dalméida`, inventory `A65668`, photographed by Chevalier Georges on 11 July 1931 at Boulogne, and describes him as a Dahomeyan delegate and politician.

Primary source: <https://collections.albert-kahn.hauts-de-seine.fr/document/monsieur-casimir-d-almeida/617a7a3ecf8b8968b335a783>.

The captured 1936 Dahomeyan Administrative Council election record lists Casimir d’Almeida as the elected Porto-Novo councillor, which supports a period council or provisional-government representative role but does not support an invented colony-wide presidency, sovereign title, or independent national-leader claim.

Role source: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Dahomeyan_Administrative_Council_election>, citing Patrick Manning, *Slavery, Colonialism and Economic Growth in Dahomey, 1640–1960* (Cambridge University Press, 2004), p. 273.

### Victorin Féliho and Richard Johnson — roster-only leads, no portrait promotion

The captured 1936 election record names Victorin Féliho as the elected Abomey councillor, Richard Johnson as the elected Ouidah councillor, and Casimir d’Almeida as the elected Porto-Novo councillor.

A contemporaneous portrait source with clear attribution and rights was not located for Victorin Féliho or Richard Johnson.

The Beninese newspaper *La Nation* corroborates Victorin Féliho as an AOF parliamentarian and Abomey communal councillor, but its article is biographical text and does not clear a portrait source or a country-leader consumer: <https://lanation.bj/societe/cinquantenaire-du-deces-de-victorin-feliho-honorer-la-memoire-dun-parlementaire-de-laof>.

The three names therefore support, at most, a parent-approved Administrative Council roster abstraction; they do not support a new named runtime character or a command roster without primary institutional evidence and a specific consumer.

### Maurice-Léon Bourgine — historical 1936 colonial administrator, rejected for this identity gate

The period governor list places Maurice-Léon Bourgine as Lieutenant-Governor of Dahomey from 15 February 1935 to 12 January 1937: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colonial_governors_of_Dahomey>.

This is a French colonial administrator representing the existing French colonial authority, not an indigenous Dahomeyan governing identity for the IW-095 provisional package, and no rights-cleared portrait consumer was established.

The French biographical reference is retained as contextual historical evidence only: <https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Bourgine>.
