## Historical identity and date gate

The Far Eastern Republic existed from April 1920 until its merger into the RSFSR on 15 November 1922. No FER flag or emblem is a historically current 1936 state symbol. A 1936 use must therefore be framed as a continuity, revival, or alternate-history institution rather than as an unbroken historical 1936 government.

The FER government approved the red-and-blue flag design on 11 November 1920, and Article 181 of the 27 April 1921 Constitution describes a red field with a dark-blue canton at the upper hoist and red `D.V.R.` letters arranged in a triangle. The constitutional description is the strongest period-anchored flag specification found.

Article 180 of the same Constitution describes the state arms as a red shield surrounded by a conifer wreath, with a sunrise and five-pointed silver star, a wheat sheaf crossed by an anchor and pointed pickaxe, and the republic letters on the wreath ribbons. The Heraldicum reference corroborates this design and records earlier provisional-government and banknote usages.

The historical design is a defensible institutional continuity identity, but its red field, star, and documented political context carry socialist or communist-era semantics. It is not evidence for a non-socialist neutral flag. If `neutral baseline` means route-neutral institutional continuity, `FER-H0-1920-FLAG` is the candidate to review. If it means non-socialist visual neutrality, the gate found no directly attested candidate.
