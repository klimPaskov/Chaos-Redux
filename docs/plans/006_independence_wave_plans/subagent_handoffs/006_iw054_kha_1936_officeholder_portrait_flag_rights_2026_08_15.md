## Exact 1936 office and institutional anchor

The safest exact institution is the **Khakass Autonomous Oblast Congress of Soviets and oblast Executive Committee** (`Хакасская автономная область`, regional Congress/`облисполком`).

The Presidium of VTsIK decree of 20 October 1930 transformed the Khakass district, within its existing borders, into an autonomous oblast that remained a territorial part of the West Siberian Krai.

The historical chronology records the first oblast Congress of Soviets in February 1931 and the election of the oblast Executive Committee, making the institution an established and continuing 1936 office rather than a reconstructed or invented body.

The 1925 RSFSR Constitution as amended before 1936 supplies the period legal framework for autonomous-oblast Congresses, executive committees, local competence, and subordination to higher Soviet bodies; it supports an autonomy route, not independent sovereignty.

The installed 1936 opening date therefore has a source-backed institutional carrier even though the current KHA package has no runtime institutional character or identity wiring.

## Exact personal officeholder candidate

**Sergey Yevgenyevich Sizyh** (`Сергей Евгеньевич Сизых`) is the strongest personal candidate for a 1 January 1936 opening.

The direct biographical chronology at Centrasia records his service as first secretary of the Khakass regional VKP(b) committee from February 1931 through October 1936, after earlier work in the Minusinsk district committee and before study at the Moscow Higher School of Party Organizers from October 1936.

The Russian-language biography identifies him as a Soviet party and state official and summarizes the Khakass regional-committee first-secretary role as 1931–36.

This is an exact role/date match for the 1936 opening date, but the candidate is a **conditional Soviet regional-party leader**, not a cleared indigenous-national or sovereign Khakass leader.

The direct biography identifies him as Russian and born at Taseevo in the Yenisei gubernia; the Minusinsk connection is an earlier administrative posting, not his birthplace or an ethnic Khakass identity claim.

The conservative runtime date must end the role in October 1936 until an archival record resolves the conflicting secondary endpoint that extends the appointment later into 1937.

The source page cites S. G. Filippov, *Territorial Leaders of the VKP(b) in 1934–1939*, Moscow 2016, p. 535; the book citation is a research lead rather than a direct archival inspection in this tranche.

## Flag and symbol route

Article 88 of the 1925 RSFSR Constitution defines the period RSFSR state flag as a red or scarlet field with gold `Р.С.Ф.С.Р.` letters in the upper hoist.

That constitutional flag is the only securely dated legal flag source found for the 1936 setting and is defensible as a **Soviet-autonomy route flag** for the Khakass regional Executive Committee.

It is not evidence of a separate Khakass national flag and cannot be presented as a neutral independent-country symbol.

The official Government of the Republic of Khakassia state-symbols page dates the current Republic flag to the Law of 25 November 2002 No. 68 and the current coat of arms to the Law of 26 March 2003 No. 11; both are post-1936 and are rejected for this package.

No separate 1930–36 Khakass flag, emblem, or legally adopted regional standard was verified in this bounded source review.

The solar sign, Khakass ornament, and related modern motifs remain cultural research leads only; without period symbol law or archival provenance, they cannot be promoted to a neutral 1936 flag.
