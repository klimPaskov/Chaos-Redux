## Flag and symbol provenance

### Existing runtime `DAH` family

Installed vanilla provides complete normal, medium, and small flag triplets under `C:/Program Files (x86)/Steam/steamapps/common/Hearts of Iron IV/gfx/flags/`.

The normal files are 82×52 uncompressed TGAs with these SHA-256 values: `DAH_neutrality.tga` `bfa74629618da992c30b95c7e775b2e5cb8b5d45577810e5f5b8f33a15362c15`, `DAH_democratic.tga` `40bbbbf7ce00d167ab96965850fe00adcf9a11a8d09a08b8a6f9f8807d2c6a10`, `DAH_communism.tga` `8f18c34624d85dd4d5b48c7d0bea4d8c83ebf1fb79989f15fcaa76888b91712d`, and `DAH_fascism.tga` `f3c18ee3f5e04de13ad96086fd39c474361b0499e31c4b4aee810308504c0daa`.

The neutrality file is a green-hoist, yellow-over-red tricolour; the democratic file adds a white hoist emblem; the communist file is green with a small red star; and the fascist file is a red, white, and dark central design.

The green/yellow/red layout visually matches the later Republic of Dahomey/Benin flag, not an identified 1936 colonial Dahomey flag. The secondary [Flag of Benin history page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Benin) dates that design’s adoption to 16 November 1959, describes it as the flag of the Republic of Dahomey until 1975, and records re-adoption in 1990. The Commons [Flag of Benin.svg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_Benin.svg) is a public-domain vector, but its file status does not make it a 1936 source.

Vanilla therefore has a usable technical flag family but no source provenance that clears it as IW-095’s 1936 baseline. Reusing it would silently import a 1959-era postcolonial identity into the opening route.

### Historical royal/banner evidence

The Commons [Royal banner of Béhanzin of Dahomey](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Royal_banner_of_B%C3%A9hanzin_of_Dahomey.svg) is a modern vector reconstruction by Samhanin dated 27 September 2019, marked Public Domain, and linked to [Hubert-Herald’s Benin/Dahomey study](http://www.hubert-herald.nl/BeninDahomey.htm). The reconstruction identifies a Béhanzin royal banner for 1889–1892, while the linked study says French forces captured banners at Abomey in 1892 and notes uncertainty over the museum’s colour interpretation and the possibility that kings used distinct banners.

This is usable only as a route-specific dynastic research lead after primary museum confirmation, never as a universal 1936 Dahomey national flag.

The British Library Mechanical Curator scan [FOOTE (1854), “Dahomey, skull ornaments and banners”](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:FOOT(1854)_p098_DAHOMEY,_SKULL_ORNAMENTS_AND_BANNERS.jpg) is a Public Domain 1854 book-page image, 1743×1203, Commons SHA-1 `c603b1a88eb9e865c18fda2af834f2b028837442`, with source links to [British Library catalogue entry](http://explore.bl.uk/BLVU1:LSCOP-ALL:BLL01001265552) and [Flickr scan](https://www.flickr.com/photos/britishlibrary/11043659705). It confirms that banners were documented in 19th-century Dahomey, but it does not establish a single 1936 state flag or ownership for a modern route.
