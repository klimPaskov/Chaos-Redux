## Historical Sakha/Yakut symbol research

The principal secondary transcription consulted was [Flag of the Yakut Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Yakut_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). Its cited official source links are the 1937 Yakut ASSR Constitution at `https://e.nlrs.ru/download/2994`, the 1925 Constitution at `https://e.nlrs.ru/online/3030`, and the All-Yakut Congress records at `https://e.nlrs.ru/online/10774`.

The official library links were retained as source authorities, but the current environment returned HTTP 403 for the downloadable/online scans. No primary scan was therefore archived into the repository, and the exact rights status of a reproduced flag image remains unknown.

The first attested Yakut ASSR flag was established by the 1926 Constitution, approved by the Fourth All-Yakut Congress on 13 February 1926, and introduced by the Yakut Central Executive Committee on 17 September 1926. Article 119 describes a red cloth with a light-blue canton at the hoist, a northern-lights/Aurora Borealis motif, and gold `ЯАССР` letters in the Yakut language. The documented period runs through 9 March 1937, so this is the strongest historically grounded 1936 civic-symbol lead.

The second flag was adopted with the Yakut ASSR Constitution on 9 March 1937. It derives from the RSFSR state flag and uses gold RSFSR/Yakut inscriptions with a smaller Yakut ASSR inscription. It is historically valid after that adoption date, but it is not a neutral indigenous civic symbol for a default 1936 release and should not silently replace the 1926-period treatment.

The 1939 inscription change and the later 1954 and post-Soviet flags are outside the 1936 route window. Modern Sakha blue/white/red/green and solar treatments were not used as period evidence.

No defensible free-standing “pan-Sakha indigenous emblem” was established beyond the constitutionally attested 1926 flag canton. The Aurora motif may be used as a research lead only after the parent approves an alternate-history/civic route and a rights-cleared flat reconstruction workflow. The modern Sakha sun/horse or other later emblems must not be back-projected into 1936 without a separate primary source and route decision.

Wikipedia’s text and any reconstructed SVG on that page are secondary research material, not a runtime asset license. No flag drawing was downloaded, generated, cropped, or processed.
