## Research basis and accepted source direction

### Opening political identity

Use Pyotr Mikhailovich Nikiforov as the primary opening provisional chair candidate.

The biographical basis is his service in the Far Eastern Republic government and as its third Chairman of the Council of Ministers from 8 May 1921 to 2 October 1922, together with his Vladivostok political activity, which makes him a better fit for the package's ordered Vladivostok and Khabarovsk rail-and-port frame than a generic invented commander.

Primary portrait source candidate: [Pyotr Nikiforov, 1922](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pyotr_Nikiforov_1922.jpg), an 800-pixel-tall candidate traced by the current research handoff to the 1932 frontispiece of *Муравьи революции*, with unknown author and a Commons PD-Russia-1996 and United States rationale pending independent rights review.

The current archive already contains the untouched 1922 original, exact source crop, review preview, captured Commons and Russian Wikipedia pages, evidence JSON, provenance text, and SHA-256 values, so the next tranche must consume that evidence rather than launch a duplicate source search.

Reserve identity only: [Alexander Krasnoshchyokov](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:AM_Krasnoshchokov.jpg), the first Far Eastern Republic leader, is historically important but is less well aligned with the package's port-first ordered anchor and must not be produced alongside Nikiforov merely to enlarge the roster.

The reserve may replace the primary only if the portrait worker rejects Nikiforov after independent identity, framing, provenance, or usable-resolution review.

Repository and installed-vanilla collision searches found no existing Nikiforov or Krasnoshchyokov character or portrait ownership in the checked character, country-history, leader-asset, interface, and localisation surfaces.

Event 005 already owns a separate fictional Far Eastern Republic Council and FEV content, so this tranche must preserve origin separation and must not reuse Event 005 names, portraits, tokens, tags, or narrative claims.

### Provisional symbol

The strongest historical continuity candidate is `FER-H0-1920-FLAG`, the 11 November 1920 government design repeated by Article 181 of the 1921 Constitution: a red field, a dark-blue upper-hoist canton, and red `D.V.R.` letters arranged in a triangle.

Its [Commons reconstruction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_Far_Eastern_Republic.svg) is CC BY-SA 3.0 and cites an SHPL scan whose underlying rights are not independently stated, so it cannot become runtime art until the source and rights owner resolves that chain and the parent explicitly accepts a 1936 revival or continuity reading.

`FER-H1-1921-CONST-FLAG`, the [public-domain constitutional-option reconstruction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_the_Far_Eastern_Republic_(Constitutional_option).svg), is a comparison and recovery reference rather than permission to silently substitute new geometry for the stronger historical candidate.

If `FER-H0-1920-FLAG` fails rights review, the parent must either explicitly accept `FER-H1-1921-CONST-FLAG` as a constitutional-text reconstruction or keep the symbol gate closed.

The historical flag's red field and political context are not evidence of a non-socialist neutral symbol, and the research handoff found no directly attested neutral 1936 FER identity.

Do not authorize `FER-SYN-CIVIC-1936` or `FER-SYN-RAIL-PORT-1936` inside this tranche; both are alternate-history design briefs that require a separate parent decision and generated-art task.

The installed vanilla democratic FER flag was visually inspected and is a white-hoist-triangle, red-upper, blue-lower design rather than the constitutional FER standard, so it must not be reused as the Event 006 provisional identity.

Do not create a four-route flag family in this tranche.

### Research limitation

The normal web-search route timed out on three attempts during this planning pass, but the current sibling research handoffs independently archived the relevant Commons, Wikipedia, constitutional, SHPL, and Heraldicum evidence and checksums.

Those handoffs still mark all portrait candidates and all runtime flag candidates fail-closed, so this plan records an implementation choice and gate sequence rather than final rights clearance.
