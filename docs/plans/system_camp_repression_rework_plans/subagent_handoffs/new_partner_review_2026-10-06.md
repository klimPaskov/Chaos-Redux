## Historical assumptions for parent resolution

The broad route dates align with the cited historical periods: Slovakia’s first transport left on 25 March 1942 and about 57,000 people were deported between March and October; Croatia’s two operations occurred in August 1942 and May 1943; Bulgaria’s deportations from Thrace, Macedonia, and Pirot occurred in March 1943; and deportations from Vichy’s unoccupied zone began in August 1942 before German occupation of the zone in November. Vichy now starts on 11 August 1942, matching the first documented Les Milles convoy. The system’s monthly first-day pulse can therefore skip that first partial August window and first report/move in September; this is the parent-accepted cadence abstraction and is tested. [USHMM, Bulgaria](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/bulgaria) documents the occupied territories and the March deportation count; [USHMM, Axis invasion of Yugoslavia](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/axis-invasion-of-yugoslavia) documents Croatia’s two operations; [USHMM, France](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/france) places the first Vichy-zone convoy on 11 August; and [USHMM, Slovakia](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-holocaust-in-slovakia) summarizes the March–October deportations. The 25 March departure date also has direct confirmation from Slovakia’s Institute of National Memory: [25 March 1942, first transport of Slovak Jews](https://www.upn.gov.sk/sk/25-marec-1942-%E2%80%93-1-transport-slovenskych-zidov-do-vyhladzovacieho-tabora/).

Vichy now starts on 11 August 1942, matching the first documented Les Milles convoy. The test sends no order on the August 1 monthly pulse and confirms the report arrives at the next month-start pulse. This can miss the first partial August window; it is the parent-accepted monthly-cadence abstraction, comparable to the accepted Hungary route, rather than an earlier-than-historical transport.

The constants’ cohort shares at `common/script_constants/camp_occ_partner_constants.txt:19,26,33,40` are documented as approximate enrollment shares on finite pre-apportioned origin cohorts, not casualty quotas. Slovakia uses about 57,000 divided by the census count of 88,951. Croatia uses 7,000 divided by the State Department’s 39,000 denominator. Bulgaria uses 11,343 divided by an approximate 13,000 target. The parent records `vic_share = 0.042` as a coarse estimate: about 10,500 handed over divided by the existing 250,000 French population ceiling. That ratio is an apportionment for this route, not a directly sourced state-by-state rate. The Croatian `cro_first_wave_share = 0.7` at `camp_occ_partner_constants.txt:51` is a scheduling abstraction because sources establish two operations and an overall transfer count but not an exact split. Tests establish how that abstraction executes; they do not establish the historical 70/30 ratio. The existing `camp_occ_arrival.violence_request_fraction` also governs arrival deaths and remains a strategic model parameter, not a sourced per-partner arrival-fatality observation.
