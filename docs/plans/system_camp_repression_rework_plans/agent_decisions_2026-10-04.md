## 6. Dated Danish policy timing and rescue to Sweden

Decision: Danish-origin occupation rows are kept out of the generic German campaign until the October 1943 deportation order, and from that date the living Danish cohort at each German-controlled Danish home state escapes to Sweden once, through the exact movement receipt.
Basis: the completion audit and the supplementary sensitivity rejected the 2,859 modeled Danish losses as historically implausible and named the missing pieces as policy timing and an origin-preserving living movement; the accepted design requires historically supported, cause-separated losses and forbids retuning the campaign rate. USHMM: the largely independent Danish government kept Danish Jews out of deportation until October 1943; about 7,200 escaped to Sweden and about 470 were deported, so 0.94 of the living cohort escaped. A rescue alone, without the dated deferral, would still have left about 2,000 deaths from 27 months of campaign before October 1943, so both parts are needed.
Behaviour: `camp_occ_origin_campaign_deferred` (origin `is_core_of = DEN`, before 1943-10-01). `camp_occ_try_danish_rescue` runs in the German monthly pass before the campaign from 1943-10-01, once per home state, moving `camp_occ_rescue.danish_escape_share` of each living Danish-origin row in whole people to Skane (state 138), or to the Swedish capital if Sweden does not hold Skane. The generic `camp_occ_transfer_survivors_exact` gains an escape mode (its first production caller): the refuge must be held by Sweden and outside the registry; one measured debit and credit or a full inverse; the arrival row keeps origin, root and German authority and is retired as released; `camp_occ_total_escaped_k` records the escaped people; one `escape` history entry per home state; no death is recorded. If Sweden is absent, a German subject or faction member, at war with Denmark, or holds neither Skane nor its capital, nothing moves and nothing is recorded. A cohort first exposed after the order escapes in its first exposed month.
Records: the Occupation violence tooltip adds "N thousand people escaped to Sweden, beyond our reach. No death is recorded for them.", and the history list shows "Escape to Sweden recorded" (wording corrected in the round 1 review, entry 11).
Evidence: `occupation/danish_rescue_source_tests.py` runs the production monthly pass July 1941 to April 1945 over the 4,192 allocated people: the pre-change rule reproduces exactly 2,859 deaths; deferral without a refuge leaves 1,589; deferral plus rescue leaves 81 deaths with 3,940 escaped. Ten cases cover once-only movement, receipts, authority/origin/root, Records, the four refusal cases, the capital fallback and a 1944 first exposure.
Limits: `needs_live_game` for the real controller series, Swedish diplomatic status and physical population receipts in a running game.

## 10. Dated Axis-partner deportation from Hungary, 1944

Decision: accept and implement the Hungarian partner route as a dated German-directed deportation from partner-held states, instead of leaving Hungarian cohorts outside the campaign unless Germany occupies them.
Basis: the accepted design calibrates the wider Holocaust from historical circumstances, not quotas; the addendum left this as a proposal needing a route-valid destination and control contract. USHMM: about 825,000 Jews lived in wartime Hungary; about 437,000 were deported, almost all to Auschwitz, between 15 May and 9 July 1944, after Germany took control of the government and until Horthy halted the transports.
Contract: the route runs only in the German monthly pass, after 1944-05-14 and before 1944-07-10, when Germany has a live radical campaign, state 88 has an operational supported killing role under German control, and Hungary exists, is not at war with Germany and is either a German subject or a fascist government in Germany's faction. A Horthy-government Axis member is not enough, as in 1941-1943.
Behaviour: each European Hungarian-held state with an unregistered foreign origin cohort is registered once under German responsibility with `camp_occ_partner_host`, at 0.53 (437/825) of its living origin ceiling after measured losses, bounded by the civilians present. Living people move to state 88 only through `camp_occ_transfer_to_custody_exact` (one measured debit, credit and bind per wave), bounded by supported throughput, resident capacity, source headroom and the monthly wave cap; arrival-phase violence applies to the bound row only. Partner hosts are never a current authority, so they take no campaign violence and cannot feed generic movement, the Danish rescue or the manual order. When the window lapses or Hungary leaves German domination, the host closes and the remainder is released at home. Records name each source state in the monthly deportation history entry.
Surface: `camp_occ_partner.hungary_deportation_share`; triggers `camp_occ_partner_deportation_window_open`, `camp_occ_partner_under_german_influence`, `camp_occ_partner_deportation_candidate`, `camp_occ_state_has_partner_authority`, `camp_occ_state_has_route_authority`; effects `camp_occ_stage_german_origin_request` (shared with ordinary enrollment), `camp_occ_register_partner_state`, `camp_occ_try_partner_deportation`; the partner proof in `camp_occ_register_cohort`, the route authority in `camp_occ_transfer_to_custody_exact`, and the partner exception in the monthly closure loop and control-change callback.
Evidence: `administration_redesign_2026-09-06/occupation/partner_deportation_source_tests.py` (twelve cases on the production monthly pass, May-September 1944): with two supported extermination levels, 235,847 of 235,849 people placed under orders move (150,000 in June, the rest in July), 153,297 arrival-phase deaths are recorded on bound rows, no campaign death is recorded inside Hungary, and every refusal case registers nothing. All other camp source suites still pass.
Limits: `needs_live_game` for real Hungarian state populations, the vanilla Hungarian government and faction path, and engine population receipts. Hungary has no decision of its own in this route; a Hungarian player response (delay or refusal) is future work.
