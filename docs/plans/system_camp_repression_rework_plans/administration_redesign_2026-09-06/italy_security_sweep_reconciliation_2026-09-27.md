# Italy colonial security sweep reconciliation

Disposition: implemented in source for payment, repeat, cutover, outcome and local garrison modifier; native consumer acceptance remains unresolved.
Acceptance basis: the user's requirement for distinct historically grounded Italian mechanics, exact shared quote/debit paths, meaningful consequences and no paid no-op; the accepted country-kit spec lists security battalions as local security units or a garrison modifier in `docs/specs/system_camp_repression_rework_specs/specs/system_camp_repression_rework_spec_part_5_country_decision_kits_focus_hooks.md` line 489.

The pre-edit `ita_raise_colonial_security_battalions` decision charged 20 Political Power natively and its effect spent 15,000 manpower, 650 infantry equipment and 180 support equipment, but strict `>` stock checks blocked the action when the player had exactly the quoted amount. Its decision row also lacked an explicit quote for the three non-Political-Power resources. The Ledger action-4 availability had the same strict comparisons. The native decision's 180-day re-enable period follows the 150-day mission timeout; repeated orders are fresh paid sweeps with renewed resistance/evidence consequences, not free repeats. The pre-edit source is frozen at `ai_evidence/italy_security_decision_before_2026-09-27.txt`, raw SHA-256 `b016b347c6b48caa9bac856e6eb88cec29680e0e9ef2a362a8fe570dd040ecb7`.

The decision, Ledger readiness and final handler now share `camp_rework_italy_security_battalions_resources_ready`, which accepts exact-stock values and rejects one unit short. The decision has a localized requirement plus native PP cost and a three-resource `custom_cost_text` with normal, blocked and hover keys; the cost hover states the native Political Power amount, while the effect tooltip explains the operation and its consequences. Installed vanilla `common/decisions/AUS.txt` lines 1640–1646 combines a native cost with a custom equipment cost, matching this structure. The same-chain prepaid-PP token is set by the native decision or Ledger debit, required by the final handler, refunded if its final route/state/stock guard fails, and cleared after use. The Ledger's 180-day slot cooldown applies only to an accepted handler receipt. Both `camp_admin_initialized` and `camp_admin_civil_initialized` block the legacy Italy Ledger action and final handler, preventing civil cutover from reactivating the retired sweep.
The offline Decision modding wiki describes `custom_cost_text` as an alternative to a native cost, while the installed vanilla Austria decision uses both fields together. The installed source takes precedence for this combination; native rendering of both charges in this exact decision remains unverified.

AI availability now checks the registered `camp_active_site_states` array that its selector actually scans. Decision, selector, selected-site Ledger and final effect all use `camp_rework_italy_security_battalions_site_ready` for a valid responsible, directly controlled, non-core site. This removes a source-level false-positive case where any controlled state could make the AI decision available despite no selectable registered row. It does not prove the array contains every historically eligible state in a running game.
The site predicate also requires a Libyan core. The order's outcome changes `libyan_resistance_pressure`, so an East African or Balkan Italian camp cannot receive a Libya-specific sweep result. Other Italian colonial actions keep their own regional pools. The modifier's state-active trigger and mission completion/cancellation gates preserve that same Libyan scope if ownership or cores change during a sweep.

Before the outcome fix, a successful mission reduced national `camp_policy_resistance_pressure` by five but raised `libyan_resistance_pressure` by two. The Italy bridge then set national pressure back up to the higher local value, erasing the intended relief for equal starting pressures. Success now applies the existing five-point relief to both variables before the retained two-point local consequence and two-point evidence increase, then clamps both pressures nonnegative. At an equal pressure of 12, the source-model success finishes at 9 local and 9 national; at 1 it floors at zero; from 15 national and 10 local it finishes at 10 and 7. Raising the battalions adds two local pressure first, so a complete successful cycle from equal pre-deployment pressure yields net one point of relief, two evidence points and continuing guard demand. A repeat raises guard burden only if battalions were not already active; recalculation keeps one fixed two-point burden instead of a transient repeat stack.

`civil_scenarios.py` executes the production readiness trigger at exact manpower/equipment quotes and one unit short, and executes the production sweep-success effect followed by the Italy bridge's actual local-to-national comparison for the three pressure fixtures above. The full civil, foundation and major-country source suites pass. The scoped new English requirement, cost and modifier keys resolve once with a UTF-8 BOM. These tests use restricted source interpreters and cannot prove the engine's native payment, decision tooltip rendering, garrison condition, mission timing or live pressure values.
The same civil source evaluator accepts a responsible, directly Italian-controlled Libyan non-core site and rejects a non-Libyan state, hostile controller or newly Italian-core state. Its failed-sweep cases leave captured and non-Libyan states physically unharmed while retaining country pressure and evidence consequences. This is a scoped source test of the regional predicate, not proof of the game's changing state cores or controllers.

The selected site now receives `ita_colonial_security_sweep_state`, a timed state dynamic modifier in `common/dynamic_modifiers/camp_repression_italy_security_dynamic_modifiers.txt`. Central `camp_rework_italy_security_modifier` values reduce required garrison by 10%, resistance damage to that garrison by 10%, and the local resistance target by five points while the paid sweep operates. This is materially below the existing CBRN occupation suppression profile and does not change shared resistance or Deaths accounting. Its one active state flag lasts for the 150-day mission plus a seven-day grace period; the modifier has the same maximum duration and an `enable`/`remove_trigger` condition requiring that the original responsible country still directly controls the state. The state begin helper removes an old instance before applying a new one and only accepts the paid order after `has_dynamic_modifier` confirms the local benefit. The end helper removes it idempotently.

Mission success, failure and ordinary cancellation clear the local modifier before the state pointer; civil cutover, expansion freeze, desert closure and site unregister also call the cleanup. The bounded existing state-control callback removes it on detected hostile or subject capture. That callback can be gated during unrelated fallout world rewrites, so the modifier's own direct-control `enable` and `remove_trigger` are the independent capture safety contract. Subject transfer now cancels the mission and ends the local benefit because the paid route requires direct Italian control. The country battalion flag can continue to represent guard commitment after a completed sweep, with evidence and resentment retained.
Mission success also requires the active state flag and exact scoped modifier. A capture followed by rapid reconquest cannot convert a sweep whose local battalions were already removed into a success before mission cancellation is evaluated; the cancellation predicate checks the missing state flag as well.
If the mission fails after Italy loses direct control, the country still receives resistance and evidence consequences, but the state harm helper is gated to a directly Italian-controlled Libyan state with the original Italian responsibility. This prevents a cancelled sweep from debiting civilians or infrastructure in a captured or subject-controlled state. A restricted source scenario exercises both direct-control failure and hostile capture; only the former reaches the site-harm stub.
