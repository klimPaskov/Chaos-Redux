# Event 039 Murder Mystery: Super-Event Text Research Handoff

Research date: 2026-08-29.

Scope: exactly the three accepted super-events in the Event 039 brief: Assassin State public reveal, World of Anarchy activation, and Brotherhood defeat aftermath.

No gameplay or localisation files were edited.

## Recommendation at a glance

- Assassin State public reveal: `Uneasy lies the head that wears a crown.` — William Shakespeare, *Henry IV, Part 2*, Act 3, scene 1, line 31.
- World of Anarchy activation: `The terrorist and the policeman both come from the same basket.` — Joseph Conrad, *The Secret Agent*, Chapter IV, spoken by the Professor.
- Brotherhood defeat aftermath: `Revenge is a kind of wild justice; which the more man's nature runs to, the more ought law to weed it out.` — Francis Bacon, essay `Of Revenge` in the expanded *Essays or Counsels, Civil and Moral*.

All three recommendations are short excerpts from old public-domain works or public-domain-era texts, subject to the project's distribution jurisdiction and the specific electronic edition's terms.

## A. Assassin State public reveal

### Selected main quote

Quote: `Uneasy lies the head that wears a crown.`

Attribution: William Shakespeare, *Henry IV, Part 2*, Act 3, scene 1, line 31, spoken by King Henry IV.

Source: [Folger Shakespeare Library, *Henry IV, Part 2*, Act 3, scene 1](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/henry-iv-part-2/read/3/1/).

Date and edition note: The play was first published in 1600, and the quoted scene is present in the Qb text; the Folger edition also records the 1623 First Folio witness.

Attribution confidence: High for wording, speaker, work, and location.

Rights note: Shakespeare's play is public domain. The underlying line is safe as a short literary quotation; retain the Folger/source edition in the research record rather than implying that the modern webpage itself is the original publication.

Fit: The line turns the movement's territorial revelation into a political irony. A movement born from attacks on leadership has acquired a crown or crown-like office, and the new authority is immediately exposed as vulnerable, watched, and uneasy. It supports the exact role of first public reveal and irreversible political shift without explaining the murderer's origin or invoking any real historical assassin tradition.

### Compared alternatives

- `There’s no art / To find the mind’s construction in the face.` — William Shakespeare, *Macbeth*, Act 1, scene 4, lines 13–15, spoken by King Duncan. [Folger scene text](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/macbeth/read/1/4/). This is a very strong mystery alternative because Duncan says it after trusting a traitor and immediately before Macbeth enters, but it emphasizes unknowable identity more than the revealed state's new political vulnerability. The play is an early-seventeenth-century work first printed in the 1623 First Folio, and the quotation is public domain. Backup rating: high.
- `Oh, what a tangled web we weave, / When first we practise to deceive!` — Sir Walter Scott, *Marmion: A Tale of Flodden Field*, Canto Sixth, stanza XVII, published 1808. [Project Gutenberg text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/4010/4010-h/4010-h.htm). This fits the investigation and the movement's layers of deception, but it is a familiar proverb-like line and is less specific to the anti-leadership movement becoming a state. The poem is public domain, and the linked text preserves the `practise` spelling used by its source edition. Backup rating: medium.

The Shakespeare crown line is recommended over both alternatives because it binds the revelation to power, succession, and the leader's immediate exposure rather than treating the event as only a mystery plot.

### Button text and cultural remark candidates

- Recommended original button reaction: `The empty chair has a claimant.` This is not a quotation or allusion, so it carries no attribution or modern copyright risk. It is restrained, preserves the movement's contradiction, and avoids declaring an uncertain personal origin as fact.
- Backup original button reaction: `A crown is still a target.` This is shorter and sharper, but it is more explicit about assassination and less distinctive than the recommended line.
- Sourced allusion considered but not selected: `The crown sits uneasily.` This is a paraphrase of the selected Shakespeare line, not a direct quotation; it would be safe as an original reaction but would duplicate the `.q` idea without adding much.

## B. World of Anarchy activation

### Selected main quote

Quote: `The terrorist and the policeman both come from the same basket.`

Attribution: Joseph Conrad, *The Secret Agent*, Chapter IV, spoken by the character called the Professor during his argument with Ossipon. This should not be presented as a literal statement made by Conrad himself.

Source: [Project Gutenberg, *The Secret Agent*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/974/974-h/974-h.htm), Chapter IV passage around the line.

Date and edition note: The linked text identifies *The Secret Agent* as first published in September 1907 and supplies the exact wording from its second-edition text.

Attribution confidence: High for the wording, author, work, chapter, and character speaker; high thematic confidence when the line is framed as the Professor's cynical analysis rather than authorial doctrine.

Rights note: Conrad's 1907 novel is public-domain-era material in the United States, and this is an eleven-word excerpt. Project Gutenberg's notice still advises checking the law where the mod is distributed.

Fit: This is the best match for the terminal movement's command contradiction. The surrounding passage explicitly sets revolution and legality against one another as counter-moves in the same game, which mirrors a movement that declares organized leadership to be its target while operating a disciplined state, intelligence network, military hierarchy, and concealed command. The character-speaker caveat is important: the quote describes the fictional movement's hypocrisy and coercive logic, not real anarchism as a whole.

### Compared alternatives

- `A state, is called the coldest of all cold monsters.` — Friedrich Nietzsche, *Thus Spake Zarathustra*, Part I, `The New Idol`, in Thomas Common's English translation. [Project Gutenberg text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm). The original work was composed and published across 1883–1885, while the linked translation is an old public-domain-era version. This is a powerful image of the state as a supposedly anti-authoritarian movement becomes a state-like command apparatus, but it is broader, more translation-dependent, and less directly about the terrorist/policeman contradiction. Backup rating: high if the project wants an overtly philosophical state critique.
- `That government is best which governs not at all;` — Henry David Thoreau, `On the Duty of Civil Disobedience`, 1849. [Project Gutenberg text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/71/71-h/71-h.htm). This is a verified historical anti-government line and is public domain, but it expresses Thoreau's political argument rather than the Event 039 movement's coercive global campaign. Using it as the main quote would risk making a real anti-authoritarian philosophy sound like an endorsement of the fictional movement's violence. Backup rating: medium-low.

The Conrad line is recommended over both alternatives because it exposes the exact hidden-command paradox at the moment the terminal campaign begins. The line's speaker and fictional context must remain documented.

### Button text and cultural remark candidates

- Recommended original button reaction: `Someone still gave the order.` This is cold, skeptical, and directly identifies the contradiction without borrowing a real political slogan.
- Backup original button reaction: `The order outlived the government.` This gives the activation a more terminal tone, but it states a stronger conclusion than the trigger necessarily proves.
- Direct slogan option rejected: `No Gods, No Masters.` Although short and traceable as a real anti-authoritarian slogan, it would import a real anarchist tradition into a fictional murder movement and could flatten the diversity of anarchist thought. It is not recommended for this package.

## C. Brotherhood defeat aftermath

### Selected main quote

The supplied short form is the opening clause of Bacon's first sentence, but the source immediately continues by warning that law should weed revenge out. To preserve that meaning and avoid turning the line into an endorsement of retaliation, use the complete source-faithful sentence:

Quote: `Revenge is a kind of wild justice; which the more man's nature runs to, the more ought law to weed it out.`

Attribution: Francis Bacon, `Of Revenge`, in *The Essays or Counsels, Civil and Moral*.

Source: [Project Gutenberg, Bacon's essays](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/575/575-h/575-h.htm), `Of Revenge`; the [University of Oregon Renascence Editions text](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~rbear/bacon.html) records the essay collection's 1597 first publication and 1625 expanded version.

Date and edition note: `Of Revenge` belongs to the expanded 1625 form of Bacon's essays. The Project Gutenberg transcription preserves the original capitalization and semicolon; the recommendation above normalizes only the initial capitalization for display.

Attribution confidence: High for wording, author, essay, and collection. The source edition is explicit, and the quote is the opening sentence of the essay.

Rights note: Bacon's essay is public domain. If the quote box requires a shorter display, the source-faithful short excerpt should end at the semicolon; do not silently replace the semicolon with a period and present the clipped clause as Bacon's complete sentence.

Fit: This is the strongest aftermath line because the Brotherhood's defeat creates a temptation for the victors to repeat the movement's methods under the name of justice. The full sentence points toward law, restraint, and reconstruction while preserving the bitterness of the violence just ended. It fits the required costly restoration, surviving records, guarded institutions, and continuing suspicion better than a triumphant victory quote.

### Compared alternatives

- `Those who cannot remember the past are condemned to repeat it.` — George Santayana, *The Life of Reason: The Phases of Human Progress*, Volume I, *Reason in Common Sense*, Chapter XII, `Flux and Constancy in Human Nature`, published 1905. [Project Gutenberg text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15000/pg15000-images.html). This is an excellent backup for captured archives, surviving records, and institutional memory, and the linked source gives a clear author and work. It is less specific than Bacon about revenge and the danger of punitive restoration. Backup rating: high.
- `The evil that men do lives after them; / The good is oft interrèd with their bones.` — William Shakespeare, *Julius Caesar*, Act 3, scene 2, lines 82–85, spoken by Antony. [Folger scene text](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/julius-caesar/read/3/2/). The play was first performed around 1599 and first printed in the 1623 First Folio. This directly supports the surviving consequences of assassination and the movement's legacy, but Antony is using the line as funeral rhetoric and political persuasion, so it is less precise about lawful restoration than Bacon. Backup rating: medium-high.

The Bacon sentence is recommended over both alternatives because it makes the aftermath morally unstable rather than merely memorable and directly warns against revenge displacing law.

### Button text and cultural remark candidates

- Recommended original button reaction: `The case is not closed.` This is restrained, fits the murder-mystery identity, and leaves room for surviving cells, records, and unresolved responsibility.
- Backup original button reaction: `Records outlive regimes.` This fits the captured-archives image direction, but it is colder and less immediately tied to the investigation.
- Sourced alternative considered but not selected: `The evil that men do lives after them.` This is a public-domain Shakespeare excerpt, but it duplicates the backup quote's theme and would make the button more declarative than the guarded aftermath role requires.

## Cultural-safety check

The [Institute of Ismaili Studies' specialist note on `Assassin`](https://www.iis.ac.uk/scholarly-contributions/assassin/) explains that the European label began as a hostile medieval designation applied to Nizari Ismailis and that later drug and secret-garden stories are legends without supporting evidence. The package therefore keeps the fictional Assassin State separate from Nizari, Ismaili, Islamic, Alamut, hashish, or `Old Man of the Mountain` imagery and does not use those legends as provenance for the event.

The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's Fall 2024 entry on anarchism](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/anarchism/) describes anarchism as a diverse family of critiques of authority and power that can include liberty, equality, community, non-coercive consensus, pacifism, and many different traditions. The Conrad line is therefore safe only when clearly framed as a fictional character's cynical description of the Event 039 movement's coercive contradiction, never as a definition of real anarchism or a claim that real anarchists share the movement's murders.

## Copyright and attribution risk

No modern song lyric, film dialogue, contemporary book passage, or game dialogue is recommended for the main quote or button text.

The selected and backup quotations come from Shakespeare, Scott, Conrad, Nietzsche's old Common translation, Thoreau, Bacon, and Santayana, all of which are public-domain-era works or translations for the jurisdictions most relevant to the cited repositories. The final project should retain the exact source URL, work, section, edition or translation, and character-speaker caveat where applicable, and should check local distribution law rather than treating a Project Gutenberg notice as universal legal advice.
