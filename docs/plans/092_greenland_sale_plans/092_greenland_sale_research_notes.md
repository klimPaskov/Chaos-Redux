# Greenland Sale: research notes

These primary historical references informed distinctions between acquisition, basing rights, territorial bundles, and the continuing responsibilities of ownership.
They do not establish a real historical auction or a predetermined winner for the fictional event.
The event remains free to occur in an alternate campaign with any valid current owner and buyer.

## H01. Purchase and basing were distinct alternatives

The US Secretary of State's telegram of 2 January 1947 records, through its accompanying note, the 14 December 1946 conversation with the Danish foreign minister.
It describes alternatives involving military facilities, defense arrangements, and outright purchase, with the Danish minister considering the proposals and the United States willing to continue the existing position during consideration.
This supports treating ownership and access as different concepts, not as interchangeable rewards.
It does not support asserting that Greenland was sold or publicly auctioned.

Source: US Department of State, Office of the Historian, FRUS 1947, Volume III, document 414.
The relevant displayed text and note were read at web-rendered lines 2–24.

## H02. A defense arrangement need not convey sovereignty

The telegram of 4 April 1941 summarizes a proposed Greenland defense agreement.
Its described provisions distinguish Danish sovereignty from American use and jurisdiction in defense areas and discuss the welfare and existing administration of Greenland's population.
The document is a description of a proposed arrangement at that date, not evidence that every provision had already entered into force.
The design implication is that purchasing a state should not be described as merely obtaining base access, and military access should not be priced as sovereign ownership unless it actually transfers.

Source: US Department of State, Office of the Historian, FRUS 1941, Volume II, document 35.
The relevant displayed telegram, including the article summaries, was read at lines 2–37.

## H03. A territorial package can include related public assets and records

The convention for the cession of the Danish West Indies was signed on 4 August 1916 and had its ratifications exchanged on 17 January 1917.
The displayed opening and Article I describe a group of islands, related public property, and relevant archives and records.
That supports the design distinction between a coherent territorial package with state-bound assets and a mere abstract transfer token.
It does not provide a price formula for Greenland or justify copying every treaty obligation into a universal auction.

Source: US Department of State, Office of the Historian, FRUS 1917, document 881, Treaty Series 629.
Only the displayed opening and Article I passage, lines 2–26, are used here.
The full 302-line page and the surrounding volume were not fully read in this pass.

## Research boundaries

No modern Greenland political development is used as a campaign rule or as a fixed buyer preference.
No historical price is converted into a universal game price.
No historical quotation, slogan, song, or finished player-facing localisation is supplied.
The research above is background for the fictional design, not a substitute for the user's brief or installed-game evidence.

The live repository and offline-wiki findings are separately recorded in the source and decision register.
Search snippets about native economic capacity did not establish a complete payment interface.
All numerical auction examples are authored design examples, not measurements of historical prices or a running HOI4 campaign.
