## Playable promise review

The event promises that the current world can be permanently rewritten without regard for what each country or state had before.

The final design delivers that promise through:

- independent absolute result generation
- increasing domain breadth through five capability evolutions
- family-specific world moods
- visible map, political, military, and owner-mechanic consequences
- no restoration or compensation
- concise before and after reports
- ordinary recovery through the changed systems
- rare achievements built around adaptation and reversal

The core play is adaptation after a sudden world transformation.

A decision layer would weaken this by giving the player a universal repair store.

## Depth review

The rough idea risked becoming a list of random variables.

The expanded design adds a coherent framework through:

- a clear family taxonomy
- dynamic selection coverage and quotas
- compatibility and dependency bundles
- owner adapters
- protected history and identity
- logical transaction behavior
- direct Chaos compression
- report importance
- AI and multiplayer contracts
- structural candidates at maximum capability
- cluster and catalog integration
- recovery and reversal achievements

This is enough design depth for the event's identity.

Further broad mechanics are not needed.

## Anti-bloat decision

Do not add:

- a Shuffle currency
- a player protection choice
- a restoration mission
- a generic recovery decision category
- a persistent custom GUI
- a super-event for every firing
- a focus tree
- new countries
- a global ranking panel
- a second Event Log
- custom 3D or animation
- an automatic structural randomizer without proof

Those additions would raise maintenance cost without improving the accepted adaptation loop.
