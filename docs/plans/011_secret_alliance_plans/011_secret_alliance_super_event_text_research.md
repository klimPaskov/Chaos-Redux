# Event 011 Secret Alliance - Super-Event Text Research

## Role

- Event ID: 11
- Slug: `secret_alliance`
- Super-event role: first reveal, hidden branch exposure, faction formation, irreversible political shift.
- Trigger context: a hidden Anti-[player country] Pact is exposed when a pact member enters war with the player. The secret pact becomes a visible faction and all members join the war.
- Required tone: betrayal, encirclement, public revelation of a conspiracy, and the realization that earlier isolated incidents were coordinated.
- Avoid: generic apocalypse language, world-ending phrasing, vague "darkness rises" style.

## Title Direction

Recommended title direction: **The Hidden Front**

Why: short, military, and specific to the reveal. It suggests the player was already fighting a second front before it became visible.

Backup title directions:

- **The Pact Unmasked** - most direct, strongest if the UI needs to emphasize the secret pact itself.
- **The Circle Closes** - emphasizes encirclement and the moment all members enter the war.
- **Behind the Curtain** - strong conspiracy tone, but avoid tying it to the Disraeli quote below because that source passage has surrounding racialized content.
- **No Isolated Incidents** - blunt and investigative, useful if the description leans into prior incidents being linked.

These are title directions, not source-verified quotations.

## Description Direction

The description should make the reveal feel like a public intelligence shock rather than a normal faction popup:

- Start with the immediate visible consequence: one hostile declaration has exposed a larger pact.
- Explain that diplomats, newspapers, or intelligence services connect earlier border incidents, sabotage, ultimatums, arms movements, or "unrelated" crises to a shared design.
- Show encirclement: the player is not facing a new enemy, but a prepared coalition that had already agreed on the moment to strike.
- Keep the faction mechanics implicit: do not overexplain "hidden faction becomes visible"; phrase it as the conspiracy being forced into the open.
- Suggested cadence: revelation first, then confirmation that every member has answered the call, then the eerie implication that the war began before anyone admitted it.

Avoid final localisation until wording is written and reviewed in the main implementation pass.

## Main Quote Candidates

### Candidate 1 - Selected

> "For nothing is secret, that shall not be made manifest; neither any thing hid, that shall not be known and come abroad."

- Speaker/author: Gospel according to Luke, King James Version translation.
- Source: Luke 8:17, KJV.
- Period: New Testament text; KJV first published 1611.
- Source URL: https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+8%3A17&version=KJV
- Source notes: Bible Gateway page marks KJV as Public Domain.
- Attribution confidence: high for KJV wording and verse location.
- Copyright/public domain note: KJV is public domain in the cited source's jurisdiction note; check UK Crown rights only if distributing in a context where that matters.
- Fit: best fit for the exact reveal role. It directly covers secrecy becoming public and hidden arrangements being known abroad. It is ominous without becoming apocalyptic.
- Risk: religious cadence may feel more solemn than espionage-driven. Still strong because the event is about exposure, not merely betrayal.

### Candidate 2

> "When sorrows come, they come not single spies, But in battalions"

- Speaker/author: William Shakespeare, spoken by Claudius.
- Source: *Hamlet*, Act 4, Scene 5.
- Year/period: c. 1600-1601.
- Source URL: https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/hamlet/read/
- Attribution confidence: high; Folger text has the passage at lines 2982-2983.
- Copyright/public domain note: Shakespeare text is public domain; Folger's site presentation/edition may have its own terms, so quote the underlying Shakespeare line only.
- Fit: excellent for the "isolated incidents were coordinated" beat. "Single spies" and "battalions" map unusually well to the reveal that scattered signs were actually a war coalition.
- Risk: begins with "sorrows," which may feel more tragic than conspiratorial if used as the main quote.

### Candidate 3

> "All warfare is based on deception."

- Speaker/author: Sun Tzu, translated by Lionel Giles.
- Source: *The Art of War*, chapter 1, section 18 in the Giles translation.
- Year/period: ancient Chinese military text, traditionally 5th century BCE; Giles translation 1910.
- Source URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/132/132-h/132-h.htm
- Attribution confidence: high for the Giles translation wording at Project Gutenberg.
- Copyright/public domain note: Project Gutenberg edition is public domain in the United States.
- Fit: strong military framing for a secret pact whose purpose was concealed until the war trigger.
- Risk: very familiar and generic. It explains doctrine more than it dramatizes the public reveal.

### Candidate 5

> "So you see, my dear Coningsby, that the world is governed by very different personages from what is imagined by those who are not behind the scenes."

- Speaker/author: Benjamin Disraeli.
- Source: *Coningsby; or, The New Generation*.

## Selected Button / Remark Recommendation

Recommended sourced remark/button direction:

Confidence: high for source and wording.

Why: It is short, public-domain, source-verified, and directly evokes the realization that separate threats were not separate at all. It avoids generic apocalypse wording and avoids modern copyright risk.

Backup remark options:

2. "Three kept the secret." - good sardonic allusion, but less immediately military.
3. "The masks are off." - clean original direction, not source-verified as a quotation.

## Copyright and Attribution Notes

- The selected main quote uses KJV wording from a source that marks KJV Public Domain.
- Shakespeare and Franklin candidates are public-domain underlying texts, but site-specific editorial presentations may carry separate terms. Quote only the underlying text and cite the source URL in docs.
- Sun Tzu candidate uses Lionel Giles's 1910 translation from Project Gutenberg, public domain in the United States.
- Avoid modern copyrighted or trademark-associated pop-culture slogans as final button text. If a modern reference is used, keep it very short and document it as a reference, not as public-domain text.
- Avoid the Disraeli candidate despite textual fit because the surrounding passage has racialized/antisemitic context that could create unwanted meaning.

## Research-Gated Warnings

- Final localisation remains gated on the main agent writing the title, description, quote formatting, and button text in the repository's active localisation style.
- If the super-event image or audio direction later becomes explicitly religious, Shakespearean, espionage-noir, or propaganda-bureaucratic, re-check the chosen quote/remark for tonal alignment.
- If any pact members are scripted to be former allies, puppets, guaranteed countries, or named ideological partners, the title and button can lean harder into betrayal. If they are mostly unknown rivals, lean harder into encirclement and public revelation.
- Do not use Disraeli as a headline or main quote without a deliberate review of surrounding context.

## Implementation Recommendation

Use **The Hidden Front** as the working title direction, the Luke 8:17 KJV quote as the selected main quote, and **"In battalions."** as the sourced button/remark direction. Write the description around a public intelligence revelation: the first open attack exposes a prepared coalition, and prior incidents are reinterpreted as coordinated steps toward the same war.
