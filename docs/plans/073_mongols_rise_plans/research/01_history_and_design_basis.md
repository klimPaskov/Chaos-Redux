# Historical research and authored design

## Boundary

The original attached brief supplies the restoration premise and desired mechanics. The expanded routes, obligations, military advantages, political choices, and tuning anchors are authored game design. They are not claims that a twentieth-century cavalry empire could historically defeat modern industrial armies.

The small research set below supports selected historical identities and visual directions. It does not establish final character genealogy, portraits, quotations, audio rights, or exact game geography. Those remain separate production research gates.

## H01: mounted organization and command

Columbia University's Asia for Educators, Mongols in World History, “Chinggis's Mastery of Organization and Military Tactics.” Accessed 20 September 2026.

Source URL: `https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/mongols/conquests/conquests_2b.htm`

The source describes decimal military organization and personal relationships between senior commanders and Chinggis Khan. It also describes feigned withdrawal. This supports using tumen organization, command obligations, and mobile doctrine as design themes. The source does not specify Hearts of Iron IV division sizes, game modifiers, modern cavalry performance, or a recoverable exact legal system.

## H02: succession and regional division

Columbia University's Asia for Educators, Mongols in World History, “The Collapse of the Empire.” Accessed 20 September 2026.

Source URL: `https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/mongols/conquests/conquests_4.htm`

The source connects succession and leadership disputes with division and describes major regional sectors including China, Central Asia, the Ilkhanids, and the Golden Horde. This supports the broad choice of regional settlements and the importance of succession. The game's exact four-package arrangement, crisis stages, and twentieth-century borders are authored design, not a direct historical reconstruction.

## H03: transregional travel and trade

Columbia University's Asia for Educators, Mongols in World History, “Pax Mongolica.” Accessed 20 September 2026.

Source URL: `https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/mongols/history/history5.htm`

The source discusses protected travel and interstate relations under Mongol rule. This supports a connected-route economic theme. It does not support claiming that the period was free of conflict or that all routes were uniformly safe. Railways, game supply sections, delivery conditions, and the proposed economic spirit are twentieth-century gameplay inventions.

## H04: Karakorum and its landscape

UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “Orkhon Valley Cultural Landscape.” Accessed 20 September 2026.

Source URL: `https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1081/`

The listing identifies Kharkhorum within the Orkhon Valley cultural landscape and describes its role as an imperial capital. This supports the historical importance of the restoration site. It does not identify a Hearts of Iron IV state or province, authorize a game capital effect, or prove the site can be represented precisely without map work.

## H05: Ilkhanid regional identity

The Metropolitan Museum of Art, “The Art of the Ilkhanid Period (1256–1353),” Linda Komaroff and Suzan Yalman, published 1 October 2001. Accessed 20 September 2026.

Source URL: `https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-art-of-the-ilkhanid-period-1256-1353`

The essay provides a regional and cultural account of Ilkhanid rule and its exchange across imperial connections. This supports a distinct Persian regional administration and visual identity. It does not establish the game's modern border set, final religious-policy mechanics, character candidates, or rights to reuse every image on the page.
