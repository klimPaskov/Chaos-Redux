# Mongols Rise

## Catalog entry

- Event ID: `73`
- Event name: Mongols Rise
- Type: Minor Fire-Once
- Status: To Be Reworked
- Chaos level: 1
- Cluster: Formables
- Cluster role: Low member

## Core idea

> Mongolia revives the empire of the Great Khans and raises a huge cavalry horde almost immediately.

Event 73 turns Mongolia into a fast, aggressive restoration campaign centered on Genghis Khan, cavalry warfare, rapid conquest, revived khanates, tribute, and a large dedicated focus tree.

The defining military rule is simple:

**Mongolia uses cavalry as its normal land army.**

The empire is extremely dangerous while attacking and expanding, but much weaker when forced to defend static fronts.

## Availability

Event 73 requires a valid Mongolian country to exist.

A reduced, puppeted, or weakened Mongolia can still become the starting point of the restoration if it can validly receive the event.

If an equivalent Mongol Empire already exists, the event is unavailable.

## Immediate rise of the cavalry horde

When Event 73 fires, Mongolia almost immediately receives a **large cavalry army**.

The opening package includes:

- many cavalry divisions
- large infantry-equipment stockpiles
- support equipment
- artillery and cavalry-compatible support
- manpower
- Army Experience
- strong cavalry commanders
- supply and logistical support for rapid campaigning

The army does not become a mixed conventional force.

Mongolia should not be handed normal infantry, motorized, mechanized, or armored divisions as its defining army.

The opening cavalry army scales strongly with the active Evolution.

### Baseline

Mongolia receives a large cavalry horde strong enough to begin attacking weaker neighbors almost immediately.

### Evolution I

The opening horde becomes substantially larger and gains stronger cavalry bonuses, larger reserves, better artillery support, and stronger commanders.

### Evolution II

Mongolia begins with a very large regional cavalry army capable of fighting much larger conventional armies through speed, breakthrough, and encirclement.

### Evolution III

The opening horde becomes enormous and can launch several rapid campaigns at once.

## Offensive strength and defensive weakness

Mongolian cavalry is built around momentum.

Its strongest bonuses favor:

- attack
- breakthrough
- speed
- organization while advancing
- reconnaissance
- encirclement
- reinforcement
- recovery
- lower supply use while moving
- combat in plains, steppe, desert, hills, and cold terrain

Its defensive profile is deliberately weak.

Possible weaknesses include:

- low entrenchment
- weaker static defense
- poor performance in long defensive battles
- reduced effectiveness in dense urban and heavily fortified terrain
- greater vulnerability when supply lines are cut
- difficulty replacing losses after momentum collapses

The player should be encouraged to keep moving.

A successful Mongolia can expand extremely quickly.

A Mongolia that loses initiative, overextends, or gets pinned into static fronts can crumble just as quickly.

## Rapid conquest opening

The early focus tree unlocks conquest almost immediately.

The first route should be:

**Restoration proclaimed -> Great Khan authority -> immediate expansion choices**

Mongolia should not need years of industry or ceremonial focuses before being allowed to fight.

Early routes can quickly target:

- neighboring Mongol territories
- northern China
- Central Asia
- Siberian approaches
- weaker nearby states
- tribute demands against vulnerable countries

## Momentum of conquest

Successful wars feed the horde.

Conquest can provide:

- captured equipment
- manpower
- additional cavalry divisions
- Army Experience
- stronger commanders
- tribute
- temporary supply support
- shortened follow-up conquest focuses

This creates the intended snowball.

Mongolia expands quickly because victory creates the resources needed for the next invasion.

If the advance stalls, defensive weakness and overstretched logistics become dangerous.

## The Great Khan

The restoration revolves around the need for a **Great Khan**.

### Heir of Genghis

The most traditional restoration route.

It emphasizes:

- the title Great Khan
- the Yassa
- military merit
- tumen organization
- tribute
- conquest
- restoration of Karakorum
- submission of defeated peoples

### The New Great Khan

A twentieth-century imperial interpretation.

The state modernizes logistics, artillery support, communications, aircraft support, and administration while keeping cavalry as the core army.

It does not replace the cavalry horde with tanks or mechanized divisions.

### Council of Khans

The empire becomes a looser confederation of revived khanates.

Regional khans gain wide autonomy while accepting the authority of the Great Khan.

## Restoration of Karakorum

Karakorum becomes a central symbol of the restoration.

It can be rebuilt as an imperial capital and military center through:

- infrastructure
- industry
- military facilities
- political legitimacy
- command bonuses
- special events
- major visual treatment

A late route can permanently move the imperial capital there.

## The Tumen system

The restored army revives the **tumen** as its main military identity.

Possible cavalry families include:

- standard Mongol cavalry
- elite tumen cavalry
- heavy cavalry
- horse-archer style formations represented through suitable modifiers
- guard cavalry
- raider cavalry
- the Great Khan's personal cavalry

The differences come from templates, support, traits, and modifiers while keeping the army cavalry-based.

## Imperial expansion

The focus tree opens several historical directions.

### Unite the Mongol peoples

Secure the immediate Mongolian and related territories.

### Northern China

Conquer Chinese industry and manpower.

### The Central Asian Khanates

Push westward and create subordinate khanates.

### The Rus

Recreate Mongol domination across the Russian steppe and potentially restore a Golden Horde-style subject.

### Persia and the Middle East

Recreate Ilkhanate-style expansion and fight for control of Persia and the surrounding regions.

### The Great Western Campaign

Open routes toward Eastern Europe, the Balkans, Central Europe, and other areas historically reached or threatened by Mongol campaigns.

### The Southern Campaigns

Expand toward India, Southeast Asia, Korea, and the rest of China.

The player should not be forced into one fixed conquest order.

## Revived khanates

Large conquered regions can become specialized imperial subjects inspired by:

- the Golden Horde
- the Chagatai Khanate
- the Ilkhanate
- Yuan-style administration in China
- other regional Mongol successor structures

The player can choose between direct rule and powerful subordinate khanates.

## Tribute

Countries can sometimes avoid immediate invasion by accepting tributary status.

Tributaries can provide:

- equipment
- resources
- manpower support
- military access
- divisions
- periodic tribute

Refusing tribute can unlock a rapid conquest route.

## The Silk Roads

A successful empire can revive a transcontinental trade network associated with the Pax Mongolica.

Connected imperial territory can provide:

- infrastructure
- railways
- trade
- resources
- construction
- supply
- economic bonuses

This creates an economic payoff for maintaining a continuous empire.

## Evolution I: The Yassa Restored

- Chaos requirement: `200+`.

The opening cavalry army becomes significantly larger.

The Great Khan formally restores the Yassa and gains deeper content around:

- tumens
- khan appointments
- tribute
- imperial law
- discipline
- succession

Cavalry receives stronger offensive bonuses and the empire can expand more quickly.

## Evolution II: The Khanates Return

- Chaos requirement: `400+`.

The opening horde becomes a major regional invasion army.

Mongolia gains much broader access to revived khanates.

These can provide armies, tribute, regional conquest, and political competition.

The empire becomes much stronger while advancing, but powerful khans can become dangerous if central authority weakens.

## Evolution III: Universal Khan

- Chaos requirement: `600+`.

The opening horde becomes enormous.

The Great Khan can claim universal rule and unlock:

- broader submission ultimatums
- rapid war goals
- stronger khanates
- enormous cavalry reinforcement
- much faster continental campaigns
- universal tributary demands

The cavalry army reaches its strongest offensive form.

Its defensive weakness remains.

A Mongolia that keeps winning can become a continental empire very quickly.

A Mongolia that is stopped, encircled, or forced into prolonged defensive warfare can disintegrate.

## Imperial succession

The restored empire has a real succession problem.

When a Great Khan dies, major khans, generals, heirs, and regional rulers can compete through:

- a kurultai
- peaceful succession
- competing claimants
- temporary division
- khanate independence
- civil war

A strong empire can survive succession.

A weak or overextended one can fragment.

## Collapse of the empire

The empire should be capable of spectacular collapse.

Important causes include:

- loss of Mongolia
- death of a weakly secured Great Khan
- loss of offensive momentum
- major military catastrophe
- powerful rebellious khans
- overstretched supply
- simultaneous revolts
- being forced into prolonged defensive warfare

The resulting successor states should remain meaningful countries rather than everything reverting instantly to pre-event borders.

## AI direction

AI Mongolia should prioritize:

1. attacking quickly
2. maintaining cavalry momentum
3. securing nearby territory
4. obtaining Chinese industry and manpower
5. expanding into Central Asia
6. using khanates when direct occupation becomes inefficient
7. avoiding static defensive wars whenever possible

AI Mongolia should become increasingly aggressive when it is winning and noticeably more vulnerable when its expansion is stopped.
