# Event 31 Random Terror super-event text research

## Scope and conclusion

This note researches the text package for the two Event 31 super-events: the first reveal of The False Revelation and the defeat aftermath.

The recommendations follow the Event 31 brief and the [Part 8](../specs/031_random_terror_specs/031_random_terror_spec_part_8_false_revelation_world_end.md), [Part 11](../specs/031_random_terror_specs/031_random_terror_spec_part_11_assets_presentation_and_localisation.md), and [Part 13](../specs/031_random_terror_specs/031_random_terror_spec_part_13_documentation_catalog_and_acceptance.md) contracts.

The entity remains unidentified, and the movement's claim that it is divine is presented as a claim made by extremists rather than as game-authoritative truth.

No Qur'anic material, sacred Islamic formula, call to prayer, sacred chant, real extremist symbol, or real extremist propaganda is used in the selected text.

Text research is complete with no text-specific blocker; audio, image, slot, event, and localisation implementation remain outside this handoff.

## Reveal: title recommendation

### Selected title: The False Revelation

This is the strongest title because it is concise, specific to the movement's final claim, and compatible with the accepted public title recorded in Part 13.

It labels the revelation or claim as false without identifying what the entity is, whether it exists independently, or whether the observed effects have a supernatural cause.

### Considered title candidates

| Candidate | Decision | Fit assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The False Revelation** | Selected. | Names the disputed claim, preserves entity ambiguity, and matches the public catalog contract. |
| **The Last Claim** | Backup. | Captures terminal escalation but is less distinctive and does not communicate the deception as clearly. |
| **The Claim** | Rejected. | Too abstract for a terminal world-end announcement. |
| **The False Prophet** | Rejected. | Shifts the focus from the ambiguous entity to a human prophet and narrows the religious representation in an undesirable way. |
| **The Unveiling** | Rejected. | Generic and implies that the event has revealed a settled truth. |
| **The Empty Heaven** | Rejected. | Overcommits to a metaphysical conclusion and risks religiously loaded hostile branding. |

## Reveal: description direction

The description should foreground observable effects, divided extremist leadership, synchronized uprisings, Muslim and civic opposition, and uncertainty about the presence.

The following is an original draft direction, not a historical quotation and not a replacement for the parent's final dynamic localisation:

> At the movement's command hall, observers report an impossible presence: light without a source, shadows that do not agree, and orders transmitted before anyone admits to giving them. Some extremist commanders have proclaimed the arrival divine; others have denied it, fled, or taken up arms against the claim. Across the world, cells rise in concert while governments, civic leaders, and Muslim religious authorities reject the revelation and try to protect those trapped beneath it. Whether apparition, weapon, fraud, or something no instrument can explain, the appearance has turned the war into a final campaign.

The parent may substitute the real command location in the first sentence when available.

The description must not expose Apocalyptic Readiness, exact eligibility values, hidden transition logic, or a canonical entity identity.

## Defeat aftermath: title recommendation

### Selected title: The Broken Claim

This title identifies the collapse of the movement's divine assertion and command structure without claiming that the entity was permanently destroyed or that its nature has been explained.

It is shorter and more specific than a generic victory title, while leaving room for scattered cells, disputed evidence, and a presence that may have withdrawn rather than died.

### Considered title candidates

| Candidate | Decision | Fit assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Broken Claim** | Selected. | Connects liberation to the collapse of the false authority and keeps the entity's status unresolved. |
| **The Empty Throne** | Backup. | Strong visual language for a fallen command, but it leans more heavily into monarchic and metaphysical imagery. |
| **The Silence After** | Backup. | Mournful and restrained, but too generic to identify the aftermath as the defeat of the false claim. |
| **After the Revelation** | Rejected. | Does not communicate defeat and can sound like the revelation was true. |
| **The Revelation Ends** | Rejected. | Implies permanent finality and conflicts with the uncertain absence and surviving cells. |
| **Humanity Prevails** | Rejected. | Generic triumphal language and inconsistent with the required cost, reconstruction, and vigilance. |

## Defeat aftermath: description direction

The description should show the fall of command centers, liberation, prisoners and displaced civilians, reconstruction, splinter cells, uncertainty, and obligations after victory.

The following is an original draft direction, not a historical quotation and not a replacement for the parent's final dynamic localisation:

> Command centers have fallen, and the roads out of them are filling with prisoners, displaced families, and survivors. Relief trains move through ruined districts as governments and communities reopen hospitals, stations, and public life; the remaining cells have scattered rather than surrendered. The presence is gone—or has merely withdrawn—and no witness can agree whether it was a being, a weapon, a fraud, or a terror shared by millions. Liberation is not safety: the dead must be remembered, the living protected, and the work of rebuilding has only begun.

This direction avoids a clean triumphant reset and gives the parent space to add a verified dynamic location or coalition context without exposing implementation-only variables.

## Copyright, translation, and sensitivity summary

The selected Shakespeare, Santayana, and Binyon wording is short, directly sourced, and based on public-domain underlying works.

The selected button lines are short enough for a super-event button and do not require a copyrighted recording, performance, translation, or adaptation.

The Project Gutenberg and Folger pages are source/transcription pages; their site layouts, annotations, and editorial matter are not being copied into the mod.

The King James Version candidate is not selected because public-domain status is jurisdiction-dependent in the United Kingdom and because the scriptural framing is less neutral for this fictional movement.

The Andersen candidate is not selected because an English translation can have rights independent of the public-domain story, and its comic exposure scene would weaken the terminal tone.

Faulkner and Baldwin remain copyright-risk alternatives only; neither is needed for the recommended package.

The package does not depict or confirm Allah, use sacred Islamic material as hostile branding, or equate Islam, Muslims, ethnicity, or nationality with the fictional extremist movement.

## Implementation recommendation for the main agent

Use **The False Revelation** / **The Broken Claim** as the final titles unless a later parent-level review changes the role.

Use the original description drafts as the writing direction, adding only verified dynamic context such as the command location.

Use the exact selected quote wording and the exact selected button wording; attribute Shakespeare and Santayana separately in the quote display according to the existing super-event convention.

Keep the reveal line as “glisters,” not the modernised “glitters,” if it is presented as a direct Shakespeare quotation.

Do not attach the reveal quote to a claim that the entity is supernatural, and do not let the defeat description claim permanent destruction when the implementation permits disappearance, withdrawal, or fragmentation.

No super-event slot, sound ID, image path, event ID, or localisation file is proposed by this text-only handoff.
