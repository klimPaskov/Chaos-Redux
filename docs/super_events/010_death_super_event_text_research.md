## 1. Mainland reveal

### Selected package

- Final title: `His Name Was Death`
- Title source: Revelation 6:8, King James Version, 1611
- Button / cultural remark: `For the living there is hope.`
- Button source: Ecclesiastes 9:4, King James Version, 1611
- Main quote: `Men fear death, as children fear to go in the dark.`
- Quote source: Francis Bacon, `Of Death`, in *The Essays or Counsels, Civil and Moral*, 1625
- Attribution confidence: High
- Copyright note: Public domain

### Why this fits

This role is the first public naming of the crisis. `His Name Was Death` directly solves the brief's naming problem without sounding like the final apocalypse. The Ecclesiastes button keeps the moment from sounding terminal. Bacon's line gives the reveal a cold public psychology: fear increases once rumor turns into official knowledge.

## 2. World-end

### Selected package

- Final title: `No More Life`
- Title source: Original event-title wording, not a direct quotation
- Button / cultural remark: `The sea gave up the dead which were in it.`
- Button source: Revelation 20:13, King James Version, 1611
- Main quote: `And we are here as on a darkling plain, / Swept with confused alarms of struggle and flight.`
- Quote source: Matthew Arnold, `Dover Beach`, first published 1867
- Attribution confidence: High
- Copyright note: Public domain

### Why this fits

The brief calls for a terminal, oceanic, global turn. `No More Life` is short, final, and broader than the shore imagery alone. The button keeps the maritime spread explicit. Arnold's lines fit the collapse of borders and order better than a purely apocalyptic scripture package; they read like governments and armies losing any stable world to defend.

## 3. Defeat aftermath

### Selected package

- Final title: `The Unfinished Work`
- Title source: Abraham Lincoln, Gettysburg Address, 1863
- Button / cultural remark: `We will remember them.`
- Button source: Laurence Binyon, `For the Fallen`, 1914
- Main quote: `It is for us the living, rather, to be dedicated here to the unfinished work.`
- Quote source: Abraham Lincoln, Gettysburg Address, 1863
- Attribution confidence: High
- Copyright note: Public domain

### Why this fits

This outcome is victory without restoration. `The Unfinished Work` is better than a triumph title because it frames defeat of Death as the start of memorial and reconstruction. Binyon's line is concise and culturally legible. Lincoln's sentence keeps the focus on the surviving political task rather than celebration.

## 4. Whole world consumed

### Selected package

- Final title: `There Was No Man`
- Title source: Jeremiah 4:25, King James Version, 1611
- Button / cultural remark: `The rest is silence.`
- Button source: William Shakespeare, *Hamlet* V.2, c. 1600
- Main quote: `Creation sleeps. 'Tis as the general pulse / Of life stood still, and nature made a pause.`
- Quote source: Edward Young, *Night Thoughts*, 1742-1745
- Attribution confidence: High
- Copyright note: Public domain

### Why this fits

This is the strongest package for the absolute terminal state. The title directly states the absence of witnesses. The Shakespeare line works as the last button because it is short, culturally legible, and entirely final. Young's lines are the best match I found for the brief's `no audience, no record keeper` end-state: not merely death, but the stoppage of life's pulse itself.
