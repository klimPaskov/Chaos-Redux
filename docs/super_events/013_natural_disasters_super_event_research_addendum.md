## 1. First abnormal disaster age reveal

### Threshold and anti-duplication decision

This role merits a super-event because it announces a ruleset change. Evolution III opens abnormal meteor impacts, whole-earth rupture, massive volcanic crisis, delayed tsunami trains, and moving storm corridors; it is explicitly severe but non-terminal. A one-time reveal gives the player a clear campaign boundary before the family-specific milestones begin.

Recommended trigger interpretation:

1. Prefer the moment Evolution III abnormal access becomes operational, not the first impact animation or first death tick.
2. If the campaign starts or Event 013 first enters with Evolution III already active, use the first abnormal chain commitment as the fallback reveal moment.
3. Set once-only state for the era reveal.
4. If the fallback reveal and a family-specific super-event qualify in the same Event 013 sequence, show the era reveal and defer/suppress the family-specific presentation for that sequence. Two super-events for one first abnormal impact would dilute both.
5. Do not use this role for Disaster Barrage merely opening the abnormal pool unless the manual scenario is intentionally allowed to reveal Evolution III for the campaign. The separate barrage candidate remains a different design question.

### Selected main-quote candidate

> “Old stories of occurrences handed down by tradition, but scantily confirmed by experience, suddenly ceased to be incredible.”

- **Author/work:** Thucydides, *History of the Peloponnesian War*, Book I, chapter 23.
- **Translation:** Richard Crawley.
- **Exact wording source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/7142/pg7142-images.html
- **Catalog/translator record:** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/7142
- **Independent text record:** https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0200%3Abook%3D1%3Achapter%3D23
- **Wording verification:** the selected sentence opening appears verbatim in the linked Crawley text. The same sentence continues with earthquakes, eclipses, droughts, famines, and plague, so the excerpt's many-calamities context is direct rather than inferred from an unrelated line.
- **Context boundary:** Thucydides is describing the exceptional sufferings surrounding a human war. He is not claiming that nature entered a literal new geological age. Using the excerpt for Event 013 is a literary design inference.
- **Rights:** Thucydides' Greek text is ancient. Crawley lived 1840-1893; the translation and the 1914 edition used by Perseus are public domain. Project Gutenberg lists the ebook as public domain in the United States. Copyright confidence: **high**.
- **Fit:** the sentence is about previously implausible reports becoming credible and is followed by several kinds of catastrophe. It suits the first reveal better than a quote about any one earthquake, storm, or eruption.

### Fiction boundary

The “abnormal disaster age” is an Event 013 campaign construct, not a scientific category. Thucydides' catalogue is cultural evidence for how a society can narrate clustered calamities; it is not evidence that meteor impacts, global ruptures, eruptions, and moving storm corridors naturally synchronize. Final prose should describe what governments and witnesses now regard as possible and what the player can observe. It should not claim a newly discovered law of geology, meteorology, or astronomy.

## 2. Delayed tsunami chain

### Threshold and anti-spam decision

This role merits a super-event only for the matrix's **large delayed chain across several separated coasts**. That accepted case has a distinct narrative structure: the initiating rupture, volcanic collapse, or ocean impact has already happened; the ocean carries an initially hard-to-see threat; warning and evacuation decisions occur; then a second major phase reaches multiple coasts. It is not just a second picture for the initiating super-event.

Recommended trigger interpretation:

1. Require a chain that targets several separated coasts or more than one ocean-facing region and is capable of an independently major death, port, or infrastructure outcome.
2. Fire once per chain, not once per coast or wave.
3. Prefer the first confirmed/committed major arrival phase, when at least one populated harbor shows withdrawal or impact confirmation and other target coasts remain pending. This matches the accepted empty-harbor image without revealing the chain before it is real.
4. Do not fire if warning/prevention work cancels the major chain before confirmation. Successful prevention remains meaningful without a super-event.
5. Ordinary Evolution II tsunamis, one-coast events, minor follow-ups, and repeat waves stay in the report, news-throttling, aftermath-card, and GUI lanes.

This gate preserves the first research pass's anti-spam concern while retaining the accepted Part 6 role.

### Scientific basis and fiction boundary

The U.S. Tsunami Warning Centers state that a tsunami is a series of long-period waves caused by water displacement. The same source identifies earthquakes, landslides, volcanic eruptions, and outer-space impacts as possible causes; explains that deep-ocean waves can cross the Pacific in less than a day; and warns that the first wave may not be the largest.

- Physics and cause source: https://www.tsunami.gov/php/tsunami_science.php
- NOAA public transcript on hours-long wave series, first-wave uncertainty, distant coast effects, and visible coastal withdrawal: https://oceantoday.noaa.gov/tsunamiawareness/
- NOAA natural-warning direction for unusual water recession: https://tsunami.coast.noaa.gov/

Important fiction boundary: the Warning Centers also state that non-seismic tsunami mechanisms usually dissipate quickly and rarely affect coastlines far from the source. A basin-wide multi-coast train from a volcanic collapse or meteor impact is therefore an Evolution III amplification, not a routine scientific consequence. A very large earthquake source is the better real-world analogue for transoceanic reach.

The spec's hours-to-four-days gameplay window should represent staggered coast arrivals, imperfect confirmation, player warning time, and aftermath reporting. It should not claim that a physical wave pauses for several days before crossing an ocean; NOAA gives under one day as a Pacific-crossing example, while dangerous waves and currents may continue for hours or days after first arrival.

- **Fit:** it turns the visual softness of a withdrawn or distant sea into the force that attacks hard ports, rails, and buildings. It suits anticipation and impact without confusing tsunami with a wind wave.
