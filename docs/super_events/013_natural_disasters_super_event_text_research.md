## Research method

The package distinguishes three levels of confidence:

- **Verified fact** means the claim is supported directly by an identified scientific or historical source.
- **Textual context** means the quotation, speaker, work, and surrounding passage were checked against the linked text.
- **Design inference** means the connection between that source and the fictional Event 013 escalation is a creative recommendation. It is not presented as science or history.

Working labels from the specification are not treated as sourced titles. A label becomes a title direction below only when its wording is traceable to an identified work or is explicitly marked as a compression.

## 1. Whole-earth rupture

### Recommendation

Keep this as the rarest tectonic super-event. It should represent the accepted fictional threshold in which connected ruptures and cascading failures become a global condition. It should not fire for a severe regional earthquake alone.

### Scientific basis and boundary

**Verified fact:** The United States Geological Survey identifies ground shaking, surface faulting, ground failure, and, less commonly, tsunami as earthquake effects. It describes surface rupture as a long, narrow zone and gives historical United States rupture lengths ranging from less than one mile to more than 200 miles.

Source: [USGS, What are the Effects of Earthquakes?](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/what-are-effects-earthquakes)

**Design inference:** A rupture spanning the whole earth is not a scientifically documented extension of those examples. It is an intentionally impossible Chaos Redux escalation. Final prose should describe what populations witness and endure rather than claim that ordinary earthquake science predicts a planet-wide crack.

### Recommended title direction

**The Earth Is Torn Apart**

This is an exact fragment from Isaiah 24:19 in the Classic World English Bible. It is source-backed and avoids the unsourced working label “Great Rupture.”

### Verified quote record

> “The earth is utterly broken. The earth is torn apart.”

- **Author and work:** Book of Isaiah 24:19. Traditional attribution is to Isaiah. The book's composition history is ancient, layered, and disputed.
- **Date:** Ancient composition. A single secure composition date should not be asserted. The linked wording is from the modern Classic World English Bible translation.
- **Context:** The passage describes the foundations of the earth trembling, the earth breaking apart, and the earth shaking violently. It is religious and apocalyptic language, not a geophysical report.
- **Exact wording verification:** [Classic World English Bible, Isaiah 24](https://ebible.org/eng-web/ISA24.htm)
- **Rights:** The World English Bible is explicitly dedicated to the public domain. [WEB public-domain notice](https://ebible.org/engwebp/copyright.htm)
- **Use note:** High confidence for quotation use. Preserve the two-sentence wording and do not attach a scientific attribution to it.

### Cultural remark and button direction

**No soil offers escape.**

This is a new paraphrase, not a quotation. It is derived from Seneca's earthquake discussion in *Natural Questions*, Book VI, Chapter I. Seneca argues that changing ground offers no escape because the whole earth is subject to movement.

- **Author and work:** Lucius Annaeus Seneca, *Natural Questions*, Book VI.
- **Date:** Written in the first century CE, commonly placed near the end of Seneca's life.
- **Context source:** [Project Gutenberg, Physical Science in the Time of Nero](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/76392/76392-h/76392-h.htm)
- **Edition details:** Translated by John Clarke, published in 1910.
- **Rights:** Project Gutenberg marks the ebook as public domain in the United States. The proposed button is original paraphrase, which further reduces dependence on edition wording.

The direction is austere and fatalistic without declaring a world-end state. It fits the specification's abnormal disaster threshold while leaving gameplay after the event.

## 2. Massive eruption

### Recommendation

Keep this as a super-event only for an eruption that reaches the specification's exceptional abnormal threshold. The event should communicate ash, darkness, evacuation, and cascading regional damage rather than use a generic explosion register.

### Scientific basis and boundary

**Verified fact:** The United States Geological Survey lists ashfall downwind and lahars in river valleys among major volcanic hazards. Its ashfall guidance describes ash as the most widespread and frequent volcanic hazard and notes that it can affect communities and farmland across hundreds or thousands of square kilometres.

Sources:

- [USGS, Learn About Volcano Hazard Zones](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/VHP/learn-about-volcano-hazard-zones-where-you-live-or-visit)
- [USGS, Ashfall is the Most Widespread and Frequent Volcanic Hazard](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/VHP/ashfall-most-widespread-and-frequent-volcanic-hazard)

**Design inference:** The Event 013 threshold is a fictional amplification. Pliny's account supplies human-scale imagery for ashfall and darkness, not evidence for the mod's exact eruption magnitude.

### Recommended title direction

**Ashes Upon Us**

This is a source-derived compression of Pliny's sentence below. It should be treated as a title direction, not as a direct quotation.

### Verified quote record

> “The ashes now began to fall upon us.”

- **Author and work:** Pliny the Younger, *Letters*, Book VI, Letter 20, addressed to Tacitus.
- **Date:** The letter recounts the eruption of Vesuvius in 79 CE. It was written decades later as part of Pliny's early second-century correspondence.
- **Context:** Pliny describes his flight with his mother from Misenum. Ash begins to fall, darkness follows, and the accumulating ash must later be shaken off.
- **Exact wording verification:** [Project Gutenberg, The Letters of the Younger Pliny](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2811/pg2811.html)
- **Edition details:** Translation by William Melmoth, edited by F. C. T. Bosanquet.
- **Rights:** Project Gutenberg identifies the source editions as public domain in the United States. Melmoth died in 1799 and Bosanquet died in 1928, which supports high confidence that this edition is also out of copyright in life-plus-70 jurisdictions. [Project Gutenberg ebook record](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2811)
- **Use note:** Quote only the selected sentence. Do not present Pliny as describing the fictional Event 013 scale.

### Cultural remark and button direction

**Shake off the ash.**

This is an original paraphrase of the same letter's survival detail. Pliny says the group had to stand periodically and shake off the falling ash to avoid being buried under its weight. The wording is concise, physical, and focused on endurance.

It should remain a paraphrase. The source translation contains stronger claims about burial and crushing that would make a button needlessly long and terminal.

## 3. Meteor impact shower

### Recommendation

Keep this as a super-event only when repeated objects survive atmospheric entry and produce confirmed destructive ground impacts. Rename the concept in design discussion when needed to avoid confusing it with an ordinary meteor shower.

### Scientific basis and boundary

**Verified fact:** NASA explains that meteors are the visible streaks produced when meteoroids enter the atmosphere. NASA's educational guidance notes that most meteoroids burn up in the atmosphere and that meteor showers normally produce many visible meteors without a corresponding field of destructive impacts.

Sources:

- [NASA Science, Meteors and Meteorites](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/)
- [NASA Space Place, What Is a Meteor Shower?](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/meteor-shower/en/)

**Design inference:** The accepted Event 013 outcome is an abnormal impact shower. The fiction begins where multiple bodies survive and strike populated or strategic areas. A bright but harmless annual meteor shower cannot satisfy the trigger.

### Recommended title direction

**The Stars of the Sky Fell to the Earth**

This is an exact source fragment from Revelation 6:13 in the Classic World English Bible. It is more specific than the unsourced working label “Skyfall” and directly supports the existing image's multiple descending trails.

### Verified quote record

> “The heavens themselves blaze forth the death of princes.”

- **Author and work:** William Shakespeare, *Julius Caesar*, Act II, Scene II. Calphurnia is the speaker.
- **Date:** The Folger Shakespeare Library dates the play's likely composition and first performance to 1599.
- **Context:** Calphurnia interprets comets and impossible sights as omens before Caesar's assassination. The line is cultural omen language, not an astronomical description.
- **Exact wording verification:** [Project Gutenberg, Julius Caesar](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1522/1522-h/1522-h.htm)
- **Date verification:** [Folger Shakespeare Library, About Julius Caesar](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/julius-caesar/about-shakespeares-julius-caesar/)
- **Rights:** Shakespeare died in 1616 and the underlying play is public domain worldwide. Project Gutenberg distributes this text as public-domain source material in the United States. [Project Gutenberg license policy](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html)
- **Use note:** The quotation frames political dread under a burning sky. Final description text should establish real impacts so the player does not read the line as an omen alone.

### Cultural remark and button direction

**Who is able to stand?**

This is exact wording from Revelation 6:17 in the Classic World English Bible. The same passage places falling stars, a great earthquake, and terrified rulers in the sixth-seal vision.

- **Author and work:** Book of Revelation 6:13-17. Traditional attribution is to John of Patmos.
- **Date:** Commonly dated to the late first century CE. The exact date remains debated.
- **Context and wording verification:** [Classic World English Bible, Revelation 6](https://ebible.org/eng-web/REV06.htm)
- **Rights:** The Classic World English Bible is explicitly public domain. [WEB public-domain notice](https://ebible.org/engwebp/copyright.htm)

The question should be used with restraint. Event 013 has no world-end branch, so the surrounding description must show survival and consequences rather than universal extinction.

## 4. Moving storm corridor

### Recommendation

Keep this as a super-event for a sustained destructive corridor crossing multiple regions. The concept can include a tornado outbreak embedded in or following a long-lived convective system. It should not fire for one local storm.

### Scientific basis and boundary

**Verified fact:** The United States National Weather Service describes a derecho as a widespread, long-lived wind storm associated with a band of rapidly moving showers or thunderstorms. Its safety guidance uses a damage swath greater than 240 miles with damaging gusts along most of the path as defining characteristics.

**Verified fact:** The National Weather Service account of the June 29, 2012 derecho describes a long-lived, rapidly moving line of thunderstorms with a continuous damage swath. It also identifies narrow corridors of stronger winds within the broader path.

Sources:

- [National Weather Service, Derechos](https://www.weather.gov/safety/wind-thunderstorms-derecho)
- [National Weather Service Baltimore and Washington, June 29, 2012 Derecho](https://www.weather.gov/lwx/20120629svrwx)

**Design inference:** A storm corridor that behaves like a moving strategic front is an Event 013 escalation. The real sources support the idea of a long damage path, not an indefinitely self-sustaining or supernatural storm.

### Recommended title direction

**Fretful Elements**

This exact phrase appears in *King Lear*, Act III, Scene I, where Lear is described as contending with the storm. It gives the outcome a literary identity without relying on the generic working label “Storm Corridor.”

### Verified quote record

> “Blow, winds, and crack your cheeks! Rage! blow!”

- **Author and work:** William Shakespeare, *King Lear*, Act III, Scene II. Lear is the speaker.
- **Date:** The Folger Shakespeare record places the play's likely composition in 1605 or 1606. The first quarto was printed in 1608.
- **Context:** Lear stands exposed on the heath and challenges the wind, rain, lightning, and thunder. The surrounding scene presents the storm as both physical danger and political ruin.
- **Exact wording verification:** [Project Gutenberg, King Lear](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1532/pg1532-images.html)
- **Date verification:** [Shakespeare Documented, King Lear first edition](https://shakespearedocumented.folger.edu/resource/document/king-lear-first-edition)
- **Rights:** Shakespeare's underlying text is public domain worldwide. Project Gutenberg identifies its source text as public domain in the United States.
- **Use note:** Preserve the exclamation pattern if used. It is part of the verified edition wording.

### Cultural remark and button direction

**Here’s a night pities neither wise men nor fools.**

This is exact wording spoken by the Fool immediately after Lear's storm challenge in the same scene.

- **Context and wording verification:** [Project Gutenberg, King Lear, Act III, Scene II](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1532/pg1532-images.html)

## 5. Delayed tsunami path assessment

### Recommendation

Do not create a standalone tsunami super-event by default.

The specification already gives the tsunami path a delayed wave train, countdown interface, reports, and evolving coastal consequences. A separate super-event risks duplicating the initiating rupture, eruption, or ocean-impact super-event and would weaken the event's anti-spam threshold.

Promote a tsunami chain only if all of these conditions are met:

1. It crosses multiple separated coasts or ocean basins.
2. It reaches an independently major death, damage, or infrastructure threshold after the initiating event.
3. It has a distinct narrative beat rather than replaying the source disaster.
4. It can fire once for the chain rather than once per wave or coast.
5. It has its own 457 by 328 image, sourced quote, cultural remark, and audio research package.

### Scientific basis

**Verified fact:** NOAA describes a tsunami as a series of waves rather than a single wave. Its warning-centre guidance notes that the first wave may not be the largest and that arrival times differ between coasts.

**Verified fact:** NOAA's frequently asked questions explain that a tsunami wave train can continue for many hours and can cross an ocean basin.

Sources:

- [NOAA Tsunami Warning Centers, Tsunami Science](https://www.tsunami.gov/php/tsunami_science.php)
- [NOAA Tsunami Warning Centers, Tsunami FAQ](https://www.tsunami.gov/?os=vbf&page=tsunamiFAQ)

**Design inference:** Those facts strongly support the existing delayed countdown and wave-train gameplay. They do not by themselves justify another super-event. The super-event threshold is a narrative and balance decision supplied by the Event 013 specification.

### Asset status

There is no completed 457 by 328 tsunami super-event image in `docs/assets/013_natural_disasters/manifest.md`.

The existing `natural_disaster_tsunami` report art and `natural_disaster_tsunami_countdown` animation serve different dimensions and interfaces. They should not be stretched or silently substituted as a super-event image. Any fallback would require explicit user approval under repository rules.

### Quote status

No quotation is recommended because the standalone tsunami super-event is not recommended. If the path is promoted later, it needs a dedicated research pass. Reusing the rupture quote or an eruption quote would blur the source event and the delayed coastal disaster.
