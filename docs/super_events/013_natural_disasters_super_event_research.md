# Event 013 Natural Disasters Evolution IV Super-Event Text Research

## Scope

- Event ID: `013`
- Event slug: `natural_disasters`
- Super-event role: first global recognition that Evolution IV abnormal disasters are no longer isolated incidents but a recurring high-chaos world condition
- This note covers title, button text, and quote research only.
- Audio, image, localisation, slot wiring, and gameplay implementation remain out of scope for this subagent handoff.

## Role fit summary

This super-event is not a world-end signal. The best text package needs to communicate pattern recognition, institutional helplessness, and the transition from isolated emergency bulletins to permanent readiness. The strongest options avoid terminal prophecy, avoid occult certainty, and avoid generic apocalypse wording.

## Recommended package

- Recommended title: `The Unquiet Earth`
- Recommended button text: `Not single spies.`
- Recommended main quote: `Nor can nature be commanded except by being obeyed.`
- Quote author: Francis Bacon
- Quote source: *Instauratio Magna* / prefatory material commonly printed with the *Novum Organum*
- Year: 1620
- Quote source URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13182/pg13182-images.html
- Button source: William Shakespeare, *Hamlet*, Act 4, Scene 5
- Button source URL: https://shakespeare.mit.edu/hamlet/hamlet.4.5.html
- Attribution confidence: High
- Copyright note: Bacon and Shakespeare are public domain. The quoted source texts are safely usable; links are documentation sources, not license claims on the hosting sites.

## Why this package fits

`The Unquiet Earth` is short, specific, and non-terminal. It covers earthquake waves, meteor impacts, storms, ashfall, and sea-linked aftermaths without sounding like a final-doomsday banner.

The Bacon quote is the strongest non-terminal main quote. It frames the super-event as the moment governments, observatories, and militaries learn that command over events has failed because nature is no longer behaving within familiar limits. It sounds institutional and philosophical rather than prophetic.

`Not single spies.` works well as the button because it turns the event's disaster-burst logic into a short reaction line. It references a verified Shakespeare line without needing a long direct quote, and it carries the sense that reports now arrive in waves rather than alone.

## Main quote candidates considered

### 1. Francis Bacon — Recommended

- Quote: `Nor can nature be commanded except by being obeyed.`
- Source: *Instauratio Magna* / prefatory text to the *Novum Organum*
- Year: 1620
- Source URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13182/pg13182-images.html
- Verified wording note: Project Gutenberg prints the longer passage as `For the chain of causes cannot by any force be loosed or broken, nor can nature be commanded except by being obeyed.`
- Attribution confidence: High
- Copyright note: Public domain
- Fit: strongest overall. It captures the exact super-event role: ministries and experts realize force of will is no longer enough, and the disaster age must be endured and studied rather than controlled.

### 2. William Shakespeare, *Hamlet* — Strong backup

- Quote: `When sorrows come, they come not single spies / But in battalions.`
- Speaker: King Claudius
- Source: *Hamlet*, Act 4, Scene 5
- Approximate date: c. 1600
- Source URL: https://shakespeare.mit.edu/hamlet/hamlet.4.5.html
- Attribution confidence: High
- Copyright note: Public domain
- Fit: extremely good for clustered disaster bursts and chained incidents.
- Why not selected: the line is about compounded human grief rather than nature itself. It is better used as a short button allusion than as the main quote.
