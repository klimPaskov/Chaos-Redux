# Event 015 Utopia Manifesto super-event text research

## Scope and authority

This note supplies the final text research package for the Event 15 regional proclamation super-event. It covers one shared title, five route descriptions, one shared button remark, one selected main quote, one backup quote, and rejected candidates.

The super-event role is an ideological and regional-order proclamation. It fires only after route formation, the island or capital-ring project, a meaningful external network, and a regional commonwealth success. Formation by itself does not justify this presentation.

The final copy below is original Chaos Redux writing except for the two quotations explicitly identified as sourced text. The descriptions derive from the accepted route and threshold requirements in:

The title identifies the regional threshold directly. Utopia is no longer an isolated proposal or a domestic experiment. It has partners, imitators, dependents, rivals, and borders that other governments must address.

The button remark makes the same change concrete through a period object. Timetables connect railways, ships, reserve deliveries, council delegations, assigned service, and reconstruction missions across all five routes. It does not claim that any route has achieved perfection.

## Final localisation-ready wording

### Shared title

**UTOPIA HAS NEIGHBORS**

### Voluntary commonwealth description

> What began as a recovered book has become a compact of towns and small states. Public assemblies ratify common guarantees, municipal stores remain open to their neighbors, and linked ports and railways carry food, tools, and delegates across borders. The member governments have kept their flags and their right to leave. Together they have made voluntary association into a regional power whose survival will depend on promises kept after the founding celebration.

Route fit:

- visible assemblies, stores, transport, and municipal links
- a voluntary regional compact rather than immediate annexation
- continued sovereignty and exit rights
- a grounded risk that consent can fail when promises are not kept

### Council union description

> Delegates from workshops, farms, civic services, and defense councils now meet across borders under a common congress. Shared stores answer shortages, production plans travel with reserve trains, and local councils send mandates instead of tribute. The union has given workers and municipalities a regional authority that no single capital can claim. Its workshops feed a common defense, and every delay at the council tables tests how long solidarity can carry the weight of command.

Route fit:

- worker and civic delegates remain the visible political actors
- production, stores, reserves, and defense cross borders
- authority belongs to a congress of councils
- deliberation and coordination remain real costs

### Planned Utopia description

> A chain of planned cities now runs through the region. Rail lines join public housing, harbors, clinics, workshops, and measured reserves under one technical standard. Expert delegations arrive to copy the surveys, and neighboring governments sign their districts to the same schedules. Lit homes and full depots show what the planners have achieved. Assignment notices at every station reveal how far the boards' authority now reaches.

Route fit:

- the city network, transport, housing, public works, and reserves are visible
- neighboring governments adopt standards through expert delegations
- achievement appears through lived infrastructure rather than abstract efficiency
- assignment remains visible as political power and social cost

### Closed island-state description

> The gates of the completed island-state have closed behind a society organized for service. Guarded stores, fortified boundaries, assigned districts, and dependent outposts now answer to one stewardship. Foreign missions watch ration columns move with military precision and find no place for refusal inside the system. The state calls its separation complete. Its neighbors see a regional hierarchy held together by reserves, compulsory labor, and checkpoints that govern every road and harbor.

Route fit:

- the boundary, stores, service system, districts, and outposts are visible
- discipline and reserve capacity explain the state's strength
- dependent territories reveal the regional order
- compulsory labor and absent refusal show the human cost without glamorizing it

### Practical commonwealth description

> The manifesto has left the cabinet table and entered the region as a habit of government. Municipal assemblies revise old rules in public, mixed institutions protect common guarantees, and reconstruction missions carry tools farther than proclamations. Neighboring states join a loose compact without adopting one constitution or one official reading of the book. Its authority rests on aid, criticism, and agreements that can be amended. Every member keeps the power to argue with its founders.

Route fit:

- public revision, plural institutions, aid, and reconstruction remain visible
- the compact does not demand one constitutional model
- amendment and criticism define the route's authority
- the wording stays modest and does not present this route as the only intelligent interpretation

### Shared button remark

**Nowhere has a timetable.**

This is original Chaos Redux writing. It is an etymological allusion, not a quotation or a modern copyrighted cultural line. The New York Public Library identifies Utopia as a play on the identically pronounced Greek terms for good place and no place. The Library of Congress likewise records More's no-place and good-place wordplay.

Source basis:

- [New York Public Library, first edition of Thomas More's Utopia](https://www.nypl.org/events/exhibitions/galleries/beginnings/item/3551)
- [Library of Congress, Mapping the Land of Utopia](https://blogs.loc.gov/maps/2022/08/mapping-the-land-of-utopia/)

Copyright note:

- the selected remark is newly authored for Chaos Redux
- it copies no wording from either institutional source
- a general exact-phrase web check returned no exact match for the selected title or remark on 14 July 2026

## Selected main quote

> However, there are many things in the commonwealth of Utopia that I rather wish, than hope, to see followed in our governments.

Recommended attribution:

**Thomas More, Utopia, trans. Gilbert Burnet**

Source record:

- author: Thomas More
- work: Utopia
- original publication: Latin first edition, Louvain, 1516
- translation: Gilbert Burnet, first published in 1684
- consulted edition: Henry Morley edition, Cassell and Company, 1901, reproduced as Project Gutenberg ebook 2130
- exact source: [Project Gutenberg, Utopia, closing paragraph](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2130/2130-h/2130-h.htm)
- independent text cross-check: [Project Gutenberg, Ideal Commonwealths, Utopia conclusion](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/18638/18638-h/18638-h.htm)
- attribution confidence: high

Context:

More's narrator closes the book by declining full agreement with Hythloday's account, then records a desire to see many Utopian practices adopted. The selected sentence preserves both the attraction of the political experiment and the refusal to call its whole system perfect.

Public-domain and copyright note:

- More died in 1535
- Burnet died in 1715
- Morley died in 1894
- the work, translation, and consulted print edition are beyond ordinary life-plus-70 terms
- Project Gutenberg identifies its source text as a work not protected by United States copyright law
- Project Gutenberg trademark conditions concern redistribution under its branding and do not create copyright in More's or Burnet's text

Route fit:

| Route | Fit |
| --- | --- |
| Voluntary commonwealth | Strong. The wish recognizes consent and provision without promising permanent harmony. |
| Council union | Strong. The line welcomes common institutions while leaving council authority open to judgment. |
| Planned Utopia | Strong. The contrast between desire and expectation suits impressive works paired with expanding assignment. |
| Closed island-state | Strong through critical distance. The narrator's refusal of complete agreement prevents the quote from blessing compulsion. |
| Practical commonwealth | Very strong. Selective adoption and continued criticism are the route's defining method. |

## Verified backup quote

> No man is an island, entire of itself.

Recommended attribution:

**John Donne, Devotions upon Emergent Occasions, Meditation XVII**

Source record:

- author: John Donne
- work: Devotions upon Emergent Occasions
- section: Meditation XVII
- publication: London, 1624
- exact source: [Project Gutenberg, Devotions upon Emergent Occasions, Meditation XVII](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/23772/23772-h/23772-h.htm)
- attribution confidence: high

Editorial note:

The Project Gutenberg text continues the clause with additional words. The selected standalone display keeps Donne's exact words and normalizes the source's terminal punctuation to a period.

Public-domain and copyright note:

- Donne died in 1631
- the 1624 work is beyond ordinary life-plus-70 terms
- the consulted Project Gutenberg text identifies the underlying work as reusable public-domain material in the United States

Route fit:

| Route | Fit |
| --- | --- |
| Voluntary commonwealth | Strong. The compact exists because small states choose material interdependence. |
| Council union | Strong. Cross-border councils and reserve institutions make the line concrete. |
| Planned Utopia | Moderate. The connected city network supports the image, though the line says little about planning. |
| Closed island-state | Strong as warning. The line judges the regime's claim to complete separation and exposes its dependence on outposts. |
| Practical commonwealth | Strong. The plural compact treats autonomy and connection as compatible. |

Reason it remains the backup:

The line is highly familiar and has appeared widely outside its original devotional context. More's closing sentence is more specific to this event, more attentive to the book's ambiguity, and less likely to flatten the five routes into one message.

## Other verified candidates

### Oscar Wilde, The Soul of Man

> A map of the world that does not include Utopia is not worth even glancing at.

- work and date: The Soul of Man under Socialism, first published in 1891
- consulted edition: Arthur L. Humphreys edition, 1900, transcribed by Project Gutenberg
- source: [Project Gutenberg, The Soul of Man](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1017/1017-h/1017-h.htm)
- rights: public domain. Wilde died in 1900 and the consulted edition predates modern copyright terms.
- attribution confidence: high
- route fit: strong for the proclamation threshold, strongest for the planned and voluntary routes
- rejection reason: it makes the map the central image, carries a universal progress claim, and gives too little room for the closed route's coercive cost

### William Morris, News from Nowhere

> If others can see it as I have seen it, then it may be called a vision rather than a dream.

- work and date: News from Nowhere, serialized in 1890 and published in book form in 1891
- consulted edition: Longmans, Green, and Company, 1908
- source: [Project Gutenberg, News from Nowhere](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3261/pg3261-images.html)
- rights: public domain. Morris died in 1896 and the consulted edition is from 1908.
- attribution confidence: high
- route fit: strong for voluntary and practical routes, moderate for council and planned routes, weak for the closed route
- rejection reason: the dream and vision contrast makes the event sound like validation of an ideal future instead of a report on institutions with visible costs

### Thomas More prefatory poem in a modern rendering

The prefatory Anemolius poem directly contrasts no-place with good-place and claims that an imagined polity has been produced through people, resources, and laws.

- work and period: prefatory material printed with early editions of Utopia, including the 1518 edition
- consulted source: [Thomas More Studies, Utopia and Selected Epigrams](https://thomasmorestudies.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Utopia-Selected-Epigram-Notes-11-23-2020-compressed.pdf)
- attribution confidence: high for the poem's presence and theme
- copyright confidence for the modern English rendering: insufficient for final use
- route fit: strong for planned and closed routes, moderate for the other routes
- rejection reason: the available modern English rendering did not present a clear reusable license, and its claim to have produced the best laws would glorify every route without distinction

## Title and remark candidates rejected

| Candidate | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| **NO PLACE TAKES ITS PLACE** | Rejected because the double pun draws attention to itself and weakens the institutional weight of the event. |
| **THE ISLAND AMONG NATIONS** | Rejected because landlocked capital-ring recipients and plural compacts need no literal island identity. |
| **THE COUNTRY BEYOND THE BOOK** | Rejected because the trigger proves a regional network, not only one transformed country. |
| **NOWHERE MADE LAW** | Rejected because it is a working evolution label in the source specification, and the research prompt forbids promoting working labels into final localisation. |
| **The book has crossed its shore.** | Rejected because shore imagery fits coastal recipients more strongly than landlocked recipients. |
| **No man is an island.** | Rejected as the button because it is a direct Donne quotation and too familiar for the short reaction slot. It remains a sourced backup main quote in its longer form. |

## Wording validation

- All five descriptions report visible institutions and regional consequences.
- No description exposes counters, focus names, trigger thresholds, rewards, or hidden-route logic.
- The planned description preserves unease through visible assignment notices.
- The closed description names compulsory labor, absent refusal, dependent outposts, and checkpoints.
- The practical description does not claim exclusive moral insight.
- The title does not reuse a working specification label.
- The button is original and has a documented source basis for its no-place allusion.
- The main and backup quotes have primary-text links, edition records, public-domain reasoning, attribution confidence, and route-fit analysis.
- Player-facing text contains no em dash, semicolon, generic new-dawn language, or fragmented filler.
