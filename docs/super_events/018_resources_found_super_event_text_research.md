# Event 018 Resources Found super-event text research

## Scope and authority

This package supplies final text and source research for the three Event 018 super-events reserved for slots 82 through 84. It covers titles, descriptions, quotations, attribution lines, and buttons only. The live reservation moved from 78 through 80 after a concurrent Event 014 package claimed the next four available display slots.

The locked fictional identity is:

- country: **The Oth-Kesh Host**
- tag: `DHO`
- leader: **Vhorruk**, a literal cave monster rather than a human adopting a legendary name

The Oth-Kesh Host and Vhorruk are original Chaos Redux fiction. None of the historical or literary sources below is presented as their origin. The quotations work as human attempts to interpret the witnessed event.

This file does not authorize gameplay, localisation, image, audio, workbook, registry, or specification changes. The three roles remain those established by the Event 018 specification:

1. slot 82 announces the emergence of an organized nonhuman state from the extraction field
2. slot 83 announces the loss of geographic containment after the Host has consumed its first continent and opened major footholds elsewhere
3. slot 84 records the Host's defeat only after a global, near-global, or comparably long and costly war

The three moments have separate dramatic jobs. Slot 82 is the birth of a country. Slot 83 is the failure of continental containment. Slot 84 is a costly peace followed by permanent vigilance. The wording does not treat emergence as an early world end or defeat as a clean return to normal.

## Research method

The source package uses direct primary texts, public-domain translations, and an official United States government record. Each candidate was checked for wording, context, attribution, and reuse within the current Chaos Redux super-event localisation. Existing quotations in slots 67 through 77 were excluded from final selection.

The player-facing copy follows these boundaries:

- descriptions state visible world conditions rather than hidden counters or trigger thresholds
- quotations contain no gameplay mechanics
- titles and buttons are marked as original writing when they are not quotations
- source language does not canonize the Oth-Kesh as biblical, Greek, Roman, or folkloric beings
- descriptions avoid comedy, growling speech, invented dialect, and generic fantasy prophecy
- the defeat text preserves casualties, reconstruction, and changed security practice

Sources were last checked on 11 July 2026.

## Slot 82: cave emergence

### Dramatic purpose

This is the first public recognition that the extraction disaster has produced a sovereign military order. It should feel like the birth of a hostile country from a place that governments had treated as a resource field. It is not a declaration that the world is ending.

### Final original text

**Title, original authored text**

`THE OTH-KESH HOST RISES`

**Description, original authored text**

> The extraction zone has collapsed into a passage wider than its deepest surveys recorded. Mineral-armored formations advance through the ruins in ordered columns, with the cave monster Vhorruk directing them toward the border roads. The fallen field has become the capital of the Oth-Kesh Host, and its war broods are already attacking every country along its borders.

**Button, original authored text**

`The field has a sovereign.`

The title gives the final country identity at the moment players need to learn it. The description shows organization through columns, command, a capital, and immediate border wars. It also establishes that Vhorruk is physically present as a cave monster.

### Final sourced quotation

> As for the earth, out of it comes bread. Underneath it is turned up as it were by fire.

- **Work and date:** *Book of Job* 28:5. The composition is ancient and its precise date is disputed. A single year should not be asserted.
- **Speaker:** The mining meditation is part of the chapter's poetic voice. It is not assigned to Vhorruk or to an Oth-Kesh speaker.
- **Translation:** World English Bible British Edition.
- **Exact source:** [World English Bible British Edition, Job 28](https://ebible.org/engwebpb/JOB28.htm)
- **Rights:** The linked edition states that it is in the public domain and may be copied freely.
- **Context:** Job 28 opens with silver mines, gold refining, iron, copper, remote shafts, darkness, and the exposure of hidden things. Verse 5 turns from the bread produced by the earth's surface to a fire-like disturbance below it.
- **Fit:** The line joins resource abundance to an unseen depth in two complete sentences. It gives the emergence a culturally specific mining image without naming the fictional species or explaining the mechanic.
- **Translation caveat:** The wording belongs to a modern public-domain translation of an ancient Hebrew text. The image of fire is poetic. It should not be treated as a technical claim about Oth-Kesh biology or geology.

**Recommended localisation attribution**

`Job 28:5, World English Bible`

### Other verified candidates

#### Pliny the Elder, *Natural History*, Book XXXIII

> We penetrate into her entrails, and seek for treasures in the abodes even of the Manes

- **Date and edition:** The Latin work dates to the first century CE. The linked English translation by John Bostock and H. T. Riley was published from 1855 to 1857.
- **Source:** [Project Gutenberg, *The Natural History of Pliny*, Volume VI](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/62704/pg62704-images.html)
- **Rights:** The ancient work and nineteenth-century translation are public domain. Project Gutenberg marks the ebook as public domain in the United States.
- **Context and fit:** Pliny condemns human beings for entering the earth in search of treasure. It is the strongest candidate for the extraction field as a product of greed.
- **Reason not selected:** The displayed words are an excerpt from a longer sentence. The Roman Manes are spirits of the dead, which could falsely suggest a folkloric identity for the Oth-Kesh.

#### John Milton, *Paradise Lost*, Book VII

> Out of the ground up rose,
> As from his lair, the wild beast where he wons

- **Date:** First published in 1667 and revised in 1674.
- **Source:** [Project Gutenberg, *Paradise Lost*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/26/26-h/26-h.htm)
- **Rights:** Public domain.
- **Context and fit:** Milton describes animals rising from the newly created ground. The physical emergence is vivid and the line requires no invented folklore.
- **Reason not selected:** The original context is creation rather than mining, state formation, or war. It makes the Host look like a collection of beasts instead of an organized polity.

#### *Revelation* 9:7, World English Bible

> The shapes of the locusts were like horses prepared for war.

- **Date and edition:** Ancient Christian apocalyptic text in the modern World English Bible translation.
- **Source:** [World English Bible, Revelation 9](https://ebible.org/eng-web/REV09.htm)
- **Rights:** The World English Bible is public domain.
- **Context and fit:** The passage describes armed forms emerging after the pit is opened. It clearly conveys military organization.
- **Reason not selected:** The locust comparison could be mistaken for a canonical description of Oth-Kesh anatomy.

## Slot 83: cave world end

### Dramatic purpose

This event marks the loss of geographic containment. The Host has turned its first continent into a connected military depth and has established major emergence fronts across other continents. The wording must make simultaneous distant breakthroughs feel final without exposing the counters that caused the event.

### Final original text

**Title, original authored text**

`THE DEEP WAR CROSSES THE SEAS`

**Description, original authored text**

> The Oth-Kesh Host has converted its first continent into a connected domain of chambers, resource basins, and marching routes. Across distant continents, mines, refineries, and industrial districts split open under stronger formations shaped by the first war. Vhorruk's armies are emerging on several shores at once, and the ground beneath the remaining countries has become a front.

**Button, original authored text**

`Every deep has found a road.`

The first sentence records continental consolidation. The second shows that later formations reflect what the Host learned during the first war. The third makes the cross-continent footholds simultaneous and visible.

### Final sourced quotation

> the firmament is embroiled with the deep.

- **Speaker, work, and date:** Prometheus in *Prometheus Bound*, traditionally attributed to Aeschylus and written in the fifth century BCE.
- **Translation:** Theodore Alois Buckley, a nineteenth-century English translation. Project Gutenberg identifies Buckley as the translator and records his life dates as 1825 to 1856.
- **Exact source:** [Project Gutenberg, *Prometheus Bound and the Seven Against Thebes*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/27458/27458-h/27458-h.htm)
- **Ebook and rights record:** [Project Gutenberg ebook 27458](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/27458). The record marks the ebook as public domain in the United States. The translator's death in 1856 also places this translation beyond life-plus-70 terms.
- **Context:** At the close of the play, Prometheus describes earth, thunder, lightning, winds, the heavens, and the deep being driven into one violent conflict by the approaching onslaught.
- **Fit:** The clause joins the ordered world above to the deep below. It captures the moment when separate continental emergencies become one world condition.
- **Translation caveat:** The quote is a terminal clause excerpted exactly from a longer sentence. Its lower-case opening is preserved because the source clause begins after a conjunction. The Greek tragedy is an analogy for cosmic scale. It is not Oth-Kesh lore.

**Recommended localisation attribution**

`Aeschylus, Prometheus Bound, trans. Theodore Alois Buckley`

### Other verified candidates

#### Hesiod, *Theogony*

> The whole earth seethed, and sky and sea

- **Date and edition:** Ancient Greek poem, commonly placed around the late eighth or early seventh century BCE. The linked English translation by Hugh G. Evelyn-White was published in 1914.
- **Source:** [Internet Sacred Text Archive, *Theogony*](https://sacred-texts.com/cla/hesiod/theogony.htm)
- **Rights:** The ancient work and 1914 translation are public domain.
- **Context and fit:** The line occurs during the struggle of Zeus and Typhoeus and gives immediate earth-wide scale.
- **Reason not selected:** Its boiling earth imagery overlaps the active natural-disaster super-event family and says less about a connected deep reaching other continents.

#### Edgar Allan Poe, *The City in the Sea*

> Hell, rising from a thousand thrones,
> Shall do it reverence.

- **Date:** Poe published the poem in earlier form in 1831 and continued revising it. The linked public-domain collection preserves the later text.
- **Source:** [Project Gutenberg, *The Poems of Edgar Allan Poe*](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/76996/pg76996-images.html)
- **Rights:** Public domain.
- **Context and fit:** The poem imagines a doomed city sinking while power rises from many thrones. The number and direction strongly match simultaneous global emergence points.
- **Reason not selected:** The word Hell would turn a physical nonhuman civilization into a religious underworld force.

#### *Revelation* 9:2, World English Bible

> He opened the pit of the abyss, and smoke went up out of the pit, like the smoke from a burning furnace.

- **Date and edition:** Ancient Christian apocalyptic text in the modern World English Bible translation.
- **Source:** [World English Bible, Revelation 9](https://ebible.org/eng-web/REV09.htm)
- **Rights:** The World English Bible is public domain.
- **Context and fit:** An opened depth darkens the wider world and releases a hostile force.
- **Reason not selected:** One biblical abyss is a weaker match for coordinated cross-continent footholds, and the surrounding locust imagery can misidentify the Oth-Kesh.

## Slot 84: conditional global or near-global defeat

### Dramatic purpose

This event belongs only after the Host has been defeated following a global, near-global, or comparably long and costly conflict. It should honor the dead, show material reconstruction, and leave the world with permanent survey and defense practices. It should not sound like a victory parade or a return to the old normal.

### Final original text

**Title, original authored text**

`THE LAST DEPTH IS SEALED`

**Description, original authored text**

> The last territory held by the Oth-Kesh Host has been retaken, and every known foothold has been broken or sealed. Survivors return to damaged resource regions beside engineers, burial teams, and soldiers carrying weapons built for the underground war. Reconstruction begins under shared survey rules and the knowledge that the earth beneath an industrial frontier can no longer be treated as empty.

**Button, original authored text**

`The living will rebuild above it.`

The description gives the defeat a physical aftermath. Burial teams hold the human cost in view. Shared surveys and retained underground weapons show that the peace has changed how states understand the ground beneath them.

### Final sourced quotation

> In peace sons bury their fathers, in war fathers bury their sons.

- **Speaker, work, and date:** Croesus in Herodotus, *Histories* 1.87.4, written in the fifth century BCE.
- **Translation:** A. D. Godley, published in 1920 in the cited Perseus edition.
- **Exact source and rights:** [Perseus, Herodotus, *Histories* 1.87.4](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0126%3Abook%3D1%3Achapter%3D87%3Asection%3D4). The page identifies Godley, gives the publication record, and licenses the display text under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 United States. Godley died in 1925, so the underlying 1920 translation is also beyond life-plus-70 terms.
- **Parallel text check:** [Scaife Viewer, Herodotus 1.87.4](https://scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg0016.tlg001.perseus-grc2%3A1.87.4?right=perseus-eng2)
- **Context:** Croesus speaks after his failed war against Cyrus and while confronting the cost of the choice that destroyed his own position. The line contrasts the expected order of family burial with the reversal caused by war.
- **Fit:** The quote makes victory secondary to the generations buried during the conflict. It supports a reflective defeat event and does not claim that peace erases the damage.
- **Translation caveat:** This is Godley's English rendering of Herodotus rather than a modern literal translation. Perseus notes that its display text is modernized. The selected sentence is complete and should be preserved without paraphrase.

**Recommended localisation attribution**

`Croesus in Herodotus, Histories 1.87, trans. A. D. Godley`

### Other verified candidates

#### H. G. Wells, *The War of the Worlds*, Epilogue

> Surely, if we have learned nothing else, this war has taught us pity

- **Date:** 1898.
- **Source:** [Project Gutenberg, *The War of the Worlds*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36/36-h/36-h.htm)
- **Rights:** Public domain. Wells died in 1946, which also places the novel beyond life-plus-70 terms.
- **Context and fit:** The narrator reflects on the dead, on human dominion, and on a planet that can no longer be assumed secure. The learning and pity fit the costly aftermath closely.
- **Reason not selected:** The quoted words are a clause from a longer sentence. The source continues with punctuation and additional wording that would violate the Event 018 sentence-style rules. The Herodotus sentence is complete and needs no editorial adaptation.

#### J. A. Holmes, United States Geological Survey Bulletin 423

> This will require wise laws and regulations based on fact and experience, and the strictest possible discipline.

- **Date:** 1910.
- **Source:** [United States Geological Survey, *A Primer on Explosives for Coal Miners*, Bulletin 423](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/0423/report.pdf)
- **Rights:** Work of the United States federal government and public domain in the United States.
- **Context and fit:** Holmes introduces an international mine-safety study after recurring deaths in coal mines. He argues that knowledge, law, and discipline must follow disaster.
- **Reason not selected:** The line is an excellent research anchor for the description's shared survey rules. Its administrative cadence is weaker as the central emotional quote.

## Implementation cautions

- Preserve the lower-case opening of the slot 83 quotation if exact transcription is the priority. If the implementation capitalizes it for display, record that as an editorial normalization rather than claiming exact typography.
- Keep Vhorruk and the Oth-Kesh identity in original description text. Do not add those names inside historical quotations.
- Do not reuse the emergence description for the world-end slot. The first creates a country. The second announces simultaneous fronts beyond the first continent.
- Do not fire or describe the defeat event for a short regional victory. Its wording assumes burial on a vast scale, devastated resource regions, multinational survey rules, and a war that permanently altered military practice.
- Keep the quoted source and the original authored button visibly separate in documentation. None of the three recommended buttons is a historical quotation.
