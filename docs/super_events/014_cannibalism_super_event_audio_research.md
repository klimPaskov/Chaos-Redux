# Event 014 Cannibalism super-event audio research

## Scope and status

This package supplies four unique, real musical recordings for the accepted Event 014 super-event roles:

- Hannibal Lecter reveal
- ordinary Hannibal Lecter world-end
- Wendigo Hannibal Lecter world-end
- eligible global defeat aftermath

The source and rights pages were checked on 2026-07-11. Composition rights and recording rights were evaluated separately. The preserved downloads match the SHA-1 values published by Wikimedia Commons. Final one-shot derivatives were exported as 44.1 kHz stereo Vorbis OGG files with matching 44.1 kHz stereo signed 16-bit PCM WAV files.

This pass intentionally does not edit event script, localisation, scripted localisation, GUI, GFX, music definitions, sound definitions, the music table, or spreadsheets. The main agent owns all final wiring.

## 1. Hannibal Lecter reveal

### Source identity

- **Work:** `Danse macabre, Op. 40`
- **Composer:** Camille Saint-Saëns
- **Composition date:** 1874; first release/performance information dates to 1875
- **Performer:** Philadelphia Symphony Orchestra
- **Conductor:** Leopold Stokowski
- **Recording date:** 29 April 1925
- **Issue:** Victor 6505-A/B, masters CVE-27929 and CVE-27930
- **Canonical source and rights page:** [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PhiladelphiaSymphonyOrchestra-DanseMacabre.ogg)
- **Upstream archive:** [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/PhiladelphiaSymphonyOrchestra-DanseMacabre)
- **Direct preserved media:** <https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/76/PhiladelphiaSymphonyOrchestra-DanseMacabre.ogg>
- **Preserved source:** `docs/assets/014_cannibalism/source_audio/hannibal_reveal_danse_macabre_stokowski_1925_source.ogg`

### Rights verification

- **Composition:** public domain. Saint-Saëns died in 1921, and the 1874 composition is outside life-plus-100-or-shorter terms and the United States pre-1931 publication threshold.
- **Recording:** Wikimedia Commons expressly marks the recording public domain in the United States under the Classics Protection and Access Act because it was published before 1 January 1926. Its 1925 publication is also beyond the European 70-year neighbouring-rights term.
- **Attribution requirement:** none stated by the public-domain source. Courtesy credit is retained below.
- **License confidence:** high.

### Editorial fit and processing

The opening gives the reveal ritual form, predatory intelligence, and a disciplined macabre dance rather than undifferentiated horror ambience. Its period orchestral texture also keeps the cue severe and historical without attaching Hannibal to a modern nation or anthem.

- Retained `00:00.000-01:54.000`.
- Applied a `0.25 s` fade-in and `6.00 s` fade-out.
- Two-pass loudness normalization targeted `-18 LUFS`. The peak target was reduced to `-1.8 dBTP` so the final Vorbis encode remained below the `-1.5 dBTP` delivery ceiling.
- Exported one-shot; no loop.
- Final OGG: `music/014_cannibalism/super_event_49_hannibal_reveal.ogg`
- Final WAV: `sound/014_cannibalism/super_event_49_hannibal_reveal.wav`

- Computer-generated, MIDI-rendered, test-tone, drone, oscillator, and sound-effect candidates were rejected by the brief.
- Existing Chaos Redux tracks were not reused because each requested role requires a unique final recording and several older entries have incomplete provenance.
