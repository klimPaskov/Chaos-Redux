# Event 028 Asteroid Impact Super-Event Audio Research

Status: selected and prepared for parent-owned super-event wiring.

Research date: 2026-08-29.

This note covers only the Event 028 impact super-event audio package. The miss path remains a close-passage news event and does not use this cue.

## Event role and selection

The cue needs to accompany the first global reveal after impact, when the main state is destroyed, surrounding regions are damaged, fragments may remain active, and atmospheric dust is beginning to spread. The music therefore needs a structured opening with immediate physical weight, sustained orchestral development, and an ending that can leave the visual aftermath unresolved without becoming a generic drone, test tone, noise bed, explosion, or placeholder.

Selected track: Johannes Brahms, *Tragic Overture, Op. 81*, performed by the Czech National Symphony Orchestra and published as the Musopen Symphony recording.

Recommended excerpt: source start 00:00.000 through 01:55.000, with a 0.10-second entry fade and a 5-second exit fade.

The opening supplies a real orchestral attack for the impact reveal, while the first 115 seconds retain enough development to carry the destruction and expanding dust without requiring a loop. The D-minor tragic-overture character communicates irreversible physical consequence better than a triumphant “cosmic sunrise” cue or an intimate solo/choral lament.

The exact composition is not present in the existing super-event catalogue, although other Brahms works are already used. This is a distinct recording and composition, not a reuse of an existing Chaos Redux track.

## Rights review for the selected recording

Composition rights: *Tragic Overture, Op. 81* was composed by Johannes Brahms, who died in 1897. The source record identifies the composition as public domain, including the United States publication basis and life-plus-100-years jurisdictions.

Recording rights: the Wikimedia Commons FLAC page states that the recording was released into the worldwide public domain by Musopen and grants use for any purpose where a public-domain dedication is not legally possible, subject to law. This is separate from the composition status and is the reason this specific performance was selected.

License confidence: high for a mod distribution when the source-page attribution and Musopen credit are retained. Musopen’s published courtesy language asks derived works to attribute Musopen and asks users not to directly sell the recording as a standalone product; this package embeds an edited excerpt in a larger mod and includes the requested credit.

Required attribution: “*Tragic Overture, Op. 81* — Johannes Brahms; performed by the Czech National Symphony Orchestra (Musopen Symphony). Recording source: Musopen, via Wikimedia Commons. Recording released to the public domain by Musopen; composition public domain.”

Source rights pages: [Wikimedia Commons FLAC record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Brahms_-_Tragic_Overture,_Op._81_%28Musopen_Symphony%29.flac), [Musopen work page](https://musopen.org/music/2120-tragic-overture-op-81/), [Musopen source archive](https://archive.org/details/MusopenCollectionAsFlac), and [CC0 public-domain dedication reference](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/deed.en).

## Edit and conversion record

The source was downloaded byte-for-byte from the direct Wikimedia Commons upload URL and preserved before editing.

The excerpt uses source time 00:00.000 through 01:55.000 with no pitch shift, time stretch, looping, noise-bed layering, or synthetic replacement.

A 0.10-second fade-in avoids a hard frame-zero transient, and a 5-second fade-out at 01:50.000 leaves the impact aftermath open for the final visual beat.

The final conversion used FFmpeg with `-ar 44100 -ac 2 -c:a pcm_s16le`, retained the source metadata, and applied `loudnorm=I=-19:TP=-2:LRA=11:linear=false` after the fades.

Conversion command:

```text
ffmpeg -y -hide_banner -loglevel warning -ss 0 -t 115 -i "docs/assets/028_asteroid_incoming/source_audio/Brahms_Tragic_Overture_Op81_Musopen_Symphony.flac" -af "afade=t=in:st=0:d=0.10,afade=t=out:st=110:d=5,loudnorm=I=-19:TP=-2:LRA=11:linear=false" -ar 44100 -ac 2 -c:a pcm_s16le -map_metadata 0 "sound/028_asteroid_incoming/super_event_028_asteroid_impact.wav"
```

The companion OGG was encoded from the verified final WAV with FFmpeg using `-c:a libvorbis -q:a 5 -ar 44100 -ac 2` and was kept separate so the requested WAV remains the canonical artifact.

The final file was decoded with FFprobe and confirmed as `pcm_s16le`, 44,100 Hz, two channels, and exactly 115.000000 seconds.
