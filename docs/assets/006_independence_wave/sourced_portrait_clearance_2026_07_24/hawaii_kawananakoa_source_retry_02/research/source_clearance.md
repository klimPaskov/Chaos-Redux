## Identity target

The requested subject is the male historical country leader David Kalākaua Kawānanakoa (1904–1953), the son of David Kawānanakoa (1868–1908). The two identities must not be conflated. Digital Archives of Hawaiʻi entries `ark:70111/1BD0`, `ark:70111/1BCX`, and `ark:70111/1CP4` use the older father's dates and were rejected as wrong-person evidence.

The Commons page for `File:David Kalakaua Kawananakoa.jpg` identifies the target as the 1904–1953 man and attributes the image to the 1925 publication George F. M. Nellist (ed.), *The Story of Hawaii and Its Builders*, Honolulu Star-Bulletin, p. 560, photographer unknown.

## Candidate search record

### Alamy 1937 photograph

The Alamy page and object URL identify the target at age 33 during a November 1937 Honolulu police investigation: <https://www.alamy.com/nephew-of-hawaiis-last-king-detained-following-wifes-death-david-kalakaua-kawananakoa-33-year-old-grandnephew-of-king-david-kalakaua-last-male-monarch-of-hawaii-and-son-of-the-princess-kawananakoa-has-been-detained-by-the-honolulu-police-during-investigations-into-the-death-of-his-asserted-common-law-wife-r-ville-kinslea-the-22-year-old-girl-was-found-dead-with-a-deep-gash-in-the-neck-and-several-facial-lacerations-after-a-party-at-her-home-1-november-1937-2BW2A3C.jpg>. The image is materially sharper and appears to show the correct adult, but the available object has an Alamy watermark and the source is rights-managed stock. No public-domain, Creative Commons, archive release, or direct reuse permission was found, so it cannot be copied or used as the identity master.

### Find a Grave adult uploads

The Find a Grave memorial image `https://images.findagrave.com/photos/2024/64/157092616_8373900f-5e22-4b24-b8f7-8a2ea3db33a1.jpeg` is a strong front-facing adult candidate, and `https://images.findagrave.com/photos/2016/14/157092616_1452868137.jpg` is a useful side/three-quarter candidate. The images are user uploads with no confirmed photographer, publication, archive, or reuse licence. The memorial page was not an ownership record, so both candidates are rejected rather than treated as public domain.

The Find a Grave image `https://images.findagrave.com/photos/2024/59/157092616_3106e6e7-eae3-4cce-a79b-bcfc3da8e65d.jpeg` is only a copy of the already-clipped 1925 Commons portrait and offers no stronger provenance or likeness evidence.

### Public-domain family and archive material

The Commons file `https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Three_Children_Of_The_Late_Prince_And_The_Princess_Kawananakoa,_1908.jpg` is a public-domain *Pacific Commercial Advertiser* image from 1908. It shows the target as a child in a three-child group, so it cannot be used to make the adult leader portrait. It is identity corroboration only.

Digital Archives of Hawaiʻi results `https://digitalarchives.hawaii.gov/item/ark:70111/1BD0`, `https://digitalarchives.hawaii.gov/item/ark:70111/1BCX`, and `https://digitalarchives.hawaii.gov/item/ark:70111/1CP4` identify David Kawānanakoa (1868–1908) or show the older generation. They are not the requested 1904–1953 subject.

Internet Archive scans of period Hawaii books were checked for a sharper adult target portrait, but the reviewed pages either contained text-only mentions, the older father, or no attributable adult image. No additional ownership-clear source was promoted.

## Conclusion

The retry closes with an explicit no-pass. The 1925 Commons source is retained only as exact, rights-documented evidence and must not be promoted. The stronger adult candidates remain blocked by missing reuse rights. A future production attempt requires an adult photograph with both an exact target attribution and a defensible public-domain, archive, or licence chain, plus enough un-clipped facial geometry to pass independent likeness review.
