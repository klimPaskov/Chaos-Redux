# Historical evidence dossier

## Research decision

The task is not to find a visually plausible internet flag. It is to establish which symbol existed, who owned it, what it did, when it was used, and whether its geometry can support either an exact reconstruction or an explicitly fictional Event 006 civic synthesis.

| Package | Strongest evidence | Historical function | Production classification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IW-018 Sardinia | Four Moors flag in the Gelre Armorial; later Sardinian civic, regional, coin, and regimental uses | long-lived Sardinian/Aragonese-derived identity symbol with changing head orientation and bandage treatment | alternate-history 1936 civic synthesis; no single exact 1936 sovereign-island flag established |
| IW-019 Sicily | 1848 Parliamentary decrees and surviving S.015 national flag | national flag of the independent Sicilian revolutionary government, tied to Italian federation and the 1848 constitutional moment | exact surviving-object field reconstruction is possible for a matching constitutional/independence route; not a neutral baseline |
| IW-021 Trieste | 1918 civic flag study; 1947 Peace Treaty; 1947-1954 Zone A/Free Territory use | City of Trieste civic flag, then the legally selected Free Territory flag | exact red-and-white civic reconstruction can support a matching civic/international route after silhouette and proportion review |
| IW-024 Banat | dated regional arms and seals, but no attested 1918 Republic flag | Austrian administrative, Serbian voivodeship, and Romanian state heraldry owned by different political projects | blocked/synthesis-required; no internet reconstruction may be presented as the Banat Republic flag |

## IW-018 Sardinia / ARX

### Official history and dated objects

The [Regione Autonoma della Sardegna history of the emblem](https://www.regione.sardegna.it/regione/identita-visiva/emblemi-istituzionali/storia-dello-stemma) provides the institutional chronology:

- Four Moors first appears in surviving lead seals of the Aragonese royal chancery, with the oldest example dated 1281 under Peter III. Those seals reached Sardinia on royal documents from 1326 onward.
- The Gelre Armorial, compiled in 1370-1386 and held in Brussels, depicts a Four Moors flag for Sardinia. The official page warns that the Sardinian entry may have been added several decades later.
- The Four Moors flag identifies Sardinia in the funeral procession imagery for Charles V after 1558.
- The first secure Sardinian printed attestation is the 1591 Cagliari edition of the Sardinian parliament's military-estate acts.
- The emblem remained in print, maps, buildings, and coins through Spanish and Savoyard periods and became a nineteenth-century identity symbol.

The saved [Gelre folio 62r file page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Page_out_of_the_Armorial_Gelre._Folio_62r_de_l%27Armorial_de_Gelre.png) shows the small banner as a white field crossed red with four black profile heads, one per quarter. The two heads on the hoist side face right and the two on the fly side face left, so all four face inward toward the cross. The drawing does not show forehead bands. This is a dated armorial depiction, not evidence of a uniform 1936 layout.

The Italian Ministry of Culture catalogs two useful Sardinian objects:

- A [post-1657 to 1699 Cagliari cathedral painting](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/2000046335), catalog code 2000046335, depicts a red St George cross on white with four black, bandaged heads apparently facing inward. The catalog notes that the eyes remain visible and the bands appear on the forehead. Function: municipal/heraldic painting, not a state flag. Cultural attribution: Sardinian workshop. Rights: catalog metadata CC BY 4.0; no object image was copied.
- A [1809 Arborea provincial cavalry standard](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0100216743), catalog code 0100216743 and inventory O.110, uses the Sardinian arms as its field: red St George cross on white with four Moor heads bandaged white on the forehead. A Savoy eagle, crown, stars, border, and regimental devices make the object a Sardinian provincial cavalry colour, not a neutral island flag. Manufacturer: Cagliari workshop. Conserved by Musei Reali-Armeria Reale. Rights: catalog metadata CC BY 4.0; no object image was copied.

### Modern legal form is not a 1936 source

The [official Sardinian emblems page](https://www.regione.sardegna.it/regione/identita-visiva/emblemi-istituzionali) defines the current flag as a white field crossed red, with one forehead-bandaged Moor head in each quarter facing away from the hoist. The current direction was fixed by Regional Law no. 10 of 15 April 1999. It is authoritative for the modern flag but must not be called the exact 1936 layout.

The saved `sardinia_traditional_four_moors_reference.png` is a 2022 CC0 reconstruction whose heads face the hoist and whose bands cover the eyes. It is retained because it is distributable and makes the historical variation obvious. It is not accepted as primary evidence for 1936.

### Geometry, colour, count, and orientation finding

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Period function | Sardinian identity and heraldic banner with royal, parliamentary, municipal, coin, and military applications; no independent 1936 island-state flag established |
| Geometry | white field; red cross reaching the edges; four head profiles in the quarters |
| Symbol count | one cross and four heads |
| Orientation | varies by dated source: Gelre and the seventeenth-century cataloged painting face inward; the modern 1999 law faces every head away from the hoist; the saved self-published pre-1999 reconstruction faces every head toward the hoist |
| Band treatment | absent in the Gelre drawing; forehead bands with eyes open in the seventeenth-century cataloged painting and 1809 standard; blindfolded in the modern reconstruction aid |
| Colour evidence | white, red, and black are secure; exact digital red and exact skin/line treatment are not secure for 1936 |
| Exact proportions | not established by the consulted period sources |
| Owner | changes across Aragonese royal, Sardinian parliamentary/identity, Savoyard, municipal, and modern regional contexts |

### Source conclusion

The Four Moors is safe as the researched Sardinian motif. A generated Event 006 baseline must be described as a **fictional 1936 Sardinian civic synthesis**, not as a recovered historical flag. Combining the inward-facing, forehead-bandaged seventeenth-century treatment with a simplified flag field is defensible only as declared alternate history. Savoy arms, an Italian tricolour, and the SPM flag family are excluded from the neutral ARX baseline.

## IW-019 Sicily / ASX

### Primary archive and surviving national flag

The [Archivio di Stato di Palermo collection of 1848 Parliamentary decrees](https://digitalibrary-saassipa.cultura.gov.it/entities/archivalmaterial/20749410-4367-4365-9f75-33392164d6ae), shelfmark Miscellanea Archivistica, Serie I, no. 107, fasc. 1, records:

- 28 March 1848: Trinacria ordered as the arms of Sicily;
- 1 April 1848: three Sicilian national flags ordered for Rome, Piedmont, and Tuscany;
- 25 April 1848: Trinacria ordered onto the national flag.

The archive scan is all-rights-reserved and reproduction requires Ministry permission, so it remains link-only.

The Italian Ministry of Culture catalog records the surviving [1848 Sicilian national flag S.015](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0100215963), catalog code 0100215963:

- rectangular Italian tricolour arranged vertically, green at the hoist, ecru/white in the centre, and red at the fly;
- one Trinacria embroidered in gold at the centre of the white panel;
- recto and verso identical;
- gold fringe, tassels, and a separate tricolour cravat with the words addressed to Piedmont;
- manufactured in Sicily in 1848 and sent to Piedmont under the 1 April parliamentary decree;
- conserved by Musei Reali-Armeria Reale, state property;
- catalog metadata CC BY 4.0; no catalog image was copied.

The flag's political function matters. It is an independent Sicilian national flag explicitly connected to the 1848 government's Italian federation policy and its constitutional-revolutionary moment. It is not a Bourbon restoration flag and not a politically neutral island emblem.

### Modern flag is post-2000

The [Regione Siciliana flag history](https://www.regione.sicilia.it/istituzioni/i-simboli-della-regione/la-bandiera) states that Sicily's red-yellow diagonal regional flag was officially adopted only by Regional Law no. 1 of 4 January 2000. The [law](https://www.regione.sicilia.it/istituzioni/regione/strutture-regionali/presidenza-regione/ufficio-rappresentanza-cerimoniale/normativa/legge-4) specifies a 2:3 rectangle with a flesh-coloured Triscele/Gorgoneion/wheat emblem whose height is three fifths of the flag height. That exact red-yellow layout is modern evidence and is excluded as an authentic 1936 flag.

### Saved reconstruction caveat

The saved `sicily_1848_national_flag_reference.svg` is a CC BY-SA 4.0 modern vector. It supplies a convenient three-panel layout and a legible Trinacria silhouette, but its fills include flesh and gold. That conflicts with the surviving S.015 catalog description, which says the central Trinacria is embroidered in gold. The dated physical object controls an exact-object reconstruction.

### Geometry, colour, count, and orientation finding

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Period function | national flag of the independent 1848-1849 Sicilian revolutionary government; surviving S.015 specifically sent to Piedmont |
| Geometry | rectangular vertical tricolour, green at hoist, ecru/white centre, red at fly; one central Trinacria |
| Symbol count | one Trinacria on the centre panel |
| Orientation | the accessible catalog metadata does not prescribe the rotational direction or exact linework of the three legs; the surviving object or a rights-cleared museum image must decide that detail |
| Colour evidence | green, ecru/white, red, and gold are secure for S.015; exact digital values are not specified |
| Exact proportions | not present in the accessible object metadata; the modern vector's 3:2 canvas is an illustrator normalization, not proof of the object's ratio |
| Attachments | gold fringe, tassels, and inscribed cravat belong to the ceremonial object; omitting them produces a flat-field normalization and must be documented as such |
| Owner | General Parliament/Government of the independent Sicilian state of 1848 |

### Source conclusion

An exact **S.015 flat-field normalization** can be commissioned for a constitutional or 1848-independence revival route: vertical green/ecru/red field, one gold Trinacria centred on the ecru panel, no modern red-yellow diagonal. It must not be silently used for traditional crown, labor, military, or patron-client routes, and it must not be described as a neutral 1936 baseline without an explicit political decision.

## IW-021 Trieste Free State / ICX

### Period civic flag and Free Territory law

Mario Fabretto's specialist paper, [Flags of Friuli-Venezia Giulia](https://fiav.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/17-22-Fabretto-FlagsOfFriuli-VeneziaGiulia.pdf), Proceedings of the XVII International Congress of Vexillology, states:

- Trieste's old traditional flag was probably similar to the present one: a white corsesca, commonly called a halberd, on red;
- after annexation to Italy in 1918, the civic flag was red with a white halberd;
- that civic flag was used by Zone A after the Second World War;
- Plate 45, figure 20 depicts a plain red rectangle with one centred, slender white corsesca: long narrow upper spear, two symmetric upward-curving side blades around a small joint, and a short straight lower shaft.

This directly supports a 1918-1936 civic flag. The paper is copyrighted 1999 and was not copied into the repo.

The [Treaty of Peace with Italy](https://treaties.un.org/doc/publication/UNTS/Volume%2049/v49.pdf), signed in Paris on 10 February 1947 and in force from 15 September 1947, provides the primary legal rule. Annex VI, Article 8 requires the Free Territory to use the traditional flag of the City of Trieste and its historic arms. The [London Memorandum of 5 October 1954](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%20235/v235.pdf) ended the two military governments and transferred civil administration. For this asset packet, 15 September 1947 to 5 October 1954 is the treaty-defined Free Territory/Zone A reference period.

The current [Comune di Trieste statute](https://documenti.comune.trieste.it/trasparenza/NUOVO_STATUTO_2016_OK.pdf) defines the historic civic arms as a silver alabarda on red. A 2020 [municipal procurement record](https://bandieconcorsi.comune.trieste.it/contenuti/allegati/DX2420.pdf) orders Trieste flags as a white alabarda on red in both 70x100 cm and 100x150 cm sizes. It confirms the subject and demonstrates that current practice does not provide one unique aspect ratio for historical reconstruction.

### Distinguish the Austrian civic-building flag

The Italian Ministry of Culture catalogs a separate [1875-1899 public-building flag of the City of Trieste](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0100215974), catalog code 0100215974 and inventory K.108:

- Habsburg horizontal red-white-red field;
- full City of Trieste arms centred on the white stripe, including eagle and alabarda;
- Triestine manufacture, recto and verso, public-building function;
- delivered by the mayor of Trieste to the Royal Armoury on 30 December 1920;
- catalog metadata CC BY 4.0; no catalog image was copied.

This object proves a different Austrian-period civic function. It must not be conflated with the plain 1918 civic flag or the 1947 Free Territory flag.

### Saved reconstruction caveat

The saved `trieste_free_territory_flag_reference.svg` uses red `#e31317` and one white, very elongated alabarda. Its Commons history contains an unresolved argument over whether that silhouette is the historical Free Territory form or a modern city stylization. It is therefore a redistributable subject/colour aid only. Fabretto Plate 45, figure 20 controls the historic production silhouette; neither source supplies an authoritative digital red value.

### Geometry, colour, count, and orientation finding

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Period function | City of Trieste civic flag adopted after 1918; Zone A flag; legally selected Free Territory flag in 1947 |
| Geometry | plain field; one centred vertical corsesca/halberd; no shield, eagle, crown, stripes, text, or UN emblem |
| Symbol count | one white/silver corsesca |
| Orientation | vertical and upright; long spear point above, symmetric upward-curving side blades, short shaft below |
| Colour evidence | red field and white/silver device secure; no authoritative digital red located |
| Exact proportions | specialist figure is approximately 3:2 but does not state a ratio; current municipal procurement uses both 7:10 and 2:3 |
| Owner | City of Trieste for the 1918 civic flag; Zone A/Free Territory for the 1947-1954 application |

### Source conclusion

The plain red-and-white civic flag is the strongest exact reconstruction candidate in this tranche and is period-valid for Trieste in 1936. Production still needs ImageGen, a checked silhouette based on Fabretto figure 20, an explicitly documented working-ratio normalization, and 10x7 readability review. No modern municipal logo, Habsburg full arms, or UN emblem should be added.

## IW-024 Banat / AXX

### No attested 1918 Banat Republic flag located

No authoritative archive, museum catalog, dated object, official history, or specialist article consulted in this tranche attested a flag of the 1918 Banat Republic. This is a negative research result, not proof that no flag ever existed. It is sufficient to reject uncited internet reconstructions as historical evidence.

The negative-evidence set included:

- Drăgan-George Basarabă, [About the Coat of Arms of Banat: Heraldic Certainties and Assumptions](https://biblioteca-digitala.ro/reviste/ACTA_TERRAE_FOGARASIENSIS/06_ACTA_TERRAE_FOGARASIENSIS_VI_2017_157.pdf), *Acta Terrae Fogarasiensis* VI (2017), a specialist survey of Banat heraldry;
- Jovana Kasas, [Conditions in Timisoara at the End of 1918 and a Previously Unpublished Document of the Serbs of Timisoara](https://istrazivanja.ff.uns.ac.rs/index.php/istr/article/view/1713), University of Novi Sad, DOI 10.19090/i.2013.24.391-400, which surveys archival evidence from the 1918 political transition but supplies no Republic flag;
- the Romanian National Heritage digital collection [Faurirea statului national unitar roman: contributii documentare banatene, 1914-1919](https://biblioteca-digitala.ro/?pub=7960-faurirea-statului-national-unitar-roman-contributii-documentare-banatene-1914-1919);
- museum and community-flag literature describing Serbian institutional flags in Banat, none of which is a Banat Republic national flag.

The internet design often labelled as a Banat Republic flag is therefore excluded. It must not be reconstructed, redrawn, or described as attested.

### Dated motifs with explicit political owners

Basarabă's study identifies two durable Banat heraldic pillars, the lion and the Forforosa Gate, but their historical functions differ:

1. **Banat of Timisoara gate, 1717/1718-1779.** The first arms for the whole historical Banat after the Austrian conquest derive from a 1717 German seal of Timisoara: a fortress gate with two towers and palisade. An 1817 Casa Savoia-Carignano medallion reduces the two towers to one. The only surviving period colour evidence described by the study is blue sky, red tower, green ground, natural brown palisade and bridge, and blue river. Eighteenth-century full arms add an imperial crown, armed eagle supporters, the motto `PROVIDENTIA AUGUSTORUM`, and confessional-imperial imagery. Function: Austrian crown-land administration, not a neutral Republic flag.
2. **Serbian Voivodeship and Banat of Timisoara quarter, 1849-1860.** Red field with one gold rampant lion, sword in the right forepaw and a severed Ottoman head in the left, copied from the Great Kikinda District arms of 1774. Function: Serbian/imperial voivodeship heraldry, not a neutral all-Banat standard.
3. **Romanian royal arms, 1921-1947.** The third quarter described by Royal Decree no. 3573 of 23 July 1921 is red over natural waves, with a gold two-arched Trajan bridge and one gold lion emerging. Basarabă records the author as Jozsef Sebestyen de Kapeni and explains that the lion is the older element and the bridge the new Severin/Trajan element. Function: Romanian state heraldry for Banat with the old Romanian Banat of Severin. The sword is a 1992 addition and must not appear in a 1921 or 1936 reconstruction.

The saved `banat_1921_romanian_arms_reference.jpg` is a National Archives of Romania scan licensed CC BY-SA 2.5. It shows the whole royal arms. It is not a Banat flag and is retained only to inspect the period quarter in context.

### Geometry, colour, count, and orientation finding

| Candidate motif | Geometry and count | Colour evidence | Date/function | Production restriction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Forforosa Gate | fortress gate, two towers and palisade; later one-tower variant; bridge/river landscape | blue sky/river, red tower, green ground, brown timber in the surviving medallion | Austrian Banat administration, 1717/1718-1779; later 1817 medallion | stripping crown, eagles, motto, and imperial imagery creates a fictional synthesis and requires explicit approval |
| 1849 lion | one gold rampant lion, sword right, severed head left, on red | red and gold | Serbian Voivodeship/Banat quarter, 1849-1860 | ethnopolitical and violent; never neutral baseline |
| 1921 bridge-and-lion | one gold lion emerging from one gold two-arched bridge over natural waves, on red | red, gold, natural water | Romanian royal state quarter, 1921-1947 | Romanian/Severin-owned; no sword before 1992; using it as a flag is a generated route synthesis |
| 1918 Republic flag | none attested in consulted sources | none | none established | blocked; do not use an internet reconstruction |

### Source conclusion

Banat remains **blocked/synthesis-required**. The source researcher does not select an ethnic or imperial owner on the parent's behalf. A later fictional multiethnic civic flag may use a simplified administrative gate only after the parent explicitly approves removal of its Austrian crown-land apparatus and accepts that the result is alternate history. The 1921 bridge-and-lion may support a deliberately Romanian-aligned route, never the neutral baseline. No Banat ImageGen prompt may call its result historical.
