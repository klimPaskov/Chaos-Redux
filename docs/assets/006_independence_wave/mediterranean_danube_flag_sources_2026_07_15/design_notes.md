# Event 006 Mediterranean and Danube flag design notes

## Decision boundary

The Event 006 package registry and accepted asset resolution place all four packages in the generated-period-civic-baseline workflow:

> Create a period civic flag from researched regional motifs. Do not use generated text, generic sacred symbols, or an invented historical flag presented as authentic.

That instruction does not turn every researched motif into a neutral baseline. A design must still match the polity, route, date, and historical function. These notes therefore distinguish exact-object reconstruction, declared alternate-history synthesis, route-specific evidence, and unresolved design work.

**Nothing in this packet is a content-ready flag or country package.** Every eventual runtime flag still requires an ImageGen pass, human review against the cited evidence, the complete HOI4 size ladder, manifest/contact-sheet work, and parent-owned integration.

## Package decisions

### IW-018 Sardinia / ARX

The securely recurring elements are a white field, an edge-reaching red St George cross, and four black-profile Moor heads, one in each quarter. The dated sources do not establish one invariant 1936 orientation, band treatment, proportion, or digital red. The proposed baseline is therefore not an exact reconstruction.

If approved, the least anachronistic civic synthesis would combine the simple flag field with the inward-facing, forehead-bandaged, eyes-visible treatment supported by the seventeenth-century Cagliari object. It must be labelled in manifests and documentation as a **fictional 1936 Sardinian civic synthesis based on attested motifs**. It must not be labelled a historical 1936 Sardinian state flag.

Route ownership:

| Route | Research-safe direction | Unresolved requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| constitutional | simple Four Moors civic synthesis | parent approval of inward-facing/forehead-band treatment and field normalization |
| traditional crown | no neutral assumption | identify the actual claimant/dynasty before selecting crown or dynastic arms; Savoy/SPM is excluded unless the route explicitly owns it |
| labor | no generic recolour or hammer-and-sickle | locate a Sardinian labor, miners', port, municipal, or cooperative institution whose symbol is licensable and route-appropriate |
| military | no fasces, Italian state eagle, or generic sword overlay | locate a Sardinian military/civic institution or commission a declared fictional local device after approval |

### IW-019 Sicily / ASX

The surviving 1848 S.015 flag provides a strong exact-object basis for a constitutional-independence route: vertical green, ecru/white, and red bands with green at the hoist and one gold Trinacria centered on the middle band. The ceremonial fringe, tassels, cravat, and text are parts of the surviving colour, not flat-field requirements. Its precise original ratio and Trinacria rotation were not established from the accessible catalog record.

The S.015 design should not become an ideology-neutral baseline by default. Its function belongs to the 1848 revolutionary government and its Italian-federation policy. The modern red/yellow diagonal Sicilian flag dates to 2000 and is not a 1936 source.

Route ownership:

| Route | Research-safe direction | Unresolved requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| constitutional | flat-field normalization of S.015 | approve normalized ratio and obtain/approve the precise all-gold Trinacria silhouette and leg rotation |
| traditional crown | no neutral Bourbon/TTS carry-over | identify the claimant and political programme before using dynastic material |
| labor | no generic red recolour, star, or hammer-and-sickle | source a real Sicilian labor, peasant, municipal, or port institution |
| military | no fasces or Italian royal/state emblem | source a route-owned Sicilian formation or approve a declared fictional civic-military device |
| patron client | no design until patron is known | patron identity and relationship must be fixed before research and commissioning |

### IW-021 Trieste Free State / ICX

The best period-matching candidate is the plain City of Trieste civic flag: red field with one centered white/silver corsesca. Fabretto identifies this as the civic form after 1918 and the later Zone A/Free Territory form; the 1947 Peace Treaty explicitly selects the traditional city flag for the Free Territory. In registry chronology, the 1918-1936 civic use is the relevant pre-war basis, while the treaty confirms continuity of function rather than creating the motif.

The corsesca is a slim vertical spear with two upward-curving side blades, a compact central joint, and a short lower shaft. It should be matched to Fabretto Plate 45, figure 20, not inferred solely from the elongated licensed Commons SVG. A working field proportion and exact digital red will be production normalizations unless better dated evidence is approved.

Route ownership:

| Route | Research-safe direction | Unresolved requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| international commission | plain red civic flag with white/silver corsesca | review final silhouette, normalized field ratio, and digital red; do not add a UN emblem |
| constitutional | same civic flag can serve the city polity | review as above; route distinction should not be manufactured by a generic overlay |
| labor | no generic red-field ideological mark | source a Triestine dockworker, railway, cooperative, or municipal labor symbol before commissioning a variant |
| patron client | no design until patron is known | identify patron, treaty relationship, and whether civic continuity remains |

### IW-024 Banat / AXX

No attested flag was located in the consulted authoritative sources for the 1918 Banat Republic. This is a scoped research finding, not a claim that no evidence can ever exist. Internet reconstructions must not be used or described as historical.

The researched motifs belong to different projects:

- the 1717/1718 Habsburg Banat of Timișoara arms use a fortress/gate complex with imperial and confessional apparatus;
- the 1849-1860 Serbian Voivodeship and Banat device uses a gold lion on red with a sword and severed Ottoman head;
- the 1921 Romanian state quarter uses a two-arched Trajan bridge and an emerging lion, without the sword later added in 1992.

None is a neutral 1918 republican flag. The package remains blocked until the parent approves a declared alternate-history strategy:

| Possible strategy | Historical ownership | Safe use boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| simplified Forforosa Gate | derived from Habsburg Banat administrative heraldry | possible constitutional-federal or international civic synthesis only if crown, eagles, motto, eye/confessional device, and imperial claims are removed; must be labelled fictional |
| bridge and emerging lion | Romanian state/Severin heraldic tradition, securely visible in 1921 state arms | Romanian-aligned route only; never a neutral multiethnic baseline; no post-1992 sword |
| sword-and-severed-head lion | Serbian Voivodeship/Great Kikinda tradition | politically specific and unsuitable for the neutral package; not approved here |

Route ownership:

| Route | Status | Requirement before commissioning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| constitutional federal | blocked | parent approval of an explicitly fictional, politically neutralized gate synthesis and a written ownership rationale |
| international commission | blocked | same gate decision plus an institution/treaty identity; no generic international logo |
| labor | blocked | locate a real Banat industrial, railway, municipal, cooperative, or workers' symbol and establish ethnic/political ownership |
| military | blocked | identify the route's army and sponsor; Romanian and Serbian heraldry cannot be neutralized by recolouring |

## Production constraints for the later asset pass

- Use ImageGen for each final visual asset, including exact historical reconstructions. The saved files are reference inputs, not runtime assets.
- Produce a single flat orthographic flag per source image. No pole, waving, folds, cloth texture, lighting, shadow, bevel, mockup, caption, date, watermark, or contact sheet in the generated frame.
- Keep symbols thick, separated, and legible enough to survive the eventual HOI4 ladder: 82x52, 41x26, and 10x7 pixels.
- Preserve symbol count and orientation. Do not let ImageGen add heads, crowns, stars, weapons, sacred symbols, text, seals, laurels, borders, or ornamental heraldry.
- Record every normalized choice, including aspect ratio, digital colours, silhouette cleanup, and small-size simplification. A normalization is not historical evidence.
- Route variants require their own sourced ownership case. Recolours and generic ideology overlays are not substitutes for research.
- Every retained CC BY-SA-derived intermediate or reference relationship must keep attribution and share-alike obligations visible in the later asset manifest.
