# Flat flag-design dispositions

These are research-grounded directions for a later flag artist. They are not historical flag claims and must not be rendered as fabric, painterly art, or a copied historical SVG. The final package should use flat fields, clean geometry, limited colors, and no text. Every proposed symbol below is intentionally abstract and route-specific.

## Symbol attribution ledger

- **Bolgar archaeology / Islamic heritage:** UNESCO only. Use a simplified arch, minaret, masonry, or river form as a restoration-route reference; never claim an ancient flag.
- **Volga-Ural federalism:** Tatarica institutional history only. Equal modules and river geometry are design abstractions, not a recovered 1918 flag.
- **Idel-Ural tamga / blue reconstruction:** FOTW and Wikimedia are contested secondary references. Do not copy or describe the reconstruction as authentic.
- **Assyrian movement flags:** AssyrianFlag.org documents 1916/1933-era movement designs and three community/church distinctions, but the archive’s image rights and retrospective interpretation are not clear enough for direct reuse. The 1973 AUA design is explicitly post-1936 and excluded.
- **Church symbols:** Church of the East, Chaldean Catholic, and Syriac Orthodox references are institution-specific. No cross, seal, or ecclesiastical heraldry may be silently promoted to all-Assyrian or all-Mesopotamian identity.
- **Levies:** Hansard, British Library, and Newcastle records establish the institution and period. They do not clear any badge or uniform artwork. Use abstract guard geometry only unless a later source review clears an exact unit mark.
- **Mesopotamian heritage:** UNESCO Mosul and Iraq Museum references support civic river/urban motifs. Do not use ancient imperial emblems or modern Iraqi state flags as shortcuts.
