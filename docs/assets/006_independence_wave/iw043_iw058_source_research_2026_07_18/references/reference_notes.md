# Reference notes

The notes below are paraphrases for prompt and provenance work; they are not reproduced page text.

## Volga / Bolgar / Idel-Ural

- UNESCO’s Bolgar entry places the site on the Volga south of the Kama confluence, describes a 7th–15th-century city and a 922 adoption of Islam, and emphasizes cultural exchange, continuity, diversity, trade, and Tatar Muslim pilgrimage. The entry describes archaeological/reconstructed features but does not establish a historical flag. Its authenticity section cautions that reconstruction can reduce authenticity.
- Tatarica’s Idel-Ural committee entry describes a January 1918 committee created by the Milli Mejlis. The planned representation included Russians, Chuvash, and Mari and aimed at negotiation, a temporary constitution, and a provisional government. The Idel-Ural State entry describes equality of nations, languages, and religions, shared federal competencies, national administrations, and contested Tatar/Bashkir politics. These are institutional grounds for equal-member geometry, not a recovered flag.
- Tatarstan National Library’s flag page records a 1991 green/white/red flag and modern color meanings. It is useful only as a modern regional palette reference and is not a 1936 Volga Bulgaria or Idel-Ural source.
- FOTW and the Wikimedia Idel-Ural SVG discuss a blue/tamga reconstruction, but the provenance is contested and the SVG is a modern uploader reconstruction. It must never be copied or labelled authentic.

## Assyrian, Chaldean, Syriac, and Aramean distinctions

- Encyclopaedia Iranica describes modern Assyrian usage as applying to East Syrian Christian communities while also explaining the historical and confessional uses of Chaldean, Syriac, and related names. The same sources distinguish East Syriac and Catholic Chaldean institutions and warn against treating names as one-to-one identities.
- The Assyrian Church of the East reference states that the Cross is its sole venerated symbol. That is a church-specific disposition and cannot become a universal Assyrian council flag without a route-specific decision.
- AssyrianFlag.org documents early-20th-century / 1933 movement flags and describes three stars as Assyrian, Syriac, and Chaldean. The archive’s reuse terms and retrospective interpretation are not sufficiently clear for direct copying. Its modern AUA flag process was in 1968–1973 and is excluded from a 1936 baseline.
- The modern Syriac Orthodox emblem on Wikimedia and current Chaldean Patriarchate material are attribution-sensitive institutional references, not Event 006 state flags.

## Levies and Mosul

- The 1932 UK Parliament Hansard record describes roughly 1,500 Assyrian Levies, British senior officers, aerodrome guarding, and grievances concerning Assyrian autonomy and minority guarantees. British Library catalogue material covers 1919–1921 recruitment/training and Assyrian refugee enlistment. A Newcastle University catalogue image shows a line of Levies inspected by British officers in Baqubah in 1920. None of these sources clears a badge or flag for reuse.
- UNESCO’s Old City of Mosul and related Revive Mosul documentation emphasize the Tigris, the city opposite Nineveh, diverse Islamic and Christian built heritage, bridges, and urban recovery. These are good grounds for civic river/bridge geometry on `MESOPOTAMIAN_FEDERATIONX`, not for ancient imperial emblems.
- The Hashemite official history page records historic Iraqi colors and two seven-pointed stars for 1921–1958. This is a historical-context reference only; FORM18 must not copy the Hashemite/Iraqi flag or imply Arab monarchy.

## Mar Eshai Shimun XXIII

- The Mar Shimun Memorial Foundation biography supports identity, title, birth date, patriarchal ordination, political role, and 1933 exile. Its “Mar Eshai 1920” photo entry is potentially useful for crop planning but has no clear reuse permission in the reviewed page.
- Qatar Digital Library/British Library material is later (1945) and useful only for identity corroboration. The LOC “Ishai d'Mar Shimun” image is a different older family member and is explicitly excluded.
