## Ownership and activation

Gods of Africa is an Event 012 subsystem owned by the African unifier created by Africa Is One, with no normal-event pool entry or unrestricted recurring world scan.
Whichever occurs second of continental formation and Evolution I starts the same guarded, host-owned 180-day consolidation interval.
Both owners call `africa_gods_of_africa_begin_consolidation`; the initializer rechecks authority, physical founding proof, host generation, and chronology, while the pending-clock receipt prevents repeated calls from restarting the interval.
The activation callback rechecks those permissions and the elapsed due date before committing ordinary tribute.

The `SCN-011` Africa Is One route seeds first-proof success and Evolution I chronology without replaying first-proof or Evolution I rewards, actions, achievements, or ordinary reports, then calls the same initializer.
An applied `World Is One` scenario is a post-security starting package and cannot indirectly start Gods consolidation or tribute through a later Evolution I call.
The applied scenario must have `africa_triggerable_scenario_type = constant:triggerable_scenario_africa_type.world_is_one`; the mere presence of `africa_triggerable_scenario_applied` is insufficient.

`africa_continental_settlement_complete` records initial continental formation, not final security.
Ordinary final security requires a successfully settled Scramble with no `africa_scramble_response_defeat`; a defeated Scramble's settled enum is insufficient.
`gods_of_africa_continent_is_secured` also recognizes the exact applied World Is One type as its declared post-security exception.
If an already authorized consolidation or active owner reaches final security, it settles retained enrollment history instead of opening or continuing ordinary demands.
The source contract is in `012_africa_gods_epoch_binding_triggers.txt`, the formation/evolution/scenario callers, and `gods_of_africa_commit_final_settlement`; native activation timing and settlement execution remain unobserved.

The committed host owns one bounded participant registry, the global Gods of Africa Strength value, the dispatch cursor, demand budget, doctrine, priority family, and ending lock. Participant countries own their personal Wrath, frozen demand contract, payment progress, defiance state, and hidden relationship history. A restoration generation prevents delayed work from resolving against a previous host.

Ordinary current-generation authority additionally requires the country to be in that host's live participant array and the shared host pointer to identify an existing committed unifier.
Matching flags or generation numbers on a nonmember cannot permit an ordinary transaction.
Final-settlement and victor reports use their separate frozen historical receipts, so this live-membership condition does not suppress completed presentation after the roster is cleared.

## Bounded census and host-transfer persistence

The one-time Event 012 census retains known ordinary country scopes from vanilla's `global.countries` array in `gods_of_africa_candidate_registry`, with one unique row per native country object.
The candidate pool includes minors, capitulated countries, and landless tags so later eligibility does not require a recurring world scan.
The unifier and special or actual nonhuman actors are excluded.
Actual enrollment separately requires an existing, non-capitulated major or player country.

`gods_of_africa_remember_candidate` inserts a scope once and advances the candidate count only for a new entry below the cap.
Release, puppet, peace-conference, and government-change callbacks remember the supplied countries after the census, including before Gods activation.
The government-change callback also covers the two sides created by a civil war.
`gods_of_africa_initialize_registry` enrolls eligible candidates after consolidation.
The private `.607` clock alone owns later 90-day reviews, while its due-date guard ignores early or duplicate deliveries without consuming the scheduled receipt.
Review archives invalid participants from a separate snapshot, releases their active-demand budget, then checks the maintained candidate pool for later major or player eligibility.
The snapshot is cleared after each review, and the 30-day owner tick does not run a second registry review.
These are source findings within the user's bounded-enrollment goal, while runtime cadence and enrollment remain validation items.

During host succession, `africa_host_transfer_stage_scalar_ledger` stages the old host's generation, Gods candidate count, census flag, and candidate array alongside the wider Event 012 ledger. `africa_host_transfer_restore_world_order_state` rebuilds the successor's candidate array and census state before the successor closes staging, while generation guards prevent delayed callbacks from resolving against the old host. A suspended transfer intentionally retains its inspectable staging docket until the transfer is resolved; this persistence is source evidence and remains bounded by the transfer lifecycle.

On annexation, the Event 012 world-order `on_annex` bridge calls `africa_begin_host_transfer` for the current host while the annexed country remains available as `FROM`; that effect invokes `gods_of_africa_cancel_on_unifier_loss` before changing the shared host pointer. The unifier's `on_capitulation` callback invokes the same cleanup. It removes live participant and contract state, removes their missions, withdraws owned protection services, closes the scheduled-clock receipts, clears the active registry, and sets public Strength to zero. Host-transfer staging carries the bounded candidate census for a successor but does not move the active Gods participant registry or public Strength. The initializer and activation trigger reject the old generation's loss receipt, so a delayed `.600` cannot restart its former authority. Returning participants retain Wrath, conduct counters, and aligned kind/day history; first enrollment initializes that ledger once. Permanent opt-out survives the cancellation of live authority and is restored to live defiance on re-entry.

## Public values

The player sees exactly two values:

- Gods of Africa Strength is a global value from 0 to 100. It combines African military readiness, industry and logistics, continental control, strategic reach, internal condition, Chaos, and Event 012 development. Ordinary refreshes move at most `strength_smoothing_step` (8) points; major defeat or recovery may request an immediate recalculation. Its five bands set the highest punishment tier the system can credibly attempt.
  Sustainment (decision L29): the military component is multiplied by `gods_of_africa_strength_sustain.floor` (0.4) plus `span` (0.6) times the weighted share of the army's rifles against their target (`num_equipment_in_armies_k@infantry_equipment` over `num_target_equipment_in_armies_k@infantry_equipment`, weight 0.6) and of fuel against tank capacity (`fuel_k` over `max_fuel_k`, weight 0.4), so an unsupplied or under-equipped army scores below a supplied one of the same size; the factor is kept in `gods_of_africa_strength_sustain_factor`.
  While an enemy holds Africa's capital, Strength loses `capital_loss_penalty` (15) points, and the first loss is applied at once (`gods_of_africa_strength_capital_loss_recorded`). Surrender progress above 0.1, 0.25 and 0.4 costs 5, 12 and 20 points, and above `surrender_immediate` (0.25) every refresh applies the new value at once, so Strength falls quickly as Africa nears capitulation.
- Wrath of the Gods is a 0 to 100 value stored separately for each participant. Compliance, meaningful aid, refusal, failed deadlines, war, occupation, treaty breaches, and defiance move it through one central transaction helper. War, occupied African territory, broken treaties, permanent defiance, and recent extreme punishment reapply hidden floors after every transaction.

Capacity, African needs, conduct history, demand repetition, punishment intent, and settlement history remain hidden. The interface communicates them through qualitative demand language, reports, tooltips, diplomatic memory, and outcomes rather than a third meter.

## Tribute loop

After activation, every eligible major or player in the maintained candidate pool is enrolled once, excluding the unifier and invalid or special actors.
Candidate and participant capacity use the native `global.countries^num` cardinality and their actual array sizes rather than a fixed tag-count ceiling or scalar bookkeeping count.
Only native country objects can enter either deduplicated registry, and registration requires successful durable candidate membership before creating live authority or history.
Every genuinely enrolled actor therefore remains in the final census after archival; newly allocated countries use the same bounded callback contract.
The 90-day registry clock handles later major or player changes, while transaction callbacks can enroll a newly eligible country immediately.
Each participant receives an introduction and a deterministic first-demand delay.
A participant can have only one active ordinary demand, and the owner dispatch budget staggers notices across the registry.

Recent repetition tracks actual consecutive committed demand family, with separate truck/artillery identity, rather than the number of relationship-history entries.
Matching demands within the configured 365-day gap increment the private streak; a different family/type or a longer gap starts at one.
The existing repetition threshold and percentage adjustment remain unchanged, and negotiation, aid, failed quotes, partial payments, and substitute responses cannot advance or replay that contract receipt.
The precise contract and source scenarios are in `common/scripted_effects/012_africa_gods_repetition_effects.md` and `012_africa_gods_repetition_2026-10-03.md`.

Demand creation freezes a family, readable amount, deadline, generation, contract identity, and status. `gods_of_africa_calculate_demand_amount` scales the amount and selects the contract band from current capacity, Strength, Wrath, Chaos, need, doctrine, conduct, and repetition, while `gods_of_africa_calculate_demand_deadline` resolves the matching constant-backed deadline. Reciprocal Covenant retains its 90% amount scale, Sovereign Exaction retains its 120% scale, and Strategic Ambiguity extends each frozen deadline by 30 days without changing the quoted amount. The `gods_of_africa_demand` constants include explicit ceremonial, standard, severe, emergency, and `punitive_band` values plus their deadline constants, including `deadline_punitive`; this is source evidence for conduct-aware escalation, not a balance approval. The family is selected from current African needs—land equipment, support equipment, fuel, manpower, industry, emergency relief, transport reserves, aircraft reserves, or a controlled steel-bearing state—and, after the corresponding focus capability is unlocked, can also request recognition, the return of named African core states, an end to support for a named enemy, or (after Security Oaths) military access, a non-aggression pact or a guarantee of Africa; see Diplomatic demands below. The strategic-resource lane uses `gods_of_africa_transaction.resources_amount` as a live state-output threshold and pays through the documented `give_resource_rights` effect; it does not fake a stockpile debit, and the participant's rights receipt prevents the same concession from being demanded repeatedly in the same host generation. Those diplomatic lanes require the relevant target condition and political-power capacity and are described under Diplomatic demands. When all maintained need flags and focus-gated diplomatic conditions are clear, the owner returns without forming a contract, so the default priority cannot create an arbitrary demand during a healthy interval. The emergency lane requires an initialized debt variable above the high-debt threshold, or above the medium-debt threshold after the needs-profile focus is completed; an absent debt variable is treated as a healthy zero during partial restoration. `gods_of_africa_set_payment_capacity_requirement`, `gods_of_africa_set_substitute_capacity_requirement`, and `gods_of_africa_set_partial_capacity_requirement` call `gods_of_africa_compute_capacity_requirement` to keep the public transfer amount separate from a hidden requirement that adds the family-specific operating floor for equipment, fuel, manpower, or industry. The capacity and affordability triggers consume those hidden requirements with the current-generation contract guards. Material compliance sends the participant's real equipment, fuel, or manpower to the African host and measures both stockpiles around the transfer: the participant is credited with what actually left its stocks (never more than the amount asked), Africa's own stock accounts follow Africa's measured gain, a short delivery stays a partial payment with the remainder still owed, and a fuel shipment is limited to Africa's free tank space (the fulfil and partial-payment decisions need room for at least one unit of fuel). A short full payment does not turn the contract into agreed terms; only a chosen partial payment does. Political families debit and credit political power, the resource lane grants real steel rights, and measured infantry deliveries also replenish the maintained Event 012 common-defence reserve used by the shortage profile. Every measured delivery is added to the participant's paid-contribution record by kind (infantry, support, trucks, artillery, armour, aircraft, fuel, convoys, trains, manpower), with separate voluntary, late-campaign, and emergency subsets; family-13 emergency relief paid against a frozen, still-current emergency is recorded as emergency assistance. A partly delivered accepted substitute becomes the contract terms: its amount replaces `gods_of_africa_demand_amount` and `gods_of_africa_compute_partial_payment_amount` recomputes the partial shipment and its capacity requirement in the substitute's units, so the mission reads, for example, 18 of 30 tanks rather than 18 of 2000; substitution and extension otherwise use the frozen contract rather than rerolling it. A payment, accepted substitute or voluntary shipment that moves nothing is credited nothing, opens a `.604` row that says it arrived empty, and closes the same answer for `gods_of_africa_timing.undelivered_retry_days` (7) days (`gods_of_africa_payment_retry_day`), so neither a player nor the AI resends it every day. A shipment that Africa could not account for (`gods_of_africa_material_transaction_unresolved`) blocks further material shipments with a visible reason, and an open material demand that it blocked lapses at its deadline as a neutral retirement rather than a failure (decision L19).

The native demand mission is synchronized to the frozen deadline immediately after activation and after an extension.
The 180-day definition is an activation anchor, while the live countdown uses the actual ceremonial, standard, severe, emergency, or punitive duration and any approved extension.
Mission and reconciliation descriptions read the engine's remaining timeout rather than an absolute day counter.
The owner tick's deadline pass is a recovery check, while the native timeout owns ordinary expiry.
Partial shipments retain the same running deadline and do not restart the mission.

Participants can comply, negotiate, substitute a valid offer, refuse, or permanently defy. Negotiation freezes up to three deterministic, capacity-checked alternatives with the contract; the participant submits one offer, while the host explicitly accepts or rejects it. Rejection raises Wrath slightly, records the attempt, restores the original payment fields, and leaves the extended demand available; returning to the demand cancels an unsubmitted offer. Refusal raises Wrath and invokes punishment review without silently ending the relationship. Defiance stops ordinary demands, records a permanent offense, raises the Wrath floor, and leaves punishment, retaliation, protection withdrawal, and conflict available. Only the reconciliation path can restore ordinary demand eligibility, and reconciliation preserves prior-defiance history.

Reconciliation is offered to a participant in permanent defiance, with an unresolved broken agreement, or with unreconciled war or occupation against Africa, and only while it is at peace with Africa and holds none of Africa's African core territory. A war with any other country neither delays the window nor cancels it (decision L23). It requires a live review deadline, an actual ten-political-power reparations transfer to Africa, and host acceptance before that deadline. The transfer records a separate paid-offer history entry. Archival, participant cleanup, and unifier loss remove pending review, payment permission, offer permission, and deadline state while retaining the actual completed transfer and durable history. Re-entry can open a fresh review through the existing owner clock; interrupted permission cannot accept reconciliation or block that review indefinitely. Only accepted reconciliation clears permanent opt-out.

Outside an active demand, `gods_of_africa_offer_voluntary_aid` provides one participant-side offering when the host's maintained need profile shows a land-equipment or fuel shortage. Land equipment is selected first when both needs are present; otherwise fuel is selected. The effect rechecks the current generation, host activity, need, war state, cooldown, and sender reserve before sending 500 units as a real measured shipment (native `send_equipment` for rifles), credits the common defence reserve with the rifles that arrived, adds the measured amount to the participant's voluntary contribution record, records an `aid` history entry, and opens the separate `chaosx.nr12.614` receipt. The first voluntary offer lowers Wrath by four and later offers by two; a 180-day participant cooldown and a 500-unit reserve floor prevent repeated tiny shipments. The host rebuilds its need profile after a successful transfer, and the existing reliable-partner memory can make the participant eligible for future protection.

If a participant enters a war with Africa while an ordinary demand is open, the owner-local tick cancels that contract once and reports the pause in `chaosx.nr12.613`. A contract with agreed terms records a broken agreement; any other contract records a wartime cancellation and raises Wrath by the bounded war amount. No ordinary demand is dispatched while the participant remains at war with Africa; after that peace, the normal registry and cooldown gates may issue a fresh contract (spec DEM-14).

A war with any other country does not pause the tribute loop; it eases it (spec DEM-04, decision L23). `gods_of_africa_participant_can_receive_demand` excludes only `has_war_with = event_target:africa_host`. For a participant at war elsewhere (`gods_of_africa_participant_is_at_war_elsewhere`) `gods_of_africa_calculate_demand_amount` lowers the demand scale by `gods_of_africa_demand_scale.wartime_delta` (20 points), and by `losing_war_delta` (25 more) once its surrender progress is above `gods_of_africa_hardship.losing_war_surrender` (0.25); `gods_of_africa_calculate_demand_deadline` adds `gods_of_africa_timing.wartime_deadline_extension_days` (30) and `losing_war_deadline_extension_days` (30 more). Above `gods_of_africa_hardship.near_capitulation_surrender` (0.5) `gods_of_africa_select_demand_family` asks only for political tribute: emergency relief while Africa has an emergency, otherwise recognition once that lane is open. When Africa can quote no demand for the chosen participant, `gods_of_africa_prepare_demand_for_participant` passes it over for `gods_of_africa_timing.unquotable_retry_days` (30), or `hardship_postpone_days` (60) near capitulation, so the dispatcher moves on to the next participant instead of retrying the same one every week. The contract freezes the easing as `gods_of_africa_demand_eased_for_war` or `gods_of_africa_demand_eased_for_losing_war`, and the `.603` demand and the mission explain it through `GetGodsOfAfricaDemandHardshipLine`.

### Diplomatic demands

Six families carry out a real diplomatic act rather than a material transfer (decision L25). Each costs a constant-backed amount of political power that is paid in full together with the act (`gods_of_africa_pay_political_tribute`, `gods_of_africa_carry_out_diplomatic_concession`); none takes a partial payment, and the partial-payment decision is hidden for them (`gods_of_africa_demand_is_diplomatic`). The demand mission shows "Complying costs N political power and is done in full" instead of a balance, and the `.604` compliance report adds a line naming what was done (`GetGodsOfAfricaResponseOutcomeLine`).

- Territory (family 11, Demand the Return of African Soil). When the participant holds an African core state of the host, this demand comes before any material need. Contract creation (`gods_of_africa_freeze_diplomatic_terms`) freezes up to `gods_of_africa_transaction.territory_state_limit` (3) named states the participant controls in `gods_of_africa_demand_states` and `gods_of_africa_demand_state_1` to `_3`; the `.603` demand, the mission and the report name them. Payment hands exactly those states to Africa through the measured core-return path (`gods_of_africa_return_frozen_core_states`). If any named state has left the participant's hands, Fulfil is blocked with a visible reason (`gods_of_africa_frozen_territory_tt`) and the demand lapses at its deadline without a failure (report kind `territory_lapsed`, 20). No material substitute can replace named land.
- End support for an enemy of Africa (family 12, End Foreign Sponsorship). Valid only while the participant gives a non-capitulated country Africa is fighting a guarantee, military access or volunteers (`gods_of_africa_country_is_supported_by_demand_participant`); this demand comes right after territory. The contract names the enemy (`gods_of_africa_support_enemy`) and the kinds of support it gives (`gods_of_africa_support_kind_guarantee`, `_access`, `_volunteers`); compliance ends exactly those through `diplomatic_relation` with `active = no` and `recall_volunteers_from`. Lend-lease is never part of the demand because no effect can end it. No material substitute can replace it.
- Recognition (family 10, Recognize the Continental Voice). Compliance adds the `gods_of_africa_recognition` opinion modifier in both directions and sets `gods_of_africa_recognition_granted`. Each recognising participant that is a major power adds `gods_of_africa_strength.recognition_per_major` (5) to the Strength continent component, up to `recognition_cap` (20), and `gods_of_africa_sync_world_history` turns the record into `gods_of_africa_world_history_recognised`, which raises a World Is One constituent's acceptance (`ai_gods_recognition_accept_factor`) and the package actor's invitation weight (`ai_gods_recognition_invite_factor`).
- Military access (15), non-aggression pact (16) and guarantee of Africa (17), after Security Oaths, Evolution III and Continental Strength or more (`gods_of_africa_host_can_demand_concessions`). Each is offered only to a participant that is not a subject, not in Africa's faction, at peace with Africa, and does not already give it (`gods_of_africa_participant_can_grant_access`, `_can_sign_non_aggression`, `_can_guarantee_africa`); access comes first while Africa is at war. Compliance runs `give_military_access`, `diplomatic_relation` with `relation = non_aggression_pact`, or `give_guarantee`.

The participant AI weighs the family: recognition and a pact are easy (`comply_family_recognition`, `comply_family_non_aggression`), access and a guarantee harder, and abandoning an ally at war or handing back land hardest (`comply_family_end_support`, `comply_family_territory`).

### Continuing support

From Evolution II, while Africa is at war, a demand for rifles or fuel becomes continuing support instead of a one-off shipment when the participant is at peace and holds every instalment above its operating floor (family `continuing_support`, 18; decisions L28 and L38; `common/scripted_effects/012_africa_gods_instalment_effects.txt`). The conversion runs at the end of `gods_of_africa_select_demand_family`, so the rifle or fuel candidate may come from Africa's priority, from Measure the Needs or from the fixed order, and a participant fighting its own war keeps the eased one-off demand. The contract freezes the material (the rifles or fuel of the converted candidate), `gods_of_africa_instalments.count` (3) whole-unit instalments of `equipment_per_instalment` or `fuel_per_instalment` (500, scaled like any demand) and the `interval_days` (30) between them. "Sign the Support Agreement" (`gods_of_africa_sign_support_agreement`) sends the first instalment at once, makes the contract agreed terms and moves its deadline to cover every instalment plus one interval of grace; "Fulfil the Demand" still pays the whole balance at once, and refusal works as for any demand. The bounded owner review (`gods_of_africa_run_owner_tick`, no new world scan or daily hook) sends each later instalment when `gods_of_africa_next_instalment_day` is reached, through the same measured transfer as any payment (`gods_of_africa_transfer_payment` moves the frozen material), so each instalment is credited to the participant's contribution record and the grade reads it through the existing infantry or fuel kinds. Signing and every instalment keep the operating floor of the material moved: `capacity_floor_fuel` for fuel and `capacity_floor_equipment` for rifles. The last instalment settles the contract through the ordinary full-compliance path. An instalment the participant cannot pay on its day ends the contract through the ordinary deadline path as a failure of agreed terms. A fuel instalment Africa has no tank room for is not the participant's failure: it waits one `interval_days`, and the contract deadline moves with it. A partly stored fuel shipment keeps its unpaid fraction in the remaining instalment count, moves the next attempt into the future and receives the same deadline extension. The mission shows the instalments left and the date the next one leaves (`GetGodsOfAfricaDemandInstalmentLine`), intermediate shipments update the mission without a popup, and one `.604` closure report summarizes all material sent and any balance still owed, and partial payment is not offered. Native lend-lease is not used because script can neither inspect nor end it.

### Agreements and broken agreements

A treaty breach is the breach of an agreement the participant actually made with Africa. Generic offending, a single refusal, or marking an offender never counts as one.

| Agreement | When it exists | How it is broken |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agreed tribute terms | Africa granted an extension, Africa accepted a substitute, or the participant chose a partial payment on the open contract (`gods_of_africa_contract_agreed`, bound to the contract ledger ID) | The contract later fails, is refused, is closed by defiance, or is cancelled because the participant attacked Africa |
| Protection compact | The participant holds Africa's paid protection (`gods_of_africa_protected`) | The participant attacks Africa |
| Accepted reconciliation | Africa accepted the participant's paid reconciliation (`gods_of_africa_reconciliation_agreement_live`); defiance replaces it | The participant attacks Africa |
| Renewed recognition | Africa accepted a capitulation victor's renewal and the victor has not won another war against it | The participant attacks Africa |

`gods_of_africa_record_treaty_breach` in `common/scripted_effects/012_africa_gods_contract_effects.txt` increments `gods_of_africa_treaty_break_count` once, sets `gods_of_africa_unresolved_treaty_break`, the permanent `gods_of_africa_prior_treaty_break`, and the `gods_of_africa_broken_treaty` Wrath floor cause (70), records a `treaty_break` history row, and applies `gods_of_africa_wrath.treaty_breach_magnitude` (20) in place of the ordinary refusal, failure, or war gain. The broken agreement ends: protection is withdrawn, the accepted reconciliation stops being live, and a renewed recognition is marked broken. "Attacks" means the participant is on the attacking side of a war with Africa (`has_offensive_war_with`, which also covers joining an attacker's war): `gods_of_africa_participant_is_aggressor_against_host`. One war breaks every live agreement at once but counts as one breach, and one war adds one Wrath gain: `gods_of_africa_cancel_demand_for_war` (bounded owner pass) records the single breach gain when an agreed contract or any live agreement stands, or the ordinary war gain (10) when none does, and the shared per-war flag `gods_of_africa_agreement_war_breach_recorded` stops `gods_of_africa_observe_agreement_war` (owner review) from adding a second gain. Without an open contract, the owner review records the breach and reports it through `chaosx.nr12.616`.

When Africa is the aggressor (`gods_of_africa_host_is_aggressor_against_participant`), nothing is a breach: an open contract is retired neutrally with a `war_cancellation` history row and no Wrath gain, every live agreement ends through `gods_of_africa_end_agreements_for_host_war` (protection withdrawn by the existing helper, reconciliation no longer live, renewed recognition ended through `gods_of_africa_victor_renewal_ended_sequence`), the history records `agreement_ended_by_host`, and `.613` or the `.616` variant "Africa Breaks Its Word" names Africa as the party that broke the agreement. War and occupation conduct marks, the war Wrath floor and the outstanding-hostility mark are recorded as for any war (decision L20). The response report `.604` and the wartime report `.613` name the broken agreement, and the refusal and defiance decisions warn when the open contract carries agreed terms.

### Conduct record

The bounded owner review observes each participant once per review through `gods_of_africa_refresh_grading_conduct`, called from `gods_of_africa_refresh_participant_floors`; there is no world scan and no daily hook.
It counts each new war episode against the current host once, records every African core state of the host that the participant controls (unique state IDs) and caches the current controlled and owned host-core counts.
War, occupation, and defiance set the permanent prior-hostility records and the outstanding hostility mark `gods_of_africa_unresolved_major_offense`, which stays until Africa accepts a paid reconciliation.
A territory payment returns the frozen named host-core states, up to the three-state contract limit; each return counts once per state, and only when Africa then owns and controls it.
The Wrath floor is the highest floor among the current causes (war 50, territory 60, broken agreement 70, defiance 70, recent extreme punishment 80).

An unresolved broken agreement keeps the participant's Wrath at or above 70 and makes it Enemy at the settlement. ## Punishment and protection

Punishment intent is calculated separately from the capability ceiling. `gods_of_africa_evaluate_punishment` first writes `gods_of_africa_punishment_intent_tier` from Wrath and escalation evidence, copies that intent into `gods_of_africa_pending_punishment_tier`, and applies the first-failure and host Strength-band ceilings. `gods_of_africa_apply_punishment` then validates and prices only the capped pending tier, fails closed from catastrophe when the extreme gate is absent, records both last punishment tiers, and clears the transient intent and pending values. Wrath, failure history, proven offenses, failed-demand severity, Chaos, and hostility increase desired punishment; Strength, African capability, evolution access, target condition, cooldowns, and valid Event 013 inputs cap what can actually happen. A first ordinary refusal is capped at a low tier. High Wrath alone never opens catastrophe. The extreme route additionally requires extreme Strength, very high Wrath, high Chaos, repeated failure or a major offense, the required evolution, a valid ultimatum, no target or global extreme cooldown, and an Africa state that is not collapsing.

Weak Africa therefore produces pressure, unrest, limited disruption, failed manifestations, and bounded sabotage. Natural disasters use the Event 013 gateway and fail closed when that gateway rejects the call. Any civilian population loss uses the shared exact state-population transaction and records Deaths once. Protection remains one host-selected action over the maintained participant registry, gated by the existing reliable-partner, current-generation, host-reserve, cooldown, non-defiance, and no-war checks. Africa pays 500 infantry equipment, 500 fuel, and 10 political power, subtracts the equipment from its common-defence reserve, credits equipment and fuel to the participant, and starts the 180-day protection cooldown; the reserve focus shortens that interval. With the logistics focus, at most one shortage-checked 20-unit convoy or train transfer may add a fourth cost type when Africa can retain more than its configured 40-unit floor after shipment.

The current focus overlay adds owner-gated services to that paid compact. The Sanctuary capability creates a guarantee only when none existed and, with Arms Against Tyranny, may add mutual market access when the pair is at peace; the judgment-branch focus `gods_of_africa_focus_no_sanctuary`, localized as “Sanctuary for Reliable Partners,” supplies that capability. Security creates military access only when absent and, with La Résistance, the Security capability, valid agencies, and an active African war, shares five points of each intelligence category, or ten under Strategic Ambiguity, about Africa's current non-capitulated enemies that the participant is not already fighting. Logistics transfers 20 convoys when the participant has fewer than 20 and Africa can send them while keeping more than its configured 40-unit floor; trains use the same test if a convoy shipment cannot be made. Relief adds stability and builds one infrastructure level in an owned-and-controlled partner capital below level five. A Relief Convoy is a separate choice in Foreign Disaster Relief; for a protected partner under the relief focus, that choice uses Event 013's preflight adapter and full existing price, and Event 013 owns the donor receipt and mission lifecycle. The shared decision filters unresolved-territory cards and selects an actionable card before charging or setting recipient state. The manpower, support equipment, trucks, convoys, fuel, stability, war-support, and temporary light construction costs are not charged by the Event 012 protection action. Expedition adds war support. Shelter may install Migration-owned shelter and medical reception capacity in the participant's current capital after Migration's food-safety and empty-service checks, without moving population.

Created guarantees, access rights, and Migration capacity have participant-owned receipts, so defiance, archive, host loss, cancellation, and final settlement withdraw only recorded services whose owner-side state still matches. Pre-existing relations and rewritten or transferred Migration services are preserved. Because HOI4 diplomatic relations are binary, a relation revoked and independently re-created for the same country pair before cleanup cannot be distinguished from the original grant; cleanup still follows the stored created-by-protection receipt and current relation check. Completed equipment transfers, construction, and intelligence already learned are historical effects, and an Event 013 relief mission already dispatched is not recalled when Event 012 protection is withdrawn. The base package works without optional DLC; AAT market access and La Resistance intelligence remain gated by their respective DLC and live agency conditions.

### Punishment families, bluffs and the final ultimatum

The tier says how hard Africa strikes; the family says how (decision L26, `common/scripted_effects/012_africa_gods_punishment_effects.txt`, `common/scripted_triggers/012_africa_gods_punishment_triggers.txt`). `gods_of_africa_select_punishment_family` reads the offence: holding Africa's core land prefers military, unrest, logistical and diplomatic retaliation; war with Africa (or unreconciled war) military, logistical, infrastructure and covert; a broken agreement diplomatic isolation first; Chaos at 800 or more disaster first; withheld equipment, fuel or manpower the stockpile first; anything else infrastructure, unrest, stockpile and diplomatic. A family is used only when it is valid for the tier and the focuses (`gods_of_africa_punishment_family_is_valid`): pressure, diplomatic isolation and stockpile losses are always available, unrest only where the target holds occupied territory, infrastructure from Disruption, covert (factory sabotage) and logistical (ports, railways and convoys) from Disruption after Reach Beyond the Coast, military losses from Disruption and disaster from Crisis after the Hand of Judgment. The family used within the last `gods_of_africa_punishment_scope.repeat_window_days` (180) is skipped when another fits (spec PUN-12), and a target near capitulation gets diplomatic isolation or pressure and is struck in one state at most (spec PUN-13).

State-targeting families strike up to 1 state at Pressure and Disruption, 3 at Crisis and 5 at the Ultimatum tier (`gods_of_africa_punishment_scope`), occupied territory for unrest and never Africa's own cores for the others; each strike draws a different eligible state at random, a drawn state the strike cannot touch (an occupied state whose resistance cannot rise) is set aside for another draw so the budget counts real strikes, and Secure Transport still stops the local attack. Material losses are the tier times a unit (`stockpile_equipment_unit`, `stockpile_fuel_unit`, `military_infantry_unit`, `military_support_unit`, `logistical_convoy_unit`) and take only what the target holds, or half of it. Diplomatic isolation adds `gods_of_africa_isolation` opinion from Africa, its faction and every protected or recognising participant. A disaster goes through the Event 013 gateway at regional severity (catastrophic at the Ultimatum tier) and fails closed into infrastructure damage. The `.605` report names the family, the states struck and the losses (`GetGodsOfAfricaPunishmentLocalConsequence`).

At Limited Strength every second punishment (`bluff_cycle`) is a bluff: it costs only `bluff_pp_loss` (2) political power, takes no stability or war support, and its report says the promised disaster weakened before it arrived.

A Tier V catastrophe is staged. Whenever its full gate opens, `gods_of_africa_issue_final_ultimatum` opens the `gods_of_africa_final_ultimatum_mission` and the `.618` notice instead of striking: within `gods_of_africa_ultimatum.deadline_days` (60) the participant may hand back every state of Africa's homeland it owns or controls and end any war with Africa in a white peace, or pay `settlement_pp` (150) political power and `settlement_equipment` (3000) infantry equipment. Handed-back land also receives the same unique-state restitution proof as a territory demand, bound to the current ultimatum. Either answer lifts the ultimatum, lowers Wrath by `wrath_relief` (25) and records `ultimatum_met` (21) in the history. Refusal or the deadline (`gods_of_africa_expire_final_ultimatum`) runs `gods_of_africa_evaluate_punishment` again, so intent, the extreme gate and the Strength ceiling are read at that moment: the catastrophe falls if Africa can still deliver it, otherwise the strongest punishment its Strength allows. The catastrophe is an Event 013 disaster and waves of civilian deaths in up to `catastrophe_wave_states` (3) different owned and controlled states drawn at random, each through the exact population transaction with the protected floor and its own Deaths entry. Every catastrophe brings its own waves, including a later one against a defiant government that receives no new demand contract. No other punishment and no ordinary demand falls on the participant while the ultimatum is open. The first Ultimatum-tier punishment, the final ultimatum and the catastrophe are Event 012 event-log milestones (`gods_first_heavy_punishment`, `gods_final_ultimatum`, `gods_catastrophe`) naming Africa and the participant; the catastrophe entry is written only when the Event 013 gateway accepted the disaster.

Let the World Answer adds the host decision `gods_of_africa_arm_offender_enemies`: Africa sends `gods_of_africa_coalition.equipment` (1000) infantry equipment from its own stocks and its common-defence reserve, and pays `pp_cost` (25) political power, to one country at war with a defiant government, an occupier or a marked offender that Africa is not fighting; it keeps `host_reserve` (1000) rifles, is closed while Africa is near capitulation, and cannot target the same government again for `cooldown_days` (180). The host receives the `.619` report.

### Final standing

Every survivor is graded against the same frozen Strength and doctrine, in the order Enemy, Favored, Respected, Distrusted:

- **Enemy**: current war with Africa, held African core territory of the host, an unresolved broken agreement, permanent defiance, unreconciled war or occupation, Wrath above 80, or three or more refusals (after an accepted reconciliation, refusals since then).
- **Favored**: no Enemy cause, Wrath below 40, at least two normalized packages of material actually sent, no failures in the applicable record, and the doctrine's record: Reciprocal Covenant 2 fulfilled demands, 80% fulfilled, current and longest streak 2, plus one package of voluntary, emergency, or wartime help, a returned state, or 4 fulfilled demands; Sovereign Exaction 4, 90%, streak 4, plus two packages of such help or 6 fulfilled demands; Strategic Ambiguity or no doctrine 3, 80%, streak 3, plus one package of such help or 5 fulfilled demands. A country with past defiance, war, occupation, or a broken agreement also needs an accepted reconciliation followed by four fulfilled demands in a row without a failure or refusal.
- **Respected**: no Enemy cause, Wrath below 70, fewer than two failures and two refusals (three under Strategic Ambiguity), and either at least half its demands fulfilled or one package of voluntary, emergency, or wartime help or a returned state. Past hostility needs an accepted reconciliation followed by two fulfilled demands in a row.
- **Distrusted**: every other survivor, including governments that were simply never asked for anything.

Normalized packages are 1000 infantry equipment, 500 support equipment, 100 trucks, 50 artillery, 25 armoured vehicles, 50 aircraft, 1000 fuel, 20 convoys or trains, or 10000 manpower; 50 political power of qualified emergency relief (one base emergency demand) counts as one emergency package. Only measured deliveries count.

### Settlement support

Favored and Respected governments are owed infantry equipment and fuel scaled to the material they sent (with a premium for voluntary and late-campaign help, up to 16 packages) and to Africa's frozen Strength; Reciprocal Covenant raises Favored support by a quarter. Favored support is capped at 50000 equipment and 25000 fuel, Respected at 10000 and 5000. A government that sent no material is owed nothing, and Distrusted and Enemy governments are owed nothing.

The obligation is frozen at the settlement and paid from Africa's real stocks: a first batch leaves at once, and the settling government's **Deliver settlement support** decision sends a further fair batch every 30 days. Each batch shares the spare equipment and fuel proportionally among the governments still owed, keeps Africa's operating reserves and a tenth of its army's equipment requirement, never overfills a fuel tank, and skips a government that is at war with Africa. The `.608` report names the standing, a short cause, and what has been delivered and is still owed.

Standing diplomacy is applied once: Favored governments gain a strong positive memory and Africa's guarantee, Respected governments a positive memory and a non-aggression pact, Distrusted governments Africa's suspicion, and Enemies a hostile memory in both directions.

- Fulfil the Demand rises for a light burden, a Continental or stronger Africa, loyal allies, and fear of a strong Africa at high Wrath; it falls for a heavy burden, a weak Africa, rivals, gamblers, and a participant losing a war.
- Refusal rises for a heavy or unpayable demand, a weak Africa, rivals, gamblers, held African land and low Wrath, and falls sharply for loyal allies and agreed terms.
- Negotiation (extension and substitute offers) rises for a heavy burden, a participant losing a war and gamblers; the substitute slots prefer a family Africa currently lacks.
- Permanent defiance scores zero without a reason in the current state (rival posture, offence record, high Wrath, or a heavy demand from a weak Africa) and rises for held African land, a hostile faction leader, and a stronger power facing a weak Africa; a dominant Africa suppresses it.
- Voluntary aid is mostly a loyal ally's act and doubles in an African emergency.
- Africa protects partners with fulfilled demands or voluntary aid before merely reliable ones, marks and escalates against occupiers and enemies at war with it first, stops both while it is itself near capitulation (`africa_action_threshold.near_capitulation`), and spares a target that can no longer pay. Leniency and pardon rise for a participant on track for Favored, in hardship, or allied. Africa accepts a substitute only of a family it currently lacks (`gods_of_africa_host_needs_family`) and otherwise rejects it.

These are source preference rules, not verified normalized choices or observed AI frequencies.
