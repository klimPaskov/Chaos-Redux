## Black Friday sale prices

During a Black Friday sale (Event 026) every Event 005 decision price is charged at the sale price (agent decision D21 in `docs/plans/026_black_friday_plans/agent_decisions_2026-10-04.md`).
Each positive cost amount is registered in `black_friday_price_table_refresh_soviet_collapse_entries` (`common/scripted_effects/026_black_friday_price_table_effects.txt`) as `global.black_friday_price_soviet_collapse_<group>_<key>`, and each `_spend` payment constant has a matching negative `global.black_friday_price_soviet_collapse_<group>_<key>_spend`.
The `can_pay_*` triggers compare the resource against the positive price with an inclusive check, so holding exactly the shown amount is enough, the payment effects add the negative global, and the cost texts, the foreign aid tier texts, and the regional faction display values read the same price.
Foreign aid tier surcharges hold the base price minus the tier price, so the base payment plus the surcharge always equals the tier price the trigger required.
Each paid decision credits one Black Friday achievement family from a hidden block at the end of its completion effect.

Kept at ordinary amounts: arms shipments, aid corridor trains, and the Ukrainian League arms quota equipment and grain compact trains and convoys, which the recipient receives one for one; Stability floors, division and mandate requirements; mission completion consumption of the Soviet objectives; the patronage, coercion, and client cabinet Stability penalties.
The shared splinter effects that focuses also call charge the sale price there too, because the price lives in the payment line; only decisions credit the achievement.
Nine splinter decisions whose reward effect carries no payment (`soviet_collapse_write_restored_charter`, `fev_negotiate_japanese_liaison_officers`, `ijx_press_trade_city_claims`, `pra_repair_the_branch_lines`, `pra_raise_mobile_supply_yards`, `pra_drive_the_junction_columns`, `dsc_register_field_hospital_columns`, `dsc_mark_the_unfinished_front_roads`, and `dsc_order_the_dead_army_forward`) pay their shown cost through `soviet_collapse_pay_custom_splinter_medium_cost` (Political Power, Command Power, and Army Experience) or `soviet_collapse_pay_custom_splinter_low_cost` (support equipment; the Stability figure in that cost text is a floor), the same amounts their sibling decisions pay through their reward effects (agent decision D22).

## Event 023 custody bridge

When Event 005 releases a state, each Event 023-registered storage site is snapshotted immediately before the release effect transfers ownership or control. The snapshot records operational, assigned, reserved, transferred, dismantled, missing, holder, owner, controller, custody, and reconciliation values for that exact state.

The post-transfer reconciliation hooks run on `on_release_as_free`, `on_release_as_puppet`, and `on_state_control_changed`. They classify the site as central Soviet custody, Soviet custody under siege, breakaway physical custody, joint custody, foreign custody, technical denial, missing, or destroyed/dismantled without altering the Event 023 ledger total.

Physical custody, technical access, command formation, and delivery integration remain separate gates. A breakaway with a site or personnel does not become an operational nuclear actor immediately. The staged Event 023 technical-access, command-formation, and delivery-integration missions must complete against the same selected site. If `SOV` disappears, Soviet-only production, evolution timing, and actor decisions stop, while valid local custody records continue through the shared ledger and later reconciliation.

The suppression side is capped and stops at the high-threat band, while terminal Union Unmade release passes remain untouched, so repeated repression cannot erase a severe crisis or block mandatory collapse paths.

The crisis board keeps all 118 numbered Soviet missions and its existing active and monthly refill caps. Each mission belongs to exactly one operational family: 37 Chain of Command, 21 Corridors and Depots, and 60 Republic Settlement. At refill, Moscow measures Authority and Obedience shortfalls, Republic Confidence and Depot Vulnerability excesses, and the influence of Foreign Appetite, League Cohesion, and Old Movement pressure. It attempts at most one live mission from each family in priority order, then fills remaining places through the original complete mission list. The opening posture shifts the safe bands before the first post-option fill, so the first board reflects the player's declared response. This is one mission board and one release scheduler, not a parallel objective system.

Each family also has a shared compromise. Compromise concludes one active family mission through its existing done flag without firing success or timeout and without incrementing either monthly outcome counter. Command compromise recovers a little Authority and Obedience while conceding Republic Confidence. Corridor compromise protects depots while conceding Authority and adding foreign and League pressure. Settlement compromise lowers Republic Confidence while conceding Authority and adding foreign and League pressure. Corridor missions require a passable original-Union state still owned and controlled by Moscow with meaningful supply-node, port, railway, or contested-border geography. All 21 corridor missions require their matching live-state condition before activation and keep checking it afterward. An invalidated state cancels the mission into the existing refill path without counting as success or failure, while a missing candidate suppresses the family instead of receiving a substitute state.

Completing any of the 118 missions needs a standing Moscow order. Restore Party Control, Send Loyalist Officers, Cut Rebel Supply Routes, and Emergency Mobilization each add one order (`soviet_collapse_moscow_orders`, capped by `soviet_collapse_soviet_objective.order_cap`), and every completed mission spends one through `soviet_collapse_register_monthly_threat_success`. The availability block of each mission lists the requirement, so a mission can no longer finish by waiting once its resource and map conditions hold. Compromise decisions neither need nor spend an order. Count the Missing Trains also needs a garrison of more than three divisions across Moscow, Smolensk, and Minsk.

Every released country records one dominant cause from the closed set of command fracture, corridor/depot loss, negotiated political break, and foreign/League-backed rupture inside the existing release setup. The cause changes its starting institutions or military package, Moscow's next preferred family, sponsor interest, neighboring-breakaway reactions, and the breakaway AI posture. It does not trigger the release and does not bypass the existing pressure gates.

The tranche adds no visual asset requirement. Command, corridor, settlement, terminal-reclamation, and terminal-aid actions reuse `GFX_decision_soviet_collapse_command_goal`, `GFX_decision_soviet_collapse_rail_goal`, `GFX_decision_soviet_collapse_cleanup_goal`, `GFX_decision_cut_rebel_supply_routes`, and `GFX_decision_soviet_collapse_foreign_intelligence`, all already registered in `interface/005_soviet_collapse.gfx`. No DDS or `.gfx` file is added or renamed.

Progressive general releases are selected from a dynamic `every_possible_country` pool once Gathering Storm or higher chaos is active and the crisis variables show real release pressure. Eligible countries must have core territory still owned and controlled by Moscow, core territory already inside the Soviet-collapse breakaway ecosystem, or be Soviet subjects with core territory, so late ordinary breakaways such as northern, Siberian, Arctic, and regional republic tags can join the same release flow as the better-known republics instead of depending on a hardcoded country list. Calm worlds keep the ordinary base-republic pool. Gathering Storm and higher tiers make internal and niche releasables available, but actual follow-on bursts still depend on union threat, progressive release pressure, failed-objective pressure, regional cascade pressure, war pressure, severe component pressure, or high urgency. Higher chaos raises the burst size once those pressure gates are met rather than releasing every eligible country immediately. Special high-chaos successor tags begin entering the progressive pool at chaos tier 3 only after severe or real crisis pressure is present. Tier 4 and tier 5 increase special-successor urgency. Union Unmade and the standalone chaos triggerable scenario are the rupture paths that can force exhaustive dynamic release passes. Ordinary monthly progression does not treat tier or elapsed time alone as enough pressure for non-base releases.

Union Crisis Threat pacing is intentionally slow in calm and Gathering Storm starts. The first-month and strong-center caps keep a stable center near contained levels, failed months add only small capped increases, and the severe preterminal band remains capped until the crisis has spent many months under real terminal pressure. Union Unmade can still happen through inactivity, repeated failed missions, war pressure, regional cascades, or high chaos, but it should not be the normal first-year result of a calm opening. Current tuning keeps moderate and preterminal monthly component caps conservative, raises real release-pressure gates, and gives successful containment more room to decay stored release pressure before a follow-on republic is selected.

Reconquest is part of the threat model. When Moscow annexes a breakaway republic through the existing `on_annex` defeat handler, the crisis receives component relief and an explicit Union Crisis Threat reduction that scales from the defeated republic's controlled states, civilian factories, military factories, foreign support tier, high-chaos status, resilience, institutions, and League organization. Weak isolated republics only reduce the crisis modestly. Stronger republics, foreign-backed republics, and high-chaos successors reduce it more. If the defeated republic was the last active member of a Baltic, Caucasus, or Central Asian regional league, Moscow records that league as defeated, gains a timed morale/confidence spirit, receives a larger threat reduction, and unlocks punitive-operation targeting against other regional League members through the existing Soviet Republic Negotiations desk.

Breakaway setup grants starting forces dynamically. The release package scales field formations, manpower, infantry equipment, support equipment, and artillery from controlled states, civilian factories, and military factories before applying chaos, war-pressure, depot, foreign-access, and terminal-collapse bonuses. Stronger republics therefore start with substantially larger armies without relying only on a short fixed major-tag list, and their spawned field formations use stronger templates as their territorial or industrial base grows. The initial-force marker is based on actual fielded divisions, and the setup path retries a minimum package for any breakaway with usable owned or controlled territory that still has no units after the dynamic package runs. Union Unmade finalization also retries the same actual-division check for every breakaway and guarantees that any newly released republic with a usable owned or controlled state fields at least one unit. The triggerable Union Unmade scenario uses the same strength model with higher intensity caps, so high-intensity starts can give Ukraine, Belarus, Kazakhstan, large Siberian republics, and industrial successors far larger opening forces than small isolated tags. Large republics and industrial successors receive especially large field-formation bursts at high intensity, while small northern or regional tags still get enough guards to survive the first month.

Existing-country focus-tree eligibility stays conservative. Event-created ordinary republic trees are limited to countries marked with `soviet_collapse_event_created_republic`. All nine generic runtime loader calls and matching regional tree selectors enforce that exact gate, so existing countries with meaningful trees retain them and receive crisis integration through the existing non-replacement surfaces.

Regional focus-tree and league classifiers must stay geographic. The Central Asian focus tree and Central Asian League cover Kazakhstan, the southern Central Asian republics, and Central Asian successor/restoration packages. Northern internal republics above Kazakhstan, including Altai and Ostyak-Vogulia, stay on internal-republic logic and must not count as Central Asian League members. Caucasus League formation is single-leader: once a Caucasus league leader or announcement exists, later Caucasus calls join that league or refresh invitations instead of creating another Caucasus faction. Union Unmade runs a terminal Central Asian League reconciliation pass after releases and league formation, so newly released eligible Central Asian republics join the existing Central Asian faction instead of remaining outside it.

Foreign patron target expansion is compact for players and broad for AI. Human patrons choose one visible republic desk at a time. Selecting a target normalizes that target into the breakaway array and marks it as the active patron target so recognition, liaison, armament, adviser, aid-corridor, construction, press, treaty, and client decisions can evaluate it even when the republic was added by a dynamic release path. Selection controls display only. Every action still enforces its own diplomatic, dependency, route, and affordability gates. Hiding and reopening a desk preserves action cooldowns, while actual annexation, reintegration, reconquest, or target resolution clears both the republic and owner-side selection state. AI patrons keep the full target list visible by default.

Union Unmade converts rather than relaxes the selected desks. Pre-collapse Moscow negotiations and foreign recognition/dependency actions require the active crisis and are removed from the terminal surface. Moscow may deliberately reopen a surviving wartime target for a reclamation-front corridor operation. An eligible foreign patron may reopen a republic fighting Moscow and establish a wartime aid corridor. This keeps a usable base, Tajikistan, dynamic non-base, high-chaos, and post-terminal scope path without pretending pre-collapse treaties remain meaningful after the Union is gone.

The Republic Release Risk Ledger is a player-facing warning board, not only a debug readout. It shows the release roll gate, urgency, stored pressure, candidate families, weights, and locks so a Soviet player can decide whether to spend attention on broad prevention or targeted republic handling before another monthly release check. Moscow's republic tools live in a separate Soviet Republic Negotiations category. A human Soviet player opens one republic desk with Show Decisions, handles treaty, negotiation, administration, ultimatum, compact, or punitive options for that target, and hides it again to keep the list compact. The selected republic desk keeps the full Moscow action set visible for that republic. Unmet treaty, negotiation, compact, ultimatum, or punitive gates disable their row instead of removing it from the open desk. AI Moscow still evaluates the full target set. Negotiation tables reduce release pressure and foreign dependency, loyal administrators deepen Moscow influence inside a contacted republic, military ultimatums pressure dangerous or foreign-backed republics, and punitive operations let Moscow directly attack a republic that has crossed the danger gates.

Foreign intervention costs should rely less on political power and more on concrete commitments. Recognition, liaison, construction, press, conference, League logistics, anti-client clauses, protection treaties, and client-cabinet moves use reduced round political-power costs while retaining their separate convoys, fuel, command power, equipment, stability, manpower, and temporary consumer-goods burdens. Foreign command-power costs are conservative and capped at 80. Fuel commitments are deliberately heavy, especially for intelligence, construction, aid corridors, League logistics, protection treaties, and client-cabinet operations. Construction missions scale from the sponsor economy through `calculate_economy_scaled_factory_grant`: civilian plus military factories are counted in full 50-factory steps, with a minimum of one sponsored factory and a cap of five, then the target receives civilian or military factories from that calculated grant. Stronger sponsors also add a small scaled institution-and-resilience bonus to ordinary aid packages, and volunteer-corps missions can raise additional field brigades from that same sponsor scale while costing more manpower, rifles, and sponsor divisions.

Foreign leverage grows slowly. Aid decisions add a reduced influence delta before it is stored, then the delta is further reduced against regional, major, industrial, military, or resilient republics. Stronger republics also decay unused leverage faster. Dominance thresholds are high: a sponsor needs sustained, target-specific investment before dependency moves can become puppet pressure, and strong republics remain locked behind the stronger total-leverage and resilience checks.

Selected patron desks use `ROOT` as the owner and `FROM` as the chosen republic. The shared registry, target variables, selected flags, availability gates, completion effects, cooldown-preserving hide/show path, and symmetric cleanup cover base republics, Tajikistan, dynamic non-base republics, high-chaos successors, and the converted post-Union-Unmade surface. The five-case audit evidence lives in the July 11 selected-target lifecycle handoff rather than a tag-specific exception.

Focus rewards avoid direct idea stacking as a normal payoff. New focus rewards should prefer variable progression, state work, units, decisions, recognition, depot control, League coordination, or mechanical route payloads rather than adding a fresh visible spirit from each focus. Republican recovery removes startup disorder through hidden idea cleanup, flags, stability, institution strength, local authority, and practical equipment instead of granting a new stabilized-administration spirit. Custom splinter doctrine and endpoint helpers use flags, formations, construction, pressure, claims, faction moves, and stockpile work instead of handing out a new focus spirit. Tiny repeated train, truck, or isolated state-building rewards should be routed through shared rail authority, field-defense, and mobile-column helpers, so the visible result is supply nodes, defended capitals, deployed field columns, depot readiness, command capacity, or route progress rather than clutter. The External Support consolidated idea is reserved for republics that have actually received foreign aid or accepted dependency patronage. Self-directed liaison or diplomacy focuses can raise liaison, recognition, resilience, or patronage risk, but they must not mark the republic as externally supported by themselves.

High-chaos/custom splinter military routes are deliberately more dangerous than ordinary republic routes. Their war-plan and hidden-doctrine payoffs create assault and raider templates, spawn equipped formations, core controlled ground, and issue dynamic war goals against the Soviet remnant, exposed neighboring breakaways, and region-specific outer corridors when the chaos tier is severe enough. Assault formations scale from controlled states, civilian factories, military factories, existing divisions, and chaos tier, so larger or later high-chaos successor states enter expansion paths with much larger forces. This keeps chaos actors expansion-driven instead of defensive focus trees that only collect small stockpile or building rewards.

Country identity rewards should live on existing consolidated spirits where possible. The Pale Railway Authority uses its Pale Railway Authority spirit and railway decisions to build railways, infrastructure, and supply hubs, while the Dead Soldiers' Congress uses its Congress spirit for mass recruitable-population pressure rather than a separate new idea.

The Dead Soldiers' Congress focus tree connects its roll-call and revenant routes to concrete expansion mechanics instead of only stockpile rewards. `Maps of Lost Armies` and `Claim the Soldiers' Road` unlock a front-road claims decision that claims the Congress' old front-road states and cores any of those states already controlled by the Congress. `Armies That Do Not Demobilize`, `Congress of the Dead Army`, and the dead-army endgame unlock or fire the dead-army forward order, which raises additional assault columns, refreshes front-road claims and cores, and issues annexation war plans against non-allied neighboring countries that can be attacked. This keeps the Congress tied to its high-chaos identity: casualty rolls, unfinished orders, and old front roads become manpower, claims, cores, and offensive wars.

The Civilian Factory of Russia is a construction-directorate successor, not a normal minor with a few factory buttons. Its construction-mandate route unlocks stronger mandate-spending decisions from the factory and builder-state focuses. `Seize Idle Construction Queues` converts stranded cranes, municipal permits, and abandoned work crews into a large civilian factory reserve plus several reopened core construction sites. `Raise the Factory-City Belt` is the heavier late route action: it spends more mandates to add a very large civilian factory reserve and turns every controlled core state into a factory-and-infrastructure site. Both decisions use the existing Construction Mandates idea and variables instead of adding new visible spirits.

The CFR focus layout keeps governance, strategy, city, factory, foreign-contract, and planner end-state lanes compact but separated so pathlines do not run through mutually exclusive choices. `The Plan Is the Law` is the planner-route payoff rather than a token modifier: it raises construction mandates, site registry depth, contract-network depth, and rebuild-network depth, rebuilds every controlled core with industry and infrastructure, adds a supply hub, and strengthens the existing factory-city decision chain without adding another national spirit.

Old Great Bulgaria's Volga route separates compact diplomacy from rival restoration. `Treat with Idel-Ural` keeps the compact lane diplomatic, while `The Volga Cannot Have Two Seals` immediately presses the Volga trade-city claim package, cores controlled trade-city claims, and prepares Idel-Ural war plans when possible. The later `Future Bulgaria File` remains the broader restoration file for routes that have not already forced the claim issue.

Union Unmade terminal collapse releases the ordinary base republics, runs the current-tier internal release pass, and then performs an exhaustive all-possible core-country sweep so every remaining releasable country in the former Union can be pulled into the terminal rupture. Remaining Soviet subjects are freed as breakaway actors before terminal war setup, so puppet states do not stay attached to Moscow after the Union has broken. Existing Central Asian League leaders absorb newly released eligible Central Asian republics during terminal league reconciliation. Before Union Unmade, internal, niche, and high-chaos successor pools are opened by evolution tier and selected by crisis pressure instead of being forced only because time has passed. Breakaway setup gives non-allied neighboring breakaways claims, war plans, and AI hostility against each other, but regional League members do not inherit the generic automatic neighbor-war payload. League wars need a mandate target: an eligible missing regional member, a contested breakaway border during severe chaos, or a high-chaos external corridor tied to the League's region. Maximum chaos can still become a multi-front rupture, but League aggression is routed through visible claims instead of blanket league-on-league wars.

The League branches are wired into the decision system instead of ending as flat reward rows. Early League focus depth unlocks League rifle and motor-column deployment decisions. Deeper League focus depth unlocks the regional security-zone mandate for regional-faction leaders. The mandate is a targeted decision and costs political power, command power, and fuel before issuing a League-backed war goal. Its target logic is shared and reusable. Central Asian leaders can pressure misaligned Central Asian republics such as Kazakhstan if they are outside the Central Asian League. Baltic leaders can press severe-crisis border claims such as Lithuania's Belarus question. High-chaos League leaders can pursue region-specific outer corridors such as Caucasus pressure to the south or Central Asian pressure toward Iran, Afghanistan, or northwestern China. The Ukrainian League branch keeps its bespoke council surface. League focuses open the League Council, arms quotas, grain relief, anti-client protection, `External Border Arbitration`, and `League Security Zone Mandates`. `External Border Arbitration` lets Ukraine spend League arbitration capacity to create a war goal against eligible neighboring non-collapse countries. `League Security Zone Mandates` spends the Black Sea security mission package to create a stronger League-backed war goal against eligible external neighbors that are coastal, major, or regionally industrial. These decisions reuse existing regional decision icons and do not require new sprites.
