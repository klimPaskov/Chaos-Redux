## Identity and boundaries

Event 23 uses the `chaosx.nr23.1` root and is a Minor Fire-Once event for `SOV` at Chaos level 2. Its opening option is deliberately additive: it calls the shared nuclear stockpile effect with an amount of exactly 100 and never replaces the pre-existing national stockpile.

The event owns authorization, actor identity, action context, selected target or site, device reservation, progression state, custody classification, and Event 23 records. The Event 23-owned shared action adapter is the one native `launch_nuke` boundary; shared nuclear systems alone own detonation consequences and terminal Fallout. Every Event 23 test, demonstration, strike, accident, and demolition enters the single shared action contract in `common/scripted_effects/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_runtime_effects.txt`, with no second consequence path.

Event 23 remains separate from Events 5, 32, 47, and 76, the shared Fallout system, and Final Silence. It uses neutral hooks only. It does not register an Arms-race cluster, add a triggerable scenario, or add a world-end catalog row. It has no focus tree, tag, portrait, flag, custom unit, counter, 3D model, scripted GUI, or animation.

## Progression ladder

The event-owned ledger begins at 100 devices from the opening grant. Each enabled evolution is contiguous and records only after its complete gate is satisfied.

| Evolution | Cumulative event-owned devices | Post-baseline increment | Event-owned reactor count | Additional gate | Disabled behavior |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| I | 175 | 75 | 2 | Chaos threshold and Event 23 actor validity | Grants, unlocks, and records nothing |
| II | 275 | 100 | 4 | Evolution I plus its Chaos threshold and valid world conditions | Grants, unlocks, and records nothing |
| III | 400 | 125 | 6 | Evolution II plus its world-state gate and another nuclear major | Grants, unlocks, and records nothing |
| IV | 600 | 200 | 8 | Evolution III plus its world-state gate | Grants, unlocks, and records nothing |

The progression effects are centralized in `common/scripted_effects/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_event_effects.txt` and the ladder values are in `common/script_constants/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_constants.txt`. The evolution pacing centers are 120, 120, 180, and 90 days for Evolutions I through IV. The log tier mapping follows the actual Chaos meter tiers: Evolution I uses `tier_2`, Evolution II `tier_3`, Evolution III `tier_4`, and Evolution IV `tier_final`.

Evolution II opens ordinary minor and breakaway coercion. Evolution III opens multi-major retaliation and the nonterminal exchange route only when the world-state gate exists. Evolution IV is the only stage at which Soviet AI first use against a nuclear major can be eligible, and it additionally requires the severe strategic-loss, war, route, command, target, device, and stand-down gates. High Chaos by itself never permits first use.

### AI phase navigation

The Soviet AI moves between task families through `sov_nuclear_bombs_ai_route_existing_phase`, run only by the hidden coalesced receiver `chaosx.nr23.182` with SOV as ROOT.
`sov_nuclear_bombs_ai_request_phase_review` schedules that receiver once for an AI Soviet actor; completion and cancel helpers, the `.160`, `.161` and `.162` verification events, evolution and incoming-attack phase requests, and the existing 30-day `on_daily_SOV` maintenance interval request reviews.
The router is one deterministic first-match table and writes at most one phase:

| Order | Condition | Board |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Human, invalid or non-Soviet actor | No change; navigation fields cleared |
| 2 | Opening doctrine, locked command, or live work (`sov_nuclear_bombs_event_navigation_is_protected`) | Held; a displaced staged test, strike or dossier board is restored |
| 3 | Atomic Moratorium | Moratorium 8 |
| 4 | Custody collapse | Collapse 7 |
| 5 | Retaliation duty or an Evolution IV first-use candidate | Retaliation 6 |
| 6 | Uninvestigated test failure | Test 3 |
| 7 | Custody duty: first audit, a dispute after a full audit interval, one hardened depot holding devices, release authority from Evolution II | Custody 1 |
| 8 | Production duty: reactor entitlement, crews, exercise, authentication, or a replacement deficit | Production 2 |
| 9 | Test purpose: one test per attained stage with a ready, reachable test site | Test 3 |
| 10 | Coercion purpose: Evolution II, device backing and a current independent enemy minor not yet coerced this stage | Coercion 4 |
| 11 | Otherwise | Custody 1 |

Rows 3 to 5 apply at once; rows 6 to 11 respect the existing 14-day `phase_action_cooldown` as a navigation dwell with one coalesced retry on the due day.
The router never pays, grants, reserves, launches, settles or applies consequences, and it never iterates over every country or state.
Evolution and incoming-attack phase writes go through `sov_nuclear_bombs_apply_event_phase_request`, so they never replace live work, a moratorium, a custody collapse or the opening doctrine for any actor.
AI eligibility follows the same purposes: assembly needs a replacement deficit below the attained stage's grant reference, the survey ignores sites that cannot host a test, hardening ignores empty depots, and coercion selection skips the counterpart already coerced at the current stage.
Because custody is the idle board, the AI enters the Atomic Moratorium only with a restraint reason (`sov_nuclear_bombs_event_ai_has_restraint_reason`: a demonstrated or employed arsenal, a serious nuclear crisis or test accident, or a verified restraint agreement), disperses a reserve once per attained stage (`sov_nuclear_bombs_ai_dispersal_stage`), and marks only retaliation or first-use states its bomber force can reach.
A survey raises readiness once; moving the survey to another site while the first holds no device only changes the selected test ground.
Aborting a prepared strike gives up its marked target unless a coercion dossier still owns that state.

### Evolution II and Evolution IV use

An Evolution II limited strike against a nonnuclear minor or breakaway goes through the coercion dossier, strike preparation, technical certification and a separate final authorization.
The AI final-authorization weight is zero only when the staged state belongs to a nuclear major and the staged first-use gate does not hold.
Outside a confirmed exchange, the retaliation selector offers lower-consequence states of an enemy nuclear major only under `sov_nuclear_bombs_event_prospective_first_use_gate`: the full Evolution IV emergency, one line of release authority, unsuspended orders, no verified stand-down and an unreserved device.
Strike preparation then stages that marked state once and enters authorization; certification, final authorization and the runtime first-use guard still decide release, and no exchange receipt is created.

Reserve dispersal is a two-endpoint action: the player selects the destination state, and the runtime locks one valid assigned source depot from the bounded Soviet custody pool when the mission starts.
Departure moves one site's assigned device into an explicit transit subset while global and actor assigned totals continue to include it.
The exact source, destination, holder and nonce persist; arrival credits the destination, while endpoint loss settles the device into returned, transferred, or missing custody before closing the same receipt.
The transit subset is never added as a second term in the conservation equation, and completion does not select another source.

Device assembly selects one registered Soviet industrial storage depot through the existing action.
The existing 120-day mission keeps its exact state pointer, id, owner and nonce; a surviving reactor program, atomic technologies, factory and Soviet ownership/control are revalidated before the batch is registered.
Loss of the depot or reactor support cancels the production order without moving work, granting devices, or passing Soviet technical access to a successor.
The bounded Part 6 registry distinguishes historical assembly/production facilities, central and backup command locations, institutional access, delivery owners, transit, and active dismantlement from physical custody.
Source lifecycle coverage does not establish save/reload conservation or a real native bomber delivery route.

## Custody, transfers, and ledger conservation

Physical custody, technical access, command formation, and delivery integration are separate state classifications. A breakaway can hold or attempt to recover devices without becoming operational immediately; staged operationalization and native stockpile movement exist in source. Event 23 does not itself expose a breakaway launch action, and operational/native delivery acceptance is still unproven.

Each breakaway keeps its own selected depot pointer and state ID through the three month-scale missions.
The existing Secure Custody action selects another depot after the current one completes or ceases to be valid, without replacing Soviet selection or another actor's project.
Physical, technical and command receipts belong to that exact site's current holder, with mismatched institutional records contested after a holder change.
Joint custody and pending Event 5 reconciliation block unilateral progress.
Country stage flags project only the selected depot's receipts, while historical operational registration remains separate.
Operationalization also requires the current holder's active delivery stage for the exact state ID, and each cancellation rechecks the corresponding local receipt.

Event 5 integration brackets its direct state transfers and release operations with a pre-transfer snapshot and explicit post-transfer reconciliation.
Each exact affected state records its owner, controller, site buckets, wider command context and bounded registry roles before the native command.
An in-progress receipt prevents a callback from closing the snapshot inside that command; the explicit post-transfer hook closes it once and captures the final context.
Bounded `on_state_control_changed`, `on_release_as_free`, and `on_release_as_puppet` callbacks retain the same reconciliation boundary.
Ordinary control changes reconcile transit and active dismantlement before custody rebinding, then compare institutional and delivery markers to the final holder without granting those capabilities.
If `SOV` disappears, Soviet-only production and evolution effects stop; valid local custody and transferred ledgers remain tracked.

The implemented Event 23 reserve, release, transfer, demolition, expenditure, and launch wrappers call ledger helpers in `023_sov_nuclear_bombs_runtime_effects.txt`. A native launch stores the exact expected post-launch `num_of_nukes` value together with the pending state and action nonce; a callback that does not produce that exact stockpile delta moves the reservation to the missing/disputed buckets and blocks breakaway operationalization or Soviet recovery until the dispute is resolved. Event 5 snapshots preserve the pending reservation on its source state and carry a generation receipt through post-transfer reconciliation. Exact mixed-stockpile attribution, ordinary control changes outside Event 5, and save/reload conservation remain live evidence items rather than source assumptions.

Site and actor gates admit exactly one device across transferred, assigned, reserved, operational, breakaway, return and dismantlement paths.
Exact one-device conservation through callbacks, save/reload and mixed native stockpiles still needs verification.
The reactor waiting verifier evaluates its state predicate inside `event_target:sov_nuclear_bombs_pending_reactor_state`.
Native construction-job identity, cancellation and entitlement reconciliation remain unresolved.

Rail security, depot recall, and joint custody require a selected breakaway site with infrastructure and railway access, and the available/completion paths also require Soviet control and a division in the selected state where that security gate applies. This is a source-level route and force predicate. It does not prove a connected end-to-end rail path, supply, or a held corridor under the live map/mission consumer.

## Native action boundary and delivery status

The installed atomic capability is the vanilla `atomic_research` plus `nukes` route. Event 23's authorization chain releases through the native `launch_nuke` effect, the same effect the vanilla nuclear raid outcomes call, behind a preflight that mirrors the installed `nuclear_strike` raid: the strategic-bomber capability (`strategic_bomber1` or the BBA `iw_large_airframe` route), at least ten deployed strategic bombers (`constant:sov_nuclear_bombs_delivery.deployed_bomber_floor`, where the `strategic_bomber` sub-unit type also covers intercontinental bombers), fuel, a controlled air base, crew certification, the exact-endpoint distance ceiling, and war with the target for combat use. No missile, thermonuclear, free-aircraft, unrelated technology, or substitute equipment is granted.

The action adapter validates the Event 23 actor, authorization, action type, target or site context, terminal-state exclusion, native technology, operational device stock, and selected-state preflight before reservation and execution. Its shared context gate permits a human Evolution III exchange action only after the explicit multi-major world gate or confirmed reciprocal exchange receipt, while Soviet AI first use against a nuclear major remains impossible before Evolution IV and still requires the complete severe strategic-loss gate. Limited non-major combat use remains a distinct Evolution II route and records generic Soviet combat use without creating a pre-Evolution-IV major first-use route.

Actor proof, release authorization and remote-recovery permission are country flags.
Exactly one detonating or non-detonating flag supplies the physical-yield profile, with absent or contradictory profiles rejected by context, callback and receipt checks.
Pending delivery retains those flags until its receipt is resolved, and context cleanup clears them together.
The shared non-yield accident endpoint checks the same flag profile and committed nonce without applying a second device debit or nuclear detonation.

Installed effect documentation describes `launch_nuke` as a detonation effect and documents no script effect that starts a raid or assigns aircraft, so the launch_nuke release behind this preflight is the accepted Event 23 delivery (decision D2; see `docs/plans/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_plans/subagent_handoffs/023_vanilla_delivery_route_audit_2026-09-26.md`).
The preflight does not model mission assignment, actual wing range or air superiority.
Vanilla nuclear raids stay available as the parallel native route.
A vanilla raid draws its device when the raid is created; while no Event 23 delivery is pending, the daily ledger refresh books that shortfall as expended devices (operational first, then assigned depot mirrors), tolerates a later surplus such as a refund, and leaves a shortfall that would reach reserved devices as a genuine dispute that a completed stockpile audit can clear once the books balance.
A Soviet `on_nuke_drop` that is not the pending Event 23 request and strikes a state the Union does not control counts as combat use (a blast in a Soviet-held state, such as another event's accident, is not recorded as use), sets public knowledge to used, breaches an active moratorium or a pending or verified stand-down or settlement, records the major receipt when the struck state's controller is a nuclear major, and refreshes achievement receipts without a second consequence call.
`common/raids/nuclear_raids.txt` copies the installed raids with one added `factor = 0` AI modifier per raid type under `sov_nuclear_bombs_native_raid_ai_restrained`, so the Soviet AI uses nuclear weapons only through the Event 23 authorization chain while human players and other countries keep vanilla raids.
