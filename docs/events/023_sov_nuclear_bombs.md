## Identity and boundaries

Event 23 uses the `chaosx.nr23.1` root and is a Minor Fire-Once event for `SOV` at Chaos level 2. Its opening option is deliberately additive: it calls the shared nuclear stockpile effect with an amount of exactly 100 and never replaces the pre-existing national stockpile.

The event owns authorization, actor identity, action context, selected target or site, device reservation, progression state, custody classification, and Event 23 records. The Event 23-owned shared action adapter is the one native `launch_nuke` boundary; shared nuclear systems alone own detonation consequences and terminal Fallout. Every Event 23 test, demonstration, strike, accident, and demolition enters the single shared action contract in `common/scripted_effects/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_runtime_effects.txt`, with no second consequence path.

Event 23 remains separate from Events 5, 32, 47, and 76, the shared Fallout system, and Final Silence. It uses neutral hooks only. It does not register an Arms-race cluster, add a triggerable scenario, or add a world-end catalog row. It has no focus tree, tag, portrait, flag, custom unit, counter, 3D model, scripted GUI, or animation.

## Progression ladder

The event-owned ledger begins at 100 devices from the opening grant. Each enabled evolution is contiguous and records only after its complete gate is satisfied.

| Evolution | Cumulative event-owned devices | Post-baseline increment | Event-owned reactor count | Additional gate | Disabled behavior |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| I | 175 | 75 | 2 | Chaos threshold and Event 23 actor validity | Grants, unlocks, and records nothing |
| II | 275 | 100 | 4 | Evolution I plus its Chaos threshold and valid world conditions | Grants, unlocks, and records nothing |
| III | 400 | 125 | 6 | Evolution II plus its world-state gate and another nuclear major | Grants, unlocks, and records nothing |
| IV | 600 | 200 | 8 | Evolution III plus its world-state gate | Grants, unlocks, and records nothing |

The progression effects are centralized in `common/scripted_effects/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_event_effects.txt` and the ladder values are in `common/script_constants/023_sov_nuclear_bombs_constants.txt`. The log tier mapping follows the actual Chaos meter tiers: Evolution I uses `tier_2`, Evolution II `tier_3`, Evolution III `tier_4`, and Evolution IV `tier_final`.

Evolution II opens ordinary minor and breakaway coercion. Evolution III opens multi-major retaliation and the nonterminal exchange route only when the world-state gate exists. Evolution IV is the only stage at which Soviet AI first use against a nuclear major can be eligible, and it additionally requires the severe strategic-loss, war, route, command, target, device, and stand-down gates. High Chaos by itself never permits first use.

The source is not re-randomized at completion; source loss or endpoint invalidation fails closed and leaves the ledger unchanged.

## Custody, transfers, and ledger conservation

Physical custody, technical access, command formation, and delivery integration are separate state classifications. A breakaway can hold or attempt to recover devices without becoming operational immediately; staged operationalization and native stockpile movement exist in source. Event 23 does not itself expose a breakaway launch action, and operational/native delivery acceptance is still unproven.

Event 5 integration uses a pre-transfer custody snapshot and post-transfer reconciliation. Immediately before each of the four Event 5 release branches, `SOV = { sov_nuclear_bombs_snapshot_event5_release_tranche = yes }` records the exact state owner, controller, site flags, and ledger buckets. Bounded `on_state_control_changed`, `on_release_as_free`, and `on_release_as_puppet` callbacks reconcile the saved snapshot after transfer. If `SOV` disappears, Soviet-only production and evolution effects stop; valid local custody and transferred ledgers remain tracked.

The implemented Event 23 reserve, release, transfer, demolition, expenditure, and launch wrappers call ledger helpers in `023_sov_nuclear_bombs_runtime_effects.txt`. A native launch stores the exact expected post-launch `num_of_nukes` value together with the pending state and action nonce; a callback that does not produce that exact stockpile delta moves the reservation to the missing/disputed buckets and blocks breakaway operationalization or Soviet recovery until the dispute is resolved. Event 5 snapshots preserve the pending reservation on its source state and carry a generation receipt through post-transfer reconciliation. Exact mixed-stockpile attribution, ordinary control changes outside Event 5, and save/reload conservation remain live evidence items rather than source assumptions.

The current pass changed the site and actor gates that had required more than one device to admit a site with exactly one device. This includes transferred, assigned, reserved, operational, breakaway, return, and dismantlement paths in the touched source. A targeted source search found no remaining `site_* > one` predicate in the inspected Event 23 decision, effect, and trigger files; exact one-device conservation through callbacks, save/reload, and mixed native stockpiles still needs verification. The reactor waiting verifier now enters `event_target:sov_nuclear_bombs_pending_reactor_state` before evaluating its state predicate, correcting the previously documented country-scope invocation. Native construction-job identity, cancellation, and entitlement reconciliation remain unresolved.

Rail security, depot recall, and joint custody now require a selected breakaway site with infrastructure and railway access, and the available/completion paths also require Soviet control and a division in the selected state where that security gate applies. This is a source-level route and force predicate. It does not prove a connected end-to-end rail path, supply, or a held corridor under the live map/mission consumer.

## Verified nuclear action route

The installed atomic capability is the vanilla `atomic_research` plus `nukes` route. The selected delivery route accepts the installed strategic bomber capability (`strategic_bomber1` or the BBA `iw_large_airframe` route), requires deployed strategic bombers and an owned, controlled airbase, and then calls the native `launch_nuke` effect with the selected state. No missile, thermonuclear, free-aircraft, unrelated technology, or substitute equipment is granted.

The action adapter validates the Event 23 actor, authorization, action type, target or site context, terminal-state exclusion, native technology, operational device stock, and selected-state route before reservation and execution. Its shared context gate permits a human Evolution III exchange action only after the explicit multi-major world gate or confirmed reciprocal exchange receipt, while Soviet AI first use against a nuclear major remains impossible before Evolution IV and still requires the complete severe strategic-loss gate. Limited non-major combat use remains a distinct Evolution II route and records generic Soviet combat use without creating a pre-Evolution-IV major first-use route. The exact native range trigger is not documented by the installed vanilla documentation, so the package uses a selected-state, ownership, controller, airbase, bomber, and native launch route and records this engine-evidence limitation instead of inventing a trigger.
