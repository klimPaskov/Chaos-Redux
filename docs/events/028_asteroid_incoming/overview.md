Status: enabled in the default event-log queue and awaiting native verification.
The source pass and its remaining engine, cross-owner, catalog, and presentation gates are recorded in [takeover_status_2026-10-06.md](../../plans/028_asteroid_incoming_plans/takeover_status_2026-10-06.md).
Current source includes crater actions, measured impact receipts, Event Details and evolution toggles, recovery missions with a grace window, emergency closure, mission-failure consequences, capital continuity, and scaled repair prices.
The earlier blanket main-crater-clearance and nuclear-separation claims are superseded by the explicit limits below.
Each design choice and its basis is recorded in [agent_decisions_2026-10-04.md](../../plans/028_asteroid_incoming_plans/agent_decisions_2026-10-04.md); catalog row changes wait in [catalog_changes_pending.md](../../plans/028_asteroid_incoming_plans/catalog_changes_pending.md) for the catalog owner.

Event 028 is a global fire-once Major event with a Calm World baseline. The shared Major-event registry owns its pacing and history classification, while `events/028_asteroid_impact.txt` owns the visible chain and the Event 028 scripted effects own the transaction.

## Gameplay flow

Before automatic selection, a read-only preflight requires three distinct countries other than the chooser, each with two usable owned and controlled states. The opening transaction selects exactly three distinct valid country/state pairs from that eligible population. It persists each country and state as a global event target, refuses wasteland and previously registered asteroid sites, and exposes a genuine miss option. Countries with only one usable state, occupied backups, or the CXT system identity cannot consume a target slot. When enabled, an invalid world shows `N/A` with the target-pool reason in the shared event view and remains outside automatic selection. A forced invalid opening retains its explicit `N/A` option.

The shared dispatcher builds the target board before dispatch and Major history accounting, and rejects a failed board without consuming the event. The chooser selects one of the three saved pairs and receives a confirmation event. Cancel returns through the internal `chaosx.nr28.8` board, preserving the existing pairs, chooser, opening date, and evolution snapshot without re-firing the native fire-once entry. The board's validity is stored once in the global flag `asteroid_incoming_board_ready`: the dispatcher sets it on a successful board, `chaosx.nr28.1` re-validates once in `immediate` (rebuilding the still-unseen board when nothing is locked and the pairs are missing or invalid), and `chaosx.nr28.8` re-validates without rerolling. The description (`GetAsteroidIncomingOpeningDesc`) and every board option read that flag, so the text and the choices agree when a pending popup is restored from a save, and the target-pool reset, lock, miss, fail-closed close, and cleanup paths clear it. Confirmation copies the selected pair into locked targets and stores the original state id and capital status. The lock lasts two days and the resolver uses the saved scope pointers, so owner, controller, tag, and subject changes cannot silently reroll the coordinates. The locked target country chooses one preparedness stance before the impact. At the lock, `global.asteroid_incoming_expected_impact_date` is set to the current date plus `asteroid_incoming_runtime.lock_duration_days` through `chaosx_convert_days_to_date_offset`, and the `chaosx.nr28.4` resolver delay is injected from the same constant, so the trajectory-locked notice (`chaosx.news.30`) names the intended state, country, continent and the real impact date without revealing fragment sites. Miss, fail-closed and finished cleanup clear the date.
The hidden resolver is queued on both the chooser and the locked target country, because a queued event is dropped when its country ceases to exist; the resolver reads no ROOT, and its existing guards make the second callback a no-op. If both callbacks are lost, the monthly `asteroid_incoming_resolve_orphaned_transaction` watchdog (called from `asteroid_incoming_update_achievements`, which runs whether or not the Chaos Meter is enabled) finds the lock past `global.asteroid_incoming_lock_expires_day`, sets `asteroid_incoming_lock_overdue` so `asteroid_incoming_locked_transaction_is_valid` accepts it, and fires the resolver on the global host; the flag is cleared by `asteroid_incoming_cleanup_finished_transaction`. A board or confirmation whose chooser is annexed closes at once through `on_annex`, and the watchdog catches any other chooser that no longer exists; both use `asteroid_incoming_close_unavailable_board`, so the fail-closed history outcome is written and no new chooser or coordinate is picked.

The impact resolver plans the main footprint and optional fragments before changing any state. It merges overlapping profiles by severity, processes each state once, measures population before and after the loss, registers only actual deaths through the shared Deaths ledger, removes civilian buildings from the main crater, and sends one consolidated report per affected country. It relocates only a country whose current capital is the locked center. Backup selection prefers controlled cores and safer territory, then uses surviving ring states when no safer candidate exists. A current planned fragment center remains a surviving candidate, while an existing or processed crater does not. Relocation accounting names the country that actually moved, even when owner and controller differ, and an annexer receives no original-target achievement identity. When no settled, fully controlled backup exists, relocation falls back to any controlled owned state, then any owned state even if occupied, then any controlled state. If none qualifies, the impact still proceeds, the capital stays at the crater, the country receives the full Government Dislocation for the capital duration, Government Beyond the Crater stays closed, and the impact report and Event Details record that the government could not move.

Each affected country's report is a few plain-text sentences for light parchment, built from its impact receipt by the report getters in `common/scripted_localisation/028_asteroid_incoming_scripted_localisation.txt`. `chaosx.nr28.5` (the country holds or owns a main-footprint state) and `chaosx.nr28.7` (fragment damage only) open with the worst-hit state, described by the country's strongest damage profile (`GetAsteroidIncomingReportOpening`), then give the civilian deaths and the number of damaged states (`GetAsteroidIncomingReportToll`) and the destroyed transport and industrial building levels when there are any (`GetAsteroidIncomingReportDamage`). The main-impact report adds the capital sentence only when the government moved or could not move (`GetAsteroidIncomingReportCapitalClause`), and the destroyed and dispersed trains, trucks and fuel only for the original target (`GetAsteroidIncomingTransportLossClause`). Both reports close with the current recovery phase (emergency, reconstruction, dust protection or crater security) and the most useful Asteroid Recovery order open now (`GetAsteroidIncomingReportNextStep`), chosen with the decisions' own visibility triggers: emergency work prefers mobile hospitals where a rescue burden is active, then the emergency rail corridor when transport levels were lost, then debris clearance. A zero count never prints, and none of these strings carries a colour code.
The impact news and the super-event name each struck continent once. Right after the fragment planner, `asteroid_incoming_record_fragment_continents` saves the first applied fragment centre on each continent, in strike order, as the global event target `asteroid_incoming_fragment_continent_<n>_state` and counts them in `global.asteroid_incoming_fragment_continent_count`; `GetAsteroidIncomingFragmentContinents` lists them after the fragment count.

The main state receives a permanent nonradioactive crater marker.
Ordinary provincial buildings use `asteroid_incoming_damage_selected_provincial_building`, while state buildings use level removal.
A full-loss profile zeroes all matching provincial installations; ring profiles process original levels in ascending cohorts and remove the rounded fraction of each province, so a reduced province cannot be selected again.
The adapter covers the ordinary provincial types registered by `028_asteroid_incoming_province_triggers.txt`, including forts, ports, facilities, camps, dams, canals, and landmarks.
This is per-province rounding, not a rounded state-total quota: four level-one forts under a 22.5 percent profile each remain at one.
Railways and supply nodes are special network objects and remain outside this ordinary setter.
The inherited main-centre damage calls address only the first matching network province; complete network destruction and queued construction or special-project cancellation are not proven and remain blocked.
The wasteland category and crater modifiers suppress normal rebuilding but do not establish that a queue was purged.
Units in the footprint are struck where they stand: `asteroid_incoming_damage_units_in_state` runs once for each processed state inside the impact transaction, before the reports, and applies one native `damage_units` call to every army in the state, of any country, with ratio strength and organisation damage read from `asteroid_incoming_unit_damage` by the state's strongest profile. The damage falls from the main centre (75 percent strength, 95 percent organisation) through the catastrophic, major and outer rings (45/75, 25/50 and 8/25), fragment profiles are lighter (35/65 at a fragment centre, 20/40 and 5/20 in its rings), and ships are struck too in the main centre and the catastrophic ring. No value removes a unit outright, and a miss or a fail-closed trajectory never reaches this step. The `launch_nuke` visual's own province damage comes on top at the main centre and each fragment centre.

Actual deaths, destroyed capacity, affected states, fragments, and bounded supported secondary disasters feed Atmospheric Dust Load. The shared world dust state moves through Residual Haze, Global Dust Veil, Impact Winter, and Severe Impact Winter, applies production, supply, air, and weather penalties, writes a separate Air Cleanliness source slot, and decays through the Event 028 maintenance hooks. Basic, organized, and hardened national protection reduce the active stage's penalties by 15 percent, 30 percent, and 45 percent. A hidden country dynamic modifier supplies the compensation beneath the existing visible protection idea, so protection cannot turn fading dust into a production bonus.

The opening Air Cleanliness injection is consumed once. Monthly reservoir pressure follows the current dust stage and global mitigation, and a date receipt prevents repeated coordinator calls on the same day from charging it twice. Falling stages and zero dust reduce the source's current pressure without refunding its historical injection. The shared Air Cleanliness recovery remains responsible for removing atmospheric contamination.

Event 013 secondary calls use one exact supported family, the affected state, and an explicit main-impact or fragment origin. The gateway validates geography and family availability. The four-call limit counts accepted scheduled jobs, and rejected families add no dust. Its own sequence and reports retain Event 028 as the caller.

At 600 Chaos, Global Fragmentation adds three to six independently planned fragment sites, two damage rings, overlap resolution, grouped reporting, and additional dust. Fragment centers prefer a valid land neighbor whenever the remaining global pool provides one, with a bounded fallback for genuinely isolated candidates. At 800 Chaos, Extraordinary Minerals is eligible only when fragmentation actually applied, and the opening snapshot records its eligibility only when Global Fragmentation was recorded in the same snapshot, so the board never announces minerals while fragmentation is disabled. Both Chaos thresholds are read once, in the opening snapshot; the shared Events Log toggles (`events_log_disabled_evolution_28_2801_1` and `28_2802_1`) are read again at impact, so disabling Global Fragmentation after the opening gives a clean main-only impact and disabling Extraordinary Minerals keeps the fragments without mineral bonuses. Evolution history rows are written only for evolutions that applied. The main crater controller receives `+100%` land-division armour and each currently controlled fragment site contributes `+20%`; the control refresh removes stale ownership benefits and rebuilds the uncapped current-control total.

Each crater site carries one persistent scientific profile. When the main crater is registered in `asteroid_incoming_apply_state_profile` and when each applied fragment centre is committed in `asteroid_incoming_choose_fragment_sites`, `asteroid_incoming_assign_crater_site_profile` rolls a `random_list` once, with weights from `asteroid_incoming_site_profile`, and stores the id in the state variable `asteroid_incoming_crater_site_profile`: dense metallic material (1), fused silicate composite (2) or shock-formed crystal and glass (3). A site that already has a profile keeps it, so nothing re-rolls it. Survey Extraordinary Material reveals it: its tooltip names the profile from the first samples (`GetAsteroidIncomingCraterSiteProfile`), the survey adds the matching named state marker (`asteroid_incoming_crater_material_dense_metallic`, `_fused_silicate` or `_shock_crystal`, each with its own in-world description and no modifier values), and the one-use `asteroid_incoming_crater.survey_research_bonus` goes to industry, synthetic resources or electronics respectively (`GetAsteroidIncomingCraterSiteResearchField`). Once any country has surveyed a site, Reconnoitre a Crater Site names its material (`GetAsteroidIncomingReconSiteProfileClause`). No profile changes armour or any AI weight. The three distinct profile icons are documented in [material_profiles.md](assets/material_profiles.md).

Ordinary recovery uses the shared decision framework under `asteroid_incoming_recovery_category`. Each country stores its pending paid project's state in `asteroid_incoming_recovery_target_state`. A pending project blocks another project in that country, while other countries retain independent targets. Finish callbacks recheck the reserved state and current control, apply the project, and always release the project. Cancellation, lost control, and annexation release only that country's receipts. The category exposes reason-specific blocked tooltips, meaningful manpower/equipment/fuel/logistical costs, reserve floors for AI and players, national closeout, cleanup, and one-time action flags. There is no dedicated Event 028 scripted GUI. The category header (`asteroid_incoming_recovery_category_desc`) shows the current dust stage, national protection, and the recovery burden the country carries now through `GetAsteroidIncomingRecoveryPhase`: the full burden, the lighter legacy after a mission success, the burden extended after a failed mission, or completed recovery. While Extraordinary Minerals is active, `GetAsteroidIncomingCraterControlClause` adds whether the country directly controls the main crater and how many fragment sites it controls; the clause is empty otherwise.

Each of the seven state-targeted recovery actions lists its highest-ranked affordable state.
Emergency order is live rescue burden, impact profile, population, then lower state id; reconstruction order is profile, infrastructure plus railway gap, then lower state id.
Both the current target and its competitors must pass the stored quote and reserve checks, including the same medical or transport first-response discount that payment uses.
An unaffordable high-priority state cannot hide a cheaper valid state.
Crater work uses `028_asteroid_incoming_crater_priority_triggers.txt`: one highest-profile ready owned site and one ready rival site, with lower state id breaking ties.
Owned work advances through security, survey, and fortification; rival work advances through reconnaissance, disruption, and an offensive plan, skipping disruption when its field or reserve conditions cannot be met.
With three dust projects, this gives at most five primary late-recovery actions.
The rival pool is the persistent mineral-site array, never the whole map.
Operation cooldown flags expire with their disruption and offensive modifiers; security lapses on a control change, while scientific survey and its research use remain permanent.

## Recovery prices

Every affected non-crater state carries one price quote that the cost text, the cost check, the reserve check and the payment all read (`asteroid_incoming_quote_state_recovery_costs`). The multiplier is `asteroid_incoming_cost.base_multiplier` plus `profile_weight` per impact-profile point plus `population_loss_weight_per_k` per thousand people the state lost (capped at `population_loss_cap`), multiplied by `1 + failure_surcharge` while the controller carries `asteroid_incoming_repair_costs_raised`, and kept between `minimum_multiplier` and `maximum_multiplier`. Quotes are written at impact, refreshed by the monthly recovery pass over `global.asteroid_incoming_affected_states`, and refreshed at once for a country that takes the surcharge after an expired mission. The prepared first responses keep their lower base amounts under the same multiplier. Dust-protection projects read a global quote scaled by the current dust stage (`dust_residual` to `dust_severe`), recomputed whenever the stage is set. Each project still uses at most four spendable cost types and must leave the `asteroid_incoming_cost` reserves behind. Crater operations keep flat prices.

At impact, the original target loses `transport_loss_per_state` of its trains, trucks and fuel for each main-footprint state it controls, up to `transport_loss_cap`. The Disperse Transport preparation keeps `transport_dispersion_preserved_share` of that loss, and the impact report lists the destroyed and preserved amounts. The preparation no longer grants equipment.

## Recovery missions

A completed state project can start one of five missions for a player government: Keep the Rescue Route Open (mobile hospitals or emergency rail corridor), Stabilize the Damaged Zone (debris clearing), Reconnect the National Network (supply spine), Restore the Outer Ring (outer-ring industry), and Rebuild the Regional Economy (rehousing, original target country only). AI countries keep every project effect but receive no mission contract, because they cannot plan construction toward a mission target.

Each mission keeps its own state pointers (`asteroid_incoming_rescue_mission_state`, `asteroid_incoming_stabilize_mission_state_one`/`_two`, `asteroid_incoming_network_mission_state` and `asteroid_incoming_network_hub_state`, `asteroid_incoming_outer_mission_state`, `asteroid_incoming_economy_mission_state`), so later projects run while a mission is active and never rewrite its objective. No more than `asteroid_incoming_recovery.active_mission_limit` (two) missions run at once. The objective can be met from the end of its hold period, which closes `asteroid_incoming_recovery.mission_grace_days` (45) before the native timeout, and the mission completes as soon as its conditions hold after that day.

Repair targets come from the pre-impact snapshot: `asteroid_incoming_set_mission_repair_targets` stores `asteroid_incoming_recovery.mission_repair_share` (60 percent) of each state's infrastructure, railway, supply hubs, civilian factories and military factories, rounded to whole levels, so a building a state never had is never required. Before any damage or capital move, `asteroid_incoming_record_preimpact_rail_link` flags each footprint state that had a rail connection to its controller's capital, and rail objectives ask only for links that existed before the impact.

- Rescue route: the named state meets its infrastructure, railway and supply-hub targets and, if it was linked before the impact, has a rail connection to the current capital.
- Stabilize: the two worst-hit damaged states the country holds that still miss their targets are captured at activation (strongest impact profile first, then the larger infrastructure and railway gap) and must both meet their targets. Success closes their local emergencies so reconstruction can begin there.
- Network: every damaged state the country holds that was linked before the impact is linked to the current capital again, every damaged state meets its infrastructure target, and the largest industrial state that is or was on the network (captured at activation) is linked to the capital. Success closes emergency work in every damaged state the country holds and sets `asteroid_incoming_network_reconnected`.
- Outer ring: no held affected state keeps the Outer Shock Zone or Light Fragment Damage, and every held state of the major impact ring meets its infrastructure and both factory targets. Success sets `asteroid_incoming_outer_ring_restored`.
- Regional economy: the monthly recovery review compares the current factory, infrastructure and railway totals across the surviving damaged states the country holds with the same pre-impact totals, and each must reach `asteroid_incoming_recovery.regional_capacity_share` (75 percent).

Every success grants the recovery legacy. The four other legacies end with the burden they replaced, but Rebuild the Regional Economy's legacy is lasting: `asteroid_incoming_regional_economy_complete` makes `asteroid_incoming_refresh_national_recovery` keep `asteroid_incoming_recovery_legacy` after the country's last local burden ends, `chaosx.nr28.6` keeps it at closeout and its tooltip says so (`GetAsteroidIncomingCloseoutLegacyClause`), and the category header reads "Lasting recovery legacy" or "Recovery complete, lasting legacy kept". A failure window still suspends it like any other legacy, and annexation clears the completion flag so a released country does not regain it. A mission whose named state leaves the country's control ends without penalty. Only an expired mission applies the failure consequences, once per named state and day: a rescue-failure Deaths entry of `asteroid_incoming_recovery_fraction.rescue_death_fraction` of the state's population (`rescue_death_fraction_heavy` in the catastrophic ring or at a fragment centre, capped at `rescue_death_cap_people`) through `apply_exact_state_civilian_population_loss` with the `asteroid_rescue_failure` reason, the return of the state's ended impact-zone damage for `mission_failure_recovery_days` (damage still running keeps its timer, and emergency-class damage reopens the local emergency), the `asteroid_incoming_mission_failure_pressure` modifier, and the full national recovery burden. `asteroid_incoming_apply_mission_failure_to_country` replaces any recovery legacy with a timed `asteroid_incoming_recovery_burden` for `asteroid_incoming_recovery.mission_failure_pressure_days` and sets the timed country flag `asteroid_incoming_recovery_failure_pressure` for the same days; while that flag runs, the monthly `asteroid_incoming_refresh_national_recovery` leaves the national idea alone and queues no closeout, and a success in that window records the legacy without swapping the idea until the window ends. The Government Beyond the Crater achievement reads the network and outer-ring success flags unchanged.

## Local emergencies

Emergency projects need a live local emergency: an active rescue burden or the Catastrophic Impact Zone, Major Impact Zone, Fragment Centre Recovery or Heavy Fragment Damage modifier. At impact, a non-crater state with such a burden joins `global.asteroid_incoming_emergency_states`; a state with only outer-shock or light-fragment damage starts with its emergency resolved. The monthly recovery pass (`asteroid_incoming_process_emergency_closures`) sets `asteroid_incoming_local_emergency_resolved` on each listed state whose burdens have all ended and drops it from the list, so reconstruction projects open there even when no emergency project was taken.

Five persistent achievements track the near miss, capital recovery, mineral collection, crater armour conquest, and global reconnection routes. The global host runs `asteroid_incoming_update_achievements` once a month from the `on_monthly` host block in `common/on_actions/chaosx_on_actions_chaos_meter.txt` that is not gated by the Chaos Meter setting, so the achievements stay attainable with the Chaos Meter disabled. Hold tracking starts only after `asteroid_incoming_impact_recorded` is set without a recorded miss, and visits only human-controlled countries, so campaigns without an impact receive no per-country Event 028 variables. Collector of Fallen Stars counts only directly controlled sites, so sites held by a subject do not count, while a player who is a subject can still earn it. Continuous-control achievements measure elapsed days from persistent start-day receipts. Loss of control and annexation reset unfinished holds. The global reconnection award checks the protection program before zero-dust cleanup removes it. The achievements retain manual and evolution disqualifiers, final localisation, and completed/grey/not-eligible icon triplets.
Each achievement has the native `<id>_NAME` and `<id>_DESC` keys read by the achievement list, and both the description and the `<id>_tooltip` text read their numbers from `asteroid_incoming_runtime`: the near-miss Chaos threshold (`minerals_chaos_threshold`), the Government Beyond the Crater deadline (`global_achievement_deadline_days`), the Collector minimum site count and hold (`collector_minimum_controlled_fragments`, `collector_hold_days`) and the Piercing hold (`main_capture_hold_days`).

## Opening AI

The three strike options of `chaosx.nr28.1` and the reopened board `chaosx.nr28.8` carry identical `ai_chance` blocks with weights from `asteroid_incoming_ai`. A protected target (`asteroid_incoming_ai_target_is_protected`: the chooser itself, a subject or overlord, a faction partner not at war with it, a co-belligerent fighting on its side, a non-aggression partner, or a guarantee in either direction) is multiplied by `protected_target_factor` (0) as the last modifier of its option, so no later `add` can lift it above zero. Before that, `strike_friendly_ground_factor` (0.25) applies when `asteroid_incoming_ai_strike_would_hit_friendly_ground` finds the target state, a neighbouring state or a state two land steps away owned or controlled by the chooser, a faction partner or a co-belligerent, the ground the catastrophic and major rings would devastate.

## Crater AI

Under Extraordinary Minerals the AI treats crater states as strategic ground in three places. The six crater decisions carry their own `ai_will_do` weights. The AI strategy plan `asteroid_incoming_crater_fronts` in `common/ai_strategy/028_asteroid_incoming_ai_strategy.txt` runs only while `asteroid_incoming_minerals_active` is set, the country is at war and its surrender progress is at most `@ASTEROID_CRATER_SURRENDER_LIMIT` (0.25), with `abort_when_not_enabled = yes`. It adds `front_unit_request` weight to fronts that cross the main crater (`@ASTEROID_CRATER_MAIN_FRONT_REQUEST`, 60) or a fragment site (`@ASTEROID_CRATER_FRAGMENT_FRONT_REQUEST`, 25), following the vanilla `CHI_bog_them_down_and_war_zones_concentrations` state-flag pattern. The same request reinforces a held site and lets the normal attack logic take a nearby enemy site when it has the strength; the plan adds no `front_control` order, so it never forces an offensive, and a country close to collapse keeps its divisions on its home front.

The peace desires in `common/peace_conference/ai_peace/028_asteroid_incoming_ai_peace.txt` add `take_states` desire only when the negotiator bids for itself and the state carries `asteroid_incoming_main_crater` (`@ASTEROID_CRATER_MAIN_PEACE_DESIRE`, 75) or `asteroid_incoming_fragment_site` (`@ASTEROID_CRATER_FRAGMENT_PEACE_DESIRE`, 30). Each fragment state carries its own desire, so several sites together count for more than one. Both values stay below the vanilla `take_our_cores` desire of 100 and far below the -1000 influence cutoffs, so cores, claims, influence range, faction leadership and subject bids still decide the settlement.

## Dust helper contracts

The four dust-protection projects (Distribute Emergency Filters, Harden Factories Against Dust, Protect Transport Reserves, Join the Observation Network) are paid when taken and grant their protection when the `decision_civilian_factory_days` work period ends. Their outcome helpers in `common/scripted_effects/028_asteroid_incoming_recovery_effects.txt` add the protection idea, completion flag and mitigation only while `global.asteroid_incoming_dust_score` is above `asteroid_incoming_dust.minimum`; a project that finishes after the sky has cleared lapses, because the zero-dust pass has already removed every protection idea and no later pass would remove one. Their tooltips say so. Distribute Emergency Filters is also hidden, and drops out of the emergency target ranking, while no dust is active.

`asteroid_incoming_refresh_national_dust_protection` runs in country scope after a protection choice, after joining the observation network, or after a global stage update. It reads the current dust flags and the country's highest protection idea, overwrites six country compensation variables, adds or updates the hidden `asteroid_incoming_national_dust_protection` modifier, and removes it at zero dust or when the country has neither a protection tier nor observation-network membership. A country with `asteroid_incoming_observation_network` adds `asteroid_incoming_dust_protection.observation_sky_fraction` (15 percent) to its air-mission and weather compensation, with or without a protection tier, and the combined share is clamped to `maximum_fraction` so it never exceeds the stage's own penalty; the modifier still disappears at zero dust. It changes no population, building, or other event modifier.

`asteroid_incoming_refresh_air_cleanliness_source` runs after the opening calculation or a monthly stage update. It reads the dust stage and global mitigation, publishes source 4's current reservoir, and records the opening injection only while its persistent receipt is absent. Its zero-dust update retains source history and removes current pressure without subtracting the original injection.

`asteroid_incoming_apply_monthly_air_pressure` is called only by the existing global-host monthly dust pulse. It applies the published reservoir once for the current date, attributes the actual shared-ledger change to the asteroid source, and resets its public temporary ledger inputs and Fallout-skip input after returning. A repeated call on the same date has no effect.

## Presentation delivery and repair-price lifetime

One persistent news queue accepts each owned bulletin once and records it before broadcast.
Each recipient also has a receipt tested by both the sender trigger and native `show_major`; its immediate block consumes that receipt before the option is answered.
A forecast replaces an unprinted opening warning, and the latest dust-stage notice replaces older unprinted stage notices.
News waits while any super-event is visible.
Slot 116 waits for open asteroid news and pending or open human impact reports, so the forecast, national report, super-event, and world-impact bulletin are separated.
The existing monthly host resumes presentation if its callback country disappears.
Forced firing during a locked course or after the passage returns the matching explanation and preserves the original opening date and outcome.

The country impact report is consumed in `immediate`, so another queued callback cannot show it twice while its option is still unanswered.
A normal quick-confirmed impact gives an unaffected player two news bulletins and one super-event.
The target also receives one preparedness choice and one country report; the chooser receives the board and confirmation, and receives a country report only if its own territory is affected.
An opening bulletin already printed before confirmation adds one; user-requested board reopening is a separate choice.
Later dust-stage notices and a national recovery closeout each appear at most once and do not stack when several dust thresholds fall in one update.

Hidden `chaosx.nr28.12` re-quotes the persistent affected-state array after the repair surcharge expires, even when the monthly recovery transaction has stopped.
Control-change hooks also refresh the changed affected state against its current controller.
Native custom costs have base, blocked, and tooltip keys; blocked amounts are red, inline rows show at most three resource values, and the tooltip retains every paid resource and factory commitment.
The AI risk penalty checks friendly ownership as well as control in the target and its first two land steps, including occupied friendly territory.
The numeric AI weights are unchanged.

Network, outer-ring and regional-economy contracts are one-time national objectives.
Repair work in another state does not reopen a completed national contract or create another failure penalty.
The network objective reconnects the pre-impact rail network and its saved industrial hub; it does not require a new railway in states that never had one.
The outer-ring objective measures Ring 2 burden through its 60 percent infrastructure/factory restoration gates, alongside removal of the outer-shock/light-fragment effects.
Objectives cover surviving affected holdings under current control, and the saved industrial hub and reserved mission state retain their separate control requirements.
These prior takeover design scopes are retained explicitly, rather than presented as the broader wording in the first audit.
