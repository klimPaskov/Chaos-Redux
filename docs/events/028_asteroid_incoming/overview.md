## Gameplay flow

Before automatic selection, a read-only preflight requires three distinct countries other than the chooser, each with two usable owned and controlled states. The opening transaction selects exactly three distinct valid country/state pairs from that eligible population. It persists each country and state as a global event target, refuses wasteland and previously registered asteroid sites, and exposes a genuine miss option. Countries with only one usable state, occupied backups, or the CXT system identity cannot consume a target slot. When enabled, an invalid world shows `N/A` with the target-pool reason in the shared event view and remains outside automatic selection. A forced invalid opening retains its explicit `N/A` option.

The shared dispatcher builds the target board before dispatch and Major history accounting, and rejects a failed board without consuming the event. The chooser selects one of the three saved pairs and receives a confirmation event. Cancel returns through the internal `chaosx.nr28.8` board, preserving the existing pairs, chooser, opening date, and evolution snapshot without re-firing the native fire-once entry. The board's validity is stored once in the global flag `asteroid_incoming_board_ready`: the dispatcher sets it on a successful board, `chaosx.nr28.1` re-validates once in `immediate` (rebuilding the still-unseen board when nothing is locked and the pairs are missing or invalid), and `chaosx.nr28.8` re-validates without rerolling. The description (`GetAsteroidIncomingOpeningDesc`) and every board option read that flag, so the text and the choices agree when a pending popup is restored from a save, and the target-pool reset, lock, miss, fail-closed close, and cleanup paths clear it. Confirmation copies the selected pair into locked targets and stores the original state id and capital status. The lock lasts two days and the resolver uses the saved scope pointers, so owner, controller, tag, and subject changes cannot silently reroll the coordinates. The locked target country chooses one preparedness stance before the impact.

The impact resolver plans the main footprint and optional fragments before changing any state. It merges overlapping profiles by severity, processes each state once, measures population before and after the loss, registers only actual deaths through the shared Deaths ledger, removes civilian buildings from the main crater, and sends one consolidated report per affected country. It relocates only a country whose current capital is the locked center. Backup selection prefers controlled cores and safer territory, then uses surviving ring states when no safer candidate exists. A current planned fragment center remains a surviving candidate, while an existing or processed crater does not. Relocation accounting names the country that actually moved, even when owner and controller differ, and an annexer receives no original-target achievement identity. When no settled, fully controlled backup exists, relocation falls back to any controlled owned state, then any owned state even if occupied, then any controlled state. If none qualifies, the impact still proceeds, the capital stays at the crater, the country receives the full Government Dislocation for the capital duration, Government Beyond the Crater stays closed, and the impact report and Event Details record that the government could not move.

The main state receives a permanent nonradioactive asteroid crater marker and no civilian population or buildings. After its measured receipt, the main centre also zeroes every province naval base and fort and wrecks its remaining supply hubs and railways beyond repair, and its wasteland category and crater modifier stop new and queued construction. Three shortest land-adjacency rings receive the accepted 50 percent, 25 percent, and 5 percent civilian-loss profiles with severity-scaled infrastructure, railway, supply, factory, and military-site damage. Fragment centers and their two rings use the separate fragment profiles and do not create nuclear or radioactive records.

Actual deaths, destroyed capacity, affected states, fragments, and bounded supported secondary disasters feed Atmospheric Dust Load. The shared world dust state moves through Residual Haze, Global Dust Veil, Impact Winter, and Severe Impact Winter, applies production, supply, air, and weather penalties, writes a separate Air Cleanliness source slot, and decays through the Event 028 maintenance hooks. Basic, organized, and hardened national protection reduce the active stage's penalties by 15 percent, 30 percent, and 45 percent. A hidden country dynamic modifier supplies the compensation beneath the existing visible protection idea, so protection cannot turn fading dust into a production bonus.

The opening Air Cleanliness injection is consumed once. Monthly reservoir pressure follows the current dust stage and global mitigation, and a date receipt prevents repeated coordinator calls on the same day from charging it twice. Falling stages and zero dust reduce the source's current pressure without refunding its historical injection. The shared Air Cleanliness recovery remains responsible for removing atmospheric contamination.

Event 013 secondary calls use one exact supported family, the affected state, and an explicit main-impact or fragment origin. The gateway validates geography and family availability. The four-call limit counts accepted scheduled jobs, and rejected families add no dust. Its own sequence and reports retain Event 028 as the caller.

At 600 Chaos, Global Fragmentation adds three to six independently planned fragment sites, two damage rings, overlap resolution, grouped reporting, and additional dust. Fragment centers prefer a valid land neighbor whenever the remaining global pool provides one, with a bounded fallback for genuinely isolated candidates. At 800 Chaos, Extraordinary Minerals is eligible only when fragmentation actually applied, and the opening snapshot records its eligibility only when Global Fragmentation was recorded in the same snapshot, so the board never announces minerals while fragmentation is disabled. Both Chaos thresholds are read once, in the opening snapshot; the shared Events Log toggles (`events_log_disabled_evolution_28_2801_1` and `28_2802_1`) are read again at impact, so disabling Global Fragmentation after the opening gives a clean main-only impact and disabling Extraordinary Minerals keeps the fragments without mineral bonuses. Evolution history rows are written only for evolutions that applied. The main crater controller receives `+100%` land-division armour and each currently controlled fragment site contributes `+20%`; the control refresh removes stale ownership benefits and rebuilds the uncapped current-control total.

Ordinary recovery uses the shared decision framework under `asteroid_incoming_recovery_category`. Each country stores its pending paid project's state in `asteroid_incoming_recovery_target_state`. A pending project blocks another project in that country, while other countries retain independent targets. Finish callbacks recheck the reserved state and current control, apply the project, and always release the project. Cancellation, lost control, and annexation release only that country's receipts. The category exposes reason-specific blocked tooltips, meaningful manpower/equipment/fuel/logistical costs, reserve floors for AI and players, national closeout, cleanup, and one-time action flags. There is no dedicated Event 028 scripted GUI.

## Recovery prices

Every affected non-crater state carries one price quote that the cost text, the cost check, the reserve check and the payment all read (`asteroid_incoming_quote_state_recovery_costs`). The multiplier is `asteroid_incoming_cost.base_multiplier` plus `profile_weight` per impact-profile point plus `population_loss_weight_per_k` per thousand people the state lost (capped at `population_loss_cap`), multiplied by `1 + failure_surcharge` while the controller carries `asteroid_incoming_repair_costs_raised`, and kept between `minimum_multiplier` and `maximum_multiplier`. Quotes are written at impact, refreshed by the monthly recovery pass over `global.asteroid_incoming_affected_states`, and refreshed at once for a country that takes the surcharge after an expired mission. The prepared first responses keep their lower base amounts under the same multiplier. Dust-protection projects read a global quote scaled by the current dust stage (`dust_residual` to `dust_severe`), recomputed whenever the stage is set. Each project still uses at most four spendable cost types and must leave the `asteroid_incoming_cost` reserves behind. Crater operations keep flat prices.

At impact, the original target loses `transport_loss_per_state` of its trains, trucks and fuel for each main-footprint state it controls, up to `transport_loss_cap`. The Disperse Transport preparation keeps `transport_dispersion_preserved_share` of that loss, and the impact report lists the destroyed and preserved amounts. The preparation no longer grants equipment.

## Recovery missions

A completed state project can start one of five missions for a player government: Keep the Rescue Route Open (mobile hospitals or emergency rail corridor), Stabilize the Damaged Zone (debris clearing), Reconnect the National Network (supply spine), Restore the Outer Ring (outer-ring industry), and Rebuild the Regional Economy (rehousing, original target country only). AI countries keep every project effect but receive no mission contract, because they cannot plan construction toward a mission target.

Each mission keeps its own state pointers (`asteroid_incoming_rescue_mission_state`, `asteroid_incoming_stabilize_mission_state_one`/`_two`, `asteroid_incoming_network_mission_state` and `asteroid_incoming_network_hub_state`, `asteroid_incoming_outer_mission_state`, `asteroid_incoming_economy_mission_state`), so later projects run while a mission is active and never rewrite its objective. No more than `asteroid_incoming_recovery.active_mission_limit` (two) missions run at once. The objective can be met from the end of its hold period, which closes `asteroid_incoming_recovery.mission_grace_days` (45) before the native timeout, and the mission completes as soon as its conditions hold after that day.

Repair targets come from the pre-impact snapshot: `asteroid_incoming_set_mission_repair_targets` stores `asteroid_incoming_recovery.mission_repair_share` (60 percent) of each state's infrastructure, railway, supply hubs, civilian factories and military factories, rounded to whole levels, so a building a state never had is never required. Before any damage or capital move, `asteroid_incoming_record_preimpact_rail_link` flags each footprint state that had a rail connection to its controller's capital, and rail objectives ask only for links that existed before the impact.

- Rescue route: the named state meets its infrastructure, railway and supply-hub targets and, if it was linked before the impact, has a rail connection to the current capital.
- Stabilize: the two worst-hit damaged states the country holds that still miss their targets are captured at activation (strongest impact profile first, then the larger infrastructure and railway gap) and must both meet their targets. Success closes their local emergencies so reconstruction can begin there.
- Network: every damaged state the country holds that was linked before the impact is linked to the current capital again, every damaged state meets its infrastructure target, and the largest industrial state that is or was on the network (captured at activation) is linked to the capital. Success closes emergency work in every damaged state the country holds and sets `asteroid_incoming_network_reconnected`.
- Outer ring: no held affected state keeps the Outer Shock Zone or Light Fragment Damage, and every held state of the major impact ring meets its infrastructure and both factory targets. Success sets `asteroid_incoming_outer_ring_restored`.
- Regional economy: the monthly recovery review compares the current factory, infrastructure and railway totals across the surviving damaged states the country holds with the same pre-impact totals, and each must reach `asteroid_incoming_recovery.regional_capacity_share` (75 percent).

Every success grants the recovery legacy. A mission whose named state leaves the country's control ends without penalty. Only an expired mission applies the failure consequences, once per named state and day: a rescue-failure Deaths entry of `asteroid_incoming_recovery_fraction.rescue_death_fraction` of the state's population (`rescue_death_fraction_heavy` in the catastrophic ring or at a fragment centre, capped at `rescue_death_cap_people`) through `apply_exact_state_civilian_population_loss` with the `asteroid_rescue_failure` reason, the return of the state's ended impact-zone damage for `mission_failure_recovery_days` (damage still running keeps its timer, and emergency-class damage reopens the local emergency), the `asteroid_incoming_mission_failure_pressure` modifier, and the full national recovery burden. `asteroid_incoming_apply_mission_failure_to_country` replaces any recovery legacy with a timed `asteroid_incoming_recovery_burden` for `asteroid_incoming_recovery.mission_failure_pressure_days` and sets the timed country flag `asteroid_incoming_recovery_failure_pressure` for the same days; while that flag runs, the monthly `asteroid_incoming_refresh_national_recovery` leaves the national idea alone and queues no closeout, and a success in that window records the legacy without swapping the idea until the window ends. The Government Beyond the Crater achievement reads the network and outer-ring success flags unchanged.

## Local emergencies

Emergency projects need a live local emergency: an active rescue burden or the Catastrophic Impact Zone, Major Impact Zone, Fragment Centre Recovery or Heavy Fragment Damage modifier. At impact, a non-crater state with such a burden joins `global.asteroid_incoming_emergency_states`; a state with only outer-shock or light-fragment damage starts with its emergency resolved. The monthly recovery pass (`asteroid_incoming_process_emergency_closures`) sets `asteroid_incoming_local_emergency_resolved` on each listed state whose burdens have all ended and drops it from the list, so reconstruction projects open there even when no emergency project was taken.

Five persistent achievements track the near miss, capital recovery, mineral collection, crater armour conquest, and global reconnection routes. The global host runs `asteroid_incoming_update_achievements` once a month from the `on_monthly` host block in `common/on_actions/chaosx_on_actions_chaos_meter.txt` that is not gated by the Chaos Meter setting, so the achievements stay attainable with the Chaos Meter disabled. Hold tracking starts only after `asteroid_incoming_impact_recorded` is set without a recorded miss, and visits only human-controlled countries, so campaigns without an impact receive no per-country Event 028 variables. Collector of Fallen Stars counts only directly controlled sites, so sites held by a subject do not count, while a player who is a subject can still earn it. Continuous-control achievements measure elapsed days from persistent start-day receipts. Loss of control and annexation reset unfinished holds. The global reconnection award checks the protection program before zero-dust cleanup removes it. The achievements retain manual and evolution disqualifiers, final localisation, and completed/grey/not-eligible icon triplets.

## Dust helper contracts

`asteroid_incoming_refresh_national_dust_protection` runs in country scope after a protection choice or global stage update. It reads the current dust flags and the country's highest protection idea, overwrites six country compensation variables, adds or updates the hidden `asteroid_incoming_national_dust_protection` modifier, and removes it at zero dust or without protection. It changes no population, building, or other event modifier.

`asteroid_incoming_refresh_air_cleanliness_source` runs after the opening calculation or a monthly stage update. It reads the dust stage and global mitigation, publishes source 4's current reservoir, and records the opening injection only while its persistent receipt is absent. Its zero-dust update retains source history and removes current pressure without subtracting the original injection.

`asteroid_incoming_apply_monthly_air_pressure` is called only by the existing global-host monthly dust pulse. It applies the published reservoir once for the current date, attributes the actual shared-ledger change to the asteroid source, and resets its public temporary ledger inputs and Fallout-skip input after returning. A repeated call on the same date has no effect.
