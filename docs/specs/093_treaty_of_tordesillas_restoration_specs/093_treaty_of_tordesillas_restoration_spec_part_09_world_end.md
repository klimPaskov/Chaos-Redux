# Treaty of Tordesillas Restoration: Two Crowns, One World


## A world-end earned by two empires

**Two Crowns, One World** begins only when the restored treaty has become a credible global order.
Both Spain and Portugal must have substantial correct-sphere reach and substantial directly integrated territory.
A powerful Spain with a tiny Portuguese ally cannot trigger it.
A powerful Portugal cannot use Spain's signature as a substitute for a Spanish empire.

The scenario is an optional world-end under the shared setting and world-end ownership rules.
It requires current Chaos strictly greater than 1000, an adopted Evolution IV, both sovereign treaty powers, an active valid faction compact, Confidence of at least 65, no unresolved material breach, and the two territorial readiness conditions below.

Both powers must control their national capitals and retain functioning independent governments.
A capitulated crown, a foreign puppet, or an unresolved national civil war blocks entry.
The territorial and political conditions must remain satisfied for 90 consecutive days before the final joint proclamation can be offered.

## Measuring real reach

Freeze the relative importance of eligible sphere states at Event 093's activation, using the current campaign population, represented land area, and industry.
Exclude the protected metropolitan homelands and the exceptional Galician settlement from both spheres' readiness denominators.
A crown must expand beyond the protected opening territory to qualify.

Within each sphere, state importance uses 50 percent population share, 25 percent land-area share, and 25 percent initial industry share.
These are normalized within that sphere.
If a component has zero total value on a supported map, omit it and renormalize the remaining weights.
The land component ensures sparsely populated territories remain relevant.

These frozen denominators prevent later civilian deaths, razed factories, or population transfers from shrinking the task.
New factories do not manufacture territorial readiness.
Changes to the map itself require the explicit compatibility review, not a quiet denominator refresh.

Let C be the weighted share under the correct crown's order and current control.
Legal ownership must belong to that crown or an eligible subordinate with that crown as its recognized patron.
Independent faction partners, temporary military access, and enemy-owned occupation do not count.
A state is counted once.

Let I be the weighted share directly owned, controlled, and cored by the correct crown.
Subject territory does not count as directly integrated.
Genuine pre-existing cores and cores from compatible other systems can count, but only with the same correct ownership and control conditions.
They do not grant Event 093 achievement credit for a project that never occurred.

Each crown needs **C at least 70 percent** and **I at least 40 percent**.
These conditions are evaluated independently for Spain and Portugal.
The readiness indicator is `min(100, 100*C/70, 100*I/40)`.
It is explicitly labelled Consolidation, not percent of the world conquered.
Its explanation shows which component is limiting readiness and how the result is calculated.

## Why the two readiness indicators are useful

The indicators tell each player whether to expand or integrate.
A country with broad subject influence but little directly integrated land needs administration.
A compact country with many cores but little wider control needs more reach.
Raising only one component cannot hide the other.

The calculation does not equalize population, factories, or military difficulty between the hemispheres.
It measures how far each crown has developed its own assigned order.
The two countries may still face very different strategic tasks, which is a reason for mutual support.

Consolidation is a derived outcome measure, not another spendable resource.
The interface does not add separate public currencies for prestige, imperial mandate, cartographic authority, or subject loyalty.
The map and project list provide the detail necessary to act.

## The final joint proclamation

After the readiness period, both governments receive a final consent step.
They see that the remaining independent world will be treated as unfinished territory of the treaty order, that an external response will intensify, and that ordinary random-event behavior follows the shared world-end framework.

One government cannot make this irreversible strategic commitment on behalf of the other human player.
A refusal leaves Evolution IV active and cancels the pending proclamation.
A later attempt needs a meaningful renewed decision and the conference cooldown.

If the world-end slot is already held by another scenario, Event 093 cannot seize it.
It can continue its ordinary imperial campaign within permitted systems.
The [shared identifier registry](../shared_identifier_registry.md) does not yet reserve a world-end ID or super-event slot for this scenario; the reservation is an open question in the [README](README.md#open-questions).

The super-event is presented once when the scenario actually begins.
Opening the offer, reaching Consolidation 100, or temporarily crossing 1000 Chaos does not play the super-event or claim completion.

## The unfinished world

The initial scenario assessment identifies remaining independent states, unsettled cross-sphere ownership, vulnerable treaty supply routes, and regions that are controlled but poorly integrated.
Each crown chooses a main theater and a secondary support commitment rather than receiving a global list of simultaneous wars.

Independent governments can accept subordination, negotiate limited terms, organize resistance, or fight.
An ultimatum has a real response period and an explicit threatened consequence.
Rejection can permit an eligible war goal or escalate an existing confrontation.
It does not annex the target through a fear roll.

The treaty's advantages come from its established resources, coordinated claims, integration institutions, and the diplomatic pressure of overwhelming success.
There are no infinitely replenishing divisions, free strategic weapons, automatic enemy capitulations, or blanket combat invulnerability.

A crown that has finished most of its half is encouraged to supply the other.
It cannot simply absorb the other crown and declare the two-sided order complete.
The political identity of the scenario remains a partition between two powers.

## External resistance during the final campaign

The remaining world can coordinate through existing factions, compatible new agreements, intelligence cooperation, and shared defensive priorities.
It does not have to join one forcibly created faction or abandon unrelated national politics.
A country may oppose Iberian domination while remaining hostile to another member of the resistance.

Counterplay includes interrupting sea routes, liberating integrated regions, defeating a treaty army in an active theater, protecting a threatened state, and exposing or exploiting a real dispute between the crowns.
A crown that uses chemical, biological or nuclear weapons or runs camps hands the resistance a further lever: the condemnation system's embargo tiers cut its arms and strategic imports, and AI targets refuse its subordination offers from Tier 4 (Strategic embargo) upward.
These are material military and diplomatic goals.
There is no universal button that subtracts treaty readiness without changing anything on the map.

Restoring independent ownership and control reduces C and can reduce I.
Merely bombing a factory does not shrink the frozen denominator or erase a core.
Resistance in a region affects its administration and may stop new integration, while actual liberation changes political control.

## Completion of the partition

A final treaty victory is stricter than the entry gate.
Every eligible state must belong to the correct crown's political order, including a valid subordinate arrangement where that route was accepted.
Independent associates do not count.
Wrong-sphere holdings and unsettled exceptions must be resolved.
Both crowns must directly integrate at least 60 percent of their weighted spheres.

The protected homelands and Galicia must be under their recognized treaty settlements, rather than occupied by an enemy or left in a contradictory sovereignty arrangement.
A cross-sphere subject needs the final settlement described in Part 08.
A temporary lease or vague promise is insufficient.

These completion conditions must hold for 90 days, with neither crown capitulated and no surviving independent opponent controlling eligible territory.
The final state is a completed Iberian world order.
The game need not forcibly close or replace the player's country.
It can record the scenario victory and allow continued play under the established framework.

The victory condition does not require pointless annexation of every permitted subordinate tag.
The world may contain local governments, but none remain outside the agreed two-power political order.

## Reversal and collapse

Falling below an entry threshold after activation does not instantly end the scenario.
Wars fluctuate.
The treaty receives a recovery period while the underlying diplomatic compact remains alive.

A sustained breakdown occurs if either crown's reach falls below 40 percent or its directly integrated controlled share below 20 percent for 180 days.
Loss of independent treaty-power status, unresolved capitulation, or annexation lasting 90 days also forces a failure review.
Deliberate dissolution by the two powers can resolve the scenario without waiting for those timers.

Recovery must restore real authority and territory.
The event does not respawn an annexed Portugal, invent a Spanish successor, or grant free cores to preserve a dramatic finale.
A legitimate restored state can be considered through the existing succession rules before the deadline.

On confirmed failure, remove only Event 093's world-threat registration and treaty-specific escalation duties.
Request the appropriate resolution from the shared world-end framework.
Do not assume that clearing this event's state automatically restarts every random event if the framework treats world-end mode as terminal.
That global behavior requires installed-source verification.

The conquered map is not reset.
Existing countries, cores, subjects, and wars remain according to their actual outcomes.
The collapse of an imperial plan should leave a meaningful world behind it.
