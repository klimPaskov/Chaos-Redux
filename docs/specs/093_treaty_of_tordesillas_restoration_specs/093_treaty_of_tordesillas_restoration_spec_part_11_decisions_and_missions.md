# Treaty of Tordesillas Restoration: decisions, missions, and presentation


## Interaction budget

Each active phase presents three to five primary actions, with six as a hard ceiling. Context selection changes the target of an action rather than spawning a decision row for every state or country. A country should normally have one to three active missions. Integration projects occupy the explicitly defined project slots, not dozens of independent mission timers.

The four custom public values remain the budget in Part 01. Detail panes can explain a score's components or a project's native requirements, but must not create new accumulating resources. History is presented as concise facts: an honored contract, an unresolved breach, a recognized Galician owner, or a current suspension.

Action labels below are internal role descriptions. They are not final decision titles, button strings, or localization keys. Costs refer to Part 14, which is the single tuning authority.

## Opening phase

| Role | Who acts and when | Commitment and result | Failure or exit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Present Galicia demand | Portugal, valid Spanish counterpart | Starts a formal answer process and territorial claim | Withdraw or accept refusal without forced war |
| Propose compensated settlement | Portugal or legal Galician owner | Opens a priced, reciprocal settlement | No payment before a valid agreement |
| Offer civil-war contract | Portugal, eligible live national belligerent | Selects one recipient and a material package | Refusal leaves no active contract |
| Recognize existing border | Both relevant governments | Resolves the dispute without requiring transfer | Either may keep the dispute dormant |
| Review escalation | Portugal, after a live refusal | Shows final pressure or war-goal route and intervention risks | Portugal may decline to escalate |

Spain's reply is a targeted diplomatic choice, not a second global category containing every Portuguese action. A 30-day diplomatic answer window is not a timed performance mission. The short window therefore does not violate the longer duration target for missions requiring substantial player action.

## Civil-war contract phase

The primary actions are view contract, deliver an installment, propose a permitted amendment, withdraw from the remaining commitment, and review the postwar settlement when relevant. The contract view identifies the actual recipient, remaining components, earning threshold, deadline, and legal status of Galicia.

The active mission is a 180-day delivery commitment. It completes automatically when the signed standard is fulfilled. There is no second claim-reward button. A supported victory opens the settlement process independently of whether the delivery timer has ended, using the early-victory rule in Part 03.

The delivery action displays one complete transaction. Selected native optional support is not debited again. Failure to deliver is not interpreted as successful aid because a decision finished its cooldown. Cancelling a mission cannot accidentally execute the success reward.

## Treaty and reconciliation phase

The primary actions are resolve the selected grievance, convene a restoration conference, answer the partner's proposal, review current obligations, and suspend or leave the compact where permitted. Only the action relevant to the current diplomatic state is available.

Reconciliation after serious breach can carry a 90-day non-aggression or restitution mission. Its success is automatic after the actual conditions are satisfied. Supporting the partner's enemy, reopening the settled Galician claim, or refusing an agreed restitution makes the reason for failure explicit.

Restoration, global partition, and universal doctrine are separate political decisions. They cannot be combined into a generic 'improve treaty' button that hides which stage is being adopted.

## Partition and expansion phase

The primary actions are inspect the selected sphere or region, propose a coordinated campaign, negotiate an eligible restitution, approach a selected outside country, and manage the treaty compact. A target selector restricts the list to relevant live countries or states.

One theater commitment can be active as an optional mission. It must use a coherent objective such as securing a defined region or an agreed access route, with 180 days or more for a significant military task. It is not an instruction to conquer a distant major in 30 days.

A theater mission does not award the target states on timeout. Success follows actual ownership or access. Failure informs AI reprioritization and diplomatic expectations without destroying the treaty over every unsuccessful offensive.

## Integration phase

The primary actions are select region, start a project, inspect or manage active projects, settle a regional dispute, and negotiate restitution. The region selector explains state eligibility, route, cost tier, and the next blocked requirement.

Projects have automatic progression and completion. The interface shows the enrolled group, time remaining, pause reason, and potential cancellation result. It does not require monthly confirmation clicks. A full project slot list blocks a new project before charging its cost.

An accelerated approach is chosen inside project confirmation rather than becoming a separate permanent decision for every region. Cancellation requires a deliberate choice with the actual refund shown. A resumed project preserves its original enrollment and payment history.

## World-end phase

The primary actions are choose the main theater, coordinate support for the other crown, approach a selected remaining country, manage restitution or integration, and inspect the final settlement conditions. A joint proclamation is offered only when all entry gates have been met and the shared scenario slot is available.

The selected target's response mission uses the 120-day standard. A broad conquest mission has at least 180 days and can use a longer duration when geography and current wars demand it. There are no simultaneous ultimatums to every independent country.

External players receive a similarly bounded category for responding to an offer, seeking defensive cooperation, and supporting a selected threatened country. A country unaffected by an active offer does not need a permanently empty event panel.

## Costs and confirmation

Each action shows the same payable amount that will be checked and paid. Re-evaluate live affordability at confirmation. A stale quote is not permission to debit a now unaffordable stockpile or to pay the old undiscounted amount while showing a new discount.

No action uses more than four spendable resource types. The common actions use one to three. Display at most three inline resource icons and place the full transaction in a readable tooltip. A political consequence such as reduced war support is not misrepresented as a discounted purchase cost.

Actions that transfer goods between countries must conserve the actual amount. Projects that consume goods must record the amount consumed. These different operations cannot share an ambiguous 'cost paid' success condition.

## Presentation-choice pass

The opening category can use a static archival-style picture of the Galicia dispute. The mature integration and expansion categories should prioritize live status over decorative pictures. A picture is not required when it reduces space for meaningful region information.

The sphere preview is a geography eligibility display. It needs exact current state shapes and dynamic ownership qualification. It is not a conventional formable that unites Spain and Portugal into a new tag, so a separate national-unification puzzle would misrepresent the design.

A native map mode with a compact selected-state or selected-region summary is the preferred solution. A bespoke full-screen GUI is not part of the accepted design. If implementation requires a new bounded panel, it must be justified, use the scripted-GUI workflow, and undergo real before-and-after rendering and click testing.

No animated category picture or portrait is required. The key information is geographic ownership and legal eligibility, which should be clear without motion. A static representation is the intended design, not a temporary fallback for unfinished animation.

## Accessibility and visibility

Do not rely on red and green alone to distinguish the two spheres. Use names, distinct patterns or borders, and readable legends. Protected states and the Galicia exception need their own symbols. A tooltip must identify the actor and owner rather than depend entirely on flag recognition.

At common supported resolutions, the cost, target, and confirmation result must remain visible without overlapping controls. Long country names, changed cosmetic names, and two human players must not break the layout. Localization needs specific overflow tests for the largest regional and diplomatic panels.
