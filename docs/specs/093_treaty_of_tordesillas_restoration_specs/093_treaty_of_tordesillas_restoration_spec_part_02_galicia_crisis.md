# Treaty of Tordesillas Restoration: the Galicia crisis


## A demand that can be answered

The opening gives Portugal a short assessment of Spain's political condition, Galicia's legal owner and controller, and the diplomatic risks of pressing the demand. Portugal can open formal negotiations, offer a compensated settlement, or leave the matter dormant. The choice to negotiate is not already a declaration of war.

Formal pressure creates a Portuguese claim, not a Portuguese core, on the resolved Galician territory. It identifies the demand and gives Spain an answer window. A refusal does not cause Portugal to declare war from inside Spain's option. Portugal receives its own subsequent decision about whether the dispute is worth escalation.

A dormant claim remains a remembered political position. It provides no passive Chaos, permanent economic penalty, or recurring demand popup. Reopening it requires a meaningful change such as Spanish civil war, a new national government, Portugal becoming independent, or an agreed bilateral conference. It is not a cheap action that can be repeated every month for rewards.

## Unified Spain

A unified Spain has three broad responses: accept a negotiated transfer, refuse, or enter counter-negotiations. A stable government with a strong army and secure allies should normally resist an uncompensated loss. A country recovering from war may value peace on its western border more highly. Those are preferences, not forced historical outcomes.

Acceptance begins settlement confirmation. The transfer must still be legal at the moment it occurs. Spain must own the land, and the agreed arrangements must not conceal an ongoing enemy occupation. When Spain owns but does not control Galicia, it can promise transfer after recovery. Portugal can accept that promise or demand different terms, but ownership is not silently rewritten while another war prevents execution.

A refusal returns the initiative to Portugal. Portugal may accept the rejection, pursue one stronger round of pressure, or seek a war goal. Spain's refusal has no compulsory effect on Portuguese military orders. Portugal cannot be forced into a hopeless war merely because the Spanish AI chose a defiant sentence.

Counter-negotiation supplies a small menu of materially different settlements. It is not an open-ended bidding minigame. The preferred terms are a compensated cession, a civil-war assistance bargain when applicable, or recognition of the existing border with a wider non-aggression understanding.

## Compensated cession

Spain can request a fixed, affordable equipment package and a reciprocal political commitment. The equipment goes to Spain. It is not deleted as an abstract purchase price while a second copy appears in Spanish stockpiles. Portugal sees the complete delivery amount, its remaining stockpile reserve, and the territorial result before confirming.

The political commitment is a defined period of non-aggression over the settled Iberian border and recognition of Spain's remaining protected homeland. It does not automatically join Spain's faction, guarantee every Spanish colonial war, or grant unlimited military access. Those are separate choices.

At completion Spain transfers Galicia and Portugal recognizes the settlement. The previous Spanish core is not deleted by Event 093. Portugal does not obtain a core immediately. The two countries gain an honored-settlement history and improved Confidence. A later attempt to revoke that settlement is a breach, even if another event has granted new claims.

A Portuguese player can refuse the price. A Spanish player can refuse the sale. Neither side loses a deposit before all live conditions are checked. If the agreement becomes impossible before transfer, unspent commitments are released and any actual refundable payment is returned once. Equipment already delivered as fulfilled compensation is handled by the agreed cancellation term, not duplicated through a generic refund.

## Recognition without transfer

When Galicia is already Portuguese, the issue is recognition. Spain may recognize the border, reserve its position without starting a war, or demand restoration. Recognition can found a later treaty. A reservation records an unresolved grievance and blocks full treaty ratification until addressed.

When Portugal decides to leave Galicia Spanish, it can sign a definitive renunciation for this event. This supplies a viable cooperative route for players who prefer an alliance to a territorial purchase. It does not remove unrelated claims from other sources. The treaty records that the Galicia dispute was settled by Portuguese restraint.

The settlement is therefore not always a Portuguese territorial victory. Spain can preserve Galicia and still become a willing partner. Portugal should receive the diplomatic value of a durable agreement, rather than a large unrelated industrial reward that makes renunciation an exploit.

## A third-party owner

If an independent Galicia or another country legally owns the disputed land, Portugal may request a three-party conference. Spain can recognize or oppose a settlement, but cannot transfer the owner's territory. The owner can accept a compensated accession, reject it, or request guarantees that leave it independent.

A refusal by the owner may create an ordinary Portuguese territorial dispute. It does not create a fictitious Spanish betrayal unless Spain had promised something it knowingly could not deliver. A third-party guarantee or faction membership remains relevant to war risk.

An independent Galician settlement can leave the restored treaty unavailable until the two national powers agree how to handle that exceptional territory. The user brief does not require every possible map to produce a treaty. It does require the result to be honest about sovereignty.

## Portugal escalates

After rejection Portugal can spend a further diplomatic effort to present a final demand. This makes the dispute more serious and may worsen Confidence. Its usefulness depends on actual pressure: relative military strength, Spanish internal disorder, relevant allies, Portuguese readiness, and whether Galicia can be reached.

A final rejection can give Portugal an event-owned territorial war goal after the normal warning period. A player must choose to use it. The war goal is narrow in purpose, but the specification does not pretend the engine guarantees a narrow war. Guarantees, factions, existing wars, and intervention can expand it.

During war, an event-mediated settlement is available only where the involved countries can legally conclude the relevant peace without abandoning other belligerents or changing another war. Both governments must accept. Where the current engine situation does not permit a separate settlement, ordinary war and peace mechanics remain authoritative. The UI must warn about that limitation before Portugal escalates.

A negotiated military settlement transfers only the agreed Galician territory, ends only the agreed eligible conflict, and records a coercive origin. A normal peace conference can produce a larger Iberian outcome, but Event 093 does not secretly award Portugal all Spain or create a treaty between an overlord and its puppet.

## Domestic reaction and long-term effects

A peaceful sale can cost Spain some war support and provoke opposition. Portugal can gain modest political prestige. Those effects should remain small enough that the real prize is Galicia and the later diplomatic relationship. The event should not buy a province with penalties larger than losing an actual war.

A coerced settlement produces lower Confidence and a concrete reconciliation requirement. A military victory is not an automatic permanent rivalry, but both countries must later acknowledge the border and accept a non-revision commitment. The defeated government can refuse. A change of government does not erase the facts of the war.

The crisis ends with a clear territorial settlement, an explicitly dormant dispute, an active war, or a deferred promise awaiting a known condition. Each outcome states who owns Galicia, what is still promised, and why treaty restoration is or is not available. There is no indefinite mission with an impossible hidden completion condition.

## Local identity

Galicia is a territorial and political subject, not a bundle of free manpower. Transfer does not move civilians, change their ethnicity, erase Spanish culture, or instantly integrate the region. Existing population, damage, resistance, and supply conditions survive through the shared systems.

At Evolution III Galicia uses the special integration rule in Part 07. That later process gives the opening prize a place in the larger event without requiring the baseline to grant an anachronistic, immediate core.
