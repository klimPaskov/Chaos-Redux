# Treaty of Tordesillas Restoration: intervention in the Spanish Civil War


## Choosing a live partner

A Spanish civil war changes the value of the original demand.
Portugal can offer a defined assistance package to one eligible national Spanish belligerent in return for Galicia after that side wins.
Nationalists are the natural first contact when political compatibility and the live war support it.
They are not a hard-coded winner or the only possible negotiating partner.

The eligible recipient must actually be a national claimant in the current Spanish conflict, be able to negotiate foreign support, and have a plausible route to a national settlement.
Regional separatists, a foreign occupier, and a stale civil-war tag are not interchangeable with that role.
A government whose policy rejects territorial bargains can refuse even when it needs equipment.

Portugal sees the competing sides, their current political compatibility, whether they own or control Galicia, and the main risk of choosing each.
Intelligence uncertainty can affect the strategic assessment.
It must not conceal which country will receive the equipment or which country is promising the territory.

Only one Galicia-for-assistance contract can be active at a time.
Portugal cannot sell the same intervention to both sides and collect a bonus from whichever wins.
Ordinary native volunteer or lend-lease actions outside the contract remain possible, but supporting the enemy of the contracted recipient is a material breach if the contract prohibits it.

## The offer

The contract identifies the recipient, the current civil-war episode, the promised territory, the agreed aid standard, the delivery period, and the settlement after victory.
Galicia is a conditional price, not a gift on signing.
The recipient can promise territory it expects to recover, but the interface explicitly marks that it does not yet control the prize.

The minimum contract has material components that work without intelligence or volunteer DLC features.
Its proposed standard package is infantry equipment, support equipment, and one selected logistical component.
Its size is fixed from the recipient's wartime scale at signing.
The concrete amounts and substitution rules are in Part 14.

Portugal chooses the logistical component when the agreement is signed.
Later substitution needs recipient approval or a pre-agreed equivalent.
The player cannot turn the easiest currently abundant stockpile into unlimited contribution credit.
A package records separate fulfillment of each component, so providing excess rifles does not stand in for all other aid.

The recipient can demand a larger package when it is strong, ask for urgent delivery when it is threatened, or accept a smaller renegotiated bargain.
A smaller contract changes the territorial obligation explicitly.
It does not promise all Galicia in exchange for one symbolic shipment and leave the outcome to an invisible generosity roll.

## Promises and performance

Aid Fulfillment measures the actual credited fraction of the signed package.
A shipment counts after the relevant payment and delivery have succeeded, not on clicking a promise, scheduling a convoy, or opening a lend-lease window.
Delivered components are capped at their agreed contribution share.

The standard contract treats 75 percent fulfillment as sufficient to earn the promised postwar transfer, provided each required component has received at least half its promised quantity.
Full fulfillment creates a stronger moral and diplomatic claim and a better cooperative start.
Below the earning threshold the recipient can still honor the original bargain voluntarily, but is entitled to renegotiate rather than being automatically labelled a betrayer.

These thresholds do not decide whether Portugal literally caused victory.
They measure delivery of the bargain both sides signed.
The distinction prevents invented combat statistics from becoming evidence and still makes sustained, useful Portuguese assistance matter.

Portugal sends the package through one Ship Installment action during the 180-day delivery period.
Each installment carries a quarter of every signed component, so four shipments complete the contract and the player never splits crates by hand.
If Portuguese stock is short, the installment ships what exists and the shortfall stays visible as outstanding.
There is no Confidence gain per shipment.
Fulfillment rises with each delivery, while political credit occurs only at meaningful contract outcomes.

## Assistance beyond equipment

Volunteers can be part of a stronger intervention when the current game rules permit them.
Their strategic effect comes from actual units fighting, not from an extra event-created army.
Native volunteers that serve the recipient for at least 90 days of the contract add a fixed 10 points of Aid Fulfillment once (Part 14).
That rewards sending soldiers without pretending to measure their exact combat contribution.

Intelligence cooperation and diplomatic recognition use their native mechanics where they exist.
They earn no contract credit and no Confidence by themselves, but a recipient that Portugal already recognizes or shares intelligence with is more willing to sign (Part 13).
No intelligence action is required to receive the baseline territorial bargain, and the specification does not invent a native raid operation to simulate generic cooperation.
Logistical support must correspond to a usable route, supplies, or actual equipment, rather than a permanently free country modifier.

Native lend-lease and volunteer support must not be debited a second time by the contract.
If the implementation cannot reliably observe a native transaction, the contract uses a clearly identified event-managed delivery for credit and treats unobserved native aid as strategically useful but uncredited.
That is a visible accounting boundary, not a silent claim that all assistance was measured.

## Spain wins before the delivery deadline

Early victory must not convert an incomplete but still timely contract into an automatic Portuguese default.
The recipient can accept the aid already delivered, allow the remaining contracted delivery during a short postwar settlement window, or renegotiate the price in light of the shortened war.

Portugal is offered a 30-day final delivery window for an otherwise valid standard bargain.
The outstanding amount is shown.
It cannot be enlarged retroactively.
Material delivered after the war still strengthens the recovering Spanish state, so the bargain can remain meaningful even when the war ended faster than expected.

A Spain that refuses an earned transfer has broken the agreement.
A Spain that proposes a revision to an unearned, incomplete contract has not automatically done so.
The player-facing text must distinguish those outcomes.

## The supported side wins

Victory means the contracted national claimant has become the recognized surviving Spanish national authority for the relevant conflict, or has a verified legal successor.
A temporary occupation of Madrid, the disappearance of one minor rival, or joining the same faction does not suffice.

The victor can honor the earned bargain, request an explicit mutually agreed revision, or repudiate it.
Honor transfers Galicia when legally executable, preserves the contract history, and produces the strongest initial basis for the treaty.
An agreed revision might defer transfer until Galicia is recovered, replace the transfer with compensation accepted by Portugal, or produce a final Portuguese renunciation.

Repudiation is a major bilateral grievance.
Portugal can demand restitution, seek a compensating settlement, or escalate the dispute.
It does not immediately declare war from the Spanish response.
The fulfilled aid remains in Spain because it was already delivered.
The event must not refund weapons to Portugal while leaving those weapons in Spanish divisions.

If Galicia is held by a foreign owner, victory creates an obligation to seek recovery and renegotiate its timing.
It does not authorize a free transfer from the third party.
Spain can act in good faith without succeeding instantly.
A deliberate refusal to pursue or settle the obligation can later become a breach.

## The supported side loses

Defeat closes the conditional Galicia bargain.
Portugal receives no territory, no refund of delivered assistance, and no magical claim against the victor based on a promise the victor never made.
Its previous territorial claim may remain, but the losing side's contract does not bind the new government.

The victorious Spain remembers Portugal's intervention against it.
It can demand reassurance, compensation, or a period of non-interference before accepting a treaty conference.
The initial postwar relationship should usually be worse than after a simple refused peacetime demand.

Portugal can try to preserve contact with exiles or withdraw diplomatically, but this event does not create a separate exile insurgency, an endlessly spawning army, or an additional hidden restoration route.
Those would require their own established event systems.

## Recipient succession, fragmentation, and Portuguese defeat

A verified successor may inherit the contract after an explicit continuity check.
Cosmetic renaming is not a new agreement.
A civil-war splinter inherits it only if the relevant political authority and obligation actually pass to that splinter.
Rival branches cannot both claim the full remaining aid.

If Portugal capitulates, becomes unable to control its delivery route, or loses the capacity to supply the remaining package, the contract pauses or renegotiates according to the visible deadline.
The recipient does not continue receiving imaginary installments.
Portugal can admit inability to fulfill, which may cost confidence but is distinct from deliberate double-dealing.

A new civil war after a completed bargain is a new political crisis, not another firing of Event 093.
It can threaten existing treaty continuity and the Galician settlement.
It cannot re-award first-contract benefits or clear the history of a prior repudiation.
