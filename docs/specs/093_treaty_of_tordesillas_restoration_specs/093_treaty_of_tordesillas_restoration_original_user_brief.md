# Treaty of Tordesillas Restoration

## Catalog entry

- Event ID: `093`
- Event name: Treaty of Tordesillas Restoration
- Original catalog name: Portugal wants land
- Type: Minor Fire-Once
- Status: To Be Reworked
- Chaos level: 1
- Cluster: Formables
- Cluster role: Medium member

## Core idea

> Portugal demands Galicia from Spain. What begins as a small Iberian territorial dispute can eventually grow into Spain and Portugal trying to restore the Treaty of Tordesillas and divide the whole world between themselves.

The Galicia dispute is the baseline event.

The global partition develops only through later evolutions.

## Baseline: Portugal demands Galicia

Portugal demands Galicia from Spain.

How the demand develops depends on Spain's current situation.

If Spain is unified and at peace internally, Portugal can pressure Spain directly to cede Galicia.

Spain can accept the demand, reject it, or allow the dispute to grow into a larger confrontation.

If Galicia changes hands, the settlement becomes an important part of the later relationship between Portugal and Spain.

## Spanish Civil War route

The event becomes much more important when Spain is fighting a civil war.

Portugal can offer major assistance to one of the Spanish sides in return for Galicia after victory.

The Nationalists are the most natural partner, especially when Portugal's government is politically compatible with them, but the exact diplomatic possibilities should follow the live Spanish Civil War rather than assume one historical result.

Portuguese support can include useful wartime assistance such as:

- equipment
- volunteers
- logistical support
- intelligence
- diplomatic support
- other meaningful aid

The stronger Portugal's contribution to the eventual victory, the stronger its claim that Galicia was the agreed price.

If the supported Spanish side wins, it can honor the agreement and transfer Galicia to Portugal.

It can also break the agreement, creating a serious dispute with the country that helped it win.

This means Portugal can sometimes obtain Galicia through alliance and intervention rather than through a direct Iberian war.

If Portugal backed the losing side, the Galicia bargain fails and relations with the victorious Spain can become much worse.

## Iberian relationship

The Galicia crisis determines how easily Spain and Portugal can cooperate later.

A peaceful transfer or honored civil-war bargain can create an early basis for Iberian cooperation.

A refused demand, broken promise, or war over Galicia can create rivalry that must be settled before the two countries can seriously restore the old treaty together.

The later Treaty of Tordesillas path should therefore remember whether the relationship began through cooperation, opportunism, betrayal, or force.

# Evolutions

## Evolution I: The Treaty Restored

- Chaos requirement: `200+`.

Spain and Portugal begin seriously discussing the restoration of the Treaty of Tordesillas.

The old agreement is reinterpreted as the basis for a new Iberian world order.

Spain and Portugal can form the faction:

**Treaty of Tordesillas**

The two powers agree that their future expansion should follow separate spheres rather than constantly competing for the same territories.

The earlier Galicia dispute affects how equal this partnership feels and which country initially holds more influence inside the restored treaty.

## Evolution II: The World Divided

- Chaos requirement: `400+`.

Spain and Portugal extend the restored treaty to the entire world.

The globe is divided into a Spanish Sphere and a Portuguese Sphere based on Treaty of Tordesillas-style partition logic.

Spain gains claims over territory belonging to its side of the partition.

Portugal gains claims over territory belonging to its side.

The partition should be geographic and dynamic rather than a manually written list of modern countries.

The original treaty line is the main inspiration, while the opposite side of the world can follow the same broader logic that later required Spain and Portugal to settle their competing claims in Asia.

Spain and Portugal do not use the world partition to claim each other's protected Iberian homeland.

Galicia remains the special territorial dispute that began the event.

## Evolution III: Integration of the Spheres

- Chaos requirement: `600+`.

Spain and Portugal begin treating conquered territory inside their assigned spheres as land that can eventually become integrated into their empires.

Both countries gain a territorial integration and coring path inspired by Anarchist Spain's coring system, but designed to be considerably faster and less tedious.

The system should favor integrating coherent regions or groups of states rather than forcing the player to process every state individually.

Spain can integrate territory from the Spanish Sphere.

Portugal can integrate territory from the Portuguese Sphere.

Large, hostile, or recently conquered regions should still require more effort than small and well-established possessions.

## Evolution IV: Two Crowns, One World

- Chaos requirement: `800+`.

The restored treaty becomes a doctrine of complete world conquest.

Spain and Portugal increasingly treat every independent country inside their respective spheres as territory that ultimately belongs under the treaty order.

The Treaty of Tordesillas faction becomes the center of a global Iberian system.

Spain concentrates on completing its half of the world.

Portugal concentrates on completing its half.

When one treaty partner controls territory assigned to the other's sphere, they should increasingly prefer transferring it to the correct partner instead of permanently competing over it.

The stronger both empires become, the more aggressively they pursue the remaining parts of their assigned world.

## Treaty of Tordesillas faction

The faction is named:

**Treaty of Tordesillas**

Spain and Portugal are its two central powers.

Its purpose is the completion of the restored global partition.

Their expansion should complement each other, with each country focusing primarily on the half of the world assigned to it.

Other countries can eventually become subordinate members or partners of this order, but Spain and Portugal remain the two treaty powers.

## World-end scenario: Two Crowns, One World

If Spain and Portugal become overwhelmingly successful and Chaos is above `1000`, the restored treaty can become a world-end scenario.

The scenario should require both powers to have made major progress in conquering and integrating their assigned spheres.

Once triggered, the remaining independent world is treated as the unfinished portion of an already established Iberian global order.

Spain dominates one half of the world.

Portugal dominates the other.

Remaining independent countries are pressured, conquered, subordinated, or absorbed into the appropriate sphere until the world is divided between the two treaty powers.

## Connections

**World Claims** can interact naturally with this event. Existing unusual claims can influence early expansion, while the restored Treaty of Tordesillas later becomes the main basis for the Spanish and Portuguese global spheres.

The **Spanish Civil War** is an important baseline opportunity. Portugal can use wartime aid as leverage to obtain Galicia and create the relationship that later makes treaty restoration possible.

Other wars or internal crises in Spain can likewise make the original Galicia demand more urgent and give Portugal more leverage than it would have against a stable unified Spain.

## Design identity

The event should keep its escalation simple and recognizable:

1. Portugal wants Galicia.
2. A Spanish crisis can turn Galicia into the price of Portuguese support.
3. Spain and Portugal revive the Treaty of Tordesillas.
4. They divide the world into two spheres.
5. They claim and integrate their respective halves.
6. The treaty becomes a plan for complete world conquest.
7. If they succeed, the world can end divided between Spain and Portugal.
