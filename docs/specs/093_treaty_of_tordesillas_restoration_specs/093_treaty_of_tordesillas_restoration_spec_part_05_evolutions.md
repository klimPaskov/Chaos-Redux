# Treaty of Tordesillas Restoration: four evolutions


## Eligibility is not a reward

The four thresholds are eligibility gates for developments in an existing event. Unlocking an evolution adds no Chaos. New claims, an integration institution, or a global campaign require the associated political and practical steps. Reaching 800 Chaos does not make two hostile Iberian governments cooperate.

The event uses the shared evolution setting and current selection conventions. When an ordered stage becomes eligible, the proposed baseline opportunity timing is approximately 90 days, modified by live circumstances. The implementation must evaluate that timing through the established MTTH workflow. This specification does not claim that an AI weight is a percentage or that a 90-day mean is a fixed deadline.

An earlier invitation that was declined is not repeated automatically every few days. A renewed invitation needs a changed circumstance and the conference cooldown. The event can remain at a lower evolution indefinitely. Once a stage has been politically adopted, a later fall in Chaos does not erase its historical claims, completed institutions, or cores. New world-end entry still requires the current strict threshold.

## Evolution I: The Treaty Restored

At **200+ Chaos**, Portugal and the recognized Spanish national government can seriously propose restoration. The Galician question must have a recognized outcome, including an agreed renunciation, and any material breach must have been repaired. Both must possess independent authority to sign. They cannot be at war with each other.

The minimum Confidence is 50. That is a necessary indicator, not the whole condition. A country still in a multi-sided civil war cannot sign away the future policy of all Spain merely because Portugal likes it. A preliminary understanding may exist during the war, while formal ratification waits for the national authority to be resolved.

The conference allows a limited restoration that commits the two powers to complementary future expansion, mutual consultation, and the protected Iberian settlement. It does not yet allocate all countries on Earth. The faction is formed or validly reorganized as **Treaty of Tordesillas** only after both governments ratify and existing faction obligations permit it.

The initial institutional position reflects the relationship in Part 04. The stronger country may chair the faction, while an honored Portuguese bargain can secure equality. The two powers can stop here and use a limited alliance without ever taking global claims.

## Evolution II: The World Divided

At **400+ Chaos**, an active restored treaty can extend its doctrine worldwide. Both governments must approve the geographic partition and its protected exceptions. They must see that the line assigns states, not whole contemporary countries, and that some existing overseas possessions may lie on the partner's side.

The declaration activates the sphere ledger in Part 06 and supplies treaty claims over foreign, claimable territory in each power's sphere. Neither power gains new treaty claims on the other's protected Iberian homeland. Galicia follows the settlement already signed. A claim is a political demand, not sovereignty, access, a war declaration, or a core.

Existing wrong-sphere possessions are listed for negotiation. The treaty does not instantly strip Spain or Portugal of its colonial holdings. It establishes the direction of future restitution and blocks those holdings from the wrong partner's new event-owned integration pathway.

Other countries receive a proportionate diplomatic response. A regional power discovers that both crowns have allocated different parts of its territory. A major power can organize opposition. A small country can seek recognition, bargaining space, or support. The external response begins at this evolution, rather than waiting until the world-end.

If either power refuses, Evolution I continues. There is no punishment for choosing a limited alliance. The treaty does not issue a globe of claims under the cover of a routine faction-management decision.

## Evolution III: Integration of the Spheres

At **600+ Chaos**, the partners can establish the regional integration system. The world partition must already be active. Each country's internal administration is its own responsibility, while both agree on the rules for sphere eligibility and cross-border projects.

The opening institution gives each partner two concurrent regional integration projects. It does not grant cores on everything already conquered. Regions must meet the ownership, control, security, and investment conditions in Part 07. Galicia has an explicit exception that gives its recognized owner a route to integrate it without moving the global line.

The institution makes newly conquered territory strategically useful sooner than a tedious state-by-state coring campaign, but still presents choices. A country can consolidate a small secure region, invest in a large recent conquest, or delay integration to keep equipment for a war. Faster integration and lower cost are meaningful focus-path alternatives, not mandatory duplicate bonuses.

The two Consolidation indicators become available at this stage. They measure balanced readiness through both territorial reach and actual integration. A partner with many puppets but almost no integrated territory cannot reach world-end readiness. A partner with a compact core empire but little reach cannot do so either.

Adopting the institution creates no automatic Chaos. The coercive consequences of a particular integration settlement and the growth of an exclusive imperial order are assessed separately in Part 15. Ordinary administrative completion is not automatically treated as civilian death or instability.

## Evolution IV: Two Crowns, One World

At **800+ Chaos**, the treaty can become an explicit doctrine of complete world conquest. Both partners must still exist as sovereign treaty powers, affirm the existing partition, and accept reciprocal duties. Confidence must be at least 60, with no unresolved material breach.

The doctrine changes strategic priorities. Spain and Portugal identify the remaining countries and regions in their assigned spheres, organize coherent theaters, pressure exposed governments, and transfer eligible wrong-sphere conquests to the correct partner. It does not erase existing wars, teleport armies, or permit unrestricted attacks on allies.

The country that is further ahead is encouraged to help the weaker partner through actual equipment, access, defensive support, or agreed joint campaigns. Aid does not let it count the weaker partner's territory as its own sphere. The design should make protecting the other crown rational, especially for a player who could otherwise annex it.

New aggressive behavior is conditional on real ability. An exhausted Portugal should recover and consolidate rather than attack every major power simultaneously. A Spain without a viable naval route should not choose a remote invasion simply because the target's state has a treaty claim.

Declining the doctrine preserves Evolution III. Both countries can maintain their integrated empires without committing to a universal final campaign.

## The path when an evolution is disabled

Disabling evolution progression before the event fires leaves the Galicia crisis playable and complete. Disabling a higher stage prevents its new activation. It does not replay the opening, take away existing national territory, or delete cores gained before the setting changed.

A mid-campaign settings change follows the shared framework's permitted semantics. The player must see whether an existing treaty feature remains active or is suspended. This event must not invent a private interpretation that bypasses the global setting.

The separate world-end setting remains authoritative. Evolution IV does not override a disabled world-end and can support ordinary conquest without triggering the scenario.

## Presentation of escalation

The presentation should expand with the political scale. Galicia uses intimate diplomatic and wartime scenes. Restoration uses a bilateral conference. Partition needs a reliable geographic preview. Integration uses regional administrative status. The universal doctrine uses strategic planning and the emerging external coalition.

Only the actual world-end warrants the planned super-event. Four evolution levels do not require four oversized presentations. Important choices must remain readable and interruptible, with their immediate outcome and longer commitment distinguished.
