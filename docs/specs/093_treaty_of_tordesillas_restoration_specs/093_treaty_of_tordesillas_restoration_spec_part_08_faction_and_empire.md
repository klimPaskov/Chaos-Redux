# Treaty of Tordesillas Restoration: faction, subjects, and expansion


## Two treaty powers within one faction

The faction's exact name is **Treaty of Tordesillas**.
Spain and Portugal are its two central powers.
The engine may require one faction leader, but that mechanical office does not create unilateral authority over the partition, the other government's homeland, or the other government's subjects.

The faction chair handles ordinary leadership functions according to the game.
Treaty decisions that change the political compact need both powers' consent.
Domestic integration projects and country-specific preparation remain national decisions.
A cooperative war can be proposed by either power and accepted according to ordinary diplomatic conditions.

Faction membership and treaty-power status are separate.
A third member does not become a third crown by becoming industrially strong, assuming mechanical faction leadership, or hosting an exiled government.
A subject of Spain does not become Spain's equal treaty partner by owning Galicia.

## Existing alliances and wars

Neither power is silently removed from an existing faction.
The conference assesses whether the countries can leave, reorganize a faction they control, or defer restoration.
Ongoing wars, guarantees, and existing members require an explicit valid transition.

If Spain and Portugal already share a faction, the treaty can be ratified politically before the faction's formal reorganization.
The exact **Treaty of Tordesillas** faction identity becomes active only after a legally valid change.
No other members are expelled or subordinated merely to obtain the desired name.

If the two countries are on opposing sides of a war, ratification is blocked.
If they are in separate wars against third parties, they may cooperate where the game's diplomacy allows it, but restoration does not merge every conflict or transfer all military access automatically.

The event never assumes an existing foreign major will surrender faction leadership because an Iberian conference asks it to.
A player must resolve that political obstacle through actual diplomacy or wait.

## Complementary campaigns

Expansion planning is organized around a few coherent theaters, not a separate mission for every country.
Each partner identifies accessible target regions in its own sphere, considers current wars and supply, and proposes support where the other partner can contribute.

Spain may need Portuguese basing or transit to reach its assigned territories.
Portugal may need Spanish material assistance to sustain a larger continental campaign.
Passage through the partner's sphere is permitted by agreement and is distinct from the right to annex land there.
A fleet crossing an ocean does not breach the partition.

Joint campaigns identify the intended beneficiary of each eligible conquest before the peace settlement.
The supporting partner can receive a negotiated reimbursement or reciprocal support, but it cannot sell the same state repeatedly.
The incentive is a stronger common order and progress by both crowns.

Ordinary claims outside the assigned sphere can remain in the country's diplomatic record.
Treaty policy discourages acting on them against the partner's interests.
It does not require every unrelated claim in the game to be removed or made inaccessible to other systems.

## Restitution of wrong-sphere territory

After Evolution II, the partners can voluntarily settle existing exceptions.
At Evolution IV, regular restitution becomes the preferred policy for new eligible conquests.
A bounded review presents the states that can legally be transferred, why they belong to the other sphere, and what the donor will lose.

The donor must own the territory.
Enemy-owned occupation cannot be transferred by this action.
Active combat, disputed legal ownership, or an unresolved peace conference postpones the transaction.
Existing native cores and protected exceptions require explicit donor consent and are never covered by standing automatic approval.

Both players can authorize a standing policy for newly acquired, non-core, undisputed wrong-sphere states.
That is consent to a defined class of transfers, not consent to give away the homeland.
The policy can be revoked prospectively.
A human recipient still sees a digest and can reject an impossible or unwanted handover where the game requires acceptance.

Transfers preserve state population, damage, buildings, resources, and relevant shared records.
They do not duplicate equipment, repair industry, clear civilian crises, or grant new cores.
The correct partner may then integrate the state through its own regional path.

A dispute over restitution first produces a request and a reasonable response period.
A government can justify a temporary wartime delay.
Deliberate permanent retention after a valid agreement damages Confidence and can suspend the compact.

## Subordinate states

Other countries can become partners, protected associates, or subjects of the treaty order.
Their status is explicit.
An independent partner can cooperate without being counted as conquered territory.
A subject participates through a recognized patron, while its state ownership remains real.

A country wholly within one sphere normally has that sphere's crown as its patron.
A country crossing the line cannot have two native overlords at once.
It receives one patron selected by its political settlement, with a documented arrangement for the other sphere's states.

A temporary cross-sphere arrangement can permit access and administration while leaving the state's legal owner intact.
For final partition credit, every subject-owned state must lie in its single patron's sphere.
Any wrong-sphere portion must be legally ceded to the proper crown or to a valid subject of that crown before it can count as settled.
A lease, nominal administrative office, or unsupported dual-overlord arrangement cannot substitute for that ownership change.
It does not give both crowns integration credit for the same population, and it is not counted as a completed final partition.
Lasting completion requires a valid agreed state settlement or a native subject arrangement that genuinely places each area under the correct treaty order.

A subject cannot be stripped of land through a popup that bypasses all its established rights and diplomatic constraints.
The relevant government or lawful overlord must participate.
Forced annexation or peace settlement can still occur through the game's existing mechanisms, with their normal consequences.

## Negotiated accession

A threatened country can accept a defined protectorate or subject arrangement, refuse, or seek a conditional compromise.
Acceptance may preserve its government, army, and a measure of internal autonomy.
The patron receives access and strategic cooperation, not immediate cores on every subject state.

A compromise might postpone accession, grant transit, or recognize a boundary.
It is useful diplomacy, but does not count as political absorption for the world-end.
The interface must distinguish a signed commercial or access agreement from subordination.

The treaty powers can offer terms tailored to the target's vulnerability.
A small isolated state has different incentives from a major whose army remains intact.
A country is never annexed just because an AI probability roll called it frightened.

## Internal identity

Spain and Portugal retain their leaders, ruling parties, flags, and meaningful native focus trees.
Their formal country names need not change when they join the faction.
Any optional cosmetic title is a separately reviewed presentation choice, not a new tag or a requirement to rename every ideology.

New subordinate members retain their grounded identities unless an existing, independently established formable or peace outcome changes them.
Event 093 does not invent generic 'Spanish Asia' or 'Portuguese Africa' tags merely to simplify the map.

No founding package grants free fleets, divisions, manpower pools, or technologies.
Expansion must use actual national resources and established military systems.
Focus rewards can support logistics, training, and cooperation within verified limits, as specified in Part 12.

## A treaty that can remain limited

A successful faction need not produce the world-end.
The partners may honor the line in selected theaters, integrate secure possessions, and reject universal conquest.
Other countries may remain independent associates.

That limited order should remain coherent and rewarding without becoming a disguised failure state.
The full division of the world is the event's extreme destination, not a compulsory ending imposed on every cooperative Portugal-Spain campaign.
