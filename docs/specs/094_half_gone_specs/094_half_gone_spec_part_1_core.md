## The missing half

One country discovers that half of its reserve equipment has disappeared. The depots have not burned down. The trains carrying equipment yesterday have not necessarily been attacked. Some guards insist that nobody entered the warehouses. Other reports are less reassuring, but none establishes an explanation that accounts for the loss. An inventory becomes the first reliable description of the disaster, because the country must start planning with what remains.

At baseline, every compatible military equipment stockpile loses 50% of its current contents. The country keeps its factories, production lines, technologies, active formations, and equipment already issued to those formations. Its immediate problem is the loss of reserves. A large army may continue fighting normally at first, then discover that ordinary battle losses and new deployment orders have become much harder to supply.

The later evolutions extend this same principle to accumulated resources, national conditions, population, and important people. They do not replace the missing half with a conventional disaster. A fuel tank need not rupture. A research team can retain its equipment while losing part of its unfinished work. An army can retain its divisions while losing a substantial portion of its commanders.

The event always acts on the current country. A recovery can be destroyed by a later firing. A country which has not recovered loses half of the smaller amount that remains. Previous inventories may explain what happened or establish a recovery milestone, but they never supply the amount removed by a future firing.

## One country receives the incident

Each normal firing selects one random valid country from the union of current major countries and player-controlled countries. A player-controlled major appears once in that union. Several players controlling one country do not give that country several chances. AI majors and player-controlled minors remain genuine candidates.

A country must be a live participant with something the incident can meaningfully affect. A pure system carrier, dead tag, or discarded temporary actor is not a target. A country need not be prosperous or safe. War, low reserves, subject status, defeat, and exile do not provide blanket immunity. A landless government can still lose equipment that it actually owns. Population and territorial categories simply require their own valid territory.

The recommended targeting rule is an equal draw between valid countries. Country size affects how much is lost through the halving rule. It does not also increase the country's chance of being selected. Target validity is assessed against the currently usable category set, including the mandatory equipment operation. An empty country with no eligible additional quantity is skipped rather than receiving a dramatic false report.

The loss is settled before the player receives the report. Acknowledging the report does not authorize, postpone, or repeat it. This preserves the sudden character of the incident and prevents a player from leaving the window open while spending or deploying the threatened reserves.

## What remains outside the baseline

The baseline concerns stored equipment. Soldiers do not disappear from divisions, aircraft do not disappear from active wings, and commissioned ships do not disappear from fleets merely because their country was selected. Production capacity, technologies, training progress, and equipment designs remain intact. Equipment being actively issued or committed to another system must have one clear owner at the instant of disappearance.

The line between reserve and issued equipment is factual rather than cosmetic. Renaming a stockpile, changing a production queue, or opening an interface cannot shelter equipment that is still in reserve. Conversely, equipment that has genuinely left reserve must not be debited again through a second representation of the same goods.

The baseline also avoids additional arbitrary punishments. It does not automatically reduce stability, kill warehouse workers, damage factories, start a fire, release chemicals, or blame a foreign country. Those would be separate events or separately selected evolved losses. The missing reserve is already consequential.

## The player learns the result

The first report identifies the affected country and the categories which actually lost something. Its main text gives the scene and the government's immediate problem. Its effects summary gives the measured loss and the amount remaining. Large equipment inventories can be grouped for readability without omitting less familiar equipment from the actual operation.

The reaction can express disbelief, administrative determination, or grim understatement. Material losses can support dry humor about flawless records and empty shelves. Population disappearances require a different register, with attention to survivors and interrupted ordinary life. A reaction never decides which category vanished after the fact.

Normal recovery remains available through production, trade, recruitment, research, and diplomacy. A limited aftermath category adds a few focused government responses where the type of loss warrants them. It does not hold the country in a permanent event-management loop.

## The mystery has no final answer

Investigations can discover that a particular depot was locked, that two inventories disagree, or that a staff member was present shortly before the incident. Such findings describe circumstances. They do not identify a universal cause, culprit, destination, or mechanism.

A government may accuse another government. A scientist may offer an explanation. The Devil may claim to know more than everyone else. Their statements remain attributed claims. The narrator, hidden completion reports, achievement descriptions, and asset choices must not quietly certify them as the answer.

Repeated firings strengthen the recognition of a pattern. They do not build an evidence meter that eventually solves it. Even a successful replacement, reconstructed archive, new recruit, or extraordinary recovery leaves the original disappearance unresolved.

## The event's place in the campaign

Half gone remains a Minor Repeatable event with Chaos level 1 and a Medium role in Various Anomalies. Its devastating late outcomes do not turn each ordinary firing into a major event or move it into another cluster. The shared repeatable-event recovery and weight-cap rules continue to control its return.

Cluster delivery invokes the same incident. It does not apply a different fraction, hit every major, or halve the selected country once for each internal cluster reference. One delivered Event 094 incident receives one target and one loss transaction. A genuinely later repeat remains free to select the same country.
