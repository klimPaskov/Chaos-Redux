# Characters and continuity

## The eligible people

Evolution III can select generals, field marshals, admirals, operatives, advisors, scientists, and other compatible important character groups. Eligibility means a living, current person belonging to the selected country's relevant roster. Assigned and unassigned members both matter. A commander does not become immune because an army is using them.

Unrecruited future characters do not count as people the country currently owns. Decorative portraits, institutional organizations, generic future recruitment options, and inactive historical tokens do not inflate the roster. A country cannot lose a scientist who has never arrived merely because the character exists in a database.

The selected group loses approximately half its valid identities. The count uses unbiased rounding for odd rosters, and the people themselves are sampled without replacement. Skill, fame, loyalty, political convenience, and cost do not automatically protect a person. Losing three experienced commanders can be more serious than losing three recent recruits, and that difference belongs to the country's real roster.

## One person can hold several roles

The disappearance acts on a person rather than one menu slot. A person who is both a scientist and an advisor is counted once when selected. Their disappearance removes or suspends every active role which requires that same person to be present. It does not leave an absent person giving an advisor bonus from another screen.

The public result identifies the selected group and then explains linked role consequences. If one selected scientist was also a national advisor, the scientist loss and the advisor vacancy describe one person. The event must not claim that a second, separately selected advisor also disappeared.

General and field-marshal classification uses the person's current role. Promotions do not produce two identities. Other overlapping groups publish their actual membership. During Evolution IV, a later category which overlaps an already selected character roster is removed from the available combinations. This conservative rule preserves a clear half of each selected group without secretly reducing a group twice or redefining its denominator after the first selection.

The conflict is based on shared people, not just similar names. Two disjoint groups can both be selected. Existing character owners may supply a more precise compatible operation later, but it must preserve the same one-person, one-disappearance rule.

## Command vacancies

When a commander disappears, their surviving forces remain in existence. Their command assignment ends or becomes vacant through the normal command system. The units do not vanish, surrender, lose half their equipment, or become an independent army simply to dramatize the missing leader.

The government must appoint available leadership or recruit and train replacements. The event does not generate an identical substitute with the same traits and experience. The command-reorganization response can help surviving and newly appointed leaders gain experience, but it does not recreate the missing individuals.

A field marshal's disappearance can affect several subordinate armies through the loss of that role. The report should distinguish that command effect from the much larger claim that the armies themselves disappeared. Naval leadership follows the same principle for surviving fleets.

## Operatives and ongoing operations

An operative who disappears is no longer available for assignments. Ongoing work requiring that person must be handed back to the intelligence owner, which decides whether to continue with surviving staff, pause, fail, or release an unconsumed reservation.

The disappearance is not automatically a capture or assassination. It does not reveal the operative's nationality to an enemy or prove that an enemy intelligence agency caused it. Sensitive identities remain subject to the owner's normal disclosure rules.

Already spent resources are not refunded because the event wants to be kind. Resources still genuinely held for an unstarted task may be released through the owner's ordinary cancellation rules. The same goods cannot be returned both by Event 094 and by the operation owner.

## Advisors, scientists, and institutions

An advisor's absence ends personal services and personally contingent bonuses. An institution which had already enacted a law, built a factory, or completed a reform does not cease to exist merely because a person associated with it is missing.

A missing scientist creates a staffing problem. Unfinished work may pause or require reassignment according to the research owner's rules. Completed technologies and built facilities remain. Research progress only receives a direct half-loss when the relevant national-value category was also selected and proved independent of the staffing consequence.

New candidates can be recruited through ordinary systems. Recruitment is the arrival of different people, not the return of the missing group. They begin with their legitimate starting skills and costs. The event never multiplies a recruitment refund or repeatedly grants a unique opening reward while trying to fill a vacancy.

## Heads of government and protected lifecycle roles

A leader who also belongs to an eligible advisor or military roster creates a succession issue. The design does not grant every dual-role person immunity. It requires a valid owner-supported transition for roles which cannot simply be left empty.

Where a normal succession path exists, the person can disappear and the country continues under the legitimate successor. The successor is not evidence of a coup or of the disappearance's cause. Where the role belongs to a complex unique character system, that system must provide an absence outcome before the character is eligible.

An unsafe character is not silently deleted and then replaced with a random generic country leader. It remains a clearly recorded implementation dependency. Full compatibility for the named Chaos Redux characters is part of the intended completed design, rather than a permanent blanket exclusion labeled as support.

## Doctor Wu

Doctor Wu can be caught when he is a valid member of a selected character group and his owner has registered the disappearance transition. The incident removes his personal availability once, including linked roles which require him. It does not invoke an ordinary death, betrayal, exile, or dismissal route merely because one happens to exist.

The proposed connected outcome separates his personal work from the institution surrounding it. Documented treatments, completed discoveries, stocks already made, and trained independent staff remain with their rightful owners. Work explicitly requiring Wu's personal participation pauses or loses that participation. Treatments already given are not retroactively undone.

The surviving institution must choose how to continue. It may preserve the unfinished program under existing senior staff, request outside expertise through an already available foreign-medicine route, or suspend a line of work which cannot continue responsibly without him. These choices operate through Wu's own staff, access, research, and medicine systems when those systems are verified.

A continuing treatment program does not prove that Wu is alive somewhere. A failed program does not prove that he is dead. The disappearance creates a personal and institutional absence. It must not reveal secret knowledge, transfer every cure to every country, or allow an unqualified generic replacement to acquire Wu's unique abilities.


## Brilliant Scientist

Doctor Warren Kruger is one identity across his advisor, scientist, and possible leadership roles. A qualifying disappearance must coordinate all of them. Removing a scientist slot while leaving the same person active as advisor or ruler would not satisfy the event.

The proposed outcome tests the independence of the institution he leaves behind. Surviving facilities, completed technologies, produced equipment, and deployed formations remain. Projects which require his personal participation pause or enter the owner's existing continuity handling. Independently maintained work can continue under the people and capabilities which the campaign has actually established.

A host which invested in independent expertise should cope differently from a host which relied entirely on Kruger. The existing Directorate distinction between Dependence and Independent Capacity provides the appropriate owner-level direction. Half gone does not establish new copies of those meters.

Where Kruger rules a state, the owner must resolve legitimate leadership continuity. The disappearance is not an automatic capitulation, assassination, containment victory, or authorization to detonate a device. A fail-deadly doctrine must evaluate its own documented trigger, rather than assuming every absent character is a confirmed killing.

Kruger's established continuity forms also matter. The owner determines whether a biological individual, an embodied machine, or another established identity is the current eligible person. Event 094 cannot invent a backup, erase a distributed civilization because it shares his name, or use a disappearance as proof of his original provenance.

Any exceptional temporal restoration remains an Event 016 operation with its own prerequisites, synchronization costs, debt, bounded target, and one-use safeguards. It does not become a free Event 094 button. Restoring a bounded prior state still does not establish where the missing person or material went.

## The lasting absence

The character remains unavailable until a legitimate owner action changes that state. Reopening an event, switching tags, loading a save, or reinstalling a role must not recruit the person again as a fresh reward.

The missing group need not receive a new character portrait package. The existing identity can be shown where appropriate, with the established unavailable state or a restrained report composition. A visual effect which depicts a proven portal, disintegration, or confirmed body would contradict the uncertainty unless a separate owner has independently established that fact for a different event.
