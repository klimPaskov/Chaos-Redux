# Evolutions and national values

## Four expansions of the same loss

The four evolutions enlarge the things that may disappear. Equipment retains its normal baseline half-loss at every tier. The country never receives an evolved report which quietly substitutes a small resource debit for the promised equipment loss.

| Evolution | Chaos requirement | Additional reach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I: Half the Reserves | 200+ | One accumulated reserve category |
| II: Half the Nation | 400+ | One category drawn from reserves and broader national values |
| III: Half the People | 600+ | One category drawn from reserves, national values, population, and character groups |
| IV: Half of Everything | 800+ | Several distinct additional categories from enabled earlier evolutions |

These thresholds are the user's thresholds. The proposed sampling details below complete the unspecified selection behavior. They do not replace the thresholds or change the removal fraction.

## The tier is chosen for this incident

Half gone is an instantaneous incident. It has no continuing disappearance front, active disease, or territorial movement which must evolve between firings. Each new firing therefore evaluates its starting conditions, including current Chaos and enabled evolution controls, before selecting its losses.

A first firing at high Chaos may immediately use the relevant enabled tiers. A repeat at high Chaos may do the same. This uses the instant, more-intense opening case described by the project event guidance. It does not start a separate ninety-day process which removes additional things while the random event itself has not fired.

A prior high-tier firing does not permit population loss during a later low-Chaos firing. History remembers that the phenomenon has occurred, while current eligibility determines what this incident can do. Crossing a threshold, showing a preview, or recording an evolution does not itself remove anything or add Chaos.

The enable controls operate independently. Disabling Evolution III removes population and character candidates even when Evolution IV is enabled. Disabling Evolution I removes its extra-reserve pool without disabling the ordinary equipment baseline. Higher enabled pools do not require a disabled lower pool to execute first. Evolution IV changes the number of additional selections, not which disabled categories may return through a side door.

Each evolution is recorded through the shared history system when its behavior first materially appears. A high-Chaos incident can record several relevant first-use entries, ordered I to IV, but only for enabled behavior actually used. A population-only extra does not fabricate an earlier reserve loss just to fill the history. The ordinary incident records its target and actual effective tier once.

## Evolution I: Half the Reserves

Equipment vanishes and one additional positive accumulated reserve is selected. The country can lose fuel when its army is relying on movement, Political Power shortly before a government decision, or experience which had been saved for a redesign. The event does not preferentially choose whatever is easiest for the country to replace.

The selection uses categories actually present. Empty pools and unsupported mechanics do not become dramatic zero-effect choices. Convoys and trains remain subject to the shared-stock exclusions in Part 2. An alias for a stock already struck by the baseline cannot enter the extra pool.

At this tier, one additional category is selected when one exists. A country with no eligible extra reserve receives the equipment loss alone. This is a genuine lack of an eligible quantity, not permission to silently omit an implemented resource family.

## Evolution II: Half the Nation

Broader national values join the selection pool. Stability and war support lose half their current values. Eighty percent stability becomes forty percent. Twenty percent war support becomes ten percent. The event does not subtract a fixed fifty points or force a negative result.

Available manpower means unassigned manpower which can actually be spent on recruitment or reinforcement. It does not mean half of all soldiers serving in the armed forces. Existing divisions, wings, and ships retain their assigned personnel unless another established owner causes a separate, independently attributable change.

Research progress refers to unfinished progress in currently active ordinary research projects. When the category is selected, each compatible active project's current progress is halved. Completed technologies, doctrines already purchased, available research slots, research-speed modifiers, and unspent generic research bonuses remain intact. A project at ninety percent falls to forty-five percent, while a project at ten percent falls to five percent.

Special-project progress and owner-specific scientific resources may join only through their own safe progress operation. They must not be substituted with lost research slots or a random technology removal. A system which cannot set unfinished progress accurately remains an explicit implementation blocker for that named category.

Intelligence-related resources enter as clearly named, owner-supported quantities. A country's own accumulated network strength may qualify when the system can reduce it coherently. Enemy information, an operation's hidden success roll, already unlocked agency upgrades, and another country's network are not automatically the selected country's reserve. Operatives themselves belong to Evolution III.

Other country-level values must have an understandable half-loss. Ordered stages, binary memberships, ideologies whose shares must sum to a whole, and institution states are not treated as arbitrary scalars. The owner may expose a suitable supporting resource instead. A loss of legitimacy or parliamentary support is possible only where the named quantity has a safe, meaningful proportional operation.

## Evolution III: Half the People

Civilian population and important character groups become eligible. The selection does not automatically take both. A country may suffer a demographic disappearance, a reduced officer corps, missing naval leadership, missing operatives, an emptied advisory circle, or missing scientists.

Population is a single national category distributed across eligible populated territory. Named character categories use valid current members, with the identity and overlap rules in Part 5. A country with many small rosters must not become far more likely to suffer a human disappearance merely because each DLC creates another list.

A human loss is reported as a disappearance. Military commands, laboratories, households, and institutions then deal with the absence. The country does not gain confirmed casualties, murder evidence, martyrdom bonuses, or automatic vengeance against a convenient foreign rival.

## Evolution IV: Half of Everything

One incident can take equipment and several additional categories. The proposed count is two, three, or four additional categories, with equal initial weight for those three counts. If fewer distinct compatible categories exist, all available selections can be used, without selecting a category twice to meet a quota.

Every additional loss is based on the common pre-incident state. Applying one category must not shrink the basis used to calculate another independent category. Shared representations require the overlap rules first. Population and available manpower, or advisors and scientists held by the same people, cannot produce accidental repeated losses through nested owner callbacks.

The name expresses the widening reach of the phenomenon. It is not a promise to halve every number in the country's save. Factories, state ownership, laws, completed technologies, divisions, and unrelated global values remain outside the defined category space.

A severe incident may therefore take half the stored equipment, half the fuel, half the current stability, and half the eligible civilian population. Another country at the same tier may instead lose Command Power, unfinished research progress, and naval leadership. Both retain the same underlying rule.

## Proposed selection balance

Selection first chooses a broad family and then a category inside that family. This prevents one extensible list from drowning out the others. At Evolution I the reserve family is the only family. At Evolution II, the proposed family weights are 50 for reserves and 50 for national values. At Evolution III and IV, they are 35 for reserves, 35 for national values, and 30 for people.

Within the people family, the proposed split is 25 for population and 75 for character groups. The character share is distributed equally among valid, nonconflicting groups. Within the other families, distinct valid semantic categories start with equal weight. A large equipment-variant inventory does not create thousands of extra lottery tickets because equipment remains the compulsory baseline.

Absent families and categories are removed before normalization. Evolution IV repeats the draw without replacement and removes conflicting categories as it goes. Its second, third, and fourth draws are therefore conditional draws, not copies of the first probability. These are starting balance proposals, not engine-tested odds.

Under the deliberately simple case where every family and both people subfamilies are valid, the first Evolution III extra has a population probability of 30% multiplied by 25%, or 7.5%. That arithmetic is a reference case only. Actual country pools, settings, and multi-selection conflicts require the mandatory probability audit.

## Severe results without duplicate punishment

The immediate removals provide the main difficulty. Half gone does not append a general national penalty for every selected category. It does not turn four actual half-losses into those losses plus an arbitrary industrial collapse and stability collapse.

Later pressure can still be real. A fuel shortage constrains operations. Lost population changes the country's demographic basis. Missing commanders interrupt command assignments. Existing owner systems may respond to changed inputs through their normal rules. Such responses should explain the consequence of a changed state, rather than pretending that the event selected another category.

A country can be struck again before recovering. The later incident is not softened to maintain a narrative arc, and the aftermath does not confer immunity. Its current values, valid categories, and settings determine the new result.
