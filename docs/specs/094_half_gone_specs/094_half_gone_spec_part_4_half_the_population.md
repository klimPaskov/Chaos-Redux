# Half the population

## A disappearance across populated territory

When civilian population is selected, the country loses roughly half its current population across its populated territory. The loss is distributed throughout the eligible population. It is not concentrated into a few unlucky states to make implementation easier, and it is not replaced by a reduction to the available manpower counter.

The proposed territorial rule uses the selected country's currently owned populated states. This includes non-core and colonial states which it owns, including owned states temporarily occupied by another power. Foreign states merely occupied by the selected country do not join its demographic pool. This provides one stable territorial ownership rule and prevents several occupying countries from claiming the same population as their own for one incident.

The ownership decision is fixed for the transaction. If a state changes hands afterward, the population which remains travels with the state through normal territorial rules. A former owner cannot continue removing people from that state through an old aftermath record. A later firing uses the later ownership map.

The population quantity is the game's authoritative state demographic measure. Fielded military manpower is a separate protected gameplay pool. The design does not claim to track every soldier's individual place of residence. It requires a real reduction of the state population measure while preserving the baseline protection of active forces.

## A national half with a local half

Every eligible state supplies its current population before any state is changed. The country-level total establishes the required whole-person loss, using the same unbiased rounding principle as other indivisible quantities. Each state first contributes the whole-number part of half its population. Any remaining people needed to reach the national loss are assigned among the states whose populations were odd.

Those residual assignments are selected once from the eligible odd states. They are not always taken from the capital, the smallest state, the lowest state identifier, or a particular region. This leaves every state within one person of its own half while preserving the country total.

This is an accounting rule, not an invitation to present enormous lists of exact local figures to the player. The report should give the national disappearance total and a concise territorial summary. Detailed state results belong to the system's records and verification surfaces.

Empty states remain empty. The event cannot create people in order to remove them. It also cannot impose a fictional minimum population floor which guarantees that a tiny state survives untouched. Native engine limits or incompatible states must be exposed as real limitations, not hidden beneath a claim of exact coverage.

## Missing is not confirmed dead

The missing people leave the active demographic population. They are not added to the confirmed Deaths total simply because a population helper normally also logs deaths. Their disappearance does not count as combat casualties, an atrocity, an epidemic death, a bombing casualty, or a migration arrival somewhere else.

The country can record an incident total of missing people for reports and recovery responsibilities. That total describes the event. It does not become a second population pool from which units may be recruited, supplies consumed, or another disappearance deducted.

Subsequent ordinary deaths among the remaining population still belong to their existing owners. Subsequent ordinary migration still moves actual people. A missing person's imagined destination must never be invented to balance a ledger. The defining uncertainty survives even when the country's administration has to make practical arrangements for absent people.

## Manpower and demographic change

Population and available manpower are related but distinct gameplay quantities. A population loss changes the demographic basis from which future recruitment and other population-dependent systems operate. It must not be implemented by destroying half the existing divisions or removing half the personnel already serving in active formations.

When available manpower is independently selected by the same Evolution IV incident, its intended direct result is half of its pre-incident unassigned pool. Any native manpower side effect of the population operation must be accounted for before a further debit is applied. Two routes into the same manpower counter cannot leave one quarter merely because each helper was told to remove half.

The required result is one planned direct loss per independent quantity. Where the installed engine or an owner does not allow population and available manpower to be separated safely, the two categories must be declared conflicting before the draw. Such a limitation is a documented compatibility constraint. It is not permission to select both, apply a hidden quarter-loss, and still report two clean half-losses.

Future mobilization and recruitment follow the remaining population and the country's normal laws. The disappearance does not silently change conscription law, grant emergency manpower, or guarantee a particular recovery curve.

## Refugees, displaced people, and local cohorts

An existing migration or displacement system may describe some people through both physical state population and a bookkeeping cohort. The physical people disappear once. The cohort owner then reconciles its representation with the physical result.

People already included in a destination state's population cannot be removed again from a reception ledger as an additional physical debit. People still in a distinct transit pool are not automatically part of the selected country's owned-state total. Their owner must establish whether a suitable independently owned population category exists before they are included anywhere else.

A cohort does not become a confirmed death report through cleanup. Nor does it produce a new arrival in an unrelated country. Custody, origin, reception burden, and the remaining cohort identity continue to be handled by the system which already owns them.

When a selected country owns a state controlled by another power, the state population result remains the same physical result. Relevant owner and controller records must reflect that result without adding two sets of disappearances. The controlling country may suffer an ordinary demographic consequence in that state, but it has not received a second direct Event 094 firing.

## Institutions after the disappearance

Factories, transport links, hospitals, schools, and homes remain on the map unless another event changes them. Their continued existence makes the empty places unsettling. Half gone does not also delete half the buildings to make the state look evenly divided.

Systems which use population as an input may naturally change their demands or capacities. A smaller population can mean fewer people requiring food as well as fewer people available to sustain institutions. Half gone does not invent a universal famine or industrial-collapse formula. Existing owners decide how their own publicly defined measures respond to the new population.

The narrative can show offices attempting to locate staff, households seeking information, and remaining workers keeping essential services open. These are consequences and response priorities. They must not be packaged as proof that the absent people have died or that a specific scientific or supernatural explanation is correct.

## Assistance for the people who remain

The affected country gains access to the household-support response in Part 6. Its purpose is to help the survivors maintain ordinary life, gather unresolved inquiries, and regain confidence in functioning public institutions. It does not restore population.

A government with resources can organize the effort faster. An exhausted government retains a slower local-assistance route, so losing population together with Political Power does not produce an interface full of unusable obligations. Neither route demands that the country rebuild millions of people within a short deadline.

International assistance can support the remaining institutions through ordinary aid, medicine, goods, and expertise. Another country cannot claim to have found the missing half merely because it sends replacements or receives later migrants. The same distinction governs Abundance, scientific reconstruction, and extraordinary bargains.

## Repeated population disappearances

A later population result uses the current owned-state populations. Fifty million can become twenty-five million, and a later firing can reduce the smaller, changed population again. A territorial acquisition or ordinary demographic change between firings belongs in the new calculation.

Each incident retains its own measured missing total. Reports can acknowledge the repetition without combining several incidents into an invented single cause. Recovery programs do not reset, duplicate their rewards, or create immunity simply because the losses are now more terrible.

A major presentation milestone may accompany the first sufficiently large national disappearance. Later incidents remain mechanically real even when they receive a less intrusive news or country report rather than another super-event.
