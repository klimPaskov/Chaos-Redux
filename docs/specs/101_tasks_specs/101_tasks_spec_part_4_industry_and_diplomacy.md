# Event 101: Industry and diplomacy

## Industrial tasks commission useful capacity

An industrial task names a practical development goal in a state the country already owns and controls. It can expand production, improve a transport bottleneck or strengthen an existing port or air region. The selected location needs a reason to matter and the country needs the capacity, technology, resources, slots and time to develop it.

These are net-outcome tasks. Their public rule accepts additional usable capacity from ordinary construction, a completed national programme, paid aid or a lawful external grant after assignment. An external event can therefore finish one of these tasks. The mission rewards the national result and does not claim to prove who clicked the construction queue. Achievements that specifically require personal construction use stricter, separately verified contribution receipts.

This policy is deliberate. It allows the task to follow real campaign development while avoiding a false claim of native construction provenance. It must be stated in the objective tooltip through concise permitted-method direction. The generator rejects a task when known queued work or incoming grants would make its entire requirement trivial immediately. It does not invent knowledge of a queue or foreign script that its adapter cannot inspect.

## Exact capacity accounting

For each target state and building type, record the physical baseline `L0`. Prefer states whose relevant baseline buildings are undamaged. Count new usable work as `max(0, U_now - L0)`, where `U_now` is the current number of undamaged usable levels of that exact building type. Also verify current physical levels and target identity. This prevents repairs of baseline damage from earning new-construction credit.

If the selected state has baseline damage, include restoring that damage in the effort and deadline before calculating new usable capacity. Alternatively select a repair-specific industrial task with a different declared success predicate. Never apply the new-capacity formula and then hide the required baseline repairs from the player.

State ownership must remain with the task country. A temporary hostile occupation can suspend useful capacity and reduce progress. Voluntarily transferring the state does not create a new baseline. Retaking it does not add its existing buildings again. Permanent external legal transfer is evaluated as cancellation or an already-earned result. Losing the state through the task country's military failure is not a free cancellation.

Buildings that are dismantled and rebuilt merely restore the frozen baseline. New levels destroyed before completion are no longer usable progress. A building must exist and be usable at the deadline or at its earlier successful commissioning check. Its later fate belongs to the campaign after the task has resolved.

## Industrial inventory

### I1: Expand productive capacity

**Opportunity.** A suitable owned state can use additional civilian factories, military factories or dockyards. The chosen type matches the country's economy and existing strategy. A dockyard assignment requires usable coast and a supported naval purpose.

**Assignment.** Select one building type and one coherent region. Calculate the target from the actual cost and sustainable usable construction allocation. Preserve necessary domestic production and essential repairs in the feasibility estimate. A small country's single new factory may represent more effort than several factories for a major.

**Completion.** Reach the committed net additional usable capacity in the selected state set. All mandatory anchors remain under the recipient's lawful ownership and usable control. A factory gained in another region does not count simply because the national total increased.

**Partial success.** At least 60 percent of required usable levels, rounded up to a whole level, mitigates failure. A one-factory task is binary. The player keeps the buildings in every outcome.

**Payoff.** The I1 row in Part 6 applies. The reward never grants another free factory, and failure applies a bounded programme setback and, where quoted, a limited confidence loss.

### I2: Improve a regional bottleneck

**Opportunity.** A named region has insufficient infrastructure, damaged supporting infrastructure or an existing transport bottleneck that a practical programme can improve.

**Assignment.** Choose either additional infrastructure or restoration of an explicitly damaged baseline. State which one is required. A restoration task records usable baseline, target restored levels and the exact damage being addressed. An expansion task uses the new-capacity rule above. Where several states are involved, identify mandatory links before optional supporting work.

**Completion.** Achieve the declared usable infrastructure levels and keep the required states accessible. When the task promises a connection, it also checks that the complete relevant connection works. A high infrastructure level in a disconnected state cannot satisfy a corridor objective.

**Partial success.** A restored or improved independent useful section can mitigate failure if listed at assignment. Random completed levels outside that section do not count.

**Payoff.** The I2 row in Part 6 applies. Failure does not undo repaired infrastructure.

### I3: Complete a supply improvement

**Opportunity.** An existing front or preparation area can benefit from a supply node, rail improvement or a verified connection to a usable port. The country can build and operate it with its present technology and resources.

**Assignment.** Freeze the exact node or connection, required building or rail levels, the supported front and the access rule. The task must specify both construction and practical usability. It is ineligible if the adapter can observe a building but cannot establish the promised connection.

**Completion.** The selected supply improvement is commissioned, connected and usable for 10 to 20 days. Any required trains, motorized supply or reserve condition is a non-consumed readiness requirement unless an existing owner action actually spends it. The tooltip must not describe retained stock as a payment.

**Partial success.** Only a complete independently useful route section can mitigate failure. A finished hub without any functioning connection is not enough.

**Payoff.** The I3 row in Part 6 applies. The reward never fakes a supply connection or spawns unaccounted equipment. If a crisis adapter uses a real equipment contribution, its owner measures delivery and owns the physical grant.

### I4: Strengthen a useful base

**Opportunity.** A coastal area or air region has an existing operational purpose and an attainable basing improvement. Select either a port programme or an airfield programme, never both merely to lengthen the task.

**Assignment.** Record the exact state, base type, physical baseline, required usable increase and any supported branch or connection requirement. The amount reflects the navy or air force the country can actually field.

**Completion.** Commission the new usable capacity and satisfy the declared operating or access condition. Relocating an air wing can meet a separately declared deployment requirement, but it cannot create construction credit. A new port that remains unreachable does not meet a task promising an operational base.

**Partial success.** Whole usable levels may mitigate failure when the programme is multi-level and the required anchor is complete. A nonfunctional site remains failure.

**Payoff.** The I4 row in Part 6 applies to the selected branch. Do not grant unrelated land bonuses to a naval project.

## Diplomatic tasks create a practical relationship

Diplomacy should involve a real partner and a useful purpose. Existing common enemies, vulnerable borders, trade needs, military access and crisis mediation create suitable opportunities. The task cannot invent a friendly relationship between sworn enemies without a plausible supported route.

A foreign player retains control over their country. The task does not force an alliance, auto-accept military access, move foreign troops, transfer foreign land or issue a message on that player's behalf. A task that requires bilateral consent must either have a valid actionable invitation or expose a comparable unilateral route toward the same practical goal from assignment.

Task-specific bonuses cannot be used to override native diplomatic restrictions. Diplomacy remains in the ordinary diplomatic interface or the existing crisis owner. Event 101 observes the outcome and adds its quoted national reward.

## Diplomatic inventory

### P1: Establish sustained working relations

**Opportunity.** The country has a relevant neighbor, supplier, potential defender or crisis partner whose relations can improve through available lawful actions. The relation is not already at the required level, the recipient can afford the effort and no known hard rule prevents the route.

**Assignment.** Freeze the partner and starting opinion. Require both a meaningful improvement and a useful final relation band, followed by a 15 to 30 day maintenance period. Starting tuning is an improvement of 25 to 50 opinion points and a positive working-relation threshold chosen from the actual baseline. The task's estimator uses the current supported relation-growth rate and modifier duration.

**Completion.** Verify an actual post-assignment recipient relation-building action, the required opinion result and the full maintenance interval. An unrelated global opinion bonus alone cannot prove the action-based version. If only a pure relation-outcome predicate is supported, it must be explicitly declared as that different subtype and audited before use.

**Partial success.** Reaching at least 60 percent of the required improvement while keeping a positive usable relationship can mitigate failure. It cannot award full success or an alliance achievement.

**Failure and cancellation.** The recipient ending its own improvement action or attacking the partner is ordinary failure. The partner's death, permanent diplomatic lock or external replacement without continuity can produce neutral closure. Routine political disagreement is a known diplomatic risk and does not automatically cancel the task.

### P2: Supply useful support

**Opportunity.** A real friendly or neutral partner needs equipment, transport or other aid that the recipient can legally provide without compromising its own essential needs. The transfer route and acceptance rules are available.

**Assignment.** Select one useful aid package. Set the delivered amount from the recipient's real surplus and the partner's supported need. Quote at most two task-relevant resources when an owner action is used. Ordinary native transfer costs remain native-owned. The task cannot debit them again.

**Completion.** Credit actual delivered support after assignment, not an offer, shipment queue or promised monthly amount. The delivery receipt binds the actor, recipient, equipment family or resource, amount, date and contract generation. The same shipment cannot complete several task receipts or be credited again after a return transfer.

**Partial success.** At least 60 percent of the intended useful delivered aid can mitigate failure, provided it remains with the intended owner or has been validly consumed in its intended use. Cancelled and returned aid is not delivered support.

**Failure and cancellation.** Voluntary cancellation, empty convoys and a self-imposed policy reversal are ordinary risks. A partner's unexpected disappearance or a route permanently closed by an unrelated settlement can close neutrally after already-delivered full success is checked.

**Payoff.** The P2 row in Part 6 applies, and the opinion change comes from the owning diplomatic system. It must not return all donated equipment plus a bonus, which would turn aid into a free-stockpile loop.

### P3: Protect a vulnerable partner

**Opportunity.** A guarantee or another supported protective commitment is legal, strategically relevant and affordable. The partner is exposed to an actual existing threat. The recipient can reasonably support the commitment.

**Assignment.** Require a new lawful protective action and maintenance for a 30 to 60 day window. If the threat activates during that window, the task uses the country's actual obligation and supported response rule. An impossible guarantee or a guarantee that cannot meaningfully involve the recipient is ineligible.

**Completion.** Keep the commitment for the full window and fulfill any task-declared legal response triggered during it. The task never demands joining a war when native rules do not allow that action. The declaration of the commitment alone is not enough for immediate success.

**Failure and cancellation.** Voluntary cancellation or refusal of an available required response fails. A peaceful external settlement that removes the threat can close neutrally unless the recipient's verified diplomatic action produced the settlement and the initial task accepts that outcome. Guaranteeing and cancelling the same country repeatedly cannot farm success.

**Payoff.** The P3 row in Part 6 applies. The task does not create a faction or automatically alter the protected country's foreign policy.

### P4: Secure a cooperative arrangement

**Opportunity.** A real alliance, military-access or crisis-settlement opportunity already exists. The task needs either a currently actionable invitation or an explicit supported alternative that the recipient can complete without foreign acceptance.

**Assignment.** Define the practical objective first. A common-defense objective can be met by joining the actual alliance or by delivering a predeclared meaningful support package while sustaining a lawful protective commitment. An access objective can be met by accepting a valid access agreement or by completing a named owner-backed alternative route already permitted by the campaign. Every alternative is disclosed at assignment, has comparable opportunity cost and leads to the same practical purpose.

**Completion.** The selected agreement becomes effective and remains useful for 15 to 30 days, or the complete alternative package succeeds under its own exact receipts. Mere relations, an invitation sent, an AI willingness score or a foreign player's chat promise does not count.

**Partial success.** A completed unilateral preparation step can mitigate failure only if it has independently useful value and was listed in the initial task. A foreign refusal does not automatically become full success.

**Failure and cancellation.** If an unexpected foreign action removes every declared route, close neutrally. If the recipient refuses an available route and lets the deadline expire, apply ordinary failure. When only another player's uncommitted acceptance could finish the task, this candidate must never be assigned.

**Payoff.** The P4 row in Part 6 applies. Do not automatically puppet, annex or change the ideology of the partner. The native agreement or crisis settlement already owns those consequences if they are part of its legitimate system.

## Costs and proof stay with the action owner

An ordinary industrial task requires the declared useful capacity outcome. Construction effort, a lawful national programme or an accepted external grant can supply it under the stated predicate. A diplomatic task can consume native political power, equipment or other resources through its real action. Event 101 does not add a universal entry fee. This keeps the challenge connected to the actual campaign activity.

If a task-owned administrative action or owner adapter presents a custom payment, it must use one current quote, inclusive affordability and exactly one debit. At most four distinct spendable resource types may be charged by a single action. Command Power is never above 60. Inline costs use the actual texticons and at most three values. Full tooltips disclose all charges, timing and retained requirements. An intended payment is not proof of an actual debit, and an offered transfer is not proof of delivery.

All bilateral routes need multiplayer tests with acceptance, refusal, partner control changes, subject restrictions, war declarations, policy changes, duplicated delivery callbacks and save reload. All industrial routes need tests for baseline damage, repairs, queued work, free legal grants, state transfers, demolition, enemy occupation and reconstruction. These tests establish the implemented predicate, not a broader authorship claim.
