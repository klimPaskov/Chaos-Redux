# Event 101: Tasks inside existing crises

## The crisis remains with its owner

A crisis task adds a clear commitment to an event that is already happening. It must not create a shortage, rebellion, partition, hostile force or diplomatic dispute merely to supply an objective. The country's actual role determines what it can be asked to do.

The owning crisis defines its actors, episode, phase, geography, legal actions, physical consequences and resolution. Event 101 subscribes to that episode and asks for a specific achievable outcome. The task can observe progress and reward the recipient's result. It does not duplicate the crisis's population transfers, military losses, money, state control, rewards or Chaos history.

Each owner exposes a versioned task adapter with eligibility, context, objective, deadline and outcome evidence. The adapter is enabled only after the relevant owner is actually implemented and tested. A catalog entry, a matching event number or a flag with a similar name is not an adapter.

## Shared crisis contract

At assignment, the task records the owner system, episode identity, generation, recipient role, required target set, live phase, intended response and any owner hard deadline. It also records what counts as the recipient's contribution. A global crisis ending elsewhere is not automatically personal task success.

The owner supplies one of four results:

| Owner result | Task treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Fresh qualifying success attributable to the recipient | Full task success if the required work and timestamp fit |
| Fresh useful incomplete work | Partial success only if the task declared that exact useful threshold |
| Recipient failure or voluntary abandonment | Apply the quoted task result once, separately from the owner's already-applied consequences |
| External closure, target invalidity or episode replacement | Check earned success first, otherwise close neutrally |

A received result includes an immutable outcome identifier and its actual completion time. Replaying a callback, reloading a save or observing a flag that remains set from an older episode cannot pay another reward. The owner retains its result record until the task acknowledges it or its documented retention policy safely accounts for the subscription.

The task's deadline must fit the response route. If the owner has only ten days left and the smallest useful action needs thirty days, that candidate is ineligible. Higher Chaos never overrides this rule.

## Two families of crisis cards

L1 and L2 attach to the implemented Famine and Migration systems, so the crisis family has real content from the first playable build.
Each still needs the versioned read-only task projection described in the shared contract, in the same style as the existing owner-to-owner projections between those two systems.
C1 to C6 name owners that do not yet expose a usable lifecycle.
They stay unavailable, with zero weight, until their owner supplies a verified adapter.
Their reward and setback rows in Part 6 are fitted to the owner contract when it exists.

## L1: Relieve a famine state

**Live premise.** The recipient owns or controls a state that the Famine system has registered at acute shortage or worse, and the state is not concealed.
A state at supply strain is too mild for a committed task.
The task is offered only while the recipient has at least one legal relief route from the Famine category.

**Possible task.** Lower the state's committed Food Security stage by two steps and hold that result for 14 days, so acute shortage falls to stable, famine to supply strain and catastrophic famine to acute shortage.
The player uses the Famine category's own actions: Release Food Stores, Import Food, Restore Relief, Escort or Airlift Relief, Requisition Food or organized evacuation.
Event 101 adds no relief action of its own.

**Scaling.** The deadline follows the owner's published stage-step holds, because a stage falls one step at a time after its recovery window.
Using those holds, the generator rejects a candidate whose shortest recovery path does not fit the owner's relief timing plus ordinary slack.
The required fall never exceeds what the recipient's current relief access, reserves and legal routes can produce.

**Completion.** The owner's committed stage reaches the target before the deadline and holds for the interval, and the owner records relief delivery, a restored route or reserve release in the window as the attributable cause.
A fall produced by concealment, by emptying the state through evacuation or by the owner's own scenario ending does not count.
Concealing hunger ends the task as an ordinary failure, because concealment is the opposite of relief.

**Partial and failure.** One stage of recovery held for the full interval, declared at assignment, mitigates failure.
Failure adds only the quoted relief-cost setback.
It never touches reserves, mortality or stage, which the Famine owner alone changes.

**Reward.** The relief-cost reward in Part 6 and at most one owner-approved secondary benefit.
Deaths, population and condemnation stay with Famine, and the task adds none of them.

**Integration requirement.** The Famine owner exposes a read-only projection of the state's committed stage, concealment flag, relief receipts and recovery holds.
Event 101 never reads raw famine variables.

## L2: Settle displaced people

**Live premise.** The recipient hosts a Migration cohort with a real integration or resettlement backlog, and the host has reception capacity that the Migration system accepts as usable.

**Possible task.** Integrate or resettle a committed number of hosted people through the Migration category's own actions.
Forced return and deportation reduce the backlog but never count, and a task cannot be offered to a host whose policy is violent enforcement.

**Scaling.** Use the host's Reception Capacity, the hosted load and the owner's integration pace, with a work window from the diplomacy and support band in Part 2.
The required number never exceeds what the owner's published integration pace can deliver inside the deadline.

**Completion.** The owner's integration and resettlement receipts after assignment reach the committed number.
A receipt carries the host, cohort generation and time, so a cohort cannot be counted twice and returning people to their origin cannot be recycled as new work.

**Partial and failure.** Whole-cohort integrations that reach 60 percent of the committed number mitigate failure.
Failure adds only the quoted integration-speed setback and never changes cohorts, reception load or border policy.

**Reward.** The integration reward in Part 6.
Migration keeps its own accounting of people, deaths and reception load.

**Integration requirement.** Migration exposes a read-only receipt projection for integration and resettlement.
Famine-driven flights that arrive through the existing survivor-flight seam count as ordinary hosted cohorts.

## C1: Recover usable fuel

**Live premise.** A Fuel Crisis or a verified fuel-related episode has reduced the recipient's ability to operate its existing forces or supply routes.

**Possible task.** Restore a meaningful operating reserve through actual production, trade or owner-supported supply, then sustain useful operation for a short verified interval. Another valid form secures an existing fuel supply route that the crisis owner has already made available.

**Scaling.** Use the country's real operating need, intended force use, lawful access to fuel and attainable recovery capacity. Quote an absolute usable fuel amount or a documented number of operating days based on frozen demand. A storage-capacity percentage alone is insufficient. Building or dismantling silos cannot make the task appear complete without restoring usable fuel.

**Completion.** A fresh post-assignment recovery or delivery result reaches the required reserve or establishes the exact route. Retain the required useful condition for 5 to 15 days if the owner supports it. Shutting down every fuel consumer solely to meet a variable demand denominator cannot reduce the frozen requirement.

**Partial and failure.** A verified useful supply tranche or a restored independent route segment can mitigate failure. Moving a fuel slider, promising imports or briefly receiving fuel that is immediately returned does not qualify. Failure adds one limited task consequence, such as a bounded preparation penalty. It must not drain the country's fuel a second time.

**Reward.** A strong temporary fuel-efficiency or logistics benefit, or a measured owner-delivered recovery benefit that has not already been awarded. Choose the quote at assignment and list exactly which owner reward it supplements.

**Integration requirement.** The catalog associates Fuel crisis with Event 114. No actual Event 114 task owner is assumed. Register this adapter only against a verified implementation with exact reserve, delivery, route and episode proof.

## C2: Stabilize inflation

**Live premise.** An actual economic owner has started an inflation episode affecting the recipient. The task requires a real stabilization route the country can afford.

**Possible task.** Complete a stabilization programme and keep the owner's public economic condition within its declared recovery band for a sustained interval. The task must use the owner's definitions of inflation, policy costs and recovery. It cannot invent a second price index.

**Scaling.** Follow the severity, relevant industrial base, resources, already-imposed economic strain and remaining programme duration. Do not ask a country with no feasible policy route to purchase its way out of a task. The owner supplies the smallest useful response and its current quote.

**Completion.** A fresh stabilization receipt followed by the required stable interval. Simply holding a large amount of political power, receiving an unrelated stability bonus or removing an economic flag manually does not prove stabilization.

**Partial and failure.** A completed policy stage that materially improves the owner's condition can mitigate failure only when that stage was declared at assignment. The task failure penalty must not repeat a consumer-goods burden, stability loss or civil-war risk already applied by the inflation owner.

**Reward.** A substantial temporary construction or production recovery benefit, or a documented improvement to an owner recovery stage. The owner must approve and apply any change to its own severity value.

**Integration requirement.** Event 100 is named Inflation in the supplied catalog, but the repository evidence found a different incomplete event in its current namespace. It is not a usable inflation owner. Event 006 has a separate DM-10 inflation consequence. That specific episode can support an adapter only under its own semantics and must not be presented as the missing global Event 100 system.

## C3: Resolve an IMRO episode

**Live premise.** The IMRO owner has created an actual political or armed episode and identified the recipient's role. The task uses that role's existing response routes.

**Possible task.** Protect an exposed state, complete an owner-supported settlement, restore a disrupted route or defeat an actual registered hostile force. The country may be a defending government, a participant in a settlement or another explicitly supported actor.

**Scaling.** Use the exact involved territory, available local forces, public political route, supplied resources and time before the owner's next phase. The task cannot require control over unrelated Balkan territory or presume that every neighboring country is a participant.

**Completion.** Recognize the owner's fresh settlement, successful defense, restored route or hostile-force defeat receipt. Preserve the political distinction between an agreement and a military victory. The reward follows the achieved result.

**Partial and failure.** A verified limited settlement or defended independent area can mitigate failure if useful to the owner. A stale uprising flag, a dead actor without attributable resolution or a state transfer with the wrong episode identity does not count.

**Reward.** A related temporary political, defensive or recovery benefit. The task adds no separate uprising, population penalty or ethnic targeting objective.

**Integration requirement.** The supplied catalog associates IMRO with Event 115. Its actual owner callbacks, actor identities and response routes need implementation and inspection before this candidate becomes available.

## C4: Meet an occupation revolt objective

**Live premise.** Event 095 or another verified occupation-revolt owner has started a real revolt involving the recipient. It has established actual actors and contested states.

**Possible task.** For the lawful participant supported by the owner, retain a vital position, protect a retreat or supply connection, achieve an owner-approved settlement, or defeat a registered hostile force. The same generic order is not assigned to both opposing roles.

**Scaling.** Use the actual revolt size, local garrison condition, accessible forces and the states whose loss would matter. The task's target set remains the owner-created conflict, not every occupied state in the recipient's empire.

**Completion.** Require the exact owner epoch and a fresh result for the recipient's assigned role. A revolt that collapses on its own without the required contribution does not automatically complete an offensive task. A defense task can complete through its declared protection window.

**Partial and failure.** Useful protected territory or a completed settlement stage can mitigate failure where declared. Enemy reconquest is ordinary risk. An external peace or actor removal that ends every permitted route can close neutrally.

**Reward.** A limited occupation-administration, logistics or military preparation benefit appropriate to the result. Event 101 does not repeat the owner's state transfers, spawned forces, cores, casualties or instability.

**Integration requirement.** A repository Event 095 chain exists, but the inspected version is primarily state transfer and force creation. A persistent episode and result adapter are required before the task can depend on a formal revolt-resolution callback.

## C5: Stabilize the aftermath of a partition

**Live premise.** A partition or independence owner has actually committed a territorial settlement and registered the affected countries, states and obligations. An obsolete pre-wave planning flag is not a live partition crisis.

**Possible task.** Protect a newly exposed border, fulfill an agreed access or relief obligation, restore a severed practical connection, or complete an owner-supported post-settlement stabilization objective. Recovery of territory is permitted only when the live owner actually gives the recipient that lawful campaign objective.

**Scaling.** Use the recipient's remaining territory, usable industry, forces, capital access and role in the settlement. A newly reduced country cannot receive a programme scaled to the factories it just lost. The settlement's surviving guarantees, subjects and diplomatic permissions constrain possible tasks.

**Completion.** A fresh post-commit obligation or stabilization receipt, with exact participant and affected-state identity. Merely appearing in a partition event is not success. Changing a cosmetic tag does not erase a continuing country's task identity.

**Partial and failure.** A complete independent obligation can mitigate failure if the owner accepts it. A settlement changed by an external participant can invalidate the original objective. The task does not silently choose a new border or reopen the partition.

**Reward.** Strong temporary administrative, construction or diplomatic recovery support. The task cannot grant back lost states or cancel legitimate subject arrangements as a generic bonus.

**Integration requirement.** The inspected Event 006 material describes Great Partition as a selectable committed scenario and explicitly marks its former pre-wave crisis inert. A supported task adapter must attach to the actual post-commit state and its future real obligations. No pre-wave callback is assumed.

## C6: Resolve a warlord or subject-autonomy crisis

**Live premise.** The Warlords owner has created a real autonomy dispute, unstable subject relationship or actual armed fragmentation. The task follows what the owner implemented.

**Possible task.** Complete an autonomy settlement, preserve a supported federation or subject agreement, sustain a practical access arrangement, or defeat an actually hostile registered splinter force. The task cannot invent a war because the event's title sounds military.

**Scaling.** Use actual subject authority, relative strength, existing claims, legal diplomacy and the involved regions. Small subjects and overstretched overlords receive different objectives. No task requires a subject to use an action reserved to its overlord.

**Completion.** Verify an owner-recorded agreement, stabilized relationship or military outcome. A subject changing autonomy level for an unrelated reason does not prove that the task's dispute was resolved. A force that was never created cannot be a target.

**Partial and failure.** One independently stabilized participant or an agreed first-stage obligation can mitigate failure when the owner defines it. Unilateral concessions or military withdrawal have the consequences stated by the initial task and the owner, with no duplicate penalty.

**Reward.** A related temporary diplomatic, administrative or military preparation benefit. The owner keeps control over autonomy, subject status, faction membership and state ownership.

**Integration requirement.** The supplied catalog associates Warlords with Event 127 and describes subject governments demanding greater autonomy. A generic armed-warlord interpretation must not replace that premise. A future accepted owner may support armed forms, but the adapter must inspect and name them explicitly.

Event 014 Cannibalism contains an event-owned armed-warlord subtype. It can supply a separately admitted adapter under the C7 rule for a suitable defense, route or actual hostile-force objective after its public outcome contract exists. It does not satisfy the missing Event 127 autonomy integration, and the task does not import unrelated owner actions merely because they share a warlord theme.

## C7: Admitting further owners

Chaos Tasks can draw from further unusual crises after their owners register compatible adapters. Suitable examples are protecting an actual threatened safe area, securing a real recovery route, completing a documented containment stage or defeating an existing hostile actor through its intended weakness.

Each new adapter must supply the same episode, authority, feasibility, progress, timestamp and result contracts as L1, L2 and C1 to C6. It also needs its own task card, related reward and penalty, asset reuse decision, non-duplication proof and validation fixtures. Registration is an explicit content addition, not permission to sample every global flag.

The task cannot create supernatural enemies, trigger terminal outcomes or demand that the player cause a disaster. High Chaos expands the kinds of existing challenges the event can recognize. It does not remove the country's capacity or consent constraints.

Two further live systems are queued candidates and are not current requirements. A condemnation-compliance card would ask a condemned country to complete one of the condemnation owner's own compliance responses and keep decay running, and the owner would keep every decay and sanction effect. A fallout-recovery card would follow the air-cleanliness owner's recovery stages for a country with a fallout region. Each needs a read of its owner contract, a side-effect ledger and a Part 6 row before admission.

## Crisis pressure and the shared crisis limit

The owning crisis keeps its normal local-crisis occupancy. Event 101 is a task attached to the existing burden and must not count the same episode as an additional local crisis slot. At most one active Event 101 task exists for the country, regardless of how many owners are active.

Trials Under Pressure prefers a useful task within the country's current problems. If the country is already overloaded, the generator reduces work, selects a bounded local response or declares the event unavailable. It never multiplies every existing crisis penalty merely to make the task seem dangerous.

Every crisis adapter includes a side-effect ledger stating which system owns physical changes, native payments, political effects, rewards and Chaos entries. Event 101's task settlement is written once. The owner's outcome remains independent, so cancelling the task cannot undo an already-completed crisis resolution.
