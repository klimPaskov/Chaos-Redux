# Event 101: War and military preparation

## Military objectives follow a real operation

Military tasks select a concrete objective in the recipient's current strategic situation. The country must have a practical way to reach, defend or prepare it. The task cannot assume access through a neutral border, an unbuilt navy, an unresearched unit or a foreign player who has not agreed to help.

Territorial success distinguishes the recipient's action from a friendly country's independent success. Coalition help is allowed. Full success still needs the recipient's declared contribution and the required final control or access condition. An ally capturing an unrelated state, an enemy losing divisions on a different front or a peace settlement handing out land is not automatically a task completion.

The implementation must use verified control, war and contribution evidence. Where the engine cannot prove unit-level kills or exact province-control history, it must not substitute total enemy casualties or daily state snapshots while claiming the stricter objective. Such a measurement gap is recorded against the affected archetype.

## Offensive inventory

### O1: Secure a front objective

**Opportunity.** The country is already at war with a controller of a useful state. The state touches a verified reachable front or an already usable friendly approach. It has a strategic reason to matter, such as industry, resources, a port, a supply node or a route junction.

**Assignment.** Select one coherent target state, or a small connected group when the country can sustain the larger operation. Store the actual hostile controller, the relevant war relationship, the approach and the selected states. The goal is not satisfied at assignment. Estimate effort from usable attacking strength, defender pressure, access, terrain and logistics.

**Completion.** The recipient causes a qualifying hostile-to-friendly capture and either controls the objective itself or has the predeclared coalition-control right needed to use it. It then maintains that condition for a consolidation period of 5 to 15 days, scaled with target size and volatility. A temporary capture that is immediately lost does not pay.

**Incomplete work.** A multi-state task can mitigate failure if at least 60 percent of its weighted strategic objective is secured and consolidated at expiry. All mandatory anchor states still matter. Capturing peripheral states without the named port or junction cannot earn partial success. A one-state task is binary.

**Changed war.** The recipient's own favorable settlement can satisfy the task only if its exact contribution and the stated final access or control result are established. An unrelated allied settlement that removes the opportunity closes it neutrally. Voluntarily withdrawing from the war without achieving the goal is failure.

**Payoff.** The O1 row in Part 6 applies. The event never transfers the target as a reward.

### O2: Capture a key position

**Opportunity.** A verified province or victory point is important to the actual war and lies within an achievable operation. The surrounding state's average control is not enough to identify the objective.

**Assignment.** Store the exact province or position identity, its current controller, its state for map presentation and the reason it matters. Pick a position whose capture requires meaningful new work. Do not assign a point already occupied by the recipient or captured by a committed action known to be completing immediately.

**Completion.** Capture the named position through a qualifying recipient operation and keep it usable for a 5 to 15 day consolidation interval. Use exact province control where available. A state-control approximation cannot satisfy this archetype.

**Failure and cancellation.** A hostile recapture resets consolidation. The task fails at the deadline if it has not rebuilt the full interval. Destruction or removal of the objective by an external owner can close the task neutrally. The original province stays the target even if the enemy relocates its capital or the map label changes.

**Proof gate.** The implementation needs supported exact-position control and a attributable capture or contribution route. If only state control is available, O2 remains an explicit implementation gap. O1 is a separate task and must not be relabelled as O2.

### O3: Open a military corridor

**Opportunity.** A specific friendly front, pocket, port or supply connection is obstructed by hostile control. The recipient can reach the obstruction and the endpoint has a continuing military use.

**Assignment.** Freeze a minimal connected set of required states or province links, its friendly endpoints and the practical access rule. The selected corridor must come from verified current geometry. A straight line between state centers is insufficient.

**Completion.** Secure all mandatory links and keep the connection continuously usable for 10 to 20 days. Recipient control, agreed allied control and military access count only as declared in the initial route. A route through a neutral state without access fails.

**Incomplete work.** Partial success is allowed only when the task lists an independently useful intermediate endpoint at assignment. It requires that endpoint and its complete usable connection. Capturing a percentage of disconnected route states does not count.

**Failure and cancellation.** Hostile disruption restarts the connection hold. A voluntary access cancellation or withdrawal is the player's risk. An external settlement that removes the pocket or makes the route permanently irrelevant can produce neutral closure after earned success has been checked.

**Payoff.** The O3 row in Part 6 applies. It does not create an invisible supply route.

### O4: Defeat an active hostile force

**Opportunity.** A named hostile country or a registered crisis force is fighting the recipient in the current war. Its defeat is attainable and strategically useful. A continent-wide enemy alliance is not treated as a small country's local hostile force.

**Assignment.** Define defeat as a specific owner-confirmed result, such as the named hostile country capitulating after the recipient's operation or an event-created force losing its active command and required stronghold. Store the war or crisis epoch and the minimum recipient contribution.

**Completion.** A fresh exact defeat receipt and the contribution requirement must both be satisfied before the deadline. Contribution can be a captured named stronghold or a verified share of this exact operation. Generic total war participation, all-war casualties or declining enemy division count are not substitutes for force defeat.

**Failure and cancellation.** The enemy changing its name or government does not create a new target if the same war actor continues. An unrelated country defeating it without the recipient's qualifying contribution closes the task neutrally. The recipient defeating it through the intended operation gives full success even if the target no longer exists when the report appears.

**Proof gate.** A casualty-count version may be added only when direct actor, enemy and combat-instance attribution is verified. The selected core archetype uses a named defeat outcome. It does not promise a generic kill counter.

## Defensive inventory

### D1: Hold a threatened state

**Opportunity.** A real hostile front, active invasion, raid owner or crisis threatens an important state the recipient currently controls. A distant war with no route to the state is insufficient.

**Assignment.** Identify the state and its strategic purpose, then require a continuous protection window normally between 30 and 90 days. The default task is strict protection: no hostile control loss during the window. If the task is instead a recover-and-hold assignment, that distinction must be stated at assignment and the timer must allow a new complete hold interval.

**Completion.** The exact state survives the entire window under the permitted friendly control condition. The player may redeploy, fortify, fight or seek legitimate coalition help. No extra action is required merely to prove that a defense was intentional.

**Failure.** Strict protection fails immediately on a verified hostile loss. The report explains the actual lost objective. A same-day loss and recapture still breaches a never-lose task. Daily snapshots alone cannot prove this rule. A recoverable version resets its hold run and continues toward the original deadline.

**External end.** If the threat ends through the recipient's qualifying military or crisis resolution, success is possible under the early-resolution rule committed at assignment. If a third party ends the threat before the required protection was demonstrated, close neutrally. There is no free defense reward for a war that ended elsewhere.

### D2: Preserve the seat of government

**Opportunity.** The recipient's current capital or principal surviving administrative center is under a credible threat, while a defense remains possible. A fully isolated capital about to fall with no usable force is not a fair assignment.

**Assignment.** Freeze the original capital state and, where supported, its exact capital position. The duration follows reinforcement access, current surrender pressure and the expected local danger. Changing the capital afterward does not move the task.

**Completion.** Retain the protected location for the full window and keep the country able to act. A predeclared owner-backed evacuation variant may instead require a real evacuation and functioning replacement administration. That is a separate crisis-backed task with its own proof, not an automatic escape clause.

**Failure.** Hostile capture of the protected position or the recipient's capitulation fails the ordinary defense task once. It does not apply a second penalty when a later occupation callback arrives. Ordinary failure uses the D2 setback in Part 6, a political coordination effect rather than a direct combat-strength collapse.

**Achievement opportunity.** Part 7 includes a demanding capital-defense achievement with exact threat, duration, natural-fire and player-control requirements. Ordinary success does not automatically meet those stricter conditions.

### D3: Keep a strategic route working

**Opportunity.** A verified rail, port or land-access route supports an active front or threatened population through an existing owner system. The recipient controls the relevant links or has a valid agreement to use them.

**Assignment.** Name the route anchors, required links and the minimum operating condition. Scale the objective with the actual affected front and repair capacity. The task cannot require a global supply simulation that the adapter cannot observe.

**Completion.** Maintain the route for the declared window. If repairs are part of the assignment, restore the damaged links before the specified repair cutoff and then keep them operational. Control alone is insufficient when the task explicitly promises an operating rail or port.

**Incomplete work.** A meaningful independent branch or delivery tranche can mitigate failure only if it was identified at assignment and remains usable at expiry. A disconnected chain has no partial value.

**Failure.** Enemy disruption follows the declared recoverable or strict rule. The recipient's voluntary demolition, policy closure or refusal to maintain access does not cancel the task. An external crisis owner withdrawing the route's purpose can close it neutrally.

## Military preparation inventory

### M1: Field an equipped formation

**Opportunity.** The country has a real manpower and equipment base, a supported formation type and time to train it. The task responds to an actual defense or preparation need.

**Assignment.** Select an existing eligible template class or a definition of qualified strength supported by the engine. Scale new fielded manpower and equipment requirements from the country's usable military size and spare reserves. State the required equipment coverage, training standard and retention interval.

**Completion.** Additional recipient-owned trained strength above the committed baseline is deployed, equipped and retained for 10 to 20 days. The requirement counts qualified manpower and equipment coverage, not just the number of division icons. Existing formations split into smaller templates, expeditionary loans, empty deployments and repeated disbanding cannot generate new credit.

**Incomplete work.** At least 60 percent of the new qualified strength, with the same quality standard and retention period, can mitigate failure. A full number of under-equipped formations does not qualify.

**Proof gate.** Per-unit training and equipment checks need actual supported inspection. Aggregate manpower, battalions and stockpiles cannot prove all qualities of individual formations. If the intended quality cannot be measured, the implementation must report that gap rather than claim full M1 completion.

### M2: Produce relevant military materiel

**Opportunity.** The country can produce a useful equipment family with its existing technology, resources and industry. Choose an actual shortage or intended preparation need.

**Assignment.** Record the exact cumulative production counter and its semantic contents, the starting value, target increment and any usable-reserve or deployed-equipment condition. A broad infantry production counter must be described as the actual covered materiel family. It must not be labelled as rifles if it also counts other equipment.

**Completion.** The production counter increases by the required amount after assignment and the declared useful availability condition is met. The task rewards actual production. Capturing, importing, receiving lend-lease, moving equipment into storage or deleting an army cannot increase the credited production delta.

**Incomplete work.** At least 60 percent of the required real output, still satisfying the declared useful destination rule, mitigates failure. The player does not need to retain all produced equipment in a warehouse when the task's purpose is equipping the army. Equipment actually deployed in the intended branch can count where the adapter supports it.

**Failure.** Production-line cancellation or diversion remains the player's choice. An externally imposed technology removal or a source counter becoming invalid requires exact repair or neutral cancellation. Routine resource shortages were part of the feasibility estimate and do not create automatic immunity.

### M3: Establish regional defenses

**Opportunity.** A threatened border, port or industrial area has room for meaningful defense construction and the country can support it. Avoid fortifying random safe inland states.

**Assignment.** Select the exact state group and defense type, such as land forts, coastal forts or anti-air. Record baseline physical and usable levels. Required new capacity follows threat exposure, construction capacity and the value of the protected region.

**Completion.** Commission the specified usable defensive capacity above the original physical baseline and retain control of the protected region. A repair-only objective is named separately. Repairing pre-existing damage cannot be presented as constructing additional fort levels.

**Incomplete work.** A complete useful defended anchor with at least 60 percent of the assigned work can mitigate failure. Scattered cheap levels outside the required positions do not count.

**Payoff.** The M3 row in Part 6 applies. It does not add another row of free forts, and failure never destroys the defenses the player built.

### M4: Make an existing air or naval branch ready

**Opportunity.** The country already has the relevant branch, equipment or ships, usable basing, fuel and a strategic role. A landlocked country without a navy cannot receive a naval assignment. A country with no aircraft technology cannot be ordered to produce a modern air force.

**Assignment.** Choose one branch. Air work can combine actual relevant production with deployment to a usable base and a required readiness interval. Naval work can require newly completed or restored suitable hulls, valid basing and an operating reserve. Quantities follow the existing branch and achievable production schedule.

**Completion.** Meet the branch's declared quality, equipment, basing and reserve conditions for 10 to 20 days. Moving the same air wings between bases or splitting fleets does not create new force credit. A hull under construction is not ready merely because its production line exists.

**Incomplete work.** A complete useful subgroup can mitigate failure only when it meets the same readiness standard. Half a ship or an unusable air wing is not partial success.

**Proof and payoff.** The implementation must verify each selected readiness predicate. The M4 row in Part 6 applies to the selected branch. If a high-value ship cannot be completed within a credible task window, choose a smaller existing-branch objective rather than lengthening a minor task into a multi-year naval programme.

## Shared military rules

The country can use normal allies and legitimate aid. The task must state when coalition control, foreign access, expeditionaries or leased equipment count. A rule that demands recipient-owned strength cannot silently accept a loan. A rule that permits a cooperative outcome cannot later penalize the player for using allies.

Targets carry an exact operation or war context. The task must not redirect itself to a new enemy after the original one disappears. Required state or position sets stay small and strategically coherent. Scenario validation covers weak minors, major powers, islands, subjects, multiple wars, coalition captures, same-day recaptures, peace conferences, civil-war actor replacement and control loss.

At full success, the event grants one related reward package. At failure, it applies one bounded related consequence. The underlying war, production and construction systems keep their own results. Event 101 never repeats their territory, equipment-loss or Chaos effects.
