# Event 101: Tasks

## A task drawn from the campaign

A country receives an urgent assignment because something in its campaign deserves attention. A threatened capital can bring a defensive order. An exposed front can bring an offensive objective. An army preparing for war can be asked to field an equipped formation. A peaceful country can be asked to complete useful construction or support a vulnerable partner. When a Chaos Redux system is already under strain, the assignment can ask the player to achieve one of its real objectives, such as lifting a starving state out of famine or settling the people a host country has taken in.

The event gives one clear goal, a visible deadline, a worthwhile reward and a consequence for failure. The player pursues the goal through the military, construction, production, diplomatic and crisis systems already in use. The task is a short commitment inside the campaign. It does not become a separate economy or a sequence of administrative purchases.

All names used for task archetypes, grades, records and internal identifiers in this package are working design labels. They are not final player-facing localisation. Text direction appears in Part 7 and the handoff prompts.

## Who receives it

In singleplayer, the player's country is the only possible recipient. In multiplayer, the event selects one eligible player-controlled country. A country with several co-op players receives one entry in the pool. A spectator is not a recipient. Subjects controlled by a human can qualify, provided the selected task fits their actual diplomatic and economic authority.

An AI-only country is never selected. A human-controlled country's allies, enemies and subjects can be targets or participants, but they do not receive their own Event 101 task through that relationship. Task-specific actions cannot be taken by an AI on the player's behalf after control is lost.

A recipient needs a government that can act, an unresolved task opportunity and enough relevant capacity to attempt it. Ordinary exile without usable territory is unsuitable for state construction or territorial defense. A human exile can qualify for a supported diplomatic or host-approved aid objective only when its adapter establishes that authority and a practical completion route. A nearly defeated country is unsuitable for ordinary expansion demands. It may qualify for a bounded survival or relief objective that its existing position can support.

The task candidate is prepared before the shared event system commits the firing and its actor. If no eligible human country has a valid objective, Event 101 is unavailable for that draw. It must not produce an empty report, award a consolation resource, pick an AI or create a crisis to make itself fireable.

## Assignment and public terms

Assignment begins the task immediately. The report's acknowledgement is informational. Leaving the report open cannot postpone the start date, accept a different target or evade the deadline.

The assignment states the following facts in the appropriate native mission and tooltip surfaces:

- The country, state, region, partner or active crisis to which the task applies.
- The work required, including any required contribution, equipment quality or consecutive holding period.
- The deadline and the conditions that constitute an early failure.
- The full success reward and the ordinary failure consequence.
- Whether useful incomplete work can reduce the failure consequence.
- Any alternative method that was part of the same task from the beginning.

The task does not move its objective after the player starts pursuing it. Its baseline, scale, reward and penalty are fixed at assignment. If the player expands industry during an industrial task, the required amount does not rise. If a new enemy joins a war, the target does not automatically expand to that enemy's entire faction. If an evolution activates, the current task retains its agreed terms.

The player always sees the exact current deadline. Calendar dates and the native days remaining must agree. A task does not use separate visible clocks for the event, mission and crisis. Where an owning crisis has a hard end date, the task's deadline is constrained by that date from the outset.

## What the player does

Most tasks create no extra task button. The player fights, builds, produces, deploys, guarantees, negotiates or performs an existing crisis action. The mission evaluates that real result and completes automatically. There is no additional payment or claim click after the objective is fulfilled.

The category shows one primary progress value and the native time remaining. A count can be used instead of a percentage when it is clearer, such as commissioned capacity or qualified formations. A task with several named states can list those targets in its tooltip and highlight them on the map. It does not turn each state into another independent mission.

The task's family and exact objective matter more than a generic difficulty number. The player should understand that a defensive task requires holding a location under an existing threat, while an industrial task requires producing additional usable capacity. Both can use one mission, but their proof, timing and failure conditions are different.

## The six families

| Family | Campaign opportunity | Player effort | Outcome being tested |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Offensive | An existing war with a reachable enemy objective | Commit forces, organize supply and secure the target | An attributable military gain that survives consolidation |
| Defensive | A real threat to a useful position | Defend, reinforce, repair or reopen the position | The promised position remains usable or survives its protection window |
| Military preparation | An existing capability gap that the country can fill | Train, equip, produce or construct defenses | Additional usable readiness rather than a nominal unit count |
| Industrial | A useful project with capacity, access and room to grow | Commit construction or complete a real national programme | Additional usable capacity in the named location |
| Diplomatic | A practical relationship or support opportunity | Spend political attention or supply real support | A sustained relationship or an attributable cooperative result |
| Crisis | A live Chaos Redux system or episode involving the country, such as famine, migration or a crisis owner's own episode | Use the owning system's existing response routes | A fresh qualifying outcome from that exact episode or state |

The detailed archetypes in Parts 3 to 5 form the content inventory. They are alternative objectives, not simultaneous layers. A country receives one task from this inventory. A family with many possible targets does not gain extra lottery tickets merely because the country is large.

Every card differs in what the player does, how success is judged and what the reward and setback touch. Part 6 names the signature reward and setback of each card, so two tasks of the same family still feel different and a reward always acts on the system the task was about.

## Success, incomplete work and failure

Full success requires the declared objective to be satisfied before the deadline. The final state must still satisfy any required control, equipment, delivery or relationship conditions. A temporary achievement that was immediately reversed is not automatically banked unless the task explicitly measures a completed action rather than a maintained condition.

Useful incomplete work can reduce the penalty for the quantity-based tasks that declare partial success. Partial success never counts as full success for achievements or history streaks. It does not pay the full reward. Binary tasks such as preventing a hostile capture do not receive an invented percentage result after the forbidden event occurs.

Ordinary failure should be felt. Its cost follows the task's subject, such as a limited military preparation setback, lost confidence in an industrial programme or a damaged diplomatic relationship. A single ordinary task cannot directly start a war or civil war, seize territory, delete a formation, force a government change or empty the country's essential stockpiles. Part 6 defines the actual consequence limits and the stronger evolved grades.

## Changes that the player did not control

The campaign can remove an objective. A target country may cease to exist. A peace settlement may make an offensive objective irrelevant. A crisis owner may end an episode through a valid outcome that did not involve the player. The task distinguishes these events from the player's own failure.

When the objective ceases to exist for an external reason, the task checks any completed work and attributable success first. If success was earned in time, it is paid. Otherwise the task can close neutrally. Neutral closure gives no reward, penalty or ordinary completion cooldown. Its receipt remains in recent history so repeated invalidation cannot immediately draw the same offer again.

An enemy counterattack, the player's voluntary withdrawal, dismantling required capacity, cancelling a guarantee, refusing an available task route or discarding needed equipment is part of the challenge. Those actions do not cause a free cancellation. The task remains active or fails according to its declared rule.

Player-control loss makes active-contract completion and settlement effects unavailable immediately. The country does not continue earning task rewards under AI control. Previously settled rewards and penalties retain their original expiries. Because a disconnected player and a deliberate handover are not reliably distinguished by the evidence available to this specification, both use neutral closure. A country-bound reentry quarantine through at least the original deadline and a consumed-offer record prevent immediate reconnect rerolls. The system does not pretend it can enforce account-level intent or prevent a human from reloading an earlier save.

## Returning later

Event 101 uses the shared repeatable-event scheduler and weight recovery. It does not run a second global event timer. Each new firing prepares a new task from the current campaign and the country's recent task history.

Recent history discourages the same archetype, exact target and crisis episode when suitable alternatives exist. It does not guarantee all six families in a fixed order. A country without a navy will not receive a naval task to complete a rotation. A country fighting a prolonged defensive war can receive several different defensive or logistical assignments when those are the sensible opportunities.

Ordinary resolution gives the country a dynamically scaled rest period before another Event 101 task. Longer commitments create longer rest periods. The common event weight still determines whether another firing happens after that eligibility returns.

## How the event develops

The three supplied evolutions change future assignments. Higher Stakes at 200+ increases the work and payoff within the recipient's capacity. Trials Under Pressure at 400+ increases the chance that a task addresses a problem the country is already facing. Chaos Tasks at 600+ draws heavily from active Chaos Redux episodes with valid owner adapters.

The supplied final label is Evolution IV. Evolution III is absent from the brief. This package keeps the three supplied names, labels and thresholds. It does not add a fourth evolution to fill the numbering gap. Part 6 defines pre-fire activation, active-task protection, shared logging and toggle behavior.

## Design acceptance

A successful implementation produces a task the player can explain without reading this document: what must be done, where, by when, what counts and what happens afterward. It also produces enough internal evidence to distinguish an earned result from a stale flag or an unrelated world change.

The package defines the full intended design. The separate implementation contract and validation report identify where engine getters, native callback behavior, crisis owner APIs and rendered presentation still need to be verified before the event can be called playable.
