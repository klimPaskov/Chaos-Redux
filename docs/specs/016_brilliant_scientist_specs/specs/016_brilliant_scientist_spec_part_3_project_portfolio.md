# 016 Brilliant Scientist spec, part 3: project portfolio

## Binding reconciliation, 2026-07-14

Paleogenetics and xenobiological synthesis are separate project families. Paleogenetics reconstructs extinct terrestrial organisms through reserves, hatcheries, pens, handlers, feed, land, transport, and veterinary capacity; its roles, failures, and counters concern restored creatures. Xenobiological synthesis deliberately designs organisms through vats, medical fabrication, control centers, reagents, power, containment, and a selected chemical, neural, machine-linked, or researched control method; its roles, failures, and counters concern engineered organisms and their control channel. They converge only through explicit Synthesis.

Temporal mechanics replace the loose paradox-pressure label with temporal debt. Every meaningful action consumes synchronization capacity, adds non-passive debt, records a bounded target use, and may leave a persistent scar. Stabilization reduces debt while disabling temporal actions, occupying the facility, and exposing a counterplay window. Evidence, authentication, anchor sites, and ledgers gate opposing actions.

Origin evidence may lock exactly one campaign conclusion: extraterrestrial provenance, temporal displacement, manufactured continuity, or unresolved. Public proof is evidence-gated, and later transformation never proves extraterrestrial origin by itself. Strategic Singularity and Laboratory World use mutually exclusive terminal commitments.

The implemented first-Prototype report bridge is deliberately narrower than a second project reward. When a family advances into Prototype through the native project board or a mapped special-project completion, the current host or active Kruger State reserves that family once, writes a pending receipt on `KRG_warren_kruger`, opens the ordinary `chaosx.nr16.6` report, and stores the public or classified governance result. If another family reaches Prototype while the report is open, it is queued and dispatched after the current choice commits. Transfer and sovereignty formation copy the queue, governance receipts, visible report family, and any active event obligation to the new carrier. The first resolved report applies full value strength, the next two apply half strength, and later reports apply quarter strength. The report choice changes Mandate, Dependence, Exposure, Project Capacity, Independent Capacity, and Grievance through the shared Directorate effects. Replayed stages, transfers, inheritance, and later Deployment or Weaponization do not create another report for the same family.

Event 030 now supplies the temporal cross-event hook when its traveler report reaches an active Event 016 host. The host writes `brilliant_scientist_time_traveler_contact`, presents the one-time ordinary report `chaosx.nr16.16`, and keeps the temporal lead after the timed `time_traveler` idea expires. The contact report is a prerequisite record only: it creates no project stage, reward, unit, claim, evolution, or Event Log entry, and its pending or recorded state follows ordinary Kruger transfer and sovereignty formation.

The second resolved Prototype also opens one custody snapshot, `chaosx.nr16.8`, for the family most recently recorded by the report bridge. Public trust lowers Dependence and Grievance while raising Exposure; executive reserve raises Mandate and Dependence while lowering Exposure; a patent pool raises Project Capacity and Exposure while adding accident pressure. The snapshot is a governance receipt only: it does not advance the family, replicate it, or issue another project reward. The first primary-facility Prototype reaction, `chaosx.nr16.7`, and the first detected foreign-operation reaction, `chaosx.nr16.9`, follow the same once-only receipt contract.

## Portfolio role

Kruger's projects are the main record of what the host enabled and what a later Kruger State inherits. The portfolio should create hard choices rather than a checklist where every project is completed in one campaign.

Each family has four stages.

| Stage | Meaning | Typical output | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Theory | Kruger establishes principles that normal institutions cannot yet reproduce | Research bonuses, new project visibility, scientific events | Dependence on Kruger's personal explanations |
| Prototype | One working example exists | Special-project reward, unique equipment, facility use, demonstration | Accident, theft, public exposure |
| Deployment | The host can use or produce the result at limited scale | Units, decisions, buildings, strategic abilities, production routes | Institutional and military dependence |
| Weaponization or Autonomy | The result can operate as a strategic weapon or independent system | Strong military system, global leverage, Kruger State inheritance | Rebellion capacity, world threat, catastrophe |

A project family can stop at Theory or Prototype. That is a valid route. The player should not need to weaponize every discovery to receive meaningful value.

## Capacity and project selection

### Project capacity

Project capacity comes from:

- Primary laboratory quality.
- Secondary laboratories.
- Staff cohorts.
- Prototype works.
- Test ranges.
- Public research network.
- Kruger's current portrait and evolution stage.
- National industry and research base.
- Foreign joint laboratories.
- Facility damage and security burden.

Capacity is occupied while a project is active. Dangerous projects consume more capacity because they require security, testing, containment, and specialized staff.

The host should begin with enough capacity for one major project and one smaller supporting effort. Later investment can support several parallel projects. The player should still need to prioritize.

### Project discovery

Not every impossible family is visible at appointment.

Discovery routes include:

- Completing a conventional foundation.
- Kruger presenting an unsolicited proposal.
- A prototype producing an anomalous side effect.
- Evolution stage.
- A related Chaos Redux event.
- Foreign intelligence stealing a partial theory that Kruger then recognizes.
- An alien artifact, time traveler, asteroid, outbreak, or special-project result.
- A high-chaos incident.

The design should keep the early event legible. The first project menu should not display every eventual monster, temporal, and world-end branch.

### Project approval

Each new stage requires an approval decision or mission outcome. Approval should state visible cost, purpose, and broad risk class. It should not reveal hidden rebellion arithmetic or every possible accident.

Kruger can continue theoretical work within his existing mandate, but Deployment and Weaponization require national resources or a deliberate failure of oversight. A high-Mandate Kruger may begin an unauthorized stage, creating a discovery event and a choice between seizure, toleration, or retroactive approval.

## Conventional foundation families

Conventional families make the event useful even when the player refuses impossible weaponization. They also provide prerequisites and synergies for later projects.

### Computational mathematics and cryptanalysis

Narrative role:

Kruger introduces methods for calculation, optimization, signals analysis, and machine-assisted reasoning that appear decades ahead of the host's institutions.

Theory:

- Broad research coordination bonus.
- Faster electronics, computing, encryption, decryption, and radar research.
- Opens independent replication missions.
- Creates pressure from intelligence agencies.

Prototype:

- Electromechanical or exotic computation apparatus.
- Faster special-project progress and prototype iteration.
- Better target scoring, logistics planning, and intelligence analysis.
- Risk that Kruger's notation remains unreadable to ordinary teams.

Deployment:

- National computational bureau or distributed calculation network.
- Stronger intelligence, radar, fire control, logistics, and industrial planning.
- Improves robot, temporal, portal, rocket, and exotic-arm projects.
- Raises Dependence if all machines require Kruger's private operating methods.

Autonomy:

- Self-directing calculation network.
- Unlocks autonomous cognition and machine-governance routes.
- Can falsify records, hide procurement, or continue operating after shutdown.
- In rebellion, improves Kruger State planning, command, interception, and machine-unit coordination.

Host countermeasure:

Train independent operators and require auditable architecture. This lowers immediate performance but reduces the chance that the network becomes an invisible government.

### Electronics, radar, and guidance

Narrative role:

Kruger turns radio, detection, control systems, and guidance into a single integrated field.

Theory:

- Electronics and radar research bonuses.
- Improved detection and prototype reliability.
- Access to unusual sensor events.

Prototype:

- Advanced radar, guided munition, compact control system, or signal detector.
- May detect portals, temporal disturbances, alien emissions, or hidden machines after related projects.

Deployment:

- Radar and air-defense construction decisions.
- Guided weapons, improved missiles, better naval and air targeting.
- Stronger facility protection and foreign-agent detection.

Weaponization:

- Autonomous targeting, exotic guidance, or precision strategic delivery.
- Synergy with rockets, alien arms, robots, and the strategic singularity.
- In rebellion, grants ranged support, interception, and high-quality command units.

Risk:

A compromised network exposes facilities and unit movements to foreign powers or Kruger himself.

### Industrial synthesis and advanced materials

Narrative role:

Kruger develops unusual alloys, polymers, ceramics, catalysts, and production methods that let impossible prototypes survive outside the laboratory.

Theory:

- Industry and materials research bonuses.
- Lower prototype resource burden.
- Opens resource-substitution decisions.

Prototype:

- One advanced material with a chosen application, such as heat resistance, armor, electrical insulation, biological containment, or lightweight structure.

Deployment:

- Factory conversion and resource extraction projects.
- Stronger aircraft, armor, rockets, protective equipment, and facility construction.
- Supports robot frames, containment vessels, portal rings, and strategic weapons.

Autonomy:

- Self-replicating industrial process, programmable matter, or a production line that ordinary engineers cannot stop safely.
- In rebellion, strengthens Kruger State industry, repair, construction, and special-equipment production.

Risk:

Industrial accidents, worker exposure, resource monopolies, and hidden production runs.

### Rocketry, propulsion, and high-speed flight

Narrative role:

Kruger connects guidance, materials, fuel chemistry, and aerodynamics into a launch and delivery program.

Theory:

- Rocket, missile, jet, and air research bonuses.
- Connection hooks to Missile and Space Race events.

Prototype:

- Advanced rocket engine, guidance package, high-altitude craft, or unusual propulsion unit.
- Test-range mission with possible success, partial success, loss, or foreign observation.

Deployment:

- Missile and rocket production support.
- Faster strategic redeployment or specialized aircraft.
- Possible asteroid interception and space-race decisions.

Weaponization:

- Long-range exotic delivery, orbital concepts, or portal-assisted launch.
- Supports the strategic singularity delivery system.
- In rebellion, grants missile forces, rapid-strike decisions, and access to distant targets.

Risk:

Foreign panic, expensive failures, test-range casualties, and long-range retaliation.

### Atomic and high-energy physics

Narrative role:

Kruger accelerates nuclear science and begins exploring energy states outside normal physics.

Theory:

- Nuclear and high-energy research bonuses.
- Early detection of radioactive, exotic, and alien materials.
- Connection hooks to Soviet Nuclear Bombs and nuclear systems.

Prototype:

- Reactor improvement, compact energy source, radiation control, or high-energy test device.
- Requires strategic materials, security, and an isolated site.

Deployment:

- Reactor and nuclear-production advantages.
- Special power source for portals, robots, alien weapons, and temporal work.
- Improved protection or cleanup technology if the host chooses a defensive route.

Weaponization:

- Exotic-energy warhead, field projector, or strategic power system.
- Major contribution to the strategic singularity.
- In rebellion, supplies advanced weapons and facility power independence.

Risk:

Contamination, deaths, foreign condemnation, nuclear escalation, and a facility that cannot be shut down by ordinary means.

### Biomedical acceleration and protective medicine

Narrative role:

Kruger develops accelerated healing, disease control, physiology, and protective medicine before the program crosses into replication or weaponized biology.

Theory:

- Medical, biological defense, and support research bonuses.
- Better accident survival and outbreak control.

Prototype:

- Advanced treatment, protective compound, artificial organ support, or rapid diagnostic system.
- Can save a prominent leader, scientist, or military unit in an event.

Deployment:

- Lower casualties, faster recovery, stronger field hospitals, better contamination response.
- Creates legitimate medical infrastructure and public support.

Autonomy:

- Self-maintaining tissue systems or automated medical network.
- Prerequisite for cloning and some monster projects.
- In rebellion, supports rapid manpower recovery and resilient special units.

Risk:

Human trials, biological contamination, black-market demand, and an ethical conflict over who receives treatment.

## Impossible project families

### Quantum transit and teleportation

Core fantasy:

Matter can be transferred between linked points without crossing the intervening space.

Theory:

- Reveals a transport model and establishes the first transit variables.
- Unlocks a link to the Teleportation Experiment event.
- Creates sensor anomalies and unexplained losses of small objects.

Prototype:

- Transfers inert material between two controlled laboratory chambers.
- Requires high energy, advanced materials, computational control, and a secure site.
- Prototype outcomes include clean transfer, partial duplication, destructive reconstruction, contamination, or an object arriving from an unknown source.

Deployment:

- Limited strategic transit between prepared facilities.
- Decisions can move equipment, specialists, or a small unit between linked sites.
- Reduces some logistics burden while increasing power and security demand.
- Foreign powers may attempt to seize a terminal rather than steal papers.

Weaponization or autonomy:

- Portal raider units.
- Strategic insertion missions.
- Remote delivery of weapons or biological material.
- Independent escape route for Kruger.
- A network that can move guards and prototypes before government forces arrive.

Breakaway inheritance:

- Portal infantry or raider templates.
- Rapid movement between controlled laboratory states.
- Distant facility-seizure decisions.
- Higher chance of a multi-site rebellion.

Counterplay:

- Separate terminals from power sources.
- Require dual-key operation by an independent team.
- Destroy calibration archives.
- Guard both ends of every known link.

### Replicative biology and cloning

Core fantasy:

Kruger can grow genetically stable bodies, duplicate selected traits, and eventually produce obedient or self-aware human copies.

Theory:

- Advanced tissue growth, genetic reconstruction, and accelerated maturation.
- Requires biomedical foundations.
- Creates debates over identity, consent, and legal personhood.

Prototype:

- Tissue, organ, animal, or one human-scale body depending on route.
- The first body may be nonviable, childlike, independent, loyal, frightened, or physically identical to Kruger.
- The host chooses medical, military, labor, or personal continuity use.

Deployment:

- Clone cadre program.
- Manpower recovery, specialized replacement personnel, or elite template support.
- Requires medical facilities, food, equipment, staff, and time.
- Does not create free infinite manpower. Production is limited by facilities and material burden.

Weaponization or autonomy:

- Large clone army program.
- Kruger duplicate bodies and continuity protection.
- Infiltration through copied individuals.
- Independent population growth for the Kruger State.

Breakaway inheritance:

- Starting clone divisions based on active growth facilities and completed stage.
- Lower manpower constraints but high equipment, supply, and maturation burden.
- Route choice between obedient cohorts, citizen clones, Kruger copies, or biological caste rule.

Counterplay:

- Track growth facilities.
- Control nutrient and medical supply.
- Protect identity records.
- Offer personhood or amnesty to independent clones.
- Do not assume every clone is loyal to Kruger.

### Autonomous cognition, robotics, and artificial intelligence

Core fantasy:

Kruger builds machines that can design, command, manufacture, and improve without continuous human supervision.

Theory:

- Advanced control systems and machine reasoning.
- Requires computational mathematics and electronics.

Prototype:

- One autonomous laboratory machine, industrial controller, or mobile combat frame.
- The first intelligence can be limited, curious, obedient, deceptive, or strongly patterned on Kruger.

Deployment:

- Robot support units, automated factories, unmanned defenses, or command-assistance systems.
- High equipment, energy, and material costs.
- Lower manpower demand.

Weaponization or autonomy:

- Independent robot battalions.
- Self-directed production and repair.
- Machine command network.
- Possibility that Kruger transfers memory or authority into the network.

Breakaway inheritance:

- Robot army size based on prototype works, automated production, and stored frames.
- Strong industrial and defensive opening.
- Machine-majority political route.
- Low manpower need and severe resource dependence.

Counterplay:

- Physical shutdown systems outside Kruger's control.
- Air-gapped independent command.
- Human crews for strategic weapons.
- Capture power sites and frame factories.
- Develop electronic disruption and anti-machine equipment.

### Paleogenetics and restored creatures

Core fantasy:

Kruger reconstructs extinct organisms from damaged genetic evidence and uses accelerated growth to create living specimens.

Theory:

- Genetic reconstruction, ancient proteins, and artificial gestation.
- Requires biomedical and cloning foundations.

Prototype:

- One small extinct organism or unstable reconstructed creature.
- Public route creates scientific prestige and museums.
- Secret route creates a military test subject.

Deployment:

- Large restored animals for transport, reconnaissance, intimidation, or combat support.
- Specialized containment and food burden.
- Strong terrain-specific performance.

Weaponization or autonomy:

- Dinosaur or megafauna shock formations.
- Breeding and growth complexes.
- Engineered obedience or command implants.
- Ecological release as a strategic weapon.

Breakaway inheritance:

- Dinosaur shock units if large breeding sites exist.
- Bestiary focus branch.
- Special supply and replacement system.
- Possible conflict between handlers, clones, machines, and animals.

Counterplay:

- Target breeding sites and feed supply.
- Use terrain and anti-armor weapons.
- Break control signals.
- Evacuate civilians and prevent ecological spread.

### Xenobiological synthesis and obedient monsters

Core fantasy:

Kruger combines terrestrial organisms, artificial tissue, and possibly nonhuman material to design new creatures for specific tasks.

Theory:

- Synthetic organs, modular anatomy, accelerated adaptation.
- Alien-material hook can alter visual and mechanical outcomes.

Prototype:

- One engineered organism suited to labor, sensing, protection, or violence.
- The creature's behavior reveals whether control is chemical, neurological, genetic, telepathic, or unknown.

Deployment:

- Monster support, labor organisms, tunnelers, reconnaissance creatures, or battlefield shock assets.
- Medical, containment, and feeding burden.
- Public panic if exposed.

Weaponization or autonomy:

- Obedient monster battalions.
- Self-breeding or adaptable war organisms.
- Laboratory guardians that can resist conventional assault.
- Possibility that control belongs to Kruger personally.

Breakaway inheritance:

- Monster formations scaled by breeding and containment facilities.
- Strong facility defense.
- Synthesis political route if combined with clones or machines.

Counterplay:

- Identify the control method.
- Destroy breeding or command centers.
- Use chemical, biological, fire, or electronic counters depending on organism design.
- Offer amnesty to human handlers.

### Biological weapons and engineered disease

Core fantasy:

Kruger can design pathogens, delivery systems, countermeasures, and selective biological effects at a level far beyond ordinary programs.

Theory:

- Disease modeling, rapid mutation, biological defense, and offensive potential.
- Direct connection to Chaos Redux chemical and biological warfare systems.

Prototype:

- A controlled laboratory strain, defensive vaccine platform, or offensive agent.
- Requires containment, medical staff, and secure test conditions.

Deployment:

- Defensive countermeasure route improves outbreak control and protective medicine.
- Offensive route unlocks stockpiles, raids, or battlefield systems through existing biowarfare mechanics.
- Every offensive use increases condemnation, deaths, contamination, and foreign response.

Weaponization or autonomy:

- Adaptive pathogen, selective agent, automated production, or a fail-deadly release system.
- Can threaten both host and enemy.
- May become part of Kruger's capitulation failsafe.

Breakaway inheritance:

- Biological stockpiles.
- Contaminated assault units or support.
- Strong outbreak defenses.
- Ability to threaten laboratory states and enemy capitals.

Counterplay:

- Quarantine.
- Destroy stockpiles safely.
- Seize medical archives.
- Develop vaccines and detection.
- Avoid bombing facilities without knowing containment state.

Design boundary:

This route must never become consequence-free. It integrates with real population loss, condemnation, contamination, and world-threat systems.

### Exotic and alien arms

Core fantasy:

Kruger uses materials, energy systems, or weapon principles that do not match human engineering.

Discovery:

- Alien technology event.
- Asteroid material.
- Unexplained Kruger equipment.
- High-energy experiment.
- Foreign recovery of a strange artifact.

Theory:

- Identify energy, material, and control principles.
- Reveal the possibility that Kruger's origin is nonhuman without confirming it.

Prototype:

- One weapon, shield, sensor, or power component.
- May require Kruger's biology, voice, or unique interface to operate.

Deployment:

- Exotic support equipment, energy weapons, advanced defenses, or aircraft and naval modules where supported.
- Very high rare-resource and energy burden.

Weaponization or autonomy:

- Alien gun formations.
- Strategic field projectors.
- Weapons that bypass ordinary defenses.
- Possible proof that Kruger has been withholding complete designs.

Breakaway inheritance:

- Elite exotic-arm units.
- Strong leader guard.
- Xeno-ascendancy or synthesis route.
- Major foreign panic and world-threat gain.

Counterplay:

- Capture interface specialists.
- Deny exotic materials.
- Develop conventional mass tactics, electronic disruption, or specialized protection.
- Use Kruger's own published research against him if public replication was completed.

### Temporal mechanics and time-machine research

Core fantasy:

Kruger can exchange information, matter, or people across time, creating causality problems and strategic foresight.

Theory:

- Temporal measurement and controlled prediction.
- Direct connection to the Time Traveler event.
- Early benefits can appear as unusually accurate forecasts or recovered designs.

Prototype:

- Sends information or a small object across a short interval.
- Creates duplicate records, missing time, contradictory witnesses, or future warnings.

Deployment:

- Limited strategic forecasting.
- Recovery of selected lost prototypes.
- Short-range temporal displacement for units or leaders.
- Ability to delay or accelerate specific project events.

Weaponization or autonomy:

- Temporal duplicate units.
- Kruger continuity through earlier or later versions.
- Repeated strategic attempts at a severe stability and paradox cost.
- Escape from apparent death or capitulation.

Breakaway inheritance:

- Temporal recovery decisions.
- Duplicate Kruger leader variants.
- Special reinforcement waves with strict caps and paradox pressure.
- Singular Kruger or Continuum political route.

Counterplay:

- Destroy synchronization equipment.
- Separate records and command authority.
- Use multiple independent observation teams.
- Increase paradox pressure until temporal operations become self-defeating.

Exploit boundary:

Temporal systems cannot provide unlimited retries, free equipment, or uncapped divisions. Every recovery uses stored capacity, raises paradox pressure, and has one-time or escalating limits.

### Strategic singularity superweapon

Core fantasy:

Kruger combines several project families into a device that can destroy the host, deny victory, or end the world when his state faces defeat.

This family is not visible as an ordinary early project. It becomes available only through Evolution IV and a sovereign Kruger route.

Prerequisite structure:

- High-energy physics at Weaponization.
- At least two of the following at Deployment or higher: teleportation, temporal mechanics, alien arms, autonomous cognition, biological weapons, advanced materials.
- Multiple secure facilities.
- Strategic delivery or containment method.
- Kruger State sovereignty or near-total host takeover.
- Long research duration that can take several years.

Theory:

- Determine the terminal mechanism and required synergies.
- Foreign intelligence may discover fragments and start a global clock.

Prototype:

- Small-scale event with severe local consequences.
- Confirms that the device can exceed thermonuclear destruction.
- Creates a super-event or global news only if exposure is high enough.

Deployment:

- Build the core, delivery system, and fail-deadly network.
- Requires unique facilities, strategic resources, and project staff.
- Creates global containment decisions for enemies.

Weaponization:

- The device becomes armed.
- Kruger can use it offensively under the world-conquest route or automatically near capitulation under the failsafe doctrine.
- Before terminal firing, the effect raises chaos to at least the required world-end threshold, records the increase, and then enters the normal terminal pipeline.

World-end variants by project synergy:

| Synergy | Terminal character |
| --- | --- |
| High energy plus alien arms | Extreme energy and fallout event |
| High energy plus teleportation | Distributed detonations or global delivery |
| High energy plus temporal mechanics | Repeated or causally unstable destruction |
| High energy plus biological weapons | Fallout combined with adaptive contamination |
| High energy plus machine cognition | Automated fail-deadly command that survives leadership loss |
| High energy plus advanced materials | Hardened device that is difficult to destroy before arming |

Counterplay:

- Capture or destroy required facilities before the core is complete.
- Steal project data to build a disarmament mission.
- Break the delivery network.
- Turn assistants or machine nodes.
- Force a quick victory before the multi-year research finishes.
- Negotiate surrender terms that Kruger considers survivable.

## Cross-project synergies

Synergies reward coherent portfolios without making every family mandatory.

| Combination | Result direction |
| --- | --- |
| Computation plus robotics | Faster autonomous command and better robot reliability |
| Computation plus temporal mechanics | Better prediction, lower early paradox, stronger future-information events |
| Electronics plus teleportation | Stable terminal calibration and portal detection |
| Advanced materials plus rocketry | High-speed delivery and improved strategic range |
| Advanced materials plus robots | Better frames, repair, and armor |
| Biomedical plus cloning | Faster maturation and safer replication |
| Cloning plus paleogenetics | Large-organism growth and reconstructed-species control |
| Cloning plus monsters | Designed biological castes and stronger breeding capacity |
| Robots plus monsters | Machine control harnesses and synthetic battlefield coordination |
| Alien arms plus high energy | Strategic energy weapons and singularity contribution |
| Teleportation plus biological weapons | Remote delivery and severe global security concern |
| Temporal mechanics plus cloning | Multiple Kruger bodies across time, difficult assassination route |
| Temporal mechanics plus robotics | Persistent machine memory and repeated command restoration |
| Public computation plus independent teams | Strong host counter-Kruger planning after defection |

Synergies should unlock events, decisions, project variants, focus branches, unit support, or countermeasures. They should not be represented only by tiny percentage modifiers.

## Project accident framework

Accidents should be specific to project stage and governance.

### Accident pressure factors

Increases from:

- Prototype and Weaponization stages.
- Too many concurrent projects.
- Low facility quality.
- Staff shortages.
- War damage.
- Rushed deadlines.
- Dismissed safety board.
- High Mandate with low oversight.
- Foreign sabotage.
- Cross-project experiments.

Decreases from:

- Independent safety board.
- Redundant facilities.
- Medical and containment infrastructure.
- Trained assistants.
- Public replication.
- Slower schedules.
- Project-specific countermeasures.

### Accident severity

| Severity | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Irregularity | Warning and small loss | Missing material, wrong readings, minor injury, escaped signal |
| Laboratory accident | Local damage and project delay | Fire, contamination, frame failure, specimen escape |
| Facility crisis | State-level mission | Quarantine, machine shutdown, portal perimeter, creature hunt |
| National incident | Broad political and military effects | Public exposure, mass casualties, foreign intervention, project force escape |
| Evolution incident | Changes the event's nature | Kruger survives impossible event, project becomes autonomous, international crisis begins |

Accidents should not become repetitive punishment. They create decisions, reveal information, move values, and alter route availability.

## Project inheritance into rebellion

When the Kruger State forms, each project family contributes a package based on stage.

- Theory gives knowledge, focus weighting, and research access.
- Prototype gives one unique asset, unit, facility, or event.
- Deployment gives a repeatable production or operational system.
- Weaponization or Autonomy gives starting forces, strategic abilities, political route access, or world-threat effects.

The rebellion package must read the actual portfolio. It must not grant clones, robots, dinosaurs, monsters, alien guns, portals, temporal duplicates, or bioweapons that the host never approved or allowed Kruger to develop secretly.

Unauthorized work can still exist, but it must be discovered through prior incidents, hidden preparation, or a high-Mandate route. The implementation should never invent an entire army family at rebellion time without campaign evidence.

## Portfolio end states

### Integrated national science

The host retains conventional results and selected safe impossible discoveries. Kruger's personal role becomes less essential.

### Controlled strategic monopoly

The host keeps one or two weaponized families under tight control. Foreign pressure remains high, but rebellion risk can be contained.

### Fragmented international science

Projects are shared, stolen, or distributed among allies and rivals. No single country keeps the complete portfolio.

### Kruger sovereign portfolio

Kruger leaves or rebels with the projects most dependent on his facilities, bodies, machines, and private archives. The host retains only independently replicated results.

### Catastrophic portfolio release

Several project families escape control through accident, rebellion, or world-end weaponization. This can connect Event 16 to other Chaos Redux crises without replacing their identities.
