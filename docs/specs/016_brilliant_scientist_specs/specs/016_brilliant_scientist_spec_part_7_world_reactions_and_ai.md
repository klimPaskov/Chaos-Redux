# 016 Brilliant Scientist spec, part 7: world reactions and AI

## Binding reconciliation, 2026-07-14

AI must evaluate paleogenetics and xenobiological synthesis as separate production and supply systems. It may choose host takeover only after the full institutional-capture contract is already true. Temporal AI uses bounded crisis targets, begins stabilization at route-specific debt bands, and attacks identified anchors only after evidence and authentication work. AI locks at most one origin conclusion and publicizes it only with sufficient evidence. Terminal doctrine changes require the full disarmament or consolidation transition and cannot oscillate from short-term score changes.

## World-reaction purpose

Kruger becomes internationally important only after his work produces evidence worth acting on. Foreign reactions should grow from the host's visible scientific lead, public fame, exposed facilities, project prototypes, and weapon use.

The first detected foreign operation resolved after a Prototype can open the ordinary host reaction `chaosx.nr16.9`. The host keeps the exact actor and operation in the transaction, then chooses a controlled exchange only when the actor is not at war and the operation was not an extraction or assassination attempt; otherwise a private warning or public accusation remains available. Controlled exchange grants the actor one capped family-theory result from the host's latest breakthrough and improves bilateral opinion, while warning lowers opinion quietly and accusation raises Mandate, Exposure, and the actor's foreign-interest pressure. The event writes one host and one Kruger-character receipt and cannot be replayed after the foreign-resolution transaction, transfer, terminal cleanup, or sovereignty formation.

The system should avoid two weak extremes.

- Every country should not receive constant generic suspicion events.
- Foreign powers should not ignore a country that suddenly completes impossible weapons and builds a private laboratory army.

Foreign play is organized around a dynamic interest score and a selected-target interface.

## Foreign interest score

Each relevant country can develop interest in Kruger based on:

- Research gap against the host.
- Rivalry, war, claims, or faction competition.
- Ideological affinity or hostility.
- Intelligence network strength.
- Distance and access to the host.
- Knowledge of Kruger's public role.
- Security Exposure.
- Known project stages.
- A related special project or Chaos Redux event.
- Need for a project countermeasure.
- Possession of an assistant, archive, artifact, or foreign laboratory connection.
- Fear created by actual weapon use.

Interest should decay when:

- Kruger leaves or dies without continuity.
- The host publishes the science and removes exclusivity.
- A project is dismantled.
- The foreign country has already obtained the desired knowledge.
- The country is too weak, occupied, or distant to act.

High interest unlocks actions. It does not guarantee hostile action.

## Foreign actor groups

### Scientific competitors

Profile:

Countries with strong research institutions, special-project facilities, or active technology competition.

Likely actions:

- Formal invitation.
- Joint laboratory.
- Assistant recruitment.
- Archive theft.
- Public challenge or demonstration request.
- Counter-Kruger research.

### Strategic rivals

Profile:

Enemies, regional competitors, hostile faction leaders, or countries threatened by the host's new military advantage.

Likely actions:

- Sabotage.
- Extraction.
- Assassination at high threat.
- Facility targeting during war.
- Support for internal opponents.
- Containment coalition.

### Allies and patrons

Profile:

Faction members, guarantors, ideological partners, or countries able to protect a weak host.

Likely actions:

- Protection pact.
- Joint security.
- Shared laboratory.
- Resource aid.
- Request for project access.
- Pressure for publication or military use.

### Opportunistic minor powers

Profile:

Countries with a severe research gap or a chance to gain prestige.

Likely actions:

- Offer asylum to assistants.
- Buy stolen papers.
- Host a secondary laboratory.
- Seek a limited project license.
- Shelter a defecting Kruger.

### Moral and humanitarian opponents

Profile:

Countries responding to human trials, bioweapons, mass deaths, clone slavery, ecological release, or uncontrolled experiments.

Likely actions:

- Public condemnation.
- Sanctions.
- Refuge for assistants and project subjects.
- International inspection demands.
- Countermeasure sharing.
- Containment or intervention when harm becomes severe.

### High-chaos collaborators

Profile:

Special regimes, nonhuman countries, cult states, or aggressive powers that see Kruger's work as an opportunity.

Likely actions:

- Exchange strange technology.
- Offer test populations or forbidden facilities.
- Join a Kruger submission network.
- Compete for world-ending projects.
- Betray Kruger after obtaining one project family.

## Player-facing foreign target management

The host's foreign-contact decisions should not display one row for every country.

Use a selected-target category or scripted GUI list.

- Show countries with meaningful interest or active contact.
- Let the player select one country.
- Display known relationship, offer, threat, and current action.
- Hide invalid or resolved targets.
- AI evaluates all valid targets without using the human selector.
- Clear selected targets when countries die, change sides, or lose relevance.

The same pattern can be reused by the Kruger State for scientist recruitment, facility seizure, technology bargains, and submission demands.

## Foreign action families

### Invite Doctor Kruger

Actor:

A foreign country with scientific prestige, diplomatic access, or a strong research gap.

Public version:

Offer a chair, institute, citizenship, major laboratory, or international appointment.

Secret version:

Offer private protection, unlimited resources, or immunity.

Host responses:

- Refuse.
- Permit a visit.
- Demand a joint appointment.
- Use the offer to obtain aid.
- Let Kruger choose.
- Expose the offer publicly.

Outcome factors:

- Kruger Grievance.
- Host Mandate and Dependence.
- Foreign facilities and project compatibility.
- Host security.
- Ideology and personal safety.
- Foreign willingness to grant autonomy.

### Recruit assistants

Actor:

Any interested country with diplomatic, academic, or intelligence access.

Methods:

- Open grants.
- Asylum.
- Industrial contracts.
- Ideological appeal.
- Blackmail.
- Extraction.

Results:

- Foreign country gains partial project progress or countermeasures.
- Host loses capacity or gains an infiltrated replacement.
- Assistant becomes a future event actor.
- Public science route can make legal exchange beneficial rather than hostile.

### Steal an archive

Targets:

- One project family.
- Facility plans.
- Prototype blueprints.
- Kruger's personal notes.
- Identity and biological records.

Success should grant a partial result, not the entire Event 16 package. The thief may receive:

- Research bonus.
- One Theory stage.
- Countermeasure.
- A special project.
- A dangerous incomplete prototype.

Incomplete theft can create its own accident or research failure.

### Sabotage a project

Targeting logic:

- Project threatening the actor.
- Most exposed facility.
- Strategic singularity site.
- Portal or temporal system that threatens ordinary defense.
- Clone, robot, or creature production site before a major war.

Outcomes:

- Delay.
- Stage regression only when the physical prototype is truly destroyed.
- Facility damage.
- Accident.
- Public exposure.
- Kruger injury.
- Assistant death.
- Foreign agent capture.

Sabotage should never erase an entire long project without giving the host a playable crisis and recovery route.

### Extract Kruger

This is a major operation.

Requirements:

- High interest.
- Real access route.
- Intelligence investment.
- Kruger willingness or a credible kidnapping capability.
- Valid recipient country.

Success variants:

- Kruger defects willingly.
- He is kidnapped.
- A clone or machine copy is taken.
- The operation captures an assistant instead.
- Kruger uses the operation to create a new independent enclave.

The host receives a response mission if the operation is detected early enough.

### Assassinate Kruger

This action is rare.

AI conditions:

- Project threat is strategic.
- Theft and diplomacy are unlikely.
- Actor is at war or in severe rivalry.
- The target's security can be penetrated.
- The actor accepts diplomatic and escalation risk.

Possible outcomes:

- Genuine death before continuity projects.
- Injury and portrait change.
- Failed attack and higher Grievance.
- Wrong target, such as a clone.
- Machine or temporal continuity activation.
- Immediate sovereignty crisis.
- Biological or singularity fail-deadly response.

### Offer joint security

Actor:

Ally, patron, or regional power.

Benefits:

- Better counterintelligence.
- Foreign units or agents support a security mission.
- Deterrence against raids.

Costs:

- Access to facilities.
- Foreign influence.
- Risk that allied security controls extraction routes.
- Diplomatic conflict if the host later hides unethical work.

### Create a joint laboratory

Requirements:

- Relations and access.
- Compatible project interest.
- Agreed facility location.
- Resource contribution.

Project structure:

- Shared Theory or Prototype progress.
- Clear ownership of staff and results.
- Exit conditions.
- War and faction-change cleanup.

Joint laboratories can reduce Dependence by creating another institution. They also create a second country with real knowledge of the project.

### Publicly challenge the program

Actor:

A scientific competitor, moral opponent, or foreign government with evidence.

Methods:

- Demand demonstration.
- Publish stolen evidence.
- Expose casualties.
- Question Kruger's identity.
- Demand inspection.

Host options:

- Demonstrate a safe result.
- Deny access.
- Publish selected work.
- Blame sabotage.
- Invite neutral observers.
- Threaten the accuser.

This action affects Exposure, prestige, legitimacy, and foreign interest.

### Build a counter-Kruger program

A country threatened by Kruger can create a defensive program without employing him.

Countermeasure families:

- Anti-machine electronic warfare.
- Clone identity and growth-facility detection.
- Creature containment and anti-bestiary weapons.
- Portal detection and terminal disruption.
- Temporal record authentication.
- Biological defense and vaccination.
- Exotic-material protection.
- Singularity disarmament and facility targeting.

Countermeasures should require intelligence or captured evidence. They should not appear globally the moment Event 16 fires.

## World-news thresholds

### Initial appointment news

The existing news event can remain, but it should be conditional.

- Public appointment creates broader news.
- Secret appointment creates only local or intelligence-aware reactions at first.
- A player who rejected Kruger can later receive news when another country appoints him.

### First impossible breakthrough

News fires only if:

- The result is public.
- An accident exposes it.
- Foreign intelligence publishes evidence.
- The host demonstrates it for prestige or deterrence.

### International scientific contest

A news or super-event threshold occurs when several countries act directly around Kruger.

### Kruger State formation

Always creates a major international announcement. Super-event treatment is justified when the new state has real territory, project forces, or strategic projects.

### Global threat

A separate super-event marks Kruger becoming an existential power, not merely surviving secession.

## Country-specific flavor variation

Final implementation should build flavor from host context without writing a separate full chain for every tag.

### Strong university country

- Appointment through a university or academy.
- Rival institutions compete for the national chair.
- Public lectures and publication carry more weight.
- Independent replication is easier.

### Industrial power

- Private laboratory or factory demonstration.
- Firms compete for patents and production rights.
- Prototype works develop quickly.
- Corporate leaks and labor incidents become more important.

### Militarized state

- Appointment through a weapons office, general staff, or secret facility.
- Security and direct military use dominate.
- Conventional and weapons projects accelerate.
- Mandate and dependence grow quickly.

### Small threatened state

- Kruger appears as a survival opportunity.
- Foreign protection and aid matter.
- One facility can dominate national politics.
- Peaceful charter can threaten state viability and needs careful territory rules.

### Colonial or overseas empire

- Remote test sites and colonial institutes become available.
- Foreign populations and subject governments may resist secret work.
- Overseas facilities create supply and independence risks.

### Exile or refugee network

- Kruger appears through displaced scholars.
- The host can build a plural scientific community.
- Foreign persecution and asylum influence assistant loyalty.

### Ideology variation

Democratic route direction:

- Oversight, publication, public legitimacy, and rights conflicts.

Communist route direction:

- State planning, collective institutions, equality of access, party oversight, and conflict over personal authority.

Fascist route direction:

- Military weaponization, racial or coercive misuse, personal loyalty, and severe consequences.

Non-aligned or monarchist route direction:

- Court patronage, national prestige, military household, private concession, or royal academy.

Ideology should shape institutions and AI behavior. It should not lock all countries into one predictable result.

Possible links:

Possible links:

- The traveler recognizes Kruger or denies knowing him.

This should become the direct event-level hook for quantum transit.

### Triggerable scenario SCN-002: Army of Clones

Clone research can alter the scenario setup.

- If Event 16 clone systems are active, the scenario can inherit Kruger technology or oppose it.
- A triggerable Kruger scenario is not required by this spec.
- Do not quietly turn the Army of Clones scenario into Event 16. Preserve its own identity.

### Chemical and biological warfare systems

- Biological projects use existing stockpiles, raids, contamination, countermeasures, deaths, and condemnation.
- Exotic or monster projects can create protective or delivery synergies only through documented mechanics.
- Kruger State must be classified correctly for systems that should exclude nonhuman actors.

### World-threat framework

Kruger becomes a source only when actual conditions make the country existential.

Suggested source condition:

- Kruger State exists or host takeover completed.
- Several weaponized projects, or one singularity project plus major military capability.
- Active aggressive expansion, global submission demands, or multi-country war.

Do not set `world_in_threat` merely because a brilliant scientist was appointed.

### Duplicate catalog idea: Crazy Scientist

The unnumbered `Crazy Scientist` idea overlaps almost every mature Event 16 theme. It should be:

- Folded into the Kruger project portfolio and focus tree.
- Marked superseded in catalog planning.
- Retained only if redesigned around a clearly different actor and fantasy.

The default disposition is absorption into Event 16.

## AI host strategy

AI route selection should evaluate:

- War state.
- Stability and war support.
- Technology gap.
- Industry and resources.
- Ideology.
- Intelligence threat.
- Foreign protection.
- Current project portfolio.
- Mandate, Dependence, and Exposure.
- Former promises to Kruger.
- Ability to survive a breakaway.
- Evolution settings.

### AI project rules

- Complete a smaller number of coherent project families.
- Do not start a high-cost project without the needed facility or resource path.
- Favor defensive or economic projects when weak.
- Favor military projects when at war.
- Treat cloning and robots as manpower alternatives based on actual shortages.
- Use bestiary routes only when the relevant project exists.
- Avoid biological weaponization unless ideology, desperation, or high chaos supports it.
- Avoid singularity research unless Evolution IV and world-end content are enabled.

### AI confrontation rules

AI should estimate whether it can control the laboratory.

- Low control and low hostility favor negotiation or charter.
- High control and moral opposition favor early confinement or dismissal.
- High dependence and war pressure favor delay.
- High grievance and high capacity make a sudden assassination or seizure irrational unless defeat is otherwise certain.
- AI should place forces around actual laboratory states before military seizure.

## AI foreign strategy

### Invitation AI

Favored when:

- Country has strong facilities.
- Host is an ally or politically compatible.
- Kruger is dissatisfied.
- Country has a major research gap.

### Theft AI

Favored when:

- Security Exposure is high.
- Intelligence network exists.
- A specific project is strategically useful.
- Diplomatic invitation is unlikely.

### Sabotage AI

Favored when:

- Project threatens the actor.
- War is likely or active.
- Target facility is accessible.
- Actor has counterintelligence protection against retaliation.

### Assassination AI

Rare and heavily gated. It should not be the default foreign response.

### Joint laboratory AI

Favored by allies, democracies, science-focused powers, and weak hosts seeking protection.

### Containment AI

Begins only after dangerous public evidence, weapon use, aggressive Kruger State action, or singularity discovery.

## Kruger State AI strategy

### Former-host priority

Violent formation:

- Defend laboratory states.
- Secure supply.
- Retake lost archives.
- Defeat or force a settlement with the former host.

Peaceful formation:

- Preserve truce.
- Use research compact.
- Seek recognition and resources.
- Break the compact only when route and grievance justify it.

### Portfolio-aware military use

- Clone AI maintains equipment and growth capacity.
- Robot AI protects power and factories.
- Bestiary AI protects feed and breeding sites.
- Portal AI defends terminals and uses raids against strategic facilities.
- Temporal AI spends paradox capacity conservatively.
- Biological AI considers condemnation and outbreak risk unless extremist.
- Exotic-arms AI preserves elite units rather than wasting them in attrition.

### Conquest AI

- Target facilities, resources, ports, rail hubs, and former project archives.
- Avoid declaring on every major before the state can sustain global war.
- Use submission offers before direct war when diplomacy route supports it.
- Escalate toward world conquest after Evolution IV, sufficient strength, and actual route completion.

### Singularity AI

- Begin research only with prerequisites and a viable multi-year plan.
- Protect core and delivery sites.
- Use deterrence while strong if route permits.
- Use failsafe when armed and near capitulation under the selected doctrine.
- Do not fire from a routine border setback.

## AI validity and cleanup

- Dead, capitulated, allied, or inaccessible foreign targets are removed.
- A country cannot invite a Kruger who no longer exists in a transferable form.
- Clone, machine, or temporal copies do not create duplicate full appointment chains.
- Disabled evolutions set AI weights for gated actions to zero.
- AI does not select project branches absent from its portfolio.
- AI does not attempt a country formation without valid territory.
- Foreign actions stop or transform after world-end.
