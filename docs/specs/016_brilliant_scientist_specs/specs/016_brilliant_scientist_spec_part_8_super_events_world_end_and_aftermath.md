# 016 Brilliant Scientist spec, part 8: super-events, world end, and aftermath

## Super-event philosophy

Event 16 can produce several campaign-scale moments. Each super-event must mark a real threshold, use a unique final track, and receive complete image, text, quote, audio, trigger, documentation, and settings-aware playback wiring.

This specification gives role and direction only. Final titles, button remarks, quotes, cultural references, and audio choices require separate research.

## Super-event package overview

| Working role label | Trigger meaning | Required status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| International recognition | Kruger becomes a globally recognized scientific figure and several countries act around him | Conditional, not automatic at Evolution II |
| Kruger State formation | A sovereign Kruger country appears with real territory and project capacity | Required for meaningful formation |
| Global Kruger threat | Kruger State becomes an existential expansion power | Required when world-threat conditions are met |
| Laboratory world | Kruger defeats major opposition and establishes a terminal global order | World-end branch |
| Strategic singularity | Armed final device fires and triggers Fallout world end | World-end branch |
| Defeat aftermath | A long, global Kruger crisis ends and reshapes international science | Conditional aftermath |

A single campaign will not necessarily show every package.

## International-recognition super-event

### Role

Public reveal and escalation.

### Trigger threshold

Require more than Evolution II alone. Suggested combinations:

- Kruger completes a globally important public Prototype.
- At least three countries have taken direct foreign actions.
- A failed assassination exposes the program.
- A joint laboratory or international demonstration changes the research balance.
- The host gains a visible strategic lead that foreign powers acknowledge.

### Player feeling

A local appointment has become an international fact. The world now treats one scientist as a strategic asset.

### Description direction

- Explain the scale of foreign attention through invitations, delegations, security measures, scientific migration, or competition.
- Keep Kruger's origin unresolved.
- Mention the host dynamically.
- Avoid generic statements that history has changed forever.

### Image direction

Generated period press scene or staged documentary scene.

Possible subject:

- Kruger surrounded by scientists, guards, instruments, and international observers.
- The human subject remains readable at 457x328.
- The portrait should match the current evolution stage.
- No generated readable text.

### Quote direction

Research a real quote about knowledge, state power, scientific responsibility, or competition. Prefer a public-domain scientific, philosophical, or political source.

### Button remark direction

Short, restrained reaction about the world beginning to compete for one mind. Research required.

### Audio direction

Period-compatible music with intellectual tension and growing public importance. It should not sound like the end of the world.

## Kruger State formation super-event

### Role

Irreversible political transformation.

### Trigger threshold

- Kruger State forms through charter, rebellion, enclave, or host takeover.
- The package has a viable country and at least one meaningful project or military asset.

A trivial one-state evacuation with no lasting country can use a normal news event instead. A playable sovereign package deserves the super-event.

### Variant description direction

Peaceful charter:

- A government has surrendered part of its sovereignty to preserve scientific cooperation.
- Border and legal uncertainty remain.
- Foreign powers decide whether to recognize the state.

Violent rebellion:

- Laboratory sites and project forces have broken from the host.
- The former host faces a war created by its own research policy.
- The image and description should reflect the inherited dominant project.

Host takeover:

- The country's institutions now answer to Kruger.
- The transformation is national rather than territorial.

### Image direction

Generated alternate-history scene with strong central composition.

Project variants:

- Clone columns leaving growth halls.
- Robot guards around a laboratory capital.
- Dinosaurs or engineered creatures behind fortified gates.
- Portal light linking separated sites.
- Several Kruger figures in a temporal command room.
- Human assistants and soldiers raising a new scientific-state flag.

Use one selected variant in a campaign. Do not create an image that tries to show every project family at once.

### Quote direction

Research a real quote about sovereignty, creators escaping control, knowledge and power, or the state. Avoid invented Frankenstein-style paraphrases.

### Button remark direction

Reaction should match formation type. Peaceful charter can be wary and formal. Rebellion can be shocked or bitter. Research required.

### Audio direction

Distinct music for state formation, combining order, scientific unease, and political finality.

## Global Kruger threat super-event

### Role

Global escalation.

### Trigger threshold

Suggested required conditions:

- Kruger State exists or host takeover completed.
- It controls a meaningful industrial and territorial base.
- It has several deployed project families or one strategic family at Weaponization.
- It is at war with multiple countries, has issued global submission demands, or has begun a world-conquest focus.
- Shared world-threat state is refreshed from actual conditions.

Do not fire merely because Kruger wins one war.

### Player feeling

The scientific state is no longer defending its laboratories. It has become a global project empire.

### Description direction

- Show project armies, captured laboratories, submission terms, and the transformation of conquered territory.
- Mention the dominant project route dynamically.
- Avoid spoiling the exact singularity failsafe unless foreign intelligence has discovered it.

### Image direction

Generated high-chaos super-event image.

Variant possibilities:

- Machine army crossing an industrial landscape.
- Clone formations around a captured capital.
- Portal network opening over several fronts.
- Engineered creatures moving beneath aircraft and artillery.
- Kruger before a global facility map, without readable generated labels.

### Quote direction

Research a real quote about power without limits, empire, knowledge used for domination, or obedience.

### Button remark direction

A short reaction that conveys strategic urgency without generic apocalypse wording.

### Audio direction

Aggressive and controlled rather than purely chaotic. The track should feel like an organized threat.

## World-threat framework

Create or reuse a threat-specific source, such as a Kruger source flag, only after real threat conditions exist.

Refresh logic should check:

- Kruger State survival.
- Military reach.
- Project weaponization.
- Aggressive war or submission network.
- Number and importance of countries threatened.
- Strategic-singularity status.

The source clears when:

- Kruger State is defeated.
- It accepts a durable settlement and dismantles global weapons.
- Project armies and strategic delivery are dismantled.
- World-end has already resolved the campaign.

The event should not create a separate parallel global threat system.

## World-end branch 1: Laboratory world

### Terminal fantasy

Kruger has transformed the world into one connected system of laboratories, project states, subject administrations, and engineered populations.

### Eligibility

- Global chaos is above the normal world-end threshold.
- No prior world end.
- Kruger controls or subjects an overwhelming share of the world.
- Every major opposing power is defeated, submitted, or reduced below the terminal standard.
- World-conquest branch completed.
- Global facility network exists.

### Terminal action

- Set the normal `world_end` state.
- Set a scenario-specific Laboratory World flag.
- Stop incompatible automatic event branches.
- Preserve only designed terminal presentation and aftermath-free end-state behavior.
- Fire the Laboratory World super-event.

### World state

The terminal world can vary by route.

Human technocratic order:

- Controlled research commonwealth under Kruger sovereignty.
- Less visibly monstrous, but political independence is gone.

Clone order:

- Replicated populations and military cohorts replace ordinary demographic limits.

Machine order:

- Automated administration and robot enforcement cover the world.

Bestiary order:

- Human settlement is reorganized around controlled engineered ecosystems and military organisms.

Temporal order:

- Political authority depends on future information and Kruger continuity.

Synthesis order:

- Multiple designed populations and systems coexist under one project hierarchy.

The world-end super-event can use dynamic description direction based on the dominant route, while retaining one slot or a carefully managed variant set.

### Counterplay before terminal state

- Defeat the Kruger State before it controls all majors.
- Break the submission faction.
- Liberate facility states.
- Turn assistants, clones, machines, or subjects.
- Destroy global portal, command, or production networks.
- Force a political settlement before terminal conquest.

## World-end branch 2: strategic singularity and Fallout

### Terminal fantasy

Kruger has completed a weapon stronger than a thermonuclear device. When his state faces destruction, the weapon is used to deny victory and push the world into the Fallout terminal scenario.

### Reconciliation with world-end rules

The user requires that the device can trigger Fallout regardless of the chaos tier at the moment of use. The global event framework requires chaos above 1000 before a world end.

The design satisfies both rules through one explicit sequence.

1. Confirm the singularity is fully researched, built, armed, and connected to its fail-deadly system.
2. Confirm Kruger is close enough to capitulation or has taken the selected deliberate-use path.
3. Calculate the difference between current chaos and a value above the world-end threshold.
4. Add at least that amount of chaos through a documented singularity source.
5. Record the resulting deaths, contamination, condemnation, and chaos history.
6. Confirm no prior world end.
7. Set `world_end` and the Fallout scenario flag through the existing terminal pipeline.
8. Fire the unique strategic-singularity super-event and stop incompatible systems.

The starting chaos tier therefore does not protect the world, while the implementation still respects the terminal contract.

### Research and construction timing

The singularity must take years if the Kruger State is not already a huge scientific power.

Time factors:

- Number and quality of facilities.
- High-energy project stage.
- Cross-project synergies.
- Captured foreign research sites.
- Assistant loyalty.
- Resource access.
- Sabotage and raids.
- Kruger State size.
- Evolution IV state.

A fast coalition victory should be able to prevent completion. A slow war of attrition gives Kruger time to arm the device.

### Device components

- Theoretical core.
- Exotic energy source.
- Containment or hardened housing.
- Delivery or distribution network.
- Automated or loyal activation system.
- Fail-deadly command.

Each component can have a separate facility or mission. This creates counterplay and prevents one hidden focus from instantly ending the world.

### Arming state

Foreign countries can discover broad stages through intelligence.

- Rumored.
- Confirmed theory.
- Prototype detected.
- Core under construction.
- Delivery network active.
- Armed.

The exact completion date can remain uncertain. The player should know when the threat is real enough to act.

### Use doctrines

#### Capitulation failsafe

Default extreme route.

- Activates when surrender progress crosses a severe threshold.
- Requires an armed device and live command network.
- AI Kruger uses it unless a negotiated surrender, temporal escape, or route-specific override exists.

#### Coercive demonstration

- Uses a limited prototype or remote target to demand submission.
- Does not automatically end the world.
- Raises chaos, deaths, contamination, and world threat.
- Can accelerate coalition formation.

#### Deliberate global use

- Available only to the most extreme conquest route.
- Kruger chooses destruction or forced transformation as an objective.
- Uses the same world-end threshold sequence.

#### Temporal escape alternative

- A temporal Kruger can abandon the current world rather than fire the device.
- This route can disable the automatic failsafe.
- It creates an ambiguous defeat or future-return hook rather than Fallout.

### Disarmament counterplay

Foreign and former-host actors can:

- Capture component facilities.
- Steal plans.
- Turn assistants.
- Break power or delivery links.
- Destroy machine command nodes.
- Close portal terminals.
- Raise paradox pressure.
- Offer a survivable surrender.
- Develop a project-specific disarmament mission.

A single conventional bombing decision should not safely remove an armed biological, temporal, or exotic device without understanding the containment risk.

## Strategic-singularity super-event

### Role

Terminal world-end moment.

### Trigger

The final device fires and the terminal pipeline begins.

### Description direction

- Describe observed physical and global consequences.
- Mention Kruger's state and the nature of the network only as far as witnesses could know.
- Avoid a technical reward list.
- Do not use generic final-darkness language.

### Image direction

Generated final-source art with strong central composition.

Possible subject:

- A laboratory core opening into a global destructive phenomenon.
- A fortified scientific city overwhelmed by white energy, portal geometry, temporal repetition, or fallout.
- Keep recognizable human and architectural scale so the image is not a flat abstract diagram.

### Quote direction

Research a real public-domain or historical quote about forbidden knowledge, final judgment, human responsibility, or destruction created by intellect. Do not invent a scientist quote.

### Button remark direction

Very short, final, and restrained. Research required.

### Audio direction

Unique real musical recording with finality. Pure drone, test tone, oscillator cue, or sound-effect bed is forbidden.

## Laboratory-world super-event

### Role

Terminal ideological and political victory.

### Trigger

Kruger completes global conquest conditions and the world-end threshold.

### Description direction

- Show the new order through laboratories, engineered populations, machine administration, or project client states.
- Use dynamic dominant-route text if supported.
- Make clear that ordinary sovereignty has ended.

### Image direction

Generated route-aware scene.

- Global command chamber, engineered capital, machine administration, replicated assembly, or portal-linked world.
- Do not use a generic map with arrows as the main image.

### Quote direction

Research a real quote about total rule, reason without restraint, engineered society, or empire.

### Audio direction

Unique music that feels ordered, triumphant, and deeply wrong rather than explosive.

## Defeat conditions

Kruger can be defeated at several scales.

### Local defeat

A small enclave is destroyed before becoming a world threat.

Aftermath:

- Normal event and decision cleanup.
- Former host archive recovery.
- Assistant, clone, machine, creature, or prototype remnants.
- No defeat super-event unless the crisis was unusually consequential.

### Regional defeat

Kruger controlled several states or defeated the former host, but did not reshape the world.

Aftermath:

- News event.
- Regional cleanup and integration.
- Countermeasure sharing.
- Persistent scientific legacy.

### Global-threat defeat

Kruger fought several major powers, controlled substantial territory, used several project armies, or nearly completed the singularity.

Aftermath package:

- Defeat super-event.
- International treaty or scientific compact.
- Facility dismantlement and inspection.
- Project survivor events.
- Reconstruction and remembrance.
- Lasting countermeasure or technology-sharing institution.
- Possible ambiguous Kruger survival based on clone, machine, temporal, or alien routes.

## Defeat-aftermath super-event

### Role

Reflective aftermath after a long global crisis.

### Trigger threshold

Require several of:

- Kruger State was a major.
- World-threat source was active for a substantial period.
- Several major countries fought it.
- Large deaths or contamination.
- Strategic-singularity project reached a late stage.
- Multiple project armies were deployed.
- Significant territory requires reconstruction.

### Description direction

- Explain what was defeated, what remains dangerous, and what institutions now exist because of the crisis.
- Avoid uncomplicated celebration.
- Mention the former host or coalition when relevant.
- Preserve uncertainty about surviving copies only if the route supports it.

### Image direction

Sourced or generated according to scene needs. Because the event is fictional, a generated period documentary aftermath is usually appropriate.

Possible subject:

- Scientists and soldiers entering a ruined laboratory.
- Clone or machine remnants surrendering.
- Civilians returning near dismantled project infrastructure.
- International inspectors around a captured core.

### Quote direction

Research a real quote about responsibility, rebuilding, vigilance, or the cost of knowledge.

### Audio direction

Unique reflective music. It should not sound like a clean military victory march.

## Postwar treaty system

A major aftermath can create an international scientific settlement.

Possible provisions:

- Registration of autonomous research facilities.
- Inspection of cloning, robotics, biological, portal, temporal, and exotic-energy work.
- Shared countermeasure research.
- Protection and asylum for assistants and project populations.
- Ban or control of strategic-singularity components.
- Technology-sharing group.
- Reconstruction aid for laboratory states.
- Rules for clone and machine personhood.
- Containment or destruction of escaped organisms.

The treaty should be a playable decision and diplomacy system only if the crisis was global enough to justify it.

## Surviving-remnant families

### Clone remnants

- Independent clone communities.
- Hidden Kruger body.
- Identity and citizenship disputes.
- Medical and genetic knowledge.

### Machine remnants

- Dormant command nodes.
- Autonomous factories.
- A network claiming Kruger still exists.
- Useful logistics or dangerous reactivation.

### Bestiary remnants

- Escaped creatures.
- Containment reserves.
- Ecological damage.
- Military handlers seeking asylum.

### Portal remnants

- Unstable terminals.
- Missing units or civilians.
- Foreign competition for intact links.

### Temporal remnants

- Contradictory records.
- Future warnings.
- A Kruger return hook.
- A coalition debate over destroying the time apparatus.

### Biological remnants

- Stockpiles.
- Outbreaks.
- Vaccine and medical knowledge.
- Severe cleanup missions.

### Alien remnants

- Artifacts and materials.
- Uncertain proof of Kruger's origin.
- Connection to alien events.

Remnants should be selected from actual project history. They should not all appear in every aftermath.

## Terminal cleanup

When either world-end branch fires:

- Set normal and scenario-specific terminal flags.
- Stop automatic Event 16 progress and foreign actions.
- Cancel missions and clear selected targets.
- Freeze or hide obsolete decision categories.
- Prevent duplicate super-events.
- Preserve terminal actor and project identity for presentation.
- Update event logs, documentation, and catalog state.
- Gate incompatible future random events through the established world-end system.
