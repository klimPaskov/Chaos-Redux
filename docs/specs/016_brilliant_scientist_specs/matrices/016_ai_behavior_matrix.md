# Event 016 AI behavior matrix

## Binding reconciliation

AI treats paleogenetics and xenobiological synthesis as distinct production, supply, operational, failure, and countermeasure systems. Host takeover is considered only after the full institutional-capture contract is already true. Temporal actions require bounded crisis targets and debt capacity; stabilization and anchor counterplay are evidence-gated. AI locks one origin conclusion at most and holds a terminal commitment until the required disarmament or consolidation transition is complete.

## Foreign action validity

- No action against dead or annexed actors.
- No extraction without a valid route and recipient.
- No joint laboratory without relations and a valid site.
- No assassination before meaningful threat and access.
- No countermeasure without evidence or direct exposure.
- No repeated theft of a completed target family without escalating cost and diminished reward.
- No hostile action against a faction ally unless betrayal or ideology rules support it.

## Kruger State expansion AI

Target score weights:

1. Former host after violent split.
2. States containing former Kruger facilities or stolen archives.
3. Power, rare resources, ports, rail hubs, and industrial centers required by portfolio.
4. Scientific facilities and special-project sites.
5. Weak states willing to submit.
6. Coalition leaders.
7. Distant ideological enemies only after global reach exists.

AI should not declare simultaneous wars beyond its supply and project capacity before Evolution IV.

## Anti-snowball AI brakes

- AI checks supply and project maintenance before new force cycles.
- AI limits simultaneous project-army production.
- AI values conventional logistics and ordinary infantry.
- AI uses integration systems before taking another large territory block.
- AI seeks resources rather than relying on free hidden grants.
- AI does not obtain absent project branches through focus selection.
- AI singularity research remains vulnerable to strategic defeat and sabotage.
