# 18. Research and design basis

## What the research can support

Event 79 is an alternate-history game system. A universal score of 100, equal race access for all players, and automatic puppeting are authored mechanics. Historical material can support distinct forms of political, military, and economic involvement. It does not prove a formula that converts foreign investment into national sovereignty.

### Several foreign military suppliers

A US Division of Far Eastern Affairs memorandum dated 12 March 1938 describes arms entering China through several routes and from several suppliers. It explicitly warns that the available information is incomplete. This supports a design in which multiple powers can supply the same target and in which delivery and supplier identity matter. It does not support assigning a particular country an automatic winning score. [H1]

### Political funding and contested allegations

A US diplomatic telegram from Tirana dated 4 March 1939 reports allegations that an Italian organizer used funding and contacts among dissatisfied Albanians in an effort against the regime. The report also records disagreement over Italian government sponsorship. This is a useful source direction for exposure records, domestic political recipients, and rival claims about foreign interference. It must be presented as a contemporary reported allegation, not silently upgraded into certain knowledge about every actor's intention. [H2]

### Assistance requires material and financing

A memorandum dated 19 September 1939 records Yugoslav requests for help purchasing arms in the United States. It discusses cash versus credit, export licensing, manufacturers, and access to production specifications. This supports separating diplomatic support from actual delivered equipment and keeping financing and delivery constraints visible. It does not establish any of this package's game costs or durations. [H3]

### Economic involvement as a distinct subject of diplomacy

The British Library's archival record IOR/R/15/2/857, catalogued by the Qatar Digital Library, identifies a Foreign Office economic-intelligence report from 31 January 1949 covering American economic involvement in several Middle Eastern countries. Only the collection description was read here, not all manuscript folios. It supports an economic-involvement research direction, not a detailed assertion about any country's dependency or a 1930s seed modifier. [H4]

## Design implications

Keep political persuasion, domestic recipients, delivered material, and economic projects distinct. Sponsors can cooperate with the same domestic movement while competing for their own control. Official reports can distinguish proved deliveries from disputed allegations. A country does not become a puppet merely because a foreign supplier appears in its stockpile.

Do not hardcode historical winners. A country-pair seed modifier requires a dated, narrow source and a current-state override. No such named-pair modifier is enabled by this package. Historical context enriches presentation and optional adapters without replacing the live diplomatic state.
