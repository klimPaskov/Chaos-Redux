# 09. Worked campaigns and replay patterns

## A player minor defeats an active major

This is an arithmetic and choice example, not a measured AI win rate. Assume the player minor has 30 starting Influence and the leading major has 45. Both are compatible with a target whose ruling ideology has at least 50% popularity. No government changes during the example.

The minor starts government support and public works on day 0. Government support finishes on day 25. Its first-family gain is 15 × 1.25 = 18.75, bringing the score to 48.75. Public works completes on day 45 and delivers one valid infrastructure level. Its first economic gain is 10 × 1.25 = 12.50, bringing the score to 61.25.

The minor starts propaganda on day 25 and finishes on day 50. Its first propaganda gain is 10 × 1.25 = 12.50, bringing the score to 73.75. A rival counter-propaganda operation finishes on day 65 and removes 10, bringing the score to 63.75 and exhausting that participant's baseline 30-day rival-loss budget.

Meanwhile, the minor starts a 500-weapon shipment on day 45 and a second government-support action on day 50. Both finish on day 75. Equipment delivery adds 20 × 1.25 = 25. The repeated government action uses S = 0.75 because its previous completion was within 60 days. Its raw gain is 14.0625, rounded down to 14.00. The combined score reaches 102.75, and the first completion sequence to cross 100 immediately puppets the target.

The minor's expenditure is 135 political power, 500 actual weapons, and one civilian factory reserved for 45 days. It used only two simultaneous campaigns in this target. This is an affordable concentrated campaign for some minors, but its availability depends on their actual resources.

The major in this example supported the government once, began a 90-day industrial project, and spent its next political slot trying to slow the minor. It is an active competitor that chose a slower portfolio. The example does not require an AI-only penalty or a scripted player victory. A major choosing a stronger sequence could win instead.

## An ideological outsider builds a viable position

Assume a sponsor starts at 5 Influence and its ideology has 5% popularity in an incompatible target. The first baseline opposition campaign completes after 30 days, raises popularity to 15%, and earns 10 × 0.50 = 5 Influence. The score becomes 10.

A second opposition campaign completes on day 60, raises popularity to 25%, and uses the improved C = 0.75 with repeated-family S = 0.75. The gain is 5.50 after quarter-point rounding, so the score becomes 15.50. A first propaganda action completes on day 85, raises popularity to 30%, and adds 7.50, bringing the score to 23.

The slow opening is intentional. The sponsor can run a material action alongside this political work. Once it creates an eligible government transition or reaches a friendlier political position, future actions become more efficient. It can also win through enough material dependence without a leadership change. There is no hidden lock requiring that its ideology already rule.

## A leadership change helps, but does not finish the contest

Assume Influence Campaigns is active. A sponsor has 60 Influence, at least 50% popularity for its pledged ideology, a valid aligned institution, and an existing character transition supplied by the target's country system. It pays 125 political power and completes the 40-day leadership challenge. There were no other opposition-family completions in the preceding 60 days.

After the permitted transition, the government matches and popularity remains at least 50%. The action earns 25 × 1.25 = 31.25. Its score reaches 91.25, so the target is still independent. The stability cost and real political transition have already occurred. The sponsor must complete another valid positive action before another country reaches 100.

If the transition's political writes are rejected by a protected national system, the action must not pretend success. The leadership operation requires a valid adapter before it can be purchased.

## Two races finish in the same day

Under The Great Game, Race A and Race B have different target IDs and generation records. A sponsor has 95 in A and 40 in B. Another sponsor has 90 in B. A gain of 10 in A puppets A and closes only A's active rows. B remains at 40 and 90. Its factories, shipments, and timers continue.

The player's selected target is B throughout this sequence. It does not matter. Completion in A reads the action's saved target identity, never the selected GUI target. Both races can finish in the same day with different masters.

## An investment outlives its original sponsor's campaign

An industrial contract needs 90 funded days. Another sponsor wins after 60 funded days. The original donor loses no completed factory because none has been delivered yet. It also receives no fictitious refund of 60 elapsed factory-days. Its future factory reservation ends, while the target keeps a project credit showing 60 of 90 funded days.

The target or its new overlord may reserve the original three-factory commitment for the remaining 30 funded days. Completion builds the single contracted civilian factory without an Influence reward. Ownership of that output follows the target state, not the old donor.

## A proxy score is not a winning result

A sponsor can lead the ranking, control two institutions, have the same ideology as the target, and remain below 100. The target is still independent. A rival can reach 100 through a mixed campaign with fewer institutional alignments and win. The institutions are tools for campaign play, not additional undisclosed victory conditions.
