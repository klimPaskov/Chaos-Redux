# 07. Evolution, concurrent races, and repeat behavior

## Baseline

The baseline has one unresolved target race worldwide. Every registered major and player can compete in that race. Baseline propaganda, government support, opposition support, public works, industrial investment, advisory work, equipment aid, and counter-propaganda are available.

A full race slot makes a new ordinary Event 79 firing unavailable. It does not erase the current race, reroll its participants, or charge the event's repeat weight again. The existing race must finish or become invalid before a new baseline target is selected.

## Evolution I: Influence Campaigns

Chaos requirement: 200+.

This evolution enables stronger political campaigns, national development programs, expanded military assistance, government-bloc support, opposition organization, cabinet alignment, leadership challenges, and deeper rivalry. The rival loss budget rises from 10 to 15 per rolling 30 days. There is still one active target slot.

An active baseline system becomes eligible for the project's maturity-based evolution after Chaos reaches 200. Use a nominal 90-day MTTH anchor, subject to the actual shared evolution framework and verified game-version adapter. Ninety days is an initial timing parameter, not a guaranteed waiting period or an independently calculated cumulative probability.

Existing actions retain the scale, receipt, duration, and promised delivery they had when started. Newly available actions appear immediately after the evolution actually activates. The current target, participant seeds, scores, institutions, projects, and rival-loss history are preserved. The larger loss budget applies to future interference using the same preceding 30-day history.

## Evolution II: The Great Game

Chaos requirement: 400+.

The Great Game raises concurrent target capacity to five. Each ordinary firing selects one further valid minor until the capacity is full. It does not create five targets from one root call. Each country has separate Influence totals, recipients, projects, intervention histories, and a separate winner.

This evolution enables strategic industrial programs, defense assistance programs, rapid realignment, military pressure on governments, and coordinated blocking campaigns. The rival loss budget becomes 20 per rolling 30 days. Sponsor campaign capacity remains three across all targets, so major powers must prioritize instead of running every available action everywhere.

An active system requires Influence Campaigns to have been active for at least 30 days and Chaos to be at least 400 before The Great Game's nominal 90-day MTTH eligibility begins. This separates the two upgrades during ordinary play. The exact timing distribution requires the project's MTTH probability tools.

Activating The Great Game upgrades the toolset for every unresolved race and expands future selection capacity. The races themselves remain independent. The global evolution does not choose a regional master, pool Influence, or grant a score bonus to an existing leader.

## Evolved openings

When Event 79 is about to open a race and has no active contest, read the current Chaos, remembered unlocked stage, and stage settings. At 200+, a permitted opening can begin with Influence Campaigns. At 400+, a permitted opening can begin with The Great Game, including its first-stage tools. The opening still creates one target, not a batch.

A previously unlocked stage is remembered for later races unless the user's event settings suppress that stage. A fall in Chaos does not remove already delivered assets or undo an active evolution. Current campaigns continue under their activated stage. Later openings use the highest permitted remembered or currently eligible opening stage.

The Great Game depends on Influence Campaigns. If the first stage is disabled, the second stage cannot bypass it. The settings panel should show that dependency. If only The Great Game is disabled, Influence Campaigns remains valid. Suppressing a stage for new openings does not retroactively confiscate assets from existing races. Any live settings operation that stops the event entirely uses the administrative closure rules, not a forged victory.

## Logging and Chaos

Record the actual stage activation once through the existing evolution log contract. An opening directly at stage two stores both prerequisite states but presents one evolved-opening record for the highest stage. It must not claim that two separate later mutations happened on the opening day.

Opening a contest, maturing an evolution, unlocking a button, increasing capacity, or recording an evolution adds no Chaos by itself. Real puppeting and real ideology changes use the ordinary shared world-change owners. Do not add an extra Event 79 point for the same puppet that the shared system already counted.

## Repeat and multiplayer behavior

Each accepted root firing reserves a new target and consumes Event 79's repeat behavior once. All reports, participant notices, investment completions, and evolution events belong to that firing or its target race. They are not additional random-event selections.

Before The Great Game, simultaneous multiplayer scheduler attempts must compete for the same single capacity slot. After it, separate accepted calls can select different valid minors. The reservation operation must prevent two calls from choosing the same target or exceeding capacity.

Former targets that remain subjects are unavailable for selection. A genuinely independent released former target may receive a fresh race after 180 days of independence. This authored cooldown prevents immediate release-and-recapture farming while allowing future geopolitical change. The history of its earlier master is retained for presentation and achievement exclusion.

Do not change the shared countdown range or repeat-weight recovery inside Event 79. The supplied mechanics guide owns those systems. This package adds target availability, capacity, and the race lifecycle to the appropriate existing selection adapters.
