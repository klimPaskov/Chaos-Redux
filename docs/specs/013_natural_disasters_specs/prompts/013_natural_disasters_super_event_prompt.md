# Super-Event Prompt — Event 013 Natural Disasters

Use `chaos-redux-super-events`. Research final title, quote, cultural remark, and audio through the proper source workflow. Do not treat any role labels in the spec as final localisation. Do not invent quotes, source-like allusions, or final audio choices.

## Super-event role

Event 013 has no world-end scenario. It may still have one super-event for Evolution IV because evolved minor events can become major enough for super-event treatment when they change how the player understands the campaign.

Working role label: **abnormal disaster age super-event** — role label only, not final title.

## Trigger moment

The super-event should fire the first time Evolution IV produces an abnormal high-chaos disaster burst automatically, or when the manual scenario launches at Maximum intensity and includes Evolution IV abnormal variants. It should not fire for ordinary baseline disasters or every meteor fragment.

Eligible trigger directions:

- first meteor shower affecting several states;
- first abnormal earthquake wave that absorbs the old Event 46 idea;
- first massive volcanic eruption with regional ashfall;
- first compressed high-chaos disaster barrage that clearly marks the event as a world condition.

## Tone

The tone should be grave and strange, not generic apocalypse. The world is not ending, but governments recognise that disaster reports now have patterns, chains, and impossible recurrence. The super-event should feel like the moment when ministries, observatories, militaries, and newspapers stop pretending these are ordinary accidents.

## Research gates

- Reveal super-event title: research required.
- Button remark: research required.
- Main quote: research required.
- Cultural reference: research required.
- Audio track: research and license verification required.

Do not use unresearched sample wording. The final text package must be documented with source links and confidence notes.

## Title direction

Short, memorable, specific to abnormal recurring natural disasters. Avoid generic titles like “The End Begins” or “World in Flames.” Good title direction should evoke the earth, sky, sea, and storm acting together without claiming world-end.

## Description direction

The description should explain:

- reports are coming from several named regions;
- earthquakes, storms, ashfall, or meteor fragments are no longer isolated;
- governments disagree on whether the pattern is natural, statistical, divine, or impossible;
- emergency offices are shifting from relief to permanent readiness;
- the world is still playable, but the rules of ordinary disaster expectation have broken.

Do not list mechanical modifiers.

## Quote direction

Find a public-domain, historical, religious, literary, scientific, or philosophical quote about nature, catastrophe, uncertainty, the earth, storms, human helplessness, or the limits of control. Prefer traceable sources over quote sites.

Candidate source directions to research:

- classical or biblical disaster/flood/earthquake imagery;
- public-domain poetry about storms, volcanoes, or nature;
- scientific/historical remarks about earthquakes or volcanoes;
- philosophical lines about human power before nature.

Keep the final quote short enough for the super-event UI.

## Button remark direction

A short, grim, memorable line or cultural reference. It may be a public-domain allusion, a short sourced historical phrase, or a very short modern reference if copyright-safe. It should not turn mass death into a cheap joke.

## Audio mood

Music should feel like awe, dread, and emergency mobilisation. Prefer 1–2 minute public-domain or clearly licensed orchestral, choral, hymn-like, or dark classical recording. Avoid drones, generated tones, sound effects, or abstract ambience unless explicitly approved.

Audio researcher must document:

- title;
- composer/creator;
- performer/recording source;
- source URL;
- license and confidence;
- duration;
- source file path;
- final `.ogg` path;
- suggested sound id;
- editing/conversion notes.

## Image direction

Coordinate with `chaos-redux-event-assets` for generated super-event art:

- strong central composition;
- cracked earth, storm wall, ash column, meteor fragments, damaged rail/port silhouettes;
- 1936–1945 documentary/painted super-event style;
- no readable generated text;
- ominous but not terminal.
