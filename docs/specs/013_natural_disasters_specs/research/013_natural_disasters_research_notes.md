# Research Notes — Event 013 Natural Disasters

These notes support the design direction. They are not final localisation.

## Authoritative references consulted

- USGS Earthquake Hazards Program: monitors and reports earthquakes, assesses impacts and hazards, and researches causes/effects. Design use: earthquake family should damage buildings, infrastructure, rail, supply, and population rather than behave like generic weather.
  URL: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards
- USGS earthquake facts: earthquakes are sudden shaking events caused by sudden slip on a fault. Design use: warning chance should be limited for ordinary earthquakes, with aftershocks/chains more plausible than exact prediction.
  URL: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/earthquake-facts-earthquake-fantasy
- USGS earthquake-triggered landslides/liquefaction note: landslides can cause fatalities, affect structures, cut roads, and block rivers; liquefaction can render ports and industrial/commercial facilities unusable. Design use: earthquake chains into landslide, rail closure, port/industry disruption.
  URL: https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/national-science-foundation/usgs-internship-opportunities/hazard-and-losses
- Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program: documents, disseminates, and archives eruption information and maintains a database of volcanoes and eruptions from the past 12,000 years. Design use: volcanic regions should be curated and ashfall/eruption families should be map-aware.
  URL: https://volcano.si.edu/
- NOAA/NWS flood safety and NOAA/NSSL flood basics: flooding and flash floods can happen quickly and threaten property, roads, and life. Design use: flood warnings, road/rail closure, bridge repair, and evacuation decisions.
  URL: https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood
  URL: https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/education/svrwx101/floods/
- NOAA National Hurricane Center storm surge maps and NOAA storm surge overview: storm surge is water-level rise above normal tide and risk can extend inland in hurricane-prone coastal areas. Design use: coastal surge should hit ports/naval bases/coastal infrastructure, not just a single abstract state.
  URL: https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/nationalsurge/
  URL: https://www.noaa.gov/storm-surge-overview
- WMO/FAO 2024 agriculture early warning article: droughts cause water scarcity, stunt crop growth, and produce substantial economic losses; floods can destroy harvests and threaten food-security pillars. Design use: drought should be a slow food/supply/refugee pressure, not a factory-collapse clone.
  URL: https://wmo.int/media/update/wmo-and-fao-partnering-enhance-early-warnings-agriculture-sector
- UNDRR drought terminology: integrated drought management includes monitoring/early warning, vulnerability/impact assessment, and mitigation/preparedness/response. Design use: drought decisions should be warning-heavy and mitigation-oriented.
  URL: https://www.undrr.org/understanding-disaster-risk/terminology/hips/mh0401
- NASA Planetary Defense: planetary defense tracks potentially hazardous near-Earth objects and mitigation efforts. Design use: Evolution IV meteor showers should be smaller abnormal skyfall events distinct from a dedicated large asteroid event.
  URL: https://science.nasa.gov/planetary-defense/
- ESA Planetary Defence: observation, data provision, warnings, and mitigation are core planetary-defense functions. Design use: observatory warning decisions for Evolution IV meteor showers.
  URL: https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Planetary_Defence/Asteroids_and_Planetary_Defence
- World Bank Resilience and Disaster Management: disaster risk management links risk reduction, preparedness, financial protection, and inclusive recovery. Design use: recovery should be a real response loop, not just damage application.
  URL: https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/topic/resilience-and-disaster-management

## Historical era anchors

These are inspiration references for report/news tone, not mandatory scripted events:

- NOAA/NCEI HAZEL entry for the 1939 Erzincan earthquake records severe fatalities and damage. Design use: 1930s/40s earthquakes belong in the HOI4 era and can be represented by collapsed towns, rail breaks, and state rescue reports.
  URL: https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/hazel/view/hazards/earthquake/event-more-info/6510
- USGS Today in Earthquake History for San Juan, Argentina, 1944 notes severe destruction and more than 8,000 dead, with some estimates up to 10,000. Design use: earthquake report images and reconstruction politics can fit the era.
  URL: https://earthquake.usgs.gov/learn/today/index.php?day=15&month=1
- UK Hansard entry on the 1942/43 Bengal cyclone reports estimated deaths, affected people, cattle killed, and huts destroyed. Design use: cyclone/flood/storm-surge impacts can be tied to crops, homes, livestock, and displaced people.
  URL: https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1943/jan/21/bengal-cyclone

## Design conclusions

1. Earthquake, flood, storm surge, drought, volcano, and meteor/planetary-defense references support distinct disaster family mechanics.
2. Warning events fit storms, floods, droughts, volcanic unrest, and meteors better than ordinary earthquakes.
3. Drought and famine chains should use missions, supply, food, refugees, and stability rather than one-time building damage.
4. Volcano and meteor variants justify generated high-chaos art, but ordinary report images can use either generated period-documentary scenes or sourced archival images.
5. Recovery should have preparedness, emergency response, and resilient rebuilding options; this aligns with disaster-risk-management practice and improves gameplay.
