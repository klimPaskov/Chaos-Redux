# Event 080: Airship
## Part 4: War, crashes, and rescue

## Flying through a war

The route is not redrawn whenever a country enters a war. The expedition can enter an active front, contested airspace, or hostile territory. It can also pass peacefully over a country whose fighting is far away. These situations must be distinguished through the current location.

A local war exposure is based on actual relevant fighting, hostile aircraft, anti-aircraft presence, or an explicit interception. It does not multiply by the number of countries owning fragments of a strategic region. One airship remains one exposed target.

| Encounter | What happens | Available response |
|---|---|---|
| Identification request | Local authorities demand identification before passage | Comply through communications, accept a short delay, or continue with a stated dispute |
| Warning interception | Aircraft approach without already destroying the ship | Accept an escort or a real diversion, or continue into the next hostile exposure |
| Forced landing order | A reachable host demands that the ship land | Land and face inspection or detention, or refuse and risk a combat incident |
| Anti-aircraft incident | Fire affects the actual route segment | Resolve damage and any resulting emergency at that segment |
| Deliberate attack | A hostile actor takes a concrete destructive action | Resolve a single combat encounter, with rescue or crash consequences |
| Capture attempt | The ship is forced down but remains intact | Start bounded custody and passenger-release work |
| Suspected espionage | Authorities object to actual observation or photography | Inspect the disputed material, restrict a demonstration, or accept detention |

A country's ability to attack must be grounded in reach. A distant rival cannot buy an instant crash over a neighbour for 30 Political Power. An interception option requires actual local capability or a properly established event-owned opportunity. Peaceful hosts can cause serious danger through mishandling or escalation, but low opinion alone is not a standing command to kill passengers.

An accusation can be false. The final text should distinguish an American allegation from a proven cause. The immediate retaliation rule does not require the allegation to be true.

## Capture and impoundment

A controlled forced landing can leave the ship intact. The host can inspect it, negotiate civilian departure, or continue impoundment. The United States can fund civilian return, negotiate release, or abandon the flight while maintaining a record of the people held ashore.

A ten-day release mission handles the immediate diplomatic episode. Failure ends the voyage as captured or impounded and moves surviving people into an accepted shared custody or settlement state. It cannot leave thousands of people in a forgotten event variable forever. A later release of those people is a population movement, not a second successful voyage.

No automatic war is introduced solely for intact capture. A war can already exist, arise through other game systems, or follow a separately justified diplomatic choice. The hard automatic rule below applies when the ship crashes in foreign-controlled territory.

## The crash

A crash is a single physical event. It uses the actual current position or actual diversion approach. It is never redirected to a more dramatic capital, a random owned state, a convenient enemy, or the last host visited.

The impact records the state, its controller at that moment, the physical ship form, the people actually aboard, the observed cause, and any preparation already completed. The route ends immediately. All pending flight, refit, experiment, and homecoming outcomes become invalid.

The result has separate components: onboard deaths, surviving people needing rescue, local civilian deaths, physical damage, any nearby military damage, and a possible subsequent fire. A total may be presented for readability, but these components retain their ownership and accounting distinctions.

## Impact severity

The proposal has three crash profiles. The encounter selects a profile through the actual accident and preparation context. They are not an additional independent roll for whether the crash occurs.

| Crash profile | Immediate onboard deaths | Initially living people needing rescue | Physical interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Partly controlled impact | 25% of those aboard | Up to 25% of those aboard | Some evacuation or control remained possible |
| Severe impact | 60% of those aboard | Up to 20% of those aboard | Major structural destruction with accessible surviving sections |
| Catastrophic impact | 95% of those aboard | The remaining living people | Destruction leaves few survivors |

Percentages are applied to the actual manifest, rounded down to whole people. People already safely ashore are excluded. The remaining living people who do not need rescue become immediately recoverable survivors. The accounting identity must hold after every transition.

Within an affected group, survival can reflect a previously recorded cabin reassignment, evacuation preparation, or location aboard. Those effects redistribute or reduce the profile's casualties through documented preparation. They cannot award more survivors than were alive at impact. Essential crew and passengers remain identifiable in the expanded report.

Prepared ground rescue reduces the initially unresolved rescue pool and later rescue deaths. It does not retroactively reverse people already confirmed dead. Moving spectators away from a landing ground reduces local exposure, not onboard casualties.

## Local civilians and damage

Baseline local exposure anchors are 50 people at an isolated or rural impact site, 500 at a populated urban approach, and 1,000 at an industrial approach with workers or dense adjacent settlement. Grand Tour doubles those anchors. Flying City multiplies them by 25. The impact profile applies a 50%, 100%, or 150% severity factor to that anchor.

These are requested losses, not unconditional removals. The actual affected state population, the physical site, completed evacuation, and the shared minimum-population rule cap the result. A rural label does not invent a crowd where none is present. A populated industrial site must be supported by the recovered location and live state features. A remote water impact has no local civilian loss.

The impact targets relevant existing buildings. At baseline, use approximately 10% damage to exposed infrastructure and 5% to an exposed civilian or military industrial group. Grand Tour uses 20% and 10%. Flying City uses 75% infrastructure damage and 50% damage to exposed industry, with ports, air bases, or rail facilities included only when the site actually contains and exposes them. The partly controlled profile halves these amounts and the catastrophic profile increases them by 50%, capped at what exists.

These percentages express design damage to existing relevant capacity. The implementation must translate them to verified native building damage units. They are not instructions to remove a percentage of arbitrary state slots or to invent unsupported engine syntax.

Flying City therefore can leave a state with extensive destroyed infrastructure and industry even before a spreading fire. It does not delete a state, erase every building unconditionally, or turn the impact into a nuclear strike.

## Nearby units and equipment

Military harm is limited to units actually in the impact state and plausibly near the approach. A baseline crash affects at most one eligible division with up to 5% strength damage. Grand Tour can affect at most two with up to 10%. Flying City can affect at most five with up to 25%. Damage is bounded by the available strength and the selected impact profile.

Native damage must account for actual equipment and personnel effects. The event must not both inflict native division losses and separately remove the same equipment from a country's stockpile. Military deaths already registered through native casualty tracking must not be entered into civilian Deaths again.

An inability to target and audit actual local unit harm is a release blocker for this part of the request. Applying an army-wide modifier or deducting unrelated reserve equipment is not an acceptable substitute.

## Immediate American retaliation

When a crash occurs in a state controlled by a country other than the United States, the United States declares war on that exact controller in the crash resolution itself. There is no intermediate war goal, waiting day, ultimatum mission, investigation requirement, or player option controlling whether the declaration happens.

The crash message and American report describe a war that has already begun. A player leaving a popup unopened cannot prevent it. If the United States is already at war with the impact controller, the existing war continues without a duplicate declaration or duplicate war-derived Chaos.

The rule applies to an ally, friendly country, neutral country, occupier, or subject if that country is the actual controller. Political relationships cannot silently suppress the requested outcome. Where a faction, subject relation, or pact blocks a native declaration, implementation must establish the smallest supported bilateral separation that permits the exact war. It must not dissolve unrelated alliances, annex the target, retarget its overlord, or replace war with a claim. Engine support and consequences require explicit validation.

The controller is captured at the instant of impact. A later territorial transfer changes rescue responsibility and fire ownership, but it does not rewrite which country was attacked by this crash-triggered declaration.

A crash in American-controlled territory is a domestic disaster and causes no declaration against the United States. A verified open-water crash has no territorial controller and causes no declaration under this rule. If the United States has ceased to exist before an unresolved flight can continue, terminate the expedition through civilian recovery. Do not resurrect a state solely to issue a war.

This is a deliberately punitive event rule. The diplomacy text may accuse a host of sabotage, negligent handling, inadequate protection, or deliberate attack, but the accusation must not be treated as proven evidence when the actual incident was mechanical or weather-related. Existing Condemnation systems can judge deliberate acts according to their own rules.

## Fires

A crash can ignite an urban, industrial, forest, or wider vegetation fire. The event shows the ignition and chooses a physically compatible handoff. It does not create a second wildfire spread engine.

Proposed ignition chances are 25% for a partly controlled impact, 50% for a severe impact, and 75% for a catastrophic impact. A forest or industrial site adds 10 percentage points, while completed local fire preparation removes 25 points. Clamp the final probability between 0% and 100%. A successful ignition must still pass the receiving system's physical eligibility rules.

Where Event 013 can own the effect, use its public disaster gateway with the actual selected state, the appropriate fire family, and Airship's external-caller history policy. Event 013 owns subsequent fire deaths, damage, spread, recovery, and state-control transfers. Airship owns the initial impact. The first fire job follows Event 013's documented future-date scheduling contract. It does not apply a duplicate same-day crash impact.

A valid fire can merge with an existing fire card under the owner system's rules. Rejection by the public API must be recorded and resolved against the cause of rejection. An incompatible urban or industrial fire must not be forced into a forest-only profile. Use a verified existing urban or industrial owner if one exists. Otherwise add the missing supported family through the disaster owner before claiming the requested fire branch is complete.

After a fire handoff, the Airship map can display the owning fire's status and link to its recovery view. It cannot maintain an independent competing fire intensity value. The shared fire can keep burning after all Airship rescue messages have closed.

## Rescue

At a land crash, the current controller receives the rescue task. A government at war with the United States still has civilians at the impact site and can perform rescue. The United States can offer documented material assistance where a valid route and accepted delivery contract exist. War does not automatically create humanitarian access.

A four-day rescue mission works with the actual initially living rescue pool. Prepared rescue aims to recover 90% of that pool, ordinary rescue 70%, and severely obstructed rescue 40%. The rest are confirmed lost only after the rescue outcome establishes their deaths. Those figures are balancing proposals and need sequence testing across the different impact profiles.

Post-crash rescue support costs 25 motorized equipment and 25 support equipment for each started group of 600 people requiring rescue, with a minimum of one package for a nonempty group. This people-based work order is separate from the pre-crash grounded evacuation service in Part 2. Do not multiply this same rescue charge again by physical form. The AI must not accept a cost larger than its available resources. Smaller partial assistance can be meaningful and must state how much of the living pool it covers.

A new controller inherits unfinished rescue responsibilities. Completed rescue work and confirmed survivor counts remain credited to their actual contributors. The population ledger must survive that transfer without duplicated people.

A resolved land crash during a missing-contact episode closes that episode immediately. The required American declaration does not wait for the search timer or wreck-discovery popup. Do not hide a known foreign territorial crash merely to prolong a disappearance story.

Ocean rescue requires a verified reachable rescue capability. The search can locate survivors, a grounded ship on a validated shore, or wreckage. It cannot invent a random land state simply to force American retaliation. An unresolved disappearance ends with missing people still in a persistent shared custody or missing-person account. It grants neither a clean-voyage achievement nor confirmed-death credit until evidence establishes the outcome.

## Aftermath

The final crash record names the actual location, the controller at impact, the current rescue controller if different, confirmed deaths, rescued people, people still missing, the immediate damage, and the fire handoff status. It distinguishes onboard nationalities or resident origins from the local population.

American mourning can produce a temporary domestic consequence. Host rescue can produce recognition even while a war continues. These do not undo the declaration, erase casualties, or remove an existing shared fire. The event ends its own flight work while leaving independently owned consequences intact.
