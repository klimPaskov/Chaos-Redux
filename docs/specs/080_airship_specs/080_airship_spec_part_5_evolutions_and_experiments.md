# Event 080: Airship
## Part 5: Evolutions and experiments

## Evolution progression

The three evolutions are cumulative. Grand Tour expands the public programme and available capacity. Experimental Flight adds live aviation trials. Flying City changes the physical scale of the expedition. None changes the route or the requirement to complete 121 two-day progression steps.

Chaos thresholds establish eligibility. A threshold crossing is not an immediate midair transformation. Use the project's evolution process, with a proposed active-voyage mean time to happen of about 90 days for an eligible next stage before contextual modifiers, following the `chaos-redux-mtth` skill.

An in-progress stage becomes operational only when its required installation or refit completes. The next stage's active-voyage eligibility process then begins. A pre-fire opening at a higher accepted stage includes its lower stages, subject to the shared evolved-opening rules.

A pre-fire evolved opening can start with an already eligible physical form under the shared event-evolution rules. During an existing voyage, the next eligible stage receives its own activation opportunity. Grand Tour and Flying City require an actual capable stop to commit their physical refit. Experimental equipment also requires a valid installation window. A stage cannot award the same upgrade twice.

Higher Chaos can accelerate the eligibility process, while a critically damaged ship pauses optional transformation work. A completed physical stage remains after Chaos falls. Activation alone adds no direct Chaos. Concrete consequences of the new behaviour are evaluated separately in the Chaos impact map.

## Evolution I: The Grand Tour

The ship becomes an international public programme. It can carry 1,500 passengers and 250 crew, with more invitations, journalists, scientific guests, merchants, and ceremonial visits. Stops offer stronger local content and more opportunities for bilateral goodwill.

If the voyage is already underway, a technical stop can perform a six-day Grand Tour refit for 100 support equipment, 50 motorized equipment, and 1,000 fuel supplied by the United States or through accepted aid. The refit requires Condition at least 75 and a suitable ground facility. Major repairs are a separate work order. The installation cannot be completed while the ship is airborne, captured, or in an unresolved emergency.

The new capacity is a maximum, not an automatic passenger increase. Passengers and additional crew board at valid stops. A ship that retains a smaller manifest still gains the public programme, but its crash cannot kill people who never boarded.

The larger programme adds four visit directions. Cultural visits favour artists, schools, and public exchanges. Diplomatic visits favour delegations and protocol choices. Commercial visits favour controlled exhibits and tourism. Scientific visits favour observers and demonstrations. A stop receives one primary programme, with no stack of four simultaneous national bonuses.

Grand Tour can improve the final diplomacy reward only after at least five successful programme stops in distinct countries and at least twenty flight steps operated in that stage or a later stage. A last-minute refit cannot claim credit for an entire international tour.

Its scale doubles service material costs and local impact exposure compared with baseline. Extra prestige is therefore accompanied by real scheduling and rescue obligations. It does not simply grant more Stability at launch.

## Evolution II: Experimental Flight

The expedition becomes a practical test platform. Experiments concern civilian aviation systems that can produce relevant Air Experience and limited research benefits. They do not unlock a separate technology tree or create free strategic bombers.

Install the experimental suite at a capable stop for 100 support equipment, 25 Air Experience, and 500 fuel, with a four-day extension. This is an optional commitment after stage eligibility. A country without sufficient Air Experience can continue the voyage and install later. An evolved opening may begin with the suite already fitted under the shared opening rules, without requiring a retroactive debit.

Only one experiment is active at a time. A normal trial requires five completed flight steps under its relevant conditions. Ground delays do not advance trial exposure. Aborting a trial removes its extra flight risk but grants no successful-trial reward. Each of the four families has one rewarded successful result per voyage, and at most four trials can be committed in total. A failed attempt consumes a commitment, so a player who wants every family must succeed first time. This prevents an unlimited search for good outcomes while parked at a stop.

| Trial family | Useful context | Cost to begin | Successful result | Failure consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Engines | A sustained flight stretch and a technical starting stop | 50 support equipment and 500 fuel | 15 Air Experience and an eligible engine-related research result | Condition loss and a repairable engine concern |
| Navigation and radio | A long passage with limited landmarks, a long remote section, or a difficult verified approach | 25 support equipment and 250 fuel | 15 Air Experience and an eligible navigation, range, or communications research result | An actual delay, a validated diversion, or a bounded radio-loss event with its search |
| Radar | Installed experimental equipment and a compatible technology stage | 50 support equipment and 500 fuel | 15 Air Experience and a compatible radar research result | Equipment failure, a technical concern, or an inspection dispute |
| Airframe and weather | Varied ordinary flight exposure that includes at least one real adverse-weather leg, without deliberately entering an unverified hazard | 75 support equipment and 250 fuel | 15 Air Experience and an eligible airframe-related research result | Structural damage requiring a proper service stop |

The exact technology identifiers must be verified against the installed game and project tree. A family without a valid research target converts its successful result to 10 additional Air Experience once, subject to the voyage's research-conversion cap. It does not unlock a guessed technology or an unrelated military doctrine.

An active trial adds the experimental exposure from Part 2. It can cause an incident during flight. Such an incident interrupts that trial and resolves the relevant damage once. At successful completion of all five required steps, the scientific result is proposed as 75% useful data, 20% inconclusive data, and 5% failed equipment with a repairable fault. A completed-result fault is not another instantaneous crash roll. An airborne destructive trial failure is already covered by the operational incident system.

Useful data earns the stated Air Experience. At most two successful families grant a 50% single-use research bonus each, with no ahead-of-time waiver. Later successful families grant their stated Air Experience only. Inconclusive data grants 5 Air Experience once for that commitment. A failed equipment result grants no research bonus. The total experiment-derived Air Experience is capped at 75 for the voyage.

Results are delivered as Air Experience and research bonuses only. A trial does not grant hidden one-off protections against later incidents.

## Evolution III: The Flying City

The airship becomes a small travelling city with passenger districts, service corridors, workshops, storage, public rooms, medical facilities, and many international guests. Its proposed manifest ceiling is 9,000 passengers and 1,000 crew. It remains one civilian expedition.

An in-progress physical transformation requires a validated major technical stop, Condition at least 75, and a ten-day refit. The proposed price is 500 support equipment, 250 motorized equipment, and 2,500 fuel. The project should treat this as a fictional expansion assembled from prepared modular sections and ground support at the stop. There is no claim that a historical airship could undergo such a conversion.

The refit must be shown on the route display and in the ship's visual form. A physical refit cannot occur over a small emergency landing field that lacks the required facilities. If no remaining original stop can support it, eligibility stays visible but the transformation does not happen during that voyage. A qualifying pre-fire opening can still start as Flying City.

At this scale, normal passenger exchange is 500 people per suitable stop and material service costs are five times the baseline. Filling the city takes many boarding stops. A nearly empty Flying City has the structural damage potential of the larger hull but only the onboard casualties of the people actually present.

New stories involve service districts, workshops, medical spaces, large shore parties, international guests, supply contracts, and organised evacuation. These expand the catalogue in Part 3. They do not add a second city-building game, recruitable army, sovereign country, taxation system, or new focus tree.

Flying City can produce a large state-level catastrophe. The large hull, facilities, cargo, and local crowd exposure increase impact damage and fire risk. The crash still uses the actual state and shared systems. A spectacular form never grants permission to use a nuclear effect as a shortcut.

The extra completion reward requires at least thirty completed flight steps in the physical Flying City form, ten of them with at least 5,000 people actually aboard, plus at least three successful scaled service stops. This proves that the expedition actually operated at that scale.

## Evolution choices and late arrival

The United States can decline an optional refit while continuing the voyage. Declining does not erase future eligibility, but it does not reroll the same stop's proposal indefinitely. The next appropriate stop can offer a still-valid uncommitted upgrade.

If an emergency interrupts paid work, use the service interruption rules. A half-finished physical form cannot be treated as both versions for different benefits. Before the refit commits, the previous capacity and crash profile remain authoritative. After it commits, the new form applies to subsequent exposure.

Research and prestige are earned from actual stage activity. Physical conversion does not retroactively change earlier casualty totals, visits, service costs, or experiment results. Completion reads the earned exposure records, not simply the highest evolution flag present on the final day.
