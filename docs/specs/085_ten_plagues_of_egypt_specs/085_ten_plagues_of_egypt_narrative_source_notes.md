# Narrative Source Notes — Ten Plagues of Egypt

Research date: 2026-09-23.
These sources establish literary and religious narrative context.
They do not establish the plagues as verified modern historical events, provide a medical diagnosis, or determine the game's numerical model.

The uploaded user brief is the authority for the worldwide adaptation, religious and ideological availability of protection, actual population loss, shared-system ownership, and the three Evolutions.
The game deliberately extends the source narrative to the whole campaign map.
It does not reproduce every distinction or narrative claim of the original religious text as a mechanic.

## Water and the first disruption

**Primary source:** Exodus 7, Mechon-Mamre Hebrew/English presentation.
Source: `https://mechon-mamre.org/p/pt/pt0207.htm`
Relevant passage: the water becoming unusable and the transition toward frogs near the end of the chapter.
The source supports the water-service identity and ordinary people seeking drinking water.
The game's twenty-day opening, industrial consequences, global reach, and casualty allowance are proposed adaptations, not claims drawn from this chapter.

## Frogs, gnats, and flies

**Primary source:** Exodus 8, Mechon-Mamre Hebrew/English presentation.
Source: `https://mechon-mamre.org/p/pt/pt0208.htm`
Relevant passages: the frogs dying and being gathered; the succeeding insect episodes.
The aftermath provides a narrative reason for cleanup remaining important after the frogs recede.
The brief's Lice and Gnats label is preserved; no exact scientific insect identification is asserted.
Disease and production effects are fictional gameplay design.

## Livestock, Boils, and storm preparation

**Primary source:** Exodus 9, United States Conference of Catholic Bishops presentation.
Source: `https://bible.usccb.org/bible/exodus/9`
Relevant passages: livestock losses, Boils, the warning to shelter people and animals before hail, and crops that remain after the storm.
These support the ordered progression, meaningful shelter preparation, and damage to only what is actually exposed.
No real pathogen or scientific causal explanation is selected.
The state's numerical mortality and building damage remain design proposals.

## What survived the hail, and the Darkness

**Primary source:** Exodus 10, Mechon-Mamre Hebrew/English presentation.
Source: `https://mechon-mamre.org/p/pt/pt0210.htm`
Relevant passages: locusts consuming what remained after hail, and the three-day Darkness.
These motivate the remaining-crop conservation rule and the short final emergency.
The game adopts a common 72-hour world deadline for playability; its universal scope and protection rules follow the user's adaptation.
Modern military penalties and rendering requirements are original design decisions.

## Passover and the final night

**Primary source:** Exodus 12, Mechon-Mamre Hebrew/English presentation.
Source: `https://mechon-mamre.org/p/pt/pt0212.htm`
Relevant passages: household preparation, neighboring-household participation, the marked doorway, and the firstborn night across social positions.
These inform the preparation's household and community scale.
The game makes the action available to all governments as explicitly requested.
Its 48-, 36-, and 24-hour operations, coverage percentages, firstborn cohort share, and hazard formula are original proposals.
