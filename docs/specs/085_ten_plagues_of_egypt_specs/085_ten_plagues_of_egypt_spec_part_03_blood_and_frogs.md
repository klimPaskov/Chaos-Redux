# 3. Water into Blood and Frogs

These two phases teach the central choice at manageable severity: protective work builds a foundation, denial preserves output and raises future risk.
They must not consume the demographic scale reserved for Livestock, Boils, Hail and Fire, Locusts and the tenth plague.

## I. Water into Blood

Freshwater in rivers, reservoirs and wells turns blood-like and unusable.
The reach is supernatural; the consequences are concrete: drinking water, sanitation, industrial cooling, cooking and military supply compete for working sources.

The Nile receives a dedicated report and picture when the map contains Egyptian territory, sent to whoever controls it.
It needs no independent Egypt tag and gives Egypt no special mechanic.
Other countries receive reports naming their actual affected states; do not invent named installations.

### Effects in affected states

- Water shortage: local supply and factory output interruption through the crisis spirit's projection.
- Civilian sickness and Stability loss, projected from the exposed share.
- A modest food shock to Famine for contaminated food and water-dependent kitchens.
- Direct mortality budget: 5 basis points of exposed population before Wrath, intensity and protection, settled once per phase (Part 15).

### Responses

- **Heed: Safe Water** (Part 14) keeps one selected affected state supplied for 10 days, by truck route or by local organization.
Full success removes most of the state's direct mortality and its Famine shock, and the working water network carries into Frogs as a cleanup advantage.
- **Shut down water-heavy industry**: a variant of the same action that trades a short, deeper output penalty in the state for faster recovery; it is not a lasting consumer-goods penalty.
- **Harden Our Hearts** keeps industry running and weakens civilian distribution.

An investigation option in the opening report reveals the next phase's footprint a few days early; it grants no cure, technology or research.

### Recession

At baseline sources recover during the second half of the phase and interrupted output returns gradually.
A recovered river does not refill food reserves or end an outbreak.
Under Evolution I the red water persists into Frogs and Lice, and the player must decide whether to keep a water route running instead of reclaiming its trucks.

## II. Frogs

Frogs pour out of waterways into homes, ministries, factories, barracks and depots.
The phase opens with disruption and disbelief, not mass death.

### Effects in affected states

- Output and infrastructure disruption recorded once per state, then easing toward clearance.
- A supply penalty at depots in the footprint.
- Direct mortality: normally zero.
- Secondary disease allowance: up to 5 basis points through the disease owner, only when uncleared districts remain at the die-off (Part 13).

Do not damage buildings every day because a frog flag remains, and do not delete a percentage of every country's arsenal.
Equipment loss needs an identified affected depot and a bounded amount.

### Responses

- **Heed: Frog Clearance** (Part 14) clears one selected state's civilian services before the die-off.
Full success prevents its sanitation aftermath; partial success shrinks it; failure leaves the backlog to worsen Lice and Flies.
- A strategic-site variant protects a key depot or rail hub with fewer resources but protects only that site and earns partial credit.
- **Harden Our Hearts** sends crews to war-critical facilities; the benefit is especially tempting because frogs look harmless.

### The mass die-off

Near the end of the phase the frogs die at once.
The report shifts from swarms in buildings to exhausted crews clearing streets.
This transition is part of the plague, not optional decoration.
Earlier cleanup decides how severe the sanitation aftermath becomes; an expired objective does not start a nationwide disease.

At higher intensity the swarms occupy several major sites while the water crisis persists.
There are no frog divisions, frog states or giant-frog invasions.

## Recurring local actors

Municipal crews, district officials, civilian kitchens and military transport officers recur in later reports when their earlier work matters.
That continuity gives the event a human thread without another meter.
