# Ten Plagues of Egypt

## Catalog entry

- Event ID: `085`
- Event name: Ten Plagues of Egypt
- Type: Major
- Status: To Be Reworked
- Chaos level: 3
- Cluster: Natural Disasters
- Cluster role: Severe member

## Core idea

> The ten biblical plagues of Egypt begin happening again, except this time Egypt is not the only land being struck. The signs spread across the entire world.

The event follows the ten biblical plagues in their traditional order:

1. Water turns to blood
2. Frogs
3. Lice or gnats
4. Flies
5. Pestilence of livestock
6. Boils
7. Hail and fire
8. Locusts
9. Darkness
10. Death of the firstborn

Every valid country is affected.

Egypt receives additional flavor and presentation because of the biblical connection, especially around the Nile, but the catastrophe itself is global.

This is one of the major mortality events in Chaos Redux. When a plague logically kills civilians, it causes real state population loss and reports those deaths to the shared **Deaths system**.

The event should leave a major demographic footprint even when the world survives.

## Global progression

The ten plagues form one large sequential crisis.

They occur in biblical order rather than being randomly selected.

Each plague lasts long enough to have meaningful gameplay consequences before the next begins.

At baseline, one plague should normally begin to subside before the next reaches full strength.

Later Evolutions allow the effects to overlap much more heavily.

The early plagues can still be dismissed by some governments as strange natural phenomena.

By the middle of the sequence, the resemblance to Exodus is unmistakable.

By the ninth plague, every government knows what the tenth plague is supposed to be.

## Divine Wrath

Each affected country tracks one public value:

**Divine Wrath**

Divine Wrath controls how severely the current and later plagues strike that country.

It rises when governments:

- repeatedly ignore warnings
- refuse civilian relief
- exploit the catastrophe
- maintain maximum war production while civilians suffer
- suppress panic through extreme violence
- choose to harden their hearts

It falls when governments:

- protect civilians
- accept economic sacrifices
- organize relief
- help neighboring countries
- complete plague-specific preparations
- heed the warnings

The event does not force one canonical explanation.

Governments may interpret the phenomenon as:

- divine punishment
- a supernatural anomaly
- an unexplained natural catastrophe
- mass hysteria surrounding real disasters
- something scientific institutions cannot yet explain

The mechanics work regardless of the country's ideology or religion.

## Harden Our Hearts

Countries can deliberately refuse to yield to the catastrophe.

**Harden Our Hearts** gives short-term benefits such as:

- War Support
- reduced military-production disruption
- reduced political panic
- temporary Stability
- continued mobilization

It also raises Divine Wrath.

Repeatedly hardening the country's heart can keep the war machine functioning during the early plagues while making the later plagues vastly more destructive.

## Heed the Warning

Countries can instead sacrifice resources to protect civilians.

The exact costs depend on the active plague and can include:

- civilian industry
- Political Power
- supplies
- fuel
- equipment
- temporary production loss

Successful preparation lowers Divine Wrath and reduces future damage.

A country that consistently prepares can survive the sequence far better than a country that ignores every warning.

# The Ten Plagues

## I. Water into Blood

Freshwater begins turning into a blood-like substance across the world.

Rivers, reservoirs, lakes, wells, and other water sources become unusable or dangerous.

Effects include:

- water shortages
- reduced supply efficiency
- factory disruption
- civilian sickness
- Stability loss
- reduced Food Security
- higher consumer-goods pressure

The Nile receives special visual and news treatment.

The first plague causes some deaths through contaminated water, failed sanitation, and secondary sickness, but it is still among the less deadly stages.

## II. Frogs

Enormous numbers of frogs emerge from waterways and enter:

- cities
- factories
- homes
- government buildings
- farms
- military bases
- supply depots

Effects include:

- production disruption
- infrastructure disruption
- supply penalties
- civilian cleanup costs
- temporary building disruption

When the plague ends, huge numbers of frogs die at once and create additional sanitation problems.

Direct mortality is limited, but secondary disease and accidents can still cause some real deaths.

## III. Lice and Gnats

Mass infestations begin affecting people and animals.

Effects include:

- reduced division recovery
- reduced organization recovery
- reduced factory output
- reduced construction
- civilian sickness
- temporary manpower inefficiency

This plague can begin causing more noticeable mortality in already weakened countries.

## IV. Flies

Immense swarms of flies cover populated areas.

They contaminate food, worsen sanitation, and spread disease.

Effects include:

- stronger civilian sickness
- reduced Food Security
- supply losses
- production disruption
- Stability loss
- increased disease pressure

Countries already weakened by the earlier plagues can suffer significant real population loss.

Those deaths feed the shared Deaths system.

## V. Pestilence of Livestock

Livestock populations begin dying on a global scale.

This damages agriculture, food supply, and rural economies.

Effects include:

- major Food Security loss
- rural economic damage
- agricultural disruption
- supply penalties
- increased famine risk

The plague itself kills animals rather than directly targeting human population.

Its major human mortality comes through food shortages, disease, and famine.

The existing **Famine** system owns genuine famine progression and famine mortality.

All resulting population deaths still feed the shared Deaths system.

## VI. Boils

A painful and highly disruptive disease begins spreading among civilians and soldiers.

Effects include:

- reduced factory output
- reduced construction
- reduced division organization
- reduced recovery
- slower training
- increased attrition
- real civilian deaths

Severe outbreaks can kill large numbers of people, especially in states already weakened by hunger, sanitation collapse, or earlier disease.

This plague should interact with existing Chaos Redux disease infrastructure rather than creating a duplicate generic disease framework.

All actual civilian deaths feed the shared Deaths system.

## VII. Hail and Fire

A supernatural storm of enormous hailstones, lightning, and fire sweeps across the world.

This is one of the most physically destructive plagues.

Affected states can suffer:

- civilian factory damage
- military factory damage
- infrastructure damage
- railway damage
- air-base damage
- crop destruction
- equipment losses
- division damage
- real civilian population loss

The storm should cause significant direct mortality.

High-Divine-Wrath countries can suffer catastrophic state-level losses.

Deaths caused by hail, fire, collapsed buildings, and destroyed settlements feed the shared Deaths system.

## VIII. Locusts

Immense locust swarms consume what remains of the world's crops.

The plague moves across states rather than existing only as one national modifier.

Effects include:

- massive Food Security loss
- crop destruction
- worsening famine
- supply penalties
- rural economic collapse

States already devastated by Hail and Fire are especially vulnerable because the locusts consume what survived.

The seventh and eighth plagues should combine into one of the event's largest indirect mortality phases through famine and infrastructure collapse.

Famine deaths are owned by the existing Famine system and feed the Deaths system.

## IX. Darkness

The world falls into supernatural darkness.

For several days, sunlight effectively disappears.

Effects include severe penalties to:

- air operations
- air detection
- naval detection
- factory output
- construction
- movement
- planning
- civilian morale

The darkness should receive a strong global visual treatment rather than functioning as an ordinary extended night.

Deaths can occur from:

- accidents
- infrastructure failures
- exposure
- violence
- collapse of already damaged civilian services

The final warning arrives during this plague.

Every government knows what is expected next.

# Preparing for the Tenth Plague

During the Darkness phase, every country receives access to a final preparation.

## Observe the Passover

A country can order emergency preparations inspired directly by the biblical Passover.

The decision requires a meaningful civilian and supply commitment.

Countries that complete it receive strong protection against the tenth plague.

The decision is available regardless of religion or ideology.

Governments may present it as:

- religious observance
- obedience to the biblical warning
- supernatural emergency procedure
- a desperate precaution
- an unexplained ritual that appears to work

Countries can refuse.

Countries with high Divine Wrath that reject the final warning face the greatest possible losses.

## X. Death of the Firstborn

The tenth plague occurs simultaneously across the world.

Firstborn people die during the night.

This is the single largest direct mortality spike of the event.

It causes a **large one-time real population loss** in every affected country.

The exact death toll scales strongly with:

- Divine Wrath
- Passover preparation
- earlier protective measures
- accumulated national preparedness

Countries with low Divine Wrath and completed preparations can reduce the losses enormously.

Countries with extreme Divine Wrath that ignored the warnings can lose a devastating share of their population.

The plague can also kill named characters, including:

- political leaders
- heirs
- generals
- admirals
- ministers
- other prominent figures

Character deaths should follow valid character state rather than blindly killing every firstborn conceptually possible.

Every civilian death caused by the tenth plague feeds the shared **Deaths system**.

## Mourning

After the tenth plague, every country enters a major mourning period.

Possible effects include:

- severe temporary Stability loss
- War Support loss
- production disruption
- government crises
- religious revival
- panic
- political blame
- investigations
- demands for reconstruction

The immediate sequence ends unless Evolution III allows the cycle to begin again.

## Demographic impact

The Ten Plagues should produce major real population loss.

The most important mortality sources are:

- contaminated water and disease
- flies and sanitation collapse
- famine after livestock pestilence
- boils
- hail and fire
- famine after locust devastation
- infrastructure collapse during Darkness
- Death of the Firstborn

The tenth plague is the largest single spike.

The cumulative deaths from the earlier plagues can still be enormous.

The event should not represent mass death only through Stability, manpower, or temporary modifiers.

Actual state population is removed, and every valid death is reported to the shared Deaths system.

# Evolutions

Because Event 085 is Chaos level 3, its Evolutions begin at 600 Chaos.

## Evolution I: Plagues Upon Plagues

- Chaos requirement: `600+`.

The physical manifestations become much more severe and the plagues begin overlapping.

A plague no longer has to disappear before the next begins.

Examples include:

- Water into Blood still damaging sanitation when frogs arrive
- flies remaining during livestock pestilence
- boils continuing during Hail and Fire
- locusts arriving while farmland is still burning
- famine continuing through the Darkness

Regional intensifications also become stronger.

Examples include:

- entire reservoirs turning completely red
- frog swarms occupying major cities
- enormous insect clouds
- severe livestock die-offs
- major military boil outbreaks
- destructive supernatural thunderstorms
- locust fronts crossing several countries

Divine Wrath has a stronger effect on severity.

Mortality rises significantly because several unresolved crises can kill population at the same time.

## Evolution II: No Land Is Spared

- Chaos requirement: `800+`.

The plagues become close to geographically comprehensive.

Almost every populated state can be struck rather than only representative parts of each country.

High-Wrath countries can suffer:

- multiple Hail and Fire strikes
- enormous locust coverage
- widespread boil outbreaks
- severe infrastructure destruction
- extreme famine
- much higher tenth-plague mortality

The world also begins reacting through mass flight.

### The Exodus

Large civilian populations attempt to leave countries and regions believed to be cursed or doomed.

The existing **Migration** system owns these population movements.

Low-Wrath countries can become destinations for enormous refugee flows.

High-Wrath countries can experience mass departures.

Governments can:

- accept refugees
- restrict movement
- close borders
- establish camps
- create evacuation corridors
- cooperate internationally

The migration can also carry food pressure, disease, and political panic into countries that otherwise prepared well.

## Evolution III: Wrath Without End

- Chaos requirement: `1000+`.

The end of the tenth plague no longer guarantees that the catastrophe is over.

If global Divine Wrath remains extremely high after Death of the Firstborn, the first signs begin again.

Water starts turning red.

The full ten-plague sequence can restart at greater severity.

The biblical order always remains:

1. Blood
2. Frogs
3. Lice or gnats
4. Flies
5. Pestilence
6. Boils
7. Hail and Fire
8. Locusts
9. Darkness
10. Death of the Firstborn

Every completed cycle can increase:

- plague intensity
- infrastructure destruction
- disease pressure
- famine severity
- direct mortality
- tenth-plague mortality

This makes the world-end branch eligible.

# World-End Scenario: The Plagues Without End

Requirements:

- global Chaos above `1000`
- Evolution III active
- all ten plagues completed at least once
- global Divine Wrath remains extremely high
- world-end branches enabled

The ten plagues begin cycling repeatedly.

Each cycle becomes harder to survive.

The world-end does not invent an eleventh plague.

Its threat comes from the biblical ten plagues repeating until civilization can no longer recover between cycles.

Repeated cycles can cause:

- enormous cumulative population loss
- famine
- destroyed agriculture
- collapsing industry
- shattered infrastructure
- disease
- mass migration
- political collapse
- repeated Death of the Firstborn mortality events

All population deaths continue feeding the shared Deaths system.

## Ending the world-end

The cycle can still be stopped.

Countries must collectively reduce global Divine Wrath through:

- civilian protection
- relief
- international cooperation
- successful plague preparations
- avoiding repeated Harden Our Hearts choices

If enough of the world lowers Divine Wrath and survives an entire cycle without pushing it back above the critical threshold, the sequence finally ends.

A world that continues hardening its heart can remain trapped in repeated plague cycles until civilization is barely functional.

# Triggerable scenario: The Ten Plagues

The manual scenario begins the catastrophe immediately rather than waiting for Event 085 to fire naturally.

Scenario intensity controls:

- starting Divine Wrath
- plague severity
- plague overlap
- geographic coverage
- mortality
- Exodus migration
- whether Wrath Without End is already eligible

Low intensity begins close to baseline.

Medium intensity begins with stronger overlap and higher Wrath.

High intensity begins near Evolution II conditions.

Maximum intensity begins near world-end conditions with widespread destruction, very high Divine Wrath, and repeated-cycle eligibility.

- plague sequencing

- Harden Our Hearts
