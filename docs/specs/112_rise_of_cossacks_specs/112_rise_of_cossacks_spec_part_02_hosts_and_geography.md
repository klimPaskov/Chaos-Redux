# Hosts, regions, and territorial commitments

Each host tradition changes the political bargain that sustains the army.
The traditions are historical reference points for a new twentieth-century movement.
Their original institutions do not survive intact merely because a state changes hands.
Military households, civilian neighbors, existing national movements, exiles, and the current government all have interests in the proposed revival.

The historical basis and source qualifications are collected in the research notes.
This import retains all twelve host families and sixteen geographic cohorts from the supplied design.
The historical parent acceptance record covers the P01 to P14 route proposals; this import does not extend that record into blanket approval of every host, geographic, or supporting gameplay detail.
See the [historical disposition record](../../plans/112_rise_of_cossacks_plans/imported_review/112_rise_of_cossacks_improvement_and_closure.md) and part 13 for the acceptance and implementation boundaries.
Working host labels are identifiers for content, rather than finished country localisation.

## The tradition registry

The registry contains twelve distinct content families.
A country has one founding tradition and can adopt at most two additional military or civil traditions at a time.
Adoption requires a real relationship, a paid training or institutional program, and a political settlement with the represented host.
An annexation does not automatically transfer its tradition.
Replacing an adopted tradition takes a retraining period and preserves the earlier diplomatic history.

The registry does not imply twelve new country tags.
Existing carriers and national identities have priority.
Some answers are autonomous districts, volunteers, military missions, or treaty partners rather than new countries.
The number of simultaneously active countries is separately bounded by the evolution director.

## Don: the military circle and its constituency

The Don module begins with a circle capable of authorizing command and service.
Its first civil choice defines who can participate in that settlement.
Established households can retain a privileged service franchise, or the host can build a territorial franchise that includes new residents and towns.
The former supplies the lower-threshold temporary compact in P01, with narrower representation and a dated review.
The latter requires the actual civilian and veteran settlement and supports the full civic outcome.
Neither option independently changes the founding grant or creates a cheaper unlisted manpower action.

A dispute over war obligations follows the first major campaign beyond the home region.
The player can negotiate a limited contingent, finance a common field army, or compel a full levy under the centralized constitution.
Each option uses actual troops and material.
Refusal leaves a host contingent unavailable for that campaign and opens an arbitration objective.
It does not instantly produce an identical rebel country or clone the refused units.

Don adoption gives another host access to a command-school project and a military-circle charter option.
Keeping both benefits requires honoring the negotiated command terms.
The Don region and its Great Military Circle provide the historical starting point, with the source's older military-rehabilitation date corrected separately in the research notes.
[Historical reference](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pagesD%5CO%5CDonregion).

## Kuban: a territorial state with infantry depth

Kuban combines a military host with a territorial political problem.
Its early program can develop an elected executive responsible to a council, a centralized ataman's administration, or a federal agreement with a larger Ukrainian or Russian state.
The governing settlement determines access to treasury reform, agricultural cooperatives, and plastun training.

The first signature objective restores a defensible route between an agricultural district, a workshop center, and a port or inland supply exit.
A host without a coast can complete the inland form of the objective.
The military payoff is a trained light-infantry formation and reliable replacement arrangements, not a copied cavalry reward.
Land arbitration remains necessary where settlement and historic expulsion created competing claims.
The host can compensate, share local administration, preserve an autonomous district, or enforce an exclusionary settlement with continuing political costs.

The Ukrainian connection and a future federal Russian connection are separate offers to actual governments.
Neither offer assumes that all Kuban residents have one national preference.
The December 1918 constitutional arrangement and the mixed mounted and plastun force structure are the historical bases.
[Constitutional reference](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CK%5CU%5CKubanPeoplesRepublic.htm).
[Military and land reference](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CK%5CU%5CKubanCossackHost).

## Dnipro: service registration and a wider republic

The Dnipro module draws on the Sich, regimental government, and the later Free Cossack territorial militia as different institutions.
They are not collapsed into one timeless constitution.
An early congress chooses the authority of the military council and the relationship between registered servicemen, territorial militia, and civil representatives.

The river program develops crossings, local workshops, reconnaissance, and protection of the towns that sustain the field army.
A river objective requires a real controlled crossing and a supplied approach on the active map.
Completing it improves operational movement and local recruitment reliability.
It does not spawn a blue-water navy on the Dnipro.

The national project can join or reform an existing Ukraine through agreement, or establish Ukrainian statehood when its legitimate carrier is available.
A surviving Ukrainian government has agency and retains its own content.
A military coup, a federal settlement, and a voluntary union have different opposition and aftermath.
The 1917 Free Cossacks support territorial organization and elected officers as historical inspiration.
[Historical reference](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CF%5CR%5CFreeCossacks.htm).

## Sloboda: from regimental districts to workshops

The Sloboda module connects a regimental territorial inheritance with an industrial twentieth-century setting.
It can become the modernization center of a wider host federation or the industrial base of a centralized state.
Local regiments request a voice over recruitment and regional expenditure.
The player can preserve those rights, standardize them through a national service law, or negotiate a temporary wartime pooling arrangement.

Its signature objective repairs and supplies a workshop and railway pair while training a replacement cadre.
The reward is production continuity, a training improvement, and the ability to convert selected mounted formations into motorized units using real trucks.
An evacuation alternative preserves machinery and trained personnel at a measured cost if the district is lost.
Only the equipment and people that actually leave arrive at the destination.

The five historical regimental areas are reference anchors for the content and map research.
Their full early-modern extent is not a twentieth-century core grant.
[Historical reference](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CS%5CL%5CSlobidskaUkraine.htm).

## Terek: land, water, and a negotiated frontier

The Terek module requires a settlement with local non-Cossack counterparts before durable regional integration.
Those counterparts represent actual local political interests and are not a single interchangeable mountain faction.
Where an existing state or autonomous carrier represents a community, negotiations use that actor.
Where none exists, an institutional local compact records the named district and its civil terms without creating a gratuitous country.

The first program repairs a vulnerable river or road system and settles access to land and water.
Its cost includes equipment, construction commitment, and time during which forces are unavailable for another project.
Upstream improvements can cause an explicit downstream dispute if the plan ignores the second district.
The player can revise the work, compensate the affected community, or accept persistent local opposition.

The payoff is a defensible transport corridor and dependable local supply.
Mountain liaison enables mixed-force training after an actual agreement.
It does not turn all Caucasus territory into a host homeland.
The historical Terek-Dagestan coalition and research on river management support these themes.
[Political reference](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CT%5CE%5CTerekregion.htm).
[Environmental history](https://www.environmentandsociety.org/sites/default/files/key_docs/barrett-5-1.pdf).

## Yaik and Ural: the service economy

The Ural module ties household military obligations to control of a communal resource.
A seasonal service calendar can preserve the productive base while providing smaller reliable contingents.
A permanent levy gives the government more immediate soldiers and reduces the labor available to the fisheries and associated transport.
Opening access to nonmember residents can increase civil support after a compensation and administration program, while older households demand protection of their service bargain.

The signature objective restores a working river economy and establishes a patrol against military seizure or smuggling.
The game represents its economic return through native civilian production and supply effects.
Fish are not a new stockpiled currency.
A blockade, loss of river access, or excessive requisition suspends the economic benefit and creates an attainable recovery objective.

The nineteenth-century account used here describes communal regulation but also carries the rhetoric of its colonial setting.
Its claims of perfect social harmony are not adopted as a neutral description.
[Contemporary reference](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Popular_Science_Monthly/Volume_43/October_1893/The_Ural_Cossacks_and_their_Fisheries).

## Orenburg: a gate into the steppe

Orenburg creates a choice between a military frontier command and a commercial gateway negotiated with neighboring governments.
Its service settlements need a functioning rail or river exit and agreements over movement across the steppe.
The signature mission establishes a protected supply route through a named junction and a consenting neighboring district or state.
An armed incursion cannot stand in for the consent requirement on the cooperative route.

The military command route receives quicker fortification and concentrated training at the cost of civil representation.
The commercial route develops repair depots, transit agreements, and access to technology or industry through actual treaties.
Either route can support a later Russian or steppe project.
Neighboring Kazakh and Bashkir political actors evaluate the proposed terms independently.

Museum holdings confirm Orenburg's distinct host identity and material traditions.
Exact regalia remain a production research task.
[Museum reference](https://ar.culture.ru/en/subject/znak-orenburgskogo-kazachego-voyska).

## Siberian and Irtysh: the price of distance

The Siberian module specializes in maintaining dispersed stations and reinforcing distant garrisons.
Its geography makes supplied connections more valuable than an uninterrupted painted map.
The player can negotiate transit with existing states, establish a federation of separate hosts, or attempt expensive direct administration.

The signature mission secures two named transport anchors and delivers an actual reinforcement convoy between them.
The convoy represents reserved equipment and personnel, with source and destination receipts.
Failure returns or loses only the resources whose route outcome warrants that result.
The successful program improves station repair, rotation of service, and small-unit deployment.

A federal Siberian partner can remain politically independent while contributing to joint defense.
Its charter must state who controls railways, pays the garrison, and can refuse a war outside the agreed region.
The historical service host and its surviving regalia provide the reference base.
[Museum reference](https://ar.culture.ru/en/subject/znamya-polka-sibirskogo-kazachego-voyska).

## Transbaikal, Amur, and Ussuri: patrons and railway authority

This family preserves separate regional identities within one bounded eastern content module.
It does not declare the three hosts to have been one homogeneous polity.
An answering organization can be based in one region and seek a compact with the others.

Foreign aid comes with a written military or transport obligation.
Accepting it can make an isolated host viable, but a patron may demand access, command influence, or participation in a distant war.
The player can meet the obligation, renegotiate it, repay a defined liability, or rupture the agreement.
The rupture does not multiply the aid already received or erase the patron's claims.

The signature objective protects a railway shipment and settles who can inspect, tax, or escort it.
An eastern host can become a constitutional railway federation or a personal military regime.
The National Archives account of the intervention illustrates the concrete conflict between atamans, foreign forces, and railway traffic.
[Archival history](https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2002/winter/us-army-in-russia).

## Danubian Sich: a refuge with conditions

The Danubian route is available through a consenting refuge, actual migration, or a historic-region answer.
It requires a valid receiving territory and preserves the receiving country's agency.
A sovereign country appears there only if the territorial agreement explicitly grants one.
Otherwise the result is a protected community, a host contingent, or an autonomous district.

The route centers on the compatibility of foreign service with the community's refuge terms.
A patron can request a campaign that the host regards as outside those terms.
The player can agree, negotiate a different contribution, or seek another refuge.
A patron switch requires notice, transport capacity, and settlement of the old commitment.
The route rewards coastal logistics, civilian survival, and negotiated autonomy.

The historical Sich's movements and the destructive consequences of the 1828 rupture provide the relevant memory.
[Historical reference](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/pages/D/A/DanubianSich.htm).

## Nekrasovite communities: continuity and selective return

This is a distinct community route with its own religious and communal concerns.
The founding host cannot simply annex its members into a military levy.
A return offer specifies freedom of worship, local self-government, land access, and whether service is voluntary.
The community can accept a limited civilian return, send volunteers, establish cultural ties, or refuse.

The signature objective prepares a receiving district and completes a paid, measured transfer.
The reward is the delivered population, a local institution, and the possibility of a consent-based tradition program.
A promise broken after arrival damages legitimacy and can trigger a departure request through the same conservation rules.
Expulsion is not an efficient shortcut to integration.

The Nekrasovite and Danubian routes can interact, but their historical conflicts and separate traditions remain visible in the design.
[Research reference](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/kadim/article/877890).

## New frontier host: a contemporary foundation

A host arising outside the strongest historic centers begins with local veterans, territorial militia, and a provisional service agreement.
It uses the region's present civilian and linguistic context in its public identity.
Its first congress chooses a command institution and a civic settlement, with no assertion that a historic host owned the state.

The signature objective binds the capital, one recruiting district, and one functioning supply exit into a viable administration.
The host can then choose a researched tradition through contact, study, returning volunteers, or a military mission.
That adoption produces a hybrid institution and records its origin.
It does not rewrite the founding population's ancestry.
This family is particularly useful for a Belarusian or northern Russian opening that later chooses a local republic, a federation, or a broader national project.

## Counting territory and partners

Direct-state formation requires ownership and control of every selected required core anchor.
Occupation alone can support a war objective but cannot complete a permanent constitutional formation.
A subject's state counts only on a route that expressly permits that subject relationship and only after the subject's consent receipt.
An ally's state never counts as the player's owned territory.
A confederation may instead satisfy a partner-anchor requirement through an independent signatory, with the named charter and a valid supply or transit relationship.

No subject or ally can be counted twice through two nested treaty chains.
A broken charter makes its anchor unmet immediately in the puzzle and suspends the benefits tied to that charter.
It does not instantly revoke an unrelated previously integrated state's core.
A selected alternative set remains selected when it becomes invalid, allowing the player to understand and repair the failed requirement.
Changing sets has a visible administrative transition and cannot farm formation rewards.

## Tradition adoption and conflicts

Every tradition agreement records its instructor or institutional source, cost, training location, required partner terms, and expiration or renewal rules.
The first additional tradition normally requires an established host, a functioning training site, a completed relationship objective, and a 120 to 180 day program.
The second requires a mature military or civic institution and a larger material contribution.
The same compatible bonus cannot stack through two differently named traditions.

Centralized standardized service can coexist with a regional tactical tradition.
It cannot simultaneously promise unrestricted local refusal of service under a confederal charter.
A civilian-equality settlement can coexist with an elected military circle.
It cannot retain an exclusive hereditary voting franchise without reopening the constitutional settlement.
These conflicts produce specific reform choices and obligations through the two existing public values.
