# Part 05. Great projects, national transformations, and durable endings

## Purpose and scale

A host can survive, modernize, and reach a satisfying regional settlement without declaring a new empire.
The great projects offer optional campaigns after that achievement.
Each changes the relationship between army, inhabitants, allied hosts, and neighboring states.
A new name, flag, capital, or national focus branch is the presentation of a completed political change.
Those changes do not substitute for the territorial, military, economic, and diplomatic work described here.

Every name in this document is a working design label.
None is final localisation.
The route codes are specification references, not allocated runtime IDs.

The historical foundations for the Golden Horde, Russian popular rebellion, Ukrainian councils, and host institutions are separated from the invented twentieth century projects in the research notes.
No route claims that all Cossacks historically shared a national program.
No route makes another population Cossack by occupying its state.

## Common rules for all projects

### Selection, commitment, and continuity

The host can examine every discovered project and select one as its current principal project.
It may simultaneously maintain one regional program and one practical migration, arsenal, or military program.
The decision interface exposes only the relevant actions and at most three active mission objectives.
The complete project index remains reachable without activating every project's rows.

Selection alone has no cost and grants no country modifier.
Starting a project's first funded preparation creates its commitment and receipt.
Changing the principal project before a constitutional settlement costs 50 Political Power and closes the unfinished project's future preparation benefits.
Actual completed buildings, trained formations, delivered equipment, negotiated property rights, and civilian safety obligations persist.
There is a 180-day cooldown before another elective project change.
An externally invalidated project can be suspended without that elective penalty.

A completed national transformation cannot be farmed by returning to its old label.
Its identity, completion receipt, state integration history, and reward receipt survive changes of government.
A later constitutional reform can change institutions and a compatible presentation.
It does not repeat founding manpower, country initialization, technology grants, cores, industry, chaos effects, or achievements.

The common entry expectation is an established host, a ratified charter, a functioning arms source, and a controllable home state.
The larger projects require at least 180 days since the rising.
Their final declarations require at least 365 days since the rising, unless a preexisting participating state already possesses the accepted institutions and receives a specific audited continuity adapter.
That exception preserves genuine prior work and never creates a shortcut from an empty carrier.

### Common state proof

Part 02 supplies the concrete geographic cohorts.
The implementation must bind them to the actual installed state map and record ownership, control, alternate groups, subject terms, and treaty evidence separately.
The selected project uses the shared state puzzle for the exact live predicate.
A required cohort is unmet when its valid alternative is absent or its treaty has lapsed.
The project remains visible with an explanation.

A direct sovereign formation needs the host to own and control its mandatory national capital anchor.
A confederal formation may count a member's own anchor only under an accepted constitutional charter that actually supplies the relevant representation, military commitment, and transit.
Ordinary faction membership, military access, a puppet label, or an expeditionary unit alone is insufficient.
A host cannot count the same partner twice through nested alliances.

Claims express an announced political demand.
Cores require the state's separate integration settlement and are not awarded across an entire historical empire.
The founding core remains intact.
A legitimate member that retains sovereignty keeps its own cores.
The principal host gains no core on that member merely because it chairs the federation.

### Common political proof

Final national settlements need Host Cohesion at least 60 and Settlement Legitimacy at least 60 unless the route states a higher requirement.
No active civilian-protection breach or overdue accepted constitutional demand may be hidden by high meter values.
A government must settle or explicitly reject such a demand before it claims completion.

A hostile or unrecognized route can still be played and can win wars.
It receives a contested-proclamation status until it meets the relevant political proof.
That status opens its own diplomacy, opposition, and compromise content.
It does not produce the full formation reward early.

The two meters are not payments.
A gate at 65 Cohesion means the army accepts the proposed settlement.
The decision does not deduct 65 points.

### Finite constitutional endorsement

A higher national political gate must be attainable by completing that project's own real constitutional work.
It must not require deliberate late payments, unrelated conquest or postponing an earlier earned reward.
Within the already funded final constitutional mission, the host can settle one finite endorsement receipt after every required mandate, physical work, service obligation and stated compliance interval is complete.
The final constitutional mission may begin at the minimum baseline in the table once its other start requirements pass.
A higher branch meter threshold is a final formation condition, not a circular prerequisite for starting the mission that earns endorsement.
Its current meters must first reach the ordinary recovery baseline, which is 60 in each meter unless the branch's own lower threshold applies.
An active material breach blocks endorsement.

The endorsement credits only the positive remaining gap to the chosen branch's exact gate, with at most 10 points in either meter.
It does not lower a higher value.
Earlier increases from the same political mandate count inside that budget, rather than being awarded again.
The receipt is marked used for this great project across all later constitutional variants, interruptions, country identities and restorations.
A subsequent political loss is not healed by replaying endorsement.
Receipt-bound repair of the actual loss and new legitimate work remain available under the ordinary rules.

### Common costs and rewards

The project costs below are proposed base costs before any accepted shared cost modifier.
Each action must display its actual quote and deduct that quote once.
Construction, equipment deliveries, and native unit training continue to consume their ordinary resources.
A stated preparation duration is not a promise of passive success.
The associated mission checks its real objective and completes automatically when that objective is achieved.

A project can replace one of the three focus-created national spirit slots.
It cannot add an unlimited collection of permanent national bonuses.
Formation itself normally grants a political settlement, a new diplomatic option, appropriate presentation, and one bounded institutional improvement.
It grants no new army.

A completed project retains postwar obligations.
The host must demobilize or finance its army, settle veteran and civilian claims, maintain its agreements, and survive leadership succession.
These are consequences of the chosen institutions, with bounded event chains and clear closure.

## GP01. Union of Hosts

### Entry and preparation

The principal host has a confederated or civilian charter, or a professional ataman willing to submit common command to a federal mandate.
It has direct diplomatic contact with two other genuine host communities.
At least one prospective member must be a living country or a politically organized host outside the principal host's original state.
Three names assigned to subdivisions of one army do not qualify.

A Congress of Hosts preparation costs 75 Political Power and opens a 150-day mission.
The mission needs representatives from three hosts, an agreed meeting location under secure control, and a published draft allocating defense, revenue, and dispute resolution.
Each invitation identifies what the invitee is being asked to contribute.
A refusal does not spend the invitee's resources or create a fake delegate.
The principal host may seek another qualified member or improve the offer.

The first congress offers two institutional settlements.
A league compact leaves taxation and recruitment local and requires assent for each external war.
A federal compact delegates defined defense expenditure and a common military staff while reserving civilian administration and ordinary domestic law.
The player sees which existing promises must change before choosing.

### The playable bargain

The league is inexpensive to maintain and attractive to small hosts.
Its mobilization requires separate member decisions.
It gains dependable defensive cooperation only when the threat falls inside the agreed protected area.
A member that declines an unrelated offensive has honored the compact.

The federal compact can maintain a standing common formation and coordinated procurement.
It requires an actual equipment and manpower contribution agreement.
Each contribution is measured and delivered from the contributing member.
The central host cannot issue an order that drains every member's stockpile.

A common staff project costs 60 Political Power and 20 Army Experience and takes 120 days.
Its objective is one joint exercise involving at least two participating countries or their specifically transferred contingents, while both retain viable home defense.
It unlocks shared training and a single coordinated operation request.
It provides at most 5 percent planning or coordination benefit through the institutional spirit slot, subject to engine binding.
It does not increase every combat statistic.

### Recognition and formation

The full Union needs three ratified host charters across at least two regional cohorts, Cohesion 65, Legitimacy 65, and 180 days without a material federal breach.
The principal host must have honored at least one actual defense, procurement, or dispute-settlement obligation.
Final ratification costs 100 Political Power and takes a 180-day constitutional mission.

Recognition is sought separately from neighbors.
A recognized union receives ordinary agreements according to the actual diplomatic system.
A rejected union can still exist.
It receives a recorded claimant dispute and must defend or negotiate its borders.

No member changes owner or tag through ratification.
Voluntary sovereign merger is a separate unanimous action.
That action must reconcile capitals, leaders, focus continuity, armies, technology policy, debts, and state integration before any physical merge.
The mere existence of this route does not authorize a generic annex-all-members effect.

### Crisis, failure, and late play

The first major offensive outside the common defensive area tests the charter.
The central government can request contributions, pay for volunteers, or fight with its own army.
It can attempt emergency compulsion only through a visible constitutional breach with member responses.
The breach uses the shared one-time Cohesion minus 10 and Legitimacy minus 8 result and can suspend the affected member's cooperation.
The same broken federal promise cannot also receive a second regional or generic breach penalty.
Members can demand arbitration, reduce participation, or leave according to their signed terms.

A member facing defeat may ask for evacuation instead of indefinite defense.
The union can save its people and preserve a government in exile without creating replacement population.
Failure to protect an impossible position does not automatically destroy every other treaty.

After victory, the union decides whether common command returns to the hosts or remains through a ratified amendment.
A successful peaceful demobilization replaces the temporary 5 percent emergency coordination benefit with one sustainable 3 percent version of the same accepted native coordination modifier in the same spirit slot.
If native scope requires the documented program-cost binding, the same 5-to-3 percentage replacement applies to that single accepted program component.
The two versions do not stack.
This is the central late-game reward.
The alliance becomes capable of surviving a peace and the departure of its founding ataman.

The AI favors the union when two or more viable partners can be connected and no member must surrender its whole identity.
It prefers a limited league over an impossible federation.
A militarily strong AI does not choose compulsion solely because it can defeat its prospective partners.

## GP02. Cossack Russia

### Historical and fictional basis

The project draws on the tension between Cossack autonomy, service to a Russian state, and popular rebellions that appealed to groups with different interests.
Pugachev's surviving proclamations combined a claimed imperial identity with promises of status and material rights and with coercive demands.
That history supports a political dilemma for a new service order.
It does not supply a living eighteenth century ruler, a legitimate modern dynasty, or a ready-made democratic program.
The present route is an alternate history national project.
See the [translated Pugachev proclamations](https://home.uncg.edu/~jwjones/russia/377readings/pugachev.html).

The host must decide whether Cossack identity remains a privileged estate, becomes a civic service tradition open to other inhabitants, or supplies the ruling cadre of a centralized military nation.
Those are incompatible answers where they allocate rights and power.
The game must represent the consequences.

### Stage one: a national appeal

Entry requires 365 days of host survival, a functioning professional army, Cohesion 60, Legitimacy 60, and either a controlled Russian heartland bridgehead or a willing Russian political counterpart.
The host may prepare the appeal from outside Moscow.
Preparation never counts as possession of the national capital.

The national congress preparation costs 100 Political Power and opens a 180-day mission.
Its objectives are a real civilian delegation from the heartland, a disclosed citizenship proposal, and a supplied route between the original host and its national campaign base.
A viable route can be a controlled land connection or a specifically accepted transit arrangement.
Disconnected map possessions do not pass.

The appeal offers former enemies three visible terms.
They can enter as equal citizens and municipalities, accept a defined military service constitution, or negotiate a protected regional relationship.
They can refuse.
A refusal gives the host a diplomatic and political problem without automatically supplying a war goal or legitimacy.

### Stage two: choose the national settlement

| Ending | Required institutional change | Main strength | Enduring cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Civic service republic | Open political membership beyond the hereditary host and place the army under an accountable national government | Wider accepted recruitment and stable integration of cities | Veterans must accept the end of exclusive political privilege |
| Military republic | Ratify a central staff, service-based promotion, a fixed command mandate, and a civilian budget procedure | Faster military standardization and coherent operational command | Mandate renewal, war finance, and officer succession remain politically dangerous |
| Popular service crown | Establish a constitutional crown or regency through a national assembly with explicit service and property guarantees | A broad symbolic compromise for monarchists, host veterans, and some rural constituencies | Dynastic legitimacy, elite resistance, and conflicting promises require continued settlement |

The civic route needs Legitimacy 70 and a completed representation reform.
It can widen ordinary recruitable access through a modest institutional modifier, capped at a proposed 0.5 percentage point increase if the installed laws and balance permit it.
That does not create new residents or instantly fill the manpower pool.
It trades away exclusive service privileges.

The military republic needs Cohesion 70, a modern staff, and a fixed review rule for emergency command.
It gains at most 5 percent training-time or organization-related benefit in the institutional slot.
The exact native modifier must be chosen as one coherent advantage.
It accepts a recurring bounded mandate review after major peace or leadership succession.

The popular service crown requires both meters at least 65 and the consent of the national assembly.
The default historical identity solution is an authentic institutional regency or vacant crown with an acting military head.
A named monarch requires a separately verified living claimant, availability, existing character ownership, and a defensible political invitation.
The design does not invent a person or certify a bloodline.
A Cossack ataman may govern under the assembly's mandate while the crown question remains unresolved.
That is a playable constitutional outcome rather than a missing feature.

The crown settlement costs 125 Political Power and 180 days.
The state must honor its first land or service guarantee before receiving its final institutional benefit.
The benefit emphasizes stability under the accepted settlement, with a proposed maximum 5 percentage point stability improvement.
It provides no claim to every land ever ruled by a Russian emperor.

### Stage three: form the successor

Final formation requires the G09 Russian heartland capital anchor at Moscow, one of the approved Tula or Ryazan alternatives, and another accepted civilian or industrial heartland anchor outside the original Don or Kuban base.
The host must own and control Moscow.
A treaty cannot substitute for sovereign control of the capital in this direct national formation.
The original host must remain viable, or its people and institutions must have been relocated through an actual accepted evacuation.

At least two required heartland states must have completed their separate civic or service settlement.
The country must have an accepted charter for each remaining required state and no ongoing civilian protection breach there.
It must also maintain 180 days of adequate access between its home and national bases.
Supply adequacy is assessed from the native supply and equipment situation rather than a custom point total.

An existing Russian or Soviet country is a real counterpart.
It may acknowledge a successor, negotiate federal union, fight, or remain a rival claimant.
The declaration does not delete its country, dissolve its wars, remove its cores, or move its leaders.
If the existing national government voluntarily merges, use a dedicated continuity transaction with its owner's agreement.

The final constitutional act costs 150 Political Power.
It changes the accepted country presentation, moves the capital only after the actual state and supply checks, unlocks the national administration group, and replaces the host institutional spirit with the chosen successor version.
It adds no divisions or factories.
The unique completion receipt prevents repeated formation rewards.

### National administration and reconstruction

The host must choose a service settlement for newly integrated industrial districts.
Territorial levies preserve local ties and reduce immediate central control.
A national professional reserve standardizes training but consumes equipment and Army Experience.
Emergency mass mobilization is available only under the normal military rules and damages the host's earlier promises when used beyond the charter.

Veterans returning to farms compete with industrial labor and reconstruction needs.
A 180-day demobilization mission can release a bounded part of the actual army according to native rules and restore civilian work.
It records the real military change.
It does not spawn a second civilian population or refund equipment that was already lost.

Land and municipal disputes resolve through representation, compensation, and service adjustments.
Coercion can maintain control while reducing Legitimacy and delaying cores.
The route never treats military victory as universal consent.

The national project also tests the original hosts.
They can retain autonomous territorial government, enter a uniform province system through agreement, or refuse centralization.
A promise of autonomy made to obtain their troops remains binding after Moscow falls.

### Failure and alternate success

A failed march on Moscow leaves an established Cossack regional state playable.
The player may abandon the national appeal, accept recognition as a regional republic, join a larger federation, or continue as a rival claimant.
The state keeps legitimately built industry and trained formations.
It loses only unfulfilled project privileges and must settle the expedition's costs.

If the national settlement loses its heartland immediately after proclamation, national reconstruction benefits suspend.
The country does not reset to an unopened Event 112.
Its flag and claimant identity can persist while the government fights for restoration or negotiates a narrower peace.

The AI undertakes this route only when it has a defensible national base, adequate equipment and fuel for its actual templates, and a plausible military or diplomatic path to Moscow.
It compares a limited regional peace against national war.
It does not convert historical ambition into a permanent high-aggression setting.

## GP03. Golden Horde revival

### Historical and fictional basis

The historical Golden Horde was a Jochid state whose core lay north of the Black and Caspian seas and whose political, commercial, and religious institutions changed over time.
Its successors cannot be reduced to one undifferentiated steppe people.
The proposed revival deliberately confronts the gap between a Cossack founding army and the legitimacy of a new order claiming that inheritance.
See [Encyclopaedia Iranica on the Golden Horde](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/golden-horde/) and [Jochi](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/joci/).

The historical state's administration and commerce supply useful design themes alongside military mobility.
The route therefore includes roads, markets, taxation, urban government, religious guarantees, and agreements with partners.
Those themes are informed by the [Oxford Nomadic Empires project](https://nomadicempires.site.ox.ac.uk/golden-horde).
Every proposed twentieth century institution below is alternate history.

A Cossack government does not become Jochid by acquiring cavalry or adopting a tamga.
A named khan requires a researched living candidate and an accepted political process.
The default is an openly provisional regency or elected leadership under a founding congress.
The imperial branch can claim historical succession while remaining contested.
It must not present an invented genealogy as fact.

### Stage one: discover a viable steppe coalition

Entry requires a Don, Yaik, Ural, Orenburg, or adopted relevant steppe tradition and a practical connection to G06 Lower Volga.
Other hosts may enter after adopting such a tradition through actual contact and training.
The project needs an established state, Cohesion 60, Legitimacy 55, and a relevant external or regional crisis.
The Hosts Answer can make contacts easier to discover.
It is not a mandatory prerequisite.

The initial diplomatic survey costs 60 Political Power and takes 120 days.
Its objective is actual contact with a Lower Volga authority and two distinct relevant political counterparts from the approved G16 list.
Possible counterparts include existing Crimean, Nogay, Bashkir, Kazakh, or Volga Tatar authorities where the campaign actually contains them.
A counterpart is a living country, a documented local authority in a relevant owned state, or another specifically modeled institution.
The game cannot create a fictional country just to fill a diplomacy slot.

Each counterpart brings a different demand.
A trading city seeks protection and reliable tolls.
A regional assembly seeks local government and representation.
A religious institution seeks credible protection.
A steppe community seeks land, migration, or service guarantees.
The content must use the actual counterpart's researched circumstances.
The player cannot satisfy every negotiation through the same payment button.

The early mission completes after three distinct mandates are received.
A mandate is an agreement to negotiate the proposed order.
It is not consent to annexation, a troop contribution, or a change of national identity.

### Stage two: settle the Lower Volga

The host needs two accepted anchor districts within G06, including an actual Astrakhan or Lower Volga administrative anchor and a second distinct district associated with the approved lower-river corridor.
The installed map binding must identify what the game represents around the historical Sarai area.
If the map does not contain a separate usable Sarai state or city, the design uses the actual administrative anchor.
It does not create a fictitious modern metropolis through a name change.

The direct state-building route must own and control its administrative capital.
The confederal route may use an agreed congress city while the city's sovereign owner retains its status.
The government screen and formation description must distinguish those outcomes.

A 180-day River and Market Settlement mission costs 90 Political Power and 300 compatible infantry equipment for the agreed security detachments.
It requires an actual completed transport or infrastructure improvement, protected access between the two anchors, and the first accepted local civilian charter.
The equipment is consumed or assigned once through the project's receipt.
The project gives no second set of fully equipped security units.

The mission's political fork concerns military finance.
Transparent fixed contributions and reciprocal market access improve civilian acceptance but limit the central government's immediate extraction.
Military tribute secures resources from consenting dependents or coercively held subjects, creates explicit obligations, and raises resistance to the new order.
Ordinary trade and construction remain the underlying economic systems.
There is no tribute-token resource.

### Stage three: choose the new order

| Settlement | Admission principle | Authority | Payoff | Principal danger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Steppe commonwealth | Negotiated membership with distinct communities retaining identity | Congress and agreed common defense | Broad voluntary cooperation, transit, markets, and a resilient coalition | Members can refuse unrelated wars or demand changes to unfair contributions |
| Imperial khanate | Central sovereignty asserted through a crown or provisional regency | A strong military government with subject charters | More direct military and fiscal coordination where control is real | Contested legitimacy, elite opposition, and recurring resistance to tribute |
| Limited Volga settlement | A regional host accepts that the imperial project exceeds its support | Existing host charter plus specific river and steppe treaties | Sustainable regional security and trade | It gives up the largest formation payoff while preserving actual achievements |

The commonwealth needs Legitimacy 70, Cohesion 60, and at least three separately ratified counterpart settlements.
At least one must be outside the principal host's original political community.
It cannot count three host-owned state councils created by one command as three independent national endorsements.

The imperial route needs Cohesion 70 and actual sovereign control of its mandatory Lower Volga base.
It also requires a congress or institution capable of authorizing its own claim and at least one genuine non Cossack political settlement.
The player can proclaim an unrecognized regency earlier, but that is a contested claim with reduced benefits.
Force alone cannot satisfy the full regional integration requirement.

Religious policy is a real constitutional issue.
The original host may include Orthodox institutions.
Potential partners may have Muslim, other religious, or secular commitments.
The route can guarantee plural institutions, establish a negotiated state religion with protected exceptions, or adopt a secular civic charter.
Each choice must identify what it changes for actual partners.
There is no conversion of whole states through a decision click.

The founding congress costs 125 Political Power and runs a 180-day mission.
The mission requires accepted membership terms, a settled capital, viable transport, and a written defense contribution rule.
It cannot complete while a required partner is actively disputing a material clause.

### Formation and territorial limits

Full formation requires the Lower Volga base, one accepted Middle Volga relationship from G07, and two distinct steppe relationships or integrated regional bases under G16.
At least one of those relationships must be a sovereign partner or an audited major regional authority with independent agency.
The commonwealth may satisfy its outer relationships through ratified federal charters.
The direct imperial formable needs ownership and control where its own sovereignty is claimed.
The selected puzzle must show which predicate each state or partner satisfies.

The final act costs 150 Political Power.
It changes the country presentation, unlocks steppe administration and common army programs, and replaces the institutional spirit.
The commonwealth's proposed institutional benefit is a modest supply or coordination advantage on agreed territory, capped at 5 percent for the chosen relevant modifier.
The imperial version may instead reduce the cost of one military standardization program while retaining its visible unresolved tribute or emergency-mandate status until the actual postwar settlement.
That status and any genuine charter breach use the exact common first-peace contract, without an additional undefined Legitimacy penalty.
It does not receive superior cavalry, research, recruitable population, and industry bonuses together.

No formation effect adds cores across Central Asia, Russia, Crimea, the Caucasus, or all former Horde territories.
Those places remain separate future diplomatic or military problems.
The route's initial claims are limited to its publicly selected and geographically justified project anchors.
Any further claim requires a new political commitment and visible response.

### Rival restorations and outside relations

A living Mongolia or the country empowered by Event 073 is an actual counterpart.
It may recognize the new order, seek a alliance, contest its succession claim, or threaten it.
The Golden Horde route must not inherit Event 073's continent-wide claims or fixed army grants.
Two successor movements can negotiate a sphere agreement without either disappearing.

Existing Crimean, Idel-Ural, Alash, Caucasian, Russian, Soviet, Turkish, or other relevant countries remain under their existing owners.
The project has no automatic entitlement to their tags or leaders.
An outside scholar or statesman can be consulted only through an era-appropriate contact.
For example, Sadri Maksudi Arsal is a researched constitutional and political reference, not an approved Jochid claimant or a character available for automatic recruitment.
His actual 1930s work in Turkey and any existing character ownership must be respected.
See the [TDV biography](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/arsal-sadri-maksudi).

Foreign powers may value access to trade, containment of rivals, or military support.
Their response depends on actual geography, ideology, wars, claims, and delivered commitments.
Recognition is never a free global opinion bonus.

### War, modernization, and the first peace

The host must convert mounted mobility into a sustainable modern army.
The commonwealth can organize partner contingents with distinct local recruitment and shared equipment standards.
The imperial route can centralize command, but taking all forces from a subject leaves its home region exposed and can violate the charter.
Motorization needs trucks and fuel.
Armor needs a real industrial or purchase program.
The historical title provides neither.

The first major peace triggers a 180-day constitutional stress test.
The commonwealth reviews unequal contributions, transit, and whether the central government kept its limited mandate.
The empire faces a tribute and service settlement with its dependent regions.
The player can reduce wartime demands, fund reconstruction, revise representation, or impose a continuing emergency.
An unsettled tribute or emergency-command obligation remains a visible unresolved mandate status until the actual parties settle it.
It blocks the dependent political benefit and further constitutional endorsement, without an automatic undefined Legitimacy drain.
An actual expired obligation repudiated through unilateral continuation uses the shared once-only charter-breach loss and can cause the affected partner to suspend participation.

An imperial succession crisis is political and finite.
It selects from verified current institutions and living accepted characters.
It does not invent a hidden heir.
The regency can become a durable constitutional executive, invite a separately researched claimant, or transform into a commonwealth after a real reform.
The last option preserves existing countries and receipts and requires settlement of affected subject terms.

Failure leaves meaningful regional outcomes.
The Lower Volga can remain a viable host state.
Trade agreements can survive a failed imperial bid.
Partners can preserve their own governments after withdrawing.
A lost restoration claim does not erase every person, state improvement, or relationship built during the campaign.

The AI prefers the commonwealth when willing partners and trade routes exist.
It considers an imperial route only when it can hold its administrative base, maintain essential garrisons, and survive the diplomatic response.
It abandons an unsupportable broad claim before sacrificing the host's entire population to a symbolic restoration.

## GP04. Ukrainian Cossack commonwealth or hetmanate

### Entry and national question

This project is available to a Dnipro host, a Black Sea or Kuban host that has established actual Ukrainian contact, or another host that has adopted the relevant tradition and earned an invitation.
Historical continuity does not remove the agency of an existing Ukrainian country.
The route distinguishes territorial civic institutions from a hereditary military estate.

A national consultation costs 75 Political Power and runs for 150 days.
The mission needs contact with the relevant Ukrainian political authority, a civic or service citizenship proposal, and a viable Dnipro base.
An existing Ukrainian government can offer federation, autonomous military service, or integration of volunteers.
It may reject a rival national project.
If it is absent, local institutions in actual controlled states provide a narrower mandate until a national assembly can be established.

The historical inspirations include Sich councils, the Free Cossacks of 1917, and the distinct political experience of the Kuban republic.
Those precedents were varied and contested.
The project does not represent one uninterrupted institution.
See the research bibliography entries for Sich Council, Free Cossacks, and Kuban People's Republic.

### Council and hetman alternatives

A council commonwealth opens political membership to inhabitants of the national territory and uses a professional army under an accountable civilian executive.
A modern hetmanate concentrates military and executive authority while defining legislative, property, and regional guarantees.
The hetmanate must choose whether the office is elected for a term, hereditary under a verified candidate, or provisional under an institutional mandate.

Pavlo Skoropadsky is a researched possible invitee for the appropriate route.
He is not installed automatically, and his return is not costless.
The country package checks his current existence, availability, other owners, and the campaign's date.
His historical dependence on outside support and conflict with competing domestic interests provide meaningful negotiation subjects.
See the [biographical entry](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CS%5CK%5CSkoropadskyPavlo).

The invitation costs 60 Political Power and opens a 120-day negotiation.
The candidate or institutional alternative must accept the actual constitutional terms.
A failed invitation leaves a playable elected or provisional office.
It cannot block the entire country because a portrait or named person is unavailable.

### National formation and modernization

Final direct formation needs the G10 Kyiv political anchor, an approved central Ukrainian anchor, and the connected G01 Dnipro base.
The host must own and control the national capital anchor.
An agreed federation with an existing Ukrainian country follows a separate continuity path and can preserve that country's carrier and meaningful national tree.
It cannot secretly replace a living country through a cosmetic change.

The commonwealth needs Legitimacy 70 and Cohesion 60.
The hetmanate needs both at least 65 plus its ratified executive mandate.
Both need two completed state settlements and a 180-day national constitution mission costing 125 Political Power.

The military program combines territorial infantry, mounted reconnaissance, artillery, engineers, and transport.
It makes room for urban industry and broad civilian citizenship.
A proposed institutional benefit reduces the time or cost of one territorial army standardization program by at most 10 percent.
It replaces the relevant spirit slot and does not grant a second permanent stack.

Kuban participation can preserve a distinct regional legislature and military traditions.
The Ukrainian government can demand more central control.
Kuban representatives can refuse, renegotiate, or choose independence.
The route must preserve that dispute rather than assuming a cultural relationship settles it.

The first peace tests land, demobilization, and the executive mandate.
If the hetman keeps emergency powers beyond the agreed term, civilian partners respond.
If councils repeatedly prevent an essential defensive mobilization, they must negotiate a narrow emergency command.
The successful ending is an institution able to manage both war and ordinary life.

## GP05. Pontic host network

This route combines coastal defense, trade, family return, and diplomatic access.
It can connect Kuban, Black Sea, Dnipro, Azov, and Danubian strands without declaring them one people or giving one host every port.

Entry needs a relevant coastal tradition and either one controlled port district or a signed host-access agreement.
A coastal compact preparation costs 60 Political Power and takes 120 days.
Its mission requires two different port authorities, a declared civilian transport policy, and an actual navigable or land route between them.

The network's first difficult choice concerns customs and military use.
Shared access improves practical movement and opens reciprocal repair.
Exclusive military rights may attract a patron while making other partners fear domination.
Each port agreement specifies who may station troops, who pays repairs, which cargoes move, and whether a partner's unrelated war can close access.

The formation gate needs two distinct G15 port districts, at least one inland host connection, and 180 days of honored passage.
One port must be under the principal host's real control or an explicitly enforceable member charter.
The other can remain a sovereign partner.
The principal state needs Legitimacy 65 and Cohesion 55.
The final compact costs 90 Political Power and creates a continuing defense and trade framework.

The military outcome uses real coastal infantry, engineers, escorts, and acquired small vessels.
The project supplies no battleships.
Only a separately verified optional R02 extraction adapter can use river or coastal access, with its actual native route, craft and equipment requirements.
Both baseline raids remain native land operations, and R01 remains ordinary infrastructure disruption.
A naval expansion beyond that scope needs native dockyards, technology, production, fuel, crews, and repair.

The first blockade tests whether the network protects civilians or uses every transport for its army.
The player may fund escorted evacuation, reroute through a willing partner, or reduce the expedition.
A port that closes access for a valid treaty reason is not automatically an enemy.
A breached agreement can be repaired through compensation and renewed guarantees.

A durable Pontic network can exist alongside a regional republic, a Union of Hosts, or a Ukrainian federation.
It cannot simultaneously enforce a sovereign claim over a partner and count that partner's voluntary treaty as unbroken.
Its enduring value is usable access and coordinated defense, not an abstract trade currency.

## GP06. Volga and Ural coalition

This project expands the Yaik coalition and Ural commons programs into a regional political settlement.
It begins from shared grievances and practical river or railway access.
Its partners can include Cossack communities, cities, peasants, and relevant Bashkir, Kazakh, or Volga authorities.
Each retains its own aims.

A coalition charter preparation costs 75 Political Power and takes 150 days.
The host needs one G08 base, one actual G07 connection, and two distinct accepted partner agreements.
At least one agreement must concern a civilian or non Cossack constituency.
The host cannot satisfy this requirement by dividing its army into renamed hosts.

The constitutional choice is between a loose alliance for defined reforms and a federal government with a common budget.
The loose alliance can dissolve peacefully after its aims are achieved.
The federation must specify taxation, courts, service, local government, and dispute resolution.
A popular rebellion that promises no future taxes cannot also receive a free modern artillery industry.

The first major project is a river and transport settlement.
It uses ordinary construction, storage, railway repair, and crossing defense.
A 180-day mission costs 60 Political Power and 150 support equipment and requires a completed transport improvement plus fulfillment of the agreed navigation or land guarantee.
The military design is a combined territorial army with mounted patrols and a small trained mobile reserve.

Final federation requires Cohesion 60, Legitimacy 70, three ratified regional charters, and 180 days of contribution and transit compliance.
The final act costs 100 Political Power.
The institutional payoff supports administration and defensive coordination through one modest replacement modifier.
Separate member countries retain their own cores and identity until they explicitly merge.

The main failure occurs after victory.
Different allies consider different promises fulfilled.
The player can let a partner leave under the charter, negotiate a new purpose, or breach the compact.
Permitting a lawful exit reduces the coalition's size while preserving its legitimacy.
Forced retention creates an explicit dispute and may prevent the final federal reward.

This route can remain a regional endpoint.
It can also supply a foundation for a later Golden Horde or Cossack Russia project.
That later turn requires new consent where its national or imperial demands exceed the coalition's original terms.
Earlier guarantees do not vanish.

## GP07. Eastern host chain

The eastern project begins with the Siberian service and trade program.
The founding Event 112 still occurred in East Slavia.
A western Siberian or eastern connection is earned through territory, a partner, or a real transit corridor.

The first network mission requires a G12 Omsk and Irtysh base and one actual station or transport connection maintained by an accepted partner.
It costs 75 Political Power and 200 support equipment and allows 180 days.
Its completion proves a functioning corridor and defended settlements.
It does not teleport troops across unowned Siberia.

A later extension may contact a G13 Transbaikal, Amur, or Ussuri community.
Those communities remain distinct.
The player chooses one corridor to support before taking on another.
Each extension requires a viable railway or maritime route, a receiving authority, and a sustainable home garrison.

The principal political dilemma is command over infrastructure.
A central military authority can impose common transport priorities at the cost of local flexibility.
A federated network requires bargaining over scarce rolling stock, repair crews, and troop movements.
Foreign patrons can offer supplies in exchange for defined infrastructure access.
Their capacity and obligations must be actual campaign facts.

The final network needs a functioning western base, one ratified eastern partner, two independent maintained transport links, Cohesion 60, Legitimacy 60, and 180 days without an essential access breach.
A 120 Political Power coordination act establishes the common staff and revenue agreement.
Its payoff is reliable partner transit, coordinated defense requests, and a bounded transport or repair benefit in the economic or institutional slot.

A broken line creates an operational crisis.
The host can repair it, negotiate another route, evacuate a vulnerable settlement, or contract the network.
It cannot call an unlimited number of new eastern armies to compensate.
The route tests whether long-distance ambitions remain affordable.

A mature chain must decide which settlements still require military administration.
Transferring ordinary civic functions to local governments can improve acceptance and reduce command flexibility.
Retaining permanent emergency administration can preserve central control while provoking partners.
The late campaign remains about distance, supply, and credible local government.

The AI limits commitments to routes it can use and defend.
It values a supplied western network above an impressive but disconnected map.
It does not start a war in China or annex a far eastern country simply to unlock a tradition.

## GP08. Durable regional independence, autonomy, and refuge

The game should recognize survival and institutional competence as success.
This endpoint consolidates Don civic rule, Kuban federation, Terek reciprocal settlement, Ural commons, sponsor service autonomy, or a protected Danubian refuge.

A regional independence settlement requires 365 days of survival, a viable home capital, a ratified charter, a sustainable arms source, Cohesion 60, Legitimacy 60, and no unresolved existential war.
The basic durable autonomy or refuge outcome uses the same 60 and 60 political threshold together with its actual receiving, service and protection requirements.
The route does not require conquest, a foreign volunteer gift or delaying an earlier completion reward to raise either meter above its ordinary recovery ceiling.
Recognition by the former owner is ideal but not mandatory when another defensible settlement exists.
A truce or stable border must use an actual enforceable diplomatic arrangement.
A timer alone cannot declare a war solved.

The consolidation act costs 75 Political Power and opens a 180-day mission.
The mission requires a real civilian improvement, adequate replacement equipment, and resolution of one outstanding service or property obligation.
Its reward replaces the temporary rising or emergency institution with a durable regional version.
The state receives access to its normal repeatable military and economic programs and contextual late events.

Service autonomy is a valid endpoint when its contract preserves defined local institutions.
It can use an existing supported subject relationship after the exact engine and counterpart terms are verified.
The sponsor cannot remove every local institution while the game continues to describe the outcome as autonomy.
A later reduction in autonomy must become a visible renegotiation or breach.

Protected exile is narrower.
The country may have lost its original state and accepted a real receiving authority's custody and political terms.
Its refugees and surviving troops are measured transfers.
The receiving country remains the owner of its state.
The host maintains only the political and military features supported by the installed exile or subject system.
If the engine cannot represent a playable landless host, the player receives the explicit choice to continue with an eligible receiving country or remain with the existing supported game state.
The design cannot promise a nonexistent playable country interface.

A successful return is optional.
It needs a departure agreement, a safe route, an accepted destination, and real people who survived.
The route can preserve a diaspora relationship if return becomes impossible.
A smaller protected community is a valid outcome of a failed imperial campaign.

The postwar campaign includes elections or mandate renewal, veteran pensions through ordinary costs, housing and property settlements, military reform, and responses to neighboring host crises.
There is no automatic victory screen that removes the player's country.
The host continues as a complete country with finite event content and normal strategic play.

## Completion releases active preparation

A successful project closes its active preparation and paid completion mission.
Its completed receipt remains in campaign history and its live agreements remain in the bounded obligation registry.
The host can then select a compatible next great project and pay that project's own required stages.
A completed Pontic network can therefore support a later Union, and a completed Volga-Ural coalition can support a later steppe or national project without erasing earlier rights.

A23's 50 Political Power abandonment charge and 180-day elective-change cooldown apply only to leaving funded unfinished preparation.
Selecting after an already completed compatible network does not pay an abandonment fee.
No selection change repeats an old completion, endorsement, delivery, construction or formation reward.
An incompatible national identity or constitution still needs its actual reform and continuity transaction before the new settlement can replace it.
The optional principal-country identity act for a completed network uses the active preparation place while its 120-day mission runs.

## Common first-peace contracts and priced recovery

Each full great project receives the narrative first-peace test specified in its own section.
The test becomes due after 60 consecutive peaceful days following completion with a safe home administration, using the common queue and interruption rules in part 04.
An explicitly priced P or GP contract for the same original promise remains the owner of its cost, window, reward and repair.
A shared regional and national review of that same promise combines into one objective and receipt, without charging both prices or imposing both failures.
The following defaults apply only where the original promise has no more specific complete contract.

| Project | Default action and window | Concrete review objective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GP01 Union | A20, 60 Political Power, 120 days | Ratify common command limits, honor any lawful refusal or exit and fulfill one currently due common obligation without stripping home defense |
| GP02 Cossack Russia | A21, 60 Political Power, 180 days | Complete one actual civilian or veteran reconstruction obligation and ratify the chosen national executive's review or succession rule |
| GP03 Golden Horde | A21, 60 Political Power, 180 days | Settle the due revenue, transit and military contribution accounts, preserve required civic representation and ratify the actual executive or regency succession procedure |
| GP04 Ukrainian settlement | A21, 60 Political Power, 180 days | Honor the national citizenship and regional terms, complete one actual reconstruction or property objective and ratify the council or hetman's continuing mandate |
| GP05 Pontic network | A21, 60 Political Power, 180 days | Maintain the actual passage and refuge terms, complete one port or livelihood obligation and settle any expired escort or service mandate |
| GP06 Volga and Ural federation | A21, 60 Political Power, 180 days | Fulfill the due common budget and river-maintenance terms and honor a partner's accepted reserved powers or lawful exit |
| GP07 Eastern chain | A21, 60 Political Power, 180 days | Complete one actual link-maintenance obligation, settle inspection and transport revenue, and transfer or renew the agreed station civil authority |
| GP08 Regional, autonomy or refuge | A21, 60 Political Power, 180 days | Complete the actual veteran, housing, livelihood or civilian-administration obligation and settle the host's current mandate or receiving status |

The base Political Power price funds the administrative review.
Any actual unpaid material installment remains visible in its existing contract and is paid once through that owner.
A completed building or delivered shipment is evidence, not a second hidden purchase.
A due review waits for an available mission place without expiring before it is shown.
Renewed war can suspend a civilian review under the shared rule, while immediate protection obligations remain binding.

Failure to complete an ordinary administrative review suspends only the institution or cooperation benefit that depends on it.
It does not create a new army, delete physical work or impose an unpriced generic political loss.
An actual broken charter promise uses the shared Cohesion minus 10 and Legitimacy minus 8 consequence once, measured after clamping and linked to that exact breach.
Lawful refusal, lawful exit or another actor's breach does not punish the principal as if it broke the promise.

Where no more specific repair contract applies, A19 recovery costs 60 Political Power and allows 150 days to settle the actual outstanding term and maintain the repaired agreement for 30 consecutive days.
Undelivered material is settled through its original remaining receipt, without creating equipment to pay it.
Recovery restores only the actual still-unrecovered loss from that same breach and the specific cooperation newly accepted.
It grants no positive surplus, repeated formation bonus or repeatable reward for manufactured arrears.

## Optional principal-country identities for completed regional networks

GP05, GP06 and GP07 first complete their actual networks or federations under the agreements above.
A treaty-only result retains every country's current national identity and flag.
The finite FL18 Pontic, FL19 Volga-Ural and FL20 Eastern flag families belong to the optional principal-country constitutional conversions in this section.
They are not rewards for signing a transport agreement.

After the relevant full project and its first-peace review, the principal can fund one 75 Political Power, 120-day constitutional mission.
It needs Cohesion 60, Legitimacy 60, a ratified home citizenship and service mandate, viable administration and no unresolved material breach.
The mission requires the actual principal's directly owned and controlled base in the table, with the needed transport functioning for 60 consecutive days.
Its participating counterparts must acknowledge that the new principal-country name does not enlarge their existing commitments or absorb their territory.
A refusal can leave the successful treaty network intact while this optional identity change remains unavailable.

| Identity consumer | Required direct principal-country base | Exact scope of the constitutional result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FL18 Pontic host state | Two distinct directly owned and controlled G15 port districts and the principal's actual inland host connection | Change the principal's compatible cosmetic national identity to its accepted Pontic state presentation |
| FL19 Volga-Ural host federation | At least one directly owned and controlled G07 anchor and one distinct directly owned and controlled G08 anchor, with ratified home regional representation | Change the principal's compatible identity to the accepted federation of its actual home regions |
| FL20 Eastern host federation | Two distinct directly owned and controlled station states, including the Omsk or approved western G12 base, with functioning links and ratified home regional representation | Change the principal's compatible identity to the accepted federation of its actual station regions |

Only the principal's existing country presentation changes.
There is no new tag, annexation, forced subject relation, partner flag change, new core, equipment, manpower, meter gain, research reset or repeated formation reward.
Independent participating countries retain their governments, flags and exact signed mandates.
Any later sovereign merger needs the separate actual country-continuity and consent transaction and cannot be implied by this cosmetic act.
The receipt survives ideology change and restoration, while the correct one of the four inspected native ideology bindings supplies the current flag.
A renewed constitutional change follows the ordinary reform policy rather than replaying this act for another reward.

## Acceptance criteria for route depth

Every implemented project must expose its entry, territorial predicate, political commitments, costs, mission objectives, counterpart responses, formation receipt, and first postwar test.
A player must be able to explain why a route is blocked and what action would resolve it.
The UI must show the exact required geography rather than a vague percentage of a historical empire.
A formation must remain correct through save and reload, country switching, a changed controller, a partner's annexation, and the loss of a required state during its final mission.

The initial rising, common military development, and a durable regional endpoint must remain playable if every optional great project is declined.
The Golden Horde and Cossack Russia projects must remain substantial if The Hosts Answer never activates.
The evolution expands opportunity and danger.
It does not supply the missing political consent, industrial base, or logistics needed to complete these campaigns.
