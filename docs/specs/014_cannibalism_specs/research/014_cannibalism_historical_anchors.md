# Event 014 Cannibalism research anchors

This note records research anchors used to shape the event. It is inspiration for design, not final localisation.

## Survival cannibalism under siege and starvation

The National WWII Museum article on the Siege of Leningrad describes the siege as lasting 872 days and notes severe starvation during the winter of 1941 to 1942. It also records documented cases of eating inedible materials and incidents of cannibalism. This supports the baseline framing of military and civilian starvation pressure without making the first incident cultic.

Source: https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/history-through-viewfinder-36

Useful design anchor:

- early reports should feel like starvation, logistics collapse, and second-hand horror
- ordinary people and soldiers should not be presented as cultists at the start
- survival conditions can be severe while organized ideology remains absent

## Isolated soldiers and battlefield cannibalism

The Australian War Memorial article on Buna and Giruwa describes Japanese defenders under severe food shortage and isolation, then notes that some resorted to cannibalism while fighting for survival. This supports the event's focus on cut-off garrisons, jungle or island pockets, and remote military breakdown.

Source: https://www.awm.gov.au/wartime/83/article-two

Useful design anchor:

- islands, jungle pockets, and isolated garrisons are strong locations for Evolution II
- the early system should track supply, medical collapse, ammunition shortage, and evacuation failure
- remote garrisons can become strange without immediately changing the whole world

## Forensic confirmation of survival cannibalism

The Smithsonian article on Jamestown describes forensic analysis of human remains showing evidence of survival cannibalism during the 1609 to 1610 starving time. This supports the event's evidence-chain design, where final proof comes through medical audit, bones, records, and investigators rather than instant omniscient narration.

Source: https://www.si.edu/stories/survival-cannibalism-historic-jamestown

Useful design anchor:

- hospital audits, burial ledgers, and forensic evidence can be decision and event themes
- the player should discover the truth through records and missions
- report images can focus on evidence rooms, field graves, and damaged ledgers

## War-crime records and intelligence reports

The National Archives PDF on researching Japanese war crimes includes references to Allied reports about stories of cannibalism among Japanese soldiers appearing in documents and prisoner statements. This supports the spread channels through prisoner reports, intelligence, and front-line statements.

Source: https://www.archives.gov/files/iwg/japanese-war-crimes/introductory-essays.pdf

Useful design anchor:

- POW statements and intelligence reports can reveal cult spread
- source uncertainty and rumor handling should be part of early text
- final event text should avoid sensational certainty until evidence is gathered

## hidden leader handling

The concealed supreme leader is fictional event content for Event 014. The historical anchor note should not present this character as a researched historical claim. Evolution II should seed the leader only through repeated cult behavior, shared orders, and unnamed rumours. Evolution III can reveal the unifier after network strength is sufficient.

The true leader name must not appear in pre-reveal player-facing text, Event Details, focus text, decisions, achievements, spreadsheet fields, or super-event research directions. Use neutral working labels such as hidden leader or concealed unifier in implementation notes.

If the Wendigo country exists, the hidden leader can fuse with it in an alternate world-end branch. This is a fictional high-chaos route and should be documented as terminal game content, not as historical research.

## Ethical asset constraint

The user requires gore for this event. Gore should be generated, fictional, stylized, or symbolic. Do not source real atrocity gore or identifiable victim imagery for final assets. Historical sources can guide structure, locations, documents, and mood, while fictional generated assets carry the explicit gore requirement.
