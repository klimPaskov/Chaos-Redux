# Historical and Cultural Research

This research supports structure, tone, and ethical boundaries. It does not supply pasteable player-facing prose. The event remains fictional and should never recreate a named real atrocity as spectacle.

## Wartime starvation and isolated formations

### New Guinea, 1944

The Australian War Memorial preserves a 1944 work by Ivor Hele depicting Australian soldiers discovering evidence of cannibalism in a Japanese hideout in the Suain area of New Guinea. The collection entry identifies the work as Second World War material and public domain.

Source: https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C170978

Design use:

- Remote formations can become cut off from supply, replacement, burial services, and normal command.
- Discovery should begin with physical evidence and missing personnel rather than a declared ideology.
- Period report art can emphasize jungle positions, abandoned stores, improvised cooking sites, and terrified investigators.
- The event should avoid assigning collective guilt to a nationality or army. Eligibility comes from campaign conditions and can affect any ordinary country at war.

The Australian War Memorial's account of the Japanese experience at Buna-Gona describes extreme starvation, isolation, and the breakdown of a strong taboo under survival pressure. This supports a baseline where soldiers cross the line before a cult exists.

Source: https://www.awm.gov.au/wartime/83/article-two

Design use:

- Field Hunger must be a serious mechanic tied to supply, casualties, war duration, and isolation.
- Early actions should restore logistics, rotate compromised units, secure burial details, and replace shattered officers.
- The first crisis should remain human, military, and containable.

## Settlement collapse and forensic discovery

The Smithsonian summarizes archaeological and forensic evidence for survival cannibalism during Jamestown's Starving Time. The evidence is presented through cut marks and the catastrophic mortality of an isolated settlement.

Source: https://www.si.edu/stories/survival-cannibalism-historic-jamestown

Design use:

- State incidents can be discovered through forensic patterns, missing graves, damaged bodies, and empty settlements.
- The system should distinguish evidence from rumor.
- A settlement or island can decline before it becomes an organized commune.
- The state recovery path should include burial, relief, health, and trust rebuilding rather than immediate normalization.

## Nazino and administrative abandonment

Nicolas Werth's archival study of Nazino Island describes deportees abandoned without adequate food, shelter, or tools, followed by mass death and cannibalism. The case is a warning about institutions creating starvation conditions and then losing control of the people trapped inside them.

Source: https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.12949142

Design use:

- Remote prison islands, deportation sites, military detention areas, and occupied territories can become Evolution II locations.
- An exploitation route can use prisoners and isolated populations as terror resources, with severe risk of creating autonomous cult power.
- Government policy can worsen the event even when the first incidents began spontaneously.
- The event should avoid direct recreation of Nazino's victims or administrative actors.

## Necessity, command duty, and the custom of the sea

The University of Minnesota Law Library's summary of Regina v. Dudley and Stephens records the 1884 rule that homicide was not justified by necessity outside self-defense. Its supporting materials also discuss the older custom of drawing lots at sea.

Source: https://lawlibrarycollections.umn.edu/classic-cases-criminal-regina-v-dudley-stephens

Design use:

- Early option families can distinguish survival claims from deliberate killing.
- Military commanders, ship captains, prison wardens, and garrison officers have a duty that becomes central to investigations and trials.
- Island and convoy incidents can include lots, missing junior personnel, and command complicity without quoting the case directly.
- Legal proceedings can restore Command Integrity or drive cult members underground depending on timing and evidence.

## Cannibalism, othering, and colonial imagery

Modern scholarship on cannibalism emphasizes how colonial powers used the category of the cannibal to mark other peoples as savage. This makes visual and naming discipline essential.

Reference direction: Shirley Lindenbaum, Thinking about Cannibalism, Annual Review of Anthropology.

Design use:

- Any ordinary country can become an initial host.
- Cannibal warlord art must use invented rough clothing and scavenged military material, never recognizable living cultural regalia.
- Country names should be regional public names such as a local Host or Commune direction, not racial or tribal labels.
- Warlord names must come from regional male name pools that match the portrait and state origin.
- The cult is an event-created ideology and military system, not an attribute of an ethnicity.

## Wendigo cultural guardrail

Research on contemporary horror adaptations warns that the Wendigo is often detached from its Algonquian context and turned into a generic monster. Event 14 must integrate with the existing Chaos Redux Wendigo country without pretending that the mod's creature is a faithful presentation of living Indigenous traditions.

Source: https://reinventionjournal.org/index.php/reinvention/article/download/906/792

Design use:

- Reuse the existing Chaos Redux Wendigo country's established visual and mechanical language after implementation discovers it.
- Do not add dreamcatchers, totem poles, generic headdresses, invented tribal runes, or claims about Algonquian belief.
- Do not use an Indigenous person as the monster portrait.
- The transformed portrait should depict Hannibal as a fictional supernatural creature.
- The branch should be described internally as a Chaos Redux crossover, not as anthropology.

## Asset sourcing conclusion

The core event images should be generated fictional period scenes. Real atrocity photographs should not be used.
