# Event 014 Cannibalism, Part 7: Hannibal Reveal and Unification

## Purpose of the reveal

Hannibal's appearance changes the player's understanding of the entire crisis. The repeated methods and coordinated attacks were connected. The local warlords were temporary authorities inside a network capable of producing one supreme country.

The reveal must be earned. A single cult cell at high chaos is insufficient. The network needs territory, independent actors, population consumption, logistics, and a viable military host.

## Internal identity

Hannibal Lecter is created through Event 14. No separate future Hannibal event is required.

The implementation uses an alternate-history Hannibal Lecter design. Portrait and event art should depict the character consistently without copying the likeness of a specific screen actor.

The character can exist internally during Evolution II. Before the reveal:

- no visible country leader assignment
- no visible character list entry
- no portrait reference on player-facing surfaces
- no named idea, focus, decision, achievement, or GUI element
- no music or asset filename displayed to players that contains the identity

After the reveal flag:

- character becomes public
- portrait is enabled
- global event or super-event can name him
- unified country uses him as leader
- internal hidden coordination terms can be replaced by public command terms

## Unification readiness

The reveal evaluation should combine several dimensions.

### Network scale

- multiple countries, communes, islands, or warlord states
- enough linked nodes to demonstrate cross-border command
- at least one stable route between major nodes

### Territorial scale

- controlled states with meaningful population
- at least one defensible capital candidate
- sufficient ports, rail hubs, or land corridors for a unified state

### Consumption scale

- cumulative population consumption through the shared Deaths system
- active Larder production
- enough recruitment and state control to sustain a major country

### Political scale

- warlords with high Network Alignment or enough military pressure to force resistance choices
- one viable host
- Evolution III enabled

### World scale

- Totalen Chaos or higher
- no existing terminal world-end
- no complete global defeat of Event 14

No single numeric threshold should decide the reveal. The system should use a weighted readiness model with hard minimums for actors, territory, consumption, and host viability.

## Convergence warning phase

Before the reveal, a short convergence phase gives ordinary countries a final chance to break the network.

Player-visible signs:

- several warlords move toward one campaign objective
- courier routes converge on one region
- aligned cells stop competing for victims
- identical rank changes appear in separate armies
- communes transfer prisoners and equipment toward a host
- warlords execute local rivals who reject shared orders

The warning phase can create a global mission to break the convergence. It must not name Hannibal.

Possible counterplay:

- defeat the likely host
- force warlords to defect or resist
- destroy a network capital
- reduce Network Reach
- liberate enough feeding states
- seize courier hubs

Success delays or cancels the reveal until readiness is rebuilt.

## Host selection

The host country becomes the core of the unified state.

Selection order:

1. Preserve a player-controlled cannibal country where map safety allows it.
2. Prefer a country with a valid capital, supply, and contiguous core.
3. Score controlled population, Larder, military strength, submitted warlords, ports, rail access, and Network Alignment.
4. Avoid a host that is about to capitulate unless the reveal is a deliberate last-resort transfer.
5. Avoid an isolated one-state host when a stronger connected actor exists.
6. Preserve route identity and characters from the chosen host.

If a player controls the existing Wendigo country and the alternate branch qualifies, the merge rules in Part 8 take precedence.

## Reveal event package

### Player feeling

The moment should feel like the cult network has become a state and its hidden command has stepped into public view. The strategy remains intelligent and predatory, but the visual presentation is violently unhinged: Hannibal Lecter commands a convergence already breaking barricades and chasing civilians rather than posing before a council. Avoid generic apocalypse wording and avoid turning him into a comic pun.

### Information revealed

- Hannibal's name and portrait
- the strongest host and capital
- existence of a coordinated command
- warlord submission process
- the scale of the network

### Information still uncertain

- exact origin of Hannibal
- how long he directed the network
- whether every prior incident was planned
- whether ordinary human limits still apply
- whether the world-end route is inevitable

### Presentation tier

Use a normal global news event when the reveal is valid but still regional. Use a super-event when the network has several warlord countries, broad territory, or a major consumed-population total.

The reveal super-event uses a unique generated action scene and unique licensed audio. The scene must show Hannibal Lecter directing visibly crazed cannibals and warlords as they breach defenses and pursue fleeing civilians. A static council, map-room tableau, calm lineup, or posed portrait is rejected. Title, quote, button remark, and track remain research gates.

## Warlord response choices

Every active warlord receives one of several response events. AI weights depend on route, personality, strength, player control, Network Alignment, and distance from the host.

### Submit and retain command

Outcome:

- country is absorbed or becomes a temporary integrated command region
- warlord becomes commander, governor, or lieutenant
- origin unit access transfers to Hannibal
- player-controlled country can continue as the unified host or receive a clear control-transfer outcome

Benefits:

- warlord survives
- regional bonuses retained
- units transfer with less disruption

### Submit and surrender the warband

Outcome:

- full absorption
- warlord becomes servant, prisoner, or minor character
- Larder and units transfer

### Bargain for autonomy

Outcome:

- subordinate Host retains temporary territorial control
- loyalty and tribute mechanic
- Hannibal can later integrate or punish it

### Resist

Outcome:

- immediate war or civil conflict with the unified state
- anti-absorption temporary bonuses
- possible ordinary-country aid
- defiant route achievements

### Challenge the unifier

Rare outcome for a powerful player or AI tyrant.

Outcome:

- succession war or duel event chain
- victory can make the challenger the host while Hannibal remains supreme leader only if the design preserves the intended reveal
- Hannibal cannot be replaced by a generic warlord as final leader

The route should reward resistance without undermining the core requirement that Hannibal becomes the revealed supreme leader of the unified cult.

## Unification mechanics

### Territorial integration

- submitted cores and controlled states transfer to the host
- disconnected islands can become governed regions or temporary subjects until naval access is secured
- resistant warlords remain enemies
- communes transfer if linked and aligned
- isolated cells become foreign network nodes

### Army integration

- local templates map into unified families
- origin specialists remain available
- excess duplicate templates are cleaned up
- warlord commanders retain origin traits
- unit experience and equipment transfer without duplication

### Larder integration

- local stores transfer with losses based on route and distance
- hidden hoards can create warlord loyalty events
- the host receives a global Larder cap based on territory and network development

### Focus integration

- local tree is replaced only for event-created host countries
- Hannibal tree loads after the reveal and host conversion
- completed local-route history should set branch variables that alter the unified opening
- existing normal countries must never receive the Hannibal tree through a blind tag collision

### Decision integration

- local decisions close or convert
- unified category opens
- active raids and missions are either inherited safely or canceled with explicit outcomes
- foreign cells remain valid targets

## Hannibal character design

### Visual identity

Hannibal should look like the person who can command the existing warlords.

Required visual cues:

- bald or closely shaven head
- asymmetrical scars that are distinct from every generic warlord
- heavy fictional blood and gore
- layered invented rough cloth, hide repairs, scavenged officer material, and a unique ruined command silhouette
- bloodshot mismatched eyes, irregular stained teeth, pallid damaged skin, and an ecstatic predatory expression
- a blood-wet skull held and actively licked during the portrait loop
- strong silhouette at 156 by 210
- no Wendigo traits in the ordinary portrait
- no actor likeness

### Animated portrait behavior

- 12 distinct generated source frames
- a complete skull-licking action arc: clutch, raise, tongue approach, contact, drag, apex, wet pull-away, second lap, lowering, swallow, and near-rest return
- jaw, tongue, lips, fingers, wrists, eyes, skull angle, blood trails, shoulders, and cloth folds independently redrawn across the frames
- static fallback
- animation activates only after reveal
- a transformed still, local mouth composite, or translation/scale/rotation/filter animation is rejected

### Trait direction

The leader needs a powerful staged trait family.

Initial revealed trait:

- network command
- warlord loyalty control
- cell coordination
- Larder centralization

Mid-route upgrades:

- global recruitment
- terror strategy
- continental logistics
- enemy collapse

Terminal upgrade:

- absurd army, supply, recruitment, and enemy-penalty effects suitable for a completed chaos-country route

## Warlord disposition system

Hannibal chooses how to use local leaders.

### Governors

- improve local Larder and control
- retain origin state bonuses
- risk betrayal

### Commanders

- preserve military traits and specialist units
- improve regional army performance
- can gain loyalty through victories

### Lieutenants

- join the central character roster
- unlock route-specific decisions
- compete for influence

### Disposable servants

- cheap short-term administration
- high purge and defection risk

### Purge targets

- immediate Larder, equipment, authority, or terror gain
- loss of commander depth
- resistance among related units and states

The player should recognize each warlord from his earlier country and portrait.

## Unified internal politics

The country can choose among three broad governance routes.

### Central command

Hannibal destroys local autonomy.

Strengths:

- highest direct authority
- efficient Larder transfers
- strongest standardized army

Risks:

- warlord rebellions
- high cost of governing distant islands

### Host confederation

Regional warlords retain armies and govern under Hannibal.

Strengths:

- origin bonuses
- broad commander roster
- easier territorial integration

Risks:

- loyalty management
- slower central decisions

### Ritual state

The cult formalizes ranks, feeding law, recruitment, and punishment.

Strengths:

- deep state control
- efficient cell and prison systems
- strong long-term consumption economy

Risks:

- administrative burden
- organized resistance and sabotage

All three routes remain powerful. They change method rather than creating one obviously correct path.

## Unified military power progression

### Early unification

The country should already be a major regional threat.

- integrated units
- strong reinforcement
- broad specialist access
- coordinated offensives
- foreign cell support

### Mature global power

The country should defeat ordinary states through a combination of:

- superior operational tempo
- enemy fear and organization damage
- population-based reinforcement
- captured-equipment conversion
- low supply dependence
- internal cells behind enemy lines

### Completed special-chaos route

The final route must become deliberately absurd.

Target experience:

- a conventional enemy needs overwhelming coalition strength and strategic counterplay before terminal lock
- Hannibal armies can continue after losses that would destroy ordinary countries
- controlled enemy population directly fuels expansion
- front collapse can cascade through cells and fear
- warlord commanders and special units make armies distinct
- the country can cross oceans and sustain global operations

Do not balance the final route by reducing it to small modifiers. Balance comes from difficult progression, early containment opportunities, hostile world reaction, anchor targets, resource requirements, route locks, and the fact that ordinary countries can stop the system before completion.

## Absorption of new warlord states after reveal

Warlord countries can still form after the reveal if foreign cells seize territory.

They receive immediate choices:

- submit
- accept autonomy
- resist
- attempt to replace the local governor

Hannibal gains rapid integration decisions. The system should not require a second reveal.

## Defeat and succession

Hannibal's ordinary country can be defeated before world-end lock.

Defeat requires:

- capture or destruction of the capital command
- loss of enough network anchors
- elimination or defection of major warlords
- Larder collapse
- destruction of remaining communes and cells

Hannibal's personal fate can be:

- killed
- captured
- missing

A missing outcome should not secretly reactivate the event after global victory. It can support ambiguous aftermath flavor while the system remains mechanically defeated.
