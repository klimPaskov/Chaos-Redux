# Event 014 Cannibalism, Part 10: Assets, Animation, and Localisation Direction

## Visual identity

Event 14 uses explicit gore and unsettling imagery. The visual package should remain coherent across ordinary war horror, ritualization, organized network activity, warlord states, Hannibal, and Wendigo Hannibal.

Gore is a hard acceptance requirement. Do not reduce the event to clean skull icons, red stains, or generic dark silhouettes. Important assets should show blood, damaged bodies, severed material, butchered military spaces, feeding sites, and physical transformation where the target size permits it.

The package should use generated fictional source art for the core horror scenes. Do not use real atrocity photographs or identifiable real victims.

## Visual progression

### Baseline palette and composition

- dirty khaki, rust, dried blood, mud, field-grey, damaged canvas
- documentary war photography composition
- human soldiers and military spaces
- gore discovered after the act
- no cult uniform or supernatural lighting

### Evolution I palette and composition

- deeper blood red, soot, candle or firelight, ritual scars, repeated marks
- cells still embedded in ordinary military structures
- organized arrangement of remains and supplies
- no global symbol tied to Hannibal

### Evolution II palette and composition

- dark crimson, blackened wood, bone, damaged ports, islands, prisons, rail depots
- repeated visual language across distant locations
- warlord states and communes
- no Hannibal face or silhouette

### Evolution III ordinary palette and composition

- centralized black, red, bone-white, military banners, massed armies
- Hannibal becomes the central visual subject
- larger-scale feeding-state and conquest imagery

### Wendigo palette and composition

- cold blue-grey, black, exposed flesh, frost, bone, blood frozen into snow
- transformed Hannibal remains recognizable
- reuse existing Chaos Redux Wendigo visual language
- no new borrowed sacred motifs

## Super-event images

Target size: 457 by 328.

### Hannibal reveal

Use only when the reveal meets super-event scale.

- Hannibal Lecter visibly commands a violent convergence in progress
- feral warlords and cannibal formations smash through a barricade and chase fleeing civilians across the frame
- the composition uses running figures, lunging attackers, overturned vehicles, active gunfire, and immediate foreground danger rather than an assembled council tableau
- explicit moving gore and ordinary human-origin horror

### Ordinary world-end

- Hannibal Lecter directs a global cannibal army while it overruns a capital in motion
- crazed attackers sprint, climb vehicles, breach defensive lines, and pursue fleeing civilians and soldiers
- the feeding-state landscape and collapsing conventional army are visible inside the action
- the composition is distinct from the reveal image and uses explicit mass horror

### Wendigo world-end

- transformed Wendigo Hannibal Lecter leads the charge through shattered frozen barricades
- visibly inhuman Wendigo and cannibal packs sprint after fleeing civilians and soldiers
- asymmetrical predatory motion, cold body horror, flying debris, and active gore dominate the scene
- entirely separate source art with no borrowed sacred or living Indigenous motifs

### Global defeat aftermath

- the last cannibal formations are destroyed during an active coalition breakthrough
- rescue teams tear open cages while civilians run through the breach and crazed attackers fight or pursue them
- ruined Host banners, gunfire, collapsing barricades, and visible gore preserve the cost of victory
- the image is kinetic final-battle action rather than a calm cleanup or burial tableau

All four super-event scenes require obvious direction of movement, foreground and background action, at least one visible pursuit or rescue beat, and a different camera angle and action silhouette. Static leaders addressing crowds, standing over maps, or posing before passive armies are rejected.

## Baseline writing direction

Viewpoint:

- officers, soldiers, medics, quartermasters, families, prisoners, investigators, and local civilians

Tone:

- grounded war horror
- concrete evidence
- fear and disgust
- uncertainty about scale

Avoid:

- cult master language
- supernatural certainty
- generic apocalypse prose
- map-summary framing
- official denial contrast formulas
- final-route spoilers

## Evolution I writing direction

- repeated rites, marks, rank behavior, and initiation
- soldiers treating the practice as doctrine
- uncertainty about whether cells imitate one another
- exploitation text should condemn the state through its actions rather than cheap jokes

## Evolution II writing direction

- empty villages
- missing ships
- prisoners and garrisons refusing transfer
- identical military methods across distant nodes
- uncertain coordination
- no personal leader reference

## Evolution III writing direction

- reveal Hannibal clearly
- communicate unification, command, and military scale
- retain uncertainty about origin and full human nature
- ordinary and Wendigo routes require distinct tones

## Option and button tone

Minor field incidents can use frightened understatement, bitter military humor, or administrative euphemism. Mass death, prisoner murder, and terminal routes should use severity, hypocrisy, cold policy, or self-damning irony rather than cheap comedy.

Cultural references need research. No invented song line, film line, proverb, scripture, or quotation should be inserted during implementation without a verified source.

## Dynamic localisation

Player-visible values should show:

- integer formatting where values are integral
- named states and countries

- cost icons

Long trigger blocks should use custom tooltips and scripted localisation.

## Anti-spoiler key discipline

Early localisation keys can use neutral internal slugs. File and sprite names stored in developer-only paths may contain Hannibal when necessary, but no player-facing lookup should resolve them before reveal.

The anti-spoiler audit must inspect:

- localisation values
- scripted localisation selectors
- GUI default text
- focus search labels
- achievement list visibility
- scenario details
- super-event slot defaults
- audio catalogue names or tooltips
- event log preview rows
- Event Details evolution catalog
- debug names visible outside debug mode
