# Event 014 Cannibalism, Part 3: Evolutions and Spread

## Evolution structure

Cannibalism has three true evolutions. Ordinary crisis stages remain baseline flow and must not be logged as evolutions.

Each evolution has two entry paths:

- Active-event evolution changes countries, cells, communes, and warlords that already exist.
- Pre-fire evolved opening changes the first Event 14 incident when the event has not fired yet and world conditions already support a more severe opening.

An evolution never needs the entry event to fire again. Active actors receive the new content through the event's own evolution transition.

Evolution pacing should use dynamic mean-time logic. A normal evolution should take meaningful time after conditions become valid. Ninety days is a useful center for ordinary evolution pacing, with faster transitions for severe network activity and slower transitions after successful containment. A pre-fire evolved opening applies immediately when the global event finally fires because the world has already met the evolution state.

## Evolution I: Ritualization

### Playable promise

The behavior stops depending entirely on hunger. Small groups create rules about who can be killed, who can eat, how spoils are divided, how courage is proved, and which officers deserve obedience. Symbols, scars, tokens, and phrases appear across units. Some soldiers participate through fear. Others treat the practice as a new military faith.

The player now fights an ideology inside the armed forces.

### Conditions

Evolution I becomes possible when:

- global chaos has reached at least the Gathering Storm tier
- Event 14 is active or has not yet fired
- one or more countries have severe Field Hunger, low Command Integrity, repeated confirmed predation, or an exploitation policy
- no terminal world-end exists
- Evolution I is enabled in settings

Strong acceleration factors:

- terror exploitation
- officer complicity
- failed public trials
- repeated prisoner disappearances
- surviving a targeted purge
- multiple compromised formations
- foreign spread

Strong slowing factors:

- clean emergency response
- high Command Integrity
- protected witnesses
- successful logistics missions
- no active cell

### Active-event entry

Every active country is evaluated separately.

Immediate changes:

- Cult Cohesion becomes visible
- surviving cells gain ritual membership state
- new suppression and exploitation decisions unlock
- compromised units can recruit across formation boundaries
- state incidents can produce hidden shrines, initiation sites, oath groups, and protected kitchens
- AI policy stance is recalculated

Countries that have already achieved clean local victory are not reactivated by the evolution alone. They remain protected until a real external source reaches them.

### Pre-fire evolved opening

The first host begins with:

- two or more compromised formations or one formation plus a prison or island site
- existing ritual evidence
- higher Cult Cohesion
- an established officer or quartermaster organizer
- a shortened first containment window
- stronger decision category presentation

The opening text still begins from military evidence. It can show deliberate repetition and symbols, but it must not imply a global leader.

### New decision families

#### Infiltrate oath groups

Use vetted soldiers, military intelligence, and protected defectors to map membership.

Costs:

- intelligence exposure
- manpower
- army experience
- risk to witnesses

Success:

- reveals organizers and linked units
- reduces Cult Cohesion

Failure:

- infiltrators disappear or convert
- cell learns investigation methods

#### Break the ritual economy

Target trophies, hidden stores, prisoner lists, kitchen networks, and black-market transport.

Costs:

- transport inspection
- military police
- local disruption

Success:

- cell loses recruitment and supply

#### Separate followers from organizers

A stronger amnesty and rehabilitation route.

Requirements:

- evidence quality
- no recent indiscriminate purge
- protected surrender location

Success:

- large cell reduction
- testimony unlocks future leaders as targets

#### Ban marks and spoils

A broad discipline policy with limited value by itself. It becomes effective when paired with inspection and rotation.

Risk:

- cosmetic compliance can hide mature cells

#### Exploit ritual terror

The dark route expands from one compromised unit into a doctrine.

Benefits:

- stronger terror attacks
- enemy surrender pressure
- occupation control

Costs:

- Cult Cohesion growth
- Command Integrity collapse
- organizers gain formal rank and transport

### New event families

- initiation survives a unit transfer
- officers use prisoner selection as promotion
- symbols appear in another regiment
- a chaplain, medic, or political officer becomes a witness or organizer
- false accusations create panic
- a cult cell protects local civilians in exchange for recruits
- an exploited terror unit disobeys a conventional order
- a foreign observer identifies the same ritual pattern abroad

### Containment

Evolution I remains containable without territorial war. The country must:

- reduce Cult Cohesion below the dormant band
- remove officer organizers
- break ritual supply and prisoner access
- complete rehabilitation or prosecution
- stabilize affected states

A country can return to normal after Evolution I. The global evolution remains recorded, so later infected countries may begin with better-organized cells.

### Evolution log direction

The log should communicate that scattered predation has become a shared ritual and ideology. It must not suggest one supreme leader.

## Evolution II: Organized Network

### Playable promise

Cells stop behaving like isolated unit cults. Military prisons, islands, occupied districts, and remote garrisons follow the same methods. Ships fail to return. Empty villages show identical removal patterns. Officers in different countries carry matching maps and schedules. Cannibal communes and warlord states become possible.

The player fights a network that can control territory.

### Conditions

Evolution II becomes possible when:

- global chaos has reached at least Rising Chaos
- Evolution I has occurred or the pre-fire opening qualifies for a direct organized form
- multiple mature cells, at least one isolated commune candidate, or deliberate state exploitation exists
- Network Reach can be initialized from real active nodes
- Evolution II is enabled

Strong acceleration factors:

- infection in more than one country
- active cannibal island
- prison or deportation network
- captured port or rail hub
- warlord emergence
- high Cult Cohesion in several countries
- successful foreign cell seeding

### Active-event entry

Immediate changes:

- Network Reach becomes visible through uncertain coordination language
- every active cell receives a network link score
- remote-state and island selection begins
- cannibal commune and country-formation rules activate
- foreign seeding decisions unlock for cult actors
- ordinary countries gain international tracking, convoy search, and network disruption tools
- the concealed supreme leader character is created internally if implementation needs a persistent character before reveal
- no player-facing reference to the character is enabled

### Pre-fire evolved opening

The first Event 14 incident can begin as an organized regional crisis when the world is already chaotic enough.

Opening package:

- several linked cells inside one host or cells in two allied countries
- one remote island, prison, occupied district, or cut-off garrison at severe risk
- higher Network Reach
- evidence of matching methods across locations
- possible immediate commune rather than a normal single-unit incident
- stronger first containment tools and shorter warlord-prevention window

Even this opening must preserve the hidden identity.

## Network node types

### Formation cell

Located in an army formation. It spreads through transfer, retreat, reinforcement, and shared supply.

### Prison cell

Located in a military prison, detention camp, or prisoner-transfer route. It has high access to victims and transport records.

### Port cell

Controls stevedores, warehouses, naval guards, and shipping manifests. It supports island spread and convoy disappearance.

### Island commune

A remote island or isolated coastal state where the garrison and local administration have ceased obeying the mainland. It can remain a state modifier or become a released warlord country.

### Occupation cell

Operates in occupied territory. It feeds on prisoners, displaced civilians, and destroyed records. Heavy-handed occupation policies make it stronger.

### Rail cell

Uses military trains, hospital transfers, and supply depots to move personnel and material.

### Warlord state

A full cannibal country with territory, units, leaders, focus content, decisions, and AI.

## Remote island sequence

An island becomes eligible when it has meaningful population and one or more of the following:

- no land connection to the host capital
- damaged or blockaded port
- convoy losses
- cut-off garrison
- military prison
- weak local administration
- mature formation or port cell

The sequence should progress through visible uncertainty:

1. delayed reports and missing ships
2. refusal of transfers
3. empty settlements or altered burial grounds
4. failed reconnaissance
5. commune control
6. release as an Island Host when territorial and country-package conditions are valid

The player can interrupt the sequence through convoy relief, evacuation, reconnaissance, blockade, negotiated surrender, or assault.

## Mainland commune sequence

A mainland commune forms when a mature cell controls a defensible state or contiguous state group and central authority is weak.

Strong candidates:

- besieged city
- occupied district
- mountain pocket
- prison complex
- rail hub separated by a front
- devastated rural region with abandoned villages

The commune begins as a dynamic state modifier. It becomes a country when:

- it controls enough territory to sustain units
- central government cannot restore control within the mission window
- a valid conflict-free placeholder tag is available
- the map result does not create an impossible enclave without supply

## Warlord emergence

Every pre-unification country is led by a generic cannibal warlord.

Leader requirements:

- male-presenting generated portrait
- bloody, bald, visibly brutal
- invented rough hides, raw cloth, bone ornaments, and scavenged 1930s to 1940s military equipment
- actual-ish personal name from a small male regional pool matching the state origin
- optional epithet after the personal name
- no generic office-title name
- no resemblance to Hannibal
- no supernatural transformation before the route earns it

The warlord is a temporary local leader. He can later become:

- commander
- local governor
- lieutenant
- rival claimant
- disposable servant
- ritual victim
- purge target

## Network coordination without revelation

Evolution II should make the mystery stronger through repeated operational facts:

- identical maps use the same marked rail hubs
- different cells attack relief depots on the same night
- warlords issue matching prisoner rules
- island signals use the same cadence
- captured officers carry copied rank tables
- separate units know the same hand signals
- foreign cells withdraw before raids reach them

Public theories can include imitation, captured manuals, traveling organizers, radio coordination, and common religious development. No theory becomes confirmed until Evolution III.

## Ordinary-country network decisions

### International cell registry

Share identified symbols, officers, routes, and state incidents with friendly countries.

Benefits:

- better foreign detection
- faster source tracing

Costs:

- intelligence exposure
- diplomatic relations work

### Inspect prisoner transfers

Target military prisons and international transfers.

Costs:

- transport delay
- guards
- support equipment

### Convoy and island search

Use naval patrols, convoys, fuel, and reconnaissance to investigate silent routes.

Outcomes:

- rescue
- false alarm
- ambush
- commune discovery
- evidence of foreign network links

### Break the courier chain

Target rail hubs, ports, officers, and radio sites.

Success:

- Network Reach reduction
- linked cells lose coordination

### Joint suppression mission

Two or more countries coordinate simultaneous action against linked nodes.

Requirements:

- diplomatic access
- intelligence sharing
- valid active nodes

Success:

- larger network reduction than sequential crackdowns

Failure:

- surviving nodes relocate across borders

## Cannibal-network decisions

### Seed a foreign formation

Spend Larder Stores, trained organizers, and intelligence access to create a cell in an enemy or unstable ally.

### Redirect a prisoner convoy

Gain victims and potential recruits while raising exposure.

### Establish a silent port

Convert a captured port state into a network anchor.

### Raise a local warband

Create units from a mature commune using local population consumption, captured equipment, and Larder Stores.

### Synchronize the hunt

Coordinate several warlord offensives or cell actions.

The decision can imply shared orders without naming their source.

## Hannibal's concealed creation

Evolution II creates the implementation conditions for Hannibal.

Internal requirements:

- create or activate the fictional character in a non-player-facing form
- store the character as the future network coordinator
- prepare portrait and character data but do not expose the portrait sprite
- keep every public character key, name selector, leader list, and country history surface blocked
- allow network logic to use hidden coordination variables without player-visible personal text

This hidden creation must not alter country leader lists, diplomacy screens, Event Details, or achievements before reveal.

## Evolution II containment

The network can still be defeated before unification.

Required strategic actions:

- destroy every active node
- liberate controlled states
- defeat or contain every warlord country
- break island and port routes
- capture or eliminate local organizers
- reduce Network Reach below dormant level
- maintain a network-free stabilization period

A world victory at this stage prevents Hannibal's reveal because no viable network remains.

### Evolution log direction

The log should communicate that separate cults now use shared methods, routes, and military direction. It can mention uncertain coordination. It must not name or show Hannibal.

## Evolution III: Global Cult and Reveal

### Playable promise

The network has enough territory, consumed population, aligned warlords, and linked cells to stop hiding its command. Hannibal appears, takes control of the strongest viable host, and begins unification.

This is the first moment when player-facing text can name Hannibal.

### Conditions

Evolution III becomes possible when:

- global chaos has reached at least Totalen Chaos
- Evolution II is active
- multiple independent cannibal nodes exist
- Network Reach has crossed a major threshold
- population consumption has crossed a major threshold
- a viable unification host exists
- the network has at least one route connecting major nodes
- no world-end state exists
- Evolution III is enabled

The reveal should be delayed if the network is weak, even at high chaos. Chaos creates possibility. Network achievement earns the reveal.

### Active-event entry

Immediate sequence:

1. Select the unification host with player-preservation rules.
2. Reveal Hannibal's character and portrait.
3. Fire a global news event or reveal super-event based on scale.
4. Open submission, resistance, and absorption choices for warlords.
5. Transfer or contest communes, islands, cells, and controlled states.
6. Activate the unified country package and focus tree.
7. Replace uncertain coordination UI with the revealed command structure.
8. Convert generic warlords into commanders, governors, lieutenants, rivals, servants, or purge targets.
9. Recalculate the cannibal world-threat source.
10. Evaluate the Wendigo crossover before the final unification package locks.

### Pre-fire evolved opening

A direct Evolution III opening is exceptionally rare and belongs mainly to the triggerable scenario or an extreme late campaign where Event 14 remained disabled until the world had already collapsed.

The entry event should still stage the reveal in-world. The scenario UI and pre-fire event list must not show Hannibal's identity before the reveal event fires.

Opening package:

- several warlord countries or communes
- strong Network Reach
- enough population consumption history to justify unification
- immediate convergence mission
- reveal event after a short in-world preparation rather than an instant setup tooltip

### Reveal scale

Use a global reveal super-event when the network controls enough territory, has several warlord countries, or has consumed a major population total. Use a normal global news event for a smaller but valid reveal. The character and unification still occur in both cases.

### Evolution log direction

The Evolution III log may name Hannibal, identify the unification, and show the actor country. This is the first evolution row that can use the final portrait and identity.

## Spread between countries

Every country-level infection needs a source type.

| Source type | Typical origin | Typical target | Player counterplay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Retreat transfer | Compromised formation | Allied or neighboring country | Screening, border control, unit rotation |
| Prison transfer | Prison cell | Receiving country or occupied state | Inspect transfers, guard reform |
| Convoy route | Port or island cell | Overseas garrison | Escort, manifests, quarantine, search |
| Volunteer return | Foreign formation cell | Volunteer home country | Demobilization screening |
| Occupation turnover | Controlled state | New controller | State inspection, relief, military police |
| Deliberate seeding | Cult or warlord decision | Enemy army or prison | Counterintelligence, network disruption |
| Warlord conquest | Cannibal country | Occupied enemy states | Military liberation |
| Refugee or survivor route | Collapsed commune | Neighboring state | Humane screening and relief, not indiscriminate closure |

Spread risk should respond to real travel and war conditions. A country on another continent with no route should not become infected through an unexplained random pulse.

## Evolution interaction with disabled settings

### Evolution I disabled

- no Cult Cohesion evolution layer
- baseline predation remains containable
- exploitation still has ordinary consequences
- no ritual ideology content

### Evolution II disabled

- no network, communes, islands, or warlord countries created through the evolution
- existing baseline state incidents must resolve without hidden network flags
- no concealed Hannibal character is activated

### Evolution III disabled

- warlord countries can continue fighting if Evolution II exists
- no Hannibal reveal
- no unified cannibal state
- no Event 14 world-end route
- no Wendigo Hannibal crossover

Settings must never leave the player with an impossible containment requirement tied to disabled content.
