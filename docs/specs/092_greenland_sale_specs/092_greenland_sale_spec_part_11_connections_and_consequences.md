# Greenland Sale: connections and consequences

## World Claims

World Claims makes a current claim a reason for stronger buying interest.
It does not give the claimant a lower required bid, priority over an equal accepted offer, or a right to buy territory the owner has not listed.
A claimant with modest industry can nevertheless become a determined competitor for a nearby possession that a remote major values less.

A claimed state acquired through the auction can later participate in whatever integration or core progression World Claims independently permits.
Event 092 does not reproduce that progression or accelerate it merely because the transfer was paid.
Any new core or compliance change must come from the owning system and its real prerequisites.

New claims can change bidder willingness during an active auction.
A new seller core is more consequential because it changes whether the seller may convey the state under the current permissions.
The market must distinguish those two cases.
It must not cancel a cheap listing merely because another country has become more interested in buying it.

## Independence Wave and other liberation events

Liberation changes what an empire still owns.
A colonial state that has become part of an independent country cannot remain in its former ruler's sale catalog.
If it was included in an active lot, the whole advertised lot is canceled and escrow is returned.
The former ruler does not receive sale proceeds for a territory that is no longer its property.

A newly independent government can become a bidder or seller under the same economic and sovereignty rules as other countries.
It is not forced to participate and does not inherit its former ruler's auction receipt by default.
A country whose entire new homeland would be the lot is protected against selling itself out of existence.

An ownership transfer does not erase an already established independence process, population claim, or promised liberation settlement in another system.
Where that system has already committed the territory to a disposition, listing is blocked.
Where its future outcome legitimately depends on the current owner, that system reassesses the new situation through its own rules.
The auction never clears another event's progression flags to make the state appear politically quiet.

## Resource discoveries and industrial change

Resources Found, Riches Found, Industrial Boom, and other actual development changes can substantially change a lot's value.
The assessment must read their resulting deposits and buildings rather than infer a value from the name of the event that affected the state.
A resource-rich Greenland becomes more expensive even when no special Greenland modifier was added.

Economic decline, damaged production, and reduced usable logistics can move value in the other direction.
A country whose homeland economy has deteriorated can also become a more motivated seller, but it cannot borrow against an auction outcome that has not occurred.
A country whose civilian capacity has collapsed earns less new reserve rather than continuing to mint points at an old rate.

A windfall in some other native or event-owned currency is not automatically Industrial Reserve.
In particular, the legacy CIC event's native market capacity cannot be copied into the auction reserve without an explicit, conservation-preserving conversion design and a proven interface.
The baseline introduces no such conversion.
It responds to actual industrial and territorial changes and to reserve earned or received through its own specified routes.

## Black Friday and shared costs

Black Friday can change ordinary costs elsewhere in the campaign and thereby change a government's competing uses for its industry.
Those changes may influence whether buying land is attractive.
They do not rewrite an accepted territorial bid or alter who owns its escrow.

The auction principal, factory-backed reserve creation, and redemption loop have the explicit exchange-and-production classification in Part 02.
A shared discount must not accidentally subsidize one side of the exchange while the other receives the full principal.
Actual-paid receipts and one-time refunds remain required even when the shared framework cannot directly handle the reserve component.
The missing adapter is a validation gate, not permission to bypass the transaction contract.

## Local hazards and shared civilian systems

A sale transfers ownership, not a clean slate.
Existing contamination, air effects, famine, displacement, local medical problems, persistent damage, and other state-bound conditions remain present.
The buyer's assessment includes the usable value and disclosed burden of those conditions.
The seller cannot make a dangerous state look safe by opening an auction category.

Population is not moved, killed, recruited, or replenished merely because the state is sold.
There is no direct Deaths increment for the auction itself.
If a continuing crisis later produces casualties or migration, its owning system records them once through the shared ledgers.
The new owner becomes responsible for its own subsequent actions without erasing responsibility for earlier acts by the seller or another operator.

Medical, scientific, industrial, and military facilities require a distinction between physical state-bound infrastructure and country-owned capability.
A physical building may remain in the state while personnel, production rights, doctrine, research, a cure license, or a national stockpile remains with its previous owner.
A special facility with important unresolved ownership or valuation behavior must be disclosed and blocked from an unsupported sale rather than priced as an ordinary empty state.
The responsible event supplies the actual interface and authority rules.

## Revolutions, civil wars, and regime change

A regime change that leaves the same owner, territory, and valid transaction conditions intact does not automatically restart bidding.
The government may reassess whether to make future bids or fund more reserve.
It cannot withdraw a funded leading bid merely because its new leadership dislikes the earlier price.

A civil war or territorial revolution that changes the owner or controller of a promised state cancels the whole lot.
A new country does not inherit the old country's auction contract without an established succession rule.
This connection applies to the actual effects of The Great Revolution and other political events, without inventing details of their separate current specifications.

If an existing special Chaos country is a valid economic government and buys territory, its owning event may have a growth milestone to evaluate.
The auction reports the actual acquisition to that owner where a supported shared interface exists.
It does not create a second copy of the actor's growth reward or a new supernatural actor merely to make the market more dramatic.

## The market's direct Chaos consequences

The catalog's Chaos level 1 is retained as event classification.
It is not a direction to add one Chaos point every time a bid, timer, or listing changes.
Publication, bidding, funding, refunding, ordinary construction redemption, no-bid outcomes, cancellation, and evolution activation have zero event-owned Chaos effect by themselves.

The proposed direct effects attach to completed changes in the use of sovereignty as a market instrument.
The first completed founding Greenland sale contributes a one-time 5-point precedent effect.
The first completed non-Greenland market sale contributes a one-time 5-point extension effect.
The first completed multi-state colonial-market sale contributes a one-time 5-point regional-commercialization effect.
The first completed strategic sale contributes a one-time 10-point strategic-order effect.
The first completed homeland sale contributes a one-time 10-point domestic-sovereignty effect.

These are five global milestones for Event 092, not five rewards available to every seller, buyer, state, or continent.
The founding exception qualifies only for its founding milestone, while the regional, strategic, and homeland milestones require a later lot published under those respective permissions.
A transaction meeting several previously unachieved milestones receives only the largest applicable direct effect and consumes the applicable milestone opportunities together.
For equal largest effects, homeland significance takes priority over strategic significance for the receipt's explanation.
This prevents one complex lot from stacking several event-owned awards for the same completed bargain.
The full direct positive contribution is bounded at 35 points when the milestones occur in separate meaningful transactions.

Native consequences such as wars, world tension, annexation accounting, ideology changes, deaths, or an existing Chaos actor's growth continue through their owning systems.
The market's additional milestone explanation concerns the distinct commercial precedent, not a second charge for the same casualty or ordinary territorial amount.
The integration audit must establish whether a generic transfer or annexation handler observes the sale and prevent duplicate recording.
A paid state transfer must not also be misreported as a military conquest by an auction-specific shortcut.

## Reversing a specific consequence

The seller whose transaction actually received the first homeland-significance Chaos receipt can reverse 5 points of that event-owned effect by completing its reconstruction objective and retaining its protected country for 90 days after the sale.
This is a single partial reversal of that identified 10-point consequence.
It cannot be awarded for every later core sale, for repeated mission callbacks, or when the original positive effect was never recorded.

Other successful reconstruction missions can restore the seller's specified stability without reducing the global meter again.
Ordinary peaceful sales, losing bids, canceled auctions, and the decision to keep a colony do not farm negative Chaos.
Later reoccupation or reconquest of purchased land is not an auction refund and does not automatically reverse the market's historical milestone.
