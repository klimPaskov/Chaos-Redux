# Greenland Sale: the living market

## A founding event and a continuing institution

Event 092 can fire once through the ordinary event selector.
Its later auctions are activity within the institution it established, not new firings of the same random event.
They do not repeatedly consume event selection, reset the fire-once state, or reannounce that Greenland has just been put up for sale.

The founding lot has priority over every later publication.
Even in a high-Chaos opening, Greenland is the only lot until that founding auction has reached a terminal outcome.
Countries may learn what the evolved market will permit, but no secondary lot is published beside the initial offer before it resolves.
This preserves the founding event's identity while allowing the resulting institution to remain useful for the rest of the campaign.

## Evolutions before the first firing

Each of the four evolutions supports a pre-fire evolved opening.
If Event 092 has not fired, the enabled stages whose Chaos requirements are already satisfied can be active when its first valid auction is published.
The opening still offers the actual current Greenland holdings under the founding rule.
It does not replace them with a colonial region, add extra lots to the announcement, change the closing time, or give countries free starting reserve.

The immediate difference is that governments know which kinds of later disposition the precedent will support.
AI planning can prepare for those possibilities and the event details can explain them.
After the founding lot ends, the active permissions determine what may be published next.
Only one initial firing is recorded, with its evolved-opening context.
Ordinary inspection, bidding, closing, and reinvestment are not logged as additional evolutions.

## Evolutions after the first firing

An active market can develop each enabled evolution when its threshold is met.
The thresholds are 200 for Overseas Auctions, 400 for Colonial Markets, 600 for Strategic Auctions, and 800 for Everything Has a Price.
The active-event development uses the project's evolution pacing with a starting MTTH design anchor of about 90 days, rather than treating the threshold crossing as an automatic instant market expansion.

The intended pacing can be influenced by relevant current pressure and opportunity.
Several plausible colonial dispositions can make a colonial-market development more credible, while major strategic competition can make the strategic stage more relevant.
The final timing and conditional weights require the dedicated probability workflow.
The 90-day anchor must not be presented as a guaranteed wait, a measured average, or a fixed cumulative chance without that evidence.

Activation unlocks the corresponding listing capability for the existing institution.
It does not require Event 092 to fire again or the original seller or buyer to remain alive.
The newly available choices and AI candidate types become accessible immediately after activation, subject to existing auction slots and publication spacing.
An active lot retains its already published territory and reserve.

Once a stage has activated, a later fall in Chaos does not erase the political precedent while that stage remains enabled.
This is a proposed persistence rule intended to avoid repeated cancellation and re-unlocking around a threshold.
The enabled-state controls remain authoritative and are handled separately below.

## Separate permissions, not an invisible all-or-nothing ladder

Overseas Auctions allows ordinary remote non-core single-state sales.
Colonial Markets allows coherent remote non-core regional sales and the regional-bundling capability used by other enabled permissions.
Strategic Auctions allows strategically important non-core single-state sales.
Everything Has a Price allows non-capital seller-core sales under the homeland conditions.

A regional strategic lot requires both strategic permission and regional-bundling permission.
A regional homeland lot requires both core-sale permission and regional-bundling permission.
A core lot that is also strategically protected additionally requires strategic permission.
Disabled capabilities are not smuggled back into the market through a higher stage's label.

A higher stage can still provide meaningful activity when a lower stage is disabled.
For example, strategic single-state auctions can operate without ordinary Overseas Auctions, and a core single-state sale can operate without regional bundling when it does not separately need strategic permission.
The baseline Greenland auction and the redemption of earned reserve remain complete regardless of which later permissions are disabled.

## Changing enabled stages during a campaign

Disabling a stage immediately stops new listings that require it.
A published lot whose required permission has been withdrawn is canceled without a sale and refunds still-refundable escrow.
A disabled stage does not leave a hidden active path that can finish by waiting for an old timer.
Already completed ownership transfers and seller receipts are not retroactively reversed.

If a stage change merely lowers the global active-auction cap, otherwise legal existing lots can finish.
No further lot is published until the count falls below the new cap.
This avoids canceling a valid ordinary auction solely because a separate strategic permission was switched off.
A lot canceled because its own permission disappeared cannot use this cap rule as an exemption.

A new seller core on a listed state changes a fundamental sovereignty condition and requires the core-sale protections to be met.
Without the necessary enabled capability, the lot is canceled.
A mere increase in a state's strategic or economic usefulness does not let the seller escape a published low reserve.
Its original strategic classification stays attached to that listing unless a separate ownership, core, capital, control, or survival restriction actually invalidates it.

## Keeping the map current

The market works from current ownership and current geography.
Its candidate knowledge is refreshed when relevant ownership, control, independence, core, capital, development, or crisis conditions change.
The active lots receive prompt checks of the particular states and countries on which they depend.
A stale candidate is not a guarantee of eligibility.

The institution does not need a daily search of every state and every country in the world.
One-time discovery, country-local candidate preparation, relevant state-change notifications, and bounded checks of active records provide the intended behavior.
A country can request a new assessment when preparing a listing, and an interested bidder can reassess the active lot it is considering.
Countries created later enter the eligible economic and nomination population without another founding event.

AI publication opportunities are paced through a rotating, bounded set of registered prospective sellers.
A quiet country with no plausible disposition should not monopolize the publication queue, and a large empire should not crowd out every smaller seller with hundreds of similar candidates.
One prepared candidate per seller is enough at a time.
Candidate rotation affects which lot is proposed next, not who is allowed to bid on a published lot.

## Market quiet and market recovery

The global cap is one active founding auction before its terminal outcome, two under ordinary or regional market permission, and three when strategic or core-sale permission supports the larger market.
Each publication has a 15-day inspection period, a 30-day no-bid opening window, and a renewable 10-day high-bid closing period.
Publications are separated by at least 15 days.
A seller has one active lot, a 90-day pause after a completed sale, and a 60-day pause after a no-bid outcome.

A cancellation caused by an external ownership, control, sovereignty, or permission change has a 30-day pause before a new listing by the same seller.
A drafted but unpublished proposal does not invoke a public-sale cooldown.
Purchased states have a 180-day resale restriction measured from the completed sale, and that restriction is not reset or cleared by a new evolution.
The normal restrictions continue to apply if the state changes owners again before that period ends.

An institution with no active lots and no worthwhile candidates remains dormant.
It can become active again when a real territorial or economic change creates an opportunity.
It does not need a recurring flavor event, forced discount day, or free reserve grant to keep its category visible.
Countries with unspent reserve retain the relevant construction access even during a quiet market.

## Saving the exact bargain

A save and reload preserves the advertised lot, seller, leader, paid escrow, current price, next bid, and remaining closing time.
It also preserves earned reserve, completed receipts, cooldowns, evolution activation, and any reconstruction objective.
A reload cannot restart inspection, release a leader's escrow twice, forget a paid sale, or treat an old partial Greenland lot as the entire island.

Terminal outcomes remain terminal.
A repeated closing callback cannot transfer the same states again or pay the seller twice.
A repeated cancellation cannot produce a second refund.
The market's history can explain a completed sale or failed auction without retaining an endless stream of ordinary bid notifications as separate world events.
