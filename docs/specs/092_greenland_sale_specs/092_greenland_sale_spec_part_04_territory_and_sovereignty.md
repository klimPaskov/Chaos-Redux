# Greenland Sale: territory and sovereignty

## A seller must own what it offers

A government can offer only states it presently owns and fully controls.
Occupation alone is not ownership for an auction.
A country cannot sell an enemy's territory during a war, offer a subject's states as though they were its own, or convey a province inside a state while promising that the rest of the state remains untouched.
The sale unit is a whole state, and a multi-state lot consists of an explicit set of those units.

The current controller must be able to deliver the possession without displacing a hostile occupying power through an auction effect.
Active hostile occupation makes the affected state ineligible even when the seller remains its legal owner.
An auction is not a bypass around combat, a peace conference, a liberation settlement, or an existing transfer whose outcome has already been committed elsewhere.

The states in a published lot are reserved against simultaneous listing in another lot.
Their membership cannot grow when the seller later conquers an adjacent state.
It cannot shrink to hide a lost port while retaining the original high bid.
A material ownership or membership change closes the whole lot and returns refundable escrow.
The owner may prepare a genuinely new listing later under the then-current market rules.

## Finding Greenland on the current map

Greenland is identified by a verified geographic membership for the installed map.
The membership may describe one state or several states, depending on the installed map.
The membership describes geography and does not prescribe an owner.
A map that divides the island into several states can therefore produce a multi-state founding lot.

A map adapter must not guess from a translated state name, a country's capital, or Denmark's possessions.
Where a map combines Greenland and unrelated territory into an indivisible state, the event must report that the promised geographic lot cannot be represented cleanly.
It must not silently sell unrelated land or redraw the map to rescue the event.

When several countries own eligible Greenland states, select the owner with the greatest number of eligible land provinces within that mapped geography.
Break an equal count by the assessed economic value of that owner's eligible Greenland holdings, and break an exact remaining tie by a stable country identifier.
These are transparent selection rules, not random favoritism or a claim that province count measures real-world area.
The resulting lot includes that seller's eligible Greenland states and names itself as a partial holding when appropriate.

Territory held by other governments remains outside the lot even when those governments would also like to sell.
They do not become compulsory co-sellers or share the proceeds.
Once Overseas Auctions is available, their remaining Greenland holdings may qualify for ordinary later listings on their own merits.
The founding event itself does not repeat to process every partition.

## The country that must remain

The capital state is protected from sale.
A country must retain at least one owned, controlled, inhabited home state after every active and pending sale is considered together.
Where the country has such a core state, the retained home state must be a core.
A country created without ordinary cores uses its existing political home and capital protection rather than being granted artificial cores for this purpose.

This protection is evaluated against the combined effect of all of the seller's listed or committed lots.
Two individually plausible sales cannot each assume that the other lot will remain as the country's surviving home.
The protected remainder is checked again before closing.
A war or other event that removes it cancels any sale that would now extinguish the seller or dispose of its capital.

A capital move does not convert a previously protected capital into an automatically saleable loophole.
A newly capitalized state also becomes protected immediately.
Draft listings must be reconsidered when capital and home-state protection changes, and published lots that lose eligibility are canceled.
The auction does not relocate a capital, release a replacement tag, or manufacture an uninhabited remnant to force settlement.

An independent Greenland country whose entire country would be the founding lot cannot sell itself through Event 092.
If it has another qualifying home territory and the offered Greenland states exclude its capital, the normal safeguards decide eligibility.
The event does not assume that an independent Greenland can never exist or that Denmark must be recreated to conduct a sale.

## Subjects and their owners

Subject countries remain real countries for bidding when they own territory and control an economically competent government.
They use their own available reserve and receive ownership in their own name.
The overlord does not automatically become the purchaser or receive the subject's refund.
Existing autonomy and economic obligations remain in force after the acquisition.

A subject that wishes to sell its own territory requires its overlord's authorization during the listing preparation.
The owner remains the seller and receives the proceeds.
The overlord's approval does not create ownership of the lot, add a second commission, or allow the overlord to sell another subject's states.
A player overlord gets a single approve-or-decline request identifying the land and the subject's expected reason for selling.
An unanswered request expires after 15 days without publication.

AI overlords approve dispositions that improve the subject's viability without sacrificing an essential shared position, and reject sales that undermine an active defense or dangerously weaken the subject.
They do not approve every sale simply because the buyer belongs to the same faction.
A change of overlord or independence during preparation requires the current political situation to be checked again.
There is no repeated permission request for each ordinary bid made by a subject buyer.
A valid authorization already attached to a published lot remains attached to that contract.
A later change of overlord alone does not allow its seller to escape the accepted terms while the ownership, control, survival, and permission conditions still hold.

## What ownership transfers

A completed sale transfers the listed states to the buyer together with their ordinary state-bound economic contents.
Buildings, deposits, infrastructure, and normal ownership consequences belong to the territory.
The auction does not also hand the buyer the seller's national equipment stockpile, research, operative network, command power, national reserve balance, or government institutions.

Divisions, fleets, air wings, and foreign forces stationed in the lot remain owned by their existing countries.
Their ability to stay, move, draw supply, or use bases follows the resulting native access and diplomatic situation.
The sale grants no special right to intern, destroy, duplicate, or commandeer them.
A seller should be warned when an important military deployment depends on access it will lose.

Claims and cores held by other countries are not erased.
The seller's existing cores are not erased merely because it sold ownership.
The buyer keeps any core it already had, but otherwise receives non-core territory with the ordinary consequences for administration, resistance, compliance, and manpower.
The sale does not award a generic integration package that makes every purchased colony immediately more loyal than a conquered one.

State damage, persistent hazards, civilian conditions, and explicitly state-bound improvements remain attached to the state.
Country-owned project privileges or temporary national bonuses do not move merely because their owner sold the land where they once operated.
Where an established system distinguishes a facility's physical presence from its operator, the physical facility remains and its operator is reconsidered through that system.
Part 11 carries that distinction into the event connections.

## Wars and promises of access

The buyer and seller must remain at peace with each other through closing.
A war between them cancels the transaction and returns still-refundable escrow.
Wars against third countries are not a blanket prohibition, but their effects on occupation, remaining territory, access, and affordability still apply.
A country cannot use an auction to make enemy-controlled states change hands without fighting for them.

The seller cannot promise the buyer transit through another country's land, a neutral harbor, an allied airfield, or a canal regime it does not control.
Existing access arrangements are disclosed as arrangements rather than sold as permanent property.
When ownership of a state genuinely changes a native canal or strait rule, the relevant rule determines the outcome.
The purchase of an adjacent coastline alone does not create an invented power to close an entire sea route.

A buyer may knowingly purchase an isolated foothold that requires later diplomacy or construction.
Its AI must include those additional burdens in willingness and should avoid acquisitions it has no plausible means to exploit.
The player may pursue such a difficult plan, but the lot description must not imply that missing access has already been solved.

## A single exchange, not a succession of gifts

The promised result is one complete exchange of the defined lot for the committed price.
A multi-state sale must not produce a sequence of player-facing partial awards that different countries can interrupt or buy separately after the price has been fixed.
The final acceptance evidence must account for every state and the full financial receipt.

A failure to deliver the complete lot cannot be reported as a successful sale.
The implementation must establish how its preflight, transfer verification, and failure recovery produce the required result before multi-state sales are enabled.
The specification does not assume that the engine provides a general atomic transaction or unrestricted rollback.
Any unsupported transfer case remains blocked and visible in the implementation report.

Once a sale has completed, later conquest, liberation, revolution, or annexation follows the ordinary campaign rules.
There is no auction insurance against losing a purchased possession in a future war.
The seller has sold its ownership, not a promise to defend the buyer forever.
