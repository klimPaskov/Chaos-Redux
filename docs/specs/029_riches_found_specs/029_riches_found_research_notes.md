# Event 029: Research notes

## Research purpose

The research supports five parts of the design:

- the economic logic of a resource windfall
- the local effects of a mining rush
- claims, infrastructure, labor, and boom-settlement conflict
- concessions, hidden ownership, public revenue, and security
- mining folklore as inspiration for a wholly fictional supernatural track

The event does not recreate one historical rush, one country, or one living mining tradition.

The research is used to identify recurring structures that can produce dynamic gameplay in any selected state.

## Resource windfall and economic dependence

### Main finding

A large mineral discovery can raise income, employment, public revenue, construction, and local demand while damaging other sectors, increasing prices, concentrating political incentives, and making the state dependent on one source of receipts.

The design applies this through one strong immediate national grant and a longer controller system whose benefit depends on development, governance, and extraction pressure.

The lasting reward is intentionally conditional.

### Dutch Disease

The IMF explanation of Dutch Disease describes how a resource or foreign-currency boom can crowd out traditional traded sectors while expanding the booming and non-traded sectors.

It also notes historical use of the model for gold discoveries.

Design application:

- Mine Development and national construction can grow quickly.
- Ordinary industry can lose labor and political attention.
- Central treasury dependence can make closure harder.
- A stabilization and diversification route provides long-term protection.
- The Resource Curse should include economic dependence alongside bribery.

Source: [Dutch Disease: Wealth Managed Unwisely, International Monetary Fund](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fandd/issues/series/back-to-basics/dutch-disease)

### Local mixed effects

The World Bank summary on local resource booms identifies higher demand for labor and inputs, possible wage and employment gains, inward migration, pressure on housing and services, higher non-tradable prices, and the importance of public institutions in converting revenue into roads, health, schools, housing, and growth.

It also notes that weak political responsiveness and weak administrative capacity can undermine the public benefit, while windfalls can fuel patronage and corruption.

Design application:

- The rush increases jobs and local business while straining housing, sanitation, health, and prices.
- Public projects require administration and missions.
- Revenue Legitimacy is separate from Mine Development.
- Local revenue sharing and public services are playable defenses against resentment.
- A mine can be highly developed while politically illegitimate.

Source: [Natural resource booms are a mixed blessing for local communities, too, World Bank](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/natural-resource-booms-are-mixed-blessing-local-communities-too)

## Revenue sharing and community agreements

### Main finding

Extractive revenue can support long-term local development when governments and communities can manage and distribute it.

Community development and benefit-sharing agreements can cover jobs, infrastructure, services, local investment, participation, and accountability.

Design application:

- Citizens' dividend, local revenue settlement, development fund, worker participation, and community agreement routes are distinct choices.
- Local spending can create real infrastructure and service projects.
- A contract can improve legitimacy when its obligations are visible and completed.
- A poorly administered local fund can still become a patronage target.

Sources:

- [Mining Community Development Agreements Source Book, World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/522211468329663554/pdf/712990WP0minin00Box370065B00PUBLIC0.pdf)
- [Raising and Sharing Revenues from Natural Resources, World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/216991468189569841/pdf/98885-NWP-PUBLIC-ADD-SERIES-MFM-Global-Practice-discussion-paperBox393184B.pdf)

## Claims, violence, and boom settlements

### Historical source value

Nineteenth-century California mining accounts provide concrete evidence for claim jumping, miners' courts, armed disputes, road agents, high prices, gambling, water rights, expensive infrastructure, shaft flooding, cave-ins, speculative losses, and rapid settlement growth.

The event uses these as structural inspiration.

It does not copy the social hierarchy, racial exclusion, or legal claims of one gold rush into every selected country.

### Claim jumping and miners' courts

The Diary of a Forty-Niner includes claim jumping, lethal violence, improvised legal authority, miners' courts, water rights, highway robbery, and rapid movement between exhausted and newly discovered claims.

Design application:

- The opening needs an enforceable claims policy.
- Claim registration can reduce conflict and create bribery.
- An emergency claims court can succeed, become corrupt, or intensify violence.
- Claims can be exhausted or abandoned at a local level without making the full event deposit trivial.
- The pay route and assay office are concrete raid targets.

Source: [The diary of a forty-niner, Library of Congress](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/calbk/069.pdf)

### Infrastructure, water, cave-ins, and gambling

The Shirley Letters describe the cost of shafts and water infrastructure, dangerous deep workings, abandoned projects after flooding, claims lost after major investment, cave-ins, and miners losing sudden fortunes to professional gamblers.

Design application:

- Mine Development should be built through drainage, reinforcement, transport, water, processing, and housing.
- A project can fail after significant investment.
- Rich output does not guarantee a rich worker or stable settlement.
- Gambling, debt, and predatory services fit ordinary rush incidents.
- Collapse risk should respond to physical development and pressure.

Source: [The Shirley letters from California mines in 1851-52, Library of Congress](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/calbk/146.pdf)

## Contracts, hidden ownership, and public accountability

### Contract disclosure

EITI guidance connects contract disclosure with compliance monitoring, revenue collection, corruption reduction, public understanding of local revenue flows, employment obligations, environmental duties, and broader reforms.

Design application:

- Publish the Contract is a meaningful decision.
- Public terms raise Revenue Legitimacy and strengthen later audits.
- A published bad contract can create opposition and should not grant an automatic reward.
- Contract compliance can be checked through a mission.
- Concession terms should cover development, local employment, security, and receipts.

Source: [Contracts and licenses, Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative](https://eiti.org/guidance-notes/contracts-and-licenses)

### Beneficial ownership

EITI and Opening Extractives material explains that the real individuals who ultimately own or control a company can be hidden behind legal entities, and that ownership data can help identify conflicts of interest, bribery, and high-risk licensing applications.

Design application:

- Require Beneficial-Owner Disclosure becomes a pre-award or renewal decision.
- Hidden owners appear through incidents. Do not expose them as a raw visible variable.
- A corrupt official can be linked to a bidder through an audit.
- A bidder can withdraw when disclosure is required.
- Contract transparency alone is not a complete cure, so oversight and public participation still matter.

Sources:

- [Using beneficial ownership data in extractive licensing and contracting, EITI and Open Ownership](https://eiti.org/document/25420)
- [Shining a light on company ownership, EITI](https://eiti.org/document/20078)

## Public and private security

### Main finding

Extractive projects can create security risks for workers, companies, and local residents.

Security providers can contribute to human-rights abuses, conflict with communities, theft, property damage, environmental pressure, and wider instability.

Security planning should distinguish public forces, private guards, community relations, risk assessment, and rules of engagement.

Design application:

- Mine police, private guards, army cordon, armed workers, and mixed security produce different order and legitimacy outcomes.
- Private guards can improve immediate order and create autonomy or abuse risks.
- The army cordon uses real unit, supply, fuel, and command requirements.
- Public evidence of killing, forced labor, entombment, or coverup feeds Condemnation.
- Security success cannot be represented only by a positive order number.

Source: [Voluntary Principles on Security and Human Rights, introduction and risk-assessment material](https://www.voluntaryprinciples.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Introduction-to-the-VPSHR_January-2021-1.pdf)

## Resource Curse governance design

### Main finding

Corruption risk can occur at licensing, measurement, pricing, sales, revenue transfer, local allocation, and spending.

Transparency, checks and balances, divided responsibility, public participation, and local sharing can reduce risk.

Design application:

- The assay office, shipment ledger, contract award, beneficial owner, treasury transfer, local fund, and spending project are separate incident surfaces.
- A single anti-corruption click cannot repair every point.
- The Independent Revenue Authority route separates licensing, measurement, receipts, audit, and spending.
- Centralization can improve control and also concentrate corruption.
- Local sharing can improve legitimacy and create another capture risk when local institutions are weak.

Source: [Raising and Sharing Revenues from Natural Resources, World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/216991468189569841/pdf/98885-NWP-PUBLIC-ADD-SERIES-MFM-Global-Practice-discussion-paperBox393184B.pdf)

## Mining folklore

### Cornish knockers and tommyknockers

University of Exeter research describes traditions about supernatural miners in old workings, warning knocks, guidance toward ore, punishment, industrial change, migration, and the continuing role of folklore in mining communities.

The research also emphasizes that these traditions changed across places and carried ethnic and community meaning.

Design application:

- Responsive knocks and danger warnings are suitable early supernatural motifs.
- The force can lead miners toward wealth and punish conduct.
- Folklore can change with labor systems, so the event connects supernatural behavior to claims, wage labor, guards, and contracts.
- The final entity remains fictional.
- The implementation should not call every underground apparition a tommyknocker.
- The event should not strip the tradition from its Cornish and diaspora context and present it as generic fact.

Source: [Knockers, Tommyknockers and the Question of Ghosts, University of Exeter Institute of Cornish Studies](https://www.exeter.ac.uk/research/centres/ics/research/cornish-diaspora/knockerstommyknockersandthequestionofghosts/)

### Museum summary

The National Coal Mining Museum records common motifs of small supernatural miners who hide tools, extinguish lights, lead people toward rich seams, and protect or trouble miners.

Design application:

- Hidden tools, extinguished lamps, and a dangerous reward motif can appear in early incidents.
- The final Event 029 force should not use the museum's physical creature description as a literal monster design.
- The event should focus on responsive behavior and the mine environment.

Source: [Tommyknockers, National Coal Mining Museum for England](https://www.ncm.org.uk/news/tommyknockers/)

## Living mining traditions and fictionalization boundary

Mining communities in several regions maintain living practices and beliefs around underground forces, protection, danger, reciprocity, and the moral terms of extraction.

Those traditions should not be copied into Event 029 as a generic demon bargain.

The final Demons Beneath the Mine track uses a fictional entity centered on valuation, contracts, possession, broken promises, and the transformation of authority into wealth.

It should not use a real deity's name, a copied altar, a copied offering practice, a living community's sacred symbol, or a claim that one tradition is evil.

A future implementation agent that wants to use a specific regional reference must perform new source research, identify the selected state and culture, and write a respectful localized variant. The reference must not become the event's universal supernatural identity.

Reference consulted for the boundary: [UNESCO digital document on the historic mining context of Potosi and Cerro Rico](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark%3A/48223/pf0000109875)

## Gold Disease invention boundary

Gold Disease is fictional.

The historical sources support sudden wealth, gambling, theft, claim violence, speculation, and obsession.

They do not support a real contagious syndrome with the mechanics in this event.

The design therefore treats Gold Disease as an invented high-chaos pattern of compulsive possession that moves through material access, payrolls, shipments, workforce rotation, guards, and political command.

It must not be presented as medical fact.

It must not use shared bioweapon or epidemic mechanics.

## The Gilded Sovereignty invention boundary

Company towns, concessions, private security, local courts, payroll control, housing, and infrastructure can create a practical authority around an extractive site.

The Gilded Sovereignty is a fictional culmination where those functions become a rival local government without creating a new map tag.

It is designed from the interaction of contract, security, settlement, and dependence mechanics.

It should not be presented as a universal historical outcome of mining.

## The Bottomless Account invention boundary

The Bottomless Account is entirely fictional.

It converts the event's ordinary accounting, contract, valuation, and promise systems into a supernatural bargain.

The payment rules are deliberately bounded.

The event does not treat unrelated deaths as currency and does not reward open-ended killing.

## Sources not used as mechanical authority

Popular summaries, unsourced quote pages, modern fictional works, film scenes, and tourism pages were not used as the factual basis for the design.

A lower-authority source can help locate a topic, but the specification relies on institutional, academic, archive, and primary-source material for its main claims.

No direct quote from the research is required in final localisation.

Any later quotation, cultural remark, or specific religious reference must be researched and sourced through the appropriate text-research workflow before implementation.
