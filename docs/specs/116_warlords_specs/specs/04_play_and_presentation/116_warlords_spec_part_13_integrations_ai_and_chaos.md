# Part 13: Integrations, AI, and Chaos

Warlords interacts with several military and internal-collapse systems. Each contributes the actors and consequences it actually owns. 116 must preserve those distinctions while making the combined campaign understandable.

Exact 114, Mutiny, Resistance, and some other owner interfaces were not available in the inspected evidence. Their interactions here are design contracts awaiting owner registration and full relevant implementation reads. Their absence does not authorize a guessed API or a substituted event ID.

## Handling overlapping crises

Before a birth, treaty, integration, or destruction, ask each relevant owner about its exact involved actors and assets. Freeze immutable owner identities before entering nested helpers. A moved selected-row index is not a person. A mutable global target is not a frozen recipient.

Reconcile overlapping custody before reserving anything. Identify each source fact by owner, current actor or asset, generation, cause, and transaction. An expired reservation is not a completed transfer, and an already transferred formation is no longer a parent asset. When 019,021, desertion, mutiny, liberation, or an ordinary war supplies conflicting claims, exclude the disputed contribution and rerun viability. Preserve the first lawful committed custody and its history. Do not create another government from the same historical territory and army.

If an existing 019 country is already the relevant personal command, register that same actor as a rival or participant. Do not split another copy out of its parent, duplicate its founder, or reset its paid liabilities. If the 019 asset owner refuses participation, remove only that unproved contribution from the proposed package and rerun viability.

If 021 is active, distinguish an institutional civil-war government from a personal military breakaway. A general can emerge as a separate command only with an actual viable footprint and a reconciled war/settlement relationship. A negotiated partition can leave several real governments. The actual agreement controls which commands remain afterward.

Hunger Lines hosts remain their own actors. A warlord may fight one, trade with one, exploit its weakness, or have a temporary common enemy. The relation follows real interests and capabilities. A mutinous division is not a Hannibal node, and Generalissimo conquest does not inherit that owner's terminal eligibility.

This boundary works in both directions. A host conquering an ordinary warlord does not automatically turn that original command into an Event 014 host. An ordinary victor conquering a host does not inherit Larder, cells, paid cannibal recruitment, or terminal permissions through annexation. Surviving host institutions, specialist assets, and modeled population consequences remain with their exact owners. Ordinary conquest, alliance, or region transfer creates no population-consumption receipt.

An unavailable optional 114, Mutiny, or Resistance adapter contributes no speculative personnel, equipment, or disruption. The ordinary 116 design must work without that optional contribution. A later registered adapter needs current actor and region, measured eligible assets or native disruption, pending-versus-completed status, and replay identity. Generic low war support or a dispatch notice cannot stand in for an exact defection or civilian-loss receipt. A core operation that actually depends on an unavailable owner's actor or asset remains blocked rather than ignoring its ownership.

## Chaos impact map

These are proposed starting bands for distinct abnormal consequences. Shared sources retain ordinary war, annexation, subjecthood, civilian death, nuclear use, and other already-accounted effects. When another owner has already charged the same political or military consequence, 116 contributes zero for that cause. An event log entry is never the cause of Chaos.

| Material consequence | Direction and starting band | Dynamic factors and repeat guard | Containment counterpart and overlap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First committed personal military secession | Increase, about 8 to 18 | Parent importance, actual force and territory lost, first origin receipt only | Avoid the ordinary war charge, actual prevention before commit grants no fictitious secession |
| Additional independent command appears | Increase, about 4 to 10 | Actual new fragmentation, remaining parent fragility, diminishing lineage/area significance | Successful containment removes current warned instability only, not previous destruction |
| Major real officer/formation defection beyond an already counted birth | Increase, about 2 to 6 | Size and strategic importance of the unique actual cohort | Zero if already covered by 019,114, Mutiny, or the birth receipt |
| A spreading command breakdown defeats meaningful containment | Increase, about 3 to 8 | Previously viable containment materially fails, bounded per actual warning | A canceled timer or an unaffordable click is not failure of containment |
| Warlord growth becomes a durable new regional military order | Increase, about 4 to 12 | First substantial controlled-capacity threshold for that lineage, once per justified band | Ordinary conquest already counted elsewhere, only distinct institutional fragmentation or coercive reach qualifies |
| Patron war materially widens the crisis | Usually zero extra, at most a separately justified 2 to 5 premium | Only a new cross-border military-order consequence beyond the shared war source | Recognition or an undelivered aid offer adds zero |
| Stronger Warlords produces a genuinely larger new rupture | Included in the birth consequence band | Scale the same receipt by actual assets and loss, do not add a second evolution charge | Evolution activation itself is zero |
| Actual domestic military unification creates a coercive command order | Increase, about 10 to 25 if not covered elsewhere | Distinct performed command integration across rivals, one origin milestone | The Supreme War State name or formation button alone is zero |
| First durable commanded foreign great power in a new theater | Increase, about 5 to 15 | Actual new international obedience and usable reach, bounded first-theater receipts with diminishing returns | No duplicate annexation or puppet charge, old conquests and liberated powers provide no new receipt |
| A real rival or major subordinate becomes independent | Decrease, about 4 to 12 | Amount of current coercive command removed, one real separation receipt with anti-cycle guard | Control credit is removed immediately, reduction cannot be farmed by repeated voluntary release and reconquest |
| Negotiated settlement closes a real active fracture | Decrease, about 3 to 8 | Performed obligations and stabilized current command, unique settlement | Does not reverse deaths, destroyed industry, or ongoing unrelated wars |
| Last current origin military crisis is durably resolved | Decrease, about 6 to 15 | Real stabilization and absence of an unresolved active command, once per origin resolution | A historical completion flag with surviving independent crises is insufficient |
| Terminal central command is actually defeated | Decrease, owner-coordinated starting band about 30 to 80 | Current abnormal military-order pressure removed, single terminal outcome | Shared terminal owner decides overlap, historical deaths and ordinary automatic-event stop remain |
| Reconstruction or migration | Zero direct premium by default | Actual existing economic and civilian owners record their consequences | No repeated gratitude or population-transfer farming |
| Evolution activation, logging, focus completion, readiness display, terminal launch | Zero by itself | These describe or enable consequences | Never grant a large amount merely to push Chaos above 1000 |

Store exact cause identity and the already-attributed owners with each contribution. A territory can change hands many times without repeatedly counting as a first regional emergence. Restoring a current command may restore its present threat contribution, but it does not mint another unique rise-of-the-order milestone.

Manual scenario setup uses its own explicit Chaos controls and origin. Subsequent real consequences use this map with normal overlap guards. Opening a manual scenario must not secretly satisfy ordinary first-fire or evolution achievement history.

## Ordinary firing and the Liberations cluster

116 is a High member of Liberations. The existing two-pass cluster selection rules remain authoritative. A nontrigger High member requires the appropriate pass-one eligible support unless it is the trigger or sole eligible member under the shared rules. Country viability and valid-general eligibility still apply. A cluster must not force a breakaway into an invalid parent to fill its membership.

Use one shared cluster pacing consequence, not a separate pacing charge for each member. A delayed event row rechecks its exact batch, history, selected target, and current eligibility. If another crisis invalidated the region or general, retire the row with the canonical reason and preserve unused ordinary fire-once eligibility. Do not reuse stale metadata from another delayed row.

Once any world end is active, ordinary automatic event and evolution dispatch stops under the shared world-end rules. Actor-owned resolution of existing wars, missions, treaties, defections, and terminal counterplay is kept distinct from that dispatcher. The branch cannot evade the stop by relabeling a new ordinary event as maintenance.

## AI roles

| Actor | Ordinary priority | Under severe pressure | What it must refuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strong parent | Secure remaining command, defend external fronts, prepare a realistic recovery | Negotiate limited terms if a second war makes recovery unsustainable | Unpayable projects, impossible arrests, automatic submission of others |
| Weak or overextended parent | Preserve a viable core, supplies, and a tolerable settlement | Regional compact or emergency leadership can outweigh reconquest | An offensive with no supply or credible force basis |
| Small warlord | Hold its base, secure replacement equipment, choose a sustainable constitution | Seek a patron, armistice, or limited submission before annihilation | Unlimited recruitment without stocks and suicidal multi-front expansion |
| Strong expanding warlord | Develop administration and industry while exploiting feasible local wars | Consolidate or compromise when overextended | Treating every neighboring country as a compulsory target |
| Rival commands | Bargain, compete, and defend autonomy according to actual interests | Temporary coalition or genuine surrender when credible | Mistaking recognition or faction membership for surrender |
| Foreign patron | Support a useful accessible actor at an affordable price | Escalate only when strategic stakes justify the real war | Undeliverable aid, free units, duplicated contracts, obligations signed for another state |
| Generalissimo before unity | Defeat or subordinate remaining actual rivals, then integrate them | Rebuild cohesion and routes before opening a larger front | Premature Supreme War State formation or empty diplomatic unification |
| Supreme War State | Maintain unity and logistics, pursue achievable major campaigns across regions | Prioritize threatened command links and genuine defections | Ignoring domestic independence while counting historical victories |
| Terminal opponents | Preserve independent capacity, cooperate where useful, break the order's real command links | Support viable surviving commands and defend productive theaters | Automatic coalition membership without actual agreement |
| Liberated successors | Secure supply and political continuity, resolve urgent local disputes | Accept practical assistance without surrendering independence by default | Recreating historic armies or immediately repeating an unaffordable revolt |

Rare routes should remain possible for a coherent reason. A skilled but weak founder may choose a bold campaign if the parent is collapsing and a patron has actually supplied him. A strong parent may recognize partition when its external survival matters more. A Generalissimo may lose unity by choosing an external gamble. High Chaos broadens instability and risk tolerance within real constraints. It does not set every aggressive action to certainty.

## Performance and persistence

Keep an origin crisis registry, actual participating actors, exact manifests, current command relationships, and relevant routes or states. Callbacks mark only affected proof stale. Bounded work queues retain their cursor and unvisited suffix when a supported iteration budget is reached. A truncated loop is not a completed census or a successful transaction.

Random selection is authoritative and persisted. Save/load reconstructs transient display state from immutable actor identities. Duplicate player requests cannot charge, transfer, or complete twice. Shared terminal competition has one authoritative winner. These are acceptance requirements awaiting actual runtime proof.

Register a CXT extension without activating the event. Daily registration may mark a new system as pending, but resources, units, research, and crisis fixtures are applied only through the user's registered debug action. No periodic free replenishment or autonomous game launch belongs to this planning package.
