# Part 14: Manual scenarios and player presentation

Manual scenarios let the player begin a particular warlord situation without waiting for ordinary event timing. They choose an initial crisis, not a completed campaign or a world-end victory. Every country and army in the setup still needs an honest physical basis.

## One host, four scenario profiles

Register one Warlords scenario family with four explicit profiles and the shared Low, Medium, High, and Max intensity choices. The numeric scenario identifier remains unallocated pending the current shared registry audit. Do not reuse SCN015 from an older proposal because the inspected shared list already uses that raw ID elsewhere.

| Profile | Low | Medium | High | Max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Single breakaway | One small viable warlord, limited defections | One substantial coherent breakaway | One strong industrial or military breakaway | One severe but still viable split using the maximum permitted real share |
| Several rival commands | Two warlords | Three warlords | Four warlords | Up to six expressly selected warlords, exact preview count required |
| Armed partition | Parent and one independent command under an actual prepared settlement | Parent and two independent commands | Parent and three independent commands | Parent and four independent commands with a more demanding but valid partition |
| Contested Generalissimo unification | Canonical 067 host and one independent rival | Host and two rivals | Host and three rivals | Host and up to five selected rivals with strong actual commands |

The count in an accepted preview is exact. An intensity's documented maximum is not permission to silently deliver fewer actors. If the chosen host cannot sustain the requested profile, return a clear reason and let the player choose a different host, profile, or intensity. Do not downgrade after confirmation.

Strength within each profile scales through actual region size, force quality and quantity, stocks, local military concentration, supply, and political strain. It does not give Low empty countries or Max unlimited armies. All final partitions are evaluated together, so every actor and the remaining parent remain viable after the complete setup.

The partition profile needs a real owner-supported treaty and exact signatories. The Generalissimo profile needs the actual 067 owner to create or resolve its canonical scenario actor lawfully. A permanently removed Generalissimo cannot be resurrected by this setup. If the owner interface is unavailable, that profile remains an explicit unavailable implementation path instead of pretending an ordinary general is equivalent.

## Confirmation and execution

Use the shared Cancel-first confirmation. Freeze the exact host, human seat, profile, intensity, actors, founder references, states, force manifests, setup options, and intended war or treaty relationships. The final mutation uses this frozen payload, not the current row or last-opened scenario window.

A human seat change is explicit. Remaining in the current parent is the default where appropriate. If the player chooses a new warlord or Generalissimo host, the transfer occurs only after that exact country has been proved playable. Multiplayer uses the authorized shared host and synchronized seat rules. One player's local UI state cannot change another player's country.

The setup may bypass ordinary event timing, Chaos entry thresholds, evolution date/tier prerequisites, and ordinary fire history. It does not bypass actual-character continuity, country existence, current wars and signatories, population, force conservation, supply, the parent survival requirement, or the terminal gate. No hidden date, evolution, or campaign prerequisite locks a scenario that the user did not ask to lock.

Where a scenario expressly selects an evolved configuration, that configuration is scenario-origin setup. It does not manufacture ordinary evolution activation history. Any explicit starting Chaos adjustment belongs to the shared scenario controls and is disclosed in the frozen preview. This pack provides no instant Final Warlord scenario and no preset that grants post-formation global victories.

Scenario transactions apply sequentially with an aggregate reserved footprint. A failure must not leave a partly initialized actor presented as a successful complete setup. Use exact pre-frontier recovery where possible and retained quarantine evidence where not. Do not silently consume the ordinary event or standard achievements after a failed manual setup.

## Information hierarchy

| Surface | What it should tell the player | What it should not become |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening event | Actual founder, origin, region, and immediate political situation | A page of implementation receipts or a preview of every hidden future |
| Map and country links | Current borders, fronts, capitals, actors, and supply context | A painted map that disagrees with game ownership |
| Parent decisions | Current commands at risk, actionable local problems, recovery and settlement choices | A global management screen for unrelated countries |
| Warlord decisions | One Command Cohesion value, current institutional route, real shortages and contracts | Several new loyalty, economy, and patron currencies |
| Generalissimo roster | Unresolved rivals, genuinely commanded actors, historical successes distinguished from present authority | A nominal puppet counter treated as effective command |
| Readiness requirements | Satisfied, missing, stale, or unknown requirements and the actual affected actors/regions/routes | A second continuous global-dominance score or omniscient enemy stockpile display |
| Event Details and log | Identity, context, current lifecycle, relationships, and appropriate history | A duplicate list of event effects or temporary helper state |
| Shared settings | Independent ordinary-event, evolution, scenario, and world-end controls in their proper registries | One checkbox that unexpectedly disables sibling owners or undoes an active ending |

Only Command Cohesion is introduced as a new continuous public value. Global industrial, population, and field-strength estimates remain private qualification evidence. Show the required thresholds and whether each is proved, plus relevant country/state links and intelligible causes of failure. Do not create extra progress bars or numeric power meters under different names. Standard native facts and explicit mission dates remain available through their ordinary surfaces.

The user must be able to distinguish a historically conquered country from a currently commanded one. A clear status marker, actual country link, and reason for exclusion are more useful than a polished total that conceals the difference.

## Map and formation visibility

The initial region is an exact set of installed states. Use native state highlighting and target tooltips to show the proposed or actual footprint. Capitals, safe routes, and remaining viable components must match the same predicate used by the transaction. A small static image cannot replace current state-control proof.

Supreme War State formation is primarily an actor-and-command requirement rather than a fixed historic territorial puzzle. Show the current roster and its exact relevant states through native map and country links. If implementation introduces a true exact-state formable subroute or a dedicated state puzzle, use the project's exact installed-state geometry workflow with non-color cues, current tooltips, and the same formation predicate. Do not create invented borders through image generation.

Prefer ordinary decision categories, targeted rows, focus content, map links, and compact existing detail surfaces. The functional categories contain live information, so they do not receive a decorative category picture with controls painted into it. A new event-owned GUI is justified only if these native surfaces fail a specific important task. It would require separate reference images, a bounded UI-worker assignment, matched scenarios, and actual render review before acceptance.

## Reports and super-events

| Moment | Presentation level | Direction and threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First breakaway | Parent/founder event and relevant news | Concrete loss of military and territorial authority, actual general and region |
| Additional breakaways | Regional reports with escalation variations | Show the changing map and rival commands, avoid identical alarm copy on every birth |
| Successful partition or civilian transition | Country event, limited news when geopolitically significant | Terms, continuing independence, and the real future of the armed forces |
| Supreme War State formation | Country event, with a super-event only for a substantial regional military order | Actual integrated campaigns and political consolidation, no implication that world domination has happened |
| International consolidation begins | Strategic news and relevant missions | Observable cross-regional obedience, constrained remaining powers, vulnerable routes |
| The Final Warlord launch | Dedicated world-end super-event | The existing military order enters its terminal campaign after the exact shared commit |
| Terminal order defeated | Structured aftermath and researched global presentation | Broken central command, surviving independent governments and armies, irreversible human and material losses |

The domestic formation super-event requires the political formation and a substantial regional impact, initially at least three meaningful rival commands integrated or an established major root operating at least 30 usable military factories under actual command. This controls spectacle only and does not require an international baseline before the international phase begins. A smaller lawful Supreme War State can form without the global super-event. A late terminal defeat merits global treatment because the actual preceding cross-regional campaign and consolidation already establish a prolonged world threat.

Super-event copy and audio remain research-owned. Research directions are in the separate handoff. No unverified quotation, final slogan, music recording, or pasted sample text is approved by the presentation table.
