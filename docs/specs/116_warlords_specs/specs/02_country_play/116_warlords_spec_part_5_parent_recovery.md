# Part 5: The parent country's response

The parent is a playable country under pressure, not a target waiting to be dismantled. It retains its existing government, national content, surviving forces, industry, population, and foreign relationships. Its problem is to decide which losses to reverse, which compromises to accept, and how much power to give its remaining commanders.

## Four strategic responses

| Route | What the parent pursues | Material and political tradeoff | Durable outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Military recovery | Defeat the breakaway, recover territory, and integrate surviving institutions | Concentrates troops and industry on an internal front while other enemies can act | Restored territory with real reconstruction and military settlement obligations |
| Regional compact | Keep or restore a common state through negotiated regional authority | Surrenders specified central powers and may limit central recruitment or policy | A functioning negotiated state if both sides actually perform their obligations |
| Negotiated partition | End the war with a recognized independent neighbor and an enforceable border | Accepts permanent territorial and strategic losses | Independent governments, actual treaties, and unresolved interests rather than a reset |
| Emergency military order | Give a command leadership greater power to recover the country | Strengthens military coordination while weakening civilian authority and increasing succession stakes | A military government, potentially connected to 067, but no automatic Supreme War State |

The choices are compatible in sequence where politics permits. A failed offensive can lead to negotiation. A violated compact can lead back to war. Emergency powers can be rolled back through an earned civilian settlement. A signed agreement does not erase the need for actual control, demobilization, or army integration.

## Immediate priorities

The first response should identify the remaining capital, the threatened routes, the actual front, and the most exposed depots. The parent can station real formations to secure them. A depot-security mission succeeds after its required places remain controlled and accessible with the assigned capacity for a sustained period. A paper appointment or an army on another continent does not satisfy it.

Reorganizing command uses available commanders and staff capacity. It can reduce confusion and make later defections less likely, but it cannot make every general permanently loyal. Replacing a commander removes his authority over the relevant troops through a real appointment. It must not duplicate him in a dormant country or silently delete another event's canonical actor.

Restoring military administration commits real industrial time, transport capacity, and available support resources. It helps the forces that can actually be supplied. If the parent still cannot feed or equip an isolated army, an administrative decision does not bypass that condition.

## Recovering territory through war

Military objectives should be specific places and operational conditions: secure the route to a threatened industrial district, retake a supply hub, defeat a field command, or recover the lost capital region. These are mission objectives resolved by actual ownership, control, army presence, and the relevant war outcome. A focus can prepare or reward a campaign. It cannot perform the conquest.

An offensive commits opportunity cost. The parent may expose another border, delay civilian reconstruction, or divert weapons from its external war. Success returns whatever remains in the recovered region. Damaged factories, lost manpower, expended ammunition, broken railways, and displaced civilians remain damaged, lost, expended, broken, or displaced until their actual systems resolve them.

The parent must choose what to do with surviving forces. A verified amnesty can incorporate specified formations and officers under a real chain of command. Disarmament removes their independent military capacity and returns only the accounted manpower and equipment permitted by the settlement. Trials or exclusion can remove eligible political actors but do not create an equipment windfall. Named protected or owner-held characters require their owner's lawful removal route.

## Preventing a later defection

When The Generals Follow is active, a credible prospective command can become a warned crisis. The parent sees the affected region, the dissatisfied command, the relevant shortages or political grievance, and the available responses. Exact hidden intentions need not be public, but the practical choices must be understandable.

Possible responses include resolving the material grievance, transferring authority through a lawful reassignment, offering a bounded regional bargain, dispersing a dangerously concentrated command through actual movement, or attempting an arrest. An arrest requires the actual character to be reachable and a credible security advantage. It is a risky intervention in an existing crisis, not a repeatable remove-general button. A failed arrest can worsen a viable escape attempt. If no viable independent region remains, failure produces domestic disruption rather than a new country from nowhere.

A response succeeds through completed facts, not merely paid costs. A player who orders supplies but loses the route has not delivered them. A general who accepts reassignment but still controls the same army has not surrendered that command. If the threatening person dies or becomes legitimately unavailable, close the specific warning without moving its progress onto a randomly chosen replacement.

## Negotiating a real settlement

A compact identifies the participating governments, territory, military command, taxation or industrial support, recruitment rights, foreign-policy obligations, and means of leaving or enforcing the arrangement. Every obligation has an actual actor and a measurable outcome. An ally or sponsor cannot sign for an independent warlord without authority.

A regional settlement can preserve local administration and a local army. If that army remains independent, it remains outside Generalissimo command even if a common flag or alliance is adopted. A genuine commanded subject can retain local government while giving the central command real authority over existing and future armed forces. The qualification rules must describe the actual arrangement.

Partition is an honest outcome. It can settle the current war and retire a particular defection warning while leaving an independent military neighbor. Claims, relations, and future wars follow the agreement and ordinary diplomacy. The original lost country does not become automatically eligible for another ordinary first firing. New owner-driven crises can interact with the surviving actors without resetting their identities.

## Reconstruction and public legitimacy

Reconstruction prioritizes damaged transport, productive capacity, veteran resettlement, and civil administration. Use the shared civilian and migration owners where actual population movement or death is modeled. Territorial transfer itself is neither death nor migration.

The player may choose a slower settlement that preserves institutions or an emergency arrangement that provides quicker military coordination. The benefits must be visible and useful. The penalties must follow the bargain, such as reduced central access to regional manpower, industrial reservations, or a more difficult return to civilian control. Avoid a permanent generic stability penalty that remains after its cause has been resolved.

## AI and player equivalence

A strong parent with a defensible front usually attempts containment and military recovery. An overextended parent facing external enemies is more willing to negotiate. A parent with severe command breakdown may accept emergency leadership or a regional compact. Desperation changes priorities rather than waiving costs or granting unearned conquest.

The AI evaluates the same available region, supplies, threats, and settlement constraints as a human. It must not repeatedly start an unaffordable recovery action, reject every feasible peace while incapable of fighting, or sign away another participant's obligations. Failed or impossible missions close with a precise reason and release only recoverable reservations.

Parent-specific content becomes dormant when its actual crisis ends. Its completed history, legitimate national effects, and unresolved reconstruction remain. Temporary anti-defection measures end when their conditions no longer apply. Ordinary national focus content remains available throughout.
