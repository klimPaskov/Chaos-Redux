# Warlords 116: research and presentation directions

Research supports the event's emphasis on personal military authority, viable regional government, material supply, and reversible conquest. It does not supply a historical blueprint that every country must imitate. The user brief defines the event. The project sources define its integration and production rules.

## Historical observations and design inferences

James E. Sheridan's chapter summary describes Chinese warlord authority through a personal army, territorial control or ambition, and considerable independence. It also emphasizes the diversity of the commanders. This supports treating a warlord as a political and military actor with several possible governing futures. It does not justify assigning one personality, ethnicity, ideology, or behavior to all generals. Only the accessible summary and relevant passage were consulted, not the full chapter. [H1]

Huaiyin Li's research-article abstract argues that regional government, fiscal institutions, and access to economic resources affected competition between military regimes in early Republican China. The design inference is to make administration, industry, and replacement capacity useful alongside personal loyalty. The article's conclusion is an analytical perspective on that historical setting, not a rule that a focus completion should automatically unify a country. Only the accessible abstract and metadata were used. [H2]

A 1919 American diplomatic report describes railway disruption, military requisition of rolling stock, and shortages affecting transport and trade. This is a concrete historical reason to treat transport as a shared civilian and military constraint. The report is a foreign diplomatic and commercial perspective and includes prejudiced judgments that the design does not adopt. Relevant transport passages were read, not the entire surrounding diplomatic volume. [H3]

These sources support the design choice to let a small regime become durable through actual government and supply. They do not establish the proposed balance numbers. The numerical thresholds come from this package's authoring decisions and require later campaign testing.

## Contextual super-event candidates

The following quotations are research candidates only. They are not final localisation, titles, options, slogans, or approved super-event text.

| Source | Short candidate | Possible function and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thomas Hobbes, *Leviathan*, Part II, chapter XVII | “And Covenants, without the Sword, are but Words” | A critical framing of a military order's insistence on enforceable obedience. It needs surrounding context and should not make coercion appear morally endorsed by the authoring voice. [Q1] |
| Carl von Clausewitz, *On War*, Book I, chapter I, section 9, J.J. Graham translation | “The result in war is never absolute” | A section heading suitable for research toward liberation or the order's defeat. The following discussion concerns the provisional character of political outcomes, fitting the distinction between historical victory and present control. It is a translation-specific wording. [Q2] |

For domestic formation, emphasize the achieved concentration of command and the political bargain it imposes. For terminal launch, emphasize the already accomplished international hierarchy and the remaining world's position. For defeat, emphasize the loss of central obedience, surviving successors, and irreversible costs. Do not use one identical quotation and image for all three moments.

The researcher supplied other literary and historical candidates in working notes. The parent selected the two directions above for continued review rather than expanding the final package into a quotation anthology. No final text choice or audio production is claimed.

## Audio direction and remaining work

Domestic formation can use a disciplined military or ceremonial cue with a restrained presentation. Terminal launch needs scale and command pressure without implying invulnerability. Defeat should allow gravity and unresolved aftermath rather than an uncomplicated victory jingle.

No final recording has been chosen. A Library of Congress catalog result for a historical *Marche slav* recording was located during exploratory research, but access, listening, excerpt choice, and recording-rights review were not completed. It is therefore not an approved asset or a recommendation to use that recording. Composition provenance, performance/recording rights, actual listening, timing, mix, and consumer export remain separate future requirements.
