# Event 061 historical research

## Research purpose

This note records the historical material used to strengthen the design of Return to Peacetime.

The event remains a global alternate-history mechanic. The sources support process, pacing, institutions, and visible details. They do not justify applying one country's exact postwar policy to every country.

## Main findings

## Reconversion required dedicated institutions

The United States Office of War Mobilization and Reconversion coordinated wartime and postwar economic planning. Its record groups include contract settlement, construction, stockpiling, surplus property disposal, manpower, veterans affairs, reconversion programming, production, stabilization, and war liquidation.

Design use:

- rearmament should require institutions and contracts, not one instant law click
- Industrial Reconversion Shock should cover supply, labour, construction, and production disruption
- Event 61 needs separate but connected factory, manpower, stockpile, and law surfaces
- a Defence Ministry, procurement office, or equivalent planning institution is a plausible recovery step for many governments

Source:

- U.S. National Archives and Records Administration, Records of the Office of War Mobilization and Reconversion, Record Group 250
- https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/250.html

## Rapid remobilization was a recognized problem

The final War Production Board report of October 1945 warned that rapid future mobilization could not be assumed. It recommended retaining a small peacetime organization that would keep industrial mobilization plans current.

Design use:

- Rearmament Readiness should reward a preserved planning skeleton
- Reconstitute the General Staff and Restart Arms Contracts should matter even before all factories return
- majors and faction leaders should usually maintain a higher peacetime Readiness target
- a country that preserves records and cadres should suffer less under The Great Demobilization

Source:

- War Production Board, *Wartime Production Achievements and the Reconversion Outlook*, October 9, 1945
- U.S. Government Publishing Office digital copy
- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-P32_4800-042ea7e5f3aabaa77ec650cc3aa0938f/html/GOVPUB-P32_4800-042ea7e5f3aabaa77ec650cc3aa0938f.htm

## Demobilization was phased and prioritized

The National Army Museum describes British demobilization beginning on June 18, 1945. Release normally followed an age-and-service number, with priority for people whose civilian work was important to postwar reconstruction.

Design use:

- The Great Demobilization needs a warning period and selection logic
- unit release should consider cadres, specialists, and strategic need
- Veteran Reintegration can support reconstruction without becoming a free manpower reward
- a demobilization result should describe transport, work, and administrative pressures

Source:

- National Army Museum, “On This Day: June 1945”
- https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/june-1945

## Transport and overseas obligations constrained release

The Australian War Memorial records that planning began before the war ended. General demobilization ran from October 1, 1945 to February 15, 1947. Shipping limits, garrison needs, and the scale of a force of almost 600,000 shaped the schedule.

Design use:

- demobilization should not resolve as an unannounced instant deletion
- overseas, expeditionary, transported, and active-front units need safety exclusions
- convoys, trains, and transport capacity are relevant to stockpile and timeline decisions
- a large country should need more time to complete the transition

Source:

- Australian War Memorial, “Soldiers to citizens”
- https://www.awm.gov.au/wartime/45/article

## Surplus disposal covered many kinds of property

The U.S. War Assets Administration disposed of surplus consumer goods, capital and producer goods, industrial and maritime property, airports, and aircraft.

Design use:

- Swords into Ploughshares can represent sale, conversion, dismantling, and parts recovery
- the evolution should not claim that every removed item was melted down
- broad equipment families can produce a reconstruction-value total
- industrial property and machine tools support a timed rebuilding benefit

Source:

- U.S. National Archives and Records Administration, Records of the War Assets Administration, Record Group 270
- https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/270.html

## Postwar transition carried economic uncertainty

Federal Reserve History describes wartime resource reallocation, restricted civilian goods, and fears that the end of military spending could produce recession and unemployment.

Design use:

- civilian conversion should create opportunity and transition cost at the same time
- Industrial Reconversion Shock should be severe even though the country gains civilian factories
- Veteran Reintegration should include employment or housing pressure
- Peace Dividend should remain bounded

Source:

- Federal Reserve History, “The Second World War and Its Aftermath”
- https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/wwii-and-its-aftermath

## Design limits

The research does not support these claims:

- every postwar country converted exactly half of its arms factories
- every country transferred a fixed share of public support into political stability
- every demobilization used the same release formula
- a legal No Army category was a universal postwar outcome

Those are deliberate Chaos Redux mechanics supplied by the event concept.

The historical evidence supports the deeper process around them:

- phased release
- contract settlement
- stockpile and surplus disposal
- transport limits
- manpower and veteran administration
- the value of preserving mobilization institutions
- the difficulty of reversing reconversion

## Regional writing guidance

Final localisation should remain globally adaptable.

Do not use United States or British agency names for every country.

Use generic institutional language such as:

- procurement office
- contract settlement board
- demobilization bureau
- reconstruction ministry
- defence planning staff
- surplus property office
- service registry

Country-specific localisation can use a local ministry or institutional form when the repository already supports it.

## Visual research guidance

Useful grounded subjects:

- factory retooling
- machine tools
- contract offices
- military depots
- surplus property yards
- railway demobilization centers
- returned uniforms and equipment
- civilian construction materials

Avoid copying a specific archival photograph without source and rights review.

The planned event images are fictional global scenes, so generated art is the default source mode.
