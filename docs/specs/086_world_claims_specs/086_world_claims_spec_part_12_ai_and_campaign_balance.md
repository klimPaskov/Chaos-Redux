# World Claims: believable ambitions and bounded expansion

## Equal automatic rules

Human and AI governments use the same frontier, Compliance, core, and formation rules. The AI does not need to open the geographic interface, click a map piece, or receive a hidden all-world claim shortcut.

The universal automatic effects are not an AI choice. Every valid government receives its applicable claims and starting entitlements. The AI's choices concern public ambitions, investments, assurances, proclamations, and whether ordinary diplomacy makes an expansion sensible.

Original major-power status must not select the only countries capable of success. A small country with a coherent new heartland can form a greater state. A major with disconnected claims and unstable occupation can remain unready.

## Candidate preference

The AI prefers candidates that fit substantial current holdings, have reachable missing territory, preserve a defensible center, and reuse a genuinely eligible native formable. It penalizes projects dominated by inaccessible territory, strong friendly states, active truces, or missing provider conditions.

An already selected ambition receives an inertia preference. Minor fluctuations do not trigger repeated switches. A switch is appropriate after a durable change in the center of power, a completed project, or a major territorial defeat that makes the old project implausible.

The three suggested candidates are selected by deterministic scoring and stable tie-breaking. They are not three random regions. Candidate discovery and ranking do not depend on country iteration order or a client-specific visual state.

## Project choices

Civil integration is useful when a state has a genuine Compliance deficit, is likely to remain controlled, and matters to the current ambition. The AI reserves sufficient equipment for its native military needs. It avoids paying to help an already cored state, repeat a consumed reward, or invest in a target about to be lost.

Connecting the heartland is attractive when the corridor anchors are defensible and a real connection can be maintained or repaired. A disconnected maritime project without a validated logistics predicate has zero validity for both AI and human countries.

A consolidation pledge is appropriate only near the selected ambition's requirements and with a credible defense. The AI does not accept a public failure penalty merely because the mission is free.

An assurance is more attractive when the shared dispute distracts from a stronger threat, when both parties have limited attainable gains, or when an existing faction relationship makes confrontation undesirable. An assurance that conflicts with an unavoidable ongoing war or an active committed offensive is invalid rather than a high-weight option.

A valid completed proclamation is normally attractive. Identity adoption can respect an existing country-owned cosmetic identity and choose territorial recognition without replacement. The AI never pays a generic cost and then separately pays the native provider's cost for the same formation.

## Probability evidence

The decision weights and Evolution timing inputs require named scenario evaluation through the project's AI probability auditor and installed engine adapter. A source-level weight of 100 is not automatically a 100-percent chance across every evaluation schedule and competing choice set.

Required scenarios include a wealthy peaceful regional power, an equipment-starved country, a threatened minor, a subject with blocked proclamation, a nearly formed state, an inaccessible island ambition, two overlapping allies, a defeated claimant, and a late global empire with many active targets.

The evidence must distinguish exact normalized choices, bounded estimates, sampled uncertain inputs, and unresolved timing. The intended preference is a design decision. The auditor supplies evidence and does not silently redesign costs or behavior to make a test pass.

## Where the power lies

The main power increase comes from repeated geographic access to claims, high starting Compliance, and eventual cores. The package does not add a stack of unrelated industrial, military, and research bonuses to every stage.

At low stages, conquest still requires the ordinary campaign and integration still takes time. At high stages, the event deliberately accelerates consolidation. The 100-percent initial entitlement and short late core standard are substantial rewards, so they must retain claimant isolation, control continuity, and consumed-grant receipts.

Optional projects help difficult targets or connections. They do not become mandatory purchases for automatic claims or ordinary earned cores. Costs scale with the work selected, not a historical major/minor classification.

## Pacing and iteration limits

Every immediate neighboring foreign state is claimed without a target-count cap. Outer-ring selections use fixed per-wave allowances, stable scoring, and a frozen starting frontier. A large country can have a large first ring, but a small country cannot recursively consume the map during one iteration.

The World Is Ours adds only one new layer from the previously established connected claim network in a firing. Newly added targets do not seed another layer in that same wave. A repeatable event can eventually reach very far without becoming an unconditional planet-wide effect.

The event uses the shared minor-event scheduler, repeat-weight recovery, caps, cluster selection, and once-per-firing accounting. It does not install an independent second countdown. Selecting World Claims inside a cluster does not charge the event engine once for every affected country.

## Work proportional to active content

A firing legitimately visits participating countries and their bounded frontier data. Between firings, control changes, paid missions, and the next due integration transitions drive the work. Persistent presentation does not justify daily or weekly country-by-state scans.

The model stores sparse claimant-state relationships. A large late campaign can still create many of them, so the implementation needs measured save size and work-queue behavior. Splitting work into engine-safe batches must preserve one logical snapshot and must not let a country's position in the queue change its results.

A delayed core check can safely award later after revalidation. It cannot award early because a queue used a stale deadline. A delayed visible summary must never override the live final formation test.

## Fair failure and recovery

Losing control interrupts integration without granting a second starting bonus on recapture. Technical cancellation and military defeat remain different. A player cannot turn project cancellation into a stockpile generator.

The single staged confidence effect prevents a pile of simultaneous spirits. Success replaces disappointment with a bounded confidence period. A public failure replaces the current confidence state with the documented disappointment period. Neither transition adds permanent Stability or War Support through repeated direct additions.

Restoring a previously completed country restores its live standing without paying the same historical proclamation reward again. A genuinely larger successor remains eligible for its own recognition after meeting its distinct territorial requirements.
