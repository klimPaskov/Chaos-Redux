# World Claims: the neighboring lands

## Catalog entry

Event ID: **086**. Event name: **World Claims**. Type: **Minor Repeatable**. Status: **To Be Reworked**. Chaos level: **1**. Cluster: **Formables**, **Low** member. This is its only cluster membership.

## A world of prospective empires

Every government starts looking beyond its borders and finding reasons why the neighboring lands belong in its future. An ordinary frontier becomes the outline of a larger nation. Old historical projects return, improvised regional unions appear, and a successful minor can acquire a political identity that nobody would have predicted at the beginning of the campaign.

The opening is universal and automatic. Every valid country receives a claim on every foreign state directly land-adjacent to territory it owns. The country does not pay to receive the claims, choose a single neighbor, or wait for a major power to nominate it. Claims do not declare war, transfer land, create a country, or grant military units. They provide ambitions that ordinary campaigns can realize.

Countries with no foreign land neighbors receive no territorial claims from that firing. They still receive the worldwide announcement and can use relevant ambitions after acquiring appropriate holdings. An island government does not receive a random mainland consolation claim.

A country's own frontier supplies the opening. Germany's claim set is different after a peace conference. Brazil's claims follow its actual South American possessions. A released state can look outward from its new borders. A former European minor with an overseas possession can develop a second center of ambition around that possession. No named country receives privileged treatment.

## The campaign loop

The core loop is claim, conquer, integrate, and reassess. A firing maps the current frontier. The country decides whether to pursue it through the normal diplomatic and military game. Territory obtained through conquest or settlement becomes part of the owned frontier after ownership changes. A later firing discovers its newly neighboring states.

Occupation alone can activate the claimant's integration benefit, but it does not move the ownership-based claim frontier. This distinction matters during a long war. An army marching far ahead of the last peace conference can integrate already marked targets while the next automatic claims still begin at the country's legally owned states.

The same event can therefore tell several different stories. A government that conquers nothing accumulates ambitions without acquiring land. An opportunist can take a border district and stop. A regional unifier can assemble a coherent greater state. A powerful high-Chaos empire can continue outward through successive theaters.

The event should remain useful without requiring the player to manage a new department, resource market, or daily sequence of buttons. Claims arrive automatically. Starting Compliance is applied automatically when the correct country controls its target. Once earned, ordinary World Claims cores are awarded automatically. Decisions let the player choose a public ambition, invest in difficult integration, and adopt an identity.

## What belongs to whom

A World Claims target is a relationship between one country and one state. It is more specific than a generic territorial claim. Several countries may mark the same state, and each keeps its own relationship, starting-Compliance entitlement, occupation history, and ambition membership.

A claim previously obtained through another event can also become a World Claims target when a World Claims wave independently reaches it. That wave records why the state now qualifies. A distant pre-existing claim does not become eligible merely because the country possesses it.

The territorial claim and the integration relationship survive independently. Another system may remove the native claim. That does not erase proven integration history or already earned cores, although the country must still satisfy the normal World Claims qualification rules for further benefits. Conversely, a generic claim without World Claims provenance gives no integration reward.

## The player's first experience

The opening announcement explains that neighboring governments are making the same discovery at the same time. Its visible effect summary shows the receiving country's new claims and applicable starting Compliance. Its acknowledgement is not an opt-out that prevents the worldwide system from functioning.

Before Greater Ambitions is active, the country sees its territorial targets and integration information without a large menu of future institutions. Once ambitions become available, the interface suggests a small number of geographically coherent projects and presents one selected project in detail. Future continental or global identities remain unrevealed until the campaign makes them relevant.

The map, ordinary diplomacy, and occupation interface continue to carry much of the experience. The additional interface should explain which territory belongs to the current ambition, which states qualify, and what the country still needs to accomplish.

## The three kinds of success

Territorial success means taking and retaining useful ground. Political success means proving a coherent ambition and choosing a greater-state identity. Integration success means making held territory function as a durable part of the country and, at later Evolutions, earning cores.

These successes support one another but are not interchangeable. Proclaiming a name does not instantly integrate the map. A state may integrate before a regional proclamation. A native formable can complete through its own existing route while World Claims continues to expand from the new borders.

## The wider world

Rival governments may pursue the same region. The stronger claimant does not delete the weaker one's ambition. A country can be surrounded by competing projects and still choose consolidation or a defensive settlement. The event changes what governments hope to become without forcing every government to attack on the same day.

High Chaos makes those hopes more expansive and integration more forgiving. It does not make an arbitrary state on the far side of an ocean a next-door neighbor. The world is consumed through connected advances and acquired footholds, not through a single all-state claim effect.
