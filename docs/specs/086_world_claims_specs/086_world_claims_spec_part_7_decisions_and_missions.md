# World Claims: choosing and building an ambition

## A compact set of actions

The main category exposes one selected ambition, up to three suggested alternatives, and only the actions that matter to the current phase. The player normally sees three to five primary actions and never more than six. Target browsing is a selection control, not dozens of duplicated paid decisions.

The central action families are choosing an ambition, supporting civil integration, connecting a heartland, resolving a public territorial dispute, and proclaiming the achieved country. A consolidation mission can accompany a new proclamation. Only two active World Claims projects may run for one country at once.

Automatic claims, the initial Compliance entitlement, and qualified core awards do not require these decisions. The player can ignore the category and still receive the universal event's normal effects.

## Choosing the public ambition

A candidate preview shows its envelope, held states, important anchors, current integration, and next reachable objectives. Selecting it is free. Its territory and anchors become the public commitment until it is completed or deliberately replaced.

The country can reconsider after 90 days when no incompatible active project would be abandoned by the switch. Cancellation never refunds a previously completed milestone or removes another country's ambition. A player should understand whether a proposed change keeps a project valid before accepting it.

The selection improves optional outer-ring priorities around the chosen project. It does not exclude claims that the automatic immediate-frontier rule must grant elsewhere.

## Civil integration

The civil-integration project addresses one selected World Claims target that the country controls. It requires a real occupation relationship and a meaningful Compliance deficit. It consumes political power and support equipment, scaled with the target region's size rather than the country's original major/minor label.

During its 120-day mission, the country must retain the target and its designated administrative anchor. The public objective is sustained civil control with the area's connection kept intact. Native occupation management remains relevant. A project cannot be started in a state already fully core to the claimant or already credited for the same project family.

Success supplies one claimant-specific gain of 10 percentage points of Compliance, capped at 100. It does not reinstate the initial grant, grant a core, or change another occupier's value. The state becomes easier to integrate, and the success is visible in the local status.

Losing the target fails the project. A lost anchor pauses progress only when the original mission explicitly includes a recovery interval. Otherwise it fails clearly rather than running to a misleading success. The initial resource commitment is not refunded after ordinary military defeat.

## Connecting the heartland

This project addresses a selected connected group of World Claims targets around an actual corridor. The country designates two held anchors and the states connecting them. It spends trucks and trains rather than purchasing abstract points.

The 180-day mission requires continued control of the corridor and a usable connection under the installed logistics rules. When damage exists, restoring the relevant native infrastructure or railway becomes the player's concrete objective. An already intact connection changes the mission into a holding commitment, with that difference shown before payment.

Success gives the participating targets one bounded preparation credit toward their control requirement. It does not create infrastructure from nowhere or quietly replace the normal construction system. Credit is capped at 25 percent and cannot reduce physical control below the minimum holding periods.

The project cannot treat military access through an ally as an owned or controlled corridor. A maritime connection needs its own supported logistics proof and appropriate held ports. If that proof is unavailable, the mission is not offered with a fake land-route predicate.

## A frontier consolidation pledge

A country close to completing its selected ambition can publicly pledge to hold the remaining essential anchors for 180 days. This mission has no additional stockpile payment. Its cost is the strategic commitment to retain the stated territory rather than collecting a token resource.

The mission does not award the formation automatically if the final map and integration requirements are missing. Success records the sustained-control milestone and may satisfy that part of the eventual proclamation test. A lost anchor fails the pledge and delays a replacement pledge by 90 days.

Failure produces a temporary 5-percentage-point Stability penalty for 90 days through the event's single staged confidence effect. It does not cause a generic civil war, remove earned cores, or consume an unrelated national spirit. The player is warned of the public consequence before committing.

The AI takes this pledge only when it can realistically defend the anchors. It does not start the mission because a deadline happens to exist.

## A border assurance

Overlapping public ambitions can produce a bilateral dispute even while ordinary claims remain on the map. The two governments can agree to suspend pursuit of their conflicting public projects in the disputed envelope for 180 days.

The offer identifies the exact countries and envelope. Each accepting party pays 25 political power. A refusal charges neither the recipient nor a fictitious acceptance fee. A stale offer is revalidated before either side pays.

The agreement does not delete native claims or cores, transfer land, force a peace conference, or waive ordinary diplomatic rules. The signatories suspend World Claims public offensive projects against one another. An ordinary war declaration or incompatible active campaign breaks the assurance and records the breach once.

Automatic World Claims waves still follow the universal frontier rule. Passive new claims do not secretly invalidate the agreement. The assurance concerns the governments' public pursuit, not whether the global anomaly writes another claim on the map.

A durable assurance can reduce a previously recorded World Claims crisis contribution, within its contribution ledger. It cannot generate unlimited negative Chaos by repeatedly exchanging offers over the same region.

## Proclaiming the achieved country

When the selected ambition satisfies its actual territorial, anchor, holding, and integration conditions, the player can proclaim the greater country. A dynamic proclamation uses a political-power cost scaled with the envelope. A native provider uses its own genuine cost and completion route instead of being charged twice.

The player chooses whether to adopt the proposed identity or retain the present name and flag. Both choices recognize the achieved territorial milestone. Adoption changes the cosmetic identity without resetting the country. Retaining the old identity does not forfeit connected remainder claims or earned integration.

A first completed ambition grants a temporary confidence effect of +10 percentage points Stability and +10 percentage points War Support for 180 days. A strictly larger successor can renew that effect after its own achievement, but effects never stack and identically shaped projects cannot reissue it.

Completion also exposes the remaining connected objectives and an appropriate successor. It does not blanket-core the envelope or transfer its missing states.

## Consolidating a proclamation

After a new identity is adopted, the player can keep consolidating the heartland or pursue the next ambition. A voluntary consolidation mission asks the country to retain its anchors and keep the required integrated share for 180 days.

Success records a durable identity milestone used by higher-scale ambitions and achievements. It does not add another stack of national spirits. Failure changes the confidence effect to its bounded disappointed form, preserves the country and its earned cores, and leaves reconsolidation possible without paying for the same proclamation twice.

This gives a new identity an aftermath while avoiding compulsory endless administration. A country can stop at a stable regional result and continue an ordinary campaign.

## Costs, fairness, and failure

The whole decision system uses only four spendable payment types: political power, support equipment, trucks, and trains. Territorial control, Compliance, time, and an intact connection are conditions, not hidden currencies. No action has more than three inline cost amounts.

Costs are frozen when a paid project begins. Later growth does not increase an already accepted bill. Affordability is checked inclusively, payment is taken once, and every visible cost matches the actual transaction. Unaffordable or impossible targets remain clearly explained rather than being offered as active traps.

Annexation, target invalidation, and a map-version mismatch have distinct cancellation handling. A failure caused by losing a war is not treated as a technical cancellation. A technical cancellation before any benefit or mission progress can return the exact unused commitment once, with an explicit receipt. It cannot create a repeatable stockpile refund.
