# World Claims: campaigns shaped by geography

The working labels below describe possible campaign patterns. They are illustrative design cases, not a fixed list of selectable nations and not finished in-game localisation. Exact state membership comes from the active map and reviewed geographic descriptors.

## A northern peninsula and its approaches

A northern country that consolidates neighboring Scandinavian holdings can first pursue a compact peninsula-centered project. Its difficult objectives are the connected interior, usable coastal approaches, and whatever southern access the live map makes strategically important.

A Greater Scandinavia-type identity becomes plausible when enough of that envelope is actually held and integrated. A successor may include the surrounding northern region, but it does not treat every country in a modern regional classification as culturally identical.

The campaign can split naturally. One claimant builds a mainland heartland. Another controls islands and coasts. Both can pursue overlapping projects while their different holdings suggest different logistics missions and next objectives.

## A divided Balkan region

A Balkan claimant may rise from a small state, an independence wave, or a postwar fragment. The initial project centers on a connected group of actual holdings rather than an automatic historical-national claim list.

The region supports difficult overlapping ambitions because several governments can hold different anchors and claim the same corridor. A mountain gap, disconnected pocket, or rival-controlled transport route can matter more than the raw percentage of land.

An existing suitable formable takes priority only for a country with the proper route. A generic greater-regional identity remains a separate project. Completing it neither deletes neighboring countries' cores nor declares that their identities have ceased to exist.

## An Arabian consolidation

A country with a substantial connected position in Arabia can discover an ambition around the peninsula and its approaches. Sparse frontier states create wide territory with relatively few state steps. This is why the system uses both geographic envelopes and controlled anchors rather than treating every five-state conquest as equivalent.

A peninsula-centered proclamation can lead toward adjoining regions only after further ownership and integration. Overseas territories across a strategic sea remain future objectives until ordinary campaign play supplies footholds.

The identity and flag direction should fit the selected political presentation without assuming a religious government, a historical dynasty, or a particular ideology that the country does not possess.

## A mainland Southeast Asian project

A claimant consolidating neighboring mainland Southeast Asian states can develop an Indochina-type ambition. Its actual origin can be a regional state, a released country, or a government that has shifted its center there.

Connected lowlands, inland routes, and several administrative centers create distinct integration tasks. A player can choose to consolidate the interior before pursuing coasts, or secure a coastal line and address a fragmented hinterland afterward.

An archipelagic successor requires a new maritime relationship and actual footholds. It cannot be obtained merely by extending a land adjacency iterator through the sea.

## An Andean corridor

An Andean project follows a mountain-spine or adjoining basin descriptor, adjusted to the game's state boundaries. A narrow connected country can have a strong regional pattern even when its territory is not compact in a simple bounding rectangle.

Its campaign emphasizes maintaining a corridor, controlling several anchors, and integrating separated stretches. A successful ambition can grow toward neighboring lowlands or a broader South American envelope after further conquest and ownership changes.

The country is not rewarded for accumulating disconnected cheap states solely to meet a count. The pattern's anchor and connection tests keep the mountain project recognizable.

## A West African regional power

A former colony, inland state, or unexpected minor can build a West African regional ambition from actual holdings. The origin tag is not a reason to deny it a greater-state future.

The geographic project can connect inland centers to a coast, consolidate territories divided among several governments, or turn a wartime occupation into a durable heartland. Existing Africa-wide content remains owned by its original event and prerequisites.

A World Claims regional success does not automatically start Africa Is One, the Gods of Africa institutions, or that event's world-order route. A compatible provider can be offered when its real requirements fit. Otherwise the new regional identity remains its own country with its own existing content.

## A river-basin state

A basin ambition requires an explicitly researched physical-geography descriptor. The game does not infer a real drainage basin from a state name containing the word river.

The campaign can recognize upstream and downstream anchors, major crossings, and held connections. A country controlling a useful middle section receives a consolidation project rather than immediate claims to every distant source and outlet.

Completing the ambition can connect the heartland, give a basin-centered identity, and reveal adjoining regions. The river supplies a territorial theme. It does not create a new water-management simulator or a generic resource bonus unrelated to the event.

## A mountain-region union

A mountain ambition uses the relevant mountain region and its habitable approaches. It does not claim every mountainous state on Earth. Separate mountain ranges are different geographic candidates.

A successful country can turn defensible highland holdings into a regional identity and then decide whether to expand toward neighboring lowlands. The consolidation project centers on a real corridor or administrative anchor, not a random demand for equipment stockpiles.

A distinct existing mountain formable can take priority when implemented and valid. An unavailable catalog idea is only a potential future connection.

## An island group

An island-group ambition begins from substantial actual holdings within one coherent group. It may use several ports or regional anchors for formation. The country must obtain missing islands through normal campaign routes when there is no land frontier to claim them.

A success can provide a group identity and reveal a wider maritime successor. The interface distinguishes held islands, currently claimable targets, and future objectives across water. No static world map pretends that all three are already integrated territory.

## A strategic-sea project

A sea-centered ambition is built from several coastal holdings around the same researched maritime space. Its anchors are on land. The project does not own the sea, remove other countries' access, or grant automatic naval superiority.

Several coastal bridgeheads can produce a trans-regional identity at higher Evolutions. The natural challenges are keeping each foothold, acquiring legal ownership, and integrating the coastal heartland. Existing navies and naval systems remain the means of achieving those goals.

## A borderlands union after collapse

Civil wars and releases can produce a patchwork that no pre-war nation anticipated. A government holding a connected group of those territories can develop a borderlands ambition centered on its actual administrative anchor.

The former owners' identities remain visible as contested claims and cores. The project must survive control interruptions and integrate the area before it earns a lasting identity. It cannot count the same original territory several times merely because its former owner fragmented into several tags.

## A country with a new center overseas

An old country's capital or main integrated holding can move into a different region. World Claims can then discover ambitions around that center without changing its original military or government package.

Existing home-region ambitions remain history or alternative projects. They are not silently rewritten to include the new overseas envelope. A deliberate selection lets the player choose whether to restore the old heartland or accept the country it has become.

## A connected shape with no famous name

A campaign may produce a long inland arc, a coastal strip, or an irregular but connected conquest. The connected-territory constructor should produce an anchor-centered ambition with a meaningful consolidation envelope.

This route gives unexpected campaigns a full identity, integration challenge, and successor. It must not be replaced by a generic unavailable button whenever the holdings fail to match the illustrative examples above.
