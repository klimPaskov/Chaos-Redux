# World Claims: the country that emerges

## An identity follows a political achievement

A dynamic identity should describe the country that actually exists. Its geographic center, territorial scale, and government form provide the name family. Its flag gives that new country a recognizable visual identity while retaining an appropriate connection to its institutional origins.

A name is not evidence that every promised state is held. The country may proclaim a regional project after meeting the defined formation threshold while still claiming its connected remainder. The territorial interface continues to distinguish held territory from the ambition's unfinished envelope.

A human player can retain the present name and flag. That choice recognizes the same territorial milestone and receives the same ordinary completion benefits. It does not install a hidden penalty or block the next appropriate ambition.

## Existing formables take priority

An existing Hearts of Iron IV or Chaos Redux formable is the preferred candidate when its actual territorial idea fits the holdings and the country can genuinely pursue that route. The integration presents its territorial requirements, outstanding conditions, and native completion action.

A provider is not eligible merely because its name resembles a region. Its country restrictions, ideology, downloadable-content requirements, focus prerequisites, subject rules, unique conditions, and ownership policy remain part of its identity. World Claims cannot bypass those conditions through a generic proclamation wrapper.

Where the provider supplies the real formation, that provider owns the identity change, native claims, native core rewards, and any broader country transformation. World Claims records its own compatible milestone once and can continue supporting subsequent eligible territory. It does not replay the provider's effect block, levy a second formation fee, or install a competing cosmetic identity in the same transition.

A provider's missing assets or unresolved integration hook block that provider bridge. A distinct, geographically appropriate dynamic ambition may still exist. It must not impersonate the unavailable provider or advertise the same protected result with its restrictions removed.

## Dynamic territory, prepared language and artwork

The territorial ambition is selected and assembled from the campaign map. Its name and flag are resolved from a finite set of prepared engine-compatible resources. The campaign does not create image files, invent new positioned interface nodes, or depend on an external text or image service during play.

This is an explicit technical interpretation of dynamic names and flags. The dynamic system recognizes many combinations of holdings and selects an appropriate greater-state identity. A build-time geographic vocabulary and visual library supply resources the engine can actually load. It is not a fixed list of country-specific conquest routes.

The vocabulary includes registered geographic descriptors, scale terms, government-form variants, and exceptional proper names owned by native providers. A connected territory that fits no broad regional name can use a validated anchor-based identity. A literal state or geographic feature name is preferable to a fabricated historical culture.

The installed engine must prove that the proposed country-name localisation and cosmetic-tag dispatch work in every context in which a country appears. Where a desired dynamic string composition is unsupported, the implementation must compile the relevant combinations ahead of time. It must report a coverage gap rather than silently fall back to raw keys or a misleading universal empire name.

## Geographic fit and name selection

A regional name requires the country's selected envelope and center to belong meaningfully to the named geography. A transcontinental label requires qualifying centers on more than one continent. A sea-oriented identity requires held shores or islands around that sea, not a distant coastal state. A river identity requires a registry-supported basin or corridor, not an arbitrary line between two capitals.

The political form follows the actual government's supported presentation. An imperial form, federation, republic, or ideological union should not all use the same final wording with a different color. The language must avoid claiming a constitutional institution that the active route explicitly contradicts.

Candidate scoring may prefer an existing valid identity over a newly invented one. After the player adopts a name, minor changes in score do not rename the country automatically. A successor identity requires an explicit completed successor or another legitimate country-system change.

Two countries may pursue overlapping geographic identities. World Claims does not reserve a region for the first country to preview it. If two completed identities would display indistinguishably, prepared institutional or anchor qualifiers distinguish them. A native provider's genuine uniqueness rule remains provider-owned.

## Flags that represent the new country

Each dynamic identity family needs a complete native flag set at the game's required sizes and supported political variants. The artwork should communicate the geographic and institutional idea, with enough visual distinction to recognize rival greater states in diplomacy and on the map.

A former minor should not receive a generic placeholder flag simply because its original tag was historically small. Its new flag can preserve a restrained founding motif within the greater-state composition. The design must remain legible at the smallest native flag size.

Prepared variants must differ in composition and symbolism where the political identity changes. Recoloring one flag four times is insufficient. Native formables reuse their own correctly installed flags instead of receiving a competing World Claims set.

The runtime resolver may select a different prepared family after a valid government change. It does not alter territorial progress or replay the formation reward. The identity record keeps the adopted family separate from temporary ideology presentation.

## Continuity of the country

A dynamic proclamation preserves the country's actual tag, leaders, military organization, units, technology, research, existing focus tree, political parties, laws, diplomatic relationships, and other country-owned content. Its World Claims ambition and integration records remain attached to the same claimant epoch.

A native provider may legitimately change some of those systems. That change remains the native route's responsibility and must be visible in its own completion action. The generic World Claims path does not duplicate such changes or create an empty replacement country.

Other event-owned identities can coexist as recorded history. When another valid system controls the current cosmetic identity, World Claims can retain the territorial achievement without repeatedly overwriting that system's presentation. A clear identity-owner handoff governs an intentional replacement.

## Successors and retained achievements

A successor must add meaningful territory or a new geographic scale. Rebranding the same envelope under another synonym does not count as a new completed ambition. Its reward key is based on the territorial project and claimant history, not the currently displayed name.

A completed smaller ambition remains in the country's history when a larger successor is adopted. Losing its territory does not delete the historical achievement or revoke permanent cores. The live interface makes the current territorial deficit visible instead of presenting historical completion as current control.

A country can stop after forming a compact regional state. A larger successor is an available future, not a mandatory administrative burden. The event remains an expansion opportunity even when the player prefers a durable regional result.
