# World Claims: recognizing a greater country

## Geography supplies possibilities

An ambition is a campaign-derived territorial project with a coherent center, a meaningful envelope, several important anchors, a completion condition, and a possible successor. Its territory follows the relationship between actual holdings and geography. It is not a fixed sequence keyed to the country's original tag.

The geographic vocabulary includes regions, subcontinents, peninsulas, island groups, strategic seas, mountain systems, river basins, researched historical spheres, connected conquered territory, and groups of neighboring countries already largely controlled. These are different ways to recognize a pattern, not a requirement to generate ten ambitions for every country.

The active map supplies actual state membership. Geographic descriptors supply meaning. Cultural or historical labels require their own researched boundaries and exclusions. A modern regional classification is not evidence that an entire population has one culture or that a government has a rightful claim.

## Discovery from actual holdings

Discovery begins with owned or controlled holdings, their connected components, and the country's current center of power. A viable candidate must have a meaningful share already held, a reachable next objective, and a clear relationship between its name and its territory.

The initial regional discovery threshold is control of at least 40 percent of a candidate envelope and at least five states. This is a starting balance value, not a historical claim. Particularly small native formables retain their own rules. Dynamic ambition rules can use a curated small-group exception only when its geography genuinely needs one and the exception is explicitly registered.

A larger country does not automatically favor the largest possible envelope. The best suggestion is often the one that explains its recent conquests and offers a useful next phase. A small government with a coherent corridor can receive a strong regional suggestion while a scattered empire receives several distinct regional possibilities.

The display presents up to three suggestions. This is a presentation limit, not a cap on what geography the system can recognize. Other valid candidates remain discoverable through regional browsing. A human selects one public ambition, and the AI makes an equivalent selection without opening the interface.

## A private possibility and a public commitment

Discovering a candidate is informational. It gives no immediate claims, Compliance, cores, or national confidence. The country can inspect requirements without paying or committing.

Selecting a public ambition identifies the government's current project and exposes its missions, diplomatic posture, and formation route. The choice does not suppress automatic global claims elsewhere. It influences the optional outer-ring priorities and which project occupies the compact interface.

A country can switch after a 90-day reconsideration period when no incompatible project is active. Switching has no free formation payout. Progress based on actual territory remains actual territory. Completed milestones remain recorded, and a changed selection does not erase an opponent's overlapping claim.

Public commitment should matter through the campaign it directs, not through a second currency. It produces concrete objectives, context-sensitive reports, and a visible political promise.

## The ambition envelope

The envelope is the full geographic project. The immediate objectives are the parts currently reachable under World Claims frontier rules. The integrated heartland is the portion the country has successfully held and administered. Keeping these sets separate prevents a preview map from becoming an accidental reward map.

A candidate's proposed envelope is frozen when publicly adopted. Its required anchors and denominator cannot shrink simply because the country loses a difficult state. Political ownership changes do not alter the geographic outline. A legitimate map-version migration is handled separately and invalidates unsupported geometry until reconciled.

The system may suggest a successor as the campaign changes. It does not silently expand an active project's requirements while the player is close to completion. The player can complete or deliberately replace the current project with clear knowledge of the change.

## Existing formables first

A matching existing vanilla or Chaos Redux formable is presented before an improvised equivalent when the country can genuinely pursue that provider's route. The native formable retains its ideology, independence, tag, focus, DLC, capital, territorial, and other prerequisites.

World Claims may reveal a relevant route, add connected missing claims, and help integrate its targets. It may not call a powerful final effect out of context, clear prerequisites, or expose content that the provider deliberately keeps hidden.

A provider can match the geography but remain inaccessible to the country. That is not a license to copy its name and full rewards into a generic bypass. The country may receive a distinct geography-based ambition with its own identity and the normal World Claims rewards. Its wording must not present it as having completed the unavailable native route.

The distinction is especially important for religious states, unique leaders, ideology-specific unions, and special Chaos countries. Shared territory alone does not entitle an ordinary country to their institutions.

## Connected conquered territory

Some campaigns do not fit a convenient named region. A government may conquer a connected arc across several regional boundaries. The system can build an ambition around that connected holding pattern, its capital or administrative anchor, and a limited neighboring envelope.

The result must remain geographically intelligible. A connected-territory ambition names the center or dominant physical feature rather than pretending that the shape corresponds to a known nation. The proposed envelope favors filling gaps and consolidating a corridor before pursuing a larger successor.

This is a first-class route, not a placeholder that disappears when no historical formable exists. It ensures that unexpected minors and newly released countries remain eligible without requiring a bespoke historical path for every possible tag.

## Neighbor-country patterns

A country that controls most of several adjacent neighbors can receive an ambition that recognizes the emerging regional union. The pattern evaluates the held states of the current campaign, not just whether a neighboring tag still exists.

Annexing one tiny neighbor does not automatically prove a subcontinent. Countries broken into several tags by civil war should not artificially inflate the number of national territories represented. Origin and geography are used together to avoid mistaking political fragmentation for a larger accomplishment.

The resulting project has real territorial anchors and integration requirements. It does not transfer the remaining neighbors or treat faction membership as consent to annexation.

## Completing and succeeding

A dynamic regional proclamation normally requires 80 percent of the envelope to be owned and controlled, every named anchor to be owned and controlled, and at least 50 percent of the qualifying territory to satisfy the integration test. Regional qualifying states must have been held continuously for 90 days. Existing cores count as integrated, but newly obtained ownership does not invent holding time.

Larger ambitions use progressively stronger ownership, integration, and anchor requirements, specified in the geography companion. A late global identity demands actual world-scale possession and integration. A large paper claim network alone is not a country.

Completion offers an identity decision, a temporary confidence period, connected remainder claims, and a suggested successor. The player may retain the old identity. The completed ambition and its rewards are recorded once even when the player later returns to its page.

Successor ambitions must strictly enlarge the achieved project, add a new geographic relationship, or move to a genuinely new center of power. Renaming the same envelope is not a new accomplishment.
