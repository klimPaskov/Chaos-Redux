# World Claims: rival futures and changing countries

## The same land, different futures

A state can belong to several World Claims projects. Each claimant sees its own progress and the governments whose public ambitions overlap it. One country's declaration does not remove another's target relationship, initial-Compliance entitlement, native claim, or historical core.

Overlapping claims alone do not produce a new crisis event every day. A dispute becomes prominent when two selected public ambitions overlap in held or immediately reachable territory and at least one government actively pursues that overlap. Passive claims in distant territory remain ordinary diplomatic information.

The dispute's content depends on the actual relationship. Two peaceful neighbors may negotiate an assurance. A country already at war can identify the opposing ambition without receiving a redundant declaration. Allies can disagree without the event dissolving their faction. Subjects retain their own claims but remain subject to their actual diplomatic restrictions.

## No compulsory war policy

World Claims does not automatically justify a war, declare a war, fabricate a peace conference, or demand that an ally surrender territory. Existing diplomatic mechanics determine what a government can do with its claims. Native faction, autonomy, truce, and war-goal restrictions remain in force.

A selected public ambition gives optional outer claims a coherent priority. It does not turn every missing state into an immediate military order. The AI should recognize situations in which a border is claimed but attacking it would be suicidal or forbidden.

The public assurance is the event's bounded alternative to continued confrontation. It suspends pursuit between the named governments without pretending that territorial ambitions have disappeared. A successful assurance can settle an event-owned crisis contribution. It does not erase the wider campaign's war or annexation contributions.

## Subjects, allies, and patrons

A subject is a valid territorial government when it owns states and has ordinary country institutions. It receives claims from its own owned frontier. Its overlord's territory is foreign to it for this purpose unless the two have actually become one country. The reverse is also true. An alliance does not suppress the universal claim rule.

A subject's ambition does not automatically count its overlord's states. A patron's ambition does not automatically count a subject's states. Dynamic proclamation requires the claimant's direct ownership and control. A native formable that explicitly permits subject holdings retains that provider's genuine rule, displayed as a provider-specific exception.

The World Claims proclamation itself requires independence. A subject can prepare, integrate territory it really administers, and preview a future identity without gaining an artificial independence button. A provider that legitimately permits a subject to form follows its own restrictions and effect chain.

A civil or connection project cannot use a patron's stockpile without an existing authorized transfer. The four payment types are taken from the country that commits to the project.

## Peace settlements and territorial transfers

The next firing always begins from the current ownership map. A state acquired at a peace conference becomes a frontier seed. A state lost there ceases to be a seed even if the country still claims it. Temporary occupation does not preserve ownership after a settlement transfers it elsewhere.

An existing World Claims relationship survives loss of ownership as the same claimant's historical relationship. Control clocks stop when control is lost, and a later recapture does not repeat a consumed starting-Compliance grant. A newly activated, numerically higher entitlement can still be delivered when that claimant next controls the state, subject to the active profile.

Territorial transfers must update the relevant relationships at the time of transfer. A shared transfer adapter may mark the affected states and countries for a bounded reconciliation. The presentation must not wait for a new global wave to discover that its stated owner has changed.

The final core award and proclamation always recheck the live map. A delayed refresh cannot grant a result based on yesterday's controller.

## Civil wars and releases

A new civil-war country or released country participates from its own owned frontier at the next firing. It receives a new claimant identity epoch. It does not inherit another government's unspent integration entitlements, paid projects, or reward receipts merely because the engine reused a familiar tag.

Native claims and cores already present in the released country remain ordinary game facts. They do not prove that this new country previously completed a World Claims ambition. Conversely, World Claims must not strip a legitimate native core simply because its own reward history is new.

A genuine same-country continuation keeps its history. A cosmetic formation is such a continuation. A provider-owned tag transfer can preserve history only through a deliberate succession adapter that proves which country continues, which records move, and which predecessor ceases to own them. Copying the entire ledger to two successors is forbidden.

The default civil-war policy treats the engine-designated continuing country as the continuing claimant and a newly created opposing country as new. A route that replaces both institutions must explicitly retire both old ownership records. This is a lifecycle rule to verify against the installed engine, not an assumption that a tag string alone proves continuity.

## Annexation and return

Annexation cancels the country's active World Claims work and removes its pending work from active queues. It does not remove its historical native cores from states or reverse legitimate effects already committed to the map.

A later release creates a new claimant epoch by default. The historical archive can explain the earlier country's ambition, but that archive cannot pay the same integration reward again. A scripted restoration route may preserve a continuing legal institution through an explicit provider adapter. That adapter must preserve one recipient, not create a second copy of its history.

A country with no owned states receives no frontier claims. Existing controlled targets can still require final control-loss reconciliation. It cannot remain an invisible source of new global claim waves through a dormant tag.

## Changes to the center of power

A country that expands overseas can discover ambitions around the new possessions. The original selected ambition remains stable until changed or completed. A capital relocation alone does not redraw every target or erase earlier achievements.

The candidate selector distinguishes an old heartland, a large new connected component, and an emerging overseas center. It offers choices based on meaningful holdings rather than automatically preferring the capital's continent. Small remote possessions can seed neighboring claims without instantly becoming a continental identity.

Political ownership changes are supported during a campaign. Changing the installed state map is a different operation. A mod update that splits, renumbers, or removes states requires a validated registry migration before the save continues with new state geometry. A failed migration must be reported rather than silently attaching an old claimant record to a different state.
