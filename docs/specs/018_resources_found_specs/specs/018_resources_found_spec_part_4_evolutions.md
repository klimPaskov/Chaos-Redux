# Event 018 Resources Found Specification, Part 4

## Evolution tracks and escalation

All names in this file are working labels for design structure. They are not final localisation.

## Evolution structure

Event 018 has four evolution stages. Each stage can affect an already active field and can also change the opening package when the event has not fired yet.

The event must preserve three different concepts:

- **Baseline lifecycle** covers discovery, appraisal, development, rush, maturity, suspension, and closure.
- **Evolution stage** changes the event’s scale or rules.
- **Incident sequence** is the gradual chain of symptoms, attacks, and public consequences that unfolds inside an evolution.

Ordinary baseline stages must not be logged as evolutions. The shared Event Details chronology records each Evolution I to IV tier once as an event-wide milestone. The first qualifying field supplies the actor and state context. Later fields retain their own physical progression and history without appending another shared chronology row. An active field can progress through Evolution I, II, III, and IV over time. A first firing at a higher enabled evolution begins with a stronger opening, but it must still introduce later horror through a readable sequence.

## Evolution pacing

Evolution checks should use dynamic mean time to happen rather than instant threshold jumps. A normal active-stage target is around 90 days, modified by field behavior and campaign state.

Factors that shorten evolution timing include:

- high Excavation Depth
- repeated resource discoveries in the same field
- maximum extraction mode
- low Workforce Safety
- high accumulated exploitation
- large field death totals
- high chaos
- active wartime requisition
- failed closure or deep test projects
- several distinct resources found in one state

Factors that lengthen evolution timing include:

- suspension
- high Workforce Safety
- controlled output
- successful safety projects
- reduced field activity
- international inspection that limits deep excavation
- active sealing work

An enabled evolution can remain unavailable if the field is permanently closed. A disabled evolution must not set recorded flags, unlock dependent decisions, or block baseline play.

## Pre-fire evolved openings

When an evolution is enabled before Event 018 has ever fired, the first discovery can use that stage’s stronger geological package. The opening still begins as a resource discovery. The owner should not receive the cave country, public monsters, or a world-end state on the first day.

The first firing at Evolution II or III must create an accelerated incident clock. It can begin with evidence that the project is unusually large or difficult, but sickness, disappearances, public attacks, and breach pressure still arrive through successive events and decisions.

Evolution IV is not a same-day opening package. If the world is eligible for Evolution IV before Event 018 first fires, the event begins with the Evolution III pre-fire package, marks the field as breach-prone, and starts a compressed but gradual breach chain. The owner retains a real opportunity to close the site before the cave country appears.

## Evolution I: The Compound Field

### Design role

Evolution I turns one discovery into a resource province of international importance. The field becomes larger, more diverse, harder to administer, and more likely to attract foreign competition. It remains an economic evolution. Supernatural content should not appear.

### Pre-fire opening

If Event 018 has not fired before Evolution I is active, the first selected state receives several independent large deposit rolls.

Recommended opening structure:

- two to four independent resource rolls
- each roll centered around the baseline amount
- duplicate resource results stack
- different results create a multi-resource field
- the owner receives one coherent discovery event rather than several identical popups

The opening should produce a field capable of changing regional trade without automatically creating every resource type.

The owner begins with:

- higher Confirmed Reserve
- stronger immediate foreign interest
- a larger appraisal project
- more transport demand
- one additional early development choice
- an increased chance that the field becomes the owner’s selected strategic project

### Active evolution entry

An existing field evolves when deeper surveys, repeated discoveries, or campaign chaos reveal connected seams, basins, or deposits.

The evolution event should show:

- new drilling cores or ore samples
- revised geological maps
- a sudden increase in commercial traffic
- local authorities struggling to zone roads, camps, depots, and claims
- foreign buyers requesting access

It should not describe the event as a warning or foreshadow monsters directly.

### Immediate mechanical changes

- add one or more independent resource rolls to the same field
- increase the field’s discovery count
- raise the practical maximum for Developed Yield
- unlock compound-field development decisions
- increase Foreign Pressure sensitivity
- enable international commission and demilitarization routes when competition becomes severe
- add multi-resource processing and transport projects
- increase the cost and duration of final closure

### Compound-field decisions

#### Unified resource authority

The owner creates one administration for several resource industries. This lowers coordination cost and smuggling, but requires domestic bureaucracy and construction capacity.

#### Separate concessions

The owner grants different resources to different partners. This can maximize investment and reduce dependence on one power, while raising rivalry and enforcement complexity.

#### Integrated processing corridor

The owner builds a shared rail, depot, and processing network. This gives a strong state-level economic payoff and makes the field more strategically visible.

#### Strategic rationing board

The owner allocates different resources among domestic industry, allies, and export contracts. This is useful during war and can create competing diplomatic demands.

### International pressure

Evolution I increases the number of foreign actors that can reach Buyer, Investor, or Strategic Claimant tiers. It can produce:

- competing bids
- foreign company missions
- covert survey teams
- smuggling syndicates
- guarantee proposals
- calls for neutral administration
- troop movements near a disputed border

### Demilitarization route

The field can become demilitarized when:

- it lies near a valid dispute
- at least two countries have serious strategic interest
- negotiations or a border incident make unilateral fortification dangerous
- the owner accepts a commission or ceasefire settlement

Demilitarization lowers seizure risk and Foreign Pressure. It limits forts, troop concentration, and military extraction. It does not prevent police, mine guards, or worker rescue forces.

### Evolution I stable outcomes

The field can stabilize as:

- a national resource complex
- a multi-partner export zone
- a neutral commission territory
- a militarized strategic basin
- a suspended reserve
- a permanently closed site

Evolution I should be enjoyable even if later evolutions never occur.

### Evolution log direction

The log title direction should communicate a discovery that has become a compound or basin-scale field. The detail should focus on several large deposits in one state and the resulting commercial and diplomatic importance. It must not reveal later subterranean content.

## Evolution II: The Sick Workings

### Design role

Evolution II begins the hidden horror through worker illness, damaged equipment, unexplained corrosion, missing crews, and deaths underground. Ordinary industrial explanations remain credible at first. The player gains a new visible value, **Subsurface Disturbance**, after the field’s medical and inspection systems can no longer explain the pattern.

The evolution should treat worker suffering seriously. It can use grim irony through company propaganda or official euphemism, but it should not use cheap comedy.

### Pre-fire opening

If Event 018 first fires while Evolution II is active, the state receives a stronger compound deposit package than Evolution I.

Recommended opening structure:

- three to five independent large resource rolls
- increased chance of duplicate stacking
- stronger initial infrastructure demand
- an active labor shortage
- one early industrial accident or medical anomaly after development begins
- an accelerated but not immediate Disturbance reveal

The discovery popup remains practical. A separate early follow-up can describe unusual equipment damage or workers falling ill after the first deep shifts.

### Active evolution entry

An active field can enter Evolution II after enough depth, repeated extraction, or failed safety incidents.

The first incidents should be ambiguous:

- persistent coughing or skin lesions
- severe fatigue after lower-shaft work
- metal supports pitting or corroding faster than expected
- lamps failing in dry galleries
- animals refusing to enter certain tunnels
- food spoiling in sealed lunch tins
- tools found bent or worn without matching use
- workers reporting knocks from unopened rock
- a crew missing after a routine inspection

The event should present observed facts and fearful behavior directly. It should not use a staged official denial formula.

### Ordinary explanation phase

The owner first receives decisions that would make sense in a real industrial crisis:

- test air and water
- inspect chemicals and explosives
- improve ventilation
- rotate crews
- establish a field hospital
- halt one shaft
- bring in outside engineers
- compensate families
- suppress reports

These actions must have real value. High safety and careful inspection should reduce deaths, slow Disturbance, and provide earlier information. The story should not make competent safety investment meaningless.

### Disturbance reveal

Subsurface Disturbance becomes visible when one of the following occurs:

- ordinary tests fail to identify a cause
- a worker survives an attack with physical evidence
- a rescue party finds a sealed passage opened from below
- equipment corrosion forms impossible patterns
- repeated knocking tracks crews through different galleries
- remains or tools are found in a place inaccessible from the known mine

The reveal does not need to confirm a species immediately. The player knows that the site is responding to excavation in a way ordinary hazards cannot explain.

### Subsurface Disturbance

Disturbance rises through:

- deep excavation
- maximum shifts
- blasting and controlled collapse
- new deposit discovery
- mass machinery use
- casualties left unrecovered
- concealment and continued operation
- failure to secure breached galleries

Disturbance falls through:

- suspension
- sealing specific shafts
- reduced shifts
- rescue and recovery operations
- high safety
- evacuation of lower works
- controlled withdrawal from deep galleries

It should not fall to zero while the field remains at extreme depth. The player can manage it, but continued exploitation keeps the underlying risk alive.

### Worker attrition

Evolution II introduces recurring population loss tied to real field activity.

Death pressure should scale with:

- active workforce size
- low safety
- high Disturbance
- aggressive extraction mode
- failed rescue operations
- the number of deep works still open
- public concealment that delays evacuation

Deaths must use the shared Deaths system and reduce real state population. The field detail should record cumulative deaths. The game should distinguish accident, occupational illness, disappearance, and creature attack when the cause becomes known.

### Creature incidents

Early creature incidents remain confined to the works.

Possible incidents include:

- food and equipment disappearing from locked stores
- rescue teams finding stripped protective gear
- a worker dragged into an unmapped fissure
- a machine room emptied of its crew
- guards firing into a tunnel and recovering no body
- a wounded creature leaving mineral-encrusted material
- tracks that avoid timber and follow ore seams
- workers refusing the night shift after hearing familiar voices below

The creatures should feel physical, organized enough to learn, and linked to resource-bearing rock. They are not ghosts and should not be identified as a known folklore species.

### Evolution II choices

#### Keep the site active

The owner accepts deaths and Disturbance in exchange for continued extraction. This route increases accumulated exploitation and future cave-country strength.

#### Restricted workings

The owner closes the deepest galleries, keeps upper works active, and invests in rescue and monitoring. This preserves part of the field and slows escalation.

#### Scientific and military survey

The owner sends protected teams to investigate. This can reveal cave anatomy, armor weaknesses, tunnel maps, or hard-attack research direction. It also risks provoking stronger incidents.

#### Conceal and militarize

The owner suppresses civilian access, replaces crews with guarded labor, and treats the site as a military secret. This preserves output and lowers public pressure for a time. It raises later panic, deaths, and creature hostility.

#### Begin full closure

The owner starts the longer closure project. Evolution II closure is more expensive than baseline closure but can still end the field cleanly before public breach.

### Evolution II failure states

- the field hospital is overwhelmed
- a rescue mission loses its force
- foreign inspectors leak evidence
- a concession partner demands continued work
- a concealed death list becomes public
- lower galleries connect in ways the survey did not predict
- Disturbance reaches the Evolution III threshold

### Evolution II log direction

The evolution log should identify a field where workers are becoming ill, equipment is corroding, and underground disappearances are no longer explained by normal mining hazards. It should not reveal the final cave country or world-end branch.

## Evolution III: The Open Breach

### Design role

Evolution III turns the field into a public regional emergency. The resource discovery becomes extraordinarily rich, often containing every strategic resource, while creatures leave the works, attack settlements, and force expensive evacuations and hunts.

This stage must remain gradual. A player whose first Event 018 firing occurs at Evolution III should first experience discovery and accelerated anomalies, then public attacks. The full crisis must not appear in the first popup.

### Pre-fire opening

The first field receives a very large Event 018 deposit of every standard strategic resource.

The opening should be enough to alter global trade routes. It can use resource amounts larger than the baseline for each type, scaled to avoid integer or map-display problems. The exact final tuning belongs to implementation.

The owner begins with:

- a globally significant all-resource field
- immediate strategic interest from several powers
- a large labor and infrastructure boom
- high initial Excavation Depth after the first development project
- an accelerated Evolution II incident sequence
- a protected minimum period before the public breach

The discovery event must not state that every resource is a trap or that monsters will appear.

### Active evolution entry

An existing field reaches Evolution III when Disturbance, exploitation, depth, and deaths pass severe thresholds and the owner has not completed closure.

The transition incident should be concrete:

- creatures break through an upper work
- a train or settlement is attacked outside the mine perimeter
- survivors reach a city with visible wounds
- a large subterranean cavity collapses into a public road or district
- field guards lose control of an access point

At this point the threat is public. Subsurface Disturbance remains relevant, but the main visible crisis value becomes **Breach Pressure**.

### Breach Pressure

Breach Pressure measures the number and activity of open routes from the deep works into settlements, transport corridors, and the surface.

It rises through:

- active extraction
- failed hunts
- unsealed shafts
- creature feeding on stored resources or machinery
- public panic that empties guarded districts
- battle damage in the field state
- failed evacuation
- deliberate military probing of the lower works

It falls through:

- sealing access points
- defended perimeter projects
- successful hunts
- controlled evacuation
- destruction of surface nests
- suspension or closure
- hard-attack equipped security forces

High Breach Pressure changes the map and population consequences. It should not be a cosmetic number.

### Public incident sequence

The crisis should escalate through several incidents rather than one generic monster event.

#### Perimeter loss

Field guards lose one or more access points. The player must reinforce, seal, or abandon the perimeter.

#### Outlying settlement attacks

Creatures raid worker camps, farms, rail depots, or small towns. Population loss begins outside the site.

#### Transport disruption

Rail lines and roads become unsafe. Developed Yield falls. Foreign contracts fail. A timed mission can require keeping a supply corridor open.

#### City intrusion

Creatures reach a major settlement or city district in the state. Civilian deaths and flight increase. The owner receives evacuation and urban-hunt decisions.

#### Regional flight

Population movement and panic spread to adjacent states. This should not become a separate migration event system. Event 018 can use existing population movement or state modifiers where available.

#### Open public knowledge

Foreign countries receive news or reaction events. Concession partners, neighbors, and military powers decide whether to aid, quarantine, exploit, or abandon the owner.

### Monster hunt decisions

Hunts should require concrete military preparation.

Costs and requirements can include:

- divisions in the field state
- infantry and support equipment
- artillery, anti-tank, or other hard-attack capable equipment
- trains and trucks
- command capacity
- supplied access
- local intelligence from workers and survivors

Hunts can target:

- surface packs
- transport tunnels
- city districts
- abandoned shafts
- food and equipment caches
- breeding chambers near the surface

Success lowers Breach Pressure and can reveal combat weaknesses. Failure costs manpower, equipment, and civilian lives.

### Evacuation decisions

Evacuation should be expensive but manageable before Evolution IV.

The player may need to:

- secure rail or road routes
- commit trains and trucks
- hold the state for a duration
- provide temporary housing capacity in another state
- abandon part of Developed Yield
- choose priority groups when time is short

A successful evacuation reduces population loss and makes final closure safer. A failed evacuation can create severe deaths and panic.

### International aid

Foreign countries can offer:

- engineering teams
- hard-attack equipment
- medical aid
- evacuation transport
- volunteer units
- intelligence on tunnels
- guarantees or quarantine enforcement

Aid creates influence and access. A foreign power should not receive ownership of the field merely for helping.

### Military exploitation

A desperate or extreme owner can continue extracting during public attacks. This produces the strongest short-term economic benefit and the highest accumulated exploitation. It also increases Breach Pressure, deaths, and the future cave-country opening army.

The game should make this choice legible. The player knows that continued extraction is worsening the public crisis, even if the exact Evolution IV formula remains hidden.

### Full sealing project

Evolution III offers the last clean prevention route before the cave country appears.

The sealing project must be difficult but achievable. It should include:

1. suspend all extraction
2. evacuate or secure workers and nearby civilians
3. regain control of surface access points
4. commit engineering and demolition resources
5. hold the state for a sealing duration
6. defeat or contain active surface packs
7. collapse, flood, cap, or fill the access network
8. remove every Event 018 resource addition from the state
9. permanently close the site
10. set a prevention state that blocks Evolution IV for that field

### Closure guarantee

A successful full sealing before the final breach has no hidden supernatural punishment. It removes all Event 018 resource additions, ends the field, prevents Evolution IV, and cleans up the cave-country origin path.

The cost is the loss of the extraordinary resource province and all field investment. This is the promised escape route.

### Partial closure

A partial closure lowers Depth, Disturbance, and Breach Pressure, removes some development, and delays the final breach. It does not permanently prevent Evolution IV.

The UI must distinguish partial closure from the guaranteed full seal.

### Evolution III failure and escalation

Evolution IV becomes possible when:

- Evolution IV is enabled
- the site is not permanently closed
- Breach Pressure remains critical
- accumulated exploitation is high enough or the incident sequence reaches its terminal breach
- the owner fails or abandons the full sealing project
- a minimum public-crisis duration has passed
- the field state remains valid for cave-country creation

The actual transition should use dynamic timing. It should not fire immediately when one value crosses a threshold.

### Evolution III log direction

The log should describe a resource field where attacks have left the excavation zone, settlements are being evacuated, and the owner must choose between continued extraction, military containment, and permanent closure. It can identify the creatures as public and physical. It should not expose the world-end condition.

## Evolution IV: The Deep Host

### Design role

Evolution IV ends the owner-managed field and creates a new playable nonhuman cave country in the site. The country is an aggressive resource-feeding army with unique deployment rules, slow armored units, no normal manpower or equipment economy, and an immediate war against every land neighbor.

The working country label in the specification is **The Deep Host**. This is not final localisation. The species, country, leader, army, and ideology need original names during implementation.

### Transition event

The final breach should be a major event with clear physical consequences:

- the field state’s access network opens into a much larger underground system
- organized armored creatures emerge in numbers
- surface settlements and field institutions collapse or are overrun
- the former owner loses control of the state
- surviving civilians and military units withdraw, are destroyed, or become part of an evacuation outcome
- the cave country establishes its capital in the field state
- all ordinary field contracts and management decisions end

The transition should not present the cave army as a random magical spawn. Its opening strength reflects the site’s exploitation history.

### Eligibility and state safety

The cave country can appear only when the field state can form a coherent country package.

The transition needs safe handling for:

- the owner being annexed during the incident chain
- the state changing hands
- the field being occupied by another country
- an existing cave country already active
- the state being isolated or lacking land neighbors
- the state being the last state of a small owner
- active civil war or faction complications
- incompatible terminal world-end state

The physical controller at breach should lose the state. The legal owner and discovering country can receive separate aftermath events.

If a cave country already exists, later Evolution IV breaches should normally create a connected uprising, transfer the new origin state if geographically valid, or create an allied brood only when the spec’s country-cap logic explicitly allows it. The preferred baseline is one persistent cave country per campaign so the nonhuman system remains coherent.

### Resource conversion at origin

The starting state’s Event 018 resource ledger is consumed into the origin package. The resources remain visible or are reinterpreted according to final balance, but the origin state is excluded from future captured-state brood capacity.

This exclusion is mandatory. The starting army already represents the resources and exploitation accumulated there. Counting the origin state again would double the opening strength.

### Starting army scaling

The starting army ranges from a credible minimum to a hard cap of 30 divisions.

A suitable design range is:

- weak, cautious, or early breach: 6 to 10 divisions
- developed breach: 11 to 18 divisions
- heavily exploited breach: 19 to 24 divisions
- extreme site exploitation: 25 to 30 divisions

The opening strength should use a combined exploitation score based on:

- total Event 018 resources added to the origin state
- number of distinct resource types
- discovery count
- maximum Developed Yield reached
- maximum Excavation Depth reached
- duration of maximum extraction
- field deaths
- failed closure attempts
- military exploitation during Evolution III
- unresolved surface nests

High Workforce Safety, successful evacuation, sealed galleries, and reduced active works can lower the opening score. The cap remains 30.

The accepted exact formula, score bands, mitigation caps, and deterministic regression fixture are maintained in `matrices/tuning_and_balance_framework.md`. The implementation centralizes them in script constants and `resources_found_calculate_cave_starting_strength`.

### Immediate war behavior

On creation, the cave country declares war on every current land neighbor. It must also refresh adjacency and declare war on newly adjacent land neighbors as it expands.

The country does not negotiate ordinary peace, join factions, accept guarantees, or participate in normal trade. It is an actual nonhuman country and a special chaos country for shared system exclusions.

### Evolution IV log direction

The evolution log should describe the emergence of an organized nonhuman country from the resource field, the loss of the state, and its immediate war against neighboring countries. It can reveal that the new army grows through captured resources. The world-end condition remains a separate terminal milestone.

## Evolution interactions with settings

Each evolution stage must respect the shared enable and disable controls.

- Disabled Evolution I does not grant compound-field evolution content.
- Disabled Evolution II prevents the sickness and underground-creature track from becoming required.
- Disabled Evolution III prevents public breach content and must allow Evolution II to settle through restriction, suspension, or closure.
- Disabled Evolution IV prevents cave-country creation and must allow Evolution III to remain containable or closable.

Disabling a later evolution must not strand the player in an unwinnable preceding stage. Every active field needs a clean alternate stabilization or closure route.

## Evolution acceptance standard

The evolution chain is complete when every stage has both a pre-fire opening and an active-field entry, uses dynamic pacing, changes player actions, records exactly one shared Event Details entry per event-wide tier while preserving later fields' local history, respects enable settings, preserves the promised closure route, and hands Evolution IV into a full cave-country package rather than a one-event enemy spawn.
