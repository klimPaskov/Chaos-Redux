# Event 018 Resources Found Specification, Part 3

## Trade, foreign pressure, commissions, and border conflict

All names in this file are working labels for design structure. They are not final localisation.

## Strategic purpose

A hundred units of a strategic resource can change wartime trade, industrial planning, and diplomatic priorities. The field should therefore create international behavior without turning every discovery into a war. Foreign countries react according to need, access, ideology, relations, claims, distance, and military opportunity.

The owner should be able to use the field to build friendships, invite investment, play buyers against each other, reserve the deposit, nationalize foreign holdings, or provoke a regional crisis. Foreign countries should have several tools before military action becomes available.

The diplomatic system needs escalation, memory, and off-ramps. A border war should be the result of a contested field and failed management. It must not be a random punishment for discovering resources.

## Foreign interest score

Every relevant foreign country can calculate an interest score for each active field. Only the highest-value candidates need active decisions or events. The system should avoid creating one decision for every country in the world.

### Positive interest factors

- the country has a current deficit in one of the field’s resources
- the country imports a large share of that resource
- the country is at war and needs the resource for active production
- the country has poor access to existing suppliers
- the field is nearby or connected by land, sea, or faction access
- the country already trades with the owner
- the country is the concession partner
- the country has a claim on the field state
- the country is a rival or enemy of another bidder
- the field has high Developed Yield
- the field contains several resource types
- the field has reached a globally significant total
- the owner is diplomatically isolated or militarily weak

### Negative interest factors

- the country has abundant domestic supply
- the route is inaccessible
- relations with the owner are extremely poor and no coercive route is viable
- the country is already overextended by war
- the field is suspended or closing
- the state is deep inside a strong hostile country
- an international commission has guaranteed access
- the country is an actual nonhuman or terminal actor that does not use ordinary trade

### Interest tiers

The exact numeric model belongs to implementation. The design needs clear behavior tiers.

- **Observer**: receives flavor, watches the field, and may begin intelligence collection.
- **Buyer**: seeks a normal trade contract or market access.
- **Investor**: offers machinery, transport, and capital in exchange for influence.
- **Strategic claimant**: uses guarantees, pressure, covert action, or demands.
- **Crisis actor**: prepares coercion, supports border incidents, or participates in a commission.

A country can move between tiers as war, trade, ownership, and field values change.

## Owner-facing diplomacy

## Invite bids

The owner can invite several interested countries to submit offers. The bidding event should compare a small shortlist rather than presenting every possible country.

Offer packages can emphasize:

- machinery and engineering teams
- rail or port construction
- civilian factories or construction support
- equipment and trucks
- fuel storage and refining
- diplomatic guarantee
- favorable trade relations
- immediate cash-like economic support represented by temporary civilian capacity
- a long-term share of output
- exclusive access
- a military supply commitment

The strongest offer is not automatically the best. Exclusive access can create rivalry. A weak country may value a guarantee more than machinery. A major at war may prefer domestic control.

## Sign an export contract

A contract should create a persistent relationship with a defined duration or renewal point. It can give the buyer priority access and give the owner machinery, construction, relations, or a staged economic benefit.

Contracts should respond to actual conditions:

- if the owner loses the field, delivery fails
- if the route is cut, the buyer can demand restoration
- if the field is suspended, the contract pauses or is breached
- if the field is closed, the owner must settle or repudiate the agreement
- if the buyer disappears, the contract ends cleanly
- if war begins between the parties, the contract terminates or converts into seizure pressure

The player should see which contract is active and what would happen if it were broken.

## Reserve strategic output

The owner can limit exports to protect domestic production. This is easier during war, shortage, embargo, or national mobilization. It increases pressure from buyers and concession holders.

A resource-rich country with no meaningful deficit should have less reason to reserve everything. AI should consider the actual domestic use.

## Use the field as diplomatic leverage

The owner can offer access in return for:

- a guarantee
- faction admission
- equipment aid
- investment
- debt relief represented by economic support
- recognition of a territorial settlement
- withdrawal of a competing claim
- support in an international commission

This action should require a real interested partner and should not create free alliances from a minor deposit.

## Balance competing buyers

The owner can avoid dependence by dividing access among several buyers. This lowers any one partner’s influence and can improve short-term terms. It raises administrative complexity, espionage, and rivalry.

The route suits neutral, democratic, commercial, or small states seeking autonomy. It should require enough administrative capacity to enforce quotas.

## Foreign-facing actions

## Request a purchase agreement

A buyer approaches the owner with a normal trade offer. Good relations, accessible routes, and owner openness increase acceptance.

The request should remain a diplomatic action. It does not automatically seize output or bypass the normal trade system.

## Offer development assistance

An investor provides machinery, specialists, construction support, or transport. In return, it gains concession influence, priority access, and a stake in later negotiations.

The owner can accept, demand better terms, or refuse. Repeated refusals from a strategically desperate country can raise pressure.

## Seek exclusive rights

A major buyer asks the owner to exclude rivals. Accepting creates a strong bilateral relationship and can draw the field into faction politics. Refusal can improve relations with rival bidders or strengthen the owner’s neutral position.

## Guarantee the field

A foreign country can guarantee the owner or the field settlement when it has strong strategic interest and enough military reach. The guarantee should be narrower in presentation than a permanent alliance, but it can use existing diplomatic mechanics where needed.

The guarantee raises the cost of seizure and can lower border-war willingness. It also increases the guarantor’s expectation of access.

## Sponsor survey teams

A foreign country sends technical teams openly or covertly. Open teams improve knowledge and relations. Covert teams can steal geological information, identify sabotage points, or support smugglers.

## Covert competition

Foreign competition should create an intermediate layer before overt conflict.

### Smuggling network

Smugglers divert output through ports, rail junctions, border crossings, company depots, and corrupt officials. Smuggling pressure rises when:

- the owner restricts exports
- prices are high
- borders are porous
- Foreign Pressure is high
- the field is commercially chartered
- several buyers compete
- local security is weak

Smuggling lowers the owner’s effective benefit and can supply a foreign sponsor. The owner can attack the network through state security, border control, rail protection, and legal market access.

### Industrial espionage

Foreign agents steal reserve maps, test results, processing methods, and contract information. Success increases the sponsor’s leverage and can reveal the field’s true value to rivals.

### Sabotage

Sabotage damages infrastructure, delays projects, raises safety risks, or makes a contract partner appear unreliable. It should require a hostile sponsor with strong interest and suitable intelligence access.

Sabotage can be exposed. Exposure creates opinion changes, a diplomatic demand, or escalation toward a commission or border crisis.

### Local political sponsorship

A foreign country can support business groups, regional politicians, labor organizers, separatists, or security factions that favor its access. The exact skin should fit the owner’s politics and avoid inventing a generic internal faction where no social base exists.

## Contract ownership and nationalization

Foreign concession influence should be visible and limited. It is not literal ownership of the state.

### Concession share

A concession partner can accumulate influence through investment, machinery, loans, and contract renewal. High influence provides stronger development and priority access. It also creates a compensation claim if the owner changes terms.

### Renegotiation

Renegotiation can produce:

- higher owner share
- higher foreign investment
- broader access for several countries
- labor and safety standards
- military security clauses
- a neutral arbitration process
- partial nationalization

### Expropriation

The owner can seize foreign assets when it has enough domestic administration, security, and political support. The partner can respond with:

- compensation negotiation
- embargo or trade pressure
- withdrawal of specialists
- sabotage support
- diplomatic isolation efforts
- acceptance if the partner is weak or relations are strong

Expropriation must not automatically cause war. It should create a dispute whose intensity depends on the field’s value, contract size, owner strength, and global situation.

### Compensation settlement

The owner can pay compensation through civilian capacity, resource deliveries, or a staged agreement. A fair settlement reduces foreign pressure and stabilizes national control.

## International commission route

An international commission is a crisis settlement for a field whose value, border position, and competing claims have made unilateral management dangerous.

It can emerge from:

- competing concession claims
- a field near a disputed border
- exposed sabotage by several countries
- failed bilateral negotiation
- a ceasefire after a border incident
- a small owner requesting protection from stronger neighbors
- Evolution I international attention

### Commission terms

The settlement can include:

- supervised export quotas
- neutral inspectors
- shared access for named countries
- limits on troop presence
- prohibition of new forts in the field state
- protection of existing civil administration
- compensation for a former concession holder
- a defined review period
- guarantees against unilateral annexation

### Demilitarization

Demilitarization should be a negotiated rule tied to the field state, not a random event effect. It should restrict military construction and concentration while allowing enough police or guard presence to protect workers and infrastructure.

The owner receives lower seizure risk and reduced Foreign Pressure. The cost is reduced freedom to fortify or use the state as a military base.

### Commission failure

The commission can fail if:

- the owner secretly militarizes the field
- a claimant violates the border
- export quotas collapse
- inspectors are expelled
- the owner nationalizes all assets without settlement
- the commission guarantors go to war
- Evolution II or III makes ordinary inspection impossible

Failure can reopen the border crisis or transfer the field into emergency national control.

## Border and contested-region logic

A resource discovery near a border should increase tension only when a real dispute can form.

### Eligible claimant

A claimant needs at least one of the following:

- a core or claim on the field state
- a claim on an adjacent disputed region that includes the field in a plausible settlement
- an unresolved border agreement with the owner
- an existing hostile border incident system
- a strong concession claim combined with a territorial route explicitly mapped by the event

Simple adjacency and resource need are not enough.

### Border crisis prerequisites

A border crisis should require:

- high Foreign Pressure
- a valid claimant
- field value above an importance threshold
- direct land access or a defined local frontier
- neither side being an invalid nonhuman actor
- no current commission settlement that is functioning
- no active terminal world-end state
- enough military and political capacity for the claimant to act

War, national weakness, and repeated failed negotiations can increase likelihood.

## Border crisis stages

### Stage A: Competing surveys

Survey teams, local guards, and boundary commissions disagree over access or the exact border. The owner can invite arbitration, reinforce its administration, share data, or expel the rival team.

### Stage B: Customs and road confrontation

Checkpoints, rail spurs, roads, or camps overlap. Smuggling and arrests increase. Both sides can de-escalate, negotiate access, or send security forces.

### Stage C: Armed patrol incident

A patrol clash causes casualties or captures. The event should record which side escalated and whether the field state was already militarized.

The owner and claimant can still accept mediation, create a commission, or withdraw troops.

### Stage D: Timed frontier mission

Each side receives a visible objective around control, supplied divisions, roads, and the field state. The mission should last long enough to act. It should not be a passive manpower or stability check.

Objectives can include:

- maintain supplied divisions in named border states
- hold the field’s rail connection
- keep the field state under control
- avoid exceeding a troop cap during negotiations
- complete a survey or fortification project
- protect a diplomatic delegation

Success improves leverage. Failure can force concessions or open the border war.

### Stage E: Border war

The border war is a limited military contest for the field state. It should use the established border-war pattern where technically appropriate.

Outcomes:

- owner victory confirms control and reduces immediate claimant pressure
- claimant victory transfers the state and physical field
- stalemate creates a ceasefire, commission, or renewed negotiation
- outside intervention can broaden the crisis only if normal diplomacy supports it

The event must not silently start a full war when the designed outcome is a limited state contest.

### Stage F: Settlement

After the contest, the parties choose recognition, compensation, demilitarization, sabotage, or renewed claims. The field inherits damage and contract disruption.

## State transfer consequences

When the field state transfers:

- Event 018 resource additions stay with the state
- Developed Yield is reduced by combat and administrative disruption
- Excavation Depth stays with the physical site
- Workforce Safety can fall due to damaged works and displaced staff
- Foreign Pressure changes according to recognition and outside interests
- contracts are reviewed
- the new owner gains the field category
- the former owner loses field actions but can retain compensation or revanchist options
- selected-field UI and event targets update cleanly

The new owner does not receive a second first-discovery reward. It receives a takeover or transfer event with its own posture decision.

## Wider diplomatic reactions

Not every interested country needs a decision. Flavor events and opinion effects can communicate the field’s importance.

Possible reaction groups:

- nearby resource-poor countries seek access
- major industrial powers send commercial missions
- rivals accuse each other of coercion or covert action
- faction leaders ask the owner to reserve output for the bloc
- neutral countries advocate a commission
- embargoed countries support smuggling
- countries dependent on the owner’s exports improve relations when deliveries are reliable
- countries harmed by nationalization or exclusion lower relations

The strength of opinion changes should reflect actual material stakes. A distant country with no need should not care as much as a neighboring wartime importer.

## Integration with normal trade

Event 018 should enhance the normal resource economy rather than replace it with a separate currency.

The field’s added resources enter the state and normal trade system. Contracts, concessions, and guarantees create modifiers, access expectations, or diplomatic behavior around that supply. They should not create a second invisible stockpile that bypasses trade.

Trade access can still be interrupted by:

- convoy loss
- occupation
- naval blockade
- broken land route
- embargo
- contract suspension
- state damage
- closure

## AI diplomacy behavior

### Owner AI

Owner AI should consider:

- domestic resource deficit
- industrial capacity
- war status
- strength relative to interested powers
- ideology and economic posture
- stability
- field defensibility
- concession influence
- current contract value
- border claims
- evolution danger

Weak owners should be willing to accept investment or a commission. Strong nationalist owners should prefer national control. Commercial and democratic owners can balance buyers. Fascist or militarized owners can reserve output and fortify. Communist owners can nationalize or create state-to-state agreements. These are tendencies rather than absolute rules.

### Buyer AI

Buyer AI should consider:

- actual deficit and imports
- route access
- war production need
- relations
- ability to provide investment
- rivalry with other bidders
- risk of owner collapse
- field value
- whether coercion would trigger a stronger guarantor

### Claimant AI

Claimants should avoid border escalation when:

- they are much weaker
- the field has a strong guarantee
- they lack supply or local access
- they are losing another war
- a commission provides acceptable access
- the field is suspended or closing

Claimants become more aggressive when the state is already disputed, the owner is weak, the field is highly valuable, and negotiations have failed.

## Connections to other Chaos Redux systems

### Event cluster exclusion

Event 018 remains outside the event-cluster system. Its resource-production, trade, and later subterranean escalation run only through its ordinary event and evolution paths.

### Tensions Rising, Event 8

A high-value field and unresolved claimant can add to regional tension or receive altered foreign-pressure behavior when Event 8 is active. Event 018 should not duplicate Event 8’s broader tension system.

### Great Embargo, Event 50

Embargoed countries value covert access and alternative contracts more strongly. Event 50 remains the owner of embargo mechanics.

### Border Conflict, Event 78

If Event 78 provides shared border-conflict infrastructure, Event 018 should use it rather than create a parallel generic war system. Event 018 still owns the resource-field prerequisites, transfer consequences, and settlement.

### Natural Disasters, Event 13

A disaster in the field state can damage development, interrupt contracts, reveal new seams, or complicate closure. Event 018 should not take over the disaster chain.

## Diplomatic completion standard

The diplomatic layer is complete when field value creates targeted foreign interest, trade and investment can improve the owner’s economy, contracts have lifecycle and consequences, smuggling and espionage offer intermediate conflict, nationalization and compensation are playable, a neutral commission and demilitarization route can resolve severe competition, and border war occurs only after valid claims and failed escalation stages.
