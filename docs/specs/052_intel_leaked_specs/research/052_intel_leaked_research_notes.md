# Event 052 Research Notes

## Research purpose

The research pass looked for historical principles that could improve four parts of Event 52:

- how stolen information loses value
- how a government responds when people, codes, and plans are compromised
- how controlled false information can exploit foreign reliance
- why the target should receive incomplete feedback instead of exact knowledge of foreign belief

The event remains fictional. Historical cases inform mechanics and presentation without assigning Event 52 to a real operation or claiming that one method always succeeds.

## Operation Fortitude and controlled belief

English Heritage's account of Operation Fortitude South describes a deception that reinforced an expectation already held by the German command. The operation combined a fictitious army, physical decoys, a large volume of false radio traffic, and careful leaks through controlled double agents. Intercepted German reactions gave the Allies evidence that the deception remained credible.

Design use:

- Poison the Leak should work best when the false material fits what a recipient already expects.
- One false order should not be enough. A strong deception needs consistent signals, posture, and controlled information.
- The target should pay an opportunity cost through tied formations, false basing, command effort, fuel, or lost operations.
- Recipient Reliance should grow through confirmation and repeated use.
- Feedback should come from observed foreign behavior, intercepted responses, allied reports, or exposed verification attempts.

Source:

- English Heritage, “D-Day Deception: Operation Fortitude South”
- https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/dover-castle/history-and-stories/d-day-deception/

## Military deception across several channels

U.S. Army University Press describes Fortitude South through spies, signals intelligence, and physical deception. The article stresses that credible false information, false radio traffic, physical indicators, and knowledge of enemy expectations worked together. It also notes that the exact causal weight of deception can be difficult to measure because the defender's institutional weaknesses and force position mattered.

Design use:

- Deception outcomes should depend on recipient capability, expectations, and actual plans.
- A target should not receive guaranteed success merely because it clicked Poison the Leak.
- The foreign mistake should be domain-specific and bounded.
- Strong success needs high Reliance, coherent preparation, and a matching recipient action.
- Partial success and failure belong in the outcome pool.
- The event should avoid claiming that a temporary planning penalty decided an entire war.

Source:

- Army University Press, “Assault on Fortress Europe”
- https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Journals/Military-Review/Online-Exclusive/2020-OLE/Carminati-Assault-Fortress-Europe/

## Double agents, counterintelligence, and operational risk

The CIA Center for the Study of Intelligence publishes Robert Cowden's study of OSS double-agent operations in the Second World War. The study is useful because double-agent work combined counterintelligence and deception while exposing services to uncertainty, compromised channels, and the risk that a controlled operation could fail.

Design use:

- Operatives and networks should face a graduated risk ladder.
- Recall and channel shutdown should be valid sacrifices, not weak cosmetic actions.
- A false contact chain should be the highest-risk deception profile.
- Strong services can protect more personnel and maintain selective channels, but they should not be immune.
- Severe outcomes should be bounded per mission or tranche to prevent an implausible cascade across every operative.

Source:

- CIA Center for the Study of Intelligence, Robert Cowden, “OSS Double-Agent Operations in World War II”
- https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/studies-in-intelligence/volume-58-no-2/oss-double-agent-operations-in-world-war-ii/

## VENONA and the slow reconstruction of hidden networks

The NSA and CIA VENONA collection documents a long cryptanalytic and counterintelligence effort built from intercepted Soviet messages and later analysis. The official NSA catalogue identifies the 1996 collection edited by Robert Louis Benson and Michael Warner.

Design use:

- Cryptologic material can reveal tradecraft, sources, and contact chains without granting instant certainty.
- Trace the Source should return practical route classes and partial understanding.
- A cryptologic breach can remain dangerous after the initial discovery because old traffic can be compared with later facts.
- Archive Confidence can grow when independent evidence matches the stolen material.
- Investigation findings should improve one response family without naming a permanent culprit.

Sources:

- NSA, “VENONA: Soviet Espionage and the American Response 1939-1957”
- https://www.nsa.gov/History/Cryptologic-History/Historical-Publications/Historical-Publications-Lists/igphoto/2002761778/
- CIA Center for the Study of Intelligence, “Venona: Soviet Espionage and the American Response”

## Research conclusions

The research supports five final design decisions.

### A leak is useful because it is current

Exposure should measure operational usefulness, not paper quantity. The archive can remain physically present after plans, codes, personnel, and production estimates become obsolete.

### Foreign governments should differ in use

Every ordinary foreign government receives the windfall, but only strategically interested and capable governments should become named exploiters.

### Deception should exploit reliance

A recipient that never trusted or used the archive should not suffer a large false-information penalty.

### Counterintelligence requires sacrifice

Closing networks, recalling people, and replacing procedures protect the target while reducing its own reach and short-term readiness.

### Investigation should remain incomplete

The event is repeatable and unexplained. The investigation can find a useful route class without reducing every firing to the same culprit.

## Research limits

- Historical deception outcomes are difficult to isolate from broader military and institutional conditions.
- The event should use historical principles, not deterministic claims.
- The sources do not support a single universal formula for deception success.
- Exact HOI4 numerical tuning requires implementation evidence and the probability audit.
- Agency and intelligence mechanics must be checked against the installed game documentation and local vanilla precedents before coding.
