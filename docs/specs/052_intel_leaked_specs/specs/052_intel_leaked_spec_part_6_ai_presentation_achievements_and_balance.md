# AI, Presentation, Achievements, and Balance

## AI design principle

AI countries should understand the event as a temporary intelligence crisis with competing priorities.

The target AI decides which danger matters most. Foreign AI decides whether the archive is useful enough to exploit and whether its relationship with the target justifies aggressive use.

AI should not click every available response. It should preserve resources, accept losses when necessary, and use deception mainly when it has enough capability to sustain it.

## Target AI priorities

### Imminent invasion or active war

A target facing a credible invasion should prioritize:

1. Rewrite Compromised Plans in the threatened domain.
2. Replace Codes and Authentication Tables when cryptologic material is active.
3. Recall Exposed Personnel when live operations or agents face severe risk.
4. Shut Down Vulnerable Channels when the service cannot protect them selectively.
5. Poison the Leak only after immediate military danger is addressed.

A target under attack should accept command friction and experience costs more readily than a target at peace.

### Strong intelligence service

A strong service should:

- use selective recalls instead of closing every channel
- preserve valuable networks when risk is manageable
- investigate named exploiters
- restore liaison channels
- attempt Poison the Leak when hostile Reliance is high
- use false contact chains only when personnel protection is strong

It should still close a route when the Personnel at Risk mission is close to failure.

### Weak intelligence service

A weak service should:

- shut vulnerable channels
- recall exposed personnel
- replace codes
- accept lost networks
- avoid high-risk deception unless the archive is already fading
- invest in compartmentation when resources permit

A weak AI should not spend most of its economy on complete recovery when survival requires factories and equipment elsewhere.

### Resource-poor target

A target with little command power, experience, equipment, or civilian capacity should choose one or two high-value responses.

It can accept a longer Exposure period. It should not attempt an unaffordable full package or loop on blocked decisions.

### Peacetime target

A peacetime target should place more value on lasting compartmentation, liaison repair, and network reconstruction.

It can attempt deception when a hostile rival is preparing war, but it should not tie down major forces for a false deployment without a credible recipient.

## Foreign AI priorities

### Hostile wartime government

A hostile country at war should exploit immediately in domains it can use.

It should prefer:

- land plans when it shares a front
- naval files when it has a meaningful fleet or invasion plan
- air files when it contests target air regions
- industrial shortages when it can bomb, blockade, or pressure supply
- personnel files when it has a capable service

Its Reliance grows quickly when the target continues the exposed posture.

### Hostile government preparing war

A government preparing war should use the archive to improve timing and target selection.

It should avoid triggering a war through Event 52 alone. The event can strengthen an existing preparation path.

### Neighbor, claimant, or regional rival

A smaller neighbor can become a named land or political exploiter even without a large agency.

It should focus on border plans, mobilization, internal weaknesses, and diplomatic intentions.

### Naval rival

A naval rival should receive strong priority only when naval or convoy domains are active.

It should not become a named naval exploiter based only on being a major power.

### Friendly ally

A friendly ally should normally:

- avoid aggressive exploitation
- warn the target
- share a sample
- quarantine its copy
- help restore a liaison channel

It can keep defensive knowledge. It can shift to aggressive use if relations collapse or war begins.

### Neutral opportunist

A neutral government can use industrial or diplomatic information for bargaining. It should rarely receive strong military exploitation without a border, claim, war plan, or major service.

## Decision AI

Each action needs a score based on present danger, cost, and expected effect.

Important ordering expectations include:

- Personnel at Risk near failure should dominate low-value reconstruction.
- Rewrite Compromised Plans should dominate general reform when an enemy is preparing an invasion in that domain.
- Replace Codes should dominate when cryptologic files and high foreign confidence are active.
- Shut Down Vulnerable Channels should dominate for weak services with severe personnel risk.
- Compartmentalize the Archive should rise in peacetime and after immediate danger falls.
- Poison the Leak should rise with service strength, hostile Reliance, target capacity, and remaining Exposure.
- False deployment should remain near zero when no credible recipient can act on the lie.
- Restore Trusted Liaison Channels should remain unavailable without a valid partner.

The implementation should use the named scenarios in the probability matrix. Exact weights should be accepted only after inspection and comparison.

## Evolution II target AI

Each AI target handles its own state.

The sequence coordinator should limit processing and reports without merging AI decisions.

When two AI targets are at war, each can exploit the other and prioritize replanning. The stronger service can gain a temporary edge by lowering its own Exposure faster.

A target should not spend against an archive that has already reached zero merely because another target remains exposed.

## Presentation direction

### Target opening report

Viewpoint: the compromised government.

Visible information:

- classified material is circulating abroad
- several archive domains are confirmed
- exact distribution is unknown
- personnel and plans may still be current
- immediate action can make the files obsolete

Tone: controlled alarm, uncertainty, and urgent professional response.

Avoid:

- generic references to an update or rework
- raw mechanics
- claims that every foreign government received identical files
- a final explanation for the source
- stock phrases about history never being the same
- long lists of modifiers

The opening option should communicate recognition of the emergency and the need to act. Final wording should be concise and in-world.

### Foreign report

Viewpoint: a government that has received or confirmed part of the archive.

Visible information:

- the target's secrets are circulating
- copies vary in completeness
- foreign ministries and commands are studying them
- the target is already changing plans

Tone varies by relationship:

- enemies show controlled interest
- allies show concern and restraint
- neutrals show caution and opportunism
- strong services show confidence mixed with authentication concerns

### Exploitation reports

Reports should name the relevant domain and observed foreign behavior.

Examples of report direction include:

- enemy reserves shift toward an exposed axis
- foreign patrols anticipate a fleet route
- hostile aircraft concentrate in a region implied by the archive
- negotiators arrive with unusual knowledge of private limits
- an operation becomes unsafe after cover details surface

These are direction notes, not final lines.

### Damage-control reports

Reports should show what changed and what it cost.

The player should understand that:

- new codes create temporary confusion
- recalled personnel abandon valuable access
- new plans take time to circulate
- closed channels reduce risk and blindness at the same time
- compartmentation hardens future systems

### Deception reports

Deception text should preserve uncertainty until a recipient acts.

A success should show the foreign mistake through deployment, operation, or diplomatic behavior. It should not announce an abstract success chance.

A failure should show contradiction, detected false traffic, or a foreign service refusing to rely on the planted material.

### Closing reports

Closing text should match the result:

- controlled recovery
- expensive recovery
- passive expiry
- severe personnel loss
- successful deception
- unresolved investigation

The incident remains unexplained even when the practical leak route becomes clearer.

## Decision category presentation

The category uses:

- one Exposure value
- a clear qualitative stage
- active domain icons or short labels
- one urgent status line
- three to five current actions
- one to three active missions
- a static category picture

The header should not imitate a debug ledger. It should avoid pipe-separated telemetry and raw variable names.

The category picture should communicate code replacement, abandoned files, compromised communications, and rapid reorganization. It must remain presentation and should not paint fake buttons or meters into the image.

## Event Log direction

Each Event 52 firing creates one normal Event Log history entry.

Baseline actor: the compromised target.

Total Compromise actor: the primary selected target, with detail text identifying that several governments were compromised when relevant.

The event detail premise should explain the global circulation of a target's classified archive and the temporary race to make it useless. It should not list decision effects, hidden Reliance, rare profiles, or future evolution surprises.

Deep Files and Total Compromise each create one evolution log entry through the shared evolution pipeline. Their evolution records are global event mutations and do not need a country actor.

Foreign exploitation reports, damage-control reports, and closing reports do not create additional normal Event Log history rows unless the shared event design requires a major milestone entry.

## Visual asset direction

The event requires a coordinated intelligence-crisis asset family.

### Existing event images

The repository already names:

- `GFX_report_event_intel_leak`
- `GFX_news_intel_leak`

The existing DDS paths should be hydrated through the Git LFS workflow and visually audited before replacement. File size alone is not enough to classify them.

Preserve the sprite names when possible.

The report image should show a period intelligence or government office overwhelmed by copied files, courier bags, code sheets, teleprinters, and hurried destruction or replacement work. It should avoid readable generated text and avoid making a map the main subject.

The news image should show the international spread of the archive through embassies, ministries, military headquarters, or diplomatic bags. It should be visually distinct enough from the target report image to justify a separate asset.

### Decision category picture

A static category picture should show the compromised system from the target's perspective. Suitable elements include open cipher cabinets, discarded code cards, teleprinter tape, empty file drawers, burned cover papers, and officers replacing authentication sheets.

The implementation artist should inspect the canonical decision-category picture reference family and the active consumer before fixing the runtime canvas.

### Icons

The package requires:

- one decision category icon
- separate decision icons for every implemented action family
- one Compromised Archive idea or status icon when the implementation uses a visible temporary idea
- achievement icon triplets for the planned achievements

Each icon family receives separate source art for its own UI size. A focus icon or idea icon should not be resized to satisfy a decision icon.

## Achievement set

Achievement names below are working labels.

### Before the Ink Dries

Purpose: reward rapid, clean containment.

Unlock direction:

- the player is the Event 52 target
- initial Exposure is at least 90
- Exposure reaches 25 or less before a severe named exploitation outcome succeeds
- no force-trigger or debug path was used

Difficulty: high.

The achievement requires immediate prioritization and real resource sacrifice.

### A Better Falsehood

Purpose: reward deep use of Poison the Leak.

Unlock direction:

- the player is the target
- Poison the Leak succeeds in one incident
- at least three hostile high-Reliance governments suffer distinct domain-relevant false-assumption outcomes before the incident closes
- no force-trigger or debug path was used

Difficulty: very high.

The achievement cannot unlock from passive confidence loss or from countries that never relied on the archive.

### Everyone Knows Everything

Purpose: reward survival during wartime Total Compromise.

Unlock direction:

- Total Compromise is active
- the player and at least one current war enemy are both targets in the same sequence
- the player reduces Exposure to zero
- the player keeps control of the capital through the incident
- no force-trigger or debug path was used

Difficulty: high.

The condition remains valid without agency DLC.

### No Names Left Behind

Purpose: reward repeated personnel protection.

Unlock direction:

- the same player country resolves three separate Event 52 incidents
- no incident produces an operative capture, destroyed critical network, or equivalent severe base-game personnel failure
- each incident began with a real personnel-risk profile
- no force-trigger or debug path was used

Difficulty: long-form high.

Agency-enabled and base-game paths should use equivalent proof.

## Balance anchors

### Incident length

- baseline unresolved: roughly 120 to 240 days
- baseline with strong active response: roughly 60 to 140 days
- Deep Files unresolved: roughly 180 to 330 days
- severe Total Compromise target: roughly 210 to 360 days

These are experience targets, not fixed durations for every country.

### Exposure

- baseline opening: 55 to 85
- Deep Files opening: 75 to 100
- severe Total Compromise opening: normally 90 to 100
- strong single emergency action: roughly 10 to 25 points when relevant
- lasting compartmentation current-incident effect: small
- lasting compartmentation future opening reduction: bounded, with a total cap that cannot erase the event

### Exploitation

- baseline named exploiters: three to six
- Deep Files named exploiters: five to nine
- severe Total Compromise named exploiters: six to ten
- ordinary concrete exploitation reports: two to five across a baseline incident
- severe personnel outcomes: normally zero or one per target, with a second possible only after a proven new tranche

### Decision visibility

- primary visible actions per phase: three to five
- hard maximum: six
- active missions: one to three

### Effect strength

The event should matter most when war, relevant archive domains, high Exposure, foreign capability, and target delay align.

At peace, the main cost is institutional disruption and rebuilding.

At war, the same archive can alter planning, expose operations, and create foreign timing advantages.

The event remains a low-severity repeatable incident. It should create pressure and opportunity without replacing the outcome of actual military strength, supply, production, and player decisions.
