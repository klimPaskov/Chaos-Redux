# Evolutions, Repeatability, Cluster Role, and System Connections

## Evolution structure

Event 52 has two global evolution stages.

The evolution state changes future Event 52 incidents and can deepen an incident already in progress. Evolution activation uses the shared evolution pacing and logging contract. It does not add Chaos by itself.

The evolution track should remain enabled or disabled through the existing Event Details controls. Disabling an evolution prevents its deeper incident behavior without breaking the baseline recovery loop.

## Evolution I: Deep Files

- Chaos requirement: `400+`
- Working evolution name: Deep Files

Deep Files means that the circulating archive includes material close to the target's active decision cycle.

The evolution can expose:

- current military plans
- deeper intelligence networks
- ongoing covert operations
- operative identities or descriptions
- resistance contacts
- code procedures
- research priorities
- strategic shortages
- commander assessments
- mobilization plans
- reinforcement assumptions
- emergency government procedures

### Evolution pacing

When the world reaches 400 Chaos and Deep Files is enabled, the evolution becomes eligible for the event-owned evolution pulse.

The ordinary pacing anchor is roughly 75 to 135 days. The evolution should not activate instantly only because the threshold was crossed.

Faster factors can include:

- Event 52 has already fired at least once
- an Event 52 incident is active at Severe or Critical Exposure
- several countries are at war
- global intelligence and diplomatic pressure are high
- another Intelligence-cluster incident is active

Slower factors can include:

- Event 52 has never fired
- no valid target currently exists
- the event is disabled
- the evolution is disabled

The shared evolution log records one Deep Files activation for Event 52. It should not record one entry per target or repeat firing.

### Pre-fire evolved opening

When Deep Files is already active before Event 52 fires, the incident begins at the deeper profile.

The opening package changes as follows:

- initial Exposure normally begins from 75 to 100
- the archive normally contains five to seven active domains
- one sensitive domain is guaranteed when supported, such as agent registries, cryptologic material, mobilization plans, or strategic shortages
- the expected Archive Freshness duration increases
- named exploiter capacity increases
- Personnel at Risk is more likely to begin immediately
- damage-control actions cost more or impose larger sacrifices
- a strong service still reduces risk but cannot force a shallow opening

The baseline one-target rule remains in force until Total Compromise is active.

### Mid-incident second tranche

When Deep Files activates during an unresolved baseline incident, it can create a delayed second tranche.

The tranche should occur only when:

- Exposure remains above the Fading stage
- the target still has at least one valid deeper domain
- the incident has not already received a deep tranche
- the evolution remains enabled

The ordinary delay is 30 to 90 days after evolution activation. This gives the target a chance to close a nearly resolved incident before deeper files surface.

The second tranche can:

- add one or two sensitive domains
- raise Exposure by a bounded amount
- reopen Personnel at Risk
- add one or two named exploiters
- restore one relevant emergency action
- increase foreign confidence in an authentic part of the archive

The tranche is a concrete consequence and can produce one event-owned Chaos increase. The evolution state itself remains neutral.

### Deep Files outcome changes

Deep Files raises the chance of:

- operation delay or cancellation
- burned cover
- operative capture when several risk factors align
- a foreign service identifying current shortages
- an enemy acting before the target completes a replan
- a deception campaign producing a strong result against a curator

It also raises the value of decisive counterplay. Replacing codes, recalling personnel, and feeding false active plans can destroy a larger part of the archive's value.

## Evolution II: Total Compromise

- Chaos requirement: `800+`
- Working evolution name: Total Compromise

Total Compromise changes the scale of one Event 52 firing.

A firing can create:

- one exceptionally severe target profile
- two simultaneous target countries
- three simultaneous target countries
- four simultaneous target countries in the rarest profile

Each target receives its own archive, Exposure, duration, named exploiters, personnel risk, decisions, missions, deception state, and cleanup.

The event still counts as one random-event firing and one pacing transaction.

### Evolution pacing

When the world reaches 800 Chaos and Total Compromise is enabled, the evolution becomes eligible for event-owned pacing.

The ordinary activation anchor is roughly 90 to 150 days.

The activation can become faster when:

- Deep Files is active
- Event 52 fired more than once
- several major wars are active
- at least two valid major powers can be selected
- an active Event 52 incident remains unresolved at high Exposure

The shared evolution log records one Total Compromise activation.

### Pre-fire target profile

When Total Compromise is active before a new firing, the suggested starting distribution is:

- severe single target: about 35 percent
- two targets: about 40 percent
- three targets: about 20 percent
- four targets: about 5 percent

These are balance anchors for the implementation probability audit. The final weights must preserve the intended ordering and must adapt safely when the valid pool is small.

A multi-target firing selects valid player and major candidates according to the accepted target rules. The source player's country can be included alongside major powers. Several random major powers can be selected without a player target.

The selection should prefer strategically connected groups when possible, such as wartime opponents, major faction rivals, neighboring powers, or countries preparing against one another. It should avoid selecting several countries that have no plausible use for each other's information when a more relevant pool exists.

A target appears only once in the sequence.

### Severe single-target profile

The severe single profile is valid when the pool contains only one target and as an intentional Total Compromise outcome when several targets exist.

It normally begins with:

- Exposure near the top of the range
- seven to ten active domains where supported
- at least one live plan or personnel domain
- six to ten named exploiters
- a longer Archive Freshness mission
- immediate emergency missions
- stronger chance of an Enemy Curator
- higher costs for complete neutralization

This profile concentrates the evolved event without inventing invalid additional targets.

### Multi-target profile

Every target is independent.

One target can recover quickly while another remains exposed. One target can use Poison the Leak while another closes all channels. A friendly country can warn one target while exploiting another.

Human players receive only their own decision category and target reports. AI targets process their own responses through equivalent actions.

The sequence uses a global participant cap for named exploiters and reports. A four-target incident should not create four times the popup volume of a baseline incident.

### Wartime mutual compromise

When two targets are already at war, both can possess exceptional intelligence about the other.

The event should mark that relationship and allow:

- stronger initial exploitation
- faster recipient Reliance
- domain-specific replanning on both sides
- reciprocal deception attempts
- rapid changes in advantage when one side lowers Exposure first

The event should not apply identical blanket combat bonuses to both sides. The active domains and each side's response determine the result.

### Mid-incident Total Compromise escalation

When Total Compromise activates during a baseline or Deep Files incident, the sequence can add one or two valid targets after a delayed escalation.

The existing target keeps its own progress. New targets begin fresh incident states.

Additional targets appear only when:

- the current sequence has not already escalated through Total Compromise
- valid targets exist
- the evolution remains enabled
- adding targets does not exceed the sequence cap

A nearly resolved target does not need to be reset upward merely because new countries become exposed.

The mid-incident escalation is a concrete consequence and can create event-owned Chaos for the added exposure.

## Repeatability contract

A later Event 52 firing creates a fresh sequence.

It does not inherit:

- prior target selection
- prior Exposure
- prior archive domains
- prior duration
- prior exploiters
- prior Reliance
- prior missions
- prior deception state
- prior investigation route
- prior cleanup progress

It can inherit real lasting results:

- captured or wounded operatives
- lost network strength
- cancelled operations
- changed military plans
- lasting compartmentation resilience
- damaged or improved liaison relations
- wars influenced by earlier information
- achievement progress
- prior target history used for repeat-aware flavour

A target can be selected again. A repeat firing against the same country should acknowledge that its services recognize the pattern, while the new archive remains independently generated.

The event becomes available again only after its current sequence is fully closed. The shared Repeatable event system then governs weight recovery and cap reduction.

## Intelligence cluster role

Event 52 joins Cluster 10, Intelligence, as a Low-severity member.

Its cluster role is broad information exposure, counterintelligence pressure, and deception opportunity.

Event 039 remains the Medium-severity fire-once member focused on leadership murder, investigation, assassin networks, and its own later escalation.

The cluster should treat the two events as complementary:

- Event 039 creates a violent intelligence and leadership shock.
- Event 052 exposes archives, plans, contacts, and foreign reliance.

A cluster firing still counts as one pacing event. Each member applies its own event state, logging, repeatable or fire-once handling, and member result.

Event 52 can skip within a cluster firing when:

- no valid target exists
- an Event 52 sequence is already active
- the event is disabled
- its Chaos level is unavailable
- the selected cluster actor cannot satisfy Event 52's target rules and no valid independent target can be selected under the cluster contract

The cluster log should show the established skip reason.

### Same-target cluster interaction

When Event 039 and Event 052 affect the same valid country, a bounded interaction becomes available.

Possible effects include:

- leaked security files reveal one investigation lead
- leaked personnel records endanger a witness or investigator
- a foreign exploiter receives a fragment related to the murder case
- a False Originals profile introduces misleading case material

The exact result depends on Event 039's public helper contract. Event 52 should not reach into Event 039's private lifecycle state.

The interaction should move one existing Event 039 pressure or lead. It should not create Event 039's cult, country, territorial, or terminal outcomes from Event 52 alone.

## Event 011 Secret Alliance connection

Event 011 already reserves a future connection for Event 52.

When Event 52 exposes a Secret Alliance member, the leak can increase the coalition's Evidence because shared correspondence or operational contacts become easier to trace.

When Event 52 exposes the coalition's target, the leak can increase Pact Readiness because members gain useful plans, weaknesses, or diplomatic intentions.

This connection should use a stable Event 52 public contract and Event 011's stable adapter. It should remain bounded and one-shot per Event 52 sequence.

Event 052 is never a prerequisite for Event 011.

## Event 008 Tensions Rising connection

When Tensions Rising is active, hostile countries can receive higher named-exploitation priority because governments are already searching for threats and opportunities.

A severe Event 52 exploitation result can create one event-specific tension flare if the Event 008 public contract supports it.

The event must not duplicate generic world-tension or Chaos changes already produced by wars, guarantees, or Event 008 itself.

## Event 050 The Great Embargo connection

When industrial, resource, diplomatic, or shortage domains are active, a stable Great Embargo adapter can let sanctioning governments target pressure more accurately.

The connection can:

- improve embargo preparation
- reveal a fuel or resource weakness
- increase a named exploiter's economic Reliance
- create a false-shortage deception route

Event 52 should not directly start The Great Embargo or remove resources.

## Event 094 Half Gone connection

When Half Gone has created a known stockpile shock, Event 52 industrial or procurement files can help foreign governments recognize which losses matter most.

The connection can improve foreign estimates or deception value. It should not repeat the stockpile loss.

## Event 097 Collaboration connection

When a stable Collaboration public contract exists, exposed contact chains can endanger collaboration networks or reveal that a foreign government has access inside the target.

The hook should change an existing collaboration pressure or risk. Event 52 should not create a new collaboration system.

## Future optional connections

Events 145 The Book, 146 Add Operative, and 147 Counterintelligence are unavailable in the current catalog snapshot.

The implementation should expose a small Event 52 owner API so those future events can connect without reading private incident ledgers. The initial Event 52 implementation should not depend on unavailable content.

Useful public facts can include:

- whether a country is an active Event 52 target
- current qualitative Exposure stage
- active archive domain classes
- whether a country is a named exploiter against the target
- whether deception is active
- whether a bounded external hook has already been consumed in the sequence

Any external action that changes Exposure or Reliance should use proof-carrying inputs, bounded limits, and an owner-controlled result.

## Chaos impact

Event 52 is destabilizing, but its low member severity requires restrained event-owned Chaos.

### Baseline manifestation

The public opening creates `+1` Chaos once per sequence.

An exceptionally severe baseline opening can create one additional point when it begins at the top of the Exposure range with several sensitive domains. This should be rare and clearly recorded.

### Severe personnel or operation consequence

The first captured operative, destroyed critical operation, or equivalent severe base-game personnel consequence can create `+1` Chaos once per target.

Lower network losses and routine operation delays create no Event 52 Chaos.

### Deep Files consequence

A mid-incident Deep Files second tranche creates `+1` Chaos once when it adds real sensitive material and raises Exposure.

Deep Files activation alone creates zero.

### Total Compromise consequence

A multi-target firing creates `+1` Chaos for each target beyond the first, capped at `+3` from added targets in one sequence.

A severe single-target Total Compromise profile creates `+2` at manifestation instead of the ordinary `+1` baseline manifestation.

Total Compromise activation alone creates zero.

### Reversal

A target that began at Severe or Critical Exposure and reaches zero before Archive Freshness expires can create `-1` Chaos once per target.

Passive expiry does not create a reversal.

### Shared-source overlap

Event 52 does not add separate Chaos for:

- wars
- annexations
- deaths
- ideology changes
- world tension already tracked by shared systems
- ordinary military buildup
- generic operation outcomes owned by another event

The Event 52 history entry should identify its own source, such as public intelligence breach, deep tranche, added target, severe personnel loss, or full neutralization.

## Anti-farming rules

- Manifestation applies once per sequence or once per target as defined by the profile.
- Severe consequence applies once per target.
- Deep tranche applies once per sequence.
- Added-target Chaos uses the number of new valid targets and a sequence cap.
- Recovery reversal applies once per target and requires active containment before the natural deadline.
- Repeated decision clicks cannot create Chaos.
- Evolution activation never changes Chaos.
- Cleanup and reopening cannot repeat a committed source.
