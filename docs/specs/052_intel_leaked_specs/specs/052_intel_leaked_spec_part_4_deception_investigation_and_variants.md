# Deception, Investigation, and Incident Variants

## Poison the Leak

Poison the Leak is the event's main counterplay route.

The target accepts that the archive cannot be recovered and uses foreign dependence on the stolen material against the recipients. The route should feel risky, deliberate, and connected to actual foreign behavior.

The route does not apply a blanket penalty to every country holding a copy. It affects named exploiters that have built sufficient Reliance in a relevant domain.

Poison the Leak becomes available when:

- the target completed at least one containment action
- Exposure remains high enough for recipients to care
- at least one named exploiter has meaningful hidden Reliance
- the target has enough intelligence or military capacity to maintain a consistent deception
- no earlier failed poison attempt has closed the route for the current incident

A strong service can enter the route earlier. A weak service may need to lower Exposure or restore a secure channel first.

## Deception construction

A successful deception combines true information, outdated information, and false information.

The target should not be able to invent any claim without cost or preparation. The false material needs to fit what recipients already believe and what the archive previously showed.

The route can use:

- changed deployments that make old files look current
- false orders placed into known compromised channels
- controlled code traffic
- misleading mobilization schedules
- dummy logistical priorities
- false diplomatic instructions
- a false contact chain
- real movements that support a false larger plan

Historical deception informs the design principle that the lie gains value from recipient expectations and feedback. The event does not reproduce a named historical operation.

## Deception profiles

### False land plan

The target creates an apparent offensive axis, reserve concentration, or defensive weakness.

The preparation can require:

- relevant army experience
- command power
- temporary planning disruption
- several divisions tied to a misleading concentration
- control of a valid rear-area or border state group

A reliant land exploiter can later:

- misplace reserves
- prepare against the wrong axis
- overestimate a weak front
- suffer a temporary planning penalty when the real operation begins

The outcome should trigger only when the exploiter has a relevant enemy relationship or military plan.

### False naval disposition

The target creates a misleading fleet base, patrol pattern, convoy route, or invasion preparation.

The preparation can require:

- navy experience
- fuel
- a fleet or capital-ship group committed to a named port
- temporary reduction in useful naval activity

A reliant naval exploiter can later:

- search the wrong sea region
- misallocate patrol or strike forces
- prepare against a false naval invasion
- expose a convoy-raiding plan

The target pays a real opportunity cost by keeping ships in the false posture.

### False air priority

The target creates a false basing plan, bombing priority, reinforcement route, or reserve-aircraft picture.

The preparation can require:

- air experience
- fuel
- aircraft based in a misleading region
- temporary mission inefficiency

A reliant air exploiter can later:

- concentrate aircraft in the wrong region
- misread the target's bombing priorities
- waste a temporary planning advantage
- expose an intended interception or strike plan

### False mobilization schedule

The target feeds a false call-up, training, reinforcement, or rail-priority schedule.

The preparation can require:

- command power
- trains or temporary transport strain
- a delayed real reinforcement cycle
- military experience

A reliant rival can later:

- attack too early or too late
- misjudge reserve arrival
- prepare against a reinforcement route that does not exist
- overcommit to a border posture

The deception should not directly force war. It changes the quality of a valid foreign plan.

### False diplomatic intention

The target creates a false negotiating limit, alignment plan, guarantee policy, or faction intention.

The preparation can require:

- a secure liaison channel
- diplomatic effort
- a public posture that supports the false private material
- acceptance of short-term opinion uncertainty

A reliant government can later:

- make a poor demand
- misread the target's faction direction
- expose its own coalition preparation
- lose bargaining leverage

This profile should never reveal or overwrite a hidden focus route.

### False industrial shortage

The target exaggerates or conceals a fuel, resource, transport, or production problem.

The preparation can require:

- temporary production inefficiency
- civilian factory commitment
- altered trade or stockpile behavior
- a real visible measure that supports the false estimate

A reliant government can later:

- target the wrong shortage
- invest in the wrong countermeasure
- misjudge the target's ability to sustain war
- apply an embargo or strategic pressure package less effectively when another stable system owns that action

### False contact chain

The target inserts controlled identities, dead drops, or communication patterns into the compromised personnel files.

The preparation requires a strong intelligence service or a restored secure channel.

A reliant hostile service can later:

- waste an operation on a false network
- expose one of its own assets
- lose network strength
- reveal which part of the archive it trusted

This is the highest-risk deception profile because failure can endanger real personnel.

## Recipient feedback

The target should gain feedback only through actual observation or intelligence success.

Useful feedback includes:

- a foreign redeployment
- a changed naval posture
- a burst of hostile network activity
- a private ally report
- intercepted planning language
- a failed foreign operation
- an exposed attempt to verify the archive

Feedback can reveal that one government has high Reliance. It should not reveal every hidden score.

A successful Poison the Leak route can convert one or more named exploiters into confirmed reliance targets. This gives the player a clear sense that the deception found an audience.

## Poison outcomes

### Full success

A full success requires a coherent deception, sufficient foreign Reliance, and a recipient action in the matching domain.

It can produce:

- a strong temporary planning or operational penalty for the recipient
- exposure of one hostile plan or network
- lower hostile Reliance in future Event 52 incidents for that government against the same target
- a major Exposure reduction because the archive's credibility collapses
- achievement progress

A full success should affect a limited number of governments. The player earns broader results by building several reliant targets before the Deception Window closes.

### Partial success

A partial success affects only some recipients or produces a smaller mistake.

The target gains information about who relied on the archive, but the false plan does not fully change the foreign operation.

Exposure still falls because contradictions reduce confidence.

### Failure

A failure can occur when the target lacks control, the false posture is inconsistent, recipients verify the material independently, or a hostile service detects the insertion.

Possible failure effects include:

- Exposure rises or its decline pauses
- one named exploiter gains confidence
- the target suffers its own temporary planning disruption
- the false contact chain endangers a real network
- the current poison route closes
- a second deception attempt becomes more expensive or unavailable

Failure should not erase the target's ordinary damage-control options.

## Seed Contradictory Orders

This is a lower-commitment countermeasure available before a full Poison the Leak operation.

The target places several incompatible but plausible orders into known leak channels. The aim is to reduce Archive Confidence and make foreign governments spend time authenticating the files.

It uses command power, support equipment, or a small military-experience cost based on the active domain.

Success:

- lowers foreign confidence
- reduces named-exploitation cadence
- slightly accelerates Exposure decline
- can reveal which recipients seek verification

Failure:

- confirms that the original archive was genuine
- raises Reliance for one strong exploiter
- delays access to Poison the Leak

The action does not create the large domain-specific mistakes of a full deception.

## Stage a False Deployment

This action begins a concrete Deception Window mission.

It asks the player to support the false archive with real visible behavior. The chosen variant must name or highlight the relevant state, port, sea area, or air region.

The player commits meaningful forces or fuel for the mission duration. The target can cancel, but cancellation counts as failure unless the recipient has already acted.

The selected false deployment should replace the action with its active mission. The category should not show several unused deception variants at once.

## Trace the Source

Trace the Source is a practical investigation, not a route to a final answer.

The target studies copy differences, access records, courier movements, cipher use, archive markings, diplomatic traffic, and foreign receipt patterns.

The action uses intelligence capacity, civilian administrative effort, and time. A country without agency mechanics can still conduct the investigation through security services, military staff, and diplomatic channels.

The investigation returns one route class:

### Insider exfiltration

Evidence points to an internal office or staff member with broad access. The exact person or motive remains uncertain.

The target gains better future protection against internal archive duplication and can improve compartmentation.

### Diplomatic duplication

Evidence points to embassy, attaché, conference, or liaison copies that spread beyond their intended recipients.

The target can rebuild liaison security and gains better diplomatic-domain recovery.

### Cryptologic break

Evidence suggests foreign reconstruction of code systems or authentication practices.

The target gains a stronger code-replacement effect and future cryptologic resilience.

### Courier chain

Evidence points to repeated physical transfers through couriers, rail stations, ports, or air routes.

The target gains stronger protection for personnel and document movement.

### Archival duplication

Evidence points to a large copied archive assembled over time from several offices.

The target gains stronger compartmentation but learns little about the final distribution.

### Several unrelated conduits

Evidence shows that different parts of the archive came from different sources.

The target learns why no single purge or arrest can solve the crisis. Recovery becomes broader but the investigation avoids a false conclusion.

### Indeterminate

Copies conflict too much to establish a practical route.

The target receives no route-specific benefit but avoids a harmful false purge.

The route class can alter later action strength and recurrence flavour. It never names a permanent culprit or closes the event's mystery.

## Rare incident profiles

Rare profiles should change play, not merely change text.

### Blank Archive

The target discovers that many original files are missing from its own offices while foreign governments hold complete-looking copies.

Conditions:

- baseline or Deep Files
- target has meaningful administrative or intelligence capacity
- no prior Blank Archive profile in the current sequence

Effects:

- higher uncertainty over active domains
- slower investigation
- weaker immediate compartmentation effect
- stronger need for friendly samples and copy comparison
- faster foreign confidence growth if their copies agree

The target can still neutralize the archive by changing real plans and systems.

### Ally's Copy

A friendly government privately provides a sample and warns the target before exploiting it.

Conditions:

- at least one valid ally or high-relation government
- that government is not a current hostile exploiter
- a secure diplomatic route exists

Effects:

- reveals one active domain
- identifies one circulation route or named exploiter
- reduces initial Exposure slightly
- unlocks or improves Restore Trusted Liaison Channels
- records the ally as a cooperative recipient

The ally does not surrender every copy held by its institutions.

### Enemy Curator

One hostile strong service has organized, indexed, and authenticated much of the archive.

Conditions:

- valid hostile or rival government with strong intelligence capacity
- relevant archive domain
- enough named-exploiter capacity

Effects:

- that government begins with high Reliance
- it can share curated sections with aligned enemies
- its exploitation cooldown is shorter
- the target can gain a larger result from deceiving it
- successful neutralization damages the curator's confidence sharply

The curator does not become a permanent event antagonist.

### False Originals

Some files contain contradictions that predate any target response.

Conditions:

- baseline or Deep Files
- archive confidence is not already minimal

Effects:

- lower initial foreign confidence
- easier Seed Contradictory Orders
- greater uncertainty over which domains are authentic
- lower raw named-exploitation strength
- a higher chance that one foreign government wastes effort on a false section

The target cannot assume that the entire archive is fake.

### Mirror Leak

The target receives fragments of foreign exploitation plans derived from its own stolen archive.

Conditions:

- at least two named exploiters
- target has a capable service or cooperative ally

Effects:

- reveals one or two named exploiters
- shows their likely domain
- improves Rewrite Compromised Plans or Poison the Leak against them
- creates a risk that the fragments are themselves deceptive

### Operational Diary

The archive includes chronological records of current operations, meetings, dispatches, and changes in posture.

Conditions:

- target has active operations or is at war
- personnel or military domain available

Effects:

- high immediate Personnel Risk
- stronger early exploitation
- faster natural obsolescence after the target changes plans and ends exposed operations
- a sharper reward for decisive first actions

### Dead Drop Rain

Physical copies appear through many embassies, ministries, military headquarters, courier offices, and diplomatic bags.

Conditions:

- wide valid recipient pool
- archive not limited to a single cryptologic source

Effects:

- more passive recipients
- lower average copy quality
- slower elimination of distribution uncertainty
- fewer exceptionally strong exploiters unless an Enemy Curator also appears
- presentation focused on repeated physical discoveries across unrelated institutions

## Variant combinations

Most incidents use zero or one rare profile.

A small number of compatible combinations are valid:

- Ally's Copy with Enemy Curator
- False Originals with Dead Drop Rain
- Mirror Leak with Operational Diary
- Blank Archive with Ally's Copy

Incompatible combinations should not appear together when they create contradictory premises. Blank Archive and False Originals can combine only when the target lacks originals and receives conflicting foreign samples, which should be a deliberately rare compound profile.

Rare profiles should never hide the core Exposure and recovery loop.

## Investigation and recurrence

A route-class finding can create one bounded lasting reform when the target completes the matching recovery action.

It should not make the country permanently immune to the same route. A repeat leak can exploit a different office, code system, courier chain, or foreign copy.

The final closing report should preserve uncertainty. The target may know how part of the archive escaped without knowing who assembled the global distribution or why copies appeared in so many places at once.
