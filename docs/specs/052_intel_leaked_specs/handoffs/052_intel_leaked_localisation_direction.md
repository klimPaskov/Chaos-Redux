# Event 052 Localisation Direction

This file gives writing direction. It does not provide final localisation.

## General voice

Use clear in-world language from the viewpoint of governments, military commands, intelligence services, diplomats, and exposed personnel.

The event should sound urgent and uncertain. The text should explain visible facts and choices without presenting a technical ledger.

Avoid:

- update-history wording
- developer terms
- raw variables or score names beyond the public Exposure label
- generic claims that the world will never be the same
- contrast formulas that pair officials with witnesses or one claim with its denial
- fake mystery built from sealed-report clichés
- long reward lists
- hidden evolution or variant spoilers
- final certainty about the source
- em dashes and semicolons

## Event name

Use `Intel Leaked` consistently for the catalog and Event Details identity unless the implementation establishes a coordinated final title change.

Popup titles can vary by target, foreign recipient, exploitation domain, recovery result, and evolution tranche.

## Target opening

Viewpoint: compromised government.

Include:

- classified material is already abroad
- several domains are confirmed
- distribution and completeness are uncertain
- current plans and personnel may be endangered
- the archive will lose value as systems change

Tone: controlled alarm and urgent professional response.

The option should acknowledge the emergency and move the player into the decision category.

## Foreign opening

Viewpoint: recipient government.

Include:

- a large archive from the target is circulating
- copies may differ
- military and civilian services are authenticating useful sections
- the target has begun changing procedures

Relationship variants:

- enemy: restrained opportunism
- ally: concern, caution, and possible warning
- neutral: verification and bargaining interest
- strong service: confidence balanced by authenticity concerns

## Exposure label and tooltip

Exposure is the only persistent custom value shown to the player.

The tooltip should answer:

- what the value means
- why the current stage matters
- what is keeping it high
- what actions can reduce it
- what threshold comes next

Use the stage names Critical, Severe, Significant, Fading, and Neutralized unless final localisation finds clearer established terms with the same meaning.

Do not show a full numeric component breakdown.

## Decision category header

Use natural short lines.

Include:

- current Exposure and stage
- active archive domains
- one urgent status, such as personnel danger, military plan danger, or deception opportunity

Do not use table-like separators, repeated pipes, or raw telemetry.

## Action text

Each action needs:

- a short final title
- a description of what the government does
- visible cost and sacrifice
- likely public consequence
- concise blocked reason

Do not reveal hidden recipient Reliance values or exact outcome rolls.

The player should understand that recalls lose access, new codes cause command friction, replanning costs readiness, channel shutdown creates blindness, and false deployment ties down real forces.

## Missions

Mission text should identify the objective, deadline, relevant domain, and failure risk.

Archive Freshness should explain that foreign information is aging but remains dangerous while current.

Personnel at Risk should identify the broad endangered group or operation without exposing hidden identities unnecessarily.

Emergency Replan should name or highlight the relevant front, port, fleet, air region, or mobilization problem.

Deception Window should explain which posture must be maintained and which broad foreign behavior the target is waiting to observe.

## Foreign exploitation reports

Use concrete observed behavior.

Direction examples:

- reserves move toward an exposed axis
- patrols appear along a known route
- aircraft concentrate in a region implied by the files
- negotiators use knowledge of private limits
- a network goes silent after cover details circulate

Do not state abstract score changes as narrative.

## Deception reports

A successful report shows the recipient's mistake through a concrete action.

A partial success shows mixed verification or limited commitment.

A failure shows contradictions discovered, false traffic rejected, or a target posture that failed to support the planted material.

Do not announce raw success chance.

## Trace the Source

The result can identify insider exfiltration, diplomatic duplication, cryptologic break, courier chain, archival duplication, several conduits, or an indeterminate route.

Every result should preserve uncertainty about who assembled the final global distribution and why copies appeared so widely.

## Evolution text

Deep Files should communicate that the archive has moved from estimates and old records into live plans, people, shortages, and operations.

Total Compromise should communicate simultaneous exposure and the danger of major powers knowing too much about one another.

Evolution text should describe the changed incident. It should not list raw Chaos thresholds inside ordinary event prose unless the Event Details evolution surface expects that information.

## Closing text

Use distinct result directions:

- controlled recovery
- expensive recovery
- passive expiry
- severe personnel loss
- successful deception
- unresolved investigation

The closing report should name lasting visible consequences and avoid claiming that every copy disappeared.

## Achievement text

Write concise descriptions that state the public challenge without revealing internal proof fields.

Working labels can be retained only when they fit the final tone and UI.
