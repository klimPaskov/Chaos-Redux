# Event 050 research notes

## Purpose

This note separates external research observations from Event 50 design inferences. The sources support general mechanisms such as resource leverage, neutral trade, enforcement, commercial evasion, and domestic political reaction. They do not dictate exact HOI4 numbers.

Research was retrieved on 2026-09-01.

## Historical and analytical observations

### Gradual restrictions can become a strategic resource crisis

The United States moved from gradual restrictions on militarily useful exports to a full embargo and asset freeze against Japan in 1941. The Office of the Historian describes serious shortages and the Japanese leadership's conclusion that it needed to act swiftly.

Design use:

- Pressure should scale with access to militarily important resources.
- Critical dependence can make Resource Seizure or wider war more attractive.
- Diplomatic settlement can remain possible even while military escalation risk rises.

Source:

- U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian, “Japan, China, the United States and the Road to Pearl Harbor, 1937-41”
- https://history.state.gov/milestones/1937-1945/pearl-harbor

### Economic warfare used more than a formal embargo declaration

The British Ministry of Economic Warfare combined blockade administration with source controls, pre-emptive purchases, war trade agreements with neutral countries, import rationing, certificates of origin, intelligence, and blacklists.

Design use:

- Coalition roles should include resource suppliers, shipping states, commercial centers, and neutrals.
- Evolution I enforcement actions can use certificates, rationing, pre-emptive purchases, and blacklists as historical inspiration.
- A coalition needs administrative and commercial costs, not free universal enforcement.

Source:

- U.S. National Archives, “RG 84: Great Britain,” discussion of the British Ministry of Economic Warfare
- https://www.archives.gov/research/holocaust/finding-aid/civilian/rg-84-great-britian.html

### Sanctions and settlement terms can be linked to access guarantees

A 1941 U.S. proposal linked Japanese withdrawal from French Indochina with assurances of access to food and raw materials. Trade cessation followed when expansion continued.

Design use:

- Diplomatic Concessions should connect the public reason to a practical settlement.
- A settlement can exchange withdrawal, restraint, access, or guarantees for restored trade.
- The target should know the public commitment before accepting.

Source:

- Foreign Relations of the United States, 1931-1941, Volume II, Document 237
- https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1931-41v02/d237

### External suppliers can decide whether a resource sanction works

An Oxford working paper on the 1935 sanctions against Italy concludes that coal restrictions could be bypassed if Germany continued supply, while an oil embargo depended on the attitude of the United States.

Design use:

- Coalition quality matters more than raw member count.
- A key supplier remaining outside the coalition can keep Pressure porous.
- The event should weight the actual importance of participating countries.

Source:

- Cristiano Andrea Ristuccia, “1935 sanctions against Italy: Would coal and crude oil have made a difference?” Oxford Economic and Social History Working Papers, 1997
- https://www.economics.ox.ac.uk/publication/1168701/ora-hyrax

### Third-country capacity shapes evasion

A research project on sanctions evasion found that third-country alignment, economic reliance on the target, commercial interest, and capacity in finance, shipping, logistics, and professional services affect participation in evasion.

Design use:

- Neutral Intermediaries need route and commercial capacity.
- Smuggling hosts should not be selected uniformly.
- Evolution I should target sectors and routes that materially enable evasion.
- Commercial actors can create pressure inside governments and coalitions.

Source:

- Liam O'Shea, Olivia Allison, Gonzalo Saiz, and Alexia Anna Hack, “Illuminating the Role of Third-Country Jurisdictions in Sanctions Evasion and Avoidance,” SOC ACE Research Paper 21, 2023
- https://www.socace-research.org.uk/publications/socace-rp21-third-country-jurisdictions-sea

### Some regimes can use sanctions as a legitimacy strategy

Research by Julia Grauvogel and Christian von Soest found that sanctions can strengthen authoritarian rule when a regime successfully incorporates external pressure into its legitimacy claims, especially when ties to sanctioning states are limited.

Design use:

- Defy the World can produce real political cohesion for some regimes.
- The benefit should depend on legitimacy, stability, ideology, and economic links.
- Defiance requires later exhaustion and cannot be a universal authoritarian bonus.

Source:

- Julia Grauvogel and Christian von Soest, “Claims to legitimacy count: Why sanctions fail to instigate democratisation in authoritarian regimes,” European Journal of Political Research, Volume 53, Issue 4, 2014
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-journal-of-political-research/article/abs/claims-to-legitimacy-count-why-sanctions-fail-to-instigate-democratisation-in-authoritarian-regimes/D343D30F3439A36F1BEC73329C491B7F

## Design conclusions derived from the research

1. Pressure must value practical enforcement capacity, not country count alone.
2. Resource dependence should change damage and strategy without adding a second public meter.
3. Neutral states should be active participants with profit, capacity, and exposure.
4. Secondary sanctions should pressure shipping, finance, certificates, re-export, and market access.
5. The target should be able to offer concrete concessions tied to the stated reason.
6. Severe isolation can encourage military expansion when a critical resource cannot be replaced.
7. Defiance can support regime cohesion only under suitable domestic conditions.
8. Enforcement and evasion should impose costs on the coalition and neutral states.

## Limits of the research

- The historical examples involve distinct institutions, wars, and legal settings.
- Modern evasion research informs actor capacity and route logic, not exact 1936 country behavior.
- The sources do not provide HOI4 balance values.
- Exact implementation must follow available engine effects, the local wiki snapshot, vanilla documentation, and live repository precedents.
