# Event 050: The Great Embargo

## Part 5: Evolutions and world economic fragmentation

All labels are working design labels. Evolution names are accepted catalog labels, while final descriptions and option wording require implementation-stage localisation work.

## Evolution principles

Event 50 has two persistent global evolutions.

An evolution changes the event's future rules. It does not replace normal crisis progression and does not add Chaos merely for becoming available or active.

When an evolution is already active before a new firing, the opening crisis begins with its expanded rules. When an evolution becomes eligible while a crisis is active, the change should arrive through normal evolution pacing. A base timing near ninety days is suitable, with dynamic acceleration from severe active conditions.

Evolution content must respect its enable state. A disabled evolution cannot set its recorded flag, unlock its decisions, apply its pressure rules, or become a hidden prerequisite for baseline resolution.

## Evolution I: Secondary Sanctions

- Chaos requirement: `400+`

### Core change

The coalition can pressure countries that continue trading with the target. A neutral state must weigh access to coalition markets against the profits and strategic value of serving the embargoed country.

Secondary sanctions expand the crisis from a direct target-coalition relationship into a contest over intermediaries, shipping, finance, certificates, and re-export routes.

### Activation before a firing

When Evolution I is already active:

- opening coalitions include more core enforcers and compliance partners
- important neutral routes are identified from the first day
- pressured neutrals receive a clear risk state
- Smuggling Networks gain higher potential relief and higher exposure risk
- Neutral Intermediaries demand a larger price for participation
- the coalition can begin with one prepared secondary-sanction action
- opening Pressure tends toward the upper part of its normal range when key neutrals comply

This should not guarantee a near-total embargo. Strong neutral powers, target allies, and profitable routes can still keep the coalition porous.

### Activation during an active crisis

When world Chaos reaches the requirement and the evolution is enabled, active crises become candidates for paced escalation.

The evolution should become faster when:

- current Pressure is at least sixty
- at least one intermediary is active
- smuggling has been exposed
- a powerful neutral is trading with the target
- several core enforcers remain committed
- the target has openly chosen defiance

It should become slower when:

- the coalition is already collapsing
- no meaningful neutral route exists
- the target is close to a negotiated settlement
- the convenor is invalid or defeated

On activation, the event records one shared evolution log entry with a relevant actor when the milestone belongs to a specific target or convenor. The first active crisis to receive the evolved behavior should not cause repeated global evolution logs for every other crisis.

## Secondary-sanction target selection

The coalition should target only countries that materially enable evasion.

A valid secondary target can be:

- an active intermediary
- a country hosting a known smuggling route
- a major shipping or financial state moving target goods
- a resource supplier that openly defies the coalition
- a participant that remains publicly committed while secretly trading

A minor country with no meaningful route should not be selected for spectacle.

### Target priority

Priority rises with:

- volume and strategic importance of the route as estimated by scriptable proxies
- shipping and financial capacity
- relevance to a target shortage
- repeated evasion
- public exposure
- coalition ability to punish the country

Priority falls with:

- alliance or faction protection
- military power
- coalition dependence on the neutral
- risk of opening another major war
- lack of evidence
- a route already closing through diplomacy

## Secondary-sanction choices

### Comply and close the route

The neutral ends trade, preserves access to coalition markets, and damages its relationship with the target. Pressure rises according to the route's importance.

### Limit the trade

The neutral accepts inspections, quotas, certificates, or selected-goods restrictions. The route provides less relief while avoiding the harshest coalition response.

### Continue openly

The neutral defies the coalition. It keeps commercial benefit and may strengthen relations with the target. It receives direct economic and diplomatic pressure from important coalition members.

### Continue secretly

The neutral conceals the route through front firms, transshipment, false certificates, or indirect finance. The route remains valuable while exposure risk rises.

### Demand protection from the target

The neutral asks for guarantees, military access, escorts, equipment, or political support before continuing. The target must decide whether the route justifies the obligation.

### Join the coalition

The neutral converts from intermediary to participant. This can produce a large Pressure increase when the route was strategically important.

## Enforcement actions

Core enforcers can use a small action family against selected secondary targets.

Useful actions include:

- threaten loss of coalition market access
- place firms on a blacklist
- tighten cargo certification
- buy strategic goods before they reach the target
- ration imports to limit re-export
- inspect shipping and insurance arrangements
- offer compensation for compliance
- guarantee the neutral against target retaliation

These actions need costs and limits. A coalition should not punish every neutral for free. Enforcement can consume political attention, civilian capacity, shipping, intelligence effort, or relations.

## Secondary-sanction consequences

Evolution I should create conflict inside the coalition.

### Hardliner pressure

Hardliners gain influence when exposed evasion repeatedly undermines the embargo. They demand stronger action and resist negotiated settlement.

### Commercial resistance

Participants that profit from secret trade can oppose enforcement. Public exposure can force them to choose between coalition standing and continued profit.

### Neutral coordination

Several pressured neutrals can coordinate limited resistance, share routes, or demand common terms. This remains a temporary response inside the event and does not create a permanent faction.

### Target leverage

The target can threaten to expose secret coalition traders, reward loyal intermediaries, or redirect contracts. These actions can lower Pressure and increase diplomatic memory.

## Evolution I balance

Secondary sanctions should make evasion more dangerous, not remove it as a viable strategy.

A well-chosen smuggling or intermediary route can still succeed. The target must invest more, protect the host, manage exposure, and accept that a failed route can strengthen the coalition.

The coalition must pay for broad enforcement. A highly aggressive secondary-sanction campaign can create neutral resentment, commercial losses, and member defections.

## Evolution II: The Embargoes Spread

- Chaos requirement: `800+`

### Core change

One firing can create several simultaneous embargo crises. The player may be targeted alongside major powers, or several majors can receive distinct crises.

Each target has its own:

- target scope
- convenor
- coalition membership
- Pressure value
- duration
- justification
- dependence profile
- decisions and missions
- settlement state
- evolution exposure
- resolution and cleanup

The coalitions can overlap. They do not need to share a target, convenor, justification, Pressure, or outcome.

## Concurrent crisis limits

The system should support two or three targets from one evolved firing. The global active-crisis cap should begin at three.

The cap protects readability, performance, and AI quality. A later repeat cannot create more crises while the global cap is full.

Target selection must remain distinct. The same country cannot receive two Event 50 ledgers at once.

At most one player target should normally be selected in one firing. A separate multiplayer setting can permit wider player targeting when desired.

## Activation before a firing

When Evolution II is already active, a new firing should:

1. build the first target through normal target selection
2. select one or two additional distinct valid targets
3. build a separate coalition for each target
4. prevent one country from receiving duplicate opening popups when it has roles in several coalitions
5. summarize overlapping obligations through compact participant reports

The number of targets should depend on available valid countries, current active-crisis count, performance limits, and world state. Two targets should be common. Three should represent a strongly fragmented high-chaos economy.

## Activation during existing crises

When Evolution II becomes eligible during one active embargo:

- the evolution waits for its own pacing
- one or two additional targets can be selected if the global cap permits
- the original crisis keeps its Pressure and coalition
- the new crises begin with fresh opening values
- the evolution log records the global milestone once
- participating countries receive grouped reports where possible

The original target should not automatically become the leader of all embargoed countries.

## World trade fragmentation

Several overlapping embargoes make trade harder even for countries that are not direct targets.

The implementation can maintain a hidden global fragmentation total derived from active crisis count, average Pressure, secondary-sanction reach, and overlap among major trading powers.

This hidden total can affect:

- cost and reliability of neutral intermediation
- convoy and shipping friction
- willingness to join new embargoes
- commercial fatigue inside coalitions
- availability of replacement partners
- world reports and AI strategy

The total must not become a second public meter. Non-targets can receive a qualitative economic-fragmentation modifier or occasional report when the impact is material.

## Overlapping membership

A country can be:

- a core enforcer against one target
- neutral toward a second target
- an intermediary for a third target

These roles remain separate.

A country should receive a grouped decision or report when several crises demand contradictory behavior. It can choose a broad commercial doctrine, then apply it across the relevant ledgers with target-specific exceptions.

Examples include:

- enforce all embargoes
- follow faction policy
- trade with allies and isolate rivals
- remain commercially neutral
- exploit every available intermediary role

The broad doctrine is an AI and presentation aid. It does not merge the ledgers.

## Embargoed-country cooperation

### Working label: Sanction-Breaker Network

Embargoed countries can cooperate through a temporary economic network. It is not a formal faction and does not create permanent alliance obligations.

Possible cooperation includes:

- bilateral barter
- pooled convoy protection
- shared neutral intermediaries
- clearing arrangements that avoid coalition finance
- exchange of surplus strategic resources
- shared smuggling intelligence
- joint pressure on a neutral corridor
- emergency production licences where supported

### Entry conditions

A target can join when:

- at least one other active Event 50 target exists
- relations or common interests make cooperation plausible
- the countries are not at war with each other
- a route exists or can be negotiated

### Network benefits

The network can lower effective economic damage, open new target responses, and reduce dependence on coalition markets.

Benefits should depend on complementary resources and routes. Two oil-poor states cannot solve oil dependence by trading the same shortage.

### Network risks

- one member can expose the network during settlement
- unequal partners can create dependency
- a coalition can coordinate secondary sanctions
- route loss can affect several targets at once
- military rivalry can collapse the network

### End state

The network dissolves when fewer than two eligible embargoed states remain. Individual trade or diplomatic agreements can persist when they have a separate valid basis.

It does not survive as a permanent faction by default.

## Cross-crisis settlements

Evolution II allows several settlement patterns.

### Independent settlements

Each target reaches terms with its own coalition. This is the normal outcome.

### Linked concessions

A participant involved in several coalitions offers a coordinated relaxation in exchange for aligned concessions. Each target chooses separately.

### Sanction-breaker settlement

Several targets jointly secure neutral trade or force commercial fatigue high enough that multiple coalitions collapse in close succession.

### Economic camps

At the highest fragmentation, groups of countries can settle into rival commercial spheres for a limited aftermath period. This should remain a modifier and diplomatic-memory outcome. It should not automatically create factions or permanent market systems.

## Chaos milestones

Event-owned Chaos changes should be sparse, one-time, and tied to concrete outcomes.

Recommended design direction:

- a modest positive milestone when a real coalition successfully establishes a broad embargo
- a modest positive milestone when the first secondary sanction is actually imposed
- a modest positive milestone when Evolution II creates an additional target
- a modest positive milestone when the target issues a serious resource ultimatum
- a modest negative milestone when the target collapses coalition unity early
- a modest negative milestone when a broad negotiated lifting succeeds

A starting value near five Chaos for each listed milestone is suitable for implementation testing. Final values require balance review.

The following produce no Event 50-specific Chaos:

- evolution activation
- normal Pressure drift
- ordinary decision clicks
- resource shortages
- coalition reviews with no concrete change
- generic wars, deaths, annexations, puppeting, faction changes, and contamination

Shared systems already own the generic sources.

## Evolution resolution and cleanup

Every ledger must close independently.

Cleanup must remove:

- target crisis marker
- public Pressure
- current stage
- coalition arrays or stored participants
- selected convenor and negotiation targets
- active smuggling and intermediary routes
- secondary-sanction marks owned only by this crisis
- active missions and decision targets
- temporary sanction-breaker membership
- scheduled review state
- crisis-owned modifiers and ideas

Durable investments, treaty concessions, diplomatic memory, and achievement tracking remain only when their own design says they outlive the crisis.

A crisis must close safely when another active crisis still uses some of the same countries. Cleanup for one target cannot remove another target's participant role.

## Evolution acceptance conditions

Evolution I is complete only when secondary targets can be selected, pressured, rewarded, exposed, and cleaned without making evasion useless.

Evolution II is complete only when several isolated ledgers can run at once, overlapping countries can hold different roles, global fragmentation affects play without another meter, and each crisis resolves without leaking state into the others.
