# Event 050: The Great Embargo

## Part 4: Coalition members, neutral states, and international bargaining

All labels are working design labels. Final event and decision wording must be written later.

## Coalition identity

The coalition is an event-owned economic alignment with a narrow purpose. It is not a faction, alliance, permanent organization, or replacement for the Condemnation system.

Each crisis has one target and one participant ledger. A country can belong to different roles in different concurrent crises under Evolution II. Its choice in one crisis does not automatically copy into another.

## Participant roles

### Convenor

The convenor gives the embargo a public center. It normally sends the opening announcement, proposes settlement terms, invites core enforcers, and calls review meetings.

A useful convenor has diplomatic weight, a current grievance, or leadership over an existing bloc. The convenor does not have to be the target's strongest economic partner.

The convenor can become weak, occupied, defeated, or politically unwilling. The coalition should then select a replacement from the surviving core enforcers or move toward collapse.

### Core enforcers

Core enforcers create most practical Pressure. Their importance comes from one or more of these roles:

- major resource supplier
- major industrial power
- faction leader
- nearby land-route controller
- major port or naval power
- commercial or financial center
- principal rival
- guarantor of threatened states

Core enforcers receive recurring choices only when the crisis state changes enough to justify them. They should not receive monthly popup spam.

### Compliance partners

Compliance partners add breadth and close secondary routes. They usually follow a core enforcer, faction leader, ideology, or regional security interest.

Most compliance partners can be processed through compact pooled logic. A country receives a direct choice when it has a strong reason to defect, a large economic interest, or a human controller.

### Pressured neutrals

Pressured neutrals begin outside the coalition. They can remain neutral, act as intermediaries, trade secretly, join the embargo, or accept secondary sanctions.

The pool should prioritize countries that can actually affect the target through geography, resources, shipping, finance, or faction links.

### Hardliners

Hardliners demand stronger enforcement and oppose concessions. They can be core enforcers or influential compliance partners. They gain weight when the target continues the behavior that caused the crisis, threatens members, or exposes smuggling.

### Commercial opportunists

Commercial opportunists profit from scarcity and leakage. They may stay publicly committed while trading through fronts, or remain neutral while charging high prices.

Their behavior can weaken Pressure and raise coalition conflict. Evolution I gives hardliners tools to punish them.

## Opening membership logic

Coalition invitations should reflect the public justification and current world state.

### Strong reasons to join

- rivalry or active hostility toward the target
- faction alignment with the convenor
- ideological opposition
- public Condemnation of the target
- threat from target expansion
- loss from a trade dispute or nationalization
- control of resources or routes central to enforcement
- expectation that the target will concede

### Strong reasons to refuse

- alliance or faction ties with the target
- economic dependence on the target
- active war that makes enforcement costly
- ideological sympathy
- fear of target retaliation
- lack of practical enforcement capacity
- conflict with the convenor
- access to large profits as a neutral intermediary

### Strong reasons for conditional participation

- support for the public goal combined with high trade costs
- desire for compensation or guarantees
- concern that hardliners seek regime change
- willingness to enforce only selected goods
- willingness to remain until one concession is met

Coalition construction should allow mixed motives. A participant can oppose the target's actions and still seek profit or limit enforcement.

## Outside-country choice families

### Remain fully committed

The participant keeps restrictions, accepts the economic cost, and supports coalition enforcement. Core participants can contribute extra Pressure when their commitment is renewed during a critical review.

This choice fits hardliners, threatened neighbors, faction leaders, ideological rivals, and countries with low economic exposure.

### Trade secretly

The participant maintains public membership while allowing covert commerce. It gains economic benefit and lowers effective coalition control. Discovery creates diplomatic and secondary-sanction risk.

The target can support the route through Smuggling Networks. The participant can also initiate secret trade when its own economic interest is strong.

### Leave openly

The participant ends its restrictions and accepts the diplomatic consequences. A core enforcer leaving should produce a visible Pressure drop. A minor partner leaving can encourage a group defection when coalition fatigue is high.

A participant should need a reason to leave, such as changed government, war priorities, concessions, commercial pain, alliance with the target, or loss of confidence in the convenor.

### Demand concessions

The participant states the policy, territorial, commercial, or security price for leaving. The target can accept, negotiate, or refuse.

The demand should connect to the participant and public justification. A distant minor should not demand unrelated territory.

### Profit as an intermediary

A neutral or defecting participant offers a replacement route. It receives economic benefit, leverage over the target, and possible coalition pressure.

The route must use actual resource, shipping, financial, land, or port capacity. The event should not let every neutral country become an equally effective intermediary.

### Pressure neutral states

This action belongs mainly to Evolution I. A core enforcer threatens loss of coalition access, blacklisting, inspection, rationing, or other economic consequences if a neutral keeps trading.

The enforcing country pays diplomatic or administrative cost when the neutral is powerful or commercially important.

## Coalition review rounds

The coalition holds bounded reviews when one of these conditions applies:

- a scheduled review date arrives
- Pressure crosses a major threshold
- a core enforcer leaves
- a major smuggling route is exposed
- the target offers a coalition-wide settlement
- the convenor becomes invalid
- Evolution I or II activates during the crisis
- the crisis reaches its planned end date

A review should produce one coherent outcome report, not a sequence of small popups from every member.

### Review outcomes

- enforcement renewed
- conditional members retained
- one or more members defect
- settlement vote begins
- neutral ultimatum issued
- convenor replaced
- coalition enters terminal fatigue
- hardliners gain control
- coalition fractures into public and covert camps

## Settlement bargaining

### Bilateral exit

The target reaches terms with one important member. That member leaves or reduces enforcement. Pressure falls according to its practical contribution.

### Core-member cascade

A second or third major defection can trigger a review where conditional members leave together. This route supports a coalition-collapse victory without requiring the target to negotiate with every small country.

### Convenor settlement

The target accepts a package supported by the convenor and a required share of core enforcers. The crisis moves to negotiated resolution.

### Hardliner veto

One hardliner can block settlement only when it has enough economic and political weight. A small member should not hold the entire system hostage.

### Separate peace inside the coalition

Under high fatigue, some members can restore trade while hardliners continue restrictions. Pressure falls into the porous band and the crisis can end later through expiry.

## Neutral intermediaries as actors

Neutral intermediaries should feel useful and vulnerable.

### Sources of intermediary strength

- relevant domestic resources
- access to the target by land or sea
- merchant shipping
- financial institutions
- large commercial sector
- treaty access to coalition markets
- ability to issue credible certificates or re-export goods
- faction ties that deter punishment

### Sources of intermediary weakness

- dependence on coalition markets
- weak shipping
- no route to the target
- current occupation or blockade
- very low stability
- fear of target retaliation or coalition invasion
- active war that consumes commercial capacity

### Intermediary progression

A route can move through:

1. offer
2. provisional acceptance
3. first delivery
4. stable route
5. exposure or consolidation
6. closure, defection, or post-crisis contract

Only the stable route should count as a full replacement-supply success.

### Intermediary rewards and costs

The intermediary can gain civilian economic benefit, resource preference, political leverage, improved relations with the target, or future contracts. It risks opinion penalties, coalition market loss, secondary restrictions, convoy loss, intelligence exposure, or diplomatic isolation.

The reward must not create a free repeatable factory loop. Temporary trade benefit and bounded contract outcomes are safer than direct permanent industry grants.

## Human-controlled outside countries

A human participant should receive a meaningful choice when it is selected as a core enforcer, important neutral, or intermediary target.

The event must not force a human country into secret trade or an open coalition exit through AI pulse logic. It can apply a default continuation if the player ignores a timed choice, with the default clearly stated.

In multiplayer, one country's pause or unclosed event should not freeze all crisis reviews indefinitely. Use reasonable response windows and deterministic default outcomes.

## Coalition memory

The crisis can record durable diplomatic memory without carrying the whole ledger into later firings.

Useful memories include:

- target honored settlement terms
- target broke an embargo settlement
- participant defected after receiving concessions
- intermediary remained reliable
- participant exposed secret trade
- target chose resource war
- coalition collapsed through commercial division

These memories can affect opinion, flavor, achievement tracking, and later selection weights. They should be small bounded factors, not permanent guarantees.

## Coalition failure conditions

The coalition should move toward failure when:

- too few core enforcers remain
- the practical enforcement contribution falls below a threshold
- the convenor cannot be replaced
- Pressure stays below twenty across two reviews
- enough participants restore trade openly
- the target secures several durable replacement routes
- the maximum duration arrives without a renewed mandate

It should not fail merely because one minor member leaves.

## Coalition consolidation conditions

The coalition can harden when:

- the target refuses a well-supported settlement
- smuggling is exposed
- the target threatens or attacks a participant
- public Condemnation rises from a valid outside source
- key neutrals join
- secondary sanctions close a major route
- the target prepares resource seizure

Consolidation should carry costs for participants. Enforcement is stronger when they are willing and able to pay those costs.

## Compact diplomacy rule

The event should never require the player to manage every coalition member individually.

The visible target category should show:

- convenor
- two or three most important core enforcers
- selected intermediary or negotiation target
- grouped status of the remaining coalition

Direct country events should go only to:

- human-controlled relevant countries
- convenor
- core enforcers
- selected intermediaries
- a participant whose defection or secret trade will materially change Pressure

Everything else can be represented through review outcomes and grouped coalition movement.
