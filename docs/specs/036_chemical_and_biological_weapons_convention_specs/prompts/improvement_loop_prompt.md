# Improvement-loop prompt

Review the implemented Event 036 after a meaningful tranche has completed.

Use `chaosx_improvement_loop_planner` with no inherited context and provide the full Event 036 spec folder, implementation files, current plans, audit reports, localisation, asset handoffs, workbook status, and known blockers.

Read `chaos-redux-improvement-loop`, `chaos-redux-event-planning`, `chaos-redux-events`, `chaos-redux-decisions-missions`, and relevant CBRN documentation.

Start from the playable promise: governments rewrite the rules of unconventional warfare, the player chooses a national posture, recurring conferences change doctrine, Condemnation shifts without erasing consequences, and Evolution III can open a strictly isolated international Chaos weapon project.

Inspect whether the implementation is shallow, repetitive, disconnected, cluttered, or overbuilt.

Focus on:

- whether each posture changes play
- whether conferences produce distinct choices
- whether opposition is viable
- whether first-use and doctrine evolutions change AI and military planning
- whether Condemnation relief preserves severe consequences
- whether the project is collaborative, readable, equal in selection, and source-isolated
- whether small countries can contribute
- whether decisions remain phased and concise
- whether assets and text communicate the mechanic
- whether achievements reward distinct mastery routes

Do not add content merely to increase size.

Do not invent a new country, focus tree, custom interface, super-event, weapon project, or terminal route without a demonstrated design gap and parent acceptance.

If the accepted design is already deep, connected, replayable, readable, and clean, write a closure handoff with only final validation, localisation, asset, documentation, and workbook tasks.

Record whether the pass recommends implementation, queues a bounded addendum, rejects further expansion, or closes the loop.

Do not spawn a second improvement pass until the first addendum is implemented, promoted, queued with a reason, or rejected with a reason.
