# Evolution I: First Use Becomes Acceptable

## Availability and pacing

Evolution I becomes eligible at `200+` Chaos.

If Event 036 is already active, the base evolution MTTH is 90 days.

The MTTH shortens when:

- several signatories are at war
- members possess chemical or biological delivery capability
- a member has recently suffered a CBRN attack
- Full Ratification is common
- Convention Standing is Established or Dominant
- a baseline first-use debate already failed narrowly

The MTTH lengthens when:

- most members hold Retaliation Reservations
- public opponents are influential
- Convention Standing is Provisional
- few members possess usable payloads or delivery systems
- the evolution is disabled

If Event 036 first fires at `200+` Chaos, Evolution I is queued on an accelerated 7 to 30 day schedule after the opening responses.

It still records its own evolution entry and does not merge silently into the entry event.

Evolution activation gives zero Chaos.

## Playable change

The central question changes from whether unconventional warfare can be prepared to whether it may be initiated.

Members begin proposing explicit first-use articles.

Military staffs request operational authority.

Opponents focus on preserving no-first-use declarations and exposing covert programs.

The player must decide whether the convention’s military benefits justify a public first-use policy.

## New public policy states

Evolution I adds a first-use policy beneath the primary national posture.

The policy is shown as a concise clause in the posture tooltip and does not become a fourth public meter.

The policy states are:

- First Use Authorized for Chemical Weapons
- First Use Authorized for Chemical and Biological Weapons
- Nuclear First Use Reserved for National Survival
- Retaliation Only
- Old Prohibition Preserved

A country can hold only compatible combinations.

Chemical Accession cannot authorize biological first use.

Retaliation Reservation remains retaliation-only until publicly repealed.

Public Rejection preserves the old prohibition unless the covert route ignores it in secret.

## First-use treaty rounds

Evolution I unlocks:

- First-Use Revision
- Missile and Delivery Cooperation Accord
- Biological Mobilization Articles

The first First-Use Revision should normally separate chemical and biological authorization.

A combined clause is valid only when membership is strongly permissive or Chaos is already near Evolution II.

A nuclear first-use clause requires a distinct vote and stronger capability gate.

## AI military behavior

A permissive policy increases AI willingness to prepare and initiate a valid action.

It does not create unconditional use.

AI still requires:

- a valid war or hostile action context
- a valid target
- a physical payload or owner-approved weapon state
- a compatible delivery system
- policy permission
- readiness
- acceptable friendly-force and self-contamination risk
- a strategic reason to use the weapon

Chemical first use should become common among aggressive, desperate, or offensively committed ratifiers.

Biological first use should remain less common because outbreak control, retaliation risk, and civilian consequences are higher.

Nuclear first use should rise moderately and remain tied to military, industrial, logistical, capital-defense, or national-survival conditions.

## Decision families

Evolution I adds decisions to:

- sponsor chemical first-use authorization
- preserve a national reservation
- repeal a reservation
- authorize biological mobilization
- expand delivery research
- publish a no-first-use declaration
- organize opposition to a first-use vote
- demand inspection of a suspected covert program

The category must show only actions relevant to the country’s posture and current agenda.

A country should not see chemical first-use authorization when it has no route to chemical warfare or when the relevant system is disabled.

## Condemnation effect

Countries that ratify the appropriate first-use article use the Evolution I multipliers from the Condemnation contract.

A country that retains a reservation uses retaliation-only treatment.

A country that initiates use without public authorization receives normal or floored Condemnation and a treaty-breach reaction.

The first-use article does not reduce atrocity, cover-up, catastrophic outbreak, populated-capital, thermonuclear, or doomsday sources.

## Research and production effect

Evolution I increases AI and player incentives for:

- chemical payload production
- biological research and stockpile preparation
- rockets and missiles
- aircraft delivery
- protective equipment
- detection and decontamination
- reserve stockpiles

Treaties may grant research bonuses and owner-approved project progress.

They must not create free payloads.

## Opposition play

Public opponents and reservation members can:

- lobby against first-use articles
- publish no-first-use commitments
- coordinate bilateral sanctions
- demand inspections
- expose covert preparation
- offer protection aid without joining offensive articles
- recruit members into a reservation bloc

A successful opposition campaign can force a narrow chemical-only compromise or defeat the vote.

It cannot remove Evolution I from the event after the evolution has been recorded.

## Concrete Chaos outcomes

Evolution I activation gives zero Chaos.

The first successful treaty that authorizes offensive chemical first use adds `+5` Chaos once.

A later biological first-use authorization adds `+5` Chaos once if it was not part of the same ratified treaty.

A failed vote, a retained reservation, or ordinary posture movement adds zero Chaos.

Actual weapon use follows the shared generic Chaos sources only.

## Event and log presentation

The evolution receives one evolution log entry tied to Event 036.

The actor is the current convention coordinator or treaty sponsor when valid.

Event Details explains that first use can become an accepted policy among signatories.

It must not list exact Condemnation multipliers or future Evolution II content.
