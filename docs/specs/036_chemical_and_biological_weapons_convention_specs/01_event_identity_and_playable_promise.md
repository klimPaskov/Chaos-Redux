# Event identity and playable promise

## Catalog identity

Event 036 replaces the old Alien Spacecraft concept completely.

The implemented entry namespace remains `chaosx.nr36.1`.

The event is registered as Minor Fire-Once, minimum Chaos level 1, Diplomacy cluster, High member severity.

The event becomes unavailable after its first firing through the normal fire-once contract.

Its recurring conference subevents remain part of Event 036 and do not create new selectable random events.

## Player experience

The opening should feel like a serious international conference whose delegates have accepted a premise that would have been unthinkable under the old taboo.

The shock comes from the public legitimacy of the debate.

Governments openly discuss battlefield gas, biological laboratories, protective equipment, delivery systems, first-use reservations, retaliation guarantees, and nuclear doctrine as normal treaty subjects.

The player is asked to choose what their country will publicly defend and what it will prepare in private.

The event must preserve room for several coherent strategies:

- embrace the convention and use its military and research network
- accept only chemical warfare while refusing biological normalization
- join for protection and retaliation while preserving no-first-use rules
- reject the convention and build an opposing diplomatic bloc
- reject it publicly while developing an undeclared unconventional capability

No route is a decorative opinion choice.

Each route changes access, exposure, diplomatic behavior, research priorities, and later treaty options.

## Global setup sequence

The entry effect follows this order:

1. Prove that Event 036 has not fired and can use the normal event framework.
2. Set the Event 036 active state and create a new membership generation.
3. Select a valid convention coordinator from ordinary diplomatic countries.
4. Build the eligible country receipt list once.
5. Present the opening response to every eligible human-controlled country.
6. Resolve AI postures from the full posture pool and current campaign state.
7. Build the active member, limited member, reservation member, public opponent, and covert opponent registries.
8. Calculate Convention Standing.
9. Apply the one-time eligible Condemnation normalization.
10. Queue the first treaty agenda after an initial diplomatic interval.
11. Record Event 036 in History once.
12. Publish the event name, actor context, Event Details premise, and opening result.

The coordinator is a durable administrative scope for scheduled Event 036 work.

It does not own the convention politically and does not receive automatic control over other members.

If the coordinator becomes invalid, ownership transfers to another valid member through a bounded replacement effect.

The event continues if no suitable major country exists.

A normal member or the triggering country can coordinate the system.

## Country eligibility

A country is eligible when it:

- uses normal civilian systems
- can hold ordinary diplomatic relations
- is not an invalid system carrier or nonhuman actor
- has a valid country scope and government
- is not already represented through another country’s duplicate response receipt

Capitulation alone does not erase a government’s diplomatic posture if the country remains a valid government-in-exile or active country.

A special Chaos actor is excluded by default.

Its owner may opt in only through an explicit compatibility trigger that proves the actor can use national research, production, diplomacy, public Condemnation, and treaty decisions without broken civilian assumptions.

## Multiplayer behavior

Every eligible human country receives its own posture choice.

One player cannot choose for another player.

The convention, standing, agendas, evolutions, and project progress are shared global state.

National posture, contribution receipts, inspection exposure, treaty reservations, and achievement tracking are country state.

The opening must not produce duplicate global setup when several players receive the response event on the same day.

One global setup proof controls initialization, while separate country receipts control responses.

A human country that is temporarily unavailable during the response window receives a safe response when it next becomes valid.

The fallback posture is Retaliation Reservation, because it preserves participation without silently authorizing offensive first use.

## Convention Standing

Convention Standing is derived from current eligible membership, member composition, active opposition, and recent treaty outcomes.

It is shown as a qualitative state:

| Standing | Meaning | Main gameplay effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Provisional | A small network has signed, but the convention lacks broad authority | Slower conferences, weaker diplomatic pressure, limited cooperation |
| Divided | Members and opponents are both influential | Competitive votes, frequent reservations, strong bilateral disagreement |
| Established | A durable international majority or a strong major-power coalition supports the convention | Normal conference cadence, full treaty cooperation, stronger pressure on holdouts |
| Dominant | Most diplomatically significant eligible states participate | Faster permissive agendas, weak member-on-member sanctions, strong pressure to accede |

Standing is recalculated only after events that can change it, such as accession, withdrawal, expulsion, collapse of a ratification round, or convention reconstruction.

It is not recalculated through a daily whole-world pass.

## Early pacing

The first treaty round should begin after enough time for the opening choice to matter.

The initial interval uses a dynamic target between 90 and 180 days.

The interval shortens when the world is already at war, several members possess CBRN capabilities, or the convention enters with Established or Dominant standing.

It lengthens when membership is Provisional, the world is at peace, or most signatories filed reservations.

The first round should usually concern protection, production rules, laboratory registration, inspection, or retaliation guarantees.

A first-use charter belongs to Evolution I.

## Failure and continuation

The convention does not disappear because the first support count is low.

A weak opening creates Provisional standing and a smaller conference network.

Public opponents can attempt to keep it weak.

Members can recruit new signatories.

A later round may establish broader standing.

This preserves the fire-once event while allowing meaningful failure, recovery, and opposition play.

## Tone direction

The opening text should use the viewpoint of governments, diplomats, military planners, medical authorities, and scientists who are being asked to turn forbidden capabilities into regulated policy.

The information visible to the player includes the convention’s public purpose, the available national posture, and the broad diplomatic consequences.

The text must not reveal exact multipliers, hidden voting scores, future project candidates, source-event bypass rules, or later evolution content.

The mood should be controlled and official, with unease created by the normal language used for extraordinary weapons.

Avoid generic crisis correspondence, abstract map descriptions, developer wording, and direct statements that the world has become more chaotic.
