# Territorial routes and sovereignty

## The territorial registry

Use one approved installed-map mapping for the northern objective, Irish homeland, Scottish homeland, Welsh homeland, optional Breton homeland, and the supported Atlantic candidates.
The proposed geographic sets below are semantic requirements, not a fabricated numeric state list.
State 119 appears as Northern Ireland in the inspected existing event.
Its identity and province membership still require verification against the active map roots.
All other numeric IDs remain unresolved in this planning package.

| Set | Intended geography | Required role | Ownership and control policy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NORTH | Northern Ireland | Opening objective | Irish control during capture, Irish ownership and control after peace |
| IRELAND | The island of Ireland as represented by the active states | Imperial homeland and federal Irish member | Ireland owns and controls all required states |
| SCOTLAND | The supported Scottish homeland state set | Empire formation or essential federal member | Empire requires Irish ownership and control, federation requires Scottish ownership and control |
| WALES | The supported Welsh homeland state set | Essential federal member and optional imperial relationship | Federation requires Welsh ownership and control, imperial relationship uses its specific treaty |
| BRITTANY | A valid Breton homeland state set | Optional associated or full partner | Breton ownership and control for membership |
| ICELAND | Supported Icelandic state group | Optional Atlantic access or acquisition | Treaty-specific access or lawful ownership, never cultural core by default |
| FAROES | A separately represented Faroese state group | Optional Atlantic access or acquisition | Same treaty-specific policy |
| AZORES | Supported Azorean state group | Optional Atlantic access or acquisition | Same treaty-specific policy |

Man and Cornwall are optional only when the installed setup supports them as meaningful separate territorial or political units.
If the relevant geography is part of a larger state, this package does not require splitting that state or annexing the larger region.
The baseline empire and federation must remain complete without these optional units.
A map-changing mod that removes an essential Scottish or Welsh state identity needs a compatibility mapping or a clear unsupported-map result.
Do not silently approximate it with unrelated states.

## Four distinct results

A claim permits the normal game's political or military route toward a target.
It is not ownership.
Occupation during war is not legal integration.
A treaty for port use or military access does not transfer sovereignty.
A core is granted only through a specific qualified integration process or retained from the pre-existing game state.
All four meanings must be consistent across focuses, decisions, map pieces, and tooltips.

## Gaelic Empire formation

The formable's base territory is all of IRELAND and SCOTLAND.
Ireland must own and control the complete required sets.
Occupation by an Irish ally or ownership by an Irish subject does not satisfy this formation rule.
A Scottish dominion is a viable preparatory relationship but is not secretly treated as annexed territory.
A negotiated union can transfer sovereignty only after explicit agreement and the normal transfer checks.

Require the three Gaelic milestones, the imperial strategic commitment, U08's mature Irish settlement, completed Scottish initial administration, Settlement at least 75, External Strain below 50, and a valid surviving Irish government.
The Irish-Scottish charter must be completed.
The northern reclamation lifecycle must be successful and closed.
The formation action costs 100 Political Power and takes no additional hidden territorial step.
It atomically rechecks the conditions, pays once, applies the identity once, and opens imperial content.

Formation grants a cosmetic country identity, its flag family, staged national-idea changes within the three-family budget, explicit future claim decisions, and imperial administration content.
It does not replace the government, recruit a ruler, annex subjects, end unrelated wars, or award blanket cores.
A later ideological change receives a compatible name and flag variant or an approved common design that remains legible.
Do not force a monarchic title on a republic.

## Scottish core integration

Scottish initial administration is a 180-day project.
It permits the imperial charter but does not immediately grant cores.
Core integration requires a further 365 continuous days of Irish ownership and control after the initial administration, Settlement at least 75, External Strain below 50, fulfilled regional guarantees, and a completed paid integration contract.
The target group is finite and approved.
A direct-rule path that has broken an autonomy promise must first revise or restore that promise through the specific political settlement.

The contract costs 150 Political Power and one civilian factory commitment for 180 days inside that longer qualifying period.
Both time and completed investment are required.
Loss of a targeted state pauses paid work and resets the continuous-control qualification for that state group.
Successful completion adds only the approved Scottish cores and records a permanent once-only result.
It does not run a whole-world loop or convert every owned Celtic-looking state into a core.

These core rules are a game-design choice for the fictional imperial union, not a claim that all Scottish communities are Gaelic or historically entitled to Irish rule.
The writing must preserve that distinction.

## Wales and Brittany under an empire

The baseline imperial route can seek an allied government, a client agreement, or an ordinary territorial campaign.
Direct acquisition uses normal occupation and administration.
This package does not grant automatic Irish cores to Wales or Brittany.
A mature client relationship can provide useful access, accepted procurement, and limited defense contributions without annexation.
A human government can reject the relationship.
Its refusal does not unlock a zero-cost transfer option.

If an imperial government promised independence while supporting a liberation, honoring that promise produces the appropriate sovereign country package.
Breaking it is an explicit choice with Settlement loss where it contradicts the Irish charter, External Strain, diplomatic damage, and ordinary occupation consequences.
It cannot be a hidden annexation buried in a reward block.

## Federal formation

The initial Celtic league requires Ireland and one eligible accepted sovereign partner.
The full federation requires Ireland, Scotland, and Wales, each owning and controlling its own approved homeland.
All three must accept the self-government, mutual-defense, and financing charters and remain valid throughout the 90-day constitution mission.
Ireland needs Settlement at least 75 and External Strain below 50.
Essential partners cannot be occupied puppets of a hostile or uninvolved state.
An agreed internal autonomy model must be representable and explicitly accepted, but the default is sovereign members.

The final formation costs Ireland 100 Political Power.
Each partner pays its accepted charter commitment through its own action.
No silent deduction is made from a human member's resources.
The result is a federal bloc with common institutions, a distinct emblem, procurement and defense content, and an Irish coordinating role.
Countries retain their identities and control.
There is no Irish core grant on Scottish or Welsh territory in this route.
The GUI qualifies each state by the corresponding member, not by ROOT ownership of every piece.

Brittany can become an associated or full additional member once it meets its own territory and charter conditions.
A membership withdrawal suspends the institutions that depended on it.
Loss of an essential member can demote the federation to a league, with no reverse annexation or erasure of other countries.
Reconstitution requires renewed conditions but never replays the once-only formation rewards or achievements.

## Atlantic territory and access

Every offer records the current counterpart, target state group, access type, duration where supported, accepted obligations, and renewal or termination rule.
A treaty with an owner that loses the port is suspended until its territorial authority is restored or a valid successor accepts it.
Military access must not be described as a permanent Irish naval base if its actual engine effect offers less control.

A sovereignty transfer through an accepted agreement changes ownership only of the approved target.
An access arrangement leaves ownership and cores unchanged.
A failed offer may lead to another candidate or a longer-range Irish operational program.
The latter needs stronger aircraft and fleet capacity and does not satisfy an achievement specifically requiring a foreign-base agreement.

No Atlantic island receives an Irish core merely because it is strategically useful.
Foreign administration creates External Strain until the associated project is completed.
A lawful release or relinquishment ends the specific obligation and its associated contribution.
