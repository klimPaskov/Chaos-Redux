# The reclamation war

## Before the firing

Resolve Ireland as the existing Irish state using the project's current country classification and identity rules.
The supported baseline is IRE.
A cosmetic name change alone must not invalidate it.
A different tag that claims an Irish identity requires an explicit repository-approved mapping and cannot be guessed from a name or flag.
Ireland needs an operating government, an owned and controlled staging location on the island, a usable land approach to the northern objective, and legal ability to enter the war.
A capitulated exile with no staging territory is not a candidate.
A subject that cannot declare war is not made independent as an incidental effect of this event.

The state registry resolves Northern Ireland.
The inspected existing event uses state 119.
That is a repository-observed reference, not proof of compatibility with an arbitrary installed map.
Every required land province in that state is part of the objective.
No offshore or British mainland state is silently added.

Do not fire if Ireland already controls the objective, even if it does not own it.
Do not fire if Ireland is already fighting the resolved holder.
That existing war cannot safely be relabeled as a new limited war or earn an emergency force for an old conquest.
Ireland may have unrelated enemies only when the tested settlement can preserve those relations.
Countries sharing a faction with Ireland require a valid, explicitly supported route into war.
The event does not secretly expel a human country from its faction to manufacture eligibility.

A separately partitioned southern Ireland is a special availability case.
For this event's use of reunification to be accurate, Ireland must own the non-northern home-island state set at launch.
Temporary occupation of an Irish-owned southern state in an unrelated war does not add that state to the reclamation objective.
This is an explicit interpretation of the user's reunification premise.
It avoids claiming that adding the North reunites an island whose South belongs to another sovereign state.
The exact southern set is installed-map data and must be resolved with the northern set.

## Owner, controller, and opponent

When the legal owner controls the North, that country is the primary opponent.
When an occupier controls it, three distinct roles must be recorded: legal owner, current controller, and the country that can legally conclude the northern transfer.
The implementation must prove that the result can be reached without transferring an uninvolved owner's territory or disrupting its independent war.
A negotiated participation or settlement consent can make such a case valid.
It may not grant Ireland the new tree before Ireland actually enters and wins the reclamation attempt.

If the owner or controller disappears between selection and commitment, selection is retried without paying assets or consuming the event.
After commitment, a holder change is evaluated against the recorded war context.
A successor that inherits the event's war and northern title may replace the target after validation.
A wholly unrelated third party does not become a free annexation target.
Its intervention can interrupt the campaign and produce an unresolved objective, with clear status and an eventual failure outcome.

## The commitment transaction

Before any effects, record the event-instance identity, Ireland, northern objective, owner, controller, primary opponent, relevant subjects and war leaders, pre-existing war relations, pre-war ownership, and opening tier.
Set the once-only grant and lifecycle state in a transaction that cannot be executed again from a second popup, save reload, multiplayer acknowledgement, or delayed callback.

Validate that the declaration is legal and that the required settlement adapter supports this exact relation pattern.
Prepare the force entitlement and valid deployment provinces.
Only then consume the fire-once selection and commit the army and declaration.
The opening army and declaration belong to the same successful firing.
A failed declaration must not leave a country with the force but without the attempt.
Implementation must establish an engine-safe ordering and compensating cleanup, not rely on a fictional atomic Clausewitz transaction.

## The battlefield objective

The initial mission lasts 180 days.
Its visible requirements are full Irish control of the North, continued Irish government survival, and five continuous days holding the complete objective.
Control means the required land provinces, not a single victory point.
Irish allies occupying the state do not count as Ireland controlling it.
A valid transfer of control to Ireland can satisfy the requirement, but allied contribution cannot bypass the war's recorded provenance.

Use a single bounded active-Ireland lifecycle evaluation with the finite objective province set.
The five-day condition must be checked at the cadence actually needed to prove continuous control.
This is an event-owned active-country timer, not a new whole-world daily scan.
Loss of any required province resets the timer to zero.
A completed timer immediately starts settlement resolution.
The player does not buy peace with Political Power or complete another focus.

At day 150, an Ireland still in the recorded war can authorize one 90-day extension when it controls part of the North, has an uncapitulated government, and can support the continuing front.
The extension costs 50 Political Power and 250 support equipment, committed once, and requires at least 5,000 available manpower.
It does not grant more free equipment or change the victory test.
The maximum attempted duration is 270 days.
The manpower requirement is an availability check, not a payment or a second manpower grant.

At expiry, failure is recorded if the objective is not secured.
The event seeks the same safe limited-war exit without a northern transfer.
If a valid isolated exit is no longer possible, failure still prevents the tree reward and emergency support ends, while ordinary hostilities remain until an ordinary peace is possible.
The report must distinguish a failed campaign from an engine-confirmed peace.
A timer cannot claim peace while the countries remain at war.

## Successful settlement

Freeze a settlement attempt only after the five-day objective proof.
Recheck Ireland, target, war relationships, state owner/controller, and objective control immediately before effects.
End only event-introduced hostilities using the proven adapter.
Transfer the northern state through the required legal owner and control sequence.
Recheck the resulting owner and controller and confirm that event hostilities have ended.
Then commit the success marker and load the focus tree.
If the adapter leaves a country in the reclamation war or fails the transfer, the event remains in settlement resolution and does not award success.

Ireland retains the North.
Temporary occupation of territory outside the objective is unwound according to the event settlement and the original legal ownership.
Pre-existing occupation in another war must not be restored from an obsolete snapshot.
A snapshot is evidence for classification, not permission to rewrite every state to its launch-day status.
Ownership of British overseas territories, other occupied states, or a third country's land is untouched.

A 365-day event-specific non-reclamation interval follows successful settlement.
Irish focus rewards cannot immediately issue a new event-scripted attack on the defeated holder.
Preparations, cultural development, Scottish diplomacy, and Atlantic projects remain usable.
At its end, an imperial route may begin a separately justified later campaign under ordinary war rules.
The opening limited settlement is never reused as a free peace button in these later wars.

## The holder's game

The human holder receives a factual report that Ireland has begun a limited northern campaign and that complete objective loss for five days permits the defined settlement.
It can defend the North, prioritize evacuation, or mobilize support there using its normal assets.
An event-owned evacuation decision may withdraw an earmarked northern reserve if a supported unit-selection method can do so without teleporting or deleting unrelated troops.
Otherwise that option is omitted and ordinary manual movement remains available.

Defensive choices can improve local readiness through paid logistics, stockpiles, or a temporary state effect.
They do not secretly scale Ireland's next grant upward.
Choosing to accept the eventual settlement early is allowed only after the same capture requirement has been met.
It accelerates presentation and normalizes relations modestly, without changing the victory requirement or guaranteeing later friendship.

## Failure and later losses

Ireland's capitulation, its disappearance, final expiry, an ordinary peace without the objective, or a definitive interruption that makes the recorded reclamation impossible ends the attempt as failure.
A valid ordinary peace reached during the attempt may count as success only if it fulfills the same northern ownership and control result after documented participation and objective proof.
Simply buying or receiving the state through an unrelated event is not victory credit.

Failure clears temporary war AI, support, missions, and event targets while preserving a permanent failure marker and the consumed initial firing.
Surviving real divisions and stockpiles remain.
The previous focus tree remains.
No forced civil war, punitive ideology change, invented national collapse, or automatic army deletion is added.

After earned reunification, losing the North does not remove the focus tree.
Northern-specific construction pauses if Ireland lacks the location.
Imperial and federal completion checks fail while their territorial conditions fail.
A recovery mission uses the ordinary military situation and cannot spawn another reclamation package.
