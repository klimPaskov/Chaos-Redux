# Ireland Reclaims the North

Ireland receives a real army and launches a war for Northern Ireland.
Taking and securing the North ends the event's reclamation war and earns a large new national focus tree.
An unsuccessful attempt leaves Ireland with the war's ordinary consequences and its previous tree.
The opening victory is the campaign's only entrance.

The later campaign asks what the reunited state intends to become.
It can build an inclusive national settlement, impose a centralized state, develop Gaelic institutions, establish a Gaelic Empire, negotiate a Celtic federation, or make Atlantic trade and naval power its main concern.
Domestic choices affect the cost and credibility of these ambitions.
A government that has refused meaningful regional rights at home cannot obtain the best federal settlement abroad without changing its policy.
An imperial government that opens several fronts must support them with industry, shipping, and a permanent army.

All branch, action, achievement, doctrine, and asset labels in this package are working design labels unless they reproduce the user's requested event or formable name.
They identify content and are not finished localisation.
Numerical values are proposed starting balance, not measured live-game results.

## The opening campaign

The Irish government, its present ideology, and its existing military remain in place.
The event does not force an ideology change, a leader replacement, or an Irish civil war.
Once the dispatcher commits a valid firing, the emergency force is delivered and Ireland enters the reclamation war.
The opening report explains the objective, granted assets, limited settlement, and the focus-tree reward before its acknowledgement closes.
Acknowledgement is not a second decision about whether the event happens.

The target is the valid holder of Northern Ireland, not a permanently assumed British tag.
A country that merely occupies the state during a separate war needs a settlement that also respects the legal owner.
The campaign must not solve that situation by stealing land from an unrelated country or ending the occupier's other wars.
A preflight that cannot establish a safe settlement must decline that candidate before consuming the fire-once event.
Ordinary Britain, including Britain leading a faction and fighting elsewhere, is a required supported case.

Ireland must hold every relevant land province of the northern objective for five continuous days.
Belfast alone is insufficient.
If control is lost, the hold timer resets.
Ireland then receives ownership of the objective through the settlement, keeps it, and leaves hostilities introduced by this event.
The defeated holder cannot force an invasion of Great Britain by rejecting a completed objective settlement.
Its choices affect its conduct during the war and its later relations with Ireland.

## What victory earns

The new tree loads exactly once after the settlement succeeds and ownership and control have been rechecked.
Existing equipment, military production, research, advisors, completed technologies, laws, and earned focus rewards remain.
The implementation must handle an in-progress old focus explicitly and never pay a completed focus reward twice.
The transition contract is in the handoff specification.

The first available focus groups offer northern reconstruction, constitutional settlement, and conversion of the emergency army into a permanent force.
These groups can progress in parallel.
The player does not spend several years completing token prerequisites before reaching meaningful decisions.
The first constitutional choice changes the way northern cooperation is obtained.
The first industrial choice determines whether Belfast initially prioritizes civilian repairs, armaments, or maritime capacity.
The first military choice decides how much of the emergency force the country can support as a standing establishment.

Surviving opening units remain.
They lose temporary emergency support and must be supplied and reinforced normally.
The tree provides training, production, doctrine, and recruitment capacity for future formations.
It does not replace a military campaign with recurring free armies.

## The national choices

Northern Settlement represents the working relationship between the reunited government and the North.
It concerns functioning institutions, accepted guarantees, reconstruction, and stable civil administration.
It does not measure an ethnic population becoming Irish.
External Strain represents the burden of foreign commitments, overseas garrisons, expeditionary support, and simultaneous expansion.
These are the only two public custom numerical values.

The domestic settlement offers regional self-government, an equal-citizenship unitary state, or concentrated central rule.
All have usable economic and military content.
Central rule provides quicker access to administrative manpower and expansion, but makes voluntary federal partners harder to obtain and increases the cost of maintaining northern cooperation.
Reconciliation takes investment and time, then lowers the cost of stable domestic administration and credible partnerships.
A later costly constitutional revision permits a player to change course without collecting both settlement payoffs.

Gaelic revival is available across these constitutional choices.
Its common sequence builds language teaching, cultural institutions, and a public civic identity.
A coercive language administration and a pluralist cultural settlement have different domestic consequences.
Neither path grants overseas cores merely because a language or symbol is culturally connected to Ireland.

## Three strategic destinations

The Gaelic Empire is a territorial and institutional formable.
It requires a reunited Ireland, secured Scottish territories, mature Gaelic institutions, a completed imperial charter, and workable domestic and external conditions.
The proclamation changes identity and opens imperial administration and military content.
It does not instantly core every claim.
Scottish integration remains a costly political process, and wider Celtic regions follow their own rules.

The Celtic route establishes sovereign partners first.
Scotland and Wales can become the essential partners in a later binding federal compact.
Brittany is an additional partner where the map and campaign make it possible.
The federation is a multinational bloc with common defense and institutions.
Its members retain their country tags, national governments, territory, and player control.
This is a deliberate federative alternative to Irish annexation.
A faction rename alone does not satisfy its formation requirements.

The Atlantic route develops ports, a merchant fleet, naval aviation, escorts, submarines, and diplomatic access.
Its advanced operations can seek island bases and overseas partnerships.
North American links offer trade, investment, volunteers, and negotiated support.
They do not create automatic Irish claims on Canada or the United States.
A primary Atlantic strategy has a full endgame without invading Great Britain.
Empire and federation governments can also use the shared naval and aviation branches.

## Evolution and replay

Evolution I increases the opening army and the later capacity for professional mobilization.
Evolution II adds deeper Gaelic and Celtic interactions and regional military options.
Evolution III expands the strongest imperial, federal, and Atlantic commitments.
All three strategic destinations and a complete military, industrial, political, and cultural campaign remain playable at baseline.
Higher tiers add scope, alternatives, and capacity.
They never remove the need to win the opening war.

The active tier is recorded explicitly.
The initial firing may use the highest enabled tier whose threshold is already satisfied.
Later upgrades use the project's timed Evolution system.
They do not happen immediately on crossing a Chaos threshold.
An upgrade during the opening war grants only the additional entitlement for the new tier.
An upgrade after reunification expands the tree's later opportunities and does not issue a second emergency army.

## A campaign with consequences

A lost reclamation war permanently closes this event's focus-tree reward.
A later ordinary conquest of Northern Ireland does not reopen the event.
A country that earned the tree and later loses the North keeps the earned tree, but loses access to territorial capstones until the necessary conditions are restored.
The new tree then contains recovery priorities appropriate to the chosen government.

Foreign expansion can produce resistance, diplomatic refusals, expensive garrisons, strained industry, and membership disputes.
The player has ways to consolidate and reduce commitments.
An empire is not required to annex every possible target.
A federation can refuse a member that will make it unmanageable.
An Atlantic power can obtain a base by treaty and retain the same strategic utility without annexation.
