# The Gaelic Revival

The Gaelic lane turns cultural development into institutions that matter to the campaign.
Its historical inspiration includes language teaching, voluntary organizations, publishing, public events, and wider national debate.
The National Library of Ireland's account of the Gaelic League supports a cultural opening that is not automatically tied to one religion or to an imperial state.
The campaign's empire and federal outcomes remain fictional design choices.
See research/072_research_notes.md for the source boundary.

## G01: Language education

Fund teacher preparation, adult instruction, and material production.
This is the first discrete Gaelic milestone.
The choice is between a voluntary national program with stronger northern compatibility and a faster civil-service language requirement with a larger early administrative burden.
The latter must disclose that trained personnel cannot be created instantly.
Completion unlocks cultural diplomacy and supports a later institutional choice.
It does not require a particular ideology or replace every state's name automatically.

## G02: Cultural institutions

Develop libraries, archives, broadcasting, performance, and language publishing as a coherent cultural program.
The player chooses whether local bodies or a central national institution administer the first funding round.
Local administration improves the path toward voluntary partner agreements.
Central administration provides faster national coordination and supports the imperial charter's institutional requirement.
Both can eventually meet the same second milestone through their own completed missions.

A concrete reward is a one-time research or production support opportunity appropriate to communications and public institutions, plus a diplomatic action unlocked by the new capability.
Avoid stacking a separate spirit for each institution.
The resulting cultural policy is held in the Settlement and State family.

## G03: Civic identity and northern participation

The government decides how Gaelic revival applies in the North.
An inclusive policy protects existing language and religious institutions while offering common national participation.
A compulsory administrative policy prioritizes uniform state practice and accepts slower northern cooperation.
Its effects operate through Settlement and administrative missions.
They do not rank ethnic groups or remove population through an abstract assimilation reward.

The inclusive choice helps qualify the federal charter.
The compulsory choice can support faster imperial administration, but cannot receive the inclusive policy's voluntary partnership discount.
A government that changes course must complete the constitutional revision process and replace the relevant benefit.

## G04: Historical military identity

Reorganize symbols, ceremonial distinctions, recruitment narratives, and formation traditions within the modern army.
The practical choice links to M04 professional infantry or M05 reserves.
A professional tradition rewards trained units and improved officer schooling.
A territorial tradition makes the reserve system more useful and connects recruitment to local institutions.

Use ordinary infantry, artillery, motorized, marine, and support systems.
Gaelic imagery does not justify supernatural soldiers, medieval weapons outperforming modern equipment, or a new unsupported battalion type.
The visual identity may distinguish route-specific uniforms or insignia through approved existing model capability, but no custom 3D asset is required for mechanical completion.

## G05: Cultural relationships abroad

Open separate offers to relevant Scottish, Welsh, and Breton institutions or governments.
A Scottish relationship can include Gaelic-language links where historically and politically appropriate.
Welsh and Breton offers concern their own languages and institutions and broader Celtic cooperation.
The offers cost political or civilian investment and need acceptance.
They provide documented cultural milestones or later diplomatic access, not automatic sovereignty changes.

At baseline, Ireland can pursue one active cultural offer at a time.
Evolution II allows two separate active offers with adequate capacity.
These are concurrency limits, not a new public meter.
A refused offer has a cooldown and may be revisited after a changed diplomatic circumstance.
Repeated payment without changed conditions cannot force success.

## G06: National charter

Complete the third Gaelic milestone by defining the revived state's public institutions and international direction.
An imperial draft, a cooperative charter, or a culturally assertive Atlantic republic can draw on the same prior work.
The future strategic commitment determines which final obligations apply.
The charter does not independently switch governments or create an emperor.

The imperial route can retain the country's existing constitutional form while declaring an imperial union.
A monarchy is not required by the user and should not be invented solely to provide a crowned portrait.
Where existing campaign politics support a monarchy, its ruler must be verified and its institution must fit the actual state.

## G07: Mature cultural policy

A mature policy requires all three milestones and a functioning northern settlement.
It improves the selected domestic family and opens a sustained route-specific benefit.
The empire receives a better administrative preparation option.
The federal route gains access to a funded common cultural convention.
The primary Atlantic route gains an additional diaspora investment relationship.
These are distinct investments and actions, not three simultaneously granted capstone rewards.

Evolution II adds a more developed external convention.
Evolution III adds institutions suited to a much larger member or territorial network.
Neither removes the costs of member consent, northern stability, education, or administration.
The cultural lane remains complete without those additions.
