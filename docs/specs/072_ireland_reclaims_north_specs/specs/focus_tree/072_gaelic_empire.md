# The Gaelic Empire

The imperial route begins as a deliberate political project after Ireland has earned reunification.
It uses Gaelic institutions to support a new state identity and pursues territorial authority around the Irish Sea.
Its ambition exceeds its historical cultural justification.
The writing should allow the government to make expansive claims while the mechanics distinguish a claim, an occupation, a negotiated union, and a core.
The route is playable through planning, force, administration, and consolidation.

## E01: Imperial commitment

Require the first two Gaelic milestones, a chosen northern constitution, and Settlement of at least 50.
Committing excludes the binding federal charter and the primary Atlantic capstone.
The government receives an imperial planning decision and a concrete military-industrial preparation choice.
It does not receive the formable name, Scottish cores, or a declaration of war.
The player sees the later Scottish territorial requirement and the remaining Gaelic milestone before committing.

A centralized northern policy can reach this preparation earlier.
An autonomous or civic-unitary government can also pursue the empire, but must define how Scottish institutions will fit its public commitments.
The later integration cost follows the selected constitutional model.
A peaceful imperial union, when offered and accepted by a valid Scottish state, must explicitly disclose its loss of independent sovereignty.

## E02: The Scottish settlement

Resolve the Scottish homeland from the installed map and existing country data.
If Scotland is independent, offer a negotiated union, an associated dominion, or prepare an ordinary coercive claim.
A human Scottish player controls its response.
A dominion relationship is a preparatory alternative and does not satisfy an Irish-ownership requirement for proclamation.
It can later enter a negotiated union, remain a partner without proclamation, or refuse.

If another country owns Scotland, Ireland may support a legitimate independence route or prepare a separately justified territorial campaign after the reclamation settlement interval.
Supporting independence does not secretly annex the newly released country.
A liberation promise must be honored or broken through an explicit consequential choice.
The latter adds External Strain and damages cooperative relations.

## E03: Irish Sea operational preparation

Develop ports, escorts, transport, reconnaissance, and an army suitable for crossing the Irish Sea.
The player chooses a protected landing operation or a longer indirect strategy based on access and diplomatic support.
A focus grants preparation and unlocks an operation decision.
Actual war execution requires valid targets, a legal declaration, and adequate transport and supply.
Do not spawn an invasion force inside Scotland or transfer the whole region merely because a focus completes.

This group uses M08 amphibious preparation, N03 escorts, and A05 maritime reconnaissance.
A powerful existing Irish navy can bypass redundant preparation requirements while retaining the actual territorial challenge.
A failed landing remains an ordinary military setback and does not trigger a second free reclamation army.

## E04: Scottish administration

After lawful acquisition, establish Scottish civil administration through one of two commitments.
A chartered provincial settlement retains defined regional institutions and has a slower initial manpower payoff but lower enduring burden.
Direct imperial administration provides earlier recruitment access and a stronger garrison requirement, with a higher Settlement and Strain cost if imposed after promising autonomy.

The initial administration mission lasts 180 days, requires Irish ownership and control of the targeted region, and uses civilian investment and political capacity.
A longer core-integration mission can follow under the rules in the territorial specification.
A state lost during the mission cannot keep progressing unseen.
Existing Scottish characters and institutions are not indiscriminately deleted.

## E05: Proclaim the Gaelic Empire

Require all Irish and Scottish homeland states in the approved state set to be owned and controlled by Ireland, three Gaelic milestones, completed Irish and Scottish constitutional charters, Settlement of at least 75, External Strain below 50, and no unresolved event reclamation war.
The exact-state puzzle and the formation decision use the same eligibility helper.
The declaration is a player-visible choice after these conditions, not an automatically paid focus reward on mere occupation.

The payoff is the Gaelic Empire cosmetic identity, original flat flag in all required sizes, staged imperial national-idea variants within the three-family budget, an imperial administration decision family, and E06 to E09 content.
Irish technology, government, units, and existing focus completion remain.
The proclamation does not annex a subject, core Wales, or transfer all British colonies.

## E06: The imperial economy

Choose a connected Irish-Scottish production system or a more naval imperial economy.
The connected system develops actual industrial and logistical locations and supports artillery, trucks, and aircraft production.
The naval system invests in dockyards, escorts, merchant capacity, and protected port approaches.
Both need usable owned locations and existing capacity checks.

A project links two real economic locations instead of awarding the same generic factory in several focuses.
The player must decide where civilian work is tied up and whether the immediate military advantage is worth slower northern or Scottish integration.
Completion can reduce the administrative Strain contribution of the developed territory.

## E07: The wider Celtic claim

Wales is a distinct next political question.
The government can negotiate a client relationship, support a friendly government, or seek direct control through an ordinary campaign.
Brittany is an optional external relationship, not a required Gaelic imperial province.
Cornwall or Man can enter the route only where the installed map and country setup represent a valid separate political or territorial unit.
Do not annex a much larger English state solely because it contains one culturally associated area.

Acquisition grants the ordinary ownership result and the explicitly authorized claims.
It does not provide automatic Irish cores.
An empire that promised self-government must choose between honoring that promise and paying the visible consequences of breaking it.
This group can be completed through a managed relationship without requiring a new conquest on every playthrough.

## E08: Atlantic dominions

Evolution II adds a larger preparation option and Evolution III adds a more developed administration sequence.
The baseline route already permits one negotiated or lawfully acquired Atlantic base through the shared maritime opening.
Advanced targets are drawn from a finite supported island set and their current holders.
Every offer distinguishes sovereignty, military access, and port use.
A base treaty does not become state ownership when a later focus completes.

The empire chooses between one strongly developed overseas position and a broader but more expensive network.
The latter adds multiple active commitments and can push External Strain into a range that blocks further expansion.
Consolidation requires real facilities and a supported garrison, not simply waiting for a meter to decay.

## E09: The enduring imperial state

The late imperial capstone requires the proclaimed formable, completed Scottish administration, mature domestic Settlement, and supported commitments.
Its alternatives are a chartered imperial union with stable regional arrangements or a more centralized expeditionary empire.
The first favors lower administration costs and dependable production.
The second favors trained expeditionary capacity and stronger mobilization at the cost of higher continuing commitments.
Replace the relevant family variants instead of stacking both outcomes.

Post-capstone play offers regional administration, naval procurement, and limited expeditionary projects with cooldowns and real costs.
The route never becomes a free annexation menu.
A player can end the campaign with a stable Irish-Scottish empire, a wider client network, or a powerful Atlantic presence without being forced to conquer the entire world.
