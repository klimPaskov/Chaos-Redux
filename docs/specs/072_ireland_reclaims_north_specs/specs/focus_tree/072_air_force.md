# The Irish Air Force

Air power supports the land victory's aftermath, island defense, sea reconnaissance, industry, and overseas operations.
The branch offers a useful defensive path at baseline and a wider maritime or expeditionary path for stronger economies.
It uses standard aircraft and verified designer or base-game alternatives.

## A01: Air establishment

Inventory existing aircraft and airfields, establish training, and choose an initial focus on fighters or reconnaissance and army support.
The first procurement is a paid contract or a modest production opportunity.
It does not provide a large modern air force regardless of date.
Existing aircraft remain and can satisfy practical requirements if they perform the intended role.

## A02: Irish aircraft production

Develop workshops, licensing or procurement, and a domestic production line.
Belfast may receive an aircraft-related industrial specialization where the research and installed industrial setup support it.
Do not invent a historical manufacturer, aircraft model, or designer portrait solely to fill a focus.
The player can choose a domestic development program with a slower payoff or a valid foreign contract with earlier equipment.
A refused foreign contract leaves a complete domestic route.

## A03: Air defense

Develop fighters, radar, airfield capacity, and interception training.
The project targets real Irish-owned states and respects already constructed levels.
The operational test requires suitable aircraft and a functioning supported air-defense network.
A passive defense strategy gains lasting value from this branch without attacking another country.
Radar and anti-air investment is paid and finite.
It is not a blanket maximum-level construction reward across the island.

## A04: Army aviation

Support reconnaissance, close air support, transport where available, and coordination with ground formations.
Choose a stronger tactical-support arm or a smaller flexible service with greater emphasis on observation and logistics.
The first choice costs more aircraft production and fuel.
The second offers a more sustainable option for a smaller army.
Army training missions must not double-count aircraft already committed to a separate overseas requirement when the engine can track that distinction.
Where it cannot, use a conservative capacity reserve and document the proxy.

## A05: Maritime reconnaissance

Develop aircraft, crews, and coastal facilities suited to the Irish Sea or Atlantic.
The operational payoff supports escorts, submarines, and convoy planning through supported modifiers and missions.
The player chooses a coastal network or an extended-range approach.
The second requires more industry and appropriate aircraft range and bases.
An unsupported exact air-region coverage calculation must not appear as a falsely precise percentage in the GUI.

## A06: Naval strike capability

Add a paid anti-shipping program after the basic reconnaissance arm is established.
It needs suitable aircraft and operational training.
The benefit is narrower than a universal air attack bonus and connects to the selected fleet doctrine.
A carrier-based version is available only when the naval branch has actually developed a carrier-capable force.
A land-based maritime arm remains a complete and useful alternative.

## A07: Long-range operations

The primary Atlantic route can invest in longer-range aircraft and forward support.
The empire can support its later overseas commitments.
The federation can organize an accepted shared maritime-air program.
Each version requires actual access and facilities or the documented long-range Irish-base alternative.
Evolution II expands the available mission.
Evolution III increases sustained expedition capacity and its costs.
No group transfers a foreign airfield without a valid agreement or ownership change.

## A08: Integrated air command

Complete a combined defensive, land-support, or maritime exercise according to the chosen air doctrine.
The reward is a selected professional improvement within the Defense Establishment family and a bounded later training opportunity.
An army-heavy Ireland receives a different operational payoff from a maritime Ireland.
The group records the real aircraft and facility requirements and a mission that cannot be completed by an empty paper air force.
Advanced countries bypass redundant research preparation, but still complete the operational commitment.
