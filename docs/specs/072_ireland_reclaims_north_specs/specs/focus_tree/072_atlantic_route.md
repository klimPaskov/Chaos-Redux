# An Atlantic Power

The Atlantic route gives Ireland a complete strategic future independent of conquering Britain.
Its central problems are maritime access, industrial capacity, safe shipping, trained crews, air coverage, and the cost of sustaining distant positions.
The common maritime opening is available to imperial and federal governments.
The primary Atlantic commitment has its own exclusive diplomatic and institutional capstone.

## T01: The maritime economy

Begin with merchant shipping and the economic use of Irish ports.
Choose a civilian shipping priority that helps trade and convoy replacement or a defense-oriented shipping priority that supports escorts and naval construction.
The initial investment grants a concrete convoy production or procurement opportunity and access to port projects.
It does not gift a battle fleet.
The choice links to U06 public finance and N01 naval establishment.

## T02: Belfast and the western ports

Develop Belfast's maritime capacity and one appropriate western Irish port using real installed locations.
Belfast can specialize in construction and repair.
The western port can specialize in Atlantic patrol support, convoy assembly, or air-sea coordination.
The first construction choice is paid and finite.
Later development depends on existing levels and available state slots.

An already developed port receives a maintenance, repair, or specialization reward instead of another impossible building level.
The exact substitute is decided during implementation and stated in the tooltip before the player chooses it.
These locations become operational requirements for advanced Atlantic groups.

## T03: North American relations

Open distinct approaches to the relevant United States and Canadian governments, as they actually exist in the campaign.
Trade, cultural ties, private investment, volunteers, and naval access are separate offers.
No relationship automatically entails joining a faction or a foreign war.
A counterpart can limit cooperation to civilian trade.

The diaspora investment round uses Political Power and an Irish civilian contribution.
On acceptance, it funds a concrete Irish project or a bounded equipment procurement contract.
It does not add unlimited manpower from an undefined overseas population.
If the counterpart is hostile, absent, or unable to invest, Ireland can pursue a slower domestic-financing alternative with a weaker diplomatic payoff but a complete route.

## T04: Atlantic access agreements

Negotiate a finite set of island and port relationships based on actual current holders.
The baseline geographic candidates are Iceland, the Faroes, and the Azores where represented by supported state groups.
These are strategic locations, not Irish cultural claims.
An access treaty, jointly funded base, lease, and sovereignty transfer must be treated as different legal and mechanical outcomes.
Only implement a lease if the installed mechanics can represent its promised ownership and expiration behavior.
Otherwise use explicit access or an ordinary accepted transfer.

A primary Atlantic government needs one mature external base relationship for its first operational circuit.
Refusal leads to another supported candidate or a longer-range sea-air preparation route from Irish ports.
There is no automatic annexation to prevent a focus dead end.

## T05: The Atlantic doctrine

Choose escort-centered sea control or submarine-centered interdiction as the primary operational doctrine.
Escorts need patrol aircraft, destroyers or other verified escort hulls, repair capacity, and convoy protection.
Interdiction needs submarine construction, reconnaissance, fuel, repair, and a realistic ability to reach the selected sea regions.
Both retain basic coastal defense and merchant support.

Doctrine choice changes the priority and discounts of N03 to N06 and A05 to A07.
It does not instantly complete the entire naval doctrine tree.
The player receives a focused research and training program and an operational mission that uses the intended forces.

## T06: Overseas capacity

Create a paid expeditionary and support program with transport, artillery, medical and logistics support, and an explicit home-defense reserve.
A player cannot count the same formation simultaneously as an overseas commitment and the retained home-defense force.
The operational mission requires ordinary unit, convoy, and port capacity through supported country-level checks.
Where a precise division-role check is unsupported, use a documented conservative proxy and explain it in the development record.

An accepted deployment creates External Strain while it remains active.
Returning the force ends that contribution once.
A failed or cut-off expedition can lose equipment through ordinary war mechanics and leaves a specific recovery contract.
The decision does not refund lost ships or recreate destroyed divisions.

## T07: A major Atlantic state

The primary Atlantic capstone requires mature Irish ports, a functioning maritime air arm, a trained navy matching the chosen doctrine, a supported merchant fleet, and a completed external-access or long-range alternative mission.
Use proposed native thresholds of 100 usable convoys, 15 relevant escort or submarine hulls in the selected operational family, and 150 suitable aircraft, subject to installed unit-query validation.
The groups must also verify actual port capacity and a completed 180-day operational program.
Raw hull count alone cannot satisfy a doctrine needing a different fleet.

The payoff is a mature Atlantic Defense Establishment variant, diplomatic recognition news, enhanced procurement options, and continuing overseas missions.
It does not force a cosmetic rename if that would conflict with the existing Irish constitutional identity.
Empire and federation countries can develop large Atlantic forces, but do not collect this exclusive primary-route political reward.

## T08: A wider Atlantic system

Evolution II adds a stronger second diplomatic relationship and more capable procurement.
Evolution III allows a second mature operational circuit and larger expeditionary support with increased commitments.
The player chooses between a tightly defended network and a broader presence that is more costly to supply.
The system uses predeclared states and target relationships.
It cannot invent new runtime state-puzzle pieces for arbitrary provinces.

Post-capstone decisions develop one base at a time, renew access, replace convoy losses through production, and negotiate a new limited commitment.
The route remains viable when the player declines aggressive expansion.
Its strength comes from maintained capacity and accepted relationships.
