# The Celtic Question and federal route

The cooperative route builds its strength through governments that have reasons to work with Ireland.
It recognizes that Scotland, Wales, and Brittany have different politics, languages, resources, and territorial situations.
A shared Celtic framing opens diplomacy but does not substitute for consent or practical support.
The route culminates in a binding multinational federation while preserving each member as a playable country.

## C01: A Celtic congress

After the early cultural and constitutional groups, invite eligible Scottish, Welsh, and Breton representatives.
Where a country does not yet exist, the congress may contact a movement only through a supported independence or exile mechanic.
It does not create a country at peace inside a foreign owner's territory.
A real government can accept, refuse, or request a narrower cultural relationship.

Ireland funds one congress with Political Power and civilian administrative capacity.
Acceptance unlocks the corresponding bilateral path.
Refusal preserves the recipient's independence and creates a cooldown.
The congress cannot be repeated every few days until a random roll yields acceptance.
A relevant change in ideology, threat, sovereignty, or Irish constitutional policy can later justify another offer.

## C02: Scottish independence and partnership

An existing Scotland receives an offer based on trade, security, and its actual position.
It can join a defensive relationship without accepting a future federation.
If Scotland does not exist, Ireland must obtain lawful release through an agreed arrangement with the holder or an ordinary successful war.
A promised independence outcome creates a Scottish government with a viable country package.

The new state is not automatically annexed or made an Irish puppet.
A human Scotland receives explicit control over major subsequent commitments.
Ireland can provide a bounded equipment and economic aid package that costs real Irish resources.
An AI Scotland evaluates its security, relations, sovereignty terms, and practical benefit.

## C03: Welsh partnership

Welsh cooperation emphasizes its own institutions, industry, coastal security, and government.
The structure mirrors the same sovereignty protections as Scotland but has distinct industrial and defense opportunities.
An industrial procurement pact can make Wales valuable even without a large independent army.
A Welsh government that has little manpower should favor shared supply and coastal defense over promises of a large expeditionary force.

The release and aid rules remain the same as C02.
Ireland cannot obtain both Welsh sovereignty and Irish ownership of Welsh territory from the same action.
The treaty screen discloses which government owns the resulting states and whether any subject relationship is part of an accepted agreement.

## C04: Brittany and associated partners

Brittany is an optional partner and does not block the baseline federation.
An existing valid Breton state can negotiate maritime and cultural cooperation.
A release route must respect its holder, current wars, existing tag, and the real state boundaries.
No Breton independence is fabricated in an unrepresented portion of a large foreign state.

Evolution II provides a deeper associated-member path.
Evolution III permits larger shared maritime and expeditionary commitments if the partner has actual capacity.
Cornish or Manx participation is optional only where a supported country or separate state exists.
Otherwise cultural contact can remain flavor and cannot be a hidden territorial gate.

## C05: A Celtic alliance

The initial league requires Ireland and at least one accepted sovereign Celtic partner with territory and a functioning government.
It forms or adapts a faction only through a legal, consent-based transition.
Countries already committed to another faction cannot be pulled out through an unannounced effect.
A human invitee sees the current membership, war obligations, and any required exit from a prior alliance.

The alliance provides diplomatic and defense opportunities.
It does not yet award the federal constitution, common procurement system, or a common military quota.
A league can remain a useful end state when Wales or Scotland cannot be made a valid federal member.
The route still offers industry, military cooperation, and Atlantic development in that campaign.

## C06: The federal constitution

For the full federation, require Ireland, sovereign Scotland, and sovereign Wales to own and control their respective approved homelands and accept the three common charters.
The charters cover mutual defense, national self-government, and common financing and procurement.
Ireland must have Settlement of at least 75, must not retain an incompatible central-rule promise breach, and must keep External Strain below 50.
The members complete a 90-day constitutional mission with their commitments still valid.

The federal formable is a bloc, not an Irish cosmetic annexation of its members.
Its reward includes a distinct faction identity and emblem, funded common institutions, defense coordination, procurement decisions, and member-specific opportunities.
No member loses its country tag, flag, national tree, territory, or human controller.
This makes the federation mechanically different from the empire and more substantial than a renamed alliance.

## C07: Common procurement

Members can contribute different things to a common program.
Scotland may offer maritime and heavy-industrial capacity, Wales industrial production and coastal support, and Ireland finance, equipment, or training.
Those roles follow actual capacity and are not fixed grants based only on nationality.
The beneficiary and each contributor receive an explicit offer showing costs and delivered equipment or production support.

Only accepted contributions count.
An unavailable factory, missing stockpile, or capitulated contributor pauses the affected contract without generating resources.
Do not implement a fictional shared stockpile that cannot be represented safely.
Use documented bilateral transfers, verified production support, or paid equipment delivery with a ledger and clear ownership.

## C08: Joint defense institutions

Fund staff exchanges, exercises, port access, intelligence cooperation where supported, and an agreed expeditionary quota.
A member selects which obligations it accepts.
Operational support uses ordinary expeditionary, volunteer, access, or alliance systems that have been verified for the installed game.
The Irish player does not directly control another human player's army through a silent focus effect.

Evolution I improves officer training and logistics.
Evolution II adds a broader maritime-defense program.
Evolution III adds the permanent joint council and a larger paid expeditionary program.
Refused obligations reduce the actual benefit available to the bloc and do not count as fulfilled capstone requirements.

## C09: Federal disputes and a lasting settlement

The late route tests a real conflict between common defense and national self-government.
An optional shared expedition, a procurement shortfall, or a port-access dispute can bring that conflict forward.
Arbitration costs time and political capacity but preserves the member relationship.
Coercion suspends relevant common benefits, raises External Strain, and can lead to a member's explicit withdrawal.
It does not automatically launch a civil war or annex the dissenter.

A mature federation earns a stronger stable-cooperation family variant after sustained fulfilled commitments and a resolved dispute or equivalent peaceful institutional test.
Members retain their own advantages.
A departure reduces common capacity and may demote the federation to a league if its essential members are no longer present.
A later valid re-entry restores institutions through a renewed contract, not by replaying all formation rewards.
