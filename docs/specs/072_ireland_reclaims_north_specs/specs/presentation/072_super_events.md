# Proclamation super-event research and presentation

Two major route formations can use the shared super-event system in addition to their required news coverage.
One accompanies the Gaelic Empire proclamation and the other accompanies the completed federal constitution.
Reunification itself uses the larger news event and the country report.
Super-events respect the project's existing presentation settings and cannot block the mechanical formation when their presentation is disabled.

## Gaelic Empire presentation

Trigger only after the imperial formable transaction succeeds.
The image should express a new imperial state built around Ireland and Scotland, with its original flag and a sense of public political ceremony.
It must not show unacquired territories as already conquered.
The government, constitutional form, and actual leader determine suitable imagery.
A crown is not a license to invent a historical emperor.

Text research should examine Irish and Scottish cultural and political traditions relevant to sovereignty, power, union, and the cost of ambition.
The final title, quotation or cultural remark, and button text require separate source verification.
Do not fill them with an unresearched song lyric, invented quotation, or a working route label copied from this package.
The text researcher must return provenance, attribution, context, and the permitted excerpt length before final wording is accepted.

Audio research should seek a period-suitable ceremonial or martial arrangement whose composition and recording rights are both documented.
An apparently old tune does not make a recent recording freely redistributable.
Check the existing music catalog for reuse and conflicts.
A new commissioned or clearly licensed recording is acceptable.
No final track is selected in this planning package.

## Federal presentation

Trigger after the full three-member federal constitution and its actual obligations are completed.
The image should show a common institution or ceremony while retaining separate Irish, Scottish, and Welsh identities.
Optional additional members appear only when their actual participation makes the image and text accurate, or use a general composition without named delegations.
The federal emblem belongs to the bloc and does not replace each member's national flag.

Text research should emphasize a negotiated political commitment and its practical responsibilities.
Do not call the result a historic federation that actually existed.
The route is alternate-history fiction.
Use verified cultural references only when their actual context fits the political choice.
Humor can acknowledge the difficulty of common government without mocking the constituent peoples.

Audio should be distinct from the imperial presentation.
A cooperative ceremonial arrangement or an original composition can express several traditions without a crowded medley.
Avoid unlicensed commercial recordings and do not prescribe a famous melody merely because it is familiar.

## Delivery and gating

Each presentation needs a 457 by 328 image, verified text and attribution, a final audio asset with rights record, native-size visual evidence, and the shared super-event integration record.
The preferred excerpt is 60 to 90 seconds and must not exceed 120 seconds under the current project guidance.
Record fades, loudness, file format, exact duration, source, license, and any editing permission according to the current audio workflow.
No final music, quote, slogan, lyric, or researched finished wording is provided here.
Those production fields are explicitly blocked pending source research.

Use separate text and audio research workers during implementation.
They must not overwrite each other's source records or manufacture a final choice to fill an empty field.
The parent confirms correctness and rights, then integrates the presentation.
Each milestone plays at most once per campaign provenance.
Save reload, reconstitution after member return, ideology change, or a duplicate callback cannot replay the formation reward or super-event automatically.
