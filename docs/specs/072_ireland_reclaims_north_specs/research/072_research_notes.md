## Historical anchor H01: Gaelic League

The National Library of Ireland's exhibition account describes the Gaelic League's 1893 foundation and its early language and cultural work.
It identifies an initially nonpolitical and nonsectarian aim, language teaching, publications, and public activities.
This supports a voluntary and institution-based cultural opening in the design.
It does not establish a historical Gaelic Empire or make the fictional imperial route a historical entitlement.
The cultural branch deliberately leaves room for different constitutional choices.

Source, accessed 20 September 2026:
`https://www.nli.ie/1916/exhibition/en/content/stagesetters/culture/hyde-macneill/`

## Historical anchor H02: British-Irish agreements

The Documents on Irish Foreign Policy transcription of the agreement signed on 25 April 1938 records negotiated defense, financial, and trade arrangements, including the transfer of treaty-port property and fortifications.
This is a basis for meaningful post-settlement port, trade, and defense diplomacy.
The event does not claim that its fictional northern war occurred or that every alternate campaign must recreate that historical agreement.
It uses negotiation as a plausible institution, with the actual current holder and government.

Source, accessed 20 September 2026:
`https://www.difp.ie/volume-5/1938/british-irish-tripartite-agreement-on-trade-finance-and-defence/2321/`
