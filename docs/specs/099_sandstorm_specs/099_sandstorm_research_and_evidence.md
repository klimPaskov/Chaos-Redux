## Primary research

### R1: source regions and long-distance effects

[World Meteorological Organization, Sand and dust storms](https://wmo.int/themes/sand-and-dust-storms), updated 16 July 2026.
The page was read in full.
It supports arid source regions, long-range transport and serious disruption to health, transport and infrastructure.
The design inference is to select physical desert regions dynamically and let dust affect territory far from its source.
The user independently requires the enormous moving wall and its worldwide escalation.

### R2: particle behavior, sight and aviation

[World Meteorological Organization, Aviation hazards: Dust Storms and Sand Storms](https://community.wmo.int/site/knowledge-hub/programmes-and-initiatives/aviation/aviation-hazards-dust-storms-and-sand-storms), updated 18 December 2025.
The page was read in full.
It distinguishes longer-lived fine particles from coarse material and describes very poor visibility and aircraft engine or electrical hazards.
This grounds the fine-dust ocean passage and the qualitative aviation consequences.
The table of in-game air penalties is authored balance, and this source does not prove a local HOI4 air API.

### R3: health and exposure uncertainty

[World Health Organization, Sand and dust storms](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/sand-and-dust-storms), dated 23 April 2025.
The page was read in full.
It identifies health risks from airborne mineral particles and gaps in exposure assessment, including the need to study combined heat and particulate effects.
The design therefore labels exposed population as a geographic estimate and its casualty coefficients as fictional tuning.
The source does not support any exact mortality rate or the proposed twenty-five-percent heat interaction.

### R4: observed ocean transport

[NASA Earth Observatory, Here Comes the Saharan Dust](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/here-comes-the-saharan-dust-92358/), 2018 article.
The relevant complete article body was read after the canonical NASA URL resolved an older failed address.
Its account of dust traveling from the Sahara across the Atlantic provides a concrete long-distance transport reference.
It informs the campaign's sea-crossing concept and visual research.
The period player interface still uses ground observations and radio reports.

## Research implications and deliberate fiction

The scientifically grounded layer is windborne mineral dust, long-range fine-particle transport, visibility loss and disruption to exposed people and machines.
The user's high-Chaos self-sustaining network of splitting, returning and merging enormous walls is deliberate alternate-history disaster fiction.
The specification preserves that identity and gives it finite material and time limits for play.

The plan does not treat a normal short convective dust event as proof of a months-long planetary wall.
It does not assign real-world casualty rates to the fictional severity bands.
It does not derive a medical dose-reduction percentage directly from a mask coverage variable.
Its modeling choices are explicit so later balance changes can be evaluated without confusing scientific evidence with tuning.

Primary visual and quotation research continues in the asset and super-event prompts because no final image, quotation or musical recording is selected in this planning package.
Those prompts require source verification before final player wording or runtime art is produced.
The current report contains no unresearched quote presented as ready-to-use localisation.
