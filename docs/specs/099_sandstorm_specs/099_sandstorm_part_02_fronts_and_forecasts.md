# Sandstorm: fronts, origins and forecasts

## Geographic source selection

The source is selected from suitable desert territory through a validated geographic catalog derived from the installed map and the shared desert registry.
The source catalog describes physical desert families, emitting land and connected source areas.
Country ownership is incidental.
The random choice first selects an eligible major source family and then a viable broad opening within that family.
This avoids allowing a desert represented by many small political states to gain many more chances solely because of map subdivision.

The mandatory coverage audit includes the Sahara, Arabian deserts, the Australian interior, the Gobi and Central Asian deserts.
Additional physically suitable families can include the Iranian plateau deserts, the Thar and Taklamakan, southern African deserts and major American arid regions where the installed map supports a broad viable source.
These are coverage candidates, not a closed list of permitted origins.
Every included family must be justified by its actual source terrain and area.
A small isolated dry province cannot qualify merely because its state passes a desert classifier.

Use equal eligible-family weights as the initial balance proposal.
Within a family, select among viable connected openings with weights shaped by available emitting area and room for a broad front.
Document the exact candidate pool and weight inspection before presenting any final probabilities.
The baseline must not exclude a valid family because its owner is a minor country, neutral, defeated or outside the player's continent.

## A broad opening

The opening ribbon should ordinarily be 600–1400 kilometers wide across the direction of travel and 450–900 kilometers deep along it.
These dimensions are balance ranges for a fantastical disaster.
The final eligible profile is selected as a coherent width, depth and speed combination, rather than drawing each dimension independently.
Its connected initial footprint must cover at least 300,000 square kilometers and intersect at least three neighboring state footprints in the supported map.
Six to twelve touched states is a useful presentation target where the state's size makes that sensible.
The physical area and connected wall matter more than achieving an arbitrary state count.
If a supported map cannot satisfy the broad opening in a proposed source family, that family requires a reviewed map-compatible profile before it enters the eligible pool.
The implementation may not add distant disconnected states just to fill a count.

The initial front contains a strong leading band and a broader dusty body.
The entire footprint is visible at entry.
Some states begin under normal conditions, while pockets at the wall can already be severe.
The source region receives a short formation interval where its geography permits one.
The player is never promised several days of warning when the storm has formed overhead.

## The moving ribbon

Each front has a leading wall, body and trailing dust.
Its width is perpendicular to motion.
Its depth runs along the direction of travel.
The leading edge enters new cells as the tail leaves old cells.
The occupied ribbon can curve across several regions and contain different local intensities.

The movement model uses physical distances and adjacency across land and sea.
One update must represent the complete area swept between its starting and ending position.
A fast wall cannot jump over intermediate residents or divisions because only its endpoint was sampled.
Political borders, military access, straits used by armies and naval supremacy do not govern wind movement.
Actual coastlines and physical sea adjacency do.

The baseline travel proposal centers on 120–240 kilometers per day, with coherent rare profiles outside that central band.
A 600-kilometer-deep front moving at 150 kilometers per day occupies a fixed location for about four days on a direct passage.
Wider evolved profiles can remain over a location for eight to twelve days before any stall allowance.
The implementation must choose the depth, minimum sustained speed and finite stall allowance together so the intended residence limit remains credible.

Normal passage targets three to seven days of local exposure.
Deep evolved walls can reach fourteen days in one continuous passage.
Longer exposure requires a separately visible return or a different front that genuinely extends the union footprint.
A hidden local timer cannot retain weather effects after the displayed tail has left.
Conversely, an arbitrary state expiry cannot clear a location while the wall still occupies it.

## Direction and speed changes

The front has directional persistence.
Most updates continue along the current broad course.
Smaller turns are more common than abrupt reversals, and changes are spatially continuous.
Desert source conditions, dry land, terrain transitions and an evolving regional wind field influence the proposed course.
Those factors are steering inputs, not hard routes prescribed by country names.

Normal steering is limited enough that a committed one-day warning remains meaningful.
Later forecast bands can bend as new observations arrive.
A sudden acceleration advances the visible wall and updates the warning corridor at the same time.
It cannot silently move the lethal footprint while leaving yesterday's map image in place.
Slow sections can accumulate a deeper body, but a finite stall allowance and material loss prevent an indefinite regional lock.

Heading, speed and width changes are selected from a complete bounded pool whose timing and weights receive the probability review specified in the execution map.
Reloading, changing map mode or opening the response panel does not redraw them.
Ordinary variation continues at every evolution stage.

## Width, mass and intensity

Material amount and local concentration are different hidden concepts.
Spreading the same material across a wider area generally reduces concentration.
A compressed leading wall can become more intense while its footprint narrows.
Splits divide the parent's available material, and merges concentrate or combine surviving material without creating more.
Fresh source draws are visible desert-emission events governed by a finite seasonal allowance.

Local intensity has four bands.

| Band | Visible and practical character |
| --- | --- |
| Weakening | A thinning dusty veil with disrupted sightlines and continuing mechanical exposure |
| Normal | A broad obscuring body with severe navigation and outdoor-work difficulty |
| Severe | A dense wall, frequent loss of nearby visual reference and dangerous transport conditions |
| Extreme | The darkest concentrated sections, near-total practical visual disorientation and acute civilian danger |

Intensity changes smoothly through neighboring cells or connected sections.
A front may have a weakening flank, a normal body and a severe core simultaneously.
The evolution stage changes the available distribution and persistence of those sections.
It does not replace all local variation with a single global severity number.

## Coastlines, ocean transit and distant landfall

The same front identity passes across a coast into sea cells and can make landfall elsewhere.
Over the sea, coarse particles settle more readily while fine carried dust remains capable of long travel.
This qualitative distinction is grounded in the [primary aviation and particle research](099_sandstorm_research_and_evidence.md#r2-particle-behavior-sight-and-aviation), while the enormous sustained fronts remain the user's disaster fiction.
This distinction supports the storm's visual texture and attenuation without removing the user's worldwide threat.
The extreme high-Chaos behavior is deliberately beyond ordinary observed dust transport.

Sea transit has a visible route, actual occupied cells and an air-operation projection.
The map must show the wall over the water, including the approach to an island or another continent.
Air operations over the affected sea face the appropriate local conditions.
Sea cells do not contain state civilian population or factories to damage.
Port and coastal effects begin only where the actual footprint reaches those facilities.

An ocean crossing cannot be a delay followed by a fresh random destination.
It cannot use military movement links to teleport through a distant strait.
The front's heading, attenuation and remaining material still determine where it goes.

## Overlapping fronts and revisits

All fronts contribute to one aggregate geographic exposure for a given interval.
The strongest local contribution dominates, while a smaller reinforcement from other overlapping fronts can increase the combined band up to extreme.
The starting proposal is strongest local density plus one quarter of the sum of other densities, capped at the extreme limit.
This is a balance composition rule, not a physical aerosol equation.
It preserves the threat of colliding walls while preventing two extreme fronts from applying two complete sets of penalties or two population-loss transactions.

When one front leaves, another remaining front continues to define local conditions.
Clearance occurs only when the aggregate footprint reaches zero.
A later return creates new exposure after a real clear interval.
The history distinguishes a continuous long passage, a second branch and a return by a related lineage.
Front identity changes caused by a merge cannot erase prior exposure or cause it to be charged again.

## Forecast contract

The current occupied area is an observed condition and is displayed accurately within the map's supported geographic resolution.
The next twenty-four hours form a committed approach band.
The twenty-four to seventy-two-hour band shows the likely corridor.
The three to seven-day watch area is broader and visibly uncertain.
Each band is derived from the same front state and steering choices used by the simulation.

Forecast presentation uses period weather observations, ground reports, pilot reports and radio communications.
Modern scientific observations inform this design's research, but the player is not given an unexplained satellite console in a 1930s campaign.
Weather information can be broadly shared across borders without revealing enemy unit strength, stockpiles, private migration records or operational orders.

The committed band is stable unless an explicitly signaled major physical transition, such as a split or merge, changes the forecast.
That transition generates a new warning and preserves enough response time wherever geography allows.
Warnings should not claim exact death totals or guaranteed safe routes beyond the committed horizon.
A locality removed from the likely corridor receives a quiet status update, not a false disaster-completion reward.

## Timing and finite closure

The season receives an immutable absolute finish at its original start plus 360 days.
Every descendant front inherits that bound.
Typical durations are balance targets produced by source material, attenuation and available finite source draws.

| Form reached | Typical global season target | Maximum simultaneously active fronts | Cumulative front-birth admissions available |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | 45–90 days | 1 | 1 |
| The Great Dust Wall | 75–140 days | 1 | 1 |
| Splinter Storms | 100–180 days | 4 | 8 |
| The Returning Storm | 140–240 days | 6 | 16 |
| The World of Dust | 180–300 days | 8 | 24 |

Birth admissions include initial desert starts, every split child and every reseed.
A merge replacement identity does not consume a new birth because it combines existing fronts and material.
Reaching a higher evolution can unlock the unused difference between the old and new caps.
Spent births and source material never reset.
Splitting alone cannot explain a longer season because it conserves material.
Longer evolved duration comes from larger initial material in an evolved opening, reduced attenuation and specifically permitted finite desert draws.

No new material draws, reseeds or split births are admitted at or after day 300.
Any pending creation request expires at that cutoff or its earlier viability deadline.
All surviving fronts are in compulsory attenuation by day 330 and retire by day 360.
Turns, mergers, overlapping coverage and delayed callbacks cannot extend that date.
Earlier natural dissipation is normal.

## Geographic experience acceptance

The player must be able to follow a baseline ribbon from a valid broad desert opening across at least one ordinary border and a coast without a disconnected jump.
An evolved trace must demonstrate widening, narrowing, speed variation, an independent branch, a curved return, desert reseeding and a real spatial merge.
Large desert states, compact states, occupied territory, coastal islands and the map seam all require attention.
The same authoritative footprint must explain visual coverage, civilian exposure and local military and air effects.
These are required implementation demonstrations, not claims that the present planning package has performed them.
