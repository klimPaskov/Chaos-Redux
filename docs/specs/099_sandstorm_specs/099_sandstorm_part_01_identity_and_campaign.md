# Sandstorm: identity and campaign experience

## Design status and reading order

This is the complete design proposal for reworking Event 099 around the user's moving global sandstorm brief.
The supplied identity, four evolution names, thresholds and core behavior are requirements.
Numerical tuning, response actions, achievements and presentation arrangements added here are proposals for implementation and review.
They have not been demonstrated in the game.
Read Parts 01 through 09 in order, then the balance scenarios, evidence register, execution maps and separate production prompts.
All newly coined action, mission, achievement, interface and asset names are working labels used to organize the design.
They are not finished localisation.
The existing user-supplied names Sandstorm, The Great Dust Wall, Splinter Storms, The Returning Storm and The World of Dust retain their exact identities.

| Catalog status | To Be Reworked |
| Entry eligibility | Chaos tier 1, using the current shared tier system |
| Cluster | Natural Disasters, existing cluster 5 |
| Cluster role | High member |
| Geographic owner | One global storm season, containing its moving fronts |
| Initial cause | Wind lifts a vast quantity of material from a dynamically selected suitable major desert |
| Primary experience | Watch a huge wall approach, prepare the threatened region, operate with severely impaired visibility, and recover as the wall moves onward |

Tier 1 is the catalog eligibility band, not a requirement for exactly one point of Chaos.
The current shared bands are 0–199, 200–399, 400–599, 600–799, 800–999 and 1000 or more.
The former Event 099 dependency on Wider Disaster Seasons is superseded by the user's tier-1 brief.
The event keeps its stable identity while acquiring a lifecycle capable of moving through several countries and across the sea.
One global entry receipt spends the fire-once opportunity, regardless of how many countries receive warnings or experience the storm.
Weather continuing, splitting or returning belongs to that same season.

## The four promises

The opening is geographically impressive.
A broad front forms across a substantial desert area, with the initial footprint ordinarily touching several neighboring states and always meeting a physical size requirement.
The first view should explain the source and the wall's present position before asking the player to respond.

The storm travels through continuous geography.
Its position, leading edge, body and tail determine which land, coast and sea areas are affected.
The map makes forward movement, lateral growth, turns and clearing visible.
A warned country may have several days to act, while a country close to the source may have only hours.
Warnings convey genuine uncertainty about later movement without disguising the current front.

Military confusion is the strongest immediate consequence.
Both sides lose the ability to coordinate attacks, identify formations, navigate and use reconnaissance or artillery effectively.
Air operations become unreliable and dangerous.
Preparation can preserve part of a force's organization or equipment, but it cannot restore clear-weather sight through an extreme wall.

Civilian and industrial consequences follow the people and facilities actually exposed in the game's geographic model.
Dense states, heavily used transport routes and large industrial centers can lose far more than empty desert.
Exposure lasts only as long as the physical front remains.
Deaths and repairable physical damage remain after the temporary weather penalties end.

## Campaign arc

### Formation and first warning

The world receives one restrained opening report establishing the desert source and the unusual scale.
Countries with territory in the initial footprint receive immediate impact information.
Countries in the committed approach area receive a local warning linked to the relevant map selection.
The player can inspect the front before making a paid decision.
The opening supplies enough context to understand that the wall may move well beyond its first country.

### Approach

The player compares threatened population, current intensity and the forecast corridor with existing military and industrial priorities.
The meaningful preparation questions are whether to suspend exposed civilian activity, protect machinery, maintain an essential corridor, prepare field formations or preserve an airfield.
Shared mask distribution and migration systems remain available through contextual links when their own rules allow action.
The response panel can apply a chosen policy to a bounded queue of threatened states, so a large country does not require hundreds of repeated clicks.

### Passage

The leading wall arrives, local penalties increase, and prolonged exposure begins accumulating physical consequences.
The player sees the same front that is producing those effects.
Existing wars continue, but reconnaissance, planning, movement and air support become much less dependable.
Sheltered populations still face residual danger.
Working transport corridors preserve supply at the cost of equipment, fuel and greater exposure among people who must travel or work outdoors.

### Clearance and recovery

The tail moves away.
Local visibility and temporary production effects return to normal as aggregate storm coverage reaches zero.
Units recover lost organization through ordinary recovery.
Native repair work addresses actual damaged buildings and railways.
The local report distinguishes weather clearance from completion of repairs and from the end of the global season.
At higher evolution stages, the forecast may show another branch approaching a recently cleared region.

### Dissipation

Surviving fronts lose material, narrow or break into harmless remnants.
The season has one immutable absolute finish bound and cannot renew it through escalation or front creation.
Final closure leaves historical consequences and continuing shared recovery work intact.
The closing report gives the player a geographic account of the journey and routes them to existing Deaths and recovery information.

## Public information budget

The entire mechanic has two persistent public value identities.

| Working label | Meaning | Decisions it informs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local intensity | Weakening, normal, severe or extreme conditions at the selected location or front | Whether movement, combat, flying, outdoor work or urgent response is tolerable |
| Estimated exposed civilians | Approximate current residents within the union of affected land, counted once at the selected geographic scale | Which populated area should receive scarce protection, evacuation capacity or emergency attention |

Estimated exposed civilians is a present geographic estimate.
It is not a sum of daily exposures, a forecast death count, a census of people outdoors or a claimed shelter headcount.
Evacuation changes the remaining population used in the estimate.
Masks and shelter policies reduce risk without pretending that protected residents have left the affected geography.
The estimate uses a visible approximation mark and sensible rounding.
The same two identities may appear in a state card, front list or selected-country view without becoming separate meters.

Position, direction, forecast dates, lifecycle status, response completion dates and qualitative infrastructure condition are contextual information.
Material, source reservoirs, front birth allowances, turn weights, protection subcomponents and cumulative dose remain hidden.
The interface does not ask the player to manage a second weather-strength score, a readiness score or a panic meter.
Existing national resources and the shared Deaths, Air Cleanliness, famine and migration panels keep their established ownership and displays.

## Success, partial success and failure

Success means preserving people and useful capacity while accepting the disruption necessary to survive the passage.
A successful government may suspend factories, reduce sorties, withdraw formations or close roads before conditions become extreme.
The weather still moves and causes some losses.
The reward is a better observed outcome, preserved equipment and faster recovery.

Partial success is common.
A corridor may remain open while an industrial district is badly damaged.
Civilian shelter may work well while an exposed army becomes disorganized.
A state can clear safely and then face a returning branch with fewer reserves.
Reports should connect each outcome to its actual exposure and response history.

Failure follows from prolonged severe exposure, unavailable resources, unsafe movement, simultaneous emergencies and choices to keep risky activities running.
Losses use current population and actual remaining facilities, so the design cannot erase the same residents or damage nonexistent factories repeatedly.
Poor countries retain a free shelter and activity-reduction response with a genuine economic sacrifice.

## Replay variation

Variation comes from eligible desert selection, initial position within the chosen source region, direction, speed, width, local intensity changes and the timing of existing wars or disasters.
Evolutions add new geographic behavior.
A quiet baseline season can remain mostly over sparsely populated territory.
Another can carry severe dust into a dense industrial corridor.
At higher Chaos, independent branches, returns and simultaneous source regions can force different countries to confront the same season in very different ways.
Origin selection does not deliberately hunt the player or the most populated state.

## Campaign compatibility

The full baseline and all evolved forms must remain playable with every supported DLC combination, including none.
Optional DLC interfaces may enrich presentation or commands only after the same intended outcome is available in the base game.
The disaster follows physical geography through occupation, annexation and changing borders.
Countries are responsible for their current civilian and industrial authority, while each army experiences the weather where its formations operate.
The nationality of the first reporting country does not assign blame for a natural disaster.

The design is finished when its full journey, response choices, four evolutions and lasting consequences can be implemented without losing those promises.
The evidence file identifies the current engine and shared-helper questions that must be resolved before implementation can make that claim.
