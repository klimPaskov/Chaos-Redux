# Sandstorm: four evolutions

## Evolution entry and ordinary weather

Formation, approach, passage, intensification, weakening and clearance are ordinary lifecycle behavior.
The storm can turn modestly, vary its speed and contain severe sections before any evolution.
An evolution changes what the season can physically do and how much of the world it can threaten.
The four evolutions are cumulative in capability.

Each evolution has two entry paths.
If the event first fires while the relevant Chaos threshold is met and evolutions are enabled, it opens in the highest eligible form with the lower capabilities already available.
If a season is already active, each next evolution uses its normal dynamic timing gate and current physical viability.
The implementation must not support only one of these paths.

The proposed active timing anchor is a forty-five-day base waiting value while eligible, with modifiers for Chaos above the threshold, persistent severe material and remaining viable season time.
The intended timing ordering is faster with substantially higher Chaos and a surviving strong front, slower while the system weakens, and impossible after the material-creation cutoff.
Exact event probabilities and cumulative timing remain subject to the verified game-version probability adapter.
No percentage chance is certified by this document.

The first active evolution requires at least ten elapsed season days.
Subsequent active advancements have at least seven days of separation and require an actual viable manifestation, not only a threshold flag.
Promotion requires enough remaining time and material for its opening behavior, with a thirty-day minimum remaining before the day-300 creation cutoff for Evolutions II through IV.
The stage is not advanced if its defining opening cannot be admitted.
The player sees a continuation of the existing season rather than a reset.

Changing Chaos upward can create new eligibility during a live season.
Falling below a threshold does not undo fronts already physically created.
Disabling evolutions before entry produces the complete baseline.
Disabling future evolution during a season prevents new stage advancements while preserving the current form's already admitted physical behavior and its original finite bounds.
Every stage flag, eligibility change, log row and content unlock contributes zero Chaos by itself.

Existing individual evolution settings, where the shared framework exposes them, use a contiguous prerequisite policy.
The highest eligible pre-fire form is the highest enabled contiguous prefix whose Chaos threshold is met and whose physical opening is viable.
A disabled lower stage prevents a higher dependent stage from newly activating and is shown as an explicit requirement.
Already admitted fronts retain their current physical capabilities and finite bounds when a setting changes.

| Settings case | Before the event fires | During an active season |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All evolutions disabled | Complete baseline | Preserve admitted physical behavior and stop future advancements |
| I disabled, II or higher enabled | Baseline until the missing prerequisite is enabled | No new higher-stage advancement through the disabled prerequisite |
| I enabled, II disabled, III or IV enabled | At most The Great Dust Wall | Retain current admitted stage and block new advancement across II |
| Contiguous enabled stages below current Chaos | Highest physically viable eligible form | Next enabled viable advancement uses its timing and admission rules |
| A lower setting disabled after a higher stage already exists | Not applicable to an already active season | Keep existing fronts and current-stage capabilities, with further advancement blocked until required settings are enabled |

This policy consumes the existing shared settings rather than adding a separate Sandstorm settings model.

## Evolution I: The Great Dust Wall

**Chaos requirement: 200 or more.**

The single wall becomes substantially broader, deeper and more persistent.
Its leading band stays severe for longer, extreme sections occur more often, and the trailing body takes longer to pass.
The increase in population exposed and intensity-time dose drives greater civilian damage without an unexplained second mortality multiplier for the same intensity band.
Military visibility is worse because severe and extreme conditions occupy a larger fraction of the operating area and last longer.

The starting geometry target is 1,200–3,000 kilometers across the direction of travel and coherent depth profiles up to 1,440 kilometers.
Every depth profile still respects its speed and stall-derived residence limit.
Some thinner fast sections remain possible.
The wall's expanded footprint must be geographically continuous.

### Active entry

An already observed dense section consolidates and the body expands over successive committed updates.
The forecast identifies the growing flanks before they reach new population wherever the available geography allows warning.
The expansion uses existing available material and reduced attenuation within the finite source budget.
It cannot create a disconnected remote storm at this stage.
The first substantial larger footprint supplies the evolution report's actual geographic evidence.

### Evolved opening

The initial desert opening is already a huge deep wall.
The source selection still uses the same broad dynamic catalog.
At least one severe section and a substantial normal body must be present at entry, with extreme conditions possible in the densest leading area.
The event must not open as a small baseline storm and merely attach the evolution name.

### Response and narrative direction

Longer preparation commitments become valuable, and preserving machinery before impact is often worth the industrial sacrifice.
The report emphasizes the wall's scale, the disappearance of familiar landmarks and the difficulty of keeping routes and formations together.
Civilian suffering is described with restraint and geographic specificity.
Routine military caution should feel useful even while the weather remains overwhelming.

## Evolution II: Splinter Storms

**Chaos requirement: 400 or more.**

A sufficiently large wall can split into independently moving fronts.
Children inherit lineage and the season deadline while receiving distinct paths, speed profiles and material allocations.
Their combined material is no greater than the parent's remaining material.
The division of material is deliberately uneven in some cases.
A thin branch can dissipate within several days while a dominant child remains large enough to become a major storm.
Concentration and steering can strengthen a surviving child locally without creating material.

Up to four fronts are active simultaneously, with eight cumulative birth admissions available at this stage.
Every split child spends an admission.
The parent is retired atomically only when all planned children have valid geometry and capacity.
An eligible split may remain pending for at most forty-eight hours while the wall visibly deforms.
If a valid split cannot occur, the deformation resolves without advancing the stage or claiming its defining outcome.
Ordinary later split opportunities can be deferred or decay with their parent under the same finite limits.

### Active entry

The current wall separates into two or three distinct substantial sections.
At least two children survive long enough to establish visibly different committed courses.
One split is enough to demonstrate the new behavior, but it cannot be represented as two labels over an unchanged single footprint.
Each threatened country receives a deduplicated warning for the front that actually approaches it.

### Evolved opening

The desert begins with one connected broad wall and an already visible developing fracture.
Within the first seventy-two hours, a committed valid split produces at least two independent courses.
The initial material and capacity are chosen to make that opening viable.
This preserves the origin identity while delivering Splinter Storms immediately enough to be recognized.

### Divergent outcomes

A small branch can weaken over the sea, a broad branch can turn inland and a dense section can maintain a threatening course toward a second country.
A state that escaped the original wall may receive a later warning from another direction.
The display identifies lineage and direction without requiring the player to manage a numbered front-count resource.
Closing one warning card never dismisses other active branches.

## Evolution III: The Returning Storm

**Chaos requirement: 600 or more.**

Large surviving fronts gain sustained curvature and can return to areas already struck.
The path physically bends through intermediate geography.
The front consumes time and material throughout the return.
Previously recovered states use their current remaining population, issued protection and repaired or damaged facilities on the new passage.

Crossing a suitable major desert can also activate a temporary emission area capable of forming new fronts.
Only the actual swept emitting land qualifies.
A country owning a desert does not automatically generate dust because another state in the country was touched.
The emission area draws from a finite seasonal source budget shared by every crossing front and lineage.
Each desert family can admit at most two reseeds during the entire season.
Repeated entry, a new child ID or a merge cannot refresh that allowance.

Up to six fronts are active, with sixteen cumulative birth admissions.
An initial source draw and each reseed have explicit material receipts.
The initial balance allows each reseed to draw at most half of that family's opening allocation, with at most one additional opening-equivalent available across its two reseeds.
The source budget is established once and never replenishes by waiting or crossing the same land again.

### Active entry

A substantial existing front begins a committed broad turn toward previously affected geography, or a real recently crossed desert begins a viable new emission front.
The report explains the observed reversal or renewed source.
Within the remaining season, both return and desert-generation capabilities must have eligible opportunities where the verified map and surviving fronts support them.
A return cannot count as the same state merely remaining continuously covered.
Its distinct recurrence requires at least forty-eight hours of complete local clearance before reentry.

### Evolved opening

The source wall begins with a viable curved course and enough remaining material for a later recurrence.
It also has at least one reachable eligible desert-contact opportunity selected from the actual geography.
The early forecast may show a broad curved watch corridor but does not reveal a scripted guaranteed outcome many weeks ahead.
The implementation must demonstrate that returns and reseeding can genuinely occur in this opening, rather than relying on a stage flag without valid geography.

### Campaign consequence

The player can no longer treat local clearance as reliable final relief.
Repairs remain useful but may be interrupted.
Mask filters, fuel, vehicles and preparation capacity can be depleted before another branch arrives.
The system begins to resemble a self-sustaining global weather pattern while retaining finite material, birth capacity and an absolute endpoint.

## Evolution IV: The World of Dust

**Chaos requirement: 800 or more.**

Several major desert regions generate enormous fronts at the same time.
These fronts move independently and can merge through genuine spatial contact.
The proposed multi-source opening contains three to five distinct eligible desert families, with at least three physically separate broad fronts establishing themselves within the same twenty-four-hour formation window.
The final number depends on the verified eligible pool and remaining active capacity.
Eligibility for this form requires enough valid families and capacity to deliver the three-source minimum.

The geometry allows walls roughly 2,000–5,000 kilometers across while retaining coherent depth and speed profiles.
Up to eight fronts can be active, with twenty-four cumulative birth admissions.
Large connected land areas can be under dust simultaneously.
Ocean paths and unaffected geographic gaps remain visible so the player can understand where the system is spreading and where it is clearing.

### Active entry

The existing season remains in place.
Additional source families that have available material begin emissions in the same formation window.
Already active source families can count toward the three-family concurrent requirement if their substantial fronts are still physically present.
New remote sources are selected from the same validated desert catalog and spend the remaining season's budgets.
They do not renew the timer or reset spent reseeds.

### Evolved opening

Three to five deserts begin broad fronts concurrently.
The first report establishes several named source regions and the geographic scale.
It does not pretend that one small source has instantly teleported across the world.
All lower capabilities remain available, so these fronts can later split, curve, revisit crossed territory and seed eligible deserts.

### Merging

Two or more fronts can merge when their actual ribbons make substantial spatial contact and their courses permit one combined body.
The initial proposal requires at least twelve hours of material contact before a stable merge, preventing one-cell brushes from constantly rewriting identity.
Their surviving material is combined under the overlap cap, with a wider body or a more concentrated wall depending on geometry.
The new identity inherits all lineage, source-use and consequence history.
Its deadline is no later than the original season stop or an earlier binding parent limit.
It does not receive a fresh lifetime, new reservoir or repeated casualty pulse.

A glancing crossing can instead remain two independent fronts with a temporarily reinforced overlap.
The player should see the distinction between a collision and a stable merged wall.
The forecast updates affected countries without replaying their original opening reports.
The required behavior is that fronts can eventually merge, preserving the user's campaign variation.
At least one verified viable Evolution IV opening and acceptance trace must contain a genuinely convergent pair that reaches a stable spatial merge before the original deadline.
The implementation cannot claim this capability from unused code or a merge flag over permanently divergent routes.
A merger is not forced in every campaign, and the global presentation milestone remains optional in occurrence.

## Optional global presentation milestone

One super-event is proposed for a demonstrated World of Dust consequence.
Presentation **120** is reserved under the [shared identifier registry](../shared_identifier_registry.md) for that milestone, with final title research still required and audio ID separately unassigned.
This finite-recovery milestone requires no world-end or triggerable-scenario identity.
Its trigger requires the actual concurrent footprint to cover at least twenty percent of world land and fifteen percent of current world population for seventy-two continuous hours, with substantial fronts from at least three desert families.
The geographic denominator is the supported map's actual land, and population uses the same current union estimate with its explicit approximation.
The trigger is a difficult global outcome, not the 800-point threshold, the stage flag or the opening of a menu.
It is optional in occurrence, with one presentation at most per season.
The separate research prompt owns final title, quote, remark, image and audio research.

## Closure at every stage

The day-300 creation cutoff, day-330 compulsory taper and day-360 absolute stop apply to all four evolutions.
The shared attenuation envelope remains full through day 330 and then declines linearly to zero at day 360, applied to the same intensity used by geography, presentation and consequences.
An active advancement that cannot deliver its defining behavior within those limits remains unadmitted.
Already active fronts weaken and clear without deleting their historical damage.
The ending distinguishes each region's recovery from the last global front's disappearance.
Several storms can cover large parts of the planet before finally fading, exactly as required by the user's design.
