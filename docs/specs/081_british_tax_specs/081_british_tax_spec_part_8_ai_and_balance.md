# British Tax: AI and balance intent

## Principles

The AI should make strategically legible choices from its actual economy, sovereignty, relations, and likely exposure to Britain. It should not refuse because a random number ignores an existential danger or accept forever because the original event used a flat probability.

AI weights are preferences relative to other eligible choices, not percentages unless a complete, normalised choice set has actually been evaluated. The package includes scenario targets and a deterministic arithmetic model, not a fabricated probability evaluation from the unavailable HOI4 MCP tools.

## Initial acceptance

Automatic treatment takes precedence for dependents and British-led allies. Other sovereign countries consider their actual industrial and consumer-goods loss, dependence on British resources, alternative suppliers, bilateral relations, existing war, Britain's reachable military strength, their own faction support, and whether a special Event 81 goal would result.

A weak, isolated minor with essential British imports should usually accept initially and plan gradual relief. A strong independent major with alternative trade has a credible refusal route. A country already fighting Britain should normally reject the claim, since acceptance cannot restore ordinary trade during the war. An AI major's special-goal exemption should matter, but must not be interpreted as immunity from ordinary British diplomacy or other wars.

## Reform priorities

The AI chooses a domain based on recoverable utility. Industrial reform becomes more attractive when the country needs military output or construction capacity. Consumer assessment reform becomes urgent when extra consumer-goods occupation is blocking development. External reform becomes more valuable when fuel, convoys, essential resources, or British export premiums threaten its economy.

A completed domain has no remaining selection weight for that stage. A British-led ally at Nominal should stop wasting Political Power on impossible final projects. A dependent should never attempt the category. A country with insufficient spare civilian capacity should consider the disclosed low-industry route rather than waiting forever for a factory it cannot retain under tax.

Political reserves matter. The AI should not start a 150-Political-Power reform if doing so prevents an urgently necessary native law or emergency action. Conversely, it should not save indefinitely while paying a much larger ongoing tax. Compare a bounded future benefit horizon, using one year at baseline and up to two years under the longer Evolution II programme.

## Hostile cancellation

The AI may cancel when its tax burden has risen, British leverage has fallen, alternative trade is secure, or an enforcement war would be tolerable. It should not cancel merely because a project has one day left or because a temporary surcharge has just expired.

A pending final consolidation strongly favours completion. Existing direct war, an already collapsed trade relationship, and severe British overextension favour cancellation. The choice remains sensitive to the AI-major exception and to a minor's special-goal exposure.

## British petitions and pressure

Britain assesses the expected retained revenue, target strength, importance of alliance cohesion, existing disputes, and its own wars. Recognising a major's lawful reform can be preferable to losing it completely. Pressure is more attractive against a weak independent taxpayer where a modest delay preserves significant receipts.

The hard subject lock is not an AI preference that can be overridden by a very friendly opinion score. A British player cannot grant a subject an economic-independence concession through an interface path unavailable to the subject itself.

## British enforcement

A goal is not a requirement to declare war. Britain weighs reachable land or naval operations, supply, coalition risk, ongoing wars, and the likely economic value of coercion. A remote microstate with no useful capacity should not receive a reckless invasion merely because the special goal exists.

Human-controlled majors still receive the user-requested entitlement rule. British AI should recognise their actual strength and possible faction support. Multiplayer opponents do not become soft targets simply because the event singles out player-controlled refusals.

## Assistance by third parties

A potential reform partner favours assistance where relations, strategic competition with Britain, and a real trade opportunity support it. It must meet its own political and economic commitments. It should refuse assistance that would consume its last usable industry during an existential war.

A partner does not need to belong to the taxpayer's faction, and assistance must not automatically create a faction. The domestic substitution route remains a fallback when no partner is willing or economically useful.

## Scenario matrix

| Scenario | Intended qualitative behaviour | Failure to catch |
|---|---|---|
| Weak neutral minor, major British naval superiority | Acceptance often preferred, later gradual relief | Frequent suicidal refusal without relevant benefits |
| Independent major, hostile relations, alternative suppliers | Refusal competitive or preferred | Treating AI-major exemption as a nonexistent special goal |
| British dependent with abundant Political Power | No independent decision route | Buying an exemption through a generic negotiation |
| Independent British-led ally at Nominal | Maintain settlement and await valid relationship change | Repeated attempts to enter final projects |
| One-civilian-factory minor | Low-industry reform remains selectable | Permanent affordability deadlock |
| Taxpayer one day from final consolidation | Complete unless a genuinely exceptional change intervenes | Cancelling to save a trivial last instalment |
| Britain in several losing wars | Concede selectively, avoid reckless enforcement | Declaring every available tax war |
| Britain powerful, target valuable and reachable | Pressure and enforcement are credible | Automatically forgiving every refusal |
| Direct war with Britain | No actual tax income and a strong rejection preference | Britain's enemies financing its armies |
| Evolution II after extensive reform | Protect earned domains and assess remaining burden | Resetting all AI plans to Full Taxation |
| Two human majors in multiplayer | Both use the human-goal rule independently | Host-only targeting or recipient confusion |
| Third-party embargo affects a reform partner | Re-evaluate real alternatives | Claiming substitute trade exists when it cannot occur |

## Balance outcomes

At baseline, an independent minor should feel a substantially larger proportional cost than a major. A major should still notice lost construction, production, and trade value. Britain's gains should become significant with a large compliant network and should collapse when that network leaves.

At Evolution II, subject and minor economies can be severely constrained. The remaining civilian-capacity safeguard and low-industry reform route prevent scripting deadlocks without making the tax harmless. Britain can become very powerful, but cannot credit unrealised assessments, retain benefits from vanished countries, or escape all ordinary logistical limitations.

The important balance question is not whether the same percentage appears in every tooltip. It is whether the actual cost, available choices, duration, and expected military danger produce the intended strategic trade-off for each country type.
