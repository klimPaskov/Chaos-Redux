New rates, timings, outcomes, achievements, and presentation choices are authored design proposals, not claims of prior user approval or engine validation. User-supplied names are preserved. ## Experience

Britain declares that every foreign economy now owes it taxes. The demand is absurd, but its effects are tangible. Factories become available to Britain, foreign consumers occupy industrial capacity that would otherwise be available domestically, and British purchases receive preferential treatment while British exports become more expensive. London explains all of this as routine administration.

A weak independent country faces a genuine strategic choice. Accepting protects access to British trade and avoids an immediate enforcement crisis, but leaves a long economic drain. Refusing restores its economy immediately and risks an embargo and British military action. Gradual reform preserves relations while taking years of political and economic effort. British dependents have a harsher position because sovereignty, rather than a sufficient pile of Political Power, determines whether they can resist at all.

Britain receives an ongoing campaign rather than a single reward popup. A large compliant network finances construction and military production. Excessive pressure encourages countries to prepare an exit, whereas selective concessions can preserve valuable relationships. Successful revenue administration can become a durable international economic order under Evolution II.

## Countries and authority

Britain means the existing ENG country identity across changes of ideology, leader, display name, and cosmetic identity. Changing its government does not transfer the beneficiary role to another country or remove the system. A vanished ENG cannot be replaced by whichever country controls London, by the current faction leader, or by the country that happens to receive the root event.

Every existing, valid country other than ENG receives an assessment when the event fires. Validity follows the project's established normal civilian-economy classifier. A small human country is not invalid merely because it has very little industry. A human economy under a special tag is not excluded merely for being unusual. A faction relationship, war with Britain, or subject relationship changes treatment rather than silently excluding the country from assessment.

The first assessment establishes a persistent legal status. Actual collections additionally require a functioning economy and a relationship in which collection is possible. Legal liability and actual collection are different. An enemy can receive the demand while Britain collects nothing during their war.

## Permission hierarchy

Apply the first matching row. Check the current relationship again whenever an action is taken or a project finishes.

| Country relationship | Initial treatment | Gradual resistance | Immediate refusal or cancellation | Peaceful final exit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ENG itself | Beneficiary only | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| British subject or a dependent whose ultimate overlord is ENG | Automatically taxed | Unavailable | Unavailable | Requires genuine independence first |
| Other non-independent country | Automatically assessed and taxed where collection is possible | Unavailable until sovereign | Unavailable until sovereign | Requires genuine independence first |
| Independent member of a faction led by ENG | Automatically taxed | Can reach Nominal Taxation | Unavailable while ENG leads | Blocked while ENG leads |
| Other independent country | May accept or refuse | Available after accepting | Available | Available |

The treatment of a non-British dependent resolves an omission in the rough brief. It does not grant that country's overlord the ability to make an unrelated sovereign refusal for it, and does not apply the special British-dependent rate. It receives its major or minor schedule after ordinary subject obligations are accounted for. This is an authored extension.

British colonies, puppets, dominions, and other applicable dependencies share the hard lock. High autonomy short of actual independence is insufficient. Good relations, a democratic government, a British concession, or a completed reform in an earlier independent period does not bypass the lock.

## Opening sequence

Britain receives a declaration event that explains the scope and economic opportunity. Its acknowledgement starts the already-selected event rather than offering a way to cancel the user-requested premise. A global news item announces the demand. Countries receive role-specific assessments that disclose their industrial burden, additional consumer-goods burden, trade treatment, and available response.

A sovereign country outside a British-led faction has 30 days to respond. Collection starts on acceptance. At expiry, an unanswered human assessment defaults to acceptance after disclosing this in the original notice. The player can still cancel later under the normal rules. AI countries decide through the acceptance model instead of relying on this timeout. Forced taxpayers enter collection immediately where eligible.

There is no initial Political Power or stability prize for accepting or refusing. Choosing between serious strategic consequences is the reward structure. London does not receive a separate flat reward for each reply on top of actual revenue.

## Persistent country states

| State | Meaning and available play |
|---|---|
| Assessment pending | Independent response window is open. No collection from that country yet. |
| Full British Taxation | All applicable schedules operate at full assessed strength. |
| Reduced Taxation | First consolidated reduction is permanent while sovereign. |
| Limited Taxation | Most domestic control is restored, but remaining claims are substantial. |
| Nominal Taxation | A small surviving British claim remains. This is the ceiling for British-led allies. |
| Tax Independence | The country has completed a peaceful exit and is exempt from future Event 81 assessments. |
| Hostile exit | The country validly refused or cancelled. Its tax has ended and the British response applies. |
| Collection suspended | A temporary collection condition overlays liability, for example war with ENG. It is not an exit stage. |
| System abolished | The worldwide authority has ended and cannot resume through this firing. |

A refusal is a final choice for this occurrence. Event 81 does not offer repeated surrender-and-refusal cycles for opinion changes, rewards, achievements, or war goals. A later British conquest may create genuine dependency and therefore a new forced liability, but this is not a second event firing.

## New countries and changing relationships

A newly created valid country is enrolled once after its existence and economic identity are settled. It receives the same sovereignty-dependent treatment and, where appropriate, a fresh 30-day response window. A released country with a provable inherited Event 81 settlement keeps the settlement rather than being charged twice through a tag change.

An independent taxpayer that becomes a British subject returns to compulsory Full British Taxation. Archive its previously consolidated reform stage. Projects in progress end without success and release their continuing costs. On genuine independence, restore the archived consolidated stage, remove the dependency surcharge, and unlock the normal independent options. A country that already earned Tax Independence regains that status after such independence. A previously valid hostile-exit exemption likewise resumes with its existing dispute history, unless a later explicit enforcement settlement lawfully replaced it. Independence does not create a fresh refusal reward or a duplicate entitlement. Conquest is not a way to erase earned history forever, and brief puppet-release loops do not count as new reforms.

Joining a British-led faction locks future refusal and the final transition. It does not retroactively erase a previously completed Tax Independence settlement. A country at Nominal cannot begin final-transition projects while locked. If the relationship changes during a final project or consolidation, pause that transition and restore the Nominal floor until eligibility returns. The lock takes effect immediately, not at the next convenient project completion.

When ENG stops leading the faction, that specific lock ends. Leaving the faction follows ordinary game rules. Event 81 neither invents a free faction-exit action nor traps a country after it has legitimately left.

## War, capitulation, and state changes

Direct war with ENG suspends actual collection immediately, including British gains attributed to the enemy. It does not by itself complete Tax Independence. A valid refusal during the war still records the hostile exit, although an unusable duplicate war goal must not be manufactured against an enemy already being fought. A peace that does not settle taxation resumes the prior liability after a 30-day reassessment period. No missed wartime tax is collected as an accumulated debt.

Capitulation is not identical to ceasing to exist. Collect only from usable capacity actually retained by a liable country, excluding capacity already consumed by occupation and other owners' claims. A government with no usable economy produces no British income. Annexation, release, civil war, and the transfer of a resource-bearing state must update both sides of the account. A split cannot duplicate either physical capacity or a completed reform.

If ENG ceases to exist, taxation ends globally and all Event 81 economic claims are removed. A later reappearance of ENG does not restart this fire-once occurrence. Part 4 defines negotiated and military abolition while ENG survives.

## Player-facing contract

The player must be able to answer four questions from the assessment and decision category: what is being taken now, what the next action will recover, what it will cost and how long it will take, and what Britain is legally able to do in response. Information about hidden administrative calculations exists to explain those choices rather than becoming another resource-management game.
