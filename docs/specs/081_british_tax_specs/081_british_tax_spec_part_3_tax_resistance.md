# British Tax: resistance and reform

## Purpose

British Tax Resistance is the long route for an independent taxpayer that wants to recover economic sovereignty without an immediate hostile break. It offers relief during the process, preserves substantial costs, and leaves Britain opportunities to negotiate or obstruct without giving it an endless veto.

The main strategic decision is which burden to dismantle first. A country preparing for war may recover military production before trade. A construction-focused minor may prioritise consumer-goods occupation. A fuel-dependent country may first remove British control of its imports and reserves.

## Public values and presentation

The mechanic has two persistent custom progress values: the current Tax Stage and completed Reform Domains within that stage. The second value is represented as three completion marks rather than a currency or an additional score. Tax Stage has a consistent subdued gold identity and reform completion a consistent green identity, with readable non-colour indicators as well.

Actual civilian, military, consumer-goods, and trade effects remain visible as normal economic modifier lines. They are consequences of the system, not extra invented resources or long-term pressure scores. The active mission has its ordinary remaining-time display. Temporary costs and invoices are shown where the player makes the relevant commitment. Do not add an authority meter, revenue currency, compliance score, influence bar, or treasury total elsewhere in the interface.

The ordinary decision category contains a static picture and a concise explanation of the current stage, relationship lock, completed domains, and the next useful action. Native targeted decisions handle counterparties. A separate custom map, tax-office management screen, or interactive state puzzle would add no necessary player choice here.

## Main actions

At most six primary actions are visible at once. Normally the player sees the three unfinished domain reforms, the current mission or consolidation action, and the hostile-exit action where permitted. Mutually exclusive variants replace a domain's entry rather than appearing as a long menu of parallel near-identical decisions.

| Action family | Main purpose | Ordinary duration before adjustment | Base Political Power cost |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Industrial reform | Reduce industrial transfer and applicable production levies | 90 days | 150 |
| Consumer assessment reform | Reduce extra consumer-goods occupation and tax-office control | 120 days | 125 |
| External privileges reform | Reduce unequal trade and expanded external claims | 120 days | 150 |
| Consolidate reform stage | Make three domain improvements a durable stage | 90 days | 100 |
| We Will Pay No More | End tax immediately through hostile cancellation | Immediate | No artificial exit fee |

These are working action-family labels except for the user's explicit hostile-exit wording. Final decision names and descriptions must be authored later from the relevant action and stage.

One core reform or consolidation mission can run at a time. A British response mission may temporarily exist alongside it. A second emergency mission can only appear when it represents a different unavoidable event and keeps the total at three. The category should not create a mission for every bill or every monthly assessment.

## Twelve meaningful domain projects

Each transition has three distinct projects. The rows describe purpose and behaviour rather than final localisation.

| Transition | Industrial project | Consumer assessment project | External privileges project |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full to Reduced | Audit foreign industrial allocations and renegotiate the first transfer schedule | Dispute the population and industry assessment used by British tax offices | Establish a non-British procurement alternative and challenge the largest preference |
| Reduced to Limited | Recover domestic authority over factory allocation and production priorities | Separate domestic consumption planning from London's calculations | Replace vulnerable import dependence and reduce British control of shipping and fuel |
| Limited to Nominal | End British supervision of domestic production contracts | Close most British tax offices and transfer their functions to domestic institutions | Restrict the remaining concessions and remove broad British procurement privileges |
| Nominal to Tax Independence | Settle the final claim on industrial output | Establish an independent final assessment and repeal the remaining collection mandate | Complete replacement agreements and terminate the surviving trade privileges |

A project completed under Evolution I or II also dismantles the new claims assigned to its domain. The player does not need to repeat a previously completed domain because a new tax label was added. The mission description identifies the new stakes before commitment.

## Costs that reflect the country

Apply a stage multiplier of 1.00, 1.10, 1.20, and 1.30 to the four successive transitions. Apply an Evolution duration multiplier of 1.00, 1.40, or 1.80. A strong negotiating position can reduce the duration to 80% of that result, while a weak minor facing much stronger Britain can increase it to 115%. Round displayed days upward to a multiple of five, with a minimum of 90 days for an ordinary project or consolidation.

Political Power costs scale by stage and by 1.00, 1.25, or 1.50 for the active Evolution. Diplomatic preparation can reduce the quoted amount by up to 20%. There is no compulsory Command Power cost for civilian paperwork. Ordinary political actions should not suddenly consume army command resources.

Industrial and consumer assessment projects reserve 5% of the country's remaining uncommitted civilian capacity, capped at five factories. The reservation lasts only while the project is active. It cannot use capacity already lost to taxation or booked by another exclusive cost. When fewer than one whole usable factory can be reserved, use the low-industry route instead: an additional 25% Political Power cost and 25% duration, without a factory reservation. A country with zero spare factories can therefore resist, but cannot do so cheaply.

External reform normally pays its Political Power cost and pursues a diplomatic project. A suitable non-British partner that is sovereign, not at war with the reforming country, and able to provide an actual relevant trading alternative can assist. A partner accepts a bounded political and industrial commitment, not a free click that instantly finishes another country's reform. A partner's assistance can reduce the project's time or cost by up to 20%, with one reduction chosen and quoted at the outset.

The domestic-substitution variant replaces foreign participation when no suitable partner exists. It uses a bounded civilian commitment or the low-industry alternative and takes 25% longer. Lack of an accessible friendly major must not make Tax Independence permanently impossible.

At most three cost types appear on one action. Display the ordinary native icons with the quoted values. A time requirement is explained clearly, and repeated checks never debit the upfront Political Power again. Affordability uses inclusive comparisons, so having exactly the quoted amount is enough.

## Immediate relief and consolidation

Successful domain completion lowers that domain to the next stage's multiplier immediately. The three completion marks identify what remains. Britain loses the corresponding benefits at the same time. The completed project is not merely a hidden prerequisite for a later all-or-nothing reward.

After all three projects, consolidation becomes available. This is a 90-day base political process that validates and implements the complete settlement. It has no additional factory reservation. At completion, the stage advances, completion marks reset for the next transition, and the country receives a role-appropriate report.

For the final transition, the temporary 5% assessment floor continues until consolidation finishes. On valid completion, it disappears, all Event 81 taxes and privileges end, the category closes, and a durable tax-exemption record remains. Peaceful completion adds no Event 81 embargo, hostile opinion modifier, or special war goal.

An unmodified baseline route takes about five years, depending on rounding and the final mission mix. A strong, well-prepared major can finish sooner. Evolution II can push the ordinary route toward a decade, with the weakest low-industry route exceeding twelve years before exceptional delays. These deliberately long anchors should receive explicit pacing review during native balance work. Costs and delays remain bounded. The country is also recovering capacity throughout that period rather than remaining at Full Taxation until the final year.

## British-led faction members

An independent British-led ally can complete the first three transitions normally. At Nominal, its category becomes a settlement overview with the faction restriction and any genuine outstanding response. Final-transition projects and hostile cancellation are unavailable while ENG leads.

If ENG loses leadership or the country legitimately leaves, the final projects become available immediately. If leadership returns during final work, preserve progress but pause the project and restore the Nominal collection floor. Final work resumes when eligibility returns. The player cannot use a queued decision to finish after the lock has reappeared.

## Dependent countries

A British dependent receives the assessment and its economic effects, but not British Tax Resistance. It cannot buy an exemption, close an office, lower a domain, enter a partner project, or exploit a diplomatic response to reduce a tax. Independence remains a normal political or military objective in the country’s broader campaign.

Upon actual independence, the category appears at the appropriate restored stage. The former subject can immediately choose a valid hostile exit or start normal reform. The tax does not disappear merely because autonomy increased by one level.

## British interference and replies

Britain may contest one core reform per country per stage. It has a 15-day response window. Its meaningful choices are to recognise the reform, negotiate a limited delay with a concrete concession, or apply bounded pressure. No response defaults to allowing the legal domestic process to continue. It does not automatically waive the remaining tax.

Pressure can create a 90-day surcharge and one additional 45-day delay for that stage. Britain spends 75 Political Power to initiate it. The taxpayer can continue, accept the temporary delay in exchange for a specified reduction of the surcharge, or cancel only the active project. Continuing can require an additional 25 Political Power once, disclosed in the response event. A taxpayer that lacks that amount takes the fixed delay instead of failing forever.

Refusing British pressure is not identical to refusing British Tax. It does not automatically grant Britain the hostile-exit war goal. Britain may threaten an embargo, but a formal pressure embargo suspends bilateral trade and British trade benefits while it lasts. It cannot create trade revenue from transactions it has prohibited.

A negotiated settlement can reduce a project’s cost or time, remove a temporary surcharge, or recognise the current domain early after its substantive work is completed. It cannot erase a British subject’s tax or complete Tax Independence for a British-led ally.

## Mission outcomes

Success requires current sovereignty, an active tax liability, the correct stage, completed committed time, and valid continuing industrial or diplomatic commitments. Completion rechecks the relationship and does not trust only the conditions at launch.

A project temporarily loses access to reserved factories if occupation, bombing-related usability, or another valid economic change removes them. Pause the mission for up to 90 days and offer conversion to the low-industry route using the remaining work, not a fresh full-duration mission. Continuing commitments are recalculated without charging the initial fee again. After that grace period, the player must convert or cancel.

A partner’s withdrawal changes external reform to the domestic-substitution route with a 25% extension on remaining work. It does not delete already completed months. Direct war with ENG suspends the active mission and its continuing costs. After peace and reassessment it resumes where legal. Becoming a British subject cancels active work but preserves consolidated history under Part 1.

Voluntary cancellation returns no upfront Political Power and awards no domain completion. It releases factory commitments immediately and retains completed prior domains. Invalidity caused by global abolition ends work cleanly because the objective has become unnecessary, not because the player failed.

## Anti-grind rules

Completed projects cannot be repeated within a stage. Refusing, rejoining, changing ideology, switching major status, or alternating faction leadership cannot duplicate progress. A successful stage gives one durable improvement, not a reusable reward event. Britain cannot indefinitely cycle demands or keep a mission stalled by declining to click a reply.
