# British Tax: Evolutions

## Shared progression

The system has exactly two Evolutions. Tax Stage describes a country's earned resistance progress. Evolution describes the international tax regime. These are different axes and are never stored or presented as the same progression.

An active Evolution requires its Chaos threshold, the relevant enabled setting, a surviving ENG, and a functioning tax network. Crossing 200 or 400 is an eligibility condition, not an instant tax increase. Active escalation uses a delayed, condition-sensitive mean-time-to-happen process with a 90-day anchor. Eligibility must have held for 30 days before the active process begins. Evolution II additionally requires Evolution I to have been active for at least 90 days.

A broad compliant base, active British war needs, and successful collection shorten the waiting tendency. A shrinking base, British capitulation, or suspended collections lengthen or suspend it. Initial tuning uses factors of 0.80 for broad compliance, 0.80 for substantial British war expenditure, and 1.50 for a network that has lost at least a quarter of its recent real receipts. Combined effective mean time stays between 45 and 360 days. These are desired waiting-time parameters, not validated probability results.

Before the root event has fired, a high-Chaos first occurrence can begin under the highest eligible enabled schedule. Starting directly with Evolution II requires both ordered Evolutions to be enabled. This does not produce two redundant global announcements. The opening assessment names the actual starting scope, and the appropriate consequences become active after acceptance or forced enrolment.

An already activated regime persists if Chaos later falls. Pending activation must recheck its prerequisites. It cannot arrive after abolition because an old delayed event remains in a queue.

## Evolution I: Everything Is Taxable

**Chaos requirement: 200+.**

Britain expands the definition of taxable economic activity. The original industrial and consumer-goods claims grow, and the two directions of privileged British trade become more unequal. British officials begin treating military output, naval production, fuel reserves, shipping, and strategic supplies as ordinary revenue categories.

The player receives an itemised change in obligations. The new burden is applied to the country's remaining British-controlled share, including partial domain reforms. A country that already reduced industrial taxation does not return to Full Taxation, and a completed trade reform also protects it proportionally from new fuel, convoy, and resource claims.

British dependents receive the expanded assessment automatically. They cannot open a separate exemption route for fuel or shipping that would bypass the broader subject lock. Independent British-led allies retain their reduction route and the Nominal ceiling. Other independent taxpayers can continue reform or choose the existing hostile cancellation.

### New play

The industrial domain now includes naval production taxation and more costly wartime claims. The external-privileges domain protects both trade terms and actual surplus assets. Completing external reform before a major naval campaign can preserve convoys and fuel. Prioritising industrial reform can recover the capacity needed to fight or construct an alternative economy.

A quarterly assessment report appears only when there is a meaningful new asset claim, a changed reserve situation, or a consequential dispute. Routine successful collection is represented through the economic state rather than a popup every 90 days.

Britain can use emergency assessments during a substantial war, at political cost and with a real cooldown. A weak taxpayer will feel these demands more severely, but reserve floors prevent the script from taking deployed convoys, charging nonexistent fuel, or making native economic values negative.

### Transition and resistance

The new schedule takes effect at the next country review. Existing project duration and upfront costs stay frozen. New projects use the Evolution I duration and cost multipliers. A project whose asset commitments have become invalid follows the pause and conversion rules rather than receiving an automatic failure.

The first material expanded collection is the outcome that can generate Evolution-related Chaos. The announcement, registration flag, and title reveal do not each generate additional Chaos.

## Evolution II: The British Revenue Empire

**Chaos requirement: 400+.**

Britain attempts to make its global revenue privileges a permanent economic order. Industrial assessments rise, consumer-goods claims can occupy much of a weak taxpayer's remaining civilian economy, and British purchases and exports become strongly asymmetrical. Expanded resource, fuel, convoy, and naval-production claims also intensify.

The British player can fund a large military-industrial economy with the capacity of countries that have accepted, lack sovereignty, or consider the cost of refusal too dangerous. Britain can approach the native consumer-goods floor while maintaining construction and military production. The system never produces a negative requirement or unbounded free industry.

Subjects face the harshest treatment because they have no domestic resistance category. Their first meaningful strategic opening is genuine independence. Independent minors retain a choice, but the cost of gradual reform and the danger of a British goal make it painful. Majors can negotiate from a stronger position, though their tax is still economically significant.

### Longer reform without erased progress

Future projects receive the Evolution II duration and cost multipliers. Already quoted work does not retroactively become longer or more expensive. All completed domains, consolidated stages, and peaceful exemptions survive. The difficulty comes from the size of the surviving British claims and the cost of dismantling them, not from resetting years of progress.

Britain's stronger pressure still obeys the one-per-country-per-stage limit. The emergency rate can reach the Evolution II surcharge, but pressure and emergency charges use the larger current value instead of an unlimited product. A legal gradual route must remain completable even against a British player that contests every available stage.

### A mature international order

The mature-order super-event is earned through sustained economic domination. It is not shown merely because Evolution II became eligible or an administrative flag changed.

For 180 consecutive days, ENG must receive real industrial collections from countries representing at least 40% of the eligible non-British industrial base and must collect from a sufficiently broad group. The breadth requirement is the smaller of eight countries and one quarter of the valid eligible country count rounded upward, with a minimum of two where two valid foreign countries exist. Single-foreign-country maps cannot earn this global-order presentation or its achievement.

During the same interval, Britain must actually relieve at least half of its otherwise applicable consumer-goods occupation through foreign contributions. If its native non-Event-81 consumer-goods occupation is already zero, replace that test with an additional real industrial-receipts requirement of at least 80 civilian-plus-military factory equivalents. Zero divided by zero is not evidence of international dominance.

The industrial-share denominator uses the non-Event-81 economic base and identity-aware successor handling. Creating additional puppet tags cannot manufacture breadth. Lost collections reset the sustained qualification interval. Once earned, the super-event can play only once, even if the network later collapses and recovers.

### British failure

Large-scale peaceful exits reduce the system's real benefits and make further escalation harder. A British defeat can end bilateral claims or enable worldwide abolition under the settlement rules. An enormous announced assessment with little actual collection is a failed imperial project, not a successful Revenue Empire.

## Event settings and history

Disabled Evolutions do not activate, award progression records, unlock their achievements, or create their presentation events. An active-regime change is recorded with Event 81, ENG as the taxing actor, the relevant Evolution, and its actual participating countries. This is one persistent event changing over time, not a fresh firing of the Negative Economy cluster each time a country receives a bill.
