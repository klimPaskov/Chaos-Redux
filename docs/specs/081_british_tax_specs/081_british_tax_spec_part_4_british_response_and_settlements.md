# British Tax: British administration, refusal, and settlements

## Britain's campaign

Britain's best result is a durable, productive network. A taxpayer that accepts a lower negotiated rate may contribute more over the campaign than a ruined minor that provokes a costly invasion. A major's refusal can be damaging without creating an automatic Event 81 war goal. Human players receive the more dangerous treatment explicitly requested in the brief.

British administration uses an ordinary decision category with targeted entries. It shows the current tax tier, an actionable list of disputes, and normal economic effects. Britain does not receive a separate invented money currency or a meter for each partner. Revenue changes are visible through civilian capacity, military capacity, consumer goods, trade, and the applicable expanded claims.

## British action set

At most five administration families are exposed. Selecting a target narrows the relevant entries without making AI behaviour depend on a human's selected target.

| Family | Purpose and cost | Restriction |
|---|---|---|
| Review a petition | Recognise a lawful reform or offer a bounded negotiated concession | Only an actual independent taxpayer with a live dispute |
| Apply pressure | Spend 75 Political Power for the one-per-stage pressure package in Part 3 | No repeated surcharge against the same stage |
| Issue an emergency assessment | Spend 150 Political Power for a 90-day wartime surcharge | Britain in a substantial war, cooldown and caps apply |
| Prepare enforcement | Spend 100 Political Power and 90 days on a visible diplomatic and military preparation | Requires an existing qualifying refusal and usable goal or continuing enforcement war |
| Negotiate a settlement | Open a bilateral concession or taxation-abolition offer | Requires the relevant diplomatic or military conditions |

Reviewing an ordinary assessment is not a free source of Political Power, stability, or permanent production. Enforcement preparation improves target-specific readiness and diplomatic commitment through existing game systems where supported. It does not spawn an army, waive naval range, remove ordinary access rules, or force a war declaration.

## Immediate refusal

A sovereign country outside a British-led faction can reject the first demand. It never receives the tax. Britain immediately embargoes it, normal trade with Britain ends, and each side receives a severe Event 81 bilateral opinion penalty. The same response applies to later hostile cancellation.

The baseline penalty is minus 100 opinion in each direction, using one owned modifier per side rather than repeated additive copies. Its initial duration is five years. Renewal requires an actual continuing enforcement dispute, with no automatic monthly penalty stacking. Ordinary unrelated opinion modifiers are preserved.

The British embargo persists while the refusal dispute remains unresolved. Britain may eventually lift the embargo through a normal diplomatic settlement without restoring taxation. An independent refusal record remains valid. Lifting the embargo does not silently re-enrol the country.

## Exact war-goal rule

Evaluate the refusing country's status when it validly refuses or cancels.

| Refusing country | Special Event 81 war-goal entitlement |
|---|---|
| Independent AI minor | Yes |
| Independent human-controlled minor | Yes |
| Independent human-controlled major | Yes |
| Independent AI major | No automatic Event 81 entitlement |
| British dependent | Refusal cannot occur |
| Independent British-led ally | Refusal cannot occur while ENG leads |

Human-controlled means any human participant, not just the host or the country from which the root event was invoked. A later change to AI control does not revoke an already issued entitlement. A later change to human control does not retroactively issue a goal for an old AI-major refusal.

The entitlement is automatic where required. Britain is not asked whether it would rather take a small Political Power penalty instead. Choosing whether and when to use a goal remains a British strategic decision. A refusal never automatically declares war.

Use a native supported war goal consistent with coercive enforcement. The existing Event 81 annexation-goal family is the baseline implementation candidate, but the player's goal is to defeat or coerce the refuser rather than being obliged to annex it. A 730-day lifetime is the design anchor where the native representation supports a finite expiry. Expiry does not restore tax or permit repeated issuance from the same refusal. If Britain already has the same usable goal or is already fighting that target, retain a single enforcement entitlement rather than creating duplicates.

## Abrupt cancellation

We Will Pay No More appears only for an independent taxpayer that is not inside a British-led faction. Its confirmation discloses immediate tax removal, the British embargo, the opinion consequences, and whether the special goal predicate applies to this country.

Once selected, remove all Event 81 claims immediately, including partially reformed taxes, Evolution levies, active commitments, temporary surcharges, and bilateral British trade privileges. Britain loses the corresponding benefit in the same outcome. Previously received fuel, convoys, or production are not magically returned, but no future instalment is collected.

The action has no arbitrary Political Power entry fee. Its cost is the hostile break. Previously invested reform effort is not refunded and does not add a second crisis reward. The country's history records that it chose an abrupt exit rather than completing the peaceful route.

## War and enforcement outcomes

An enforcement war uses the ordinary war system, alliances, access, supply, and peace mechanics. Britain may be powerful because it is financed by other taxpayers, but Event 81 does not guarantee its victory. Fighting an independent major because that country is human-controlled should be a substantial risk.

A British victory that makes the country a genuine British dependent creates compulsory liability. A peace that leaves the country independent can restore taxation only through an explicit settlement accepted as part of that peace. An ordinary ceasefire or territorial concession is not automatic consent to British Tax.

An independent loser accepting a taxation settlement returns at Full Taxation, keeps a record of previously consolidated resistance, and can use normal independent reform after a 180-day settlement period. The settlement cannot forbid independence forever. A restored subject remains subject to the hard lock. Repeated enforcement settlements cannot award opening rewards or duplicate achievements.

If Britain loses the enforcement war, a settlement can secure bilateral tax exemption and lift Event 81 embargoes and penalties against the victor. That result is distinct from worldwide abolition. Defeating a British expedition does not erase taxes paid by countries that were not covered by the agreement.

## Bilateral negotiated recognition

A sovereign taxpayer can receive explicit recognition of its earned settlement after final consolidation. A former refuser can negotiate restored trade after at least 180 days without direct hostilities and with Britain willing to resolve the dispute. Neither route imposes tax again by default.

A taxpayer accepting an ordinary rate concession remains within the system at its current consolidated stage, with the concession limited to the named domain and a stated duration or durable reform result. Britain cannot use a misleading concession popup that appears to promise independence but leaves a hidden Evolution levy.

For a British-led ally, a settlement can recognise Nominal Taxation and remove pressure. It cannot provide final Tax Independence while ENG leads. A dependent cannot open this negotiation at all.

## Worldwide abolition while Britain survives

Worldwide abolition is an explicit settlement outcome, not an automatic consequence of any British defeat. It is available after a war involving ENG has reached a peace in which Britain has capitulated and the victorious settlement authority can impose terms on it. A country that merely occupies one British overseas state does not gain this power.

The victorious authority receives an abolition demand tied to that settlement. Acceptance or an applicable imposed settlement ends the Event 81 system for every country, including British dependencies. It does not change their political independence by itself. Britain's identity and continued existence are irrelevant once the tax authority has been abolished.

A negotiated worldwide repeal is also possible when a sovereign opposition has already secured Tax Independence or a hostile-exit exemption for at least half of the initial non-British eligible industrial base, ENG is no longer receiving a meaningful network benefit, and Britain consents to a global agreement. This path gives an exhausted British player an exit without requiring annihilation. It does not let a weak foreign country unilaterally click away the whole system.

For the negotiated route, use industrial shares calculated from the initial eligible non-British economic base and adjusted only for permanent country removal and merged successor identities. Splitting a country into many tags does not create an artificial diplomatic majority. Subjects that have never become sovereign cannot supply voluntary consent to the opposition count.

## Cleanup and aftermath

A bilateral end removes only that country's Event 81 obligations and Britain's benefits attributed to it. Worldwide abolition removes all Event 81 taxes, targeted privileges, collection schedules, surcharges, continuing project costs, and event-owned enforcement claims where removable. Unrelated trade restrictions, wars, guarantees, occupations, and opinion modifiers remain.

If the engine cannot selectively remove an Event 81-created goal or embargo without also removing an unrelated identical restriction, ownership-aware handling is an explicit implementation gate. Do not advertise a complete legal settlement while the UI still offers a stale Event 81 enforcement goal.

News coverage distinguishes a peaceful national settlement, a hostile break, a British victory, and worldwide abolition. The joke remains Britain's ordinary administrative tone even when officials are forced to dismantle the entire arrangement. The economic aftermath should be immediately visible to both the former taxpayers and Britain.
