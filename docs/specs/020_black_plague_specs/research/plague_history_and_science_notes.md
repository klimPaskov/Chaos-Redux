# Plague History and Science Notes

## Research purpose

These notes ground the event in real plague transmission, historical containment, regional mortality, transport networks, and the history of medical response. The Black Plague strain itself is fictional and intentionally more dangerous than ordinary plague. The Rat Nation evolutions are supernatural alternate-history content.

## Disease forms and speed

The World Health Organization describes plague as a severe disease caused by *Yersinia pestis*. Untreated pneumonic plague can become fatal within 18 to 24 hours after onset, while early antibiotics and supportive care can be effective. This supports a design in which early recognition and treatment can save a state, while delayed recognition can create a steep mortality curve.

Design translation:

- early Incubating phase gives the player a limited response window
- treatment coverage strongly reduces deaths when it reaches people
- Severe Crisis accelerates rapidly after hospitals and administration fail
- the fictional Black Plague strain can be more lethal without making every opening death immediate

Source:

- World Health Organization, Plague fact sheet, https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/plague

## Transmission routes

The United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention lists infected flea bites, contact with contaminated tissue or fluid, and respiratory droplets from pneumonic cases among plague transmission routes. Historical plague ecology is more complicated than a single rat-only model.

Design translation:

- local vectors support state adjacency and local shelter control
- close contact supports household, hospital, troop, and refugee routes
- higher-severity respiratory behavior supports faster evolved spread
- rat states are a supernatural mutation layer, not a claim that ordinary rats alone explain every historical outbreak

Source:

- CDC, How Plague Spreads, https://www.cdc.gov/plague/causes/index.html

## Containment and contact control

A CDC review of pneumonic plague outbreaks found that documented twentieth-century outbreaks were often rapidly controlled after recognition through isolation, observation, and contact tracing. This supports strong containment mechanics with a high cost rather than a design where quarantine is cosmetic.

Design translation:

- surveillance can reveal Incubating states early
- quarantine, contact tracing, and controlled movement can stop spread
- quarantine without relief can increase deaths even when it reduces transmission
- player choices should determine whether control arrives before state collapse

Source:

- CDC Emerging Infectious Diseases, Epidemiologic Determinants for Modeling Pneumonic Plague Outbreaks, https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/10/4/03-0509_article

## Historical quarantine institutions

Historical plague control included quarantine, sanitary cordons, ships' health documentation, and dedicated isolation facilities. Dubrovnik's 1377 isolation rule and Venice's fifteenth-century lazaretto system are frequently cited examples. These measures also created economic, political, and ethical costs.

Design translation:

- border corridors, port inspections, quarantine, and isolation facilities belong in one shared response system
- port and border measures should damage trade, supply, relations, and stability
- harsh measures need relief corridors and monitoring rather than functioning as free protection

Source:

- Eugenia Tognotti, Lessons from the History of Quarantine, from Plague to Influenza A, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3559034/

## Wu Lien-teh and the Manchurian plague

Wu Lien-teh is strongly associated with the response to the 1910 to 1911 Manchurian pneumonic plague, including masks, isolation, movement control, and the management of infected bodies. His work provides a period-relevant medical response anchor.

Design translation:

- masks and protective practice can be part of treatment and personnel safety
- burial and cremation management can lower disease load and worker risk

Sources:

- World Health Organization, History as a partner in public health, Wu Lien-teh material, https://www.who.int/westernpacific/newsroom/feature-stories/item/history-as-a-partner-in-public-health
- historical review material accessible through the United States National Library of Medicine, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/

## Haffkine and plague vaccination

Waldemar Haffkine developed an early plague vaccine in Bombay in 1897. This supports a period-appropriate countermeasure route based on research, production, field use, and uneven access rather than a modern instant cure.

Design translation:

- countermeasure progress should have diagnostic, treatment, production, transmission, and cleanup milestones
- industrial production and delivery matter
- access can differ by state and country
- a complete national protocol still requires local cleanup

Source:

- historical review of Haffkine's plague vaccine, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4078488/

## Trade routes, ports, and rivers

A quantitative study of plague outbreaks in pre-industrial Europe found that major trade routes had a dominant relationship with plague spread, with ports and navigable rivers shaping outbreak geography.

Design translation:

- rail, port, convoy, river, and transport hubs should create route pressure
- good infrastructure helps medical relief and also allows uncontrolled movement
- Evolution II should use real port connections rather than random island infection
- port closure should carry a large supply and trade cost

Source:

- Yue, Lee, and Wu, Trade routes and plague transmission in pre-industrial Europe, Scientific Reports, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-13481-2

## Regional mortality variation

Palaeoecological research indicates that Black Death mortality varied substantially across European regions. Cultural, ecological, economic, social, and climatic conditions produced very different outcomes.

Design translation:

- one flat global death rate would be historically weak and poor gameplay
- state population, capacity, infrastructure, war, treatment, and local control should shape mortality
- a neglected origin can become catastrophic while a prepared state survives
- the event can support very large losses without applying identical loss everywhere

Source:

- Izdebski and colleagues, Palaeoecological data indicates land-use changes across Europe linked to spatial heterogeneity in mortality during the Black Death pandemic, Nature Ecology & Evolution, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01652-4

## Military movement and conflict

Historical disease spread often followed armies, conflict, and trade movement. War also damages the institutions needed for surveillance, treatment, and burial.

Design translation:

- frontline concentration, strategic redeployment, occupation, supply routes, and refugees increase exposure
- army cordons create a military tradeoff
- troop-route restrictions can save civilians while weakening a front
- a War Brood origin archetype is plausible within the fictional evolution

## Biological warfare precedent and safety boundary

Historical sources document attempts to use plague as a weapon, including Japanese Unit 731's plague-related biological warfare program. The specification uses that history only to justify an abstract special-project and condemnation route.

The planning package does not provide pathogen engineering, cultivation, dissemination, or operational instructions. The special project is intentionally expressed through fictional high-level choices about security, safety, command, research conflict, and strategic consequences.

Design translation:

- a long weaponization project is historically and thematically grounded
- project iterations should focus on accidents, secrecy, ethics, security, and political control
- existing game delivery systems handle deployment abstractly
- weaponization creates condemnation, retaliation, and domestic risk

Source:

- historical biological warfare review material, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1200679/

## Design conclusions

1. The outbreak should begin locally and grow through routes.
2. Recognition and treatment must matter early.
3. Quarantine should work and carry real economic and humanitarian costs.
4. Ports and troop routes should become major strategic links.
5. Mortality must vary by state and response.
6. Countermeasure progress should improve survival and cleanup without instant removal.
7. Weaponization should be slow, abstract, risky, and politically destructive.
8. Rat Nations and the Rat King should be clearly supernatural alternate-history evolutions.
