## Primary economic and historical references

These references informed the plausibility of the design. The index, thresholds, ten-day cadence, costs, recovery periods, political deadlines and scoring relationships are authored gameplay choices. They are not estimates of actual national inflation or empirically calibrated macroeconomic coefficients.

**European Central Bank, “Transmission mechanism of monetary policy.”** [Primary explainer](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/intro/transmission/html/index.en.html), retrieved 7 October 2026. The ECB describes variable and uncertain policy lags and channels through borrowing, investment, spending and expectations. The design inference is that a changed order should not instantly erase the effect of an earlier policy. This source does not justify a literal ten-day transmission period.

**Deutsche Bundesbank, “Inflation – Lehren aus der Geschichte.”** [Primary institutional article](https://www.bundesbank.de/de/aufgaben/themen/inflation-lehren-aus-der-geschichte-614516), 15 October 2012. Its discussion of wartime finance, scarce goods and the disruption of everyday exchange informed the escalating contracting and household-pressure scenes. The design uses those mechanisms as historical inspiration, without claiming that all wars or all price controls necessarily produce hyperinflation. The article's dated monetary-policy target wording is not used as current guidance.

**U.S. National Park Service, “Home Front Illicit Trade and Black Markets in World War II.”** [Primary public-history article](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/home-front-illicit-trade-and-black-markets-in-world-war-ii.htm), updated 16 November 2023. Its account of rationing, controlled prices, constrained supplies and evasion informed the distinction between temporary household protection and actual material repair. The conditional rebound is a game design inference. It is not a claim that controls always fail or that black-market activity follows a fixed numerical rule.

**Deutsches Historisches Museum, “Die Währungsreform 1923.”** [Primary museum history](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/kapitel/weimarer-republik/innenpolitik/waehrungsreform-1923), Arnulf Scriba, 6 August 2015. The account connects the introduction of the Rentenmark in November 1923 with restrictions on monetary circulation and fiscal restraint. It informed the choice to pair a currency settlement with a sustained government commitment. Its exact institutions and historical officeholders are not transplanted into every affected country.

No source supplied the exact gameplay numbers. None of these references establishes the political identity of a country's future opposition in a live campaign. That identity must come from actual game state and verified country sources.
