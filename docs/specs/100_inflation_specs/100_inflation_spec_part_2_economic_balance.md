# Inflation: economic balance

## A readable model with causes that can change

The index moves at an economic review every ten days. Current economic pressure, the stance already in effect, active programmes and any evolved rebound contribute to the movement. Immediate decisions can also change the index between reviews. Every actual change can start or interrupt recovery and can alter the political danger.

The ten-day cadence is a gameplay compression. Monetary transmission in actual economies involves uncertain lags and several demand, credit and expectations channels. The design borrows the existence of delayed consequences without assigning historical meaning to a ten-day interval. The research companion identifies the primary references and the limits of that analogy.

For design discussion, an ordinary review is:

`new index = bounded(old index + current external pressure + effective credit stance + programme effects + evolved effects)`

The external-pressure range of -10 through +20 already includes the unresolved-crisis baseline of +5. The baseline must not be added a second time. Programme effects and the bounded evolved runaway contribution are separate because they represent interventions and distinct feedback mechanisms.

## Current external pressure

The economy supplies changing inputs, not a fixed difficulty score. Use ratios so a small country and a major can face proportionate choices. Evaluate distinct causal families before applying the total cap. A shortage should not be charged again under a second label merely because two events mention it.

| Cause family | Starting contribution | What must be happening | How the player can change it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unresolved inflationary pressure | +5 | The active episode still carries its underlying monetary and contracting disturbance | A stable policy combination offsets it during the episode. Full recovery removes the episode's baseline. |
| Wartime fiscal commitment | 0, +5 or +10 | Actual war combined with a substantial military share of the functioning economy or a comparably evidenced wartime spending burden | Reduce procurement, shorten an excessive mobilization commitment, or accept restraint elsewhere. A nominal distant war alone should not always receive the highest value. |
| Material and logistical shortage | 0, +5 or +10 | Meaningful current production-resource deficits, fuel or supply disruption affecting a substantial part of useful capacity | Restore the actual input, transport connection or trade allocation. A paid decision cannot conjure a nonexistent import route. |
| Damaged productive capacity | 0 or +5 | Industrial damage is material relative to controlled usable industry | Allocate real construction to restoring the damaged capacity. Lost foreign occupation capacity is not automatically repairable domestic damage. |
| Political disruption | 0 or +5 | Current instability substantially impedes ordinary economic administration | Restore stability through the country's existing institutions and avoid repeated disruptive policy shocks. |
| Verified demand contraction | 0, -5 or -10 | An actual depression or other evidenced contraction suppresses spending and investment | Use appropriate stimulus and productive commitments. Weak economic output by itself does not prove that demand is falling. |
| Connected event burden | One relevant bounded contribution | A current owner-confirmed overheating or tax effect is materially distinct from an already counted cause | Address the originating event and the actual burden it creates. |

Useful first tuning thresholds are around 40% and 70% military commitment for the wartime ladder, 10% and 25% material shortfall for the shortage ladder, and 10% damaged industrial capacity for the damage contribution. These thresholds require suitable real game inputs. They are balance anchors, not permission to substitute a fictional treasury or an unmeasurable economy statistic.

For political disruption, stability below 35% is a reasonable first severe trigger, with relief only after a small recovery margin. A five-point hysteresis margin is the initial tuning choice. The event's own temporary stability modifier is separated from pre-existing institutional weakness when determining opening severity so the crisis does not retroactively invent a worse starting history.

An opening risk assessment groups these same causes. Two reports about the same shortage count as one cause. Two or three distinct strong families can justify +70. Failure memory can move an otherwise borderline opening up one severity step, but cannot by itself produce an immediate +100 opening.

## Credit stance and delayed commitment

The policy ladder has seven positions.

| Selected position, working label | Index effect per review once effective | Economic sacrifice while maintained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency restriction | -20 | About 20% less construction throughput and 10% less factory output |
| Strong restriction | -10 | About 10% less construction throughput and 5% less factory output |
| Moderate restriction | -5 | About 5% less construction throughput |
| Neutral | 0 | No stance-specific sacrifice |
| Moderate support | +5 | About 5% less military factory output as resources support the civilian economy |
| Strong support | +10 | About 10% less military factory output and 5% less construction throughput |
| Emergency support | +20 | About 20% less military factory output and 10% less construction throughput |

The penalties are opportunity costs attached to policy. They are not purchases of permanent economic output. Stimulus transfers scarce capacity toward sustaining demand and civilian activity, so wartime equipment production can suffer even when the price index is improving.

The ongoing stance sacrifice follows the effective stance. Selecting an easier policy cannot remove the cost of the old restriction while its final movement is still committed. When the review promotes the selected stance, the corresponding stance-cost profile changes for the following interval. The selected destination's tooltip previews that future cost. Immediate emergency replacement changes the effective stance and its current cost together, while retaining any separately incurred ninety-day emergency burden.

The ordinary tighten and ease decisions move one position and share a ten-day voluntary-change cooldown. A country at neutral therefore cannot reach emergency restriction through three immediate clicks. Both decisions show the destination and explain the lag. Selected and effective positions are described qualitatively. Their fixed effects appear in the relevant tooltips.

The review sequence is precise:

1. Account for the interval that has just elapsed under the actual price state.
2. Apply the review movement using the previously effective stance and current valid programmes.
3. Resolve any programme milestone due at that instant and classify the resulting index.
4. Reconcile stable progress, extreme exposure and political outcomes from the resulting state, with successful reprieve before a tied deadline.
5. Promote the selected stance to become effective at the following economic review.

A decision made between reviews waits through the next review. It normally first affects the index at the following one, giving a delay of just over ten to twenty days depending on when it was chosen. The tooltip explains this behavior. The review cannot silently use the newest selection for an earlier interval.

For example, a country at +20 has -20 already effective and has just selected -10. With +5 external pressure, the next review moves it to +5. The old restriction has done useful work, but a further -10 review would move it to 0 only if other pressures stay unchanged. Repeatedly selecting tighter policy near this point can carry the country below -10 before it completes recovery.

## Emergency monetary action

Extreme or imminent severe overcorrection needs an economically reachable emergency response. This action can immediately install the appropriate emergency stance or cancel a dangerous effective stance to neutral. It is available when the country is already severe or when the known next committed movement would enter a severe band. It can also cancel a clearly wrong-signed emergency commitment during a currency settlement before that commitment produces severe deflation.

The action creates a 90-day adjustment burden, a five-point stability loss capped to the actual nonnegative reserve, and a twenty-day emergency-action cooldown. Its lasting opportunity cost is approximately another 10% construction and 5% factory output loss within the existing adjustment spirit. Repeated use refreshes the costly burden and incurs a fresh actual stability loss. It does not accumulate an unlimited number of spirit slots.

The ordinary stance controls remain usable after their normal cooldown. Emergency action changes the commitment immediately, while later ordinary changes again obey the lag. This gives a tiny country a last-resort route even when it has little political power or no spare block of civilian factories. The economic sacrifice remains real at zero political power and low stability.

The access guarantee has no flat minimum resource bill. In the extreme case of zero affected throughput and zero usable stability, the measured immediate material loss can also be zero. The ninety-day burden still applies to capacity restored or acquired during that period, and the twenty-day emergency cooldown still applies. The action creates no permanent asset or Chaos reward. This proportional treatment is deliberate and must not be described as a positive payment that did not occur.

## Productive repair and demand policy do different work

A supply project helps the government remove an actual shortage or repair the productive damage behind rising prices. Fiscal restraint suppresses demand while the cause remains. Using both can make a heavily strained country recover without requiring a permanently extreme credit stance.

A successful productive project does not award an arbitrary permanent factory bonus. It verifies the material objective and removes the pressure that is no longer present. When the review already recognizes the repaired capacity or restored input, the project cannot claim a second recurring reduction for the same repair. Temporary project sacrifices end on their stated schedule.

Demand support is useful in deflation even when inflation began the episode. Tax relief, public investment and targeted credit raise the same signed index. They can become inappropriate when the index rises back toward stability. Their expiry and any committed follow-through remain visible, allowing a government to withdraw support in time.

## Severity has material consequences

The current crisis spirit uses replacement stages. Inflation damages contracting, investment and industrial coordination. Deflation suppresses investment and productive activity. Stable recovery still carries a modest residual disruption until the episode resolves.

| Current economic state | Factory output | Construction throughput | Temporary stability modifier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stable recovery | 0 | -5% | 0 |
| Inflation | -5% | -5% | 0 |
| Severe inflation | -15% | -10% | -5 percentage points |
| Hyperinflation | -30% | -25% | -15 percentage points |
| Deflation | -5% | -10% | 0 |
| Severe deflation | -20% | -30% | -10 percentage points |

These effects are substantial enough that doing nothing has a strategic cost. They are initial balance targets. The implementation should use the appropriate inspected native modifiers and confirm their stacking behavior. The design does not translate a five-point price change into a false real-world output estimate.

At most two Event 100 national-spirit slots exist. One shows current crisis severity. The other combines the active adjustment commitments and their aftermath. Its tooltip separates policy, fiscal, repair and emergency sacrifices. Moving to a new tier replaces the old tier. Multiple projects contribute to the same owned adjustment stage.

Adjustments remain noticeable but bounded. A normal policy-plus-programme combination should avoid exceeding roughly 35% construction and 25% factory output loss from the adjustment slot. An exceptional currency settlement or emergency reversal may reach a 40% construction or 30% factory-output adjustment ceiling. The crisis spirit is separate. These ceilings limit overlapping programme burdens, not the ordinary effects of war or unrelated national spirits.

The 40% construction and 30% output profile of emergency budget consolidation is the combined ceiling for that adjustment slot. It is not added to another uncapped copy of the stance or emergency penalty. Those individual obligations remain recorded and continue for their real durations when the dominant programme ends. An emergency action taken while already at the ceiling still incurs its actual stability debit and its ninety-day obligation.

## A major settlement can stop the upper runaway

Currency Crisis enables a comprehensive settlement in severe inflation. It combines a monetary break with a sustained fiscal commitment. It is one programme with a ninety-day obligation, a first settlement milestone at thirty days, and sixty days of follow-through.

While fully maintained, the programme contributes -15 at each economic review. At the thirty-day milestone it produces a further -45 correction. If the effective stance is still -10 or -20, or the underlying supply problem has not been restored, one additional -20 contraction shock occurs. The two conditions do not stack into two shocks. The programme's risk can therefore be forecast before the milestone.

That shock has a real economic aftermath even when its extra downward movement helps contain high inflation. It causes one actual stability loss of up to five points and a ninety-day burden of 10% construction and 10% factory-output loss from the milestone. The burden uses the existing combined adjustment slot and its ceiling, with its remaining duration preserved after other programmes end. Unsupported supply also leaves the damaged-currency cause in place until real restoration occurs. Deliberately accepting a hard landing is a costly choice.

At Evolution IV, its maintained hard constraint pauses the runaway contribution. The basic external pressure and the ordinary stance remain active. Abandonment restores runaway eligibility and produces the disclosed release adjustment. This is a major sacrifice that can regain control, with no promise that it will land in the stable band automatically.

The final twenty days taper the recurring programme effect as defined in Part 3. The internal forecast covers the complete known release and the ordinary stance changes needed before it, even when that requires looking farther ahead than the next two reviews. The player receives timely qualitative warnings and the policy terms without another persistent meter.

The worst ordinary upper extreme has a demonstrable route. Starting at +150 with the capped +20 external pressure, emergency restriction of -20 and the maintained settlement's -15 produces -15 per review while runaway is paused. After three reviews the index is +105 before the milestone. The -45 correction and applicable -20 shock bring it to +40. A government that began the programme promptly can then earn political reprieve. It must still manage the following restrictions and maintain stability for the longer recovery period.

This is arithmetic from the proposed model. It does not prove that AI chooses the route, that a particular country can pay every implementation cost, or that the engine executes the deadlines correctly. Those are explicit validation cases in the handoff.

## Deflation recovery and the second overshoot

A country at -90 with external pressure of -10 can still improve. Emergency support at +20 and a paid demand programme at +10 produce +20 per review. Approaching -10 calls for reducing support before the lagged intervention pushes prices back into inflation. A programme that cannot be ended instantly remains an important part of that forecast.

Severe deflation recovery does not erase the worst-severity record or shorten the stable period. A move from hyperinflation into severe deflation can therefore carry a ninety-day recovery requirement even after the government reverses course. Its history continues to describe the same badly managed episode.

The design's essential requirement is reachability in both directions. Every eligible country has a costly route to lower a rising index and a costly route to raise a falling one. The strongest combination must eventually need to be unwound. The AI and player use the same real costs, delay and episode history.
