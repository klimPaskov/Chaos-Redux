# Inflation

## A government must learn when to stop tightening

A major or player-controlled country enters a price crisis. Its first problem is rising prices, but the quickest reduction is rarely the safest recovery. Restrictive policy continues working after the government changes its mind. Spending cuts can suppress demand while damaging production. Controls can hide the pressure until shortages force prices upward again. A government that follows the index down without anticipating its own commitments can turn inflation into a deep contraction.

The player manages one signed price-pressure index. Success requires keeping it inside a stable band for a continuous period. The event therefore rewards a sequence of decisions: contain the initial rise, repair the pressures that sustain it, reduce emergency intervention before it overshoots, and maintain a workable balance while the economy recovers. Ignoring the crisis and suppressing every price increase with the strongest available measure are both dangerous strategies.

Economic collapse eventually gives the country's actual opposition an opportunity to remove the government. A plausible constitutional transfer changes the administration in the existing country. Refusal can lead to a civil war with that same opposition. The successor inherits the economy that caused the confrontation.

All named actions, programme labels, achievement concepts, incident labels and asset labels in this package are working labels, not final localisation. The event and evolution names explicitly supplied by the user retain their catalog identity.

## The price-pressure index

The index is an authored economic indicator, not a literal annual inflation percentage. A value of +100 means the event's hyperinflation state. It does not claim that the country has precisely 100% measured annual inflation. This distinction keeps the balancing problem readable without pretending that HOI4 supplies a complete monetary economy.

| State | Index | Immediate meaning for play |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Severe deflation | -100 through -60 | Demand and investment are collapsing. Stimulus and the removal of restrictive commitments take priority. |
| Deflation | Greater than -60 and below -10 | Prices are falling beyond the stable range. Continued tightening prolongs the crisis. |
| Stable prices | -10 through +10, inclusive | The continuous recovery attempt can advance while commitments still operate. |
| Inflation | Greater than +10 and below +60 | A manageable rise requires restraint and attention to the causes. |
| Severe inflation | +60 through values below +100 | Ordinary restraint becomes costly, recovery takes longer, and evolved effects become relevant. |
| Hyperinflation | +100 through +150 | Sustained extreme exposure threatens the government and may require a major settlement. |

The index is bounded at -100 and +150. Ordinary movements use five-point steps. Thresholds and durations are initial gameplay tuning anchors. Their purpose is to make the consequences of policy legible, with enough room to reverse course before a mistake becomes irreversible.

The negative and positive extremes are deliberately asymmetric. Hyperinflation has an accelerating upper tail at the highest evolution. Severe deflation remains fully dangerous, with a political-failure extreme beginning at -80. Deflation does not become a safe hiding place from an inflation event.

## How an episode begins

Each firing evaluates countries as they exist at that moment. The initial union consists of current majors and player-controlled countries. A country present in both groups appears once. It must have a valid territorial government, use the shared normal civilian-economy classification, and have room under the shared three-local-crisis cap. A small player country remains eligible.

An ongoing Inflation episode or a country-specific re-entry cooldown excludes a second opening there. The selector can find another eligible country. If the candidate pool is empty, the event is unavailable in the normal pool. An empty pool does not produce a report about an arbitrary country or a fictitious crisis.

The shared load preference gives otherwise comparable candidates more weight when they have fewer existing local crises. Event 100 contributes one counted provider throughout its active economic and political phases. An inherited continuation is retained even if a civil-war successor no longer meets the original major-or-player selection rule. Initial eligibility must not strand a country created by the event's own consequences.

Opening severity uses current economic risk, not a permanently assigned country tier.

| Opening | Typical current situation | Starting index |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Containable disturbance | Functioning industry, limited shortages, relatively calm politics and little unresolved crisis history | +30 |
| Strained economy | One strong or several moderate pressures, such as substantial wartime demand with a resource bottleneck | +50 |
| Broad crisis | Several distinct pressures, heavily damaged productive capacity, severe instability or repeated recent failures | +70 |
| Evolved severe opening | High Chaos with the relevant evolutions enabled and a currently vulnerable economy | Up to +90 |

A severe opening begins below hyperinflation so the recipient can respond. It does not pre-fill the political exposure clock. The opening report identifies the recipient's two or three most important current causes, the direction of the immediate risk, and the existence of delayed policy effects. It should not list hidden formulas or predict a future political challenger as a certainty.

## The central sequence of choices

The country receives an ordinary economic decision category. Two persistent policy decisions move the selected credit stance toward tighter or easier conditions. Contextual support decisions offer fiscal changes, productive repair, household price protection, public works and, when evolved, a currency settlement.

Credit changes have a delay. At each ten-day economic review, the stance already in effect moves the index. Only afterward does the newly selected stance become effective for the following review. The government therefore has to consider the next movement and the commitment behind it. A new order does not erase yesterday's policy.

The economic task changes as the episode develops.

| Situation | Useful question for the player |
| --- | --- |
| Opening inflation | Is the rise mainly excess demand, a supply constraint, or both? |
| Falling inflation | How much restraint is already committed, and when should it be eased? |
| Stable band | Can the present combination remain stable for the whole recovery period? |
| Deflation | Which restrictive commitments should be withdrawn, and how much support can the economy absorb? |
| Extreme prices | Can a major programme restore control before the political deadline? |
| Successor government | Which inherited obligations are still useful, and which will cause another overshoot? |

The game should answer these questions through present causes, policy direction, costs and clear consequences. Hidden bookkeeping supports the episode but does not become an additional confidence, debt, wage, reserve or legitimacy meter.

## A successful episode

The stable range must be held continuously. A mild episode requires 30 days, an episode that actually reached severe inflation or severe deflation requires 60, and an episode that reached hyperinflation or suffered a currency or severe deflation shock requires 90. The requirement is fixed at the start of each attempt from actual episode history. Leaving the band interrupts the attempt immediately.

A recovery attempt does not suspend policy. The index can cross the band under a powerful old commitment and continue into deflation before recovery finishes. The player succeeds by establishing a combination that keeps prices stable throughout the required period.

Recovery removes the active crisis and its shared load claim. Paid commitments finish or wind down under the terms shown when they were accepted. The reward is restored economic function, political survival where still possible, and a bounded reduction in the Chaos attributable to the episode. Recovery does not award permanent factories or a permanent statistical bonus for repeatedly inviting inflation.

## A failed episode

Thirty cumulative days at +100 or above, or -80 or below, can produce a political ultimatum. Severe but briefly non-extreme prices do not erase that exposure. The warning gives a complete displayed deadline and a chance to earn reprieve through sustained economic improvement.

At failure, the strongest plausible actual opposition has the greatest ordinary chance of becoming the challenger. The same selected movement leads a peaceful transfer or an armed challenge. Popularity, institutions, organization, territorial feasibility and existing country politics determine what is credible. Political consequences must not replace a real country's identity with an unrelated generic nation.

A successor receives time to govern, with 90 days before fresh actionable extreme exposure can begin. Economic penalties, delayed stances, damaged industry and outstanding commitments remain. A civil war divides extensive assets and obligations between its sides. It does not duplicate the predecessor's stockpiles or create two fully equipped copies of its army.

## What makes repeat play different

Recurrence uses the country's new situation. A former major can become ineligible for a new random opening, a new player country can enter the pool, war can change a previously calm economy, and a restored country can inherit unresolved historical weakness through a valid identity relationship.

Two bounded hidden failure-memory steps make repeated failure more dangerous. They influence starting risk, institutional patience and the later re-entry interval. Genuine recovery followed by sustained calm can remove them. Successful play never permanently immunizes a country from another crisis.

The four cumulative evolutions deepen the same balancing problem. Wage-Price Spiral makes suppressed pressure rebound. Currency Crisis adds a costly confidence-restoration programme and contraction risk. Economic Radicalization strengthens plausible opposition during severe hardship. Hyperinflation accelerates the upper extreme while leaving recovery possible through major sacrifice and sustained control.
