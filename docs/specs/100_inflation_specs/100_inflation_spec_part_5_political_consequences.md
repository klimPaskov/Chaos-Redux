# Inflation: political consequences

Economic collapse threatens the government when extreme prices continue beyond the country's capacity to restore control.
The opposition comes from the country's actual political situation, and the same selected movement leads a peaceful transfer or challenges a government that refuses to leave.
The economy continues through the political change: a new party inherits the price crisis, damaged production, existing commitments, and the consequences of previous failures.
Names in this part identify mechanics and writing direction.

## The political ultimatum

Thirty cumulative days at a price index of +100 or above, or −80 or below, start a political ultimatum.
Extreme exposure is retained while the country remains in either severe band, so brief movement away from the extreme cannot erase its earlier failure.
Thirty consecutive days outside both severe bands clear that exposure.
Outside both severe bands means strictly above −60 and strictly below +60. The boundary values themselves remain severe.

The ultimatum normally lasts 30 days.
Severe institutional weakness or repeated failure shortens it to 20 days, while functioning institutions and recent real improvement can justify 40 days.
The deadline is frozen when issued.
Its report explains who is demanding a change, why the government has reached this point, the deadline, the improvement needed to stop it, and the consequence of continued failure.

Twenty continuous days outside both severe bands stop the ultimatum, provided that the known committed next movement does not return the index directly to an extreme. The forecast includes effective and selected stances, due programme corrections and releases, and the next unapplied review in its actual worker phase.
This political reprieve does not complete economic recovery.
The country still needs its required continuous period inside the stable band of −10 through +10, including the longer recovery requirement after severe or extreme damage.
Reprieve is evaluated before political failure when its completion and deadline expiry share the same timestamp.

One earned extension protects a country that has already made sustained progress.
If expiry arrives after at least 10 continuous nonsevere days and the delayed-policy forecast remains nonsevere, extend the deadline by exactly the remaining time needed to complete the 20-day reprieve condition, up to 10 additional days.
Explain the achieved improvement and the revised deadline visibly.
An ultimatum receives this extension only once.
If the nonsevere streak breaks during the extension, continuity resets and the extension cannot be granted again.
Political payments, promises, and repeated requests cannot move the deadline.

A stopped ultimatum cannot reopen from retained exposure alone.
Renewed extreme exposure can create another warning while the old exposure remains, but it must provide the complete new deadline.
An index jump or delayed update cannot create an already-expired warning.
When a deadline expires without reprieve or an earned extension, commit one political failure and resolve it once.

The political objective takes the recovery objective's visible slot.
Stable progress continues internally under its normal continuity rules and becomes visible again after reprieve.
The maximum public quantity count remains four: the signed price index, two distinct support-project timers, and one recovery-or-political timer.
Extreme exposure and the live reprieve streak remain internal.

## Choosing the actual opposition

At political failure, build the candidate pool from current non-ruling parties and explicitly established domestic movements.
Candidates need an actual political identity, a valid leader or verified succession route, and a plausible means of forming a government or mounting the relevant challenge.
Current popularity provides the main measure of strength.
Existing organization, parliamentary standing, regional roots, and command support can modify that assessment where the country actually represents them.

An ordinary candidate requires at least 5% support.
A stronger native organization below that floor may qualify only when the current country provides concrete evidence of its organization and accession route.
An empty party label, an extinct movement, an unavailable leader, or an invented faction cannot supply that exception.
The largest plausible opposition should usually lead, while another substantial movement can sometimes prevail.
The weighting must strongly favor real support and give invalid choices no chance. A nominal baseline must not make negligible movements competitive with major parties.

Coalition support counts only when an existing country mechanism supports the actual partners and their government formation.
Its leading party must be identifiable.
Unrelated opposition groups cannot be combined into an invented organization merely because their popularity totals are convenient.

The warning may identify the leading movement at that moment, but the actual challenger is selected and frozen when political failure is committed.
That identity remains the same through concession, refusal, or a forced succession.
Report choices and repeated callbacks do not reroll it.
If its leader becomes unavailable during resolution, use the same party's valid succession rules.
If another legitimate political event has already brought that party into government, revalidate the current situation instead of overthrowing it with a stale consequence.

Same-ideology opposition is eligible only when the country already recognizes a distinct rival party or movement and a route by which it can take power.
A different minister or personal leader within the incumbent party does not constitute the party takeover required here.
A cabinet reshuffle can describe an institutional response during the warning, but it cannot satisfy an opposition-transfer outcome or its achievement conditions.

## Concession and refusal

A peaceful transfer requires functioning means of accession and a government willing to use them.
Elections, parliamentary replacement, negotiated resignation, or another established constitutional route can provide that basis.
Ideology alone does not establish whether the institutions work.
Current support, wartime pressure, control of the armed forces, previous repression, and active political routes influence the incumbent's willingness and the opposition's prospects.

Where concession is plausible, the incumbent can transfer office to the selected opposition.
The country retains its national identity and existing territorial government while its actual party and valid leader change.
Current party support is preserved unless the established accession route explicitly changes it.
The new government receives time to act through the 90-day political reprieve, while economic penalties, policy commitments, and the recovery objective remain in force.

A government that refuses faces the same opposition in a native civil war when the country can sustain a valid split.
Dictatorships have a stronger refusal preference, but surrender remains possible where their actual political route allows it and resistance has become untenable.
A constitutional government with failing institutions can also resist or fracture.
Neither ideology grants immunity from the economic crisis nor automatically starts a war.

The political choice must explain the selected movement, the expected succession or conflict, and the inherited economic burden.
Concession changes who governs. Refusal risks losing the government and the country through fighting.
These consequences remain in force after the ultimatum resolves.
Economic interventions remain available until their own conditions end them.

If the native civil-war flow lets the player join the opposition, present that as an explicit choice with the country's actual supported control-transfer behavior.
Otherwise the player remains with the government they currently control.
Changing political sides does not reset the economy or generate a second set of resources.

## A civil war within the existing nation

The opposition is a domestic claimant to the affected country.
It uses that nation's existing political identities, eligible characters, appropriate flags, equipment, military voices, and unit graphics.
Its initial fighting strength comes from inherited national forces, followed by ordinary recruitment and production using its own territory and resources.

The split must provide two functioning territorial governments.
Each side needs valid controlled land, a capital, supply, and usable access for its inherited forces.
Where the country records opposition roots or regional political organization, use that evidence to shape the front.
Otherwise use the country's supported native civil-war allocation without inventing a historical constituency.
The distribution should produce coherent holdings, a playable front and a capital connected to useful territory.

Opposition strength follows its actual support and organization, adjusted by established command loyalty and the incumbent's remaining control.
An economic extreme does not by itself give a minor movement most of the army.
The conflict redistributes the existing military and industrial base. Ordinary national recruitment and production determine subsequent growth.

A starting military-allocation anchor is the selected opposition's current support percentage, bounded between 20% and 60% of transferable regular strength. Verified organization or command loyalty may shift that share by five or ten percentage points within the same bounds. Round only through the native whole-unit allocation rules and preserve material totals. The 20% lower anchor provides a potentially viable challenger without granting it most of the army. It is not an equipment-generation allowance.

Territory should aim at a comparable share of useful controlled domestic capacity while respecting actual opposition roots, coherent holdings and legal capitals. Whole-state geography can prevent an exact percentage, so choose the closest viable native split and record its actual industrial and military result. Air and naval inheritance follow validated national command and basing rules, using the common share only when no more specific current-country evidence exists. A country that cannot support two viable governments uses its defined domestic-succession result.

Research, supported production capabilities, and the existing national focus-tree identity continue through the appropriate native inheritance route.
Completed focuses do not fire their rewards again.
A running focus cannot become two paid completion projects through the split.
The continuing government retains its active project unless its political route invalidates it, while the challenger receives a valid completed-focus history without a duplicated pending reward.
Reunification preserves the victor's valid progression and resolves incompatible branches through the country's established political rules.

Existing foreign wars, faction relationships, subjects, volunteers, expeditionary forces, and military access retain their appropriate native treatment.
The internal crisis cannot erase an external war or create unrelated territorial claims and international ambitions.
Any constitutional, diplomatic, or election-rule change must be a deliberate consequence of the actual accession route.
The normal shared systems retain responsibility for Chaos caused by war and ideology changes, so the same rupture is not charged again as an identical economic consequence.

## Material conservation

The civil-war split redistributes the predecessor's deployed forces and national resources.
Both sides' combined inheritance must account for free and deployed manpower, garrisons, equipment reserves and variants, aircraft, ships, fuel, trains, convoys, factories, and production commitments.
Equipment inside a transferred formation cannot simultaneously appear as a fresh stockpile on its new side.
The predecessor's population and industrial capacity cannot be copied into two complete national economies.

Characters and commanders follow the country's legitimate assignment and succession rules.
A unique commander cannot become two independently available copies simply because the country split.
Account for any native losses, destroyed equipment, or deliberate industrial damage as actual consequences of the transition. Record their cause and quantity.

Subsequent recruitment draws from each side's real manpower and equipment.
Its factories produce replacement material under ordinary rules and the inherited economic penalties.
Captured stocks and transferred units change ownership once.
Repeated reports, reopened decisions, save restoration, or reunification cannot grant the initial inheritance a second time.

## The economy after a change of government

A peaceful successor inherits the current index, worst realized severity, active and lagged credit stance, support projects, historical paid-transaction quotations, paid costs, and remaining obligations. Any new payment receives a fresh current quote. Historical quotes preserve refund and audit facts. Every future debit requires a validated current quote.
The restrictive stance still due at the next review remains due after the party changes.
A project that already delivered its effect does not become refundable through political succession.
If a government-specific commitment becomes impossible, apply its established cancellation terms and retain the effects already incurred.

Both civil-war sides inherit the predecessor's price-pressure intensity and actual economic damage.
The index measures an economic condition, so both can begin at the same value.
Factory reservations, material obligations, repair targets, and other divisible commitments follow actual capacity, control, and ownership.
An already-paid national benefit cannot be copied in full where that would double its material result.
Each side's later reviews use its own war burden, shortages, controlled industry, policies, and feasible support commitments.

The continuations share one episode lineage, with a distinct current country owner for each live economy.
The original failure is consumed once across the family.
Each side retains the relevant severity and failure history, while unrelated crisis claims are not blindly duplicated.
An inherited continuation remains active even when a civil-war side is no longer a major or player-controlled country.
The initial targeting restriction must not abandon a crisis created by Event 100's own political outcome.

Reunification consolidates the continuing economy and reconciles inherited obligations through real control and remaining commitments.
It cannot duplicate failure memory, pay one recovery reward twice, or recognize several achievements for a single political transaction.
The economy still requires genuine stabilization under the normal recovery rules.

## Protection from repeated political failure

A completed transfer or split gives the successor 90 days of political reprieve.
The original consumed failure cannot act against either continuation again.
Economic damage, management, policy lag, and recovery requirements remain active throughout that period.
Fresh actionable extreme exposure begins only after the reprieve ends.
Another political attempt then requires 30 fresh extreme days and its complete frozen ultimatum deadline.

An unresolved civil war blocks a further Event 100 split within the same lineage.
Where its actual challenger and political owner match the new backlash, apply the consequence within that existing conflict.
If a compatible transfer to the selected actual opposition is necessary, use the valid national succession route while preserving the existing war.
A locked political owner uses the bounded pending-aftermath rule below.

A later distinct political failure receives a new failure identity and a new resolution.
Hidden failure memory remains capped at two steps, and reunification does not add multiple copies of the same history.
The country re-entry cooldown begins when the economic episode actually ends: normally 180 days, extended to 270 after political rupture, with the accepted severity and history scaling within 120–360 days.
A party change alone does not finish the economic crisis or open a fresh episode.

## Invalid splits and bounded political aftermath

When the country cannot sustain a civil-war split but the selected actual opposition is valid, transfer national authority through a supported constitutional route or a coercive domestic succession.
Use the same selected party and its valid leader in the existing country.
A forced opposition cabinet is a real change of governing party, not a cosmetic minister reshuffle.
Classify a coercive succession openly as coercive and preserve political-rupture history and economic damage.
It creates no rebel tag and is not recorded as a civil war.

Pending aftermath is reserved for an absent valid opposition party or an active country-owned political transition that prevents a compatible transfer.
It lasts at most 30 days.
Economic management and penalties continue, and the original political failure remains recorded.
This pending state adds no extra Chaos and cannot repeatedly add failure-memory steps or issue new failure rewards.

Reevaluate after a meaningful change in actual political support, valid leadership, territorial feasibility, or the owning political route, and once at the end of the 30-day period.
A newly feasible consequence uses the current valid opposition and resolves the existing failure once.
It does not create a second economic episode or restart a full political ultimatum.

If a valid political change is still impossible at the final check, close the political transaction as government survival under emergency aftermath.
Apply an additional temporary 10-point stability penalty for 90 days through the existing adjustment/aftermath spirit.
The event retains its maximum of two spirit slots.
Expiry removes this temporary contribution without restoring unrelated losses or clearing economic damage.

This exception grants no political success, economic recovery, or achievement.
Preserve failure memory, the price crisis, current obligations, and ordinary recovery requirements.
The government receives a 90-day political reprieve from closure of that transaction, followed by the requirement for 30 fresh extreme days before another ultimatum can begin.
The exception is limited to the country's actual lack of a viable party or compatible political transition. An unsplittable map with a valid opposition uses domestic succession instead.

## Country-state handling

| Current country situation | Political and economic result |
| --- | --- |
| Functioning territorial government | Select the actual opposition and resolve concession, refusal, or a valid native civil war. |
| One-state or otherwise unsplittable government | Use constitutional or coercive succession to the same selected actual opposition. |
| No valid party or locked country-owned transition | Enter the bounded 30-day pending aftermath, followed by the defined emergency-survival outcome if still impossible. |
| Existing civil war | Preserve the current conflict and its owner. Resolve compatible backlash or succession without another generic split. |
| Subject country | Preserve the appropriate subject relationship and use its established national or overlord political route. |
| Government in exile or without territorial authority | Suspend unavailable domestic actions and resolution, release invalid active ownership as required, and preserve a non-success history. |
| Annexation or disappearance | End invalid active ownership without stabilization credit and retain the relevant durable failure record. |
| Later restoration or release | Use the restored country's current economy and normal eligibility while retaining verified relevant failure memory. |
| Transition into a nonhuman country | Apply the shared civilian-system classification and close invalid ordinary political and economic ownership coherently. |

## Opposition growth, AI, and presentation

Evolution III strengthens plausible actual opposition during prolonged severe hardship through existing political support.
Growth reflects loss of confidence in the incumbent or other current parties, remains bounded, and slows when the hardship eases.
A party's established economic program can justify a stronger response where that program is actually represented.
Ideology alone does not prove that every movement promises radical economic change.

The incumbent AI prioritizes an economically achievable reprieve while the ultimatum remains open.
At failure it weighs actual opposition strength, functioning accession routes, command loyalty, and the cost of another front.
Dictatorial refusal remains a preference shaped by circumstances.
The successor reviews inherited economic policy immediately, particularly a pending restriction that could turn improvement into severe deflation.
It pays the same costs, experiences the same lag, and uses the same economic information as a player.

Reports distinguish the warning, concession, refusal, civil-war onset, coercive succession, pending aftermath, and emergency survival.
Each explanation identifies the actual political actors and what the country must manage next.
The existing economic category supplies contextual actions and the current objective.
Two support commitments and one objective may be tracked alongside the signed index. Exposure and reprieve continuity add no extra public meter.

Use the country's valid existing leaders, portraits, flag sets, equipment, voices, and military graphics.
An actually required missing historical portrait needs an attributed source and the established portrait-production process.
An invented likeness or generic new ruler cannot replace an available political identity.
Writing and art should make the transition legible while keeping attention on the damaged economy the new authorities have inherited.
