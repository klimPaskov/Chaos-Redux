# Inflation: decisions and missions

## A small set of relevant choices

The category presents three to five primary actions in ordinary play and never more than six in a phase. Two positions belong to tightening and easing credit. The remaining positions expose the fiscal, productive and household choices that matter in the country's current situation. An emergency monetary action replaces a less urgent entry when the forecast calls for it.

Related alternatives use a temporary selection state in the same ordinary native decision category. Entering it spends nothing. The list shows the two credit decisions, up to three specific programme decisions and a return entry, keeping the maximum at six. Every specific variant has its own native decision consumer and complete quoted cost. Accepting it commits the payment, effects and duration once, then returns to the main list.

The major-containment selection shows emergency consolidation and enabled currency settlement alongside the two stance entries and return. Household and productive selections follow the same pattern with their relevant alternatives. The return entry uses an inspected existing navigation symbol. A selection entry can reuse a suitable icon from its own decision family. This gives each of the fifteen planned action subjects a real consumer without putting every variant on the main list. Context invalidation returns to the main list without payment or a timer reset.

There are two support-project slots. The institutional slot carries spending cuts, taxation, procurement restraint, tax relief, price controls, a wage-price agreement or a currency settlement. The productive slot carries industrial repair, bottleneck relief, targeted credit or public investment. The main credit stance exists alongside them. Programmes competing for the same slot cannot run together.

An active programme's row becomes its progress and commitment review. It does not disappear merely because prices cross a band. A reversal can make an inherited commitment undesirable, which is a reason to manage or cancel it under its real terms. Cancellation does not convert a consumed benefit into a refundable purchase.

## Primary actions by situation

| Current phase | Primary category entries | Contextual selection behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mild or moderate inflation | Tighten, ease, fiscal restraint, productive repair, household protection | Fiscal selection compares spending, taxation and procurement where each is genuinely relevant. |
| Severe inflation | Tighten, ease, fiscal programme, productive repair, currency settlement if enabled, emergency monetary action when justified | Household protection remains available through the institutional programme selection. It does not become a seventh primary row. |
| Hyperinflation | Tighten, ease, major containment, productive repair, active commitment review, emergency monetary action | Major containment offers the costly emergency budget route and enabled currency settlement as competing programmes. |
| Stable attempt | Tighten, ease, active institutional commitment, active productive commitment | Unneeded new crisis programmes withdraw. Relevant orderly release or cancellation choices remain. |
| Deflation | Tighten, ease, tax relief, targeted credit or public investment, emergency monetary action when justified | A real industrial repair can replace the productive expansion offer when physical damage is the stronger cause. |
| Political ultimatum | The current economic entries, with the political deadline occupying the objective slot | Government concession or refusal uses the political report. It does not hide the economic rescue choices. |
| Inherited crisis | The current economic entries plus the inherited commitment report | The government can inspect and change inherited policy immediately under the same payment and lag rules. |

The full decision family remains documented in Event Details. Context should explain why an alternative is unavailable. It should not rotate arbitrarily or withhold stimulus just because inflation was the original premise.

## Credit decisions

**D01, tighten credit**, moves the selected stance one step toward restriction. It is available during an active episode when the selected stance is above the tightest position and the shared ten-day ordinary-policy cooldown has elapsed. It does not require political power. Its real cost is the industrial sacrifice of the destination stance. The next review still uses the old effective stance.

**D02, ease credit**, moves one step toward support under the same cooldown. In inflation, easing can be the correct way to avoid overshoot. In deflation, it is the ordinary route toward stimulus. A player may deliberately choose the wrong direction, with the forecast and consequence visible. AI willingness for a clearly worsening ordinary move should approach zero unless an imminent committed shock makes that direction rational.

Both actions remain available during stable attempts and political warnings. They cannot reset the review date, recovery progress, external pressure or historical severity. They share one cooldown so alternating them cannot promote policy twice within a single review interval.

## Institutional programmes

The values below are starting balance targets. A one-time loss of five stability points means five percentage points of the actual country reserve. Output and construction penalties are ongoing opportunity costs, not debited currencies. Ordinary fees require full affordability. The explicitly identified emergency routes cap their stability debit to the actual nonnegative reserve while retaining their full industrial and time sacrifice.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| D03B, emergency budget consolidation | Index at least +80, or a known next review at least +100, 90 days | -30 immediately, then -15 per review. Maintained programme pauses evolved runaway. | About -40% construction and -30% factory output, plus a one-time stability loss up to 10 points |
| D04, temporary higher taxation | Inflation above +10, functioning territorial administration, 60 days | -10 per review | 25 political power and 5 stability points on acceptance, with about -10% factory output and -5% construction |

| D06, tax relief | Index below -10, 60 days | +5 per review for the first 30 days, then +10 per review for the last 30 | About -10% construction and -5% military factory output as the revenue reduction is supported |
| D09, price controls | Index at least +30 with a relevant shortage or household-price problem, 30 days before release | -15 immediately, with no general recurring reduction. Conditional release pressure follows. | About -5% factory output and -5% construction while enforced |
| D10, wage-price agreement | Evolution I with outstanding rebound or an active supply/war cause, 30 days | Prevents new wage liability and retires up to 5 points of existing liability per review | About -5% factory output, with the agreement maintained for its full term |

### Choosing among restraint programmes

Spending reductions are the ordinary accessible fiscal route. They make the government less able to build and repair while reducing demand. Higher taxation suppresses demand faster but places a more direct political and productive burden on society. Procurement restraint protects civilian activity by accepting weaker military production and lower war support. The alternatives should differ in strategic consequence, not merely in how many political-power points they cost.

Programme effects do not automatically rewrite economic laws or national focuses. The country's existing law changes remain meaningful separate actions. If an actual allocation change has already removed a pressure contribution, the programme must not receive a duplicate reduction for that same allocation under another name.

Emergency budget consolidation is a drastic extension of D03. It is available at every evolution configuration. At Evolution IV its maintained hard constraint halts the runaway term, so disabling Currency Crisis cannot remove the recovery route. Its immediate -30 correction represents a sudden contraction in demand and contracts. It can overshoot if adopted just before another large correction.

At +150, with +20 capped external pressure and an already effective -20 stance, consolidation opens at +120 and moves through +105, +90, +75, +60 and +45 over the next five reviews. Used promptly, it leaves the extreme band before accumulating thirty extreme days. The construction and military-output sacrifice is exceptionally large for ninety days. Its exceptional sacrifice makes it appropriate when immediate containment becomes necessary.

Emergency consolidation and currency settlement use the same institutional slot. Their recurring effects and runaway pause cannot stack. Switching from one to the other requires the old programme's explicit settlement or cancellation. The immediate consolidation correction is consumed once and cannot be renewed by reopening the report.

### Tax relief and delayed support

Tax relief begins gently and becomes stronger after thirty days as the stimulus reaches the economy. Its later +10 effect is disclosed when accepted. The player therefore has to consider the programme's second phase when credit is already easing.

If the economy becomes inflationary, the government can begin an orderly withdrawal under the ordinary programme rules below. The original opportunity-cost obligation remains due through its original date. Withdrawal gives no new downward index impulse and no refund. Early emergency cancellation is available when waiting would create a severe opposite crisis.

### Controls and negotiated release

Price controls protect households while suppressing part of the visible price increase. They do not create goods or repair production. When the underlying shortage remains at expiry, the baseline release adjustment is +10 once. If the cause was genuinely repaired, that baseline release is zero. The decision explains this condition before acceptance.

Evolution I adds the bounded wage-price liability described in Part 6. That additional accumulated obligation is settled through its stated installments. It is not charged again as a second copy of the baseline +10 release. The evolution distinguishes wage repricing from the release of the original price restriction.

The government can convert an active control programme into a wage-price agreement in the same institutional slot. It does not receive a second -15 correction or reset the original release obligation. The agreement prevents further liability and retires the permitted amount while employers and workers accept temporary constraints. An agreement with no actual liability and no relevant protective purpose should not be an attractive AI purchase.

Only one price-control correction is permitted in a rolling ninety-day period within the episode. Renewing the order preserves its existing liability and settlement schedule. This prevents cheap repeated suppression while allowing a genuine continuing protective policy.

### Currency settlement and its two milestones

At acceptance, the settlement begins the ninety-day fiscal commitment. A supply assessment records whether capacity is adequate or whether an actual repair obligation remains. A country with damaged industry can proceed through the explicitly disclosed risky route, but that route carries the additional contraction shock if restoration is still unsupported at day thirty.

The day-thirty correction uses the stance that actually applied at that review. A newly selected easier stance that has not yet become effective cannot cancel the shock. Strong restriction and unsupported supply restoration trigger one additional -20 correction through an OR condition. They do not produce two separate penalties.

The conditional shock also causes one actual stability loss up to five points and ninety days of 10% construction and 10% factory-output loss from its milestone. It shares the capped adjustment spirit, with the obligation's actual remaining duration preserved after other programmes end. Its material cost is disclosed even when the extra -20 price movement is desirable. Unsupported restoration leaves the damaged-currency pressure until the real objective is met.

The remaining sixty days sustain the programme contribution, with the final twenty-day taper described below.
The government may need to ease credit or add a productive expansion while continuing to pay for the settlement.
Supported first-phase completion at day thirty removes the continuing damaged-currency pressure, as defined in Part 6, while the paid fiscal follow-through continues.
Recovery history still records that a currency crisis occurred.

The single programme timer first counts to the thirty-day settlement milestone and then counts the remaining sixty days. The full ninety-day obligation is disclosed at acceptance. This makes the dangerous correction visible without adding a second simultaneous currency timer.

An unsupported first phase is an explicit risky outcome. It receives the correction and the disclosed shock, but retains the damaged-currency cause until the actual restoration requirement is later met. A funding interruption before the milestone receives no correction. Neither result creates a refund or a fresh settlement opportunity for the same paid phase.

## Productive and demand projects

| ID and working label | Entry and duration | Objective and economic result | Real commitment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D07, industrial repair | Material damaged controlled industrial capacity, up to 60 days | Restore at least half the snapshot's selected damaged industrial levels, rounding up. The repaired cause ceases to contribute when actually removed. | About -15% construction elsewhere while repair allocation receives a targeted 20% repair-speed improvement |
| D08, bottleneck relief | A current material resource or transport bottleneck, up to 40 days | Reduce the selected shortage to no more than half its opening severity, or remove it entirely when already small. Actual trade, transport or production inputs must change. | Freight route spends quoted existing trucks or trains where useful. Domestic reallocation instead accepts about -20% construction for the term. |
| D12, targeted credit | Index below -10, 40 days | +5 per review, beginning at the next review, to support functioning productive borrowers | About -5% military factory output and -5% construction |
| D13, public investment | Index below -10 or a validated recovering demand crisis, 60 days | +10 per review after the first ten days, with an actual domestic works commitment | About -15% military factory output and -10% construction, with no permanent free building award |

Industrial repair identifies up to five relevant controlled states, prioritizing the most consequential repairable industrial damage. It does not scan the world from the visible decision. A starting repair target of one damaged level requires that level to be repaired. A country that loses a target to occupation must revalidate its remaining objective and receive an honest progress result.

If the physical repair completes early, the material result is recognized immediately. The accepted minimum thirty-day allocation remains paid, so a nearly finished building cannot create a one-day free programme. If the sixty-day deadline arrives without restoration, the project ends with a failed material objective and no extra price reduction. Actual completed repair remains useful because it is real capacity, not a revocable prize.

Bottleneck relief offers a freight commitment only where the relevant equipment exists and the transport problem can actually use it. Initial quoted anchors are 25 trucks or 5 trains for a small affected industrial footprint, scaled in bounded five-unit steps with that footprint. These are alternative costs, never a demand for both merely to make the tooltip look substantial. Equipment is committed or consumed according to the inspected native route and its receipt.

A small country without suitable equipment can use domestic reallocation. That route makes a real construction sacrifice while the player changes the trade or production allocation that caused the shortage. Neither route gives unavailable resources, bypasses a blockade, invents foreign access or guarantees repair of an impossible connection. If the selected cause is not addressable through either route, the decision explains the material obstruction and fiscal or credit measures remain available.

Targeted credit is a smaller, shorter response than public investment. It suits a shallow overcorrection or an inherited restriction that is already being eased. Public investment is a larger commitment and reaches the index after one review. The latter can therefore restart inflation if stacked with emergency support and tax relief after demand has recovered.

## D14, emergency monetary action

This is the immediately effective action described in Part 2. Its selection offers only the appropriate emergency direction and cancellation of a dangerously wrong-signed effective stance. It carries the actual stability debit, ninety-day adjustment burden and twenty-day emergency cooldown.

An AI or player does not receive a free reversal because the ordinary credit ladder takes time. The emergency report identifies the old commitment it will replace, the resulting effective stance and the industrial cost. It leaves paid support programmes intact. Ordinary stance changes after it again follow the normal lag.

## Commitment review, cancellation and expiry

An active programme offers continued execution and, where meaningful, an orderly exit. This review is a management surface for the existing programme, not another paid project or a fresh opportunity to receive its opening effect.

Ordinary restraint or stimulus can begin an orderly exit after thirty days have been paid. A recurring effect of -5 or +5 ends at the next unapplied review. A recurring effect of -10 or +10 becomes -5 or +5 for one review and then ends. The original contracted opportunity cost remains due through its original date. There is no extra price impulse and no fractional two-and-a-half-point movement.

Early emergency cancellation of an ordinary programme ends its future recurring index effect at the next unapplied review while preserving the original contracted cost through the original due date. It gives no refund or new price impulse, except an explicitly owed price-control release. The active slot becomes free only when the index effect actually ends. An emergency stance action can address immediate monetary danger while that cancellation is pending.

Emergency consolidation and currency settlement are ninety-day commitments. Their scheduled expiry is previewed at least two reviews in advance. During the final twenty days, recurring programme pressure tapers from -15 to -10 and then -5 before ending. The main credit stance still operates, so the government can replace a disappearing fiscal effect with an appropriate ordinary policy.

For currency settlement, the accepted description of sixty-day follow-through includes this final twenty-day taper. Runaway remains paused while the paid major programme remains valid, then returns only if the index is still in the hyperinflation range. Expiry at stable prices cannot create runaway by itself.

For a programme accepted on a review date, day 70 still applies -15 before its taper milestone. Day 80 applies -10 and day 90 applies -5, followed by expiry. A programme accepted between reviews uses the same absolute milestone dates and the next unapplied review. The internal forecast includes the whole known release and every required stance cooldown and promotion date. A two-review display preview is not the limit of AI planning.

Abrupt cancellation of a major programme retains its original economic obligation through the original ninety-day due date, ends future unearned index effects, and applies a one-time +10 release pressure. If currency damage remains, it remains a cause. The day-thirty correction cannot be replayed after a canceled or completed settlement. Currency settlement and emergency consolidation share a 120-day major-programme reuse interval from the previous acceptance. Canceling one cannot unlock the other for another opening correction.

When a programme becomes impossible through territory loss, succession or another owner's action, use that programme's actual completion and payment record. A lost repair target cannot still award full restoration. An already consumed stability loss is not refundable. Returned equipment cannot exceed the original actual payment less what was genuinely used or lost. Economic success and political succession are never general refund events.

## Missions with consequences

| Objective, working label | Completion | Interruption or failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous stabilization | Hold -10 through +10 for the episode's current 30, 60 or 90-day requirement | Any excursion resets the continuous attempt. Paid policies remain in effect. |
| Productive restoration | Meet the actual repair or shortage target within its project term | Missing the target ends that project's special support without a fictitious repair reward. |
| Institutional programme | Complete the stated paid phases, including settlement and follow-through where applicable | Cancellation and funding failure use their disclosed release and aftermath rules. |
| Political ultimatum | Earn the defined twenty-day nonsevere reprieve before the deadline, including the earned-extension rule | Failure commits the actual political consequence once. |

The project timers and one current recovery-or-political objective give at most three active mission surfaces. The signed index brings the conservative public-quantity count to four. Every timer has a distinct decision attached to it. The short crisis periods are deliberate emergency pacing, while the major programmes and severe recovery provide sustained commitments.

## Payment and explanation quality

The current quote, affordability gate, displayed cost and actual debit must describe the same transaction. A paid selection rechecks the quote immediately before commitment. A stale selection cannot reserve a slot, spend twice or silently change the programme into another one.

No action spends more than four resource types. These policies normally spend zero, one or two. The compact inline cost presents at most three values, with the complete tooltip naming every cost and distinguishing temporary output losses from actual payment. Unaffordable choices explain the missing resource. Emergency capped-stability routes disclose the actual amount paid and still impose their full economic sacrifice.

Every action's final text must explain the current economic purpose, delayed or immediate effect, real cost, duration and relevant expiry risk. Reports can use restrained irony about administrative confidence where appropriate, but humor must not hide a policy's consequence or mock people experiencing the crisis.
