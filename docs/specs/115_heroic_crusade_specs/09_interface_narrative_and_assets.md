# Interface, narrative, and visual direction

## Presentation choice

Use ordinary decision categories with a simple formatted bloc-cohesion value and phase-filtered actions. A static category picture is eligible because this design has no dedicated interactive panel, meter control, state puzzle, custom tabs, or animation in that category. The cohesion display is a number and a short condition, not an additional interactive meter.

Use one current-role category picture at a time, selected for founding or command, protection and relief, or settlement. Keep its dimensions and layout tied to the current category-picture reference consumer. A final pixel size cannot be invented before that consumer is inspected. The asset prompt names the exact reference workflow.

Membership, charter versions, known targets, surviving governments, current readiness and historical outcomes belong in the shared Event Details framework using its established extension points. The event must not redesign Event Details, the event log, global settings, world-end settings, super-events, or shared registries under an event-UI task.

Camp Administration keeps its three existing tabs, Administration, Locations and Records. Migration, Resistance and Condemnation retain their own controls. A link opens the correct owner context. It cannot issue a second copy of the same order or imply that a row displayed by Event 115 owns that shared transaction.

## Information hierarchy

The decision view begins with the current actor's role, the selected charter and the most urgent current problem. Cohesion and its main current causes follow. Up to six context-relevant actions and one to three active missions occupy the remaining event control budget. A player should be able to identify whether they are choosing a political commitment, supplying a military operation, or handling a shared civilian obligation before opening a tooltip.

The charter explanation identifies the persecuting authority's definition and distinguishes it from a neutral demographic fact. Current target coverage can be verified, partial or unresolved. Exact numbers appear only from supported owner evidence. A country-level religious affiliation is never presented as a census.

Readiness uses a checklist of current requirements with dates and map context where justified. It names the government, region, route or obligation that is missing. It does not show a historical global domination bar or count dead people toward completion. Opponents see publicly known preparations and meaningful uncertainty about concealed intent.

## Accessibility and interaction states

Every enabled, unavailable, pending, partial, failed, completed and retired action needs a distinct textual state. Color supports those differences but never carries them alone. The cohesion bands use words and simple status marks. Tooltips explain the exact current cause of unavailability, including a missing route, rejected charter, insufficient resource, invalid target data, or lost authority.

Long country and group names must wrap without covering costs or controls. Localized numbers use the project's supported integer formatting. Directional icons and symbols must remain readable at native scale. Religious symbols are identified in source notes and never used as the only cue for a political route.

Confirmation events put free cancellation first and show the frozen target, actual commitment, charter and material consequences. The player cannot accidentally accept a new target set because a country name changed after a click. A mission showing partial relief separates the living people helped from the continuing obligation without hiding a failed accounting state behind a success color.

## Narrative surfaces and direction

| Surface | Viewpoint and information | Required variation | Framing boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founding report | The affected government and its institutions, with immediate domestic and foreign facts | Founder profile, current leader or council, live capital, first charter, existing war | Do not present an entire population as the movement or imply historical inevitability. |
| Invitation and refusal | Recipient government evaluating concrete obligations | Actual targets, protected communities, sovereignty, route and existing faction | Shared religion alone never supplies consent. |
| Client foundation | Local authority created by conquest and its sponsor | Region, origin identity, imposed or negotiated status, supply and local opposition | Country creation is not evidence of popular approval. |
| Doctrine crisis | A current leadership dispute over the expanded charter | Old and new definitions, domestic consequences, members that assent or refuse | No invented scripture, slogan, source quotation or flattering atrocity language. |
| Protection and displacement | Threatened people, local organizers, receiving governments and feasible routes | Actual living outcomes, agency, limitations, destination and remaining need | Avoid passive victim inventories and claims of complete rescue without receipts. |
| Liberation | Change of control, surviving people, responsible authority and immediate needs | Actual release, destroyed infrastructure, displaced cohorts and continued danger | No resurrection, instant recovery or transfer of guilt to the liberator. |
| Founder defeat | Loss of one government and the status of surviving clients | Which governments remain, what they control, current compact and wars | No false worldwide victory while independent crusaders continue. |
| Terminal preparation | Known current multi-region capability and a political commitment window | Current missing conditions, opposing actors and uncertainty | No predetermined ending or invisible threshold reward. |
| Terminal transition | The order's global commitment and the remaining struggle | Actual order and opposing world, surviving governments and theaters | It describes a fictional persecuting project without celebrating its victims' destruction. |
| Defeat and aftermath | Surviving world, losses, displacement, government settlement and continuing shared consequences | Ordinary versus terminal conflict, actual scale and unresolved regions | A victory report cannot promise the end of all suffering or restart ordinary automatic firing. |

All final titles, report prose, options, focus names, descriptions, decision text, achievement text, Event Details text, quotes, super-event buttons and music choices are separate authoring work. The package provides directions and dynamic fields, not draft localisation. Final writing must use researched religious and regional vocabulary and preserve the distinction between historical evidence and the fictional international order.

## Identity asset states

The existing founder retains its national identity assets unless an authored government or cosmetic transformation actually changes them. Concentrated command and negotiated order can share a national flag while showing different institutional portraits, party naming and staged ideas. A temporary policy choice does not require a new country name or flag.

Each created client needs a local identity appropriate to its origin, an imposed crusader administration state, an independent-adherent state when sovereignty and authority change, and a repudiated or restored state where its political settlement changes the public identity. A cosmetic flag alone cannot signify completed repudiation while persecution continues.

Real national symbols require researched exact designs. Every new flag is generated as a flat reconstruction or a clearly fictional flat design under the event-assets rules. It must be compared manually for geometry, colors, orientation and symbols before conversion. No waving cloth, invented historical heraldry, false lettering or scenery is permitted in runtime flags.

Fictional administrations in real regions use authentic institutional imagery and institutional naming for their governing councils. They do not get invented individual biographies or faces disguised as historical figures. A wholly fictional high-Chaos polity would require a separate accepted country design before fictional person portraits were appropriate.

## Production and verification

Use the current event-assets reference root and its catalog and contact sheet before production. The separate asset prompt lists source modes, role ownership, exact native sizes and format requirements. Icons need native transparency where the consumer uses alpha. Resizing an unrelated icon family is not original focus or decision icon design.

Report, news and super-event scenes may be sourced when the source accurately represents the historical context and its license permits use. Fictional international meetings, client administrations and terminal scenes generally need generated art with an explicit alternate-history source note. A sourced photograph cannot be relabeled as evidence of the fictional crusade.

The current design uses static visuals. Motion would add production and UI complexity without solving a clear interaction need. If a later accepted addition requires animation, it must use separately authored or generated frames, an actual frame-sheet contract, a static fallback and runtime verification. Transforming one still into pseudo-animation does not satisfy the frame-animation skill.

The use of ordinary military families means existing appropriate soldier, weapon, vehicle, ship, aircraft, counter and voice assets remain the military presentation. A later decision to add a visually different unit changes this scope and triggers the full counter, sound, equipment and 3D pipeline. It cannot be shipped as a renamed infantry type with missing assets.

## Super-event boundaries

Plan two super-event families. The Final Crusade plays once when the terminal transition actually commits. The movement-defeat family plays once only for the end of a genuinely international and costly movement, including terminal defeat. A contained founder's early collapse uses an ordinary report. The defeat family can vary text and imagery context for ordinary and terminal outcomes while retaining one justified historical occurrence per episode.

Every super-event needs a unique framework identity, image, researched title and button, sourced or approved text treatment, an individually licensed musical recording, cue timing, sound wrapper, settings, logging and native playback verification. Composition rights and recording rights are separate. A cue should be no more than two minutes unless separately approved. The spec does not invent a quotation, music track or public-domain assertion to fill this requirement.

The source researcher, super-event text researcher, audio researcher, portrait worker, icon artist and generated-art worker have distinct responsibilities. Final integration and completion claims remain with the parent implementation agent. An attractive preview is not evidence that a flag, portrait, texture, sound, or sprite is correctly wired in the native game.
