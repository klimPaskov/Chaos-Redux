# Focus routes and progression

All route and anchor labels in this file are working labels, not final focus names or localisation. This is branch architecture with concrete outcomes. Exact node counts, coordinates, IDs, and connection routing follow source inspection during implementation.

## Tree ownership

Existing countries retain their meaningful national focus content. Heroic Crusade adds bounded route content through the project's source-verified focus integration method. A government seizure alone does not authorize replacing a living country's tree. If the required additive method cannot preserve the original tree, implementation must resolve that integration explicitly before claiming completion.

Countries actually created by the event receive the appropriate event-created country tree, selected by origin and geography. The event-created marker is mandatory for that tree's runtime loading. A restored vanilla country that was already alive does not qualify merely because its tag also appears in a client list.

The native tree must contain a valid non-shared anchor and resolve every shared-focus reference. Dynamic branch visibility and geographic changes require the actual supported layout refresh. Tree selection is not assumed to rerun whenever political flags change. Preserve completed physical preparation and correctly settle unfinished work when a tree or route changes.

The same political decisions remain comprehensible outside the focus screen. Focuses provide preparation, institutional capacity, strategic commitments, and major transitions. Current map control, receipt-backed resource delivery, membership assent, and shared civilian outcomes decide whether those transitions succeed. Focus completion cannot fabricate territory or perform an unvalidated population transaction.

## Common branch families

The normal-zoom tree should make five branch families immediately visible: governing authority, material sustainment, armed forces, international relationships, and territorial settlement. Political routes occupy distinct parts of the authority branch and connect to relevant development and diplomacy anchors. The settlement branch changes meaning after repudiation or defeat.

| Branch family | Early work | Middle choices | Late payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Governing authority | Establish who actually commands, expose institutional opposition, choose a political route | Resolve charter disputes, delegate or concentrate authority, manage a change of targets | Stable command, negotiated order, independent adherence, or repudiation |
| Material sustainment | Audit real stockpiles and routes, repair a supply bottleneck, choose production priorities | Local industry versus sponsor dependence, military commitments versus civilian recovery | A viable regional base or a costly withdrawal from unsupported ambitions |
| Armed forces | Organize existing formations, training and command, secure defensible deployment | Defensive depth, limited offensive preparation, naval or air support where viable | A sustainable campaign force with actual replacements |
| International relationships | Invitations or protection requests, clear obligations, conditional access | Shared campaigns, leadership settlement, sanctions response, asylum agreements | Durable compact, recognized independence, or defensive coalition |
| Territorial settlement | Decide occupation purpose and regional administration | Viable client formation, local negotiation, liberation and restoration | An enduring government with real obligations and a defined postwar position |

A focus belongs to one primary branch family or a visible convergence. Cross-branch prerequisites should express a meaningful dependency, such as supply preparation before overseas campaigning. They should not create decorative crossings or require a long detour through an unrelated political route.

## Founder route A: concentrated command

This route concentrates the movement's military and political command in the founding authority. It is attractive to a government able to provide the resources it demands. It risks alienating members that value sovereignty or disagree with targets.

The early anchors establish a unified command procedure and identify the material obligations it can realistically enforce. The player commits actual equipment, army availability, and administrative effort. The outcome is access to coordinated campaign proposals and a clearer chain of responsibility, with an obligation schedule that can later be broken.

The middle fork concerns delegation. One branch places reliable local authorities in viable client regions and gives them enough resources to fight. The other retains direct administration of strategically important territory and accepts the corresponding occupation and supply burden. The client branch spreads the movement's reach but creates actors that can outlive the founder. Direct administration concentrates industry but exposes the founder to resistance and overstretch.

The late anchors establish a durable common command, settle a leadership dispute, and prepare multi-theater logistics. They do not declare The Final Crusade. They provide the political and logistical ingredients that the separate current-position tests can recognize. A highly centralized order that loses its leader can fragment sharply, and dependent clients may refuse the successor's demands.

Refusing a target expansion is possible but politically costly within this route. The founder can preserve an earlier charter and risk a doctrinal split rather than automatically accepting Purity Doctrine. The evolution unlocks that crisis. It does not remove the route's capacity to make a political choice.

## Founder route B: negotiated order

This route builds a compact among governments that accept a common persecuting order while insisting on negotiated sovereignty and campaign obligations. Its breadth comes from bargaining, not automatic religious unity.

Early anchors set consultation rules, identify common enemies under the current charter, and negotiate transit or defensive commitments. A country willing to grant access but unwilling to adopt persecution remains a limited partner. A focus cannot promote it into full membership without its assent.

The middle fork trades speed for durability. One branch establishes a common supply and defense agreement with limited shared command. The other forms regional commands that can pursue agreed objectives with greater autonomy. Regional commands make independent survival more likely. They also mean the central bloc cannot count every local army as freely available for a global campaign.

The late anchors resolve a collective leadership arrangement and a written mechanism for charter revision. A target expansion needs renewed assent. A government that refuses can leave with negotiated military settlements or remain in a narrower separate compact. This route can achieve terminal readiness only when the surviving governments actually recommit to the global campaign and the current charter.

The route's distinctive reward is the ability to sustain cooperation among capable governments with different political interests. It does not gain a passive penalty exemption for persecution, nor a generic bonus to every diplomatic action. Its payoffs are actual agreements, access, supply commitments, and a more resilient succession procedure.

## Founder route C: rupture and repudiation

This route contests the governing movement from inside the founding state. It is available to a player who chooses to resist the seizure and to AI governments under conditions that make repudiation plausible. It can also become available after military defeat, leadership conflict, or a charter threatening the country's own protected institutions and communities.

The early anchors secure a real institutional base for opposition, preserve an existing military chain of command where possible, and stop the movement from monopolizing every resource. They can seek external guarantees or recognition. Those foreign responses are opportunities with alternatives, not mandatory single-country gates.

The middle fork is negotiated withdrawal versus coercive removal of loyalist authority. Negotiation can reduce immediate fighting when enough institutions comply. It requires verifiable cessation of persecution and a settlement of military obligations. Coercive removal requires defined loyalist-controlled locations, a force and supply disposition, an eligible alternative government, and a bounded internal conflict. It cannot use repeated uncontrolled generic civil wars as an infinite political mechanic.

The late anchors establish a successor government, settle live custody, begin civilian recovery, and determine whether the country seeks neutrality or joins the defense of other threatened countries. Crusader clients receive their own choices. The founder's reform cannot auto-flip their governments, end their wars, or release people still under their control.

Repudiation removes doctrine-dependent privileges and restores ordinary access according to the actual settlement. It does not stack every command bonus from the other routes or absolve earlier responsibility. The route can avert the terminal branch while leaving a difficult international and civilian aftermath.

## Client route A: delegated authority

A client choosing delegated authority accepts sponsor command in exchange for material and political support. The first anchors secure a viable capital, support a coherent defensive formation, and establish how the sponsor's allocation reaches it. The sponsor's promises must be backed by actual resources and routes.

The middle choice is frontier service versus local consolidation. Frontier service prepares one feasible campaign with the sponsor. Local consolidation invests in supply and administration while negotiating reduced offensive obligations. Both can keep the client within the order. The sponsor cannot force an unsupported distant war by treating every defensive unit as an expeditionary force.

The late anchors formalize local institutions and an enduring command relationship. These create the client's capacity to survive a sponsor collapse. If support fails, the same branch provides an exit into independent adherence or repudiation based on current politics. It does not strand the tree at a dead sponsor trigger.

## Client route B: independent adherence

This route preserves the crusade while rejecting external command. Its early anchors build local supply, recall committed forces where native rules permit, and establish a government able to pay its own obligations. Independence requires a genuine diplomatic or military settlement, not a focus that ignores subject restrictions.

The middle fork chooses a regional crusade or a successor compact. Regional action prioritizes reachable enemies and defensible territory. A successor compact seeks current agreements with other adherents and must supply its own command structure. Neither receives the original founding package again.

The late payoff is a persistent independent state whose actions must be addressed by opponents even if the first founder no longer exists. A successor compact can eventually qualify for The Final Crusade, using the same branch identity and one global terminal guard. It must pass its own current membership and durability tests.

## Client route C: local break and protection

This route repudiates the doctrine and aims to preserve the region from both loyalist persecution and destructive external reconquest. Early anchors identify an actual alternative authority, protect local institutions, and seek a feasible guarantor or independent defense plan.

The middle choice is negotiated separation or a defined break with the sponsor. Shared custody and persecution must actually stop under the new authority. If loyalist institutions retain a state or military formation, the route has a concrete territorial and force problem to solve. A new flag cannot bypass it.

The late payoff is a recognized successor or restored national government with a defensive army, reconstruction priorities, and a choice over asylum, return, and international alignment. Recognition can be delayed by earlier conduct or disputed borders. It does not require an unrelated foreign AI to click one particular focus.

## Regional military and economic modules

Frontier clients gain rail and supply preparation, defensive deployment, and a limited offensive planning branch. Maritime clients gain port repair, convoy replacement, coastal protection, and inspected invasion prerequisites. Interior clients gain local sustainment, access negotiations, and a reduced commitment path. These modules change real strategic choices and production priorities.

Countries with substantial existing industry can choose equipment standardization or heterogeneous imports, each with replacement and dependency consequences. Small countries can choose a compact force they can sustain or a larger force dependent on continuing aid. No branch promises an abstract army size without manpower and equipment.

Air support uses the actual aircraft and airfields available to the country. Naval support uses actual ports, convoys, ships, fuel, and coverage. A landlocked country's branch does not display meaningless shipbuilding rewards. Geography determines which modules appear, and the map inspector must confirm their predicates.

## Opposition progression

Existing opponents receive additive preparation and settlement anchors where the project's focus integration supports them. Their core actions remain available through the phased decisions and missions. The progression supports early warning and protection, coordinated defense, sustained reception and aid, liberation, and postwar recovery.

The meaningful fork is how much military and industrial capacity to devote to the crisis while other national priorities remain. A government can build a defense coalition, specialize in reception and transport, or organize a regional resistance and liberation campaign. These paths can cooperate. They are not ideology quotas and do not require every country to choose the same alliance.

Opposition focuses must use current achievable conditions. A focus preparing liberation of a selected region opens a target-specific operation only while there is a valid region and a route. If another ally liberates it first, progress transitions into relief or settlement according to the actual state. It never fails with an orphaned target or awards a second liberation reward.

## Duration, rewards, and spirit budget

Use a 35-day preparation anchor, a 55-day substantial institutional anchor, and a 70-day major strategic anchor. Actual duration bands depend on administrative control, industry, supply, current war pressure, route difficulty, and earlier preparation, within the focus owner's supported dynamic method. A major region-wide transformation should not be faster than its underlying political and material work. Longer mission processes can continue alongside focus preparation without charging the same resources twice.

At most three focus-tree-owned national spirits are active for a country. Use three staged slots: governing arrangement, mobilization and sustainment, and external obligations or recovery. The opening event's governing condition shares the governing slot where appropriate. Route transitions replace or remove stages in those slots. Existing unrelated national spirits remain under their own owners, and the budget cannot be evaded by assigning identical event modifiers to another interface.

Rewards prioritize concrete production access, supply preparation, diplomatic agreements, a paid force transfer, an institutional transition, a mission or decision, a verified region, and a route-specific settlement. Generic passive modifiers support those outcomes only when their role is clear. No death quota, equipment-farming cycle, repeated factory grant, automatic core expansion, or route-stacking reward is permitted.

Focus rewards and their linked operations share one settlement owner. Native completion rewards can execute before the focus is marked completed, so an immediate check of that same completed-focus flag cannot be the sole authorization for a valid reward. Use the recorded operation and a fresh completion receipt. Reserving resources in a selection effect also needs explicit lifecycle cleanup. The ordinary focus contract cannot borrow a continuous focus's cancellation callback. Automatic interruption, route loss, government replacement and tree reload must each settle the exact unfinished reservation through the owner. A disappearing spirit or focus branch does not by itself repeal doctrine or release custody.

## Exclusions, convergence, and failure

Concentrated command and negotiated order are mutually exclusive governing arrangements, with one authored expensive transition if current members agree. Repudiation closes doctrine-dependent late anchors and reuses relevant recovery infrastructure. Client delegation and independence cannot both supply contradictory command benefits. An earlier useful supply repair survives a political change because the railway still exists.

Common military and economic preparation can converge where it genuinely serves multiple routes. Political end states remain distinct. A focus that depends on a foreign government has alternative eligible partners, a domestic preparation path, or a meaningful closure when the opportunity disappears. Missing actors cannot strand the whole tree.

The implementation route coverage map must account for every branch and regional module in this file. Any merged, removed, renamed, or substituted route must be reported with its gameplay consequence. Every visible focus needs search filters. Large separated branches need Focus Navigation anchors that follow route visibility. Normal-zoom review must show clear branch families, readable links, no overlap, and no empty cosmetic branches.

## Resolved actor and regional ownership

The deterministic module and actor-to-interface rules in `11_authored_origins_and_country_setup.md` govern this tree. Existing opponents use the complete decision and mission progression. Optional inspected focus anchors enrich that progression and never become a prerequisite for protection or refusal. A living member retains its tree and uses event and decision responses for membership, command refusal, withdrawal and repudiation. The founder branch remains a required additive integration for the two authored founder profiles.
