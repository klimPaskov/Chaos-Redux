# The order, membership, and diplomacy

All structural labels below are working labels, not final localisation.

## Political movement and military faction

The crusading order is a political association whose governments maintain a persecuting doctrine and military commitments. Its main coordinated bloc is represented by an international crusading faction when native faction rules permit formation. Movement affiliation and native faction membership are related but distinct. This distinction preserves existing wars, alliances, clients, and countries that later continue independently.

The founder establishes the first charter and initially holds leadership. A compatible government receives a real proposal naming the doctrine, current targets, defense and war obligations, material contribution, command relationship, and exit consequences. It can refuse, offer limited cooperation, seek a negotiated compact, or join. A refusal does not silently apply a persecution policy or create a war goal against that government.

An actor in a preexisting faction cannot be silently removed from it. It can cooperate outside the new faction, negotiate departure under the existing diplomacy rules, or remain an opponent. Any transition that requires native faction leadership, subject autonomy, war participation, or peace handling must be resolved through the corresponding owner. The order cannot use an event membership flag to bypass a native diplomatic prohibition.

Native faction creation can include subjects or initialize relationships automatically. That mechanical inclusion does not constitute charter acceptance. Every counted government needs its own current consent and contribution record. A faction leader changing, joining another faction, or losing authority does not transfer the right to approve persecution or the final campaign on behalf of the other governments. Use the installed compatible faction creation method without adding a new faction-goal or DLC subsystem to this baseline.

## Actor states

| Political state | Military relationship | Persecution responsibility | Counts toward a coordinated terminal bloc? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founding government | Leads the initial faction and compact | Owns its orders and actual operations | Yes, only while all current capability tests hold |
| Full member government | Signed mutual campaign commitment and usable operational access | Owns its conduct and any orders it actually executes | Yes, while its commitment and real capacity persist |
| Dependent crusader client | Subject relationship plus a local government and forces | Local operator and issuing sponsor recorded separately where supported | Yes for actual controlled capacity, counted once |
| Independent adherent | Continues the doctrine and wars after independence or departure | Remains responsible for its own persecution | Only after it signs and sustains a current operational compact |
| Limited partner | Access, supply, mediation, or a specific war agreement | No automatic adoption of the target charter | No |
| Refusing government | No crusading obligation | No guilt inferred from religious similarity | No |
| Repudiating former member | Ends doctrine and enforcement, may need to fight loyalist institutions | Retains responsibility for its earlier conduct | No |
| Dormant claimant | No real governed territory or supported force | Historical record remains | No |

An independent client that keeps fighting is therefore still an actor opponents must confront. Its independence can simultaneously weaken the founder's coordinated position. Counting every sympathetic former client as guaranteed global war capacity would defeat the user's current-position requirement.

## Compatibility and invitations

Compatibility has four independent tests: doctrine, actual targets, sovereignty, and strategic practicality. Shared religion is evidence for a possible conversation, not a completed test.

Doctrine compatibility asks whether the two governments accept one another's religious authority or can negotiate a limited institutional arrangement. Target compatibility asks whether the new charter threatens a protected community, domestic population, ally, ruling institution, or an existing promise of the prospective member. Sovereignty asks whether the recipient accepts command and dependency obligations. Practicality asks whether it can contribute or receive support over actual routes without abandoning a more urgent war.

An invitation records the exact charter version and promised obligations. If Purity Doctrine changes the target set before acceptance, the proposal becomes stale and must be presented again. A recipient never accepts an expanded target definition through an old confirmation. A government targeted by the current charter cannot be treated as fully compatible merely because it shares the founder's religion.

Repeated invitations are bounded. One live proposal exists for the same sender, recipient, and charter. A refusal closes that proposal and applies a 180-day anchor before a materially changed proposal may be considered. War, a new target charter, a change of government, or a genuine security emergency can alter the next offer. A routine daily retry cannot farm relations, cohort changes, or event history.

## The compact's material obligations

Full membership has three negotiable commitments. The government contributes a bounded force or equipment allocation, accepts operational access appropriate to its geography, and recognizes the compact's procedure for campaigns and leadership. The allocation uses existing divisions, actual stockpiles, existing military factories, or a concrete timed production commitment. It never creates a generic reserve of free crusader soldiers.

There are two main command arrangements. A centralized arrangement lets the leader coordinate common campaigns and demands a larger, more reliable material contribution. It can respond quickly but concentrates political risk and strains distant clients. A negotiated arrangement preserves greater national control, limits automatic expeditionary demands, and requires consultation before major campaigns or target expansion. It can keep a wider alliance together at the cost of slower collective action. Both can sustain the crusade. Neither reduces responsibility for persecution.

Limited partners can grant transit, sell or provide supplies through supported diplomacy, join one defensive war, mediate withdrawal, or accept displaced people. Their obligations are explicit and do not count as a full member vote or terminal commitment. A relief agreement cannot secretly become military access for the crusaders.

## Bloc cohesion

Bloc cohesion is the event's only persistent public custom numeric value. It describes whether the current coordinated governments can carry out joint commitments. It does not measure religious truth, popular faith, civilian obedience, military strength, or the number of victims.

Use a 0 to 100 display with a neutral numerical value, a short qualitative condition, and non-color status marks. The founding anchor is 50. The value responds to real events and to the condition of due commitments. Completed shared operations, delivered material support, respected autonomy agreements, stable leadership, and mutually accepted charters can raise it. Unpaid obligations, unauthorized target expansion, supply abandonment, leadership seizure, broken defense promises, defeated clients, and departure reduce it.

| Band | Political consequence | Available recovery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 24 | Joint campaigns pause. Dependent members reconsider obedience. Leadership challenge becomes urgent. | Settle an actual obligation, negotiate reduced commitments, or accept fragmentation. |
| 25 to 49 | Joint actions require individual assent. Risk of refusal increases. | Repair access, deliver support, mediate disputes, or change command arrangements. |
| 50 to 74 | Normal compact operation. Members still make their own political decisions. | Maintain promises and military feasibility. |
| 75 to 100 | Reliable coordination supports the highest level of collective planning. | Continued logistics and consent are required. Strength does not remove route or military tests. |

Cohesion never decays or increases simply because a numeric day tick passes. A due review examines unfulfilled obligations, continuing supply failures, or restored operation and applies bounded consequences for the current episode. A stable quiet bloc can remain stable. Repeated completion of the same promise or toggling the command arrangement cannot generate unlimited gain.

Cohesion affects willingness and the availability of collective actions. It cannot make an unreachable enemy reachable, operate an abandoned camp, give a country nonexistent equipment, or automatically force a member into a new target doctrine. Exact change anchors and cooldowns are in the tuning specification.

## Leaving and repudiating

A member can announce withdrawal from the compact. Existing wars, leased units, supply obligations, subject restrictions, and access agreements must be settled according to their actual owners. Withdrawal can involve a preparation mission, recall of forces, negotiated guarantees, or a contested break. The political choice is available even when completion is temporarily constrained. The interface explains the constraint and offers an achievable next step.

Leaving the faction and ending persecution are separate choices. A departing government that retains the doctrine becomes an independent adherent. It remains a threat to targeted people and continues existing wars unless peace or defeat ends them. A repudiating government must revoke targeting orders, halt new detention, resolve live custody, end forced labor or killing under its authority, and allow shared liberation or release procedures to complete. Continuing perpetrators can resist those orders. A visible political reform flag cannot cover a still-operating persecution system.

The movement can expel a member for refusing the charter or abandoning obligations. Expulsion ends compact credit and joint command. It does not delete the country, transfer all its states to the founder, dismiss its army, or erase its earlier responsibility. An expelled adherent can form a successor compact after demonstrating its own sustained agreements. A government expelled for protecting threatened people can seek defensive guarantees.

## Leadership succession

The founder has precedence, not immortality. Loss of its capital, capitulation, destruction of its command institutions, or a successful internal repudiation opens succession. Surviving governments can accept a capable successor, create a negotiated council, or divide into separate adherent blocs.

Successor suitability uses surviving state capacity, actual forces, access to other members, willingness to accept obligations, and recognition. It never selects a capitulated map label with no army simply because it owns the oldest event flag. Existing war participation and subject relationships are retained. New leadership inherits only commitments the surviving governments still recognize.

A successor must rebuild the durability record for The Final Crusade. Founder loss invalidates the old common readiness certificate. It does not reset actual history or erase an independently qualifying region's records, but the reconstituted command must demonstrate 120 days of continuous operational commitment before it can use those regions in a new terminal candidacy.

## Diplomacy for opponents

Threatened governments can form a defense compact while keeping their existing factions. A compact coordinates warning, guarantees, equipment assistance, reception, and actual war support. It does not automatically create a global super-faction or dissolve ongoing ideological and colonial disputes. Military entry and civilian protection are separate agreements with separate capacity tests.

A country can protect a targeted minority while refusing a war alliance, fight a crusader government while closing its borders, or welcome displaced people while lacking a useful army. These are different policies with different consequences. Shared Condemnation evaluates conduct. Membership in an opposition coalition does not absolve violations by that coalition's governments.

Neutral mediation can secure local withdrawals, exchanges administered by existing owners, or the recognition of a repudiating government. It cannot erase the dead or guarantee a worldwide peace between surviving independent actors. The player receives the actual parties, affected territory, living populations, and enforceable terms before commitment.
