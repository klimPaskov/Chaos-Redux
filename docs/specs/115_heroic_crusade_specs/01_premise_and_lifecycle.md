## The central experience

A militant religious movement takes governing authority in a suitable country and establishes an international crusading faction. It declares one religious, racial, or ethnic group an enemy of its order. Its member governments can attack countries associated with that group and persecute targeted people in territory they control. Conquests can produce militant client states that have their own governments, armies, supply problems, political choices, and responsibility for their actions.

The crisis is an argument over who commands a transnational movement and whether that movement can maintain power. An invitation can be refused. A government can leave. An army can lose its supply route. A client can obey its founder, continue the crusade independently, or repudiate it. A threatened country can seek protection without surrendering its independence. Communities can organize escape and resistance. A religious authority sharing the movement's broad tradition can condemn it, compete with it, protect its victims, or become a conditional ally.

The event name belongs to the movement's claim about itself. The presentation makes persecution, coerced labor, displacement, and killing legible as acts of the responsible governments. It never treats an entire religion or ethnicity as naturally committed to this movement. Shared religious identity does not determine a country's policy, and a government's policy does not establish the identity or wishes of its population.

The ordinary event can end in a contained founding state, a regional war, a fragmented international network, a government rupture, or a costly military defeat. A separate terminal branch can transform an already powerful current bloc into The Final Crusade. The event must remain worth playing when that branch is disabled or never becomes eligible.

## Design commitments

| Commitment | Result in play |
| --- | --- |
| A real international faction | Invitations, obligations, refusal, exit, expulsion, access, commands, and material contributions have concrete consequences. |
| Governments remain actors | Founder defeat does not remove independent clients, their armies, wars, or persecution. |
| One initially selected target group | Target selection is coherent, researched, and recorded. Purity Doctrine is the explicit expansion of that definition. |
| Actual people remain in shared accounting | Detention is a custody change, displacement is movement, and death is measured population loss. The event cannot fabricate subgroup totals. |
| Opposition changes the situation | Protection, coalition defense, rescue, resistance support, defections, and liberation can remove capability and avert terminal escalation. |
| Current strength determines escalation | Holding a conquest once, killing a particular number of civilians, or accumulating a conquest counter cannot unlock the ending. |
| Choices have an economic and military basis | Troops, equipment, civilian industry, supply, transport, diplomacy, and local authority matter alongside political power. |
| A readable interface | One persistent public custom value, bloc cohesion, is shared across the event. Existing national and shared-system statistics retain their existing interfaces. |

## Conditions for the first manifestation

The automatic event joins the normal Minor Fire-Once pool from Chaos level 1 after the identity migration is resolved. It uses the project's normal pool and timing controls. The event does not create a new competing global timer.

A candidate founder must be a living human government with controlled territory, a viable capital, an existing administrative and military base, and an authored political context that makes a militant religious seizure or conversion of government intelligible. Candidate data must distinguish the governing institution from the population's religion. A country lacking reliable religious or political context is not filled in from its name, ideology, flag, or geography.

The candidate must also have a feasible initial target profile and at least one reachable political or military relationship that can make the crisis international. Reach can be a land frontier, a usable port and sea route, or access through a willing government. No naval power is invented for a landlocked founder. No existing irreplaceable event actor, nonhuman country, or terminal owner can be overwritten. An ongoing war can change suitability, but being weak or small does not automatically exclude an interesting founding crisis.

Use an authored shortlist with explicit provenance and a bounded dynamic suitability pass. The shortlist contains political contexts, possible institutions, target profiles, and geographical opportunities. It is not a list of religions considered dangerous. A manually requested opening runs the same identity, authority, target, and map validation. If no complete candidate exists, it explains the failed requirement to the requester and does not consume the fire-once record, charge resources, change governments, or leave a partial faction.

The first manifestation is committed only when one country actually acquires the movement's governing authority and the first target charter is valid. Setup is one transaction. A failed capital choice, invalid target record, or faction conflict unwinds provisional setup before the main event is marked as fired. Once that real manifestation succeeds, later suppression or defeat does not make the event fire again.

## The opening political crisis

The founding state inherits its existing population, factories, armed forces, research, diplomacy, wars, and debts. The movement captures part of the state's government and military command. It does not receive a free army or erase a meaningful national focus tree. The initial governing authority can be an existing sourced actor where the alternate history is defensible or an institutional council represented by authentic institutional imagery. Grounded countries do not receive an invented historical person's face or an unsourced claim about a real person's genocidal beliefs.

The first report establishes the government seizure, the declared target, what is publicly known, and the immediate foreign response. A player in the founder can direct the new government, contest the seizure through the rupture route, or support a negotiated withdrawal from the crusade when the internal balance permits it. These responses begin different processes. A single free refusal cannot both cancel the crisis and preserve all of the movement's military advantages.

There is a preparation interval before a broad foreign campaign can begin. The founding movement can seek recognition, organize military obligations, and formalize its persecution policy through the shared Repression and Camps owner. Opponents can mobilize protection and reception during the same interval. The initial target country's existing wars and alliances continue. No target receives a surprise global war declaration merely because it appears in an opening report.

The initial preparation anchor is 90 days, adjusted by current military readiness, internal opposition, equipment shortage, existing war urgency, access, and external resistance. A government already fighting an eligible enemy may continue that war immediately. Opening preparation is not immunity for civilians and does not suspend the shared systems' existing actions.

## Ordinary lifecycle

These are baseline phases, not evolutions. Several fronts can be in different local states while the international movement has one recorded lifecycle.

| Phase | Entry | Available play | Exit or setback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founding authority | Valid seizure and target charter committed | Organize government, reject or contest invitations, establish first protection arrangements, prepare military obligations | Suppression, contained survival, or a functioning international compact |
| International formation | At least one other government signs an operational commitment or a first conquest creates a viable client | Negotiate obligations, contest leadership, support an ally, establish defensive coordination | Members leave, sponsor overreaches, coalition forms, or campaigns begin |
| Campaign expansion | An active crusader pursues a reachable war or establishes control over a new region | Conquest, client creation, relief, resistance, defensive war, occupation choices | Supply failure, liberation, client rupture, negotiated withdrawal, or durable consolidation |
| Consolidation or fragmentation | Territory, governments, and commitments stabilize or break apart | Rebuild logistics, share costs, repair or replace leadership, leave the compact, stop persecution, support successor clients | Renewed campaign, ordinary containment, collapse, or terminal eligibility |
| Defeat and settlement | The remaining movement governments lose the capacity or political commitment to sustain the crusade | Liberate, release, shelter, resettle, demobilize, preserve evidence, restore government | Shared aftermath persists after event-owned active controls close |

The lifecycle can move backward. A bloc that loses a port can return to consolidation. A client that leaves the compact can still fight its own crusading war. A founding government's repudiation does not impose repudiation on every other member. The interface reports the surviving governments and the practical reason the crisis continues.

## Failure, containment, defeat, and dormancy

**Containment** means a living movement has been denied expansion or reliable foreign support. It retains its government and the ability to persecute where it still controls territory. Containment can weaken cohesion and prevent terminal readiness, but it is not liberation and does not close the underlying camp system.

**Political defeat in one country** means that country's government has ceased sustaining the doctrine, has lost the institutions that can enforce it, or has been replaced. The new authority must actually terminate persecution and settle custody through the shared owner. A flag change or cosmetic regime name alone does not qualify.

**International defeat** requires every surviving government sustaining the movement to have been defeated, repudiated, or rendered incapable of continued political and military action. A stateless claimant with no governed territory, meaningful armed formation, or operational sponsor is a dormant historical remnant. A government in exile with forces and an active sponsor remains an opponent until that support ends or it capitulates. Dormant remnants do not generate endless invisible world-threat ticks or keep the event UI permanently active.

**Dormancy** preserves historical identity, earlier responsibility, and any valid unresolved civilian records. It does not periodically regenerate countries, troops, claims, or camps. Reactivation requires a real existing actor to restore authority through a defined political or military action within this event's continuing history. It never refires the fire-once event or repeats opening rewards.

## Player roles and information

The founder manages an unstable international project with genuine costs and potential domestic opposition. A member government chooses how far to commit and can withdraw. A client must survive with a coherent local economy and armed force while deciding whether to accept external command. An opponent balances military protection with civilian reception and its own wars. A neutral can remain outside the conflict, grant asylum or access, impose sanctions, or recognize a repudiating successor without joining a new faction.

Facts visible to each role are separated from hidden intention. A signed commitment is public to its parties. A secret plan is not shown to an enemy as an exact timer. Publicly exposed camps and documented civilian losses appear through the shared evidence and Deaths surfaces. Unverified demographic coverage is identified as uncertain, never displayed as a precise population percentage.

The hard crisis rule for player countries applies to supported pressures such as coalition response, supply vulnerability, resistance, and the time required to stabilize a new government. It is mandatory and has no event-specific off switch. It must not invent additional civilian losses, falsify displayed costs, change a chosen target secretly, or bypass the same physical and political rules used by the AI.

## Scope and depth

The package specifies one baseline event, one evolution, one independent terminal branch, ordinary and terminal opposition, complete client archetypes, focus-route architecture, phased decisions and missions, source ownership, assets, AI, and validation. It uses ordinary military unit families because the movement changes recruitment, command, doctrine, and supply rather than the physical nature of soldiers. It does not require a new unit model, a second camp interface, or an additional population engine to express its premise.

Implementation must satisfy the full civilian contract and country packages before the event can be marked playable. Missing shared demographic integration is a named prerequisite to complete, not a permission to replace targeted persecution with an unmeasured generic modifier.
