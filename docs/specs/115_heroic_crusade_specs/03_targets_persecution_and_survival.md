# Targets, persecution, and civilian survival

## Target selection is a political act

The founding charter selects exactly one target group. It records the movement's stated classification, the political context of the choice, the governments associated with the threatened population, and the target definition's version. Religion, ethnicity, and racialized classification are different kinds of data. A racialized category records a persecuting authority's ascription. It does not assert a biological taxonomy or make the entire classified population politically uniform.

Each authored founder profile contains a small, coherent set of possible initial targets. Candidate profiles require sourced historical or cultural context, an intelligible alternate-history departure, and a usable shared population representation. A random worldwide list of real minorities is not an acceptable substitute for that work. The player may influence a plausible internal doctrine dispute when their route gives them authority. They cannot use a freeform menu to label any arbitrary country or demographic description for extermination.

Initial target choice evaluates the movement's own factional claims, prior political conflict, reachable countries, threatened communities within controlled territory, opposition response, and strategic overreach. It must not optimize for the largest killable population. Where several choices are valid, the choice creates different diplomatic and domestic problems. A target chiefly living abroad creates access and coalition problems. A target substantially present at home creates immediate dissent, displacement pressure, and resistance. A target protected by a powerful religious authority can divide otherwise compatible governments.

The selected definition remains stable until a real doctrinal change occurs. Border changes, a new ruler, or altered faction membership cannot silently recategorize living people. A targeted person moving to a friendly country does not cease to belong to the threatened cohort, although the receiving authority can protect them. A country leaving the order does not instantly change its population's identity.

## Country association and living population are separate

| Evidence | What it can authorize | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sourced government or institutional affiliation | A diplomatic relationship, a threat assessment, or a country-level campaign claim | The religious or ethnic identity of every resident |
| A country protecting a threatened group | A political dispute or a request for defense | Membership of that government's entire population in the group |
| An authored shared cohort with documented provenance | Bounded persecution, custody, movement, and loss of the represented living people | An exact demographic census beyond the dataset's scope |
| A camp custody record | Responsibility for actual detained survivors and site outcomes | A new population pool independent of the state population |
| A displaced cohort and accepted reception receipt | The living people moved and their current host | A second population credit when integration is later recorded |
| A discovery or survivor account | Public knowledge and a Condemnation or Resistance input | A fabricated body count or an automatic diplomatic response by every country |

A campaign claim can concern a government that shelters targets, even when that government is not itself associated with their religion or ethnicity. The event must explain the government's political role rather than reclassifying its civilians. Such escalation carries diplomatic and cohesion consequences appropriate to attacking a protector.

## Required shared target-population extension

The supplied sources define state population, generic cohorts, custody, and measured physical movement. They do not establish a complete religious, racial, or ethnic living-population registry. The inspected camp implementation also has aggregate pools that cannot prove targeted-group identity. Event 115 therefore requires an explicit shared extension before group-specific persecution is complete.

This extension belongs with the shared population, custody, and migration owners. It is not an event-private population simulation. It must be useful to any feature that needs overlapping living-group identities and must preserve the current total population owner.

Each represented living cohort needs a durable identity, origin, current location, current living amount, group memberships, source and confidence, custody status, displacement status, and references to the physical transactions that changed it. The same person can belong to more than one targeted group. The effective target set is a union of living cohorts or disjoint intersections, not a sum of all group totals.

Authored initial amounts must fit the actual map date and the shared population total. Incomplete research can be represented as bounded or unknown coverage, with its limitation shown. It cannot become an exact number by multiplying the whole state population by an invented percentage. A genuinely synthetic alternate-history cohort must be explicitly authored as such and reconciled with actual state population. Adding identity metadata never creates people.

The shared owner must support partitioning a cohort when only some members move, enter custody, are released, or die. It must preserve group membership across that split and maintain lineage so repeated actions cannot select the original unsplit amount. Identity changes, conversions, and competing racial ascriptions require authored rules and auditable history. They are not a free player exploit for erasing persecution or guilt.

If a state lacks sufficient target evidence, the event may still show the political threat, diplomatic claim, military control, and presence of an enforceable exclusion policy. It must block an unsupported numerical group-specific custody or killing transaction and expose the coverage gap to implementation validation. A release cannot conjure a survivor cohort from a generic state modifier. This is an explicit completion gate, not the final shipped behavior for eligible Event 115 target profiles.

## Persecution policy and shared administration

The event supplies the responsible government, exact charter version, validated target set, controlled territory, current enforcement authority, and political policy choice. Repression and Camps owns the actual detention, deportation, forced labor, killing, maintenance, site purposes, workforce, custody, closure, and records.

An event can order the responsible owner to consider or apply a valid persecution policy. It cannot bypass the owner's eligibility, physical transaction, resource, authority, or custody rules. It cannot create an Event 115 extermination button that directly subtracts a generic fraction of a state's population. The movement's declared intent is visible through its doctrine and actions. The concrete operation and human consequences remain auditable in the shared system.

The existing Camp Administration interface has Administration, Locations, and Records tabs. Event surfaces link to the correct country, state, and responsible authority there. They do not reproduce the administration controls, create a new atrocity efficiency meter, or copy monthly deaths into an event-owned counter. A player's orders operate through the same owner validation as the AI's orders.

Persecution consumes enforcement, administration, supply, and political capacity under the shared mechanics. Seized property, coerced labor, and confiscation are represented only where the existing owner already accounts for them. Event 115 grants no extra military strength, equipment, manpower, achievements, or cohesion for civilian killings. Atrocity consequences can still increase world Chaos through the shared Deaths and related systems.

## Physical loss and responsibility

Civilian killings reduce the actual state population. The required shared contract measures the positive physical decrease and records that measured amount in Deaths without debiting population again. Target-cohort living totals, site custody, and state population must reconcile to the same transaction. A requested number is not proof that it happened.

The complete pinned-source review found a version distinction that implementation must resolve. The generic exact-loss helper returns its planned, clamped and rounded amount; it does not itself measure the population after mutation. Camp Administration measures the actual decrease around its nonlogging call and then uses record-only Deaths registration. Preserve that measured owner transaction and the newer supplied requirement for a protected population floor. Do not treat the older generic helper's success or reported amount as proof of actual loss. Part 12 specifies this boundary and links it to the source evidence.

Responsibility records distinguish the government operating a site or institution, a government issuing an order where the shared system supports that evidence, and the current owner or controller of the state. Liberation does not make the liberator responsible for the previous operator's acts. Independence does not erase a client's acts, and a sponsor's defeat does not erase evidence against it.

The country receiving national Deaths statistics is a separate attribution from the government responsible for persecution. The inspected Deaths country field credits that country's totals; it is not a culpability interface. Camp's current caller passes its original responsible country, so the shared owners must explicitly reconcile the intended national-loss attribution with their responsibility record. Residence, present control or a government flag cannot invent a victim's nationality. The event does not silently relabel the existing field or blame a liberator.

The Deaths setting follows the physical owner's actual enablement rules. At the inspected revision, disabling Deaths prevents Camp Administration's whole death transaction, including its physical debit. Event 115 must respect that gate. A physical loss already applied by an authorized owner remains real and cannot resurrect the living cohort; enabling recording later cannot replay it as a new casualty. A proposal to decouple Camp's physical execution from its recording setting would be a separate explicit shared-owner change. This design does not bypass that setting or create an alternative global deaths ledger.

Historical responsibility and public knowledge differ. A concealed operation can leave an internal record before a foreign government has evidence to impose sanctions. Exposure must come through actual discovery, witnesses, inspections, liberated records, or other established shared evidence. Neither perfect omniscience nor automatic permanent concealment is the default.

The inspected Condemnation source entry is additive and does not provide general operation deduplication by itself. The accepted action and a later evidence-discovery transition therefore need durable receipts in their actual shared owners. Correcting or publishing evidence must never repeat the underlying custody, movement or physical loss.

## Flight, rescue, and reception

Threatened communities can respond before a foreign army arrives. Shared Migration and Resistance can create escape requests, hiding and local support, displacement pressure, and organized resistance. The event supplies the threat and available sponsors. It does not portray civilians as passive stocks assigned entirely by states.

A protection agreement needs a willing receiving authority, real receiving states, feasible routes, and capacity. A border promise is not an accepted movement. A blocked sea lane, hostile transit state, lost port, or overloaded destination can prevent or limit movement. A relief mission should then offer a concrete response such as finding another destination, restoring a route, increasing reception capacity, or protecting people where they remain.

The same physical owner handles voluntary flight, supported evacuation, forced deportation, and later return through appropriate policy and consent rules. Each action validates its own legitimacy and destination conditions. Event 115 cannot pass a forced transfer through a relief label to bypass those conditions.

The conservation contract is actual origin debit minus route deaths minus actual destination credit equals zero. Route deaths are a subset of the people who actually left. Survivors are credited once. Receiving-state and receiving-country loads change symmetrically. Later integration, resettlement, reunification, or political recognition changes status and obligations without adding the same people again.

Player relief choices concern transport, receiving capacity, food, housing, military protection, and diplomacy. A completed operation earns progress because living people reached a valid protected status, not because a requested quota was selected. Partial success shows the actual survivors, remaining people, continuing danger, and resource settlement. Failure cannot display a success event or award a rescue achievement.

The inspected transfer owner settles complete living cohort rows. A partially completed relief mission can consist of several fully finalized tranches while later tranches remain pending or fail. Moving only part of one existing row requires a supported shared split first; an incomplete debit or destination credit is not a successful tranche. Success requires the complete transfer, reception and cohort-binding receipt, with a positive survivor arrival for rescue. An all-death result cannot earn rescue credit. The current corridor evacuation path requests zero route deaths; other authorized movement roles may have their own documented route-loss rules. Corridor cleanup can clear its local proof fields, so historical rescue credit consumes durable shared transaction history.

## Resistance and local political agency

The intended shared Resistance integration receives current coercion, doctrine, custody, displacement, destruction, external aid, and changes in control. This broad intake is a required owner contract, not a claim that a general callable service already exists. The selected complete sources expose native state resistance, Camp's resistance accumulation and narrower genocide-crisis support and disruption hooks. Implementation must connect the supported forms to those actual owners and add the missing shared adapters explicitly. Event-specific hooks can provide a sponsor, a negotiated political objective, or a liberation opportunity. They cannot duplicate resistance strength in an independent Event 115 army meter.

Support missions require an actual delivery route or an owner-supported covert path, an eligible local actor, a bounded resource contribution, and a clear objective. Aid can help preserve a threatened population, disrupt a persecuting institution, sustain local defense, or assist liberation. The specification does not prescribe real-world sabotage techniques. Strategic outcomes, costs, exposure, and civilian consequences are enough for this game system.

Resistance is not homogeneous. A local actor can oppose persecution and also resist an external liberator's annexation. Sponsorship does not automatically convert communities into the sponsor's ideology or grant free cores. The settlement route must acknowledge who actually governed and fought in the territory.

## Liberation

Liberation begins when the defeated crusader authority loses effective control over a region. The control-change hook immediately invalidates that authority's pending local orders and terminal credit. The new controller then follows the shared custody, site-security, release, evidence, and reception procedures.

Where camps or live custody remain, closure requires the appropriate shared receipt. Living survivors can need care, food, shelter, documentation, transport, and a safe destination. Release can occur into local civilian life when safe and supported. It does not require every survivor to migrate. People whose homes remain unsafe can stay displaced or choose another valid destination.

The liberator can secure and release, preserve a site for investigation under the owner's rules, dismantle it after custody is settled, or transfer lawful administration to an eligible successor. No choice restores people who died. A region's change of flag does not instantly clear displacement, rebuild homes, or remove recorded atrocities.

If a crusader retakes the state, it must establish actual authority again. It cannot reclaim already moved survivors, rewind the liberation receipt, or execute an old target list against a new population. If a liberator itself commits repression, the same shared responsibility and civilian rules apply.

## Settlement and return

Return is a separate voluntary or otherwise owner-authorized movement with current origin, destination, safety, capacity, and authority checks. An old home becoming friendly is an opportunity, not a command to move every displaced cohort back. Changed borders, destroyed housing, current local conflict, and the receiving government's policy can make another outcome preferable.

The event closes its active political controls when the movement is defeated, while shared Deaths, Migration, Resistance, Repression and Camps, and Condemnation continue carrying the consequences. The surviving map can contain depopulated states, displaced communities, ruined industry, unreconciled custody evidence, and unstable successor governments. The result remains playable and historically legible within the save.

## Terminal population charter

Before terminal commitment, the effective target set is the enacted initial group plus accepted Purity Doctrine additions, with overlapping people counted once. The Final Crusade introduces a separate authored universal population charter, whose positive inclusion rule and explicit exclusions are defined in Part 07. It does not silently interpret every foreign population as the founder's original target group.

The terminal classifier has three outcomes: included, excluded and unclassified. A supported explicit exclusion veto takes priority over a recognized religious identity. A person is excluded for failing the positive rule only when the relevant identity is actually known. Missing identity data remains unclassified and cannot authorize numerical detention, movement or killing. Country membership, a flag, national majority, state ownership or government ideology never supplies the missing personal identity.

Every numerical action still passes through the shared identity and civilian owners in Part 12. The terminal charter changes the applicable policy. It does not move or kill anyone at activation, does not turn unknown people into a fabricated group, and does not erase the prior charter, receipts or responsibility. A negotiated recognition amendment changes the positive set only after disclosed consent. Removing an explicit exclusion requires an explicit repeal and owner reconciliation.
