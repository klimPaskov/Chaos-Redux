# War, conquered regions, and crusader countries

All country and route labels below are working labels, not final localisation.

## Campaigns use the real map

A campaign needs a political enemy, a practical approach, a force that can fight, and enough supply to sustain it. A country's association with the target group can justify a movement claim in the fiction. It does not remove native war rules, existing guarantees, faction obligations, access restrictions, naval requirements, or peace mechanics.

The first campaign should be a comprehensible regional objective. A founder with a threatened neighbor faces a land war and possible intervention. A maritime founder faces ports, convoys, naval cover, and landing capacity. A landlocked founder needs a frontier or negotiated transit. A campaign against a distant protected population can draw resistance from governments that would otherwise be compatible with the order.

Crusader AI selects an attainable campaign and can postpone it when current forces, supply, or domestic control are inadequate. Opposition can deny a route, fortify a frontier, protect a port, isolate a client, or compel a government to withdraw. Losing a campaign matters through actual territory, units, industry, access, and confidence. It is not replaced by a hidden event progress penalty.

## Conquest does not automatically create a client

After gaining stable control of a coherent region, the occupying government chooses among continued occupation, a negotiated withdrawal or settlement, and founding a crusader client. The local population is never presumed to support the doctrine. A client creates a political and military authority, with resistance and legitimacy problems determined by the actual region and its history.

Creation requires a viable capital, defensible territory, an administrative base, a supply connection or credible local sustainment, a real allocation of forces and equipment, and a compatible diplomatic disposition. A single isolated captured province, an inaccessible port with no transport, or land still under immediate enemy control cannot become a fully supplied fighting state merely through a release button.

Prefer a suitable existing national identity when it represents the region. Reuse must respect whether that country is already living, its current government, its cores and territory, and other event ownership. A client is not allowed to overwrite an unrelated country occupying the same tag. A genuinely new identity requires the complete vanilla, mod, Workshop, and local-mod tag collision audit before any tag, flag, portrait, or namespace is locked.

Creation is atomic across territorial transfer, government setup, forces, costs, membership, and history. The sponsor cannot retain a transferred equipment allocation and also give it to the client. If validation fails before commitment, no country is partially created. If a native ownership transfer changes an input during commitment, preserve a recoverable receipt and settle the actual outcome rather than applying the whole setup again.

## Territory and capital rules

The selected core region is a contiguous controlled state group where possible. Islands use a coherent authored island or port group. The candidate list is visible before commitment, with disputed and unavailable states marked separately. Founding a client grants only the territorial status justified by the selected origin and route. It does not automatically grant cores on every conquest, all members of a target religion, or every territory held by its sponsor.

Capital selection prioritizes an actually controlled political and supply center with usable infrastructure, industry or a viable administrative role, and a connection to the client's territory. A predeclared ordered fallback list handles loss of the preferred capital. The fallback must be inside the client's real controlled area and must not seize a neutral country to create a capital. A country with no viable remaining capital or territory transitions through normal capitulation or an explicitly supported exile state.

Client release and peace settlement must use one shared territory eligibility definition. A focus completion or an old snapshot of owned states cannot promise a region the sponsor no longer controls. Controller and owner are both checked. A disputed state remains an unresolved territorial issue rather than disappearing from the plan.

## Country archetypes

### Founding state

The founder is an existing state whose government has been captured by the movement. Its national economy, army, diplomatic history, technology, and meaningful focus tree survive the seizure. Its opening problem is divided authority over institutions and resources. It must choose between concentrating command, negotiating a wider order, and dismantling the movement through the rupture route.

Its military plan follows its actual location and equipment. A strong continental founder can begin with a substantial army but attract a powerful defense coalition. A small maritime founder has diplomacy and transport opportunities but cannot conjure a continental force. The starting order spirit combines the political seizure and command instability. Later route changes transform that slot rather than stacking a new permanent spirit for every milestone.

### Continental frontier client

This client holds a coherent land frontier and a supply connection to a sponsor or local industrial base. It needs a capital behind the front, a garrison sufficient to secure key points, field units funded by a recorded sponsor allocation, and infantry equipment production or reliable imports. Its main political problem is the sponsor's demand for an offensive while local authority remains fragile.

Its early focus choices repair rail and supply access, integrate the transferred command, and choose who bears the burden of occupation. Its military branch can emphasize a defensible frontier or a prepared limited offensive. Expansion requires new sustainment and a real local government. Its autonomy route can retain the crusade but refuse offensive orders, making it a persistent independent opponent after the sponsor falls.

### Maritime bridgehead client

This client holds a viable port and a defensible local hinterland or island group. Its package includes actual convoy access, a protected supply route, coastal defense, transport capacity, and a realistic source of fuel and equipment. A navy is transferred only through supported native ownership and a real sponsor contribution. Ships are not duplicated from a sponsor fleet.

Its early choice is between concentrating resources on survival and preparing a new overseas campaign. Its economy depends on port throughput, convoys, fuel, and the cost of imported equipment. Losing the principal port can remove terminal region credit immediately. A second port or local production can provide resilience if actually built and connected. The focus route cannot skip naval invasion prerequisites or require a naval DLC to function.

### Interior client under supply pressure

This client is possible only when local production and a credible access agreement can support a government. It begins with limited forces, constrained imports, and a dispute over dependence. It cannot launch distant wars while surrounded by states refusing transit. The important decisions concern opening a route, reducing military commitments, investing in local sustainment, or leaving an impossible compact.

Its political identity differs from the frontier client's. Local commanders and civic institutions have stronger bargaining power because the sponsor cannot easily enforce orders. A sponsor that withholds supplies may cause an independent crusader government, a negotiated repudiation, or a bounded internal conflict. No outcome gives it free access through a neutral state.

### Independent adherent successor

A client or member becomes a successor by retaining a real government, controlled territory or a supported force, the doctrine, and an intent to continue the crusade. It keeps actual forces, equipment, wars, and records. It receives no second founding army, opening stockpile, technology package, or first-region Chaos reward.

It can build a new compact, seek limited partners, dispute the old founder's authority, or conduct a more regional campaign. Its focus and decision access reflect its existing route. It does not reset to an untouched client tree. A new terminal candidacy requires actual current agreements and a fresh durability period.

### Repudiating and liberated successor

A government leaving the doctrine faces loyalist institutions, damaged civilian administration, unresolved custody, displaced people, and diplomatic distrust. It must secure its authority, stop persecution, release or lawfully transfer living custody, and provide a credible political settlement. An internal reform does not retroactively make it the liberator of its own earlier victims.

The successor keeps the region's actual industry and surviving armed forces after legitimate settlement. It can retain ordinary military expertise while losing event-specific command privileges tied to persecution and crusading allegiance. Its focus path supports reconstruction, civilian protection, demobilization or defensive reorganization, and diplomacy. Recognition depends on verified conduct and agreements, not a one-click ideology switch.

## Starting force and reinforcement envelope

Use ordinary infantry, cavalry or motorized formations where appropriate, artillery and support companies already available to the country, and ordinary air and naval units when its geography supports them. The movement's differences arise from recruitment, command reliability, equipment mix, supply, and political commitments. Its soldiers do not need a new physical silhouette, combat class, equipment type, or custom battalion to make these choices work.

For tuning, a small viable frontier client controlling roughly three ordinary states and two military factories can begin with a sponsor allocation supporting about 12 to 24 infantry battalions across a few divisions, plus only the support equipment it can afford. This is a reference envelope, not a free spawn entitlement. Actual units are clamped by transferred or locally available manpower, equipment, deployment control, supply, and the sponsor's ability to survive the transfer. A bridgehead normally begins with a smaller defensive allocation and a larger transport burden. An industrial successor inherits its existing army rather than being normalized to this small-client example.

Reinforcement uses recruitment laws, actual manpower, production, captured stockpiles where native rules allow, and explicit aid. Civilian killing must never yield recruitable manpower. Camp labor must not be converted into a special pool of free crusader replacements. Aid equipment, units, fuel, and convoys are deducted or transferred once, with the receiving outcome measured or proven.

Country templates must avoid both extremes of empty unsupported divisions and instant elite armies. The equipment mix can be obsolete or heterogeneous when the sponsor and captured region justify it. Training and modernization cost time and production. Research bonuses or grants remain limited to relevant existing technologies with verified prerequisites, provenance, and no-DLC behavior.

The chosen native creation path must prove the actual resource outcome. The offline references describe OOB equipment factors that grant equipment without debiting reserves, and a `create_unit` equipment factor of zero that can produce full equipment. An empty-looking template is therefore not a funding mechanism. Bind the exact template, equipment model, producer, source debit and created formation to one receipt. Do not turn an obsolete donated stockpile into the latest unlocked equipment. Native manpower draws, civil-war stockpile splits and disband refunds must be reconciled with escrow so that neither initialization nor cleanup charges or refunds the same resource twice. These native details require installed-version verification before use.

## Occupation, autonomy, and peace

The sponsor must choose how much territory to administer directly and how much to delegate. Direct control preserves command and industry access but increases the burden on the sponsor. Delegation creates a local army and government that can survive independently and demand autonomy. Neither automatically removes resistance or the cost of civilian administration.

Independence changes who commands and supplies the country. It does not end its wars or its persecution by definition. A peace settlement can dismantle the compact in one region while other governments continue elsewhere. The event therefore evaluates living governments, wars, authority, and current doctrine after each material settlement.

Creating an absent country and freeing a living subject are different native operations. Releasing an already living tag cannot stand in for changing its autonomy. Bind any independence operation to the current overlord and actual autonomy relation, and declare the intended war and occupied-state behavior explicitly. A dynamic country must receive its valid territory within a supported persistent creation transaction, with unwanted inherited cores reconciled before completion. Territorial ownership alone does not establish control, supply or authority over custody. Capitulation handling must preserve or account for surviving forces at the correct native phase, before any later callback assumes that units or equipment still exist.

Liberation can restore a legitimate existing country where the map, surviving actors, and political settlement support it. It can also produce a recognized successor rather than a forced restoration to a vanished political order. The package requires explicit regional authorship for those alternatives. A global generic country-release script cannot decide legitimacy from a core alone.

## DLC and implementation completeness

Core formation, membership, campaigns, client survival, target policy, persecution integration, relief, focus routes, evolution, and terminal behavior must work without DLC. Optional autonomy, intelligence, MIO, raid, naval, or trade features can enrich a supported route only after a source-verified equivalent core outcome exists. The base implementation uses ordinary diplomacy, decisions, missions, and supported military families. No critical protection or resistance objective depends on an optional agency or raid mechanic.

This package adds no new doctrine tree or equipment family. Any copied client technology or research bonus still requires technology inspection, rendering, and comparison. New country templates and ordinary unit configurations require their normal source and map validation. Choosing existing units removes unnecessary asset production, not country-package depth or verification.
