# Decisions, missions, and opposition

All action and mission labels below are working labels, not final localisation. Numeric amounts are proposed balance anchors, not claims about historical resource consumption or installed implementation.

## Interaction model

Use ordinary decision categories with phase and role filtering. A country sees at most six primary actions at once and normally one to three active missions. A current selected country, state group, or relief request determines the relevant target-specific actions. The actor can browse valid targets through the project's existing supported selection method. The AI evaluates all useful valid targets independently of the human's current selection.

The event does not need a dedicated management window. Bloc cohesion is a concise formatted value with its current causes. Membership and readiness explanations use the shared Event Details framework. Shared Camp Administration, Migration, Resistance, and Condemnation retain their own controls. Links to those owner surfaces are navigation, not duplicate execution buttons.

Actions move through availability, current quote, commitment, active process, measured outcome, and settlement. A target disappearing, a charter changing, a border closing, or a government losing authority can invalidate an action. The response must distinguish a genuine failed operation from a stale action that should never have committed.

Native decision eligibility and mission activation do not replace that operation state. Startup-only `allowed` checks cannot maintain current affiliation. A custom cost display does not debit resources, and direct activation can bypass the usual visibility or activation tests. Scripted removal can omit the ordinary removal effect. Validate and create the operation receipt before opening a funded mission, and settle cancellation explicitly before removing its surface. Target-specific fire-once settings do not deduplicate the same operation offered through a focus, another sponsor or Tasks.

## Cost and duration rules

Every substantive family has a material cost or requirement in addition to political authority. A formal refusal, an information request, or canceling an uncommitted proposal is an administrative action and is free. Leaving the doctrine is not made impossible by an arbitrary fee. The political and military work of completing a contested departure can still consume real resources.

Each action has at most four spendable resource types. Command Power never exceeds 60. The inline display shows at most three values with correct texticons, with a complete breakdown in the tooltip. Non-consumed requirements such as a controlled port, available army, or existing guarantee are identified separately. A reserved industrial commitment is a real cost and counts toward the limit.

Use the shared universal cost owner and inclusive affordability. The displayed quote, available check, custom cost trigger, payment, effect-time recheck, AI equivalent, refund, and cleanup must agree. A timed factory commitment cannot also be charged as an invented second stockpile of industrial points. Native and custom costs cannot debit the same resource twice.

Three size bands guide missions. A local or bilateral task normally starts at 90 days. A substantial regional task uses 120 to 180 days. A large campaign, multi-state resettlement, or succession process uses 180 to 365 days. A 30 to 60-day emergency is allowed only for an actual imminent battlefield or route loss and must remain achievable from current conditions. Failed diplomacy cannot impose a hidden two-week rescue deadline on an unprepared country.

Duration and cost scale from the current actor, target, distance, route, force, administrative reach, industry, affected living amount where known, equipment, war urgency, prior preparation, and existing commitments. They have explicit minimums and maximums. An action does not demand more equipment than its recipient can meaningfully use merely because world Chaos rose.

### Mission: establish the first operational compact

The founder seeks one willing government with a practical material and military commitment. The 120-day anchor scales with diplomatic distance, target controversy, competing wars, and whether a land or sea route exists. A signed symbolic statement without access or a usable contribution does not count.

Success automatically records the compact and makes the next campaign planning actions available. It adds no free army and no second founding reward. Failure leaves a contained founder and changes the movement's political options. It does not force an uninvolved country to join to keep the script moving.

### Mission: honor a defense promise

A member under a real attack can request the support its compact actually promised. The mission names the threatened theater, available forms of assistance, and the amount or military participation that would satisfy the promise. A 90-day anchor can shorten when a current front creates an immediate emergency.

Success is verified delivery or valid war assistance under the diplomatic owner. It raises cohesion once for that episode. Losing the route, the recipient repudiating the charter, or the war ending can cancel or transform the obligation. A failed promise can lower cohesion and strengthen withdrawal or succession options. Sending equipment to the wrong country cannot satisfy the mission.

### Mission: sustain a forward region

The current holder must maintain a viable capital or command center, supply, administration, and actual field forces for a 180-day anchor. The mission begins after conquest or client creation and uses current evidence. It cannot be completed through an empty province or an uncontested event flag.

The result is a proven local base and completion of a distinct obligation. Supply loss suspends progress under the durability rules, and loss of control resets the affected local tenure. The mission grants no extra population, permanent blanket combat bonus, or repeated first-region Chaos. It can contribute evidence to terminal readiness, but it is not itself a terminal unlock.

### Mission: withdraw from an unsustainable front

A member can negotiate the return of troops, equipment and responsibilities from a theater it can no longer support. The 120 to 180-day anchor depends on route, war state and native diplomatic constraints. A successful withdrawal preserves actual surviving forces, settles commitments, and changes political relationships.

The mission does not teleport surrounded units, reclaim equipment already lost, or move detained people through an army withdrawal. Civilian evacuation and custody remain separate shared-owner operations. A route collapse can produce a real military loss or a revised extraction objective rather than a full refund of resources already consumed.

### Mission: maintain a protected destination

A receiving government sustains actual accepted reception for a 120 to 180-day anchor. It must keep the destination under valid protection, maintain the shared reception and food obligations, and avoid unauthorized forced return. It can seek another willing destination if conditions deteriorate.

The mission's progress follows living cohorts and shared receipts. It does not require an arbitrary kill-or-rescue total. Success can unlock recognition, further negotiated support, or an achievement condition. Any material aid reward must come from a real agreeing sponsor or an existing shared reward owner and must not exceed its recorded commitment.

### Mission: protect a threatened route

This mission is offered only when the player can affect a real route through territory, diplomacy, transport, or military action. A country with no army, fleet, border, or diplomatic access to the route receives a different feasible task. The standard anchor is 120 days. An actual closing front can create a shorter emergency with a shown cause.

Success is the owner's recognition of usable access for the intended operation, followed by the relevant actual movement or defense. Merely controlling a distant port or opening a diplomatic menu does not count. If an ally resolves the route first, the task becomes support for reception or closes without duplicate credit.

### Mission: liberate and stabilize a region

This is a 180 to 365-day campaign objective for a country able to fight in the region or materially support an allied liberator. The objective has two linked results: ending the defeated crusader authority's control and meeting the shared obligations for surviving people and sites. The military phase can succeed while relief remains incomplete.

The displayed progress names current territorial conditions, active local authority, custody obligations, and the remaining achievable relief work. It never presents a percentage that treats dead civilians as uncompleted rescue. Once the authority is defeated, prior losses stay recorded. A later counterattack can invalidate control without replaying earlier survivor releases or evidence discoveries.

## Doctrine crisis and terminal family

Purity Doctrine presents the exact new charter to each affected government. Its primary choices are assent, request a narrower agreement where the movement's internal politics permit it, refuse the change, or begin withdrawal or repudiation. These are political decisions with material follow-through, not a repeatable doctrine shopping interface.

The Final Crusade preparation surface explains current readiness. Its actions concern missing logistics, unstable members, current theaters, and opposition response. It never offers a button to buy eligibility with Chaos or civilian deaths. The final commitment is a unique validated transition under the shared world-end owner. Before it commits, players can stop it by changing real current conditions. After it commits, defeating the movement remains possible but ordinary automatic events stay stopped.

| Member in an active war | Current campaign, aid, obligation, order response, selected territory, withdrawal | Up to three missions, immediate defense before expansion |

The rows describe a budget, not fixed duplicated buttons. One action changes with the selected target and current phase. Shared-system links consume visible space and are included in the six-row planning budget when presented beside primary actions. Lower priority valid opportunities remain discoverable through the selected-target view and the owner's normal interface. They do not flood the decision list.

## Integrity, AI, and rewards

Objective success is automatic. A completed mission cannot require a click to claim an indefinitely renewable reward. The success check revalidates the current target and the specific episode, then settles once. A success condition achieved by an ally follows the authored shared-credit rule, not an extra full reward for every country that had a mission open.

Each pending confirmation freezes actor, target, charter, price quote, operation identity, and intended outcome. A free Cancel option comes first in an event confirmation so timeout cannot accept a war, a transfer, or a targeting change. Effect-time checks reject stale authority and stale targets. A refund settles the exact payment receipt and cannot refund an older completed action.

Only unconsumed, provably refundable commitments are returned after a genuine underway operation fails. An equipment shipment destroyed in a real operation is not restored because the mission timer expired. Conversely, a stale click rejected before a valid operation must not keep the paid resources or grant a scripted cooldown or failure reward. Native cooldown behavior after a refunded click remains an implementation validation point.

AI decisions use the same cost, target, route, authority, and outcome predicates. AI must decline impossible rescue tasks, preserve a defensive reserve, and avoid repetitive invitations, aid loops, client releases, and doctrinal reversals. The probability audit must test the actual candidate pool and current score semantics. A willingness score is not a click probability.

## Settlement rules resolved by the decision audit

Every material operation has one durable identity comprising the founding or manual episode, initiating actor, recipient or exact target, action family, applicable charter, and an operation nonce. A quote, mission, shipment, shared-system receipt, task contribution and cohesion outcome reference that operation. A new callback or a new selected target does not create a new copy of an existing obligation.

One primary owner settles the operation. Other sponsors receive explicit contribution receipts for the actual resources or military support they supplied. An Event 101 task and an Event 115 mission can recognize the same contribution but cannot issue two material rewards for it. A route-opening milestone and a later movement are related records with different outcomes, not two claims that the same people were rescued twice.

The payer of negotiated support is the government making that contribution. The recipient's separate reception or military obligation uses its own payer and child receipt. The invitation sender does not pay the recipient's contribution implicitly. A subject's payment must use supported authority over that resource. Treaty promises, access, available forces and a receiving government's policy are requirements unless the quoted action actually consumes or reserves them.

| Cost state | Required settlement |
| --- | --- |
| Quoted but uncommitted | No debit. Cancellation simply retires the quote. |
| Reserved before physical work | Record payer, resource, quoted amount, amount reserved, amount consumed and amount released. A stale rejection returns the full refundable debit and releases future capacity. |
| Transport or support equipment assigned | Consume or transfer only under the transport or military owner's actual operation rule. Unused committed equipment is released. Real verified loss remains lost. |
| Fuel used in a valid operation | Charge the owner-reported actual consumption, within the accepted quote. Unused reserved fuel returns. Zero movement cannot justify inventing a fuel-consumption record. |
| Timed civilian-factory commitment | Track elapsed actual allocation and remaining future allocation. Release unused future time on cancellation. Do not credit past factory-days as equipment, PP or new factories. |
| Partial successful movement | Settle measured survivor delivery, route losses and actual resources used. Release the unused remainder. Report partial status and remaining need, with no full-success reward. |
| Failed or inconsistent physical accounting | No success or population load is published. Retain the exact recovery receipt or prove rollback of this invocation. Never compensate with an unrelated generic population addition. |

Costs that genuinely scale with the operation's accepted living amount use the actual authorized tranche. Fixed preparation costs are disclosed separately. The owner must validate the operation and its capacity before consuming a fixed preparation cost. This prevents repeatedly charging a full quote for a route that was already invalid when clicked. A campaign under way can incur real costs even if its military objective is later lost, provided the receipt proves them.

The liberation mission has explicit phases. The military phase uses a 180 to 245-day anchor and succeeds when the specific defeated crusader authority loses the relevant control through the operation or a documented allied contribution. Its automatic milestone changes the active mission to the relief and settlement phase. The latter normally receives 90 to 120 days, keeping the complete planned operation within 365 days. Only one phase occupies a visible active mission slot at a time.

Military success remains in history while relief is pending. Final success requires current protection and the applicable shared custody, release, site and reception obligations. If a counterattack removes protection, the current phase becomes a renewed protection problem without replaying earlier release or evidence. A region with no live custody or applicable site obligations can satisfy the relief predicates immediately, but only through a valid owner proof rather than a missing event flag.

Purity Doctrine disabled, ineligible or rejected follows the baseline paths in `07_purity_doctrine_and_final_crusade.md`. Baseline offers remain valid unless their actual charter was superseded. The terminal stop and continuation lists in that file and the integration contract apply to all mission workers, including independent adherents. No broad world-end early return may strand shared civilian or successor work.
