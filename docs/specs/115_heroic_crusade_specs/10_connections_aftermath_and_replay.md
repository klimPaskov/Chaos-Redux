## Malta Crusaders, Event 038

Malta reacts according to the religious direction, political route and institutions it has actually developed. A shared religious vocabulary can lead to negotiation, rivalry over authority, conditional cooperation, or direct opposition. Actual target definitions, the treatment of threatened communities, sovereignty, and operational obligations decide compatibility.

A protective Malta can offer reception, medical or relief support through existing shared owners, conditional diplomatic protection, and a defense partnership. A militant Malta can contest leadership or consider a scoped alliance. A Malta whose own people, protected institutions, or accepted doctrine are threatened becomes a plausible opponent. None of those choices occurs from matching one religion flag alone.

Event 038 retains its ownership of Malta, the Holy See, Kingdom of God, its existing believer politics, and its own Holy World terminal specification. Heroic Crusade cannot recreate those countries under new tags, force Malta into the order, replace its tree, or make Malta's ending a stage of this event.

The pinned Malta package also specifies a hidden regional campaign called Operation The Final Crusade. That is separate from the user-approved Event 115 terminal title The Final Crusade. Preserve separate runtime identities, artwork, audio, trigger ownership and event-history entries. A substring match on the title must never connect them.

The old Malta cross-event paragraph for Event 126 prohibits an independent religious faction or terminal. The user's current brief explicitly requests those features. The migration therefore needs a narrow update of that stale paragraph, preserving Malta's own route ownership while allowing this independent compatible or rival order. This package does not modify the existing Malta specification or claim that its detailed terminal and hidden routes are implemented.

## The Holy Realm

The Holy Realm reacts according to its developed religious order, government, recognized authority, current enemies, and route. Its own political development remains available. A route protecting groups targeted by the crusade can oppose it. A route accepting the crusade's authority and actual charter can negotiate a limited alliance or full commitment. A route claiming competing universal authority can become a rival even where the two use similar religious language.

A joint military operation does not combine the two events' ending conditions. Holy Realm retains its own terminal ownership and any existing aftermath routing. Heroic Crusade retains strict Chaos greater than 1000 and its own current-bloc tests. The global terminal guard arbitrates simultaneous candidates without renumbering or relabeling either one.

The inspected Holy Realm overview contains a reserved Final Crusade contrast hook but describes the cross-event material as unimplemented. Treat it as a reserved integration point requiring an actual owner contract. A reserved flag is not proof that a religion or target comparison already works.

The complete pinned Holy Realm overview and effects route its current terminal completion as a request to the shared maximum-intensity Fallout aftermath, preserving Final Silence cause memory. Older descriptions of regional strike waves are legacy design or compatibility evidence. Heroic Crusade must not copy that routing into its own final war. Holy Realm's super-event and audio identities 8 and 9 remain occupied compatibility identities even when one route delegates presentation or aftermath elsewhere.

The inspected Holy Realm ritual gate also requires a stage flag through its final-path helper while forbidding that same flag in the ritual predicate. This is a static contradiction in that owner's source, not a tested engine result. It remains a Holy Realm reconciliation issue and does not authorize changing its route here. The Heroic adapter cannot use that contradictory ritual trigger or automatically initialized reserved flags as proof of a working diplomatic or terminal interface. It needs current positive contact facts, actual government consent and its own validated compatibility contract.

## This is SPARTA!!!

The user's connection identifies this as Event 111. Supplied and pinned catalog snapshots use original 116 for This is SPARTA!!!, while old 111 is a different catalog entry. Use the semantic connection and record the crosswalk until migration resolves the runtime identity.

SPARTA's developed religious direction and actual political order decide its response. A compatible martial government can negotiate command, territory and military support. A government protecting a targeted group or rejecting the crusade's authority can oppose it. A direction incompatible with the founder's charter cannot be turned into an ally solely because both have militarized presentation.

Any alliance uses real units, equipment, supply, access and obligations. The event does not borrow SPARTA's unique units without its provider and asset contracts. If the current SPARTA implementation is unavailable, the connection remains a guarded dormant hook and cannot block the core Heroic Crusade campaign.

## Tasks, Event 101

An active crusade can provide Tasks with a concrete defense or relief objective when that player can achieve it. The task broker considers the player's controlled territory, army, industry, transport, diplomatic access, receiving capacity, current wars and commitments. It selects one useful objective rather than a generic demand to defeat the whole movement.

Eligible objective templates are supporting an existing defense promise, making a real route usable, preparing and maintaining reception, delivering verified aid to an eligible resistance actor, helping liberate a reachable region, or securing and assisting survivors after actual liberation. Each uses its shared owner receipts and the current phase's reasonable time band.

A player unable to reach the military front can receive a material or reception role. A country with no feasible contribution receives no crusade task. If another actor completes the objective or the crusader government falls, Tasks can transform the objective into relevant settlement work or close it without penalty and duplicate reward. The Task and Event 115 mission cannot each award full credit for the same paid shipment under different identifiers.

Current callable Tasks and SPARTA APIs were not established by the bounded repository inspection. Their adapters are proposed integration work with explicit validity guards. Core event progression cannot depend on these presently unverified optional connections.

## Other simultaneous crises

Other wars, famine, displacement and territorial changes remain real. Heroic Crusade cannot claim sole ownership of a state's population losses or repurpose a crisis country's special mechanics by copying its flags. When a shared system already knows the cause, the event adds its political context through that owner.

Several religious or ideological movements can coexist and oppose one another. Their targets, actual institutions, sovereignty claims and strategic relationships decide cooperation. No global religion flag merges them into a single army or shared terminal timer. Existing unrelated world-threat sources remain active when this event is contained or defeated.

Human crusader governments remain ordinary human populations for shared civilian systems. Special Chaos-country registration is bookkeeping for unusual actors. It does not make these governments nonhuman or exempt them from famine, migration, deaths, repression or ordinary military costs.

## Ordinary endings

| Outcome | Political result | What persists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early founder suppression | The movement fails to maintain the captured government before it builds an international base | Actual opening losses, custody, local damage, responsibility and any displacement |
| Contained regional order | One or more governments still sustain the doctrine but have no credible broad expansion | Their persecution, local wars, resistance, sanctions and opportunities for protection or reform |
| Negotiated fragmentation | Governments leave the common compact with different political settlements | Independent adherents remain opponents, repudiating states still owe civilian settlement, native wars continue where unresolved |
| Founder defeated, clients survive | The original command is gone | Every real successor government, army, war, institution, target charter and responsibility record |
| International military defeat | All sustaining governments lose current continuation capacity | Shared losses, displaced populations, ruined regions, evidence and successor politics |
| Widespread repudiation | Governments cease doctrine through actual authority and shared-owner settlement | Earlier responsibility, unrecovered industry, refugees, and surviving armed or institutional opposition |

None of these outcomes is an automatic global peace treaty. A threatened state can emerge with contested borders. A liberator can have its own political ambitions. A client can split from its sponsor without joining the opposition. Settlement remains attached to concrete actors and the map.

## Terminal aftermath

When The Final Crusade has committed, ordinary automatic event firing stays permanently stopped regardless of victory, defeat or prolonged stalemate. Existing systems and the final war continue. The terminal history remains visible after the last crusader government falls.

The defeat presentation should show the surviving world with factual scale from the save. It can refer to destroyed governments, liberation, loss and displacement only when those outcomes actually happened. The absence of recorded Deaths under disabled logging does not imply that physical losses were zero. The presentation must use the supported evidence rather than reconstructing an invented total.

Recognition, reconstruction, safe return, continued asylum, demobilization and preservation of records can extend beyond the military conflict. These are ordinary shared consequences and successor decisions, not a second event-owned world-end sequence. The final struggle can end with devastated but living states rather than a tidy restoration of the original map.

## Replay structure

Replay comes from a different valid founder, target charter, government and map situation, command arrangement, international response, conquered client geography, internal doctrinal dispute, and succession outcome. The same core rules support a narrow regional crisis and a world-spanning order. Hidden mass bonuses or random unsupported demographic selections are unnecessary.

The primary authored founder and conditional alternative are defined in the country-origin supplement. Their historical anchors establish political institutions and an initial victim profile. The international order, new client administrations and terminal campaign remain declared alternate history. Additional founder profiles require the same source, identity, target, country and asset completeness before entering the selection pool.

## Manual scenario

Plan one manual scenario that launches the founding crisis through the same validated setup as the ordinary event. Its structural name is a working label and its scenario ID remains unassigned. The player can choose a supported founder role or a currently feasible threatened or protecting role, subject to the selected authored profile and current map.

The scenario follows the shared framework's bypass of ordinary event-history and minimum-tier selection constraints. It still requires suitable actors, supported target data, real country setup, no conflicting active episode, and compatible global terminal state. It does not silently restore a dead historical leader, partition an unrelated living country, grant unsupported demographics, or create a free multi-region army.

A later manually requested episode has its own scenario receipt and history under the scenario owner. It cannot create two simultaneously active copies of the order, erase earlier civilian records, or duplicate a currently living client. The ordinary fire-once history remains consumed and is never reset as a side effect of manual replay.

The initial scenario may coordinate a small documented Chaos setup with the shared scenario owner, without forcing World Collapse. Its ordinary subsequent political and shared-system consequences remain active. The Final Crusade retains strict Chaos greater than 1000, both settings, current capability, durability and the single-active-ending guard even under manual setup.
