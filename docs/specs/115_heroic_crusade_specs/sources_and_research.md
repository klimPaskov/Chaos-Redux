## Reasoning derived from the sources

The design makes political affiliation an institutional choice because a shared religion does not establish agreement with persecution. It gives threatened communities agency, requires both departure and reception capacity, and leaves meaningful rescue work after military liberation. These are explicit design inferences from R1 through R3, not purported engine facts.

Romania and conditional Slovakia are selected authored origins because their historical institutional contexts permit a concrete alternate-history divergence. The three client contexts are selected for different military and administrative problems. A maritime base needs real sea access. A frontier administration needs defensible supply and a lawful accounting of inherited forces. An interior administration must govern a connected region. The military rules themselves are gameplay proposals.

The terminal population charter described in Parts 03 and 07 is invented for the event. Its positive religious inclusion rules describe the persecuting government's fictional doctrine. They do not state the beliefs of the underlying religious communities or certify a real person as an adherent.
