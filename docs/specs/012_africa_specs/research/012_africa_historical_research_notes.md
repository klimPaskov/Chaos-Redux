## Research framing

UNESCO describes the General History of Africa as a project launched in 1964 to reframe African history from an African perspective. This supports a spec that treats African history as a dense source of institutions, old states, trade corridors, religious centers, and political memories rather than a list of colonial borders.

The African Union and Agenda 2063 sources support the design contrast between an aspirational integrated Africa and the mod's chaotic 1930s war-game fantasy. The event should borrow the unity and self-determination themes as inspiration while remaining alternate-history, unstable, and gameplay-driven.

Marcus Garvey, the UNIA, and the Black Star Line support a diaspora route. The route should not be a simple manpower button. It should include shipping, ports, housing, Black-owned commerce, propaganda, engineering, medical cadres, and political backlash.

## Polity anchor notes by region

### Nile, Nubia, and Sudan corridor

- Kerma, Kush, Meroe, Nobatia, Makuria, Alodia, Funj, and Darfur can anchor Nile and Nubian restoration content.
- Use river logistics, Nile forts, old Christian Nubian and Islamic Sudanese memory, desert cavalry, and cotton or grain projects.
- Flags and symbols need sourcing. Some old polities have archaeological or manuscript motifs rather than modern flags, so historically grounded fictional synthesis may be needed.

### Maghreb and Sahara

- Garamantes, Numidia, Mauretania, Fezzan, Tuareg confederations, Ifriqiya, Tlemcen, and coastal Maghreb routes can anchor Sahara and North African content.
- Use wells, desert roads, caravan memory, mechanized desert columns, oasis forts, and Mediterranean port politics.
- Avoid treating desert peoples as generic raiders. The design should distinguish trade, water control, mobility, and old city networks.

### Western Sahel

- Wagadu, Mali, Songhai, Tekrur, Jolof, Kaabu, Futa Toro, Futa Jallon, Mossi, Hausa states, Sokoto, Kanem-Bornu, Wadai, and Bagirmi can anchor rich Sahel country packages.
- Use cavalry, river trade, scholarly cities, Islamic reform states, griot memory, caravan taxation, and anti-colonial rail politics.
- Timbuktu and Niger Bend content should interact with education, archives, desert logistics, and trans-Saharan trade, not only conquest.

### Guinea Coast

- Ife, Oyo, Benin or Edo, Dahomey, Allada, Whydah, Asante, Bono, Denkyira, Dagbon, Kong, and Bamum provide many direct names and internal political paths.
- The Met and Penn sources support Oyo, Benin, and Dahomey as powerful states with armies, art, court ritual, and client relationships.
- Use royal court ceremony, cavalry, iron technology, port trade, stool or palace symbolism, and local rivalries.

### Central Africa and Congo Basin

- Kongo, Loango, Ndongo, Matamba, Kuba, Luba, Lunda, Kazembe, Chokwe, Mangbetu, and Azande support river, forest, court, and trade routes.
- The Met Kongo chronology supports Kongo as a large centralized state with Mbanza, nobility, taxation, long-distance trade, and Portuguese relations.
- Use river depots, forest logistics, court regalia, Christian and local religious intersections, ivory and copper routes, and resistance to outside concessions.

### Horn, Red Sea, and Somalia

- Aksum, Zagwe, Adal, Ifat, Harar, Ajuran, Mogadishu, Majeerteen, and Warsangali support Red Sea, highland, Islamic, Christian, and Indian Ocean routes.
- Aksum should support coinage, stelae, Red Sea trade, Ethiopian highland legitimacy, and Christian imperial memory.
- Somali and Red Sea polities should use ports, maritime trade, caravan routes, and clan or sultanate structures carefully.

### Swahili and Indian Ocean

- Kilwa, Sofala, Mombasa, Malindi, Lamu, Pate, Zanzibar, Comoros, Madagascar variants, Merina, Sakalava, and Betsimisaraka support Indian Ocean content.
- UNESCO and Met sources support Kilwa and Songo Mnara as Swahili coast sites tied to Islamisation and Indian Ocean trade.
- Use coral architecture, harbor dues, dhow routes, gold and ivory flows, island politics, and convoy missions.

### Great Lakes

- Buganda, Bunyoro, Ankole, Rwanda, and Burundi support lake kingship, cattle, hill defense, and highland politics.
- Use lake corridors, royal drums, cattle wealth, mountain routes, and refugee or border missions.

### Southern Africa

- Mapungubwe, Great Zimbabwe, Mutapa, Rozwi, Butua, Barotse, Zulu, Xhosa, Basotho, Swazi, Tswana, Ovambo, Herero, Nama, and Gaza support southern content.
- UNESCO and Met sources support Great Zimbabwe as a Shona center with major stone architecture and trade links.
- Use gold, copper, cattle, railways, fortification, highland logistics, settler reaction, and RSA civil war branches.

## Design lessons from research

1. The continent-unification fantasy should be layered by trade corridors, old capitals, ports, railways, courts, and religious centers.
2. Restored countries should exist as playable local actors with direct names and local roles.
3. Integration should use state projects and political legitimacy, not instant cores.
4. Ports, rivers, railways, and resources should matter as much as front lines.
5. The diaspora route should be logistical, cultural, and economic, not only demographic.
6. The high-chaos routes should use myths, landscapes, animals, disasters, and supernatural forces as clearly alternate-history or nonhuman content.
