# Event 12 Africa historical research and bibliography

## Purpose

This note records the research basis used to turn Event 12 into a continent-scale political system rather than a generic conquest route.

It supports:

- the Charter League
- regional congresses
- protection-first decolonisation
- restored and revived polities
- trade and infrastructure routes
- diaspora return
- South Africa's special civil war branch
- source-mode rules for flags, portraits, symbols, and event art

This is a planning bibliography. It is not a substitute for specialist review of every final state group, leader, flag, court title, or language string.

## Research method

The design uses several kinds of sources:

- African Union and United Nations material for continental unity and decolonisation
- university and archival material for Pan-African congresses
- UNESCO World Heritage material for historic centres, kingdoms, trade cities, and living institutions
- museum research essays for statecraft, court systems, material culture, trade, and regional political networks
- South African historical material for the 1939 wartime split and anti-war movements
- United States public-history material for diaspora politics and the Universal Negro Improvement Association

The planning package converts historical anchors into gameplay themes. It does not claim that one modern event host is a direct legal successor to every historical state.

## Pan-African political anchors

### Continental unity as a political programme

The African Union describes Pan-Africanism through unity, self-determination, freedom, progress, and collective prosperity. This supports a design where continental unity is an institutional and developmental project rather than only an annexation reward.

Design deductions:

- Charter Authority should matter alongside territorial control
- economic integration should be visible
- members should retain agency
- unity can take federal, confederal, republican, monarchical, socialist, military, or high-chaos forms
- the post-unification route should have a continental development and diplomatic programme

Source:

- [About the African Union](https://au.int/en/overview), African Union

### Congress politics and the 1945 Pan-African Congress

The University of Manchester preserves material relating to the 1945 Pan-African Congress. University material identifies the congress as a meeting point for activists and future leaders who connected anti-colonial politics with national liberation strategies.

Design deductions:

- congresses should be active political institutions
- regional and continental delegates should bargain over membership and constitutional form
- the Charter League should have activist, labour, intellectual, government, and diaspora channels
- the event should not start with instant government unanimity

Sources:

- [Research Collections](https://www.library.manchester.ac.uk/aiu-race-centre/collections/archives/researchcollections/), University of Manchester Library
- [Symposium to celebrate the 75th anniversary of the 5th Pan-African Congress in Manchester](https://blog.gdi.manchester.ac.uk/symposium-to-celebrate-the-75th-anniversary-of-the-5th-pan-african-congress/), University of Manchester Global Development Institute

### Decolonisation as international law and political conflict

The United Nations adopted the Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples on 14 December 1960. The Event 12 campaign begins earlier, so the declaration is used as later historical context rather than as a 1936 legal instrument.

Design deductions:

- anti-colonial claims should be framed through self-government, protection, recognition, and withdrawal
- outside powers should have choices between negotiated withdrawal and war
- the unifier should be able to recognise provisional governments and support liberation movements
- global reaction should evolve with date and world state

Source:

- [Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples](https://legal.un.org/avl/ha/dicc/dicc.html), United Nations Audiovisual Library of International Law

## North-East Africa and Nile anchors

### Kush and Meroe

UNESCO identifies the Island of Meroe as the heartland of the Kingdom of Kush, a major power centred between the Nile and Atbara rivers.

Design deductions:

- Kush is a high-legitimacy restoration candidate in the Nile region
- river transport, sacred centres, royal sites, and desert logistics should shape its package
- Kush, Nubia, Makuria, and Alodia require overlap rules rather than simultaneous unlimited claims

Source:

- [Archaeological Sites of the Island of Meroe](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1336/), UNESCO World Heritage Centre

### Aksum

UNESCO describes Aksum as the heart of an ancient state that was a major power between the Eastern Roman Empire and Persia.

Design deductions:

- Aksum should have Red Sea, trade, monument, and royal legitimacy mechanics
- its restoration route should interact with Ethiopia, Eritrea, coastal access, and religious authority
- it should be a major regional anchor rather than a cosmetic release

Source:

- [Aksum](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/15/), UNESCO World Heritage Centre

### Harar

Harar's heritage supports a city-centred political package with Islamic scholarship, trade, walled-city defence, and regional diplomacy.

Design deductions:

- Harar should be a compact Tier A or strong Tier B package
- its focus identity should favour trade corridors, diplomacy, urban defence, and religious authority
- it should not be absorbed into a generic Ethiopia route without a negotiated settlement

Source:

- [Harar Jugol, the Fortified Historic Town](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1189/), UNESCO World Heritage Centre

## Swahili coast and Indian Ocean anchors

### Kilwa and Songo Mnara

UNESCO describes Kilwa Kisiwani and Songo Mnara as major East African ports whose prosperity depended on Indian Ocean trade with Arabia, India, and China. Kilwa also minted currency.

Design deductions:

- the Swahili route should be maritime and commercial
- ports, customs, convoys, coastal cities, and island relations should matter
- Kilwa, Zanzibar, Mombasa, Pate, Lamu, Sofala, and other coastal packages should form a network rather than copies of one state
- a Swahili federation should require port and trade-route integration

Source:

- [Ruins of Kilwa Kisiwani and Ruins of Songo Mnara](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/144/), UNESCO World Heritage Centre

## Great Lakes anchors

### Buganda

UNESCO describes the Kasubi Tombs as a major spiritual centre for the Baganda and a living tradition linked to the kingdom.

Design deductions:

- Buganda should retain a real court, sacred, and political identity
- integration can preserve a kingdom inside a federation
- royal continuity, local legitimacy, lake transport, and regional competition should shape its package

Source:

- [Tombs of Buganda Kings at Kasubi](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1022/), UNESCO World Heritage Centre

## Central African anchors

### Kongo

The Royal Museum for Central Africa describes Kongo as a long-lived, well-documented kingdom with diplomacy and trade connections. The Metropolitan Museum describes Kongo as a centralised state with provincial administration and important trade routes.

Design deductions:

- Kongo should have diplomatic, Christian, royal, provincial, and trade-route branches
- the package should acknowledge overlapping modern borders
- a Kongo federation should require negotiation across several countries and regional authorities
- restoration should not automatically erase local states

Sources:

- [The Kongo kingdom](https://www.africamuseum.be/en/discover/history_articles/kongo-kingdom), Royal Museum for Central Africa
- [Central Africa, 1400 to 1600 A.D.](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/afc.html), Metropolitan Museum of Art

### Luba and Lunda

The Metropolitan Museum describes Luba and Lunda as large multiethnic Central African states. It highlights sacred kingship, councils, incorporation of client rulers, trade routes, and the spread of political forms.

Design deductions:

- Luba and Lunda should excel at federating local rulers
- council and royal routes should coexist
- client-state integration should require tribute, recognition, and constitutional choices
- Luba-Lunda can become a federation without instant direct annexation

Source:

- [Kingdoms of the Savanna: The Luba and Lunda Empires](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/kingdoms-of-the-savanna-the-luba-and-lunda-empires), Metropolitan Museum of Art

### Kuba

Metropolitan Museum material emphasises Kuba royal continuity, courtly innovation, regalia, and individual royal symbols.

Design deductions:

- Kuba should have a strong court identity and regnal-symbol system
- portrait, seal, and court assets should be distinct
- a Kuba member can preserve internal monarchy inside a federal route

Source:

- [Kingdoms of the Savanna: The Kuba Kingdom](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/kingdoms-of-the-savanna-the-kuba-kingdom), Metropolitan Museum of Art

### Yeke

AfricaMuseum material describes the Yeke state under Msiri as a large state connected to ivory, copper, salt, and long-distance trade.

Design deductions:

- Yeke should be a resource and caravan-route package
- its claims should overlap with Luba, Lunda, Kazembe, and modern Katanga arrangements
- restoration should create bargaining over mines, trade corridors, and political legitimacy

Source:

- [The Yeke and the Congo Free State, from confrontation to collaboration](https://www.africamuseum.be/en/discover/history_articles/the_yeke_and_the_congo_free_state), Royal Museum for Central Africa

## West African anchors

### Asante

Metropolitan Museum material connects Asante court art with proverbs, cohesion, prosperity, and royal statecraft.

Design deductions:

- Asante should have court, military, gold, trade, and confederal political themes
- proverb and regalia research can inform later localisation and icon work
- its route should be distinct from generic Akan or Ghana packages

Source:

- [Art of the Asante Kingdom](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/art-of-the-asante-kingdom), Metropolitan Museum of Art

### Dahomey

UNESCO identifies the Royal Palaces of Abomey as material testimony to the Kingdom of Dahomey and its organised royal power. Museum material also provides context for court religion, Oyo influence, warfare, and Atlantic trade.

Design deductions:

- Dahomey should have a powerful court and military identity
- its package must acknowledge coercion and the Atlantic slave trade without turning atrocities into a joke
- restoration should create difficult legitimacy and memory choices
- Oyo-Dahomey relations should be a specific rivalry and settlement problem

Sources:

- [Royal Palaces of Abomey](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/323/), UNESCO World Heritage Centre
- [Arts of Africa](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/arts-of-africa/inside-the-exhibition), Metropolitan Museum of Art

### Benin, Owo, and Ijebu

Metropolitan Museum material describes reciprocal exchange of courtly forms among Benin, Owo, and Ijebu.

Design deductions:

- Benin or Edo should be a major package
- Owo and Ijebu should remain available as distinct local or regional packages
- cultural exchange should support shared icon motifs without making the countries mechanically identical
- the Lower Niger restoration web needs negotiated overlap

Source:

- [Exchange of Art and Ideas: The Benin, Owo, and Ijebu Kingdoms](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/exchange-of-art-and-ideas-the-benin-owo-and-ijebu-kingdoms), Metropolitan Museum of Art

## Southern African anchors

### Great Zimbabwe and Khami

UNESCO identifies Great Zimbabwe as an important Shona trading centre and Khami as a major stone-built site linking Great Zimbabwe with later Zimbabwe history.

Design deductions:

- Great Zimbabwe should be a civilisational and political claim rather than a direct copy of one historical government
- Mutapa, Rozwi, Khami, and Great Zimbabwe need a restoration web with mutually exclusive or federal outcomes
- stone construction, trade, cattle, gold, and regional legitimacy should shape the packages

Sources:

- [Great Zimbabwe National Monument](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/364/), UNESCO World Heritage Centre
- [Khami Ruins National Monument](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/365/), UNESCO World Heritage Centre

### South Africa in 1939

South African History Online records the political split between J. B. M. Hertzog, who favoured neutrality, and Jan Smuts, who supported joining the Allies. It also describes the anti-British Ossewabrandwag as an anti-war Afrikaner movement with a paramilitary wing.

Design deductions:

- the RSA special case should be a civil war rather than an automatic tag switch when RSA is in the Allies
- the Allied government, continental coalition, and anti-British forces should not be treated as one political bloc
- Allied intervention and later peace require explicit conditions
- the civil war needs regional political support, defections, and foreign behaviour

Sources:

- [Second World War and its impact, 1939 to 1948](https://sahistory.org.za/article/second-world-war-and-its-impact-1939-1948), South African History Online
- [Former South African Prime Minister and State President John Vorster is born](https://sahistory.org.za/dated-event/former-south-african-prime-minister-and-state-president-john-vorster-born), South African History Online

## Madagascar and island anchors

### Merina and Ambohimanga

UNESCO describes the Royal Hill of Ambohimanga as a royal city, burial site, sacred landscape, and place associated with national identity.

Design deductions:

- Merina should have royal, sacred, highland, administrative, and island-unification themes
- Madagascar routes should distinguish highland, coastal, republican, royal, and high-chaos identities
- island routes need shipping, food, and port mechanics rather than continental land-war copies

Source:

- [Royal Hill of Ambohimanga](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/950/), UNESCO World Heritage Centre

## Diaspora research anchors

### Universal Negro Improvement Association and global African bonds

The United States National Park Service describes the UNIA as a worldwide movement that encouraged pride in African heritage and sought bonds among people of African descent.

Design deductions:

- diaspora return should include political and organisational networks
- the route should include citizenship, transport, housing, employment, and trust
- Afro-American participation should be explicit
- return must remain voluntary

Source:

- [Places of Malcolm X](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/places-of-malcolm-x.htm), United States National Park Service

### Garveyism, return, and Black internationalism

Smithsonian material describes Marcus Garvey's mass mobilisation and a return-to-Africa vision that circulated across the diaspora.

Design deductions:

- a Garveyist or related route can exist as one current within a wider diaspora system
- the system should include ideological competition and not reduce the diaspora to one leader
- return politics should interact with Ethiopia, Liberia, ports, shipping, citizenship, and anti-colonial movements

Source:

- [Black History in Roots Reggae Music](https://folklife.si.edu/magazine/black-history-in-roots-reggae-music), Smithsonian Center for Folklife and Cultural Heritage

## Research guardrails

### Historical restoration is not automatic consent

A historical name can be politically meaningful without granting uncontested modern legitimacy.

Implementation should ask:

- who supports the restoration
- which institutions survived or are reconstructed
- which modern states and communities are affected
- what borders are claimed
- whether the identity is federal, royal, republican, religious, regional, or symbolic
- how conflicts are settled

### Heritage sites are not identical to historical borders

UNESCO heritage descriptions support historical importance. They do not establish the exact state borders that implementation should use.

State-group design needs a separate map review.

### Real leaders and symbols need source review

Do not generate real historical leaders, real historical flags, or attested symbols. Use a source manifest and record uncertainty.

### Sensitive histories need sober handling

Slave trading, colonial violence, forced labour, massacres, disease, displacement, and atrocity cannot be used as throwaway comedy.

Humour belongs in fictional rulers, absurd regnal names, nonhuman politics, high-chaos units, and surreal diplomatic situations.

### Polity registry confidence

The polity matrix separates:

- high-confidence major packages
- historically grounded regional packages needing map review
- rare or local candidates needing specialist review
- fictional high-chaos identities

A candidate's presence in the matrix does not guarantee final implementation.

## Further research required before implementation

- exact state groups for every polity
- contemporary claim and sensitivity review
- surviving royal and traditional institutions in the 1936 start context
- real leader availability by date
- flag and symbol sourcing
- political party and movement names
- regional military traditions
- language and title review
- religious and sacred-site treatment
- African-majority diaspora organisation research beyond the first anchor set
- balance between restored polities and modern anti-colonial republics
- region-specific colonial administration and withdrawal paths
