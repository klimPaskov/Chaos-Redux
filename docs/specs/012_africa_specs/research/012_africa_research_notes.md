# Event 012 Africa — Research Notes and Design Application

This note records historical, cultural, and mythic anchors used to expand Event 012 into a staged Chaos Redux design. It is not final localisation. It is a research and implementation handoff for writers, asset workers, super-event researchers, and implementation agents.

## Research boundaries

The event is alternate-history fantasy. Historical references are used as anchors for names, regional mechanics, route flavour, and asset direction, not as claims that the fictional unifier is a direct continuation of any real movement. Real people, real flags, real organisations, and real symbols require source review before they are used as final assets or localisation.

The design deliberately separates human African polities from high-chaos nonhuman or supernatural actors. The high-chaos routes can create supernatural courts, animal-led forest entities, or nature avatars, but the implementation must never imply that real African peoples are animals or monsters. Nonhuman countries must be explicitly nonhuman in shared classification triggers and player-facing text.

The user-provided king-name strings are kept as untranslated source-language court/ruler display-name flavour for Event 012-created or recast identities. Neutral IDs/debug names avoid raw phrase text.

## Core historical anchors

### Pan-African organisation and continental unity

The African Union describes itself as a continental body of 55 member states. It was launched in 2002 as successor to the Organization of African Unity. The OAU was founded in 1963 when 32 independent African heads of state met in Addis Ababa, and its aims included removing colonialism and apartheid, promoting unity and solidarity, and coordinating cooperation for African peoples and states.

Design use:

- The unifier’s legitimacy route is built around a congress/charter fantasy rather than immediate annexation.
- “Paper cores” become a political claim that must be turned into real integration through legitimacy, regional trust, authority, and local administrative work.
- The Charter League is inspired by continental organisation and anti-colonial solidarity, but is an alternate-history wartime league rather than the historical OAU.
- Addis Ababa, Accra, Cairo, Dakar, Lagos, Johannesburg/Pretoria, Nairobi, Timbuktu/Gao, and other major regional centres can become ceremonial, diplomatic, or branch anchors depending on the selected unifier and route.

Sources to verify before implementation:

- African Union official history and overview: https://au.int/en/overview
- African Union OAU/AU history page if needed for deeper wording.

### Decolonisation wave and varied liberation paths

The US Office of the Historian summarises that between 1945 and 1960, three dozen new states in Asia and Africa gained autonomy or independence from European colonial rulers, and that decolonisation varied from peaceful transition to long revolution.

Design use:

- The Africa event should create many different liberation pathways, not one generic war button.
- Native African subject authorities, protectorates, charter members, provisional republics, military commands, and regional congresses should appear before full integration.
- The route should support peaceful charter accession, protectorate defence, anti-colonial intervention, local uprisings, and hard wars against external occupiers.
- African countries already at war with colonisers should get aid and faction protection before they are pressured into integration.

Source:

- US Office of the Historian, Decolonization of Asia and Africa, 1945–1960: https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/asia-and-africa

### Fifth Pan-African Congress and diaspora politics

Reliable sources describe the 1945 Manchester Pan-African Congress as a key anti-colonial gathering. The University of Manchester event page states that from 15 to 21 October 1945, Manchester hosted 90 delegates at All Saints for the Fifth Pan-African Congress, with participants including George Padmore, Kwame Nkrumah, Mrs Marcus Garvey, Ras Makonnen, W.E.B. Du Bois, and Jomo Kenyatta. ArchivesHub describes it as organised by George Padmore and Kwame Nkrumah, attended by 90 delegates, with 26 from Africa.

Design use:

- The diaspora return route should be a political and industrial mechanic, not a free manpower button.
- African-American and wider diaspora return should create legitimacy, skills, officer cadres, labour missions, cultural tensions, intelligence attention, and foreign backlash risk.
- The super-event researcher can investigate Pan-African Congress-linked quotations or public-domain writing as possible quote directions, but must verify exact wording and copyright status.

Sources:

- University of Manchester event page, Pan African Congress: https://events.manchester.ac.uk/event/event%3Ay11-h4b404vg-kahpln/
- ArchivesHub, Pan-African Congress 1945 and 1995 Archive: https://archiveshub.jisc.ac.uk/data/gb3228-34
- History Workshop, Fifth Pan-African Congress 75 years on: https://www.historyworkshop.org.uk/black-history/the-fifth-pan-african-congress-75-years-on-a-landmark-moment-in-british-radical-history/

### South Africa, World War II, and the RSA branch

South African History Online records that South Africa was divided in 1939 over whether to join the war: J.B.M. Hertzog wanted neutrality while Jan Smuts supported Britain and the Allies. Hertzog resigned, Smuts became prime minister, South Africa joined the war on the Allied side, and South African forces served in North Africa, Ethiopia, Madagascar, and Italy.

Design use:

- If RSA is selected and RSA is in the Allies, the event should become a civil war rather than a clean transformation.
- The civil war should not be a generic duplicate. It should pit Allied/Smuts-aligned state continuity against the Continental Proclamation faction, with internal opposition, foreign pressure, African majority politics, colonial military obligations, and industrial stakes.
- If the continental side wins, Allied peace should be handled through an aftermath treaty or forced peace package rather than by leaving a permanent Allied war that breaks the event.

Source:

- South African History Online, South Africa and the Second World War: https://www.sahistory.org.za/article/south-africa-and-second-world-war

### Regional empires, trade networks, and state-building references

UNESCO’s General History of Africa overview identifies ancient civilizations, the spread of Islam and Bantu expansion, great kingdoms and empires, and internal/external trade as major historical themes. Britannica describes Mali as a 13th–16th century trading empire rising under Sundiata and peaking under Mansa Musa, with key cities including Niani, Timbuktu, and Gao. Britannica describes Songhai as a great West African trading state of the 15th–16th centuries, centred on the middle Niger and extended under Sonni Ali and Askia Muhammad.

Design use:

- The Crown Congress route can draw on memories of imperial, royal, and trading-state legitimacy without claiming to restore one single real empire across all Africa.
- Regional authority subjects can be named after geographic networks rather than ethnic caricatures: West African Congress Authority, Sahel Caravan Authority, Maghreb Coastal Congress, Nile-Horn League, East African Railway Congress, Congo Basin Charter, Great Lakes Council, Zambezi-Stone Cities Authority, South African Liberation Congress, and Indian Ocean Congress.
- Industry routes should include railways, ports, river routes, caravan routes, mineral districts, and administrative capitals rather than only generic factories.

Sources:

- UNESCO, General History of Africa overview: https://www.unesco.org/en/general-history-africa
- Britannica, Mali historical empire: https://www.britannica.com/place/Mali-historical-empire-Africa
- Britannica, Songhai empire: https://www.britannica.com/place/Songhai-empire

## Mythic and high-chaos anchors

### Orisha, Vodun, and nature courts

Britannica describes orisha as divinities of Yoruba, Edo, Ewe, and Fon traditions, often linked to convergences of natural force, ancestors, deified objects, and cultural imagination.

Design use:

- High-chaos divine/nature mechanics should be region-specific, respectful, and explicitly alternate-history fantasy.
- These mechanics should produce strange weather warnings, river omens, forest barriers, drought predictions, or disaster bargains rather than generic “magic damage.”
- “God powers” should be framed as public rumours, rituals, sacred offices, strange meteorology, or nature court interventions, preserving uncertainty at first.

Source:

- Britannica, Orisha: https://www.britannica.com/topic/orisha

### Ananse and political trickster networks

Britannica describes Ananse as an Akan character known for insight and wisdom, with shape-shifting and trickster traits.

Design use:

- Ananse-inspired content should become an intelligence/rumour/espionage route, not a literal cartoon spider state by default.
- The high-chaos “Ananse Web” can manipulate colonial administrations, expose collaborators, confuse supply lines, and create misinformation missions.
- If represented visually, use symbolic webs, masks, stories, and radio networks rather than caricature.

Source:

- Britannica, Ananse: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Ananse

### Mami Wata and river/sea power

National Museums Scotland describes Mami Wata as a water spirit symbolising wealth, healing, and destruction, with snake and divination associations, and notes that Mami Wata practices survived the transatlantic slave system.

Design use:

- Mami Wata direction fits coastal, river, convoy, port, and diaspora return mechanics.
- At high chaos, the sea/rivers can become a route for storm warnings, convoy bargains, port sabotage, healing missions, or destructive floods.
- This content should be visually powerful but not played as a cheap joke.

Source:

- National Museums Scotland, Mami Wata: https://www.nms.ac.uk/explore-our-collections/stories/global-arts-cultures-and-design/mami-wata

## Regional design consequences

The design should not treat Africa as one homogenous territory before unification. The unifier can proclaim “Africa is one,” but the mechanics should prove that unity is a campaign project. Each region should have different first problems, integration costs, resistance risks, economic opportunities, and branch flavour.

Recommended regional axes:

- West Africa: congress politics, coastal ports, Sahel links, old empire memory, Atlantic diaspora return.
- Sahel: caravan routes, desert logistics, anti-colonial mobile forces, drought/famine pressure, religious legitimacy.
- Maghreb: Mediterranean diplomacy, colonial garrisons, ports, desert mountain frontiers, Europe-facing pressure.
- Nile-Horn: river, Red Sea, Ethiopian state memory, Italian/Eritrean/Somali colonial routes, mountain warfare.
- East Africa: railway corridors, ports, Lake Victoria, Swahili coast, British/Italian colonial pressure.
- Congo Basin: rubber/mineral extraction memory, forest logistics, river systems, high-chaos primate/forest guardian path.
- Great Lakes: population, lakes, internal borders, rail/port objectives, regional negotiation.
- Southern Africa: mines, settler politics, apartheid/pre-apartheid racial order, Allied alignment, RSA special civil war.
- Indian Ocean islands: ports, naval access, Madagascar, supply corridors, sea-spirit high-chaos route.

## Uncertainty and source-sensitive items

The implementation agent must not treat this note as source approval for final quotations, final cultural remarks, final audio, final historical flags, or real leader portraits. Those belong to `chaos-redux-super-events` and `chaos-redux-event-assets` workflows.

Any final leader portrait for a real person must be sourced. Fictional leaders, symbolic councils, high-chaos divine authorities, forest councils, or nonhuman entities can use generated assets, but must include manifest notes and safe naming rules.

Historical flags and attested movement symbols must be sourced and documented. Fictional continental flags, green covenant flags, supernatural banners, and nonhuman flags can be generated.

## Design principles derived from research

1. The event should begin with a proclamation and a congress, not a map wipe.
2. African countries should be saved, invited, influenced, integrated, or resisted before they are annexed.
3. Colonising powers should react through threats, sanctions, intervention, and the “Scramble for Africa” crisis after real unification momentum appears.
4. RSA in the Allies must turn into a civil war because the real wartime political split gives the branch a strong historical anchor.
5. Old empires and myths should produce route identity, not a single universal restoration fantasy.
6. High chaos should make the event stranger, but the baseline should still be playable and grounded.
7. The final world-end path should require both completed African unification and the existence/success of other continent unifiers, so “The World is One” feels earned rather than a random late-game button.


## Deep niche-polity expansion research notes

This section supports the Niche Polities and Higher-Tier Absurd Paths file. It expands the pool of regional authority cards and route polities so the unifier has many possible local partners, rivals, and high-chaos signatories.

### Broad research frame

UNESCO’s General History of Africa frames African history through long-term regional development: ancient civilizations, Islam and Bantu expansion, trade and cultural exchange, kingdoms and empires, internal development, external trade, colonial domination, post-1935 politics, and African diasporas. Design consequence: the event should treat “Africa is one” as a hard political project across many histories, not a blank continental paint bucket.

Sources:

- UNESCO, General History of Africa overview: https://www.unesco.org/en/general-history-africa
- African Union overview: https://au.int/en/overview

### West African and Nigerian polity anchors

Britannica’s Nigeria history page lists many indigenous polities before British conquest, including Hausa states such as Kano, Katsina, Zaria, and Gobir; Kanem-Borno; Jukun/Kwararafa states; Igala, Nupe, and Ebira; Ife and Oyo; Benin; Warri; Calabar; and the Ijaw city-states of Nembe, Elem Kalabari, Bonny, and Okrika. It also describes Borno as a long-dominant central Sudanic power and Hausaland as a crossroads of trans-Saharan and forest-zone trade.

Design use:

- Nigeria and the Gulf of Guinea should not collapse into a single “West African Council.”
- Hausa, Kanem-Bornu, Yoruba/Ife-Oyo, Benin, Nupe/Igala, Nri/Aro/Delta, and Jukun/Kwararafa need separate cards or route hooks when the region is central.
- Many cards should be authority cards rather than full tags, because the region is dense and a tag for each would clutter the map.

### Central African polity anchors

Britannica describes the Kingdom of Kongo, Portuguese contact in 1483, Afonso I’s centralizing and diplomatic ties, and conflicts around the Atlantic slave trade. It describes Matamba as Nzinga’s base in her long war with Portugal. It describes Luba-Lunda states and Kazembe as major central African state systems connected to long-distance trade. Kuba is described as a federation of smaller nearly autonomous states, and Bamum as a kingdom centred at Foumban with Njoya’s schools and script/pictographic writing.

Design use:

- Kongo, Ndongo-Matamba, Luba-Lunda-Kazembe, Kuba, and Bamum should be high-priority central African cards or route polities.
- The Congo Basin should combine river logistics, resource sovereignty, archive/artifact politics, craft networks, and forest/nonhuman high-chaos routes.
- Kuba’s federation structure supports a federal authority-card mechanic; Bamum supports administration/script UI flavour; Matamba supports anti-colonial war road content.

### East Africa, Horn, and Indian Ocean anchors

Britannica describes the Swahili coast through island and port settlements, Muslim towns, coral mosques, imports from India/Persian Gulf/China, and trade with Madagascar. It describes Somali coastal cities and sultanates such as Adal and Ajuran. Madagascar history notes Merina/Imerina, Sakalava, Betsimisaraka, Antemoro, and early Afro-Arab commercial networks and written Antemoro texts.

Design use:

- The Swahili city-states should be a port league with convoys, coastal defense, monsoon routes, and Indian Ocean diplomacy.
- Somali and Horn content should distinguish Ajuran/Benadir ports, Adal/Harar gates, northern sultanates, and Dervish anti-colonial memory.
- Madagascar needs multiple authority cards: Merina/Imerina, Sakalava, Betsimisaraka, Antemoro/Zafindraminia, and Indian Ocean port offices.

### Southern African anchors

Britannica describes Great Zimbabwe, Torwa, Mutapa, and Rozwi links to gold and ivory trade, and southern African kingdoms including Zulu, Swazi, Sotho, and Ndebele. It describes Lozi/Barotseland and annual floodplain movement.

Design use:

- The Stone-Cities package should connect Mutapa/Rozvi/Great Zimbabwe to resource routes, archaeology, and southern industry.
- The southern settlement layer should avoid a single generic Nguni/Sotho puppet; it needs Basotho mountain content, Swazi/Zulu/Ndebele regiment settlements, Xhosa/Thembu/Pondo hearings, and RSA branch integration.
- Lozi/Barotseland should use seasonal/floodplain logistics rather than generic river bonuses.

### Sahara and Mossi anchors

Britannica describes Mossi states as independent West African kingdoms around the headwaters of the Volta. Oxford/Classics-linked research describes Fazzan foggaras as hundreds of underground irrigation canals associated with Garamantian use from roughly c. 400 BCE to 700 CE, and EurekAlert summarizes research on Garamantes foggara/qanat groundwater systems.

Design use:

- Mossi states support a route about roads, cavalry/savanna columns, and centralised traditional offices.
- Garamantes/Foggara Works support a desert-water, engineering, and high-chaos underground-water route.

### Mythic and nonhuman anchors

Britannica describes Orisha as deities of Yoruba people also venerated among Edo, Ewe, and Fon traditions. Britannica describes Ananse as an Akan figure known for wisdom and trickster traits, normally spider-shaped but able to change form. National Museums Scotland and the Fowler Museum describe Mami Wata as a powerful water-spirit tradition across Africa and the African Atlantic, protective and dangerous.

WWF describes the Congo Basin forests as spanning around 500 million acres across six countries and holding major great-ape biodiversity. WWF also notes western lowland gorillas in Central African forests, chimpanzees in Central African forest habitat, and bonobos only in forests south of the Congo River in the DRC.

Design use:

- Orisha/Vodun/Mami Wata/Ananse references are route directions and require final text research if used in localisation.
- Gorilla, chimpanzee, bonobo, elephant, crocodile, and baobab routes are explicitly nonhuman/supernatural actors. They are not human African countries and cannot replace human authority cards.
- Congo Basin high-chaos content can be absurd and strong because the event tier supports it, but classification and localisation must be safe and explicit.

### Design conclusion

The event should ship with a Polity Registry system. The registry gives the player a curated rotating set of authority cards rather than dumping every possible polity as a map tag. Some authorities can become subjects or route polities, and a small number can become full absurd/nonhuman actors at high chaos. This preserves depth, avoids clutter, and makes “Africa is one” feel like a negotiated, fought-over, and eventually impossible continental project.
