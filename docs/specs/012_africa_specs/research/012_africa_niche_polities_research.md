# Event 012 Africa — Niche Polities, Regional Histories, and High-Chaos Inspiration Research

This research addendum expands the Event 012 Africa package with a deeper roster of regional authorities, historical-revival subjects, high-chaos mythic routes, and explicitly nonhuman ecological actors. It is a design research file, not a claim that all listed polities should be reproduced literally or that modern communities should be treated as static survivals of old states.

## Research boundaries and sensitivity rules

- Historical polities are used as inspiration for alternate-history country packages, focus routes, decision families, flags, councils, and event flavour. The implementation must avoid presenting any modern ethnic, religious, or indigenous community as exotic, primitive, nonhuman, or inherently supernatural.
- High-chaos supernatural actors are **route-created fantasy institutions**. They must be clearly distinct from ordinary human regional authorities.
- Gorilla, chimpanzee, bonobo, elephant, okapi, or other nonhuman actors are **actual nonhuman special chaos countries or ecological pact entities**, never human African countries under animal names.
- Sensitive histories involving slavery, conquest, colonial violence, genocide, or forced labour must be framed as contested burdens, reform choices, reparative justice, or rejected practices rather than glamorous power fantasies.
- Real flags, real religious objects, real royal symbols, real sacred regalia, and real historical leader portraits require sourced asset work. Fictional/high-chaos variants can be generated, but must be marked fictional.

## Continental unity and the modern unity hook

The baseline Charter League should stay anchored in the idea that continental unity is a political project before it becomes an absurd high-chaos force. The African Union notes that in May 1963, 32 heads of independent African states met in Addis Ababa to create the OAU, Africa's first post-independence continental institution, and describes the OAU as a pan-African vision for a united, free Africa in control of its destiny.

Source:
- African Union overview, `https://au.int/en/overview`.

Design application:
- The early Charter League should sound like an impossible 1930s premonition of later continental institutions, not an instant empire.
- The unifier's early news should frame the claim as a congress, charter, league, or liberation authority.
- The high-chaos version can twist the phrase into literal metaphysics: once enough charter signatories, legacy authorities, and continent-unifier allies exist, the slogan becomes a rule of the world.

## RSA civil-war branch anchor

South African History Online summarizes the 1939 wartime split: Hertzog preferred neutrality, Smuts wanted to fight with the Allies, Hertzog resigned, Smuts succeeded him, and South Africa joined the war on the Allied side. It also notes the postwar role of DF Malan's HNP and wartime economic/social strain.

Source:
- South African History Online, `https://sahistory.org.za/article/second-world-war-and-its-impact-1939-1948`.

Design application:
- RSA-in-Allies should remain the only branch where the Africa event begins as a domestic rupture instead of a simple unifier proclamation.
- The civil war can now receive optional niche-authority support: Basotho mountain corridors, Lozi floodplain asylum, Herero-Nama memory compact advisors, Indian Ocean blockade offices, and African Congress port workers.

## West Africa and Sahel anchors

### Mossi states

Britannica describes the Mossi states as a complex of independent West African kingdoms around the Volta headwaters from roughly 1500 to 1895, including Wagadugu/Ouagadougou, Yatenga, Tenkodogo, Fada-n-Gurma, Mamprusi, Dagomba, and Nanumba.

Source:
- Britannica, Mossi states, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Mossi-states`.

Design application:
- Add the **Mossi Kingdoms** as a Volta/Upper Volta legacy authority. Its grounded route is cavalry scouts, administrative chiefs, and Volta river logistics. Its high-chaos route exaggerates the title mythology into an absurd bureaucratic claim: every country receives a royal audit notice from the “kingdom of the world,” which becomes a darkly comic precursor to the World Is One branch.

### Oyo empire

Britannica describes Oyo as a Yoruba state north of Lagos that dominated much of the region between the Volta and Niger at its apogee from 1650 to 1750, and notes that wealth and trade helped Oyo maintain cavalry and a trained army.

Source:
- Britannica, Oyo empire, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Oyo-empire`.

Design application:
- Add the **Oyo** as a savanna/forest-edge authority. Its grounded route is cavalry tradition converted into scouts, motor columns, and mounted propaganda detachments. At high chaos it unlocks the **Tsetse Ghost Cavalry**: a fantasy route where spectral or impossible cavalry ignore terrain in controlled savanna provinces but collapse if supply and Legitimacy are weak.

### Asante empire

Britannica notes that Asante reached its greatest interior extent under Opoku Ware after early 18th-century factional chaos, and that Asante expansion depended on firearms acquired through coastal trade. Because Asante history is entangled with both state formation and Atlantic slavery, the spec must not romanticize that trade.

Source:
- Britannica, Asante empire, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Asante-empire`.

Design application:
- Add the **Asante** as a Gold Coast/Akan legacy authority. Its grounded route is gold, roads, court diplomacy, and ceremonial legitimacy. The high-chaos route should use a fictionalized “stool refuses conquest” motif only after asset research confirms what real symbols can be referenced safely; otherwise use a generated alternate regalia seal.

### Dahomey women warriors / Mino

Britannica describes the Dahomey women warriors, also called Mino or Ahosi, as women soldiers of Dahomey known for combat prowess and protection of the king. It lists regiment types including huntresses, riflewomen, reapers, archers, and gunners, and notes their conflicts with neighboring kingdoms and French colonial forces.

Source:
- Britannica, Dahomey women warriors, `https://www.britannica.com/topic/Dahomey-women-warriors`.

Design application:
- Add the **Dahomey Kingdom** as an elite infantry and palace-security legacy package. The route should avoid flattening Dahomey into a single pop-culture trope; it should include hard questions about palace militarism, forced recruitment, and how the new Congress reforms old institutions. High chaos can unlock a terrifying all-women guard route, but it must still have clear costs, training limits, and ethical failure states.

### Aro and Nri

Britannica identifies Arochukwu as the headquarters of the Aro, an Igbo subgroup that dominated southeastern Nigeria in the 18th and 19th centuries, and the seat of the Chuku shrine/oracle. Britannica also notes the Eze Nri as a priest-king with religious rather than political power over large parts of the Igbo-inhabited region well into the 20th century.

Sources:
- Britannica, Aro, `https://www.britannica.com/topic/Aro`.
- Britannica, Nri, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Nri`.

Design application:
- Add the **Aro Confederacy** and **Nri Kingdom** as paired authority candidates. The grounded route is law, mediation, trade networks, ritual sanction, and legitimacy. The high-chaos fork becomes an “oracle predicts the next colonial move” system, but the player must choose between exploitative fear-politics and a purifying/restorative path that rejects coercion. This pair should be one of the moral checks on the whole event.

### Bamum and the script of Njoya

Britannica describes Bamum as a kingdom centred at Foumban in Cameroon. It notes that Njoya, the 16th mfon, invented a writing system around 1895, revised it six times into a syllabary of 83 characters plus numerals, created books on history/customs, medicine, religion, and maps, and established schools for the script.

Source:
- Britannica, Bamum, `https://www.britannica.com/topic/Bamum`.

Design application:
- Add the **Bamum Kingdom** as a literacy, code, intelligence, and administration package. Grounded effects include faster integration, better event-log detail, coded orders, and reduced paper-core burden. At high chaos, the **Shumom Protocol** route lets symbols alter decision availability and local weather rumours; this must be presented as supernatural fiction, not as a factual claim about the script.

### Wadai / Ouaddai and the Chad Basin

Britannica describes Wadai as a historical kingdom east of Lake Chad and west of Darfur, founded in the 16th century with a Muslim dynasty around 1630, independent by the 1790s, and expanding chiefly at Bornu's expense.

Source:
- Britannica, Wadai, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Wadai`.

Design application:
- Add the **Wadai-Bornu Sultanate** as a Chad Basin/Sahel authority. Its grounded route is caravan roads, desert scouts, Islamic court diplomacy, and Lake Chad supply. At high chaos, it can unlock **The Desert Remembers Roads**, where old routes temporarily appear as strategic movement corridors if the player maintains water depots and local trust.

## Central and west-central Africa anchors

### Kongo

Britannica describes Kongo as a former kingdom south of the Congo River in present Angola and DRC, traditionally founded about 1390 and originally a loose federation of smaller polities that integrated conquered territories as royal patrimony.

Source:
- Britannica, Kongo, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Kongo-historical-kingdom-Africa`.

Design application:
- Add the **Kongo Kingdom**. Grounded route: church/mission memory, river diplomacy, west-coast ports, and federated provincial administration. High chaos: the old capital becomes a “city of names” where regions integrated peacefully can be called by chosen public names, reducing resistance but increasing pressure from colonial powers.

### Kuba

Britannica describes Kuba as a former African kingdom in what is now DRC, founded about 1600 by migrants from the lower Kasai and actually a federation of smaller, nearly autonomous states such as Bushongo and Ngongo.

Source:
- Britannica, Kuba, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Kuba-historical-kingdom-Africa`.

Design application:
- Add the **Kuba Kingdom**. Grounded route: autonomous federation model, artisan legitimacy, decentralised subject management, and culture/industry icons. High chaos: the **Loom of Oaths** animates the Congress Board, turning broken integration promises into visible tears in the UI.

### Luba-Lunda and Kazembe

Britannica describes the Luba-Lunda states as a complex of states in present DRC from the late 15th to late 19th century; it identifies Luba around the Lualaba headwaters, Lunda around the Kasai/Kwango region, and Kazembe southeast in the Luapula/Lake Mweru area.

Sources:
- Britannica, Luba-Lunda states, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Luba-Lunda-states`.
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, “Kingdoms of the Savanna: The Luba and Lunda Empires,” `https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/kingdoms-of-the-savanna-the-luba-and-lunda-empires`.

Design application:
- Add **Luba-Lunda Kingdoms** and the **Kazembe Kingdom**. Grounded route: memory administration, copper/river logistics, oath networks, Lake Mweru supply. High chaos: old memory boards become route-state UI cards that remember broken promises and can punish forced annexations.

### Mutapa / Rozvi / Stone Cities

Britannica describes Mwene Matapa as a dynastic title for rulers in southeast Africa between the Zambezi and Limpopo from the 14th to 17th century, associated with Zimbabwe; Nyatsimba in the late 15th century is described as the actual creator of the empire and first to bear the title.

Source:
- Britannica, Mwene Matapa, `https://www.britannica.com/topic/Mwene-Matapa`.

Design application:
- Add the **Mutapa-Rozvi**. Grounded route: forts, mines, goldfields, Zambezi diplomacy, old stone-site legitimacy. High chaos: **The Walls Remember Hands** makes ruins and forts become active defensive nodes, but only in states with restored infrastructure and high local trust.

## East Africa, Horn, Nile, and Indian Ocean anchors

### Aksum

Britannica describes Aksum as a powerful northeastern African kingdom originating in Ethiopia, linking African, Mediterranean, and Asian trade networks, adopting Christianity in the 4th century, and controlling Red Sea trade routes at its height.

Source:
- Britannica, Aksum, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Aksum-ancient-kingdom-Africa`.

Design application:
- Expand the existing Nile-Horn route with an **Aksum**. Grounded route: coins, stelae, Red Sea trade, Christian/Islamic diplomacy. High chaos: stelae become signal towers that mark safe routes across mountain and Red Sea states.

### Nubia / Kush / Makuria / Alodia

Britannica Kids summarizes that by the late 500s CE, Nubia had three Christian kingdoms—Nobatia, Makuria, and Alodia—and that Muslim Arabs did not conquer the Nubian kingdoms for hundreds of years, with Alodia lasting until about 1500.

Source:
- Britannica Kids, Nubia, `https://kids.britannica.com/kids/article/Nubia/353548`.

Design application:
- Add the **Nubia**. Grounded route: Nile fortresses, desert border diplomacy, Christian/Islamic archive routes, anti-desert supply. High chaos: **The Baqt Never Expired** turns ancient treaty memory into a strange diplomacy mechanic for Egypt/Middle East unifier interactions.

### Kilwa and the Swahili coast

Britannica describes Kilwa as a former Islamic city-state off the coast of southern Tanzania, founded in the late 10th century and one of the most active commercial centers on Africa's east coast before Portuguese disruption.

Source:
- Britannica, Kilwa, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Kilwa`.

Design application:
- Add the **Kilwa Sultanate**. Grounded route: ports, convoys, dockyards, Indian Ocean trade, anti-blockade missions. High chaos: dhows carry impossible messages and allow one-off evacuation/intervention missions between friendly African and Indian Ocean ports.

### Ajuran / Somali hydraulic and coastal states

Additional source work is needed before final implementation, but this package can use the **Ajuran Hydraulic Sultanate** as an inspiration route only if asset and localisation notes mark it as source-dependent. The route fantasy is grounded in water control, wells, river engineering, and coastal resistance, then escalates into high-chaos water-route manipulation.

Implementation note:
- Before final implementation, source a stronger Ajuran/Geledi/Warsangali/Majeerteen reference through the asset/research workflow. Until then, keep exact titles and final flags as `needs_source_review`.

### Buganda and Bunyoro

Britannica describes Buganda as founded in the late 14th century, with centralized control under the kabaka; by the 19th century it had become the largest and most powerful kingdom in the region. Britannica describes Bunyoro as an East African kingdom west of Lake Victoria that flourished from the 16th to 19th century and started losing territory to Buganda around 1800.

Sources:
- Britannica, Buganda, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Buganda`.
- Britannica, Bunyoro, `https://www.britannica.com/place/Bunyoro`.

Design application:
- Add the **Lake Victoria Kingship Board** with Buganda and Bunyoro as rival candidate cards. Grounded route: lake logistics, royal councils, military chiefs, rail/lake ports. High chaos: royal drums become a warning network that predicts raids and weather events.

### Merina and Madagascar

Britannica describes the Merina as entering Madagascar's central plateau in the 15th century, establishing a small kingdom, and organizing irrigation projects for wet-rice cultivation.

Source:
- Britannica, Merina, `https://www.britannica.com/topic/Merina`.

Design application:
- Add the **Merina Kingdom** and optionally **Sakalava Sea Kings** after additional sourcing. Grounded route: rice irrigation, highland forts, island autonomy, marines. High chaos: a “thirteenth hill” appears as a hidden route state, enabling a Madagascar-first Indian Ocean union path.

## Southern Africa anchors

### Lozi / Barotseland

Britannica describes the Lozi/Barotse people as occupying the Zambezi floodplain and moving between villages in the plain and the margin in response to annual flooding.

Source:
- Britannica, Lozi, `https://www.britannica.com/topic/Lozi`.

Design application:
- Add the **Lozi Floodplain Kingdom**. Grounded route: floodplain logistics, movable capital, riverine supply, autonomy. High chaos: the flood calendar becomes a weaponized seasonal mechanic that can drown roads, reveal hidden supply routes, or protect retreats.

### Basotho / mountain kingdom

Additional sourcing should use South African History Online and Britannica Lesotho pages for implementation, but the spec can define the **Basotho Kingdom** as a route inspired by Moshoeshoe's consolidation of scattered Sotho communities under mountain defence. It should focus on forts, pass defence, refugee protection, and anti-colonial mountain diplomacy, not on conquest.

Implementation note:
- Source final details before writing localisation or asset prompts for real symbols.

### Herero-Nama memory compact and Kalahari protectorate

These routes must be handled with special care because they touch genocide, dispossession, and indigenous communities. They should not be absurd joke routes. If included, they are reparative and defensive authority packages:

- **Herero-Nama:** justice, return, memorialization, anti-genocide pressure, and refusal of forced integration.
- **Kalahari Protectorate:** survival, desert knowledge, land-protection missions, and strict no-forced-annexation rules.

These are not high-chaos monster paths. They are moral tests for the unifier.

## Explicitly nonhuman ecological actors

WWF describes the Congo Basin as exceptionally large and species-rich, with iconic wildlife including gorillas, forest elephants, chimpanzees, and buffalo. IUCN's great ape page states that most of the world's gorillas and about one-third of chimpanzees live in Western Equatorial Africa, and identifies western lowland gorillas and central chimpanzees as threatened by poaching, disease, habitat loss, corruption, and habitat access.

Sources:
- WWF, Congo Basin, `https://www.worldwildlife.org/places/congo-basin/`.
- IUCN SSC PSG SGA, Western Lowland Gorillas and Central Chimpanzees, `https://www.iucngreatapes.org/western-lowland-gorillas-and-chimpanzees`.

Design application:
- The **Great Forest Congress**, **Gorilla Highlands**, **Chimpanzee Marshes**, **Bonobo Basin**, **Forest Elephants**, and **Okapi Forest** are permitted only as explicit nonhuman/high-chaos ecological actors.
- They should have special AI, limited diplomacy, no normal human politics, no human name pools, generated nonhuman/symbolic portraits, and shared `is_actual_nonhuman_country` registration if implemented.
- The humane route grants forest autonomy and ecological defense. The exploitative route gives strong short-term sabotage/manpower/terrain benefits but causes Legitimacy collapse, international horror, and possible rebellion by the forests themselves.

## Design consequences for the updated spec

1. The regional authority system should become two-layered: broad administrative regions plus optional niche legacy authorities.
2. The player should not spawn every niche authority in one campaign. Use a card/selector system, regional caps, and route preferences.
3. Niche authorities should be playable enough to matter if released, but not all require full giant focus trees. Use a shared **Legacy Authority Mini-Tree** with country-specific focus names, decisions, values, and local events.
4. High-chaos absurdity should be route-gated and costed. It should not appear in grounded baseline campaigns unless the world is already strange.
5. The implementation agent must mark source-dependent symbols/flags/portraits as `needs_source_review` until the asset workflow confirms source mode.
