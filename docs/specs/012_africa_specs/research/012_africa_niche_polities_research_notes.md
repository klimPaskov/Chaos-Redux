# Event 012 Africa — Expanded research notes for niche polities, subject tags, and high-chaos inspirations

This research note supports the expanded Event 012 Africa spec package. It is not final localisation. It identifies historical polities, regional institutions, and mythic/supernatural inspirations that can be turned into releasable subject packages, focus-tree routes, decisions, assets, and higher-chaos absurd paths.

The design rule for this pass is separation. Grounded African polities, kingdoms, sultanates, trade cities, clan confederations, and liberation councils are treated as human political actors. High-chaos animal, spirit, nature, and supernatural polities are explicitly nonhuman or supernatural and must never be implemented as caricatures of human African societies.

## Research basis and usable design anchors

### Continental and anti-colonial unity

The existing spec already anchors the unifier in pan-African and anti-colonial fantasy. The expanded subject layer should make that fantasy less generic by letting the unifier work through named regional memories and institutions rather than only modern state borders. Historical names are used as symbolic revival labels, not as claims that the 1936 populations would automatically share one political identity.

### Sahel, western savanna, and Guinea forest

| Inspiration | Research anchor | Design use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ghana, Mali, Songhai, Hausa, Kanem-Bornu | Britannica places Ghana, Mali, Songhai, Hausa states, and Kanem-Bornu among major early western African kingdoms/states around the southern Sahara. | Use as the **Archive of the Western Roads**, a scholarly/logistics subject route that recovers old caravan ledgers and turns them into rail, supply, and legitimacy missions. |
| Kanem-Bornu | Britannica notes Bornu was originally a province of Kanem; after the 14th century power shifted to Bornu. | Use as **Lake-Bornu Custodians**, a Lake Chad authority that manages water, cavalry tradition, and desert-frontier diplomacy. |
| Mossi kingdoms | Britannica describes independent Mossi kingdoms around the Volta headwaters from about 1500 to 1895, including Wagadugu and Yatenga. | Use as a durable inland federation package with cavalry, fortification, and anti-encirclement bonuses. |
| Jolof/Wolof empire | Britannica describes the Wolof empire as a 14th–16th century federation in inland Senegal, with satellite states such as Cayor and others. | Use as a coastal-inland federation package with river diplomacy, port arbitration, and anti-puppet clauses. |
| Dahomey | Britannica describes Dahomey as a kingdom in southern Benin that flourished in the 18th and 19th centuries. | Use as an Abomey Guard package: disciplined guards, coastal-fort pressure, and a route that can become authoritarian or anti-slavery/anti-colonial depending politics. |
| Oyo, Benin, Asante | Britannica and museum summaries support old states of the Guinea forest and savanna transition, including Benin and Oyo/Dahomey contexts. | Use as separate non-generic packages: Oyo cavalry/roads, Benin guilds/bronze court, Asante stool diplomacy and gold routes. |
| Futa Jallon/Futa Toro, Massina, Sokoto, Segu/Bambara | Well-known western African Islamic, reform, and state-building traditions. Some require further source verification before final localisation. | Use as Islamic/legalist, reformist, and river-bureaucratic variants in the Charter League. Keep final names research-gated if uncertain. |

### Nile, Nubia, Sudan, Horn, and Red Sea

| Inspiration | Research anchor | Design use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kush, Napata, Meroe | University of Chicago/ISAC and Britannica identify Kushite centers including Kerma, Napata, and Meroe; UNESCO identifies Meroe's archaeological landscape. | Use as a **Kush-Meroe** and high-chaos **Iron Pyramid Circuit** route. Grounded version protects antiquities, railways, and desert crossings. High-chaos version uses impossible bronze/iron monument machinery. |
| Christian Nubian kingdoms | Britannica Kids notes Nobatia, Makuria, and Alodia as Christian kingdoms in late antique/medieval Nubia; Muslim Arabs did not conquer them for centuries, and Alodia fell around 1500. | Use as **Makuria-Alodia**, a defensive river kingdom package that can guard Nile crossings and become a bridge between Christian, Islamic, and secular routes. |
| Funj/Sennar | Britannica describes the Funj dynasty as ruling 16th–19th century Sudan, with authority across Gezira, Kordofan, and Fazughli. | Use as **Funj Sultanate of Sennar**, a Nile/Blue Nile authority with gold-field, cavalry, and river-control missions. |
| Aksum | UNESCO describes Aksum as a powerful ancient state between the Eastern Roman Empire and Persia, with ruins from the 1st to 13th centuries and a Red Sea/Adulis trade connection. | Use as **Aksum**, with Red Sea trade, highland legitimacy, and monument-route identity. High-chaos route animates stelae as oath witnesses, not as generic magic. |
| Adal/Harar, Ajuran, Aussa/Afar, Beja | Britannica and other summaries identify Adal and Ajuran as major Horn/Swahili-era powers; Ajuran ruled significant Horn regions between the 13th and late 17th centuries and used centralized administration. | Use as Horn packages: Adal/Harar frontier, Ajuran water-law state, Aussa/Danakil corridor, Beja Red Sea scouts. |

### Swahili coast, Great Lakes, central Africa, and Indian Ocean

| Inspiration | Research anchor | Design use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kilwa and Songo Mnara | UNESCO describes Kilwa and Songo Mnara as Swahili trading cities thriving through Indian Ocean trade with Arabia, India, and China, exporting gold and ivory and minting currency in the 11th–14th centuries. | Use as **Swahili Coast**, a port/currency/trade authority that supports dockyards, convoys, ports, and anti-blockade decisions. |
| Buganda | Britannica describes Buganda as founded in the late 14th century, ruled by the kabaka, and by the 19th century one of the largest/powerful regional kingdoms. | Use as **Buganda**, a Great Lakes political authority with lake supply, capital legitimacy, and council/king route tension. |
| Bunyoro | Britannica describes Bunyoro as flourishing from the 16th to 19th centuries west of Lake Victoria and losing territory to Buganda around 1800. | Use as a rival/resistant Great Lakes package: Bunyoro-Kitara restoration can cooperate with Buganda or create a Great Lakes split. |
| Luba-Lunda | Britannica describes Luba-Lunda states in present DRC from the late 15th to late 19th centuries; the Met describes Luba political forms as durable and flexible. | Use as **Luba**, **Lunda**, and **Kazembe March**, with vassal-like influence rings and integration that relies on prestige rather than instant annexation. |
| Kongo | Britannica describes Kongo as formed in the late 14th century and becoming a prosperous trading center and major west-coast power by the mid-15th century. | Use as **Mbanza Kongo Restoration**, a western-central Christian/diplomatic/package with coastal trade, missionary memory, and anti-Portuguese colonial tension. |
| Loango, Ndongo/Matamba, Kuba, Teke | Historically relevant central African polities and courts needing final source verification for localisation. | Use as secondary subject packages around Kongo/Luba/Lunda so central Africa is not represented by a single generic tag. |

### Southeastern Africa and islands

| Inspiration | Research anchor | Design use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Great Zimbabwe | UNESCO dates Great Zimbabwe's construction between 1100 and 1450 AD and identifies Hill Ruins, Great Enclosure, and Valley Ruins. The Zimbabwe Bird is a major symbolic motif. | Use as **Great Zimbabwe**, with fortress/stonework, construction, and omen-bird route. High-chaos version can unlock a **Bird of the Walls** omen network. |
| Mutapa and Rozvi/Butua | Historically associated with successor/regional formations around Great Zimbabwe and the plateau. Needs final source verification for localisation. | Use as **Mutapa** and **Rozvi Butua Guard**, controlling gold, plateau forts, and anti-colonial border missions. |
| Lozi/Barotse | Britannica identifies Lozi as a complex of about 25 peoples in Barotseland. | Use as **Barotse Kingdom**, a river/flood logistics authority, with flood-warning mechanics and seasonal road decisions. |
| Merina/Imerina | Britannica notes Imerina was founded near the end of the 16th century and that Andrianampoinimerina reunited the kingdom around 1797; Antananarivo became capital. | Use as **Merina Kingdom**, a Madagascar subject package with island logistics, anti-naval blockade, and court-building path. |
| Sakalava, Betsimisaraka, Comorian Shirazi links | Significant island/coastal identities needing final source checks. | Use as Indian Ocean subject packages: convoy, port, and island autonomy mechanics. |

### Mythic, religious, and high-chaos inspiration

These are not treated as human ethnic states. They become supernatural, symbolic, or nonhuman actors only after high-chaos unlocks.

| Inspiration | Research anchor | Design use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yoruba orisha | Britannica describes traditional Yoruba religion as including a supreme creator and hundreds of lesser gods/spirits. | Use as **Orisha Court** route family: lightning, river, forge, justice, and road spirits represented as supernatural or institutional pressures, not human countries. |
| Shango | Britannica describes Shango as Yoruba deity of thunder, lightning, and justice, with related names in Edo/Fon traditions. | Use for high-chaos thunder ultimata, disaster-warning, and artillery/air-weather motifs. |
| Oshun | Britannica describes Oshun as a river orisha associated with water, purity, fertility, and love. | Use for river purification, flood warnings, refugee healing, and legitimacy recovery. |
| Ananse | Britannica describes Ananse as a spider/human trickster figure in Akan traditions. | Use as **Ananse Web**, an intelligence/sabotage/false-treaty route. Must be supernatural/symbolic, not an ethnic tag. |
| Mami Wata | National Museums Scotland describes Mami Wata as an African water-spirit tradition/pantheon with influence across regions through trade routes. | Use as **Tidemark Court**, a supernatural port/convoy/water route with coastal decisions and maritime warnings. |
| Great Zimbabwe birds | Met/Britannica-derived sources describe the birds as status/religious/royal authority symbols. | Use as omen-bird route, focus icon family, and high-chaos scout/prophecy mechanics. |
| Great apes and forest fauna | IUCN identifies western lowland gorillas and central chimpanzees as threatened in Western Equatorial Africa due to poaching, disease, and habitat loss. WWF identifies mountain gorilla habitat in DRC, Uganda, and Rwanda. | Use as explicit nonhuman forest polities at high chaos: Gorilla Nation, Chimpanzee Assembly, Bonobo Kinship, and Great Ape Envoys. They should be conservation-revenge/fantasy actors, not stand-ins for human peoples. |
| African forest elephant | Britannica identifies African forest elephants in tropical forests from Senegal to DRC and critically endangered status from habitat loss/poaching. | Use as **Great Herd Compact**, an absurd but mechanically useful supply/engineer/cavalry-adjacent unit path. |

## Design cautions from research

1. Avoid implying every historical polity is a simple modern nation. Many should be authorities, councils, chartered protectorates, regional courts, revived offices, city-leagues, or symbolic subject tags.
2. Avoid treating present-day ethnic groups as fantasy monsters. Absurd animal and spirit states must be explicitly nonhuman/supernatural and unlocked only at higher chaos.
3. Keep the user-provided source-language joke names untranslated when used as Event 012 court/ruler display flavour; neutral internal ids and serious institution names remain separate.
4. Historical flags, symbols, and real leaders must be sourced through the asset workflow before implementation. Generated assets are appropriate for fictional flags, spirits, supernatural portraits, nonhuman leaders, and high-chaos route art.
5. Madagascar, Swahili coast, Great Lakes, Sudan/Nubia, Horn, Central Africa, and the western Sahel need distinct content so “Africa is one” does not reduce the continent to a single generic conquest tree.
