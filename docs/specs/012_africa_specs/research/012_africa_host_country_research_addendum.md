# Event 012 Africa host-country research addendum

## Purpose and limits

This addendum supports the host playbooks, constitutional-route deepening, and first priority member tranche. It does not attempt to write a complete history of every African society or settle disputed territorial, dynastic, linguistic, religious, or constitutional questions.

The research was used to identify campaign problems that can create distinct play:

- constrained sovereignty
- protectorate and mandate division
- regional representation
- land and labour settlements
- concession and commodity control
- corridor and port access
- monarchy and civic-government relationships
- military administration
- voluntary federation and withdrawal
- island and mainland balance

Broad country studies are useful for orientation and bibliographies. They are not the final authority for a country package. Detailed implementation should add specialist sources, local scholarship, period documents, and linguistic review for the exact leader, institution, flag, border, title, and event text being implemented.

## Research method

The expansion used a narrow evidence rule. A historical point was kept when it changed the playable problem or prevented a misleading design. Decorative facts that did not change choices were left out.

The design deductions follow four rules:

1. Colonial administrative units are not treated as culturally uniform states.
2. Historic polities are not granted their maximum remembered borders.
3. Political institutions are represented as changing coalitions, not frozen ethnic traits.
4. A sourced historical problem becomes a mechanic only when the player can respond through several plausible strategies.

## Horn and Nile anchors

### Ethiopia

The 1935 to 1936 Italian invasion, Ethiopia’s appeal to the League of Nations, and the failure of collective security justify an opening built around survival, foreign guarantees, supply, and scepticism toward promises that carry no material support. The Library of Congress preserves the 1936 League speech and identifies the invasion, the fall of Addis Ababa, and the unsuccessful appeal for aid.

Design consequence:

- Ethiopia’s first proof should be protection delivered in practice.
- A diplomatic promise without a corridor, equipment, or force commitment should not satisfy the mandate.
- Imperial legitimacy, regional government, officer reform, and Red Sea access should remain separate problems.
- Aksum should be a compact constitutional or cultural restoration inside the modern Horn, not a replacement for all Ethiopian politics.

Sources:

- Library of Congress, *Speech by His Majesty Haile Selassie I at the Assembly of the League of Nations*, 1936: https://www.loc.gov/item/2021667904/
- Federal Research Division country-study mirror, *Ethiopia*: https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/

### Egypt and Sudan

The 1936 Anglo-Egyptian Treaty addressed military, civil, and Sudan questions while leaving a constrained sovereignty problem. Contemporary British parliamentary debate and later academic analysis show that the treaty belonged to a political struggle involving the Wafd, palace opposition, British military interests, the Sudan, and regional security.

Design consequence:

- Egypt begins with sovereignty that must be renegotiated rather than a simple independence switch.
- Suez, the Nile, and Sudan cannot be collapsed into one modifier.
- Egyptian leadership of Africa needs a Sudan settlement and a canal policy that other members can understand.
- Sudan’s host play should begin from regional congress and transport rather than an automatic Egyptian or British inheritance.

Sources:

- UK Parliament, *Anglo-Egyptian Treaty*, House of Commons debate, 24 November 1936: https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1936/nov/24/anglo-egyptian-treaty
- SOAS repository, *The Anglo-Egyptian treaty of 1936 with special reference to the contemporary situation in Egypt and the Sudan*: https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/output/404430/the-anglo-egyptian-treaty-of-1936-with-special-reference-to-the-contemporary-situation-in-egypt-and-the-sudan
- Library of Congress, *Sudan: A Country Study*: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/su/sudancountrystud00berry/sudancountrystud00berry.pdf

### Somali territories

The country-study record describes Somali populations divided among British Somaliland, French Somaliland, Italian Somaliland, the Ogaden, and Kenya’s Northern Frontier District. It also distinguishes British and Italian colonial economic systems and records the earlier Dervish resistance.

Design consequence:

- The Somali package should begin with voluntary association between territories.
- Pastoral movement, wells, ports, and customs are core political questions.
- The event must not create automatic cores over every Somali-inhabited area.
- A corridor that preserves mobility can be more valuable than early annexation.

Source:

- Library of Congress, *Somalia: A Country Study*, text edition: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/so/somaliacountryst00metz_0/somaliacountryst00metz_0_djvu.txt

## North African anchors

### Morocco

Research on Moroccan reformism describes a nationalist movement that by 1934 had become a national political and social reform movement, while the Plan of Reforms still worked within the language of the protectorate settlement. This supports a route where the sultan, reformist nationalists, protectorate zones, the Rif, and Tangier can cooperate or conflict.

Design consequence:

- Morocco’s early tree should include reform, reunification, and sovereignty strategies.
- The sultan and nationalist movement should not be treated as permanently identical.
- Spanish, French, and international-zone questions should create separate steps.
- Saharan ambition should follow a settled Moroccan constitution and viable logistics.

Sources:

- J. P. Halstead, *The Changing Character of Moroccan Reformism, 1921 to 1934*: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-african-history/article/changing-character-of-moroccan-reformism-19211934/4F8A0B72561E96D5D0CDF70E71632A19
- Barbara De Poli, *The Nationalist Movement in Morocco and the Struggle for Independence*: https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/article/annali-di-ca-foscari-serie-orientale/2016/52/art-10.14277-2385-3042-AnnOr-52-16-2.pdf

### Algeria

The Algerian package needs several political currents, rural and urban organisation, unequal citizenship, land, settler power, and the possibility of a long colonial war. The design avoids reducing these currents to one inevitable postwar movement.

Design consequence:

- Citizenship and land are constitutional questions that change every route.
- A republican route needs more than changing the ruling party.
- A people’s route needs rural production, urban labour, and military liberation to negotiate with each other.
- A military route should accumulate emergency-rule debt and settler-war risk.

Sources:

- Library of Congress, *Algeria: A Country Study*: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/al/algeriacountryst00metz_0/algeriacountryst00metz_0.pdf
- William B. Quandt, *Algerian Nationalism*: https://www.jstor.org/stable/48572292

### Tunisia

Research on French Tunisia places the sovereignty question at the centre of nationalist and protectorate politics from the rise of Neo-Destour in 1934. The continuing role of the bey and the growth of mass nationalist organisation create a genuine constitutional fork.

Design consequence:

- The bey, nationalist movement, labour, rural reform, and ports need distinct focus groups.
- A republic can grow from organisation and elections.
- A constitutional crowns route can preserve a limited royal institution.
- Foreign military pressure can create a temporary command path without making it the normal destination.

Source:

- Mary Dewhurst Lewis, *Divided Rule: Sovereignty and Empire in French Tunisia, 1881 to 1938*: https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1525/j.ctt4cgf5h

### Libya

The historical record describes prolonged Italian campaigns against resistance in Cyrenaica and the later incorporation of coastal provinces into Italy. The package therefore needs regional compact, resistance integration, colonial settlement damage, and difficult coast-interior logistics.

Design consequence:

- Cyrenaica, Tripolitania, and Fezzan should affect representation and command.
- Resistance victory does not automatically create a functioning national administration.
- Desert logistics and port control should determine military scale.
- A compact federal or confederal Libya should be a complete success route.

Source:

- US Department of State, Office of the Historian, *Foreign Relations of the United States, The Conference of Berlin, 1945, Libya background*: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945Berlinv01/d246

## West African anchors

### Liberia

The 1930 international inquiry into forced labour and the later history of Firestone’s concession show why Liberian sovereignty cannot be represented as an uncomplicated anti-colonial advantage. Formal independence coexisted with coercive labour, political exclusion, foreign financial pressure, and corporate leverage.

Design consequence:

- Liberia needs a concession and public-finance problem.
- Citizenship and hinterland representation must be part of the continental mandate.
- The player can renegotiate, nationalise with replacement capacity, form public partnerships, or preserve concessions under enforceable labour rules.
- A successful route should turn recovered revenue into visible roads, schools, administration, and partner aid.

Sources:

- US Department of State, Office of the Historian, *International Commission of Inquiry into Slavery and Forced Labor in Liberia*, 1930: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1930v03/d335
- Princeton and Slavery Project, *What Princeton Owes to Firestone’s Exploitation of Liberia*: https://slavery.princeton.edu/stories/what-princeton-owes-to-firestone

### Nigeria

The Library of Congress study places the modern colonial state in the 1914 amalgamation of northern and southern Nigeria and notes the rise of Lagos political organisation, including the National Youth Movement’s electoral ascendancy in 1938. This supports a host package centred on federal bargain, regional administration, transport, indirect rule, and national coalition.

Design consequence:

- Nigeria is not a blank large host with extra manpower.
- Federal representation, Lagos and interior balance, emirate institutions, rail and river links, and national movement politics should interact.
- A strong central executive without regional consent should create withdrawal and military risks.
- Nigeria’s scale should increase administrative burden as well as opportunity.

Source:

- Library of Congress, *Nigeria: A Country Study*, text edition: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/ni/nigeriacountryst00metz_0/nigeriacountryst00metz_0_djvu.txt

### Gold Coast and Asante

The country-study material confirms the long centrality of cocoa production and the importance of mining and transport. The existing research package also identifies chiefly institutions, elected politics, and the need to distinguish the Gold Coast host from an Asante restoration.

Design consequence:

- Producer revenue, transport, chiefs, elected bodies, mining, and Asante autonomy need a negotiated settlement.
- Cocoa and gold do not become a free national bonus.
- Producer prices, roads, processing, and public revenue determine whether export wealth builds trust.
- Asante receives a compact constitutional package with its own consent and port problem.

Sources:

- Library of Congress, *Ghana: A Country Study*, text edition: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/gh/ghanacountrystud00berr_0/ghanacountrystud00berr_0_djvu.txt
- Gold Coast Aborigines’ Rights Protection Society archival orientation in the same study and its bibliography.

## Central African anchors

### Congo Basin

The country-study literature provides a broad orientation to the enormous distances, river systems, regional political identities, mining centres, concessionary administration, and weak national institutions inherited by the Congo. It supports a host system where a functioning corridor and a regional congress matter more than raw territory.

Design consequence:

- Belgian Congo begins with transport, administrative transfer, labour, and regional legitimacy problems.
- Katanga, Kongo, Luba, Lunda, Kuba, and other packages are selected through viable regional outcomes.
- Mining revenue must be linked to local processing, labour, transport, and regional shares.
- A full tag is activated only when it has access, institutions, and a distinct political role.

Sources:

- Library of Congress, *Zaire: A Country Study*: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/za/zairecountrystud00medi_0/zairecountrystud00medi_0.pdf
- Library of Congress, text edition: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/za/zairecountrystud00medi_0/zairecountrystud00medi_0_djvu.txt

### Angola and Portuguese colonial systems

The Angola country study and Portugal study provide broad context for colonial labour, resource extraction, coastal and interior disconnection, and the economic role of colonies in the Portuguese system.

Design consequence:

- Angola’s first proof is a paid-labour public corridor.
- Port and rail access, concession review, and regional representation should precede large expansion.
- Kongo overlap is a congress issue.
- Portuguese intervention risk affects every southeast and Atlantic island compact without creating a separate global currency.

Sources:

- Library of Congress, *Angola: A Country Study*: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/an/angolacountrystu00coll_0/angolacountrystu00coll_0.pdf

## East African and Great Lakes anchors

### Uganda and Buganda

The Uganda study describes the 1900 Buganda Agreement, mailo land, chiefly taxation, the distinct treatment of other kingdoms, and the growth of cotton production. It also records the enduring Buganda-Bunyoro question and the lost counties.

Design consequence:

- Uganda begins with an asymmetric federal problem.
- Buganda’s royal and legislative institutions, mailo land, cotton revenue, lake access, and Bunyoro grievances require separate settlements.
- Buganda can be a sovereign member, federal component, or rival.
- Successful development must produce visible benefits outside Buganda.

Source:

- Library of Congress, *Uganda: A Country Study*, text edition: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/ug/ugandacountrystu00byrn_0/ugandacountrystu00byrn_0_djvu.txt

### Kenya and the East African corridor

The Kenyan package is grounded in the relationship among land, settler power, labour, the railway, ports, pastoral access, and regional political exclusion. The focus design uses these as connected pressures instead of a generic independence chain.

Design consequence:

- Land settlement changes constitutional legitimacy and military risk.
- The railway is both a development route and a source of host concentration.
- Pastoral access and regional government need named state or region work.
- East African federation becomes plausible only after the host solves enough of its domestic land problem.

Source:

- Library of Congress, Federal Research Division, *Country Profile: Kenya*: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/copr/Kenya.pdf

## Southern African anchors

### South Africa

South African History Online records the 1936 removal of African voters from the Cape common roll, the Development Trust and Land Act, and the All Africa Convention that brought together more than 500 delegates from towns, rural areas, reserves, the Transkei, Zululand, and the protectorates. These sources support a civil-war and constitutional package involving more than an elite split inside the existing dominion.

Design consequence:

- An Allied South African host must face the mandated civil war.
- The continental coalition needs African political organisations, rural and urban delegates, protectorate actors, labour, land, and military defectors.
- Victory must dismantle the exclusionary order and produce a peace mechanism with the Allies.
- A settler-led victory, military coalition victory, federal settlement, republic, people’s route, and failed coalition require different aftermaths.

Sources:

- South African History Online, *State policies and social protest, 1924 to 1939*: https://sahistory.org.za/article/state-policies-and-social-protest-1924-1939
- South African History Online, *General South African History Timeline: 1930s*: https://sahistory.org.za/article/general-south-african-history-timeline-1930s
- Library of Congress, *South Africa: A Country Study*: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/so/southafricacount00byrn/southafricacount00byrn.pdf

### Southern Rhodesia and Great Zimbabwe

Research on Rhodesian land politics identifies the Land Apportionment Act of 1930 as a central legal division of land. The design therefore treats land, mining, railway control, settler government, and African political exclusion as the host’s unavoidable opening problem.

Design consequence:

- Southern Rhodesia cannot become the African host through a cosmetic switch alone.
- A constitutional rupture, settler conflict, or liberation coalition must settle land and representation.
- Great Zimbabwe is a compact restoration mandate tied to living communities, land, heritage custody, mining, and transport.
- Mutapa and Rozwi remain alternate or associated outcomes instead of three simultaneous maximum-border restorations.

Source:

- Josiah Brownell, *The Collapse of Rhodesia*: https://sahistory.org.za/sites/default/files/archive-files/josiah_brownell_the_collapse_of_rhodesia_populabook4you.pdf

## Indian Ocean anchors

### Madagascar and Merina

The Indian Ocean country study records French annexation in 1896, the exile of the queen and prime minister, and the emergence of nationalist sentiment among Merina intellectuals in Antananarivo. The broader study also places Madagascar beside Mauritius, Comoros, and Seychelles in a regional island context.

Design consequence:

- Madagascar needs a highland and coastal constitutional settlement.
- Merina can be a constitutional kingdom, a federal component, a republic, or an island-wide claimant only through regional confidence.
- Ports, roads, labour, land, and island defence determine practical authority.
- The Islands Council must provide serious play for small members without pretending they can sustain mainland-sized armies.

Sources:

- Library of Congress, *Indian Ocean: Five Island Countries*: https://www.loc.gov/item/95016570/
- Library of Congress, text edition: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/in/indianoceanfivei00metz_0/indianoceanfivei00metz_0_djvu.txt

## Priority restoration research boundaries

The sixteen priority packages are implementation candidates, not final territorial judgments. The following matters require package-specific specialist review before final scripting:

- exact capital and fallback state groups
- claimant and succession validity
- public name and adjective by route
- constitutional titles and court terminology
- religious and civic representation
- historical flags and symbols
- overlaps with neighbouring modern and restored countries
- local language localisation
- starting commanders and political figures
- the distinction between sourced history and alternate-history synthesis

A package should remain a compact autonomy, cultural body, associated council, or dormant candidate when this research cannot support a safe full country.

## Research deductions that changed the design

The research led to six concrete revisions:

1. Host identity now changes the first proof mission, route validity, early rivals, and post-unification legacy.
2. Federal, republican, royal, people’s, military, and confederal routes now answer country-specific constitutional questions.
3. Land, labour, concession, corridor, island, and regional-representation problems receive explicit focus groups and crisis missions.
4. Priority restorations separate cultural recognition from territorial integration.
5. South Africa’s Allied branch is a coalition civil war with a constitutional aftermath and peace logic.
6. High-chaos content remains a later mutation. It does not replace the researched human opening.
