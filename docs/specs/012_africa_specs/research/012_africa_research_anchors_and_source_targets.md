## Earlier anchor notes

# 012 Africa second-pass research anchors

This file records source anchors used to shape the second-pass expansion. It is not final localisation and does not settle super-event quotes, title wording, cultural remarks, or audio.

## Core continental and diaspora framing

- UNESCO General History of Africa: https://www.unesco.org/en/general-history-africa
  - Use as a broad reference anchor for treating African history as deep, regional, and continent-wide rather than a short list of obvious states.
- African Union Agenda 2063 overview: https://au.int/en/agenda2063/overview
  - Use only as modern Pan-African integration inspiration. Do not copy modern institutional language into 1930s player-facing text.
- Marcus Garvey and Black Star Line, National Archives: https://www.archives.gov/nhprc/projects/catalog/marcus-garvey
  - Use as source direction for diaspora return, Black Star route motifs, and Back-to-Africa political memory.
- Marcus Garvey individual page, National Archives: https://www.archives.gov/research/african-americans/individuals/marcus-garvey
  - Use with caution because the source discusses controversy and prosecution. The route should not make Garvey himself the only diaspora reference.

## UNESCO and heritage anchors

- Aksum, UNESCO World Heritage Centre: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/15/
  - Aksum anchors Horn highland restoration, stelae motifs, Red Sea route direction, and highland defense.
- Ruins of Kilwa Kisiwani and Ruins of Songo Mnara, UNESCO World Heritage Centre: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/144/
  - Kilwa anchors Swahili coast maritime restoration, coral-stone architecture, and Indian Ocean trade direction.
- Great Zimbabwe National Monument, UNESCO World Heritage Centre: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/364/
  - Great Zimbabwe anchors plateau restoration, stone-city motifs, and heritage protection missions.
- Archaeological Sites of the Island of Meroe, UNESCO World Heritage Centre: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1336/
  - Meroe anchors Kush, Nile heritage, living-statue high-chaos direction, and river integration.
- Timbuktu, UNESCO World Heritage Centre: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/119/
  - Timbuktu anchors Songhai, scholarship, manuscript protection, Sahel route legitimacy, and anti-destruction missions.

## Museum and overview anchors

- The Met Heilbrunn Timeline, Guinea Coast 1600 to 1800: https://artistproject.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/09/afg.html
  - Useful for Asante, Oyo, Dahomey, courtly art, and coastal trade direction.
- The Met Heilbrunn Timeline, Eastern and Southern Africa 1000 to 1400: https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/07/afa.html
  - Useful for Great Zimbabwe, gold and copper mining, stone structures, and southern route assets.
- The Met Heilbrunn Timeline overview: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/timeline-of-art-history
  - Useful as a museum-authored reference path for visual and cultural motifs.

  - Use as a general source direction for Kongo river restoration and Atlantic contact. Confirm with stronger sources during implementation if exact flags, leaders, or symbols are needed.

## Notes for later researchers

The package uses direct country names and avoids office-like map names. Institution labels such as congress, court, command, charter, and league are mechanics, routes, or internal bodies. They are not public map names unless paired with a direct country identity that remains readable.

For diaspora content, treat return as a contested settlement system with consent, local reception, housing, industry, ports, convoys, and diplomacy. Do not write it as frictionless mass relocation.

For high-chaos content, keep nonhuman and supernatural actors clearly nonhuman or supernatural. Do not map them onto real African ethnic groups, languages, or religions as caricature.
