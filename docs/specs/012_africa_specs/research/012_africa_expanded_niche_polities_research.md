# Event 012 Africa — Expanded Niche Polity and High-Chaos Research Notes

This note expands the Africa event beyond broad regional authorities. It gives the implementation agent a researched pool of niche historical polities, cultural-political identities, ecological actors, and high-chaos fantasy routes that can be used as releasable subjects, Charter League members, regional authority upgrades, hidden focus routes, decision targets, or late-game absurd branches.

This is not a source approval file for final quotes, final super-event localisation, final real leader portraits, or historical flags. Any final quote, cultural remark, audio, real portrait, attested flag, or sacred symbol still requires the proper super-event or asset workflow.

## How to use these anchors

The grounded event should use these anchors as regional flavour and gameplay identity. The higher the chaos tier, the more these anchors may mutate into impossible states, symbolic councils, ecological pact countries, and nonhuman actors. The baseline should never pretend that every historical polity literally returns unchanged in 1936; most should appear as congress offices, regional councils, military academies, cultural claims, charter authorities, or restoration movements inside the wider Pan-African Charter League.

Design uses:

- **Baseline to Evolution I:** researched names, decision text, regional missions, local leaders/councils, subject names, unit styles, faction office names, and resistance/federation flavour.
- **Evolution II:** historical restoration packages, regional elite units, specialized authorities, stronger local trees, and limited high-chaos rumours.
- **Evolution III:** cross-regional sponsor states, mythic-technical branches, ecological ultimatums, and continental export missions.
- **Evolution IV:** explicitly absurd actors, nonhuman countries, living-landscape states, supernatural courts, and dynamic union names.
- **World-end path:** only after other continent unifiers exist and pursue their own world-end paths; these anchors become “world assembly” subdelegations rather than instant annexation rewards.

## Source reliability bands

- **High confidence:** UNESCO, Britannica, major museum pages, conservation organizations, or primary/archival pages.
- **Medium confidence:** museum essays, specialist educational pages, or source summaries that are credible but not enough for final localisation or flag sourcing.
- **Use only as inspiration:** Wikipedia snippets, popular history sites, tourism pages, myth summaries, or unsourced cultural claims. These may guide route ideas but should not be repeated as factual loading-screen lore without verification.

## Niche authority design patterns

### Pattern A — Charter member with heritage office

Use for grounded polities such as Kongo, Asante, Buganda, Merina, Kanem-Bornu, and Great Zimbabwe. The authority is a human political/cultural office inside the African Charter League. It has a council portrait, a flag or emblem, regional decisions, and small forces. It does not claim to restore a full precolonial state unless the player chooses a crown/restoration path.

Mechanics:

- Starts with `Regional Trust`, `Local Mandate`, and `Charter Debt` variables.
- Can grant legitimacy, integration speed, regional units, or local compliance.
- Can resist if forced into annexation.
- Historical flag/symbol source mode depends on whether the implementation uses real symbols or fictional congress emblems.

### Pattern B — Restoration subject

Use when the crown route or regional autonomy route chooses to revive a polity as a named subject or federation member.

Mechanics:

- Requires a focus route and state-control threshold.
- Starts as autonomous subject/faction member.
- Gains an identity package, leader/council, name, flag, party names, starting forces, and focus/decision micro-branch.
- Can become a permanent federation member, protectorate, or integrated state.
- Stronger restoration subjects may refuse integration or challenge the unifier’s authority.

### Pattern C — Specialist military school

Use for Mossi cavalry, Oyo cavalry, Dahomey guard, Highland schools, Desert columns, elephant logistics, river guards, and port guards.

Mechanics:

- Does not require a new country tag.
- Unlocks templates, commanders, decisions, missions, and unit-related achievements.
- Costs equipment/XP/manpower/supply, not only political power.
- Should add pressure if spammed.

### Pattern D — Archeology-to-chaos chain

Use for Sao terracotta, Benin bronze constructs, Great Zimbabwe walls, Kush/Meroë pyramids, Aksum stelae.

Mechanics:

1. Baseline: cultural archive or heritage-protection decision.
2. Evolution II: special construction, propaganda, or unit-support unlock.
3. Evolution III/IV: impossible construct or living monument branch if Green Covenant/Mythic Charge is high.
4. Failure: foreign panic, local fear, artifact damage, construction collapse, or diplomatic rupture.

### Pattern E — Nonhuman sanctuary country

Use for primate, bonobo, okapi, lemur, and similar absurd actors.

Mechanics:

- Must be explicitly nonhuman or ecological/supernatural.
- Must be registered as special/nonhuman in implementation.
- Has no normal ideology labels, no human leader-name pool, no ordinary conscription, and no claims to human-populated capitals.
- Uses sanctuary control, habitat missions, trust, and pact status instead of normal annexation.
- Can join final world assembly only as a delegation/pact, not as a human state.

## Higher-tier absurd path additions

### Evolution II additions — “The Continent Finds Instruments”

- Elephant Logistics Corps becomes a real unlock with terrain, supply, and intimidation roles.
- Regional authorities can reveal one researched heritage office each.
- Congo Basin, Lake Chad, Zambezi, and coastal states can begin Green Covenant reports.
- The unifier may open “Archive Protection” decisions to prevent later high-chaos routes from becoming hostile.

### Evolution III additions — “The Continent Exports the Pattern”

- Historical authority offices can sponsor sister movements outside Africa.
- Heritage offices become diplomatic weapons: Kongo Atlantic, Kilwa Indian Ocean, Aksum Red Sea, Kanem-Bornu Sahara, Merina Indian Ocean, and Lozi/Zambezi corridors.
- First nonhuman sanctuary actors may appear if the player used habitat protection and avoided extractive abuse.
- If the player used forced integration, the same actors appear hostile or neutral instead.

### Evolution IV additions — “The Continent Stops Behaving Like a Map”

- Living monuments, nonhuman sanctuaries, floodplain courts, ghost convoys, and mirage actors can become visible tags or scripted mechanics.
- Dynamic union names must include niche-authority prestige if relevant. Examples: an Africa + Middle East + Nile-Horn axis can use an Afro-Red Sea name; Africa + India Ocean + Asia can use Afro-Asian Oceanic Union; Africa + Europe + Asia can remain Afro-Eurasian Union.
- Normal diplomats begin treating the unifier as a world-chaos pole, not only a regional state.

## AI use

AI should not randomly activate every niche route. It should choose based on geography, host identity, and route.

- West African hosts prefer Asante, Benin, Nri, Aro, Mossi, Oyo, Dahomey, Jolof/Futa, and diaspora offices.
- Sahel/Sahara hosts prefer Kanem-Bornu, Sao, Garamantes, Mossi, Tuareg/Toubou, and Nommo-observatory branches.
- Nile-Horn hosts prefer Aksum, Kush/Meroë, Adal-Harar, Ajuran, and Red Sea offices.
- East African/coastal hosts prefer Kilwa, Ajuran, Swahili, Buganda, Great Lakes, and Indian Ocean offices.
- Central African hosts prefer Kongo, Kuba-Luba-Lunda, Kazembe, Congo Basin, Lozi, and primate/forest sanctuary routes.
- Southern hosts prefer Great Zimbabwe, Mutapa/Rozwi, Lozi/Zambezi, South African Liberation Congress, and stone-wall branches.
- Madagascar/Indian Ocean hosts prefer Merina, coastal Malagasy offices, Kilwa, Mami Wata/tidemark branches, and lemur parliament high-chaos route.

AI route restrictions:

- Ordinary AI should not take Evolution IV absurd branches unless chaos is already high and the branch is geographically relevant.
- Militarist AI may use specialist schools and forced integration, but should avoid nonhuman pacts unless desperate.
- Federal/reform AI should prefer heritage offices and peaceful integration.
- Crown AI should prefer restoration subjects and small thrones.
- Green Covenant AI should prefer habitat compact, nonhuman pacts, tide/flood routes, and living monument routes.
- If a nonhuman actor becomes hostile, AI should contain or negotiate before using extractive industry in its habitat.
