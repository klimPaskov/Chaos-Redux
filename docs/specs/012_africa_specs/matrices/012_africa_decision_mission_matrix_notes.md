# Event 12 Africa decision and mission matrix notes

## Purpose

`012_africa_decision_mission_matrix.csv` converts the Charter League, integration, diaspora, economy, rival-bloc, high-chaos, Scramble, and world-order designs into 102 implementation-ready action concepts.

The matrix is planning material. Keys are stable working identifiers, not final player-facing localisation. Exact costs, durations, targets, state groups, and AI weights remain tuning work, but every row already defines the action owner, target, purpose, dynamic cost basis, duration band, success, partial success, failure, value changes, AI logic, and cleanup.

## Expansion additions

The ten revision rows deepen systems already present in the package. They do not create new parallel currencies or categories.

- Every grounded constitution receives at least one crisis action that tests its governing promise.
- The hidden Covenant receives an obligation review with bounded success, partial success, failure, and cleanup.
- A failed host opening can recover through a demanding mission rather than ending the event or receiving a free reset.
- Compact polities can be promoted through a regional congress only when they have viable territory, institutions, access, and a distinct role.
- Unified Africa must review emergency institutions after war instead of carrying wartime government into the endgame without consequence.

## Lifecycle standard

The normal lifecycle is:

1. A focus, event state, member relationship, region state, or evolution reveals an action family.
2. A decision or mission selects one valid country, state, region, route, or world-order target.
3. Costs are computed from distance, target size, host capacity, war state, confidence, authority, burden, pressure, readiness, and route policy.
4. The action creates visible progress and can succeed, partially succeed, or fail.
5. The result updates public mechanic values and relationship state.
6. Temporary targets, missions, access rights, units, project flags, and selected-country state are cleared when the action resolves or becomes invalid.

No row is intended to become a passive political-power shop item. Political power appears only where political attention is part of a broader cost package.

## Target selection and clutter control

The main decision category should not display all 102 rows at once. Use the following staged presentation:

- one selected member or outside-state card at a time for country-targeted actions
- one selected region for congress, corridor, restoration, and claim-overlap actions
- active mission caps based on Charter Authority and administrative capacity
- route and phase filters that hide obsolete actions
- emergency-only rows for war, famine, disaster, outbreak, departure, and intervention states
- replacement of early actions by stronger later versions rather than permanent accumulation
- separate post-unification pages for Scramble and world-order actions
- AI access that does not depend on human selector clicks

## Dynamic cost standard

Cost formulas should draw from concrete resources and visible pressure:

- infantry and support equipment
- trucks, trains, convoys, aircraft, fuel, and ships
- army, navy, air, and command experience
- manpower and temporary division commitments
- civilian and military factory burden
- stability, war support, legitimacy, and member confidence
- diplomatic credibility, intelligence exposure, and patronage risk
- local support, corridor access, state control, and project capacity
- ecological concessions, contamination control, and disease countermeasures for high-chaos routes

Costs should rise with distance, state size, local hostility, existing Integration Burden, active projects, hostile naval control, and overlapping claims. They should fall with corridor readiness, high confidence, compatible constitutional clauses, local support, regional readiness, and prior successful cooperation.

## Success and failure standard

Every timed or risky action needs all applicable outcomes:

- full success that materially changes relationship, territory, logistics, military power, development, or world-order state
- partial success that preserves investment but leaves a new problem
- failure that creates a real consequence, such as authority loss, confidence loss, burden, rival pressure, colonial pressure, damaged access, destroyed equipment, unrest, disease spread, or war
- cancellation and cleanup when the target dies, changes sides, becomes invalid, reaches terminal state, or resolves the problem through another route

## AI standard

AI should compare the action against:

- target survival value and regional strategic importance
- distance, supply, access, naval risk, and force ratio
- Charter Authority, Integration Burden, Colonial Pressure, and readiness
- target confidence, ideology, government continuity, and rival influence
- whether the action supports the chosen constitutional route
- whether an existing mission already addresses the same target
- whether failure would create an unrecoverable war or member cascade

High-chaos AI actions require explicit evolution and route gates. Ordinary AI must not enter disease, elemental retaliation, nonhuman recruitment, or terminal continent-war actions accidentally.

## Exploit controls

Implementation should prevent:

- repeated free divisions or equipment through mission restart loops
- confidence farming on a single harmless target
- repeated annexation or coring without integration cost
- parallel contradictory accession offers
- simultaneous exit and integration actions for one member
- repeated disaster punishment without Ecological Wrath or cooldown cost
- continent sponsorship stacking beyond sustainable capacity
- terminal-war preparation against invalid, allied, or unreachable targets
- tag switching or puppet release that resets obligations and costs

## Validation handoff

The decision and mission auditor should compare every implemented row to this matrix and record whether it is implemented, merged, queued, rejected, or superseded. The implementation report should also include active mission caps, phase visibility, dynamic cost inputs, AI use, cleanup, and at least one manual scenario for each family.
