# Event 012 Africa AI route matrix notes

## Purpose

`012_africa_ai_route_matrix.csv` defines 64 AI profiles for regional hosts, individual full host dossiers, constitutional routes, member relationship states, outside powers, high-chaos actors, and continent-scale movements.

## Current reconciliation boundary (2026-08-11)

All 64 profile predicates, loaders, registry calls, policy vectors, and 102-action dispatch paths are source-present, and the acceptance ledger records all 64 AI profiles as `implemented`. The probability surface still requires the named MCP scenario audit because the strategy-plan adapter does not expose a usable final surface; this is an evidence limitation, not permission to replace the bespoke profiles with flat weights or to call the registry complete at campaign-balance level.

Exact local pools remain distinct from bounded dynamic profile, action, decision, focus, strategy, and world-order evaluations. No profile omission or normalized current probability claim is inferred from an incomplete MCP manifest.

The matrix prevents one generic AI weight table from controlling a system whose actors have different goals. It gives each profile route preferences, avoidance logic, focus priorities, decision behaviour, diplomacy, military use, mechanic-value policy, rare-content policy, invalid-route blockers, and cleanup.

## Profile groups

- 9 regional host overlays
- 22 full host-country overlays
- 7 constitutional routes
- 8 member and rival relationship states
- 5 outside-power reactions
- 6 high-chaos actor profiles
- 7 continent and world-order profiles

The regional host overlay remains the fallback for every country. The individual profile modifies it with country-specific survival problems, first priorities, constitutional risks, diplomatic constraints, and high-chaos gates.

## Core host AI cycle

1. Stabilise the selected host and secure the capital region.
2. Solve the country-specific opening problem before claiming broad continental authority.
3. Build one reliable protection and logistics corridor.
4. Protect nearby threatened African states before opening broad integration.
5. Form the League only when it can sustain guarantees.
6. Choose a constitutional route from ideology, member composition, war state, legitimacy, host institutions, and military pressure.
7. Develop one or two regions deeply before opening every region at once.
8. Keep Integration Burden below the route ceiling.
9. Respond to member confidence crises before coercive expansion.
10. Prepare for the Scramble only after Africa approaches unity.
11. Sponsor another continent only when Africa is stable enough to absorb the obligation.

## Country-specific AI

A full host profile must change play. It should alter at least:

- the first focus priorities
- the preferred proof mission and corridor
- the acceptable constitutional routes
- the member or rival most likely to create an early crisis
- the military geography and force policy
- the values the AI protects most strongly
- the conditions under which the high-chaos route is permitted

An individual profile must fall back to its regional profile when the host identity becomes invalid. It must not leave stale targets or continue a route whose country-specific institutions no longer exist.

## Route validity

An AI route must be weighted to zero or safely bypassed when its required state is missing. Examples include:

- federal route without a viable member base
- council-of-crowns route without recognised monarchies
- People’s Union without worker, producer, food, land, or industrial institutions
- military route without a credible army or existential war
- high-chaos Covenant without Evolution III and valid actors
- restoration action when the polity is already active, impossible on the current map, or blocked by an unresolved stronger claim
- continent sponsorship when the target continent package is absent or already terminally resolved
- terminal war against an allied, merged, dead, unreachable, or invalid continent actor

The AI must never choose a route merely because a focus is visible.

## Member agency

Member AI should judge League membership from delivered benefits, obligations, confidence, route compatibility, local politics, and outside pressure. A strong member may demand senior status or lead a rival bloc. A protected state may accept aid while refusing integration. A leaving member may return through consent after a peaceful settlement.

No AI member should accept annexation because its opinion is positive alone. Integration needs the relationship state, confidence, clause, route, local support, and burden conditions defined in the Charter system.

## Outside-power AI

Outside powers use five broad approaches:

- negotiated withdrawal
- sanctions and containment
- expeditionary intervention
- recognition and partnership
- opportunistic patronage

Their choice should respond to force ratio, naval access, colonial holdings, war exhaustion, ideology, trade exposure, coalition support, African readiness, and high-chaos escalation. Great powers should not launch a doomed inland expedition without ports, supply, air access, or coalition strength.

## High-chaos AI

Nonhuman and supernatural actors are sovereign or system actors with their own interests. They are never treated as generic recruitment pools.

High-chaos AI requires:

- Evolution III or equivalent forced high-tier opening
- content-enable checks
- valid origin region
- explicit actor state
- rights, bargain, wrath, disease, or containment logic
- bounded targeting rules for disaster and disease use
- defined treaty, integration, defeat, containment, or rampage outcomes

Ordinary AI must not enter these routes accidentally.

## World-order AI

Africa and other continent unifiers should compare partnership, union, sponsorship, rivalry, and war. They should consider ideology, historic grievances, recognition, shared enemies, reach, logistics, member confidence, integration burden, and the sustainability of postwar settlement.

A continent actor should not accept puppet status as the normal result of sponsorship. A negotiated two-continent union requires consent, constitutional compatibility, and a long preparation chain. Terminal wars require the world-end path and valid reachable rivals.

## Validation handoff

Implementation validation should include:

- one normal simulation for every regional host overlay
- one targeted opening test for every full host-country overlay
- route-choice tests for all seven constitutional paths
- member acceptance, refusal, exit, return, and rival-bloc cases
- at least one sanctions and one expeditionary Scramble response
- high-chaos disabled and enabled cases
- one successful and one failed continent sponsorship
- terminal route blockers with unresolved or allied continent powers
- cleanup after annexation, civil war, government change, target death, route lock, and world_end

The completion auditor should receive a disposition for every profile and should flag generic flat weights where the matrix calls for campaign-state logic.
