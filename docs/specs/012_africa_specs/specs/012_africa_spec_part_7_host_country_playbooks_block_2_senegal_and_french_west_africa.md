## Senegal and French West Africa

### Starting tension

A Senegal-centred French West African host begins with the Four Communes and their citizenship history, the authority of the federal colonial administration, Dakar’s port and military importance, Islamic and traditional networks, rural producer interests, and vast territories whose populations did not share the communes’ political status. The host must prove that Dakar is a continental door rather than another imperial capital.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is citizenship in a narrow enclave and administration over a vast federation. It tracks commune politics, rural representation, labour, religious and local authority, Dakar’s military role, interterritorial revenue, and the voice of inland territories. It can mature into a West African federation, a broad republic, a council of territorial governments and traditional authorities, a socialist labour and producer union, a military liberation command, or a confederation of former federal territories.

### First proof mission

Build an inland aid corridor from Dakar through at least two territories while winning formal support from a non-Senegalese regional council. Full success requires shared command of the mission. Partial success completes the corridor but confirms Dakar’s dominance.

### Host focus modules

- **Citizenship beyond the communes.** Expands political rights without pretending that one legal model fits every territory.
- **Dakar and the inland capitals.** Establishes rotating congresses, revenue shares, and interterritorial offices.
- **Railways of the federation.** Turns colonial extraction routes into member-serving corridors.
- **Marabouts, unions, veterans, and councils.** Builds a coalition across institutions that cannot be reduced to one party.
- **The French West African settlement.** Decides whether the inherited federation becomes one state, a federation, or a confederation of sovereign members.

### Route mutations

Federalism is the most natural route but must protect inland territories from Dakar. The republic route uses commune institutions and mass politics while widening citizenship. The crowns route is less central but can recognise traditional states and religious authorities in a protected chamber. The people’s union route gains strong railway, port, veteran, and producer mobilisation. Military continentalism grows from Dakar’s strategic assets and colonial troops, with a high risk of replacing civilian federalism. Confederation allows the former territories to retain sovereignty while using shared transport and diplomacy.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

French Sudan, Guinea, Côte d’Ivoire, Mauritania, Niger, Upper Volta, and Dahomey should have distinct confidence values. Côte d’Ivoire or an inland coalition can reject Dakar’s fiscal leadership. Religious authorities can oppose secular centralisation without being treated as a single bloc. A coercive host can produce a federation-without-Senegal rival.

### Military and economy

The host’s advantage is Dakar, naval access, airfields, rail, and veterans. Its weakness is the enormous distance and uneven development of the federal territory. Development should use corridor projects chosen by member councils, not free factories concentrated in Senegal.

### Post-unification signature

A successful host can create a rotating continental congress, common civil-service school, or interterritorial revenue formula. Dakar may remain a strategic capital, but it should not become the only centre by default.

### High-chaos gate

Sahel, river, or oracle content requires high chaos and should use fictional entities rather than coding living religious or ethnic communities as magical states.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Senegal/FWA prioritises at least two inland endorsements before deep integration. It avoids treating every former federal territory as an automatic core. The overlay uses one federal relationship system rather than separate diplomacy systems for each territory.

## Sierra Leone

### Starting tension

Sierra Leone begins with a sharp political and legal divide between Freetown’s colony, the protectorate, and their different institutions. It also has a distinctive Atlantic and diaspora connection, a strategic harbour, and limited economic depth. Its continental leadership depends on reconciling the colony-protectorate divide and using the port for shared African purposes.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is two political countries inside one territory. It tracks Freetown representation, protectorate authority, mining and trade concessions, education, interior roads, and the legitimacy of coastal elites. It can become a federal republic, a broad civic republic, a council of protectorate and urban authorities, a labour and mining union, a small military port state, or an Atlantic confederation.

### First proof mission

Open a protected port-to-interior aid route and recruit both colony and protectorate institutions into the mission. Full success creates a trusted West African liaison centre. Partial success leaves the domestic divide unresolved.

### Host focus modules

- **One legislature, two histories.** Designs representation before any continental constitution is proposed.
- **Freetown for African service.** Builds harbour, medical, educational, and communications functions.
- **The protectorate has a voice.** Links infrastructure and political authority.
- **Mines under public terms.** Rewrites concession obligations and funds local development.
- **Atlantic return without privilege.** Makes diaspora links voluntary and locally accountable.

### Route mutations

Federalism and confederation fit the domestic duality. The republic route is credible after franchise expansion. The people’s union route grows from labour and mining. Military continentalism is narrow and defensive. The crowns route can protect local authorities but should not invent a national monarchy.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Liberia, Guinea, and inland actors can resist a Freetown-centred project. A host that privileges returnees or coastal institutions can trigger an interior sovereignty bloc.

### Military and economy

The host relies on harbour defence, small professional forces, medical and training missions, and mining revenue. Its continental value is service and access, not mass army size.

### Post-unification signature

Sierra Leone can provide a continental legal college, medical service, or Atlantic citizenship office.

### High-chaos gate

Forest or Atlantic supernatural material remains late, fictional, and separate from real communities.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Sierra Leone favours federalism, republic, or confederation and seeks strong allies. The overlay remains compact because the country’s differentiation comes from the colony-protectorate settlement and port role.

# Central Africa and the Congo basin

## Belgian Congo

### Starting tension

The Belgian Congo begins as a vast extractive colony with major mineral wealth, mission and company institutions, limited African political participation, long transport corridors, and regional societies that were never represented by one colonial centre. Its continental project can command resources, but it starts with almost no legitimate mandate to command people.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is an extraction state without citizenship. It tracks company power, mission services, labour coercion, regional administration, Force Publique loyalty, river and rail access, urban worker politics, and the absence of representative institutions. It can mature into a federal Congo republic, a regional confederation, a council of restored and modern authorities, a workers and resource union, a military emergency state, or a highly decentralised Charter member system.

The player must build political institutions while keeping the enormous transport network functioning. Immediate central nationalisation can remove company control but also collapse technical management, exports, and supply. Gradual Africanisation can preserve output but risk entrenching foreign leverage.

### First proof mission

Maintain a complete mineral or river corridor from an interior region to a port while replacing at least one coercive labour or company practice with a member-serving institution. Full success then funds a protected African partner. Partial success keeps production but leaves the host politically discredited.

### Host focus modules

- **A country larger than its administration.** Creates regional assemblies and administrative training before national centralisation.
- **The companies answer to law.** Audits concessions, labour, security forces, and infrastructure obligations.
- **River, rail, and port.** Turns the Congo transport network into a visible Reach system with chokepoints.
- **The Force Publique settlement.** Chooses Africanisation, regional guards, a professional national army, or continued foreign command under limits.
- **Kongo, Katanga, Kasai, the river, and the east.** Establishes a multi-region constitutional conference without treating these labels as exhaustive identities.
- **Resources for members.** Connects mining revenue to regional development and continental aid.

### Route mutations

Federalism is the strongest peaceful route and should include revenue-sharing and regional vetoes. The republic route builds citizenship and a national civil service but risks centralising Léopoldville. The crowns route works through selected restored polities and regional institutions, with no single invented Congo-wide crown. The people’s union route mobilises mineworkers, transport labour, farmers, and anti-concession politics, but it faces capital flight and foreign intervention. Military continentalism gains the Force Publique and strategic minerals, yet carries the highest mutiny and regional-breakaway risk. Confederation can preserve major regions as powerful members and may be the most stable early choice.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Katanga’s mineral power, Kongo political identity, Kasai interests, eastern Great Lakes ties, and river regions must remain distinct. A host that centralises revenue or army command can produce a mineral secession, a restored-polity coalition, or a foreign-backed breakaway. Angola, French Equatorial Africa, Uganda, and Tanganyika can each sponsor different corridors and claim influence.

### Military and economy

The host has manpower, minerals, river transport, and an existing colonial force, but weak national cohesion and severe distance. Military expansion should depend on Africanising officers, controlling depots, and protecting corridors. Economic rewards should be large only when the player completes regional projects and prevents one centre from taking all revenue.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Congo host can create a continental resource dividend, regional infrastructure equalisation fund, or river navigation authority. A coercive host can instead leave a central security and mining bureaucracy that increases long-term Burden.

### High-chaos gate

Forest entities, living rivers, giant fauna, or mineral-born actors require high chaos and clearly nonhuman presentation. They must not be coded as hidden versions of Congolese peoples.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Congo prioritises regional administration, corridor integrity, and company control before continental war. It should prefer federalism, confederation, or a carefully built people’s union. The overlay uses a small number of strategic regions rather than hundreds of local tags.

## Angola

### Starting tension

Angola begins under Portuguese colonial rule with a long Atlantic coast, an administrative and settler centre around Luanda, plantation and forced-labour structures, regional kingdoms and identities, a difficult interior transport network, and neighbouring Congo and southern African connections. Its continental project must choose how liberation relates to labour, land, language, regional authority, and the Kongo border.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is a coastal colonial state over a fragmented interior. It tracks forced labour, settler property, assimilado privilege, regional representation, plantation and mining interests, railway access, and the legitimacy of armed versus civic movements. It can mature into a federal republic, a central republic, a restored-council settlement, a socialist liberation state, a military front government, or an Atlantic and Congo-basin confederation.

### First proof mission

Secure one of the major east-west corridors from the interior to the coast while replacing compulsory labour with paid mobilisation or public works. Full success also establishes a negotiated relationship with a Kongo-region actor or a southern neighbour. Partial success wins the corridor but leaves labour coercion in place, raising Burden.

### Host focus modules

- **Labour cannot be the state.** Ends, reforms, or disguises coercive labour, with consequences that the mechanic remembers.
- **Luanda and the plateaus.** Builds representation and supply beyond the capital.
- **The Benguela connection.** Turns rail access and mineral transit into a continental corridor question involving neighbouring states.
- **Kongo across the border.** Opens federation, autonomy, cultural links, or rivalry without giving automatic transborder annexation.
- **Movements before ministries.** Decides how liberation organisations, local authorities, workers, churches, and officers share power.

### Route mutations

Federalism gives regional assemblies, Kongo autonomy, and corridor revenue sharing. The republic route can build national citizenship and education but risks Luanda centralism. The crowns route uses selected historical institutions as constituent authorities rather than inventing one monarchy. The people’s union route is strong on labour, land, and resource control, with major foreign and settler resistance. Military continentalism can unify armed fronts but is prone to factional command and external sponsorship. Confederation creates a safer bridge between Atlantic, Congo, and southern regions.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Kongo actors, central highland authorities, eastern corridor communities, and southern pastoral regions can resist Luanda. Portugal and foreign concession holders can arm rival forces. The Belgian Congo, Northern Rhodesia, and South Africa can compete over rail and mineral access.

### Military and economy

Angola’s forces should grow from defectors, local mobilisation, railway guards, port units, and foreign aid. The country has useful ports and resources, but east-west logistics are fragile. Development should repair rail, roads, local food production, and processing before granting large continental armies.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Angolan host can establish a continental labour code, Atlantic rail and port corridor authority, or transborder cultural autonomy standard.

### High-chaos gate

Ocean, forest, or ancient-host material must remain fictional and high-chaos. It cannot attach supernatural traits to living communities.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Angola prioritises labour reform, one functioning corridor, and a regional coalition. It avoids simultaneous Congo, southern, and Atlantic wars. The overlay does not attempt to simulate every later liberation movement as a separate complete route.

## French Equatorial Africa and Congo-Brazzaville

### Starting tension

A Brazzaville-centred or federal French Equatorial African host begins with a thin administration over enormous distances, concession-company legacies, river transport, forced recruitment and labour memories, and territories whose economic and political interests diverge sharply. Its strength is geography and a river crossroads. Its weakness is a weak common mandate.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is a federation designed for administration rather than consent. It tracks Congo river access, Chad and Oubangui distance, Gabonese coastal and forest interests, concession power, local military recruitment, and Brazzaville’s ability to serve rather than dominate. It can become a federal republic, a confederation, a council of territorial and restored authorities, a labour and producer union, or a military river command.

### First proof mission

Connect the Congo river system to one inland territorial capital and complete a medical, food, or military aid mission chosen by that territory’s council. Partial success builds the route but confirms Brazzaville as the only beneficiary.

### Host focus modules

- **Four territories, one decision table.** Establishes separate confidence and representation.
- **The river carries both ways.** Reorients extraction routes toward regional supply and aid.
- **Concessions and public land.** Reviews company rights, labour, and forest access.
- **Chad is not the coast.** Requires a distinct Sahel policy and long-range logistics.
- **Brazzaville as a meeting place.** Makes the capital useful through radio, diplomacy, medical service, and training.

### Route mutations

Federalism and confederation are primary. The republic route requires territorial citizenship and decentralised administration. The crowns route uses regional historical packages without placing them under a single invented dynasty. The people’s union route builds from labour, transport, and anti-concession policy. Military continentalism depends on river and Sahel commands and risks a permanent officer federation.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Chad, Gabon, Oubangui, and Congo interests can form a territorial coalition against Brazzaville. The Belgian Congo and Cameroon can compete for river access. A host that ignores the Sahel can lose Chad to another regional bloc.

### Military and economy

Forces are small, mobile, and dependent on river, road, and foreign equipment. Development should be distributed by territorial mission outcomes. The host should gain strategic Reach, not free industrial parity with Nigeria or Egypt.

### Post-unification signature

A successful host can create a continental river navigation service, equatorial medical corps, or territorial equalisation rule.

### High-chaos gate

Living-river and forest content requires high chaos and clearly nonhuman entities.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Equatorial Africa favours federalism or confederation and seeks one strong external partner. The overlay groups the inherited territories into meaningful strategic actors without duplicating a full tree for each inside the host campaign.

# East Africa, the Horn, and the Indian Ocean

## Kenya

### Starting tension

Kenya begins as a settler colony with severe land inequality, African political exclusion, a large Asian commercial and professional community, strategic rail and port infrastructure, pastoral and agricultural regions, and colonial armed forces. A Kenyan continental project must settle land, citizenship, representation, and the ownership of the railway before it can claim to liberate others.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is a colony built around settler land and imperial transport. It tracks African land pressure, settler resistance, Asian representation, urban labour, pastoral mobility, railway control, and the loyalty of colonial forces. It can mature into a plural federal republic, a central mass republic, a council of regional and traditional authorities, a workers and farmers union, a military liberation command, or an East African confederation.

The land settlement must have several viable methods, including negotiated purchase, restitution, redistribution with compensation, communal trusts, or radical seizure. Each method changes foreign reaction, local confidence, food output, and settler flight or resistance.

### First proof mission

Keep the Mombasa-to-interior rail corridor open while implementing the first land or representation settlement in a named region. Full success then supplies Uganda, Tanganyika, Ethiopia, or a Somali partner. Partial success secures the railway but leaves the political grievance active.

### Host focus modules

- **The land commission has power.** Turns survey and legal work into real settlement decisions.
- **Three political publics.** Addresses African, Asian, and European representation without pretending each is internally uniform.
- **The railway is a public road.** Changes freight priorities, labour, and regional access.
- **Pastoral borders are not empty.** Creates mobility and security agreements in northern and eastern regions.
- **East Africa by consent.** Opens Uganda, Tanganyika, and Zanzibar cooperation only after domestic representation improves.

### Route mutations

Federalism offers the strongest regional and minority settlement. The republic route can build a mass franchise but faces settler and regional resistance. The crowns route may protect regional monarchies and councils but does not justify a Kenyan crown. The people’s union route links land, labour, railway, and plantation reform. Military continentalism uses experienced colonial forces and transport, but it risks settler or officer capture. Confederation is a strong East African outcome when neighbouring confidence is high.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Uganda fears Kenyan settler domination. Tanganyika can oppose Nairobi’s economic primacy. Somali and pastoral regions can reject border enforcement. Settler forces can create a rival administration or call foreign intervention. A plural settlement that fails one major constituency can produce sabotage rather than immediate civil war.

### Military and economy

Kenya has rail, Mombasa, settler agriculture, colonial forces, and regional access. It also has a highly political land economy and long vulnerable borders. The military path should tie mobile units and East African regiments to supply, land settlement, and officer reform.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Kenyan host can establish a continental land and mobility court, public railway charter, or multi-community citizenship guarantee.

### High-chaos gate

Highland, rift, animal, or nature content must remain fictional and cannot transform real pastoral or agricultural communities into fantasy actors.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Kenya prioritises land and citizenship before coercive East African leadership. It favours federalism, republic, people’s union, or confederation according to the domestic coalition. The overlay does not reproduce every land dispute as a separate focus.

## Uganda

### Starting tension

Uganda begins as a protectorate centred on Buganda but containing several kingdoms, chiefdoms, and regions with different relations to the colonial state. Cotton wealth, mailo land, the railway route through Kenya, lake transport, and the question of Buganda’s autonomy shape its continental opening.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is a protectorate with one privileged kingdom and several wary regions. It tracks Buganda confidence, Bunyoro grievances, other kingdom and district representation, cotton producers, land tenure, religious and political institutions, and dependence on Kenyan transport. It can become a negotiated federation, a republic, a council of kingdoms, a producer and labour union, a military lake state, or an East African confederation.

### First proof mission

Secure a Lake Victoria and rail supply chain while gaining formal support from Buganda and at least two non-Buganda regions. Full success sends aid to a neighbouring African partner. Partial success demonstrates capacity but raises fears of Buganda domination.

### Host focus modules

- **The kingdoms and the protectorate.** Creates a constituent conference with separate consent.
- **The lost counties and old grievances.** Opens arbitration, autonomy, or restoration solutions before wider integration.
- **Cotton money, public roads.** Connects producer revenue to local development and common transport.
- **A lake command, not a lake empire.** Builds transport and defence with negotiated port access.
- **The Kenya dependency.** Develops alternative routes through Tanganyika or wins binding access guarantees.

### Route mutations

Federalism and the crowns route are especially deep. Federalism must balance Buganda with other regions. The crowns route can create a real council of kingdoms with rotating precedence and a non-royal chamber. The republic route needs a settlement for hereditary institutions. The people’s union route challenges mailo land, producer marketing, and chiefly privilege. Military continentalism can use lake mobility but risks one-region command capture. Confederation supports a wider East African union while preserving domestic autonomy.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Bunyoro, non-kingdom districts, religious groups, and neighbouring countries can oppose Buganda dominance. Kenya can control access. Tanganyika can offer an alternative alliance. A coercive host may split into a royal bloc and a republican or regional coalition.

### Military and economy

Uganda’s strengths are population concentration, agriculture, lake transport, and regional military traditions. Its weaknesses are external port dependence and internal constitutional rivalry. Military growth should use lake guards, regional regiments, and corridor objectives rather than generic divisions.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Ugandan host can create a continental chamber of constituent states, lake navigation authority, or protected monarchy framework.

### High-chaos gate

Lake, royal-fire, or ancient-host material requires high chaos and must remain separate from living kingdom identities.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Uganda seeks a domestic constituent settlement and alternative port access before external expansion. It chooses the crowns route only when multiple kingdoms retain confidence. The overlay uses region variables and a small number of settlement events rather than separate full trees for each kingdom.

## Tanganyika

### Starting tension

Tanganyika begins as a former German colony under British mandate, with a large territory, modest settler presence compared with Kenya, indirect rule, important cash crops, lake and rail corridors, and a political identity less consolidated than its geography suggests. Its continental project can lead through balanced East African diplomacy and corridor development.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is a mandate without a national compact. It tracks local authority, cooperative and producer organisation, rail and port control, former German infrastructure, Asian and coastal commercial communities, settler interests, and the relationship between mainland and Zanzibar. It can mature into a federal republic, a broad republic, a council of regional authorities, a producer union, a military mandate state, or an East African confederation.

### First proof mission

Restore or secure one central railway corridor and one lake or port connection, then use both to complete an aid mission chosen with a neighbour. Full success proves Tanganyika can connect regions without dominating them. Partial success improves Reach but leaves the national compact unresolved.

### Host focus modules

- **A mandate returns its authority.** Converts external trusteeship into a constituent process.
- **Rail from the lakes to the ocean.** Makes transport the first strategic achievement.
- **Cooperatives and local councils.** Builds political capacity through producers and administration.
- **The mainland and Zanzibar.** Opens association, federation, confederation, or separate membership.
- **Between Kenya and the south.** Lets the host balance East African and southern corridors.

### Route mutations

Federalism is a strong route when local councils mature. The republic route builds a broad national movement and civil service. The crowns route is limited to protecting local authorities and any associated monarchic members. The people’s union route grows from cooperatives, railway labour, and anti-concession policy. Military continentalism is weaker at the opening and becomes credible only under invasion or regional collapse. Confederation can make Tanganyika the mediator of an East African bloc.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Zanzibar can refuse absorption. Kenya may compete over East African leadership. Uganda depends on access but resists domination. Southern neighbours can prefer other rail corridors. A host that ignores coastal and island interests can lose its port strategy.

### Military and economy

Tanganyika should invest in rail, ports, lake shipping, agriculture, and regional administration. Its army grows through East African troops, railway guards, local mobilisation, and foreign training. It should not receive early mass industrial rewards.

### Post-unification signature

A successful host can establish a continental mandate-to-sovereignty doctrine, cooperative development bank, or intermodal corridor authority.

### High-chaos gate

Lake, mountain, forest, or ancient-host content requires high chaos and clear fictional framing.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Tanganyika favours federalism, republic, or confederation and prioritises corridors. It avoids early military centralism without an external threat. The overlay does not split every administrative region into a separate political minigame.

## Somali territories

### Starting tension

The Somali host layer applies to British Somaliland, Italian Somaliland, French Somaliland, an event-created Somali state, or another valid Somali-capital actor. The region begins divided among colonial powers and Ethiopia, with pastoral mobility, ports, clan and religious institutions, dervish memory, and sharply different colonial administrations. The continental project must not reduce the region to a single irredentist war.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is one people dispersed across several borders and administrations, combined with real local political diversity. It tracks port access, pastoral routes, colonial armies, local councils, religious authority, relations with Ethiopia and Kenya, and the legitimacy of unification claims. It can mature into a federal Somali republic, a regional confederation, a council settlement, a socialist pastoral and port union, a military liberation state, or a wider Horn compact.

### First proof mission

Keep one port-to-interior pastoral and supply corridor open while negotiating safe movement or protection with a neighbouring state. Full success helps another African partner without launching an automatic border war. Partial success builds the corridor but raises irredentist pressure.

### Host focus modules

- **The five-point claim is debated.** Separates cultural unity, federation, autonomy, and war options.
- **Ports of different empires.** Gives Berbera, Mogadishu, Djibouti, and other ports distinct diplomatic and logistics roles.
- **Pastoral routes under guarantee.** Creates cross-border mobility and drought-response agreements.
- **The dervish inheritance.** Offers resistance prestige, religious-political memory, and a warning against permanent war government.
- **A Horn congress.** Requires Ethiopia, Somali actors, Djibouti, Eritrean interests, and Kenya to negotiate security and access.

### Route mutations

Federalism works through territorial autonomy and mobility guarantees. The republic route builds common citizenship and administration. The crowns route is not a default monarchy, but it can protect sultanates and traditional authorities within a council. The people’s union route focuses on ports, pastoral cooperatives, land access, and anti-colonial mobilisation. Military continentalism has a strong liberation logic but high permanent-war risk. Confederation can create a Horn compact without forcing every Somali-inhabited region into one tag.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Ethiopia and Kenya can form a territorial defence coalition. Djibouti and port cities can prefer separate membership. Strong local authorities can reject central command. A host that treats mobility agreements as annexation tools loses trust quickly.

### Military and economy

Forces are mobile, lightly equipped, and dependent on ports, wells, pack transport, and foreign arms. Development should prioritise water, veterinary services, roads, ports, and local markets. It should not assume dense industry.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Somali host can establish a continental pastoral mobility charter, drought-response service, or shared-port guarantee.

### High-chaos gate

Drought prophecy, ocean powers, or impossible animal formations require high chaos and must remain clearly nonhuman or supernatural.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Somali hosts prioritise one corridor, one diplomatic settlement, and one partner before wider claims. Ordinary AI should prefer agreements over simultaneous wars with Ethiopia, Kenya, and colonial powers. The overlay does not create a separate full route for every clan or colonial zone.

## Madagascar

### Starting tension

Madagascar begins as a French colony with the memory of the Merina monarchy, other regional identities, a distinctive Indian Ocean history, plantation and concession structures, weak integration between highlands and coasts, and strategic isolation. Its continental project must decide whether the island leads Africa, the Indian Ocean, or a bridge between them.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is an island colony with a contested centre. It tracks highland administration, coastal and regional confidence, monarchy memory, nationalist organisation, labour and concession interests, port access, and the relationship between African and Indian Ocean identity. It can become a federal Malagasy republic, a central republic, a restored constitutional monarchy, a workers and peasants union, a military island command, or an island confederation linked to Africa.

### First proof mission

Secure two island corridors between a major port and inland region, then deliver maritime aid to an African or Indian Ocean partner. Full success requires regional consent and convoy protection. Partial success confirms the host as an island power but not yet a continental leader.

### Host focus modules

- **The island is not only the highlands.** Establishes regional representation and rotating project sites.
- **The memory of the crown.** Allows restoration, ceremonial continuity, republican settlement, or rejection.
- **Ports into the western Indian Ocean.** Creates links with Comoros, Mauritius, Seychelles, Zanzibar, Mozambique, and East Africa.
- **Plantations, rice, and public land.** Defines labour, food, and concession reform.
- **A continental claim across water.** Requires repeated maritime service before deep African leadership unlocks.

### Route mutations

Federalism and confederation fit the island’s regional and maritime position. The republic route can build a strong civil service but must include coastal confidence. The crowns route can restore Merina authority only through a constitutional settlement with other regions. The people’s union route focuses on labour, land, food security, and anti-concession policy. Military continentalism depends on naval and air investment and risks an island fortress mentality.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Coastal and southern regions can resist highland domination. Comoros and other islands can reject Malagasy seniority. East African states may view the island as peripheral until it proves maritime reliability. France and other naval powers can isolate the host.

### Military and economy

Madagascar should build ports, convoys, coastal defence, airfields, roads, rice and plantation reform, and island shipping. Its army is limited by sea lift. The host’s strategic advantage is maritime reach, not free continental land access.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Malagasy host can create an Indian Ocean council, island convoy guarantee, biodiversity and agricultural research network, or rotating maritime capital.

### High-chaos gate

Living forest, giant fauna, ancestral, or stone content requires high chaos. It must remain clearly supernatural and never code Malagasy ethnic groups as fantasy species.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Madagascar prioritises domestic regional settlement, naval access, and island diplomacy. It seeks at least one mainland partner before claiming continental leadership. The overlay does not create separate full systems for every island community.

# Southern Africa
