# Event 12 Africa specification, part 9

## Priority restored and regional member country packages

Public country names in this file are working candidates. Historical borders, titles, institutions, symbols, and claim overlap require final specialist review. These packages deepen selected Tier A identities from part 4 and the polity catalog. They do not promote every catalog entry to the same scope.

## First deep implementation tranche

The first tranche should cover a geographically and politically diverse set of countries that can act as loyal members, autonomous federal states, rival-bloc leaders, or successor hosts. A selected country receives a compact but complete playable package. A country that appears only as a short transition or cultural autonomy does not need the full package until promoted.

A full priority package normally contains:

- a safe territorial and capital plan with reduced-map fallbacks
- a starting political problem and idea lifecycle
- six to twelve country focuses or a regional overlay with equivalent depth
- one distinctive decision or mechanic family
- dynamic starting forces and reinforcement
- at least three viable political outcomes
- a League relationship policy
- a refusal, withdrawal, or rival path
- post-settlement play
- route-aware AI and visible assets

## Asante

### Playable promise

Asante is a wealthy and politically prestigious inland-coastal power whose campaign is about restoring authority without simply recreating nineteenth-century conquest. It should balance the Asantehene and court, Kumasi institutions, stools and constituent authorities, cocoa and gold revenue, roads to the coast, relations with the Gold Coast government, and neighbouring Akan and non-Akan communities.

### Safe formation and territory

The preferred formation uses Kumasi and a compact historical-cultural core. Wider claims are negotiated through the regional congress. Coastal ports, northern territories, and neighbouring states are never automatic core territory. If Kumasi is unavailable, the restoration can remain a cultural council, autonomous member, or deferred claimant.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting idea represents a restored court with prestige but incomplete administration. It includes weak modern supply, competing constituent authorities, dependence on external ports, and a disputed relationship with the Gold Coast host.

It can evolve into:

- a constitutional kingdom with a representative council
- a federal state inside the Gold Coast or Africa
- an Asante republic retaining cultural institutions
- a producer and worker compact linked to cocoa and mining
- a military restoration centred on the Golden Stool and regional hosts

A court-only solution improves legitimacy but leaves revenue and representation unsolved. A purely republican abolition can create rural and traditional resistance.

### Focus and decision modules

- restore or redefine the council around the Asantehene
- settle constituent authority and succession
- turn cocoa and gold revenue into public roads and schools
- negotiate secure port and rail access
- create a regional arbitration role for overlapping Akan claims
- choose a royal guard, federal contingent, or national army
- decide whether the Golden Stool is a constitutional symbol, direct source of power, or high-chaos relic only after reveal

The distinct decision family manages **stool and council legitimacy**. Decisions appoint local representatives, settle disputes, fund public works, and respond to succession or court crises. It is not a passive prestige meter.

### Forces and economy

Starting forces use a small royal guard, local infantry, trained veterans where available, and mobilisation decisions tied to equipment and local support. Economic growth comes from producer boards, cocoa transport, mining agreements, local processing, and port access. It should not receive a free export boom while cut off from the coast.

### League relationship

Asante can become a protected cultural member, autonomous federal state, sovereign League member, or West African rival. It resists direct annexation and reacts strongly when the host backs Oyo, Edo, or another claimant in an unrelated dispute as a precedent for court interference.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Asante prioritises court settlement, port access, and revenue before expansion. It favours crowns, federalism, or a constitutional republic. The package does not create separate tags for every stool or constituent authority.

## Oyo

### Playable promise

Oyo is a restoration of political and commercial authority across a region shaped by Yoruba urban centres, old imperial memory, cavalry traditions, changing trade routes, and overlapping claims with Ife, Dahomey, Edo, and modern states. The campaign asks whether Oyo becomes a compact kingdom, a federation of Yoruba centres, a republic, or a military corridor power.

### Safe formation and territory

The core should be compact and centred on a viable capital and local support. Ife remains a distinct sacred and political centre. Coastal cities, Dahomey, Edo, and wide historical tributary areas become diplomatic or congress questions, not automatic ownership.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is prestige without a settled centre. It tracks the Alaafin or alternative executive, council authority, relations with Ife, urban autonomy, cavalry and motor transport, and access to ports.

The lifecycle can produce:

- a constitutional Kingdom of Oyo
- a Yoruba federal republic
- an urban council federation
- a people’s union of farms, markets, and transport workers
- a military restoration that risks conflict with every neighbour

### Focus and decision modules

- settle Alaafin, council, and urban authority
- negotiate a sacred-political compact with Ife
- convert cavalry corridors into modern road and motorised logistics
- secure port access through treaty, federation, or war
- arbitrate Yoruba city interests without forcing uniform administration
- choose relations with Dahomey and Edo through border congresses
- create regional market and agricultural infrastructure

The distinct decision family manages **the corridor and city compact**. The player secures roads, market access, local government support, and military transit. A corridor can be commercially open, federally administered, or occupied, with different Confidence and Burden effects.

### Forces and economy

The army begins with local infantry, mobile or cavalry-inspired units, and urban guards. Later focuses can create motorised regional brigades or federal contingents. Economic play centres on markets, roads, agriculture, local industry, and access to coastal trade.

### League relationship

Oyo can be a strong member or rival. It is likely to resist a Nigerian host that has not settled regional federalism. It can lead a Yoruba-centred bloc, but that bloc must negotiate with non-Yoruba neighbours rather than claim them by category.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Oyo seeks Ife settlement and one port route before wider claims. It avoids simultaneous conflict with Nigeria, Dahomey, and Edo unless military or high-chaos conditions justify it. City interests use variables and events rather than dozens of municipal tags.

## Sokoto

### Playable promise

Sokoto is a major Sahelian and northern Nigerian political restoration with emirate institutions, Islamic scholarship, pastoral and agricultural economies, caravan and rail routes, and a complicated relationship with British indirect rule and the wider Nigerian state. Its route is about constitutional religious authority, emirate autonomy, reform, and regional leadership.

### Safe formation and territory

The initial state should be compact and centred on a viable Sokoto and north-western base. Wider caliphate-era claims become a diplomatic network, emirate compact, or congress process. They do not create immediate ownership over all northern Nigeria or neighbouring states.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is restored authority entangled with colonial administration. It tracks the Sultan, emirates, scholars, courts, taxation, pastoral mobility, non-emirate communities, railway access, and relations with Nigeria.

The lifecycle can create:

- a constitutional caliphate with a representative consultative chamber
- an emirate federation inside Nigeria or Africa
- a northern republic with protected religious courts
- a reformist social and producer state
- a military frontier command

The route must address slavery’s historical legacy and modern rights directly. It must not romanticise coercive institutions or make religious authority exempt from Charter protections.

### Focus and decision modules

- renegotiate emirate authority and taxation
- establish a constitutional role for scholars and civic representatives
- protect pastoral routes and water access
- build rail and market links without southern fiscal domination
- decide the place of non-emirate and minority regions
- create a regional education and legal reform programme
- settle relations with Kanem-Bornu, Niger, and Nigerian regional governments

The distinct decision family manages **emirate compact and reform**. Decisions handle local taxation, court jurisdiction, education, pastoral access, and federal obligations. Repression can hold the compact together temporarily and increases resistance.

### Forces and economy

Forces use cavalry and mobile traditions, local infantry, frontier guards, and later motorisation. The economy depends on livestock, crops, markets, roads, rail, and drought resilience. It should gain military depth through mobility and local support rather than free heavy industry.

### League relationship

Sokoto can become a major federal state, sovereign member, council-of-crowns anchor, or Sahel rival. It resists a secular centre that abolishes all local institutions without negotiation, and it also resists a military host that uses emirates as recruitment districts.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Sokoto prioritises internal compact, pastoral routes, and Nigerian settlement. It favours crowns, federalism, or confederation, with republic or people’s union available through real reform coalitions. The package does not model each emirate as a separate playable country by default.

## Kanem-Bornu

### Playable promise

Kanem-Bornu is a Lake Chad and trans-Sahel power whose campaign centres on a divided historical region, caravan routes, lake transport, drought and food security, mobile warfare, scholarship, and overlapping claims across several modern borders.

### Safe formation and territory

The core uses a viable Lake Chad region and capital. Wider Kanem and Bornu claims are staged through agreements with Chad, Nigeria, Niger, and Cameroon. Sao, Kotoko, Wadai, and other overlapping identities can become protected members or rivals.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is a lake realm divided by colonial borders and weak supply. It tracks regional representation, water and pasture, caravan tolls, court or republican legitimacy, and relations among lake communities.

The lifecycle can produce:

- a restored sultanate under a constitutional council
- a Lake Chad federation
- a republican lake state
- a cooperative food and transport union
- a mobile military state

### Focus and decision modules

- create a four-border Lake Chad congress
- restore or replace dynastic authority
- build lake transport, wells, veterinary networks, and grain reserves
- protect caravan and pastoral routes
- create mobile regional forces and lake guards
- settle Sao, Kotoko, Wadai, and neighbouring claim overlap
- choose whether the lake is national property, shared trust, or confederal commons

The distinct mechanic is **lake level and corridor resilience** as a strategic readiness system, not a literal climate simulation. Drought, war, blocked routes, and neglected projects raise food and movement costs. Shared investment improves regional confidence.

### Forces and economy

Starting forces use cavalry or mobile infantry, lake patrols, and frontier guards. Growth depends on wells, depots, livestock, equipment, and control of routes. The economy is food, trade, livestock, local processing, and transport.

### League relationship

Kanem-Bornu can lead the Sahel council or form a rival Lake Chad compact. It strongly resists borders and projects imposed from distant coastal capitals without local representation.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Kanem-Bornu protects food and water, then seeks one cross-border compact before military expansion. The lake system uses strategic states and missions rather than detailed hydrology.

## Manden

### Playable promise

Manden is a restoration or federation inspired by Mali and broader Mande political and cultural histories. Its campaign is about river trade, gold and agriculture, local authority, Islamic and non-Islamic institutions, old capitals and routes, and the danger of claiming an empire larger than its modern legitimacy.

### Safe formation and territory

The core should be compact around a researched cultural and political centre. Wider Mali-era territories become a federation or diplomatic programme. Songhai, Segu, Massina, Futa, and coastal states retain separate claims and cannot all be absorbed through one restoration focus.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is an imperial name with a modern legitimacy gap. It tracks local councils, historic and religious authorities, river access, trade, rural support, and relations with neighbouring restoration candidates.

The lifecycle can create:

- a Manden federation
- a constitutional kingdom or council settlement where supported
- a republic using the historical name
- a cooperative river and producer union
- a military empire that creates high regional resistance

### Focus and decision modules

- survey capitals, routes, and surviving institutions
- hold a Manden constituent congress
- settle relations with Songhai and Sahel neighbours
- restore Niger River transport and market links
- build gold and agricultural revenue under public rules
- choose cultural federation, political statehood, or continental constituent status
- create griot, archive, school, and cultural institutions without turning them into fantasy mechanics

The distinct decision family manages **river congress and overlapping legacy**. It rewards negotiated cultural and economic networks even when territorial restoration remains limited.

### Forces and economy

Forces use river guards, mobile infantry, cavalry traditions where plausible, and local militias. Growth depends on river control, equipment, food, and regional legitimacy. Economic development focuses on river transport, agriculture, markets, gold, and local processing.

### League relationship

Manden can become a respected federal member, cultural centre, Sahel leader, or military rival. Its peaceful route gains authority through arbitration rather than claims.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Manden prefers federation, republic, or confederation and avoids maximal historical borders without high legitimacy. Cultural institutions stay meaningful through decisions and diplomacy, not dozens of flavour-only focuses.

## Kongo

### Playable promise

Kongo is a transborder restoration with court and Christian history, Atlantic trade memory, modern urban and rural politics, resource corridors, and territory divided among colonial states. Its campaign is about whether a historical identity becomes a compact kingdom, federal cross-border state, republic, cultural commonwealth, or military project.

### Safe formation and territory

The core should centre on a researched Kongo heartland and viable capital. Wider claims across Belgian Congo, Angola, and French Congo require regional congresses, plebiscites, autonomy agreements, or cultural association. Direct transfer of all claimed states is forbidden.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is a court and people divided by colonial frontiers. It tracks claimant legitimacy, church and civic institutions, local authorities, urban workers, river and port access, and the reaction of three surrounding colonial systems.

The lifecycle can create:

- a constitutional Kingdom of Kongo
- a Kongo federation across agreed regions
- a Kongo republic
- a worker and community union
- a military restoration that risks foreign intervention

### Focus and decision modules

- verify the claimant, court, or institutional leadership
- call a cross-border Kongo congress
- negotiate with Angola, Congo, and Belgian Congo authorities
- secure river and Atlantic access
- settle church, local council, and state relations
- create common education and legal institutions
- choose protected cultural commonwealth or territorial federation

The distinct decision family manages **cross-border community consent**. Cultural and citizenship links can grow without state transfer. Territorial integration requires local support and a settled corridor.

### Forces and economy

Starting forces use local guards, defectors, river troops, and later regional infantry. The economy uses river trade, ports, agriculture, mining links, and local processing. A landlocked or encircled Kongo must negotiate access before large mobilisation.

### League relationship

Kongo can be a major Congo-basin member, federal state, or rival. It reacts strongly when a Congo or Angola host treats restoration as a tool for annexation. It can also mediate across colonial borders.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Kongo prioritises claimant legitimacy, local consent, and access. It avoids fighting all three neighbouring colonial systems without a powerful League. The package uses one cross-border consent system rather than separate mechanics for every colonial section.

## Buganda

### Playable promise

Buganda is a powerful kingdom and administrative centre with a kabaka, Lukiiko, clan institutions, mailo land, cotton wealth, religious-political traditions, lake transport, and a fraught relationship with the wider Uganda protectorate. Its campaign is about autonomy, federal leadership, and whether Buganda can cooperate without dominating Uganda.

### Safe formation and territory

Buganda begins with a compact kingdom. Wider Ugandan authority requires a negotiated federal or confederal settlement. Bunyoro’s lost counties and other regional claims are explicit congress matters.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is a privileged kingdom inside a larger protectorate. It tracks kabaka authority, Lukiiko representation, clan and religious politics, mailo land, cotton revenue, Bunyoro grievances, and non-Buganda confidence.

The lifecycle can produce:

- a constitutional Buganda kingdom inside a Ugandan federation
- a sovereign League member
- a federal Uganda led through negotiated institutions
- a Buganda republic retaining cultural bodies
- a people’s union that reforms land and chiefly privilege
- a military lake power that creates strong regional fear

### Focus and decision modules

- define kabaka and Lukiiko powers
- settle mailo land and producer revenue
- arbitrate the lost counties
- create equal representation with other Ugandan regions
- build lake transport and guards
- choose sovereign membership, Ugandan federation, or East African confederation
- manage religious and civic institutions without reducing politics to sectarian labels

The distinct decision family manages **kingdom confidence and federal balance**. Benefits gained by Buganda can improve Uganda only when other regions receive transparent shares.

### Forces and economy

Forces use royal guards, lake units, regional infantry, and later federal contingents. Economic play centres on cotton, land, lake transport, roads, and education.

### League relationship

Buganda is a natural crowns-route member and a difficult federal member. It can lead a royal bloc, but other states can unite against its dominance. Peaceful arbitration greatly increases its continental legitimacy.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Buganda prioritises constitutional status, land, and Bunyoro settlement. It seeks East African leadership only after gaining non-Buganda confidence. The package does not create each clan as a separate political actor in script.

## Aksum

### Playable promise

Aksum is an Ethiopian and Red Sea restoration route built around a historical imperial centre, highland administration, church institutions, trade access, and the tension between a compact historic identity and the modern Ethiopian state. It should feel like a constitutional and cultural refounding project, not a license to erase Ethiopia or claim every territory once linked to ancient trade.

### Safe formation and territory

The compact formation uses an Ethiopian or Eritrean highland core selected by the restoration congress. Wider Red Sea claims begin as cultural, commercial, or archaeological interests. Eritrean ports, Tigrayan districts, Afar routes, and neighbouring highlands require separate consent and access settlements.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is **ancient legitimacy inside a modern political order**. It tracks the standing of the imperial government, regional authorities, church and civic institutions, Eritrean confidence, Red Sea access, and whether the restoration is cultural, federal, dynastic, republican, or coercive.

The lifecycle can produce:

- a constitutional Kingdom of Aksum within Ethiopia
- a federal highland state with negotiated Eritrean participation
- an Aksum republic centred on heritage, trade, and education
- a church-civic commonwealth with protected religious pluralism
- a military restoration that damages Ethiopian and Eritrean confidence
- a high-chaos ancient host route only after the supernatural reveal

### Focus and decision modules

- convene a highland restoration congress
- define the relationship with Ethiopia and Eritrea
- protect church, mosque, local council, and civic representation
- recover Red Sea trade through negotiated port access
- establish archaeology, archives, and heritage law
- modernise roads and highland logistics
- choose dynastic continuity, constitutional autonomy, or republican refounding

The distinct decision family manages **heritage claim versus living consent**. Monument restoration and cultural recognition can advance without territorial transfer. Political integration requires regional confidence and a lawful settlement.

### Forces and economy

Forces use highland infantry, territorial guards, mule logistics, engineers, and later Red Sea support obtained through compact agreements. The economy combines agriculture, roads, education, small industry, heritage institutions, and port access.

### League relationship

Aksum can become a respected crowns-route or federal member. It is a poor early direct-integration target because Ethiopian and Eritrean politics can make the restoration explosive. A successful autonomy settlement gives the League a model for restoring a historic identity without dissolving a modern state.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Aksum prioritises a compact settlement, Ethiopian recognition, and Red Sea access. It avoids expansive claims unless a hostile Ethiopian collapse or high-chaos route removes normal restraints. The package shares the Ethiopia and Eritrea regional systems rather than creating a second complete Horn mechanic.

## Harar

### Playable promise

Harar is a walled-city and trade-corridor state whose strength comes from urban institutions, Islamic scholarship, caravan commerce, Oromo and Somali hinterland relationships, and routes linking the Ethiopian interior to the Gulf of Aden. Its campaign is about remaining a city-centred polity while building enough corridor influence to survive.

### Safe formation and territory

Harar begins as a compact city and surrounding district. The Somali plateau, Ogaden routes, Dire Dawa corridor, and Gulf access are negotiated economic and security zones. They do not become automatic territorial claims.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is **a city without secure access**. It tracks urban legitimacy, merchant confidence, scholarly institutions, hinterland agreements, Ethiopian relations, Somali relations, and railway or caravan access.

The lifecycle can produce:

- a protected city-state
- an autonomous emirate within a Horn federation
- a mercantile republic
- a scholarly commonwealth
- a corridor federation with negotiated Somali and Oromo participation
- a militarised emirate that can provoke Ethiopia and Somali actors at once

### Focus and decision modules

- restore municipal and court institutions
- negotiate caravan and railway rights
- protect scholarly and religious endowments
- establish hinterland councils
- secure water, food, and market access
- choose city-state neutrality, Horn association, or corridor federation
- prevent commercial privilege from becoming political exclusion

The distinct decision family manages **corridor guarantees**. Harar gains revenue and security from access agreements, but each external patron increases dependency and can demand political influence.

### Forces and economy

Forces are compact and defensive. They use city guards, mounted patrols, railway or caravan security units, and League volunteers. Economic growth relies on markets, transit fees, agriculture, crafts, education, and transport.

### League relationship

Harar can be an observer, protected member, or specialist mediator. It is valuable to the League because it can connect Ethiopian, Somali, and Red Sea systems. Coercive integration usually creates a corridor crisis.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Harar seeks guarantees and diversified access before territorial ambition. It avoids a two-front war unless the League provides overwhelming protection. The package uses one corridor ledger instead of separate mechanics for every trade route.

## Kilwa

### Playable promise

Kilwa is a Swahili coast restoration built around port cities, island-mainland commerce, Muslim institutions, maritime law, inland trade, and competition among coastal centres. Its central question is whether a restored sultanate, republic, or city league can unite commerce without turning the coast into one city’s empire.

### Safe formation and territory

The initial state is Kilwa and its immediate coast where a viable release is possible. Zanzibar, Mombasa, Lamu, Pemba, Mozambique Island, and other Swahili centres join through a maritime congress, association, or treaty network. Inland corridors remain negotiated commercial routes.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is **a famous port inside a plural coast**. It tracks harbour capacity, merchant confidence, religious and civic institutions, island participation, inland corridor access, and rivalry among cities.

The lifecycle can produce:

- a Sultanate of Kilwa
- a Kilwa republic
- a Swahili city league led by rotating councils
- a coastal federation with mainland representation
- a people’s maritime union of dockworkers, producers, and towns
- a naval command state that can lose city confidence

### Focus and decision modules

- reopen coastal and island councils
- settle customs and port dues
- protect merchant, labour, and community representation
- create a common maritime court
- negotiate inland rail and caravan access
- choose one capital, rotating institutions, or a distributed city league
- build a coast guard without turning it into a conquest fleet

The distinct decision family manages **port rivalry and common customs**. Each port can accept shared tariffs, naval patrols, or common courts separately. A city that loses confidence can withdraw or sponsor a rival league.

### Forces and economy

Forces use coastal guards, marines, dhow patrols, port engineers, and later a small blue-water force. The economy depends on ports, fishing, trade, ship repair, plantations under reform, inland access, and customs revenue.

### League relationship

Kilwa can lead an Islands and Indian Ocean association even when it is not the continental host. It is a valuable naval partner and a likely opponent of a host that centralises every port under one customs office.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Kilwa prioritises port agreements, customs stability, and naval access. It chooses territorial war only after maritime isolation or a hostile blockade. The package uses one city-league system for the wider Swahili coast rather than separate full mechanics for every port.

## Nubia

### Playable promise

Nubia is a Nile-border restoration that can draw from Kush, Makuria, Alodia, Christian, Islamic, riverine, and local traditions without claiming a single uninterrupted political identity. Its campaign is about water, archaeology, local communities, border government, and the relationship between Egypt and Sudan.

### Safe formation and territory

The compact formation covers a negotiated Nile region in northern Sudan, southern Egypt, or both when local and state conditions allow it. Wider Kushite, Makurian, or Alodian claims become heritage and diplomatic projects. They do not grant automatic cores across the Nile valley.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is **a borderland claimed by two capitals and several memories**. It tracks village and town consent, Egyptian and Sudanese recognition, Nile access, displacement risk, heritage institutions, and whether the country defines itself as a kingdom, republic, federation, or river commonwealth.

The lifecycle can produce:

- a Kingdom of Nubia
- a Nubian republic
- a Nile federation with Egyptian and Sudanese guarantees
- a Makurian or Alodian cultural restoration with modern civic government
- a workers’ river union
- a militarised border state vulnerable to foreign manipulation

### Focus and decision modules

- survey communities and heritage sites
- negotiate dual recognition with Egypt and Sudan
- protect water and land rights
- choose capital and administrative language policy through congress
- build river transport, irrigation, and local industry
- settle archaeological custody and museum policy
- create border guards answerable to civilian or royal institutions

The distinct decision family manages **river rights and dual recognition**. Projects succeed fastest when both upstream and downstream authorities accept them. Unilateral dam, land, or security policy raises cross-border pressure.

### Forces and economy

Forces use river guards, desert patrols, engineers, and light infantry. The economy relies on irrigation, transport, agriculture, local mining, tourism and heritage where appropriate, and negotiated links to Egyptian and Sudanese markets.

### League relationship

Nubia can be a bridge member and arbitration centre. It can also become the nucleus of a Nile rival bloc if Egypt or Sudan dominates the League. Its best route rewards shared institutions rather than territorial maximalism.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Nubia seeks recognition and water guarantees before expansion. It avoids war with both Egypt and Sudan unless one collapses and the other accepts a settlement. The package uses the League’s existing basin and corridor systems rather than a new Nile simulation.

## Luba and Lunda

### Playable promise

Luba and Lunda are linked but distinct Central African restoration packages. Each can exist independently, cooperate through a shared congress, or compete for mining districts and political influence. The design uses court, lineage, trade, regional authority, and modern civic institutions without reducing either polity to a single royal claimant.

### Safe formation and territory

Each begins with a compact regional base chosen from viable Congolese or neighbouring states. Kazembe can appear as a related but separate council or associated country. Overlapping claims are resolved through congress and local preference. The system never activates every historic maximum border at once.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The shared starting problem is **two political traditions inside a mining and transport frontier**. Luba tracks court legitimacy, regional councils, mining labour, rail access, and urban centres. Lunda tracks claimant legitimacy, cross-border communities, trade routes, local authorities, and relations with Angola and Zambia.

Their lifecycles can produce:

- separate kingdoms
- separate republics
- a negotiated Luba-Lunda federation
- a looser council of kingdoms and provinces
- worker and producer unions connected to mining centres
- rival military states competing for the Copperbelt and Katanga

### Focus and decision modules

- verify claimants or choose civic governments
- establish local consent commissions
- settle mining revenue and labour protection
- negotiate rail and river access
- determine the status of Kazembe and overlapping districts
- create joint courts or a rotating congress
- choose federation, association, peaceful separation, or rivalry

The distinct decision family manages **overlap settlement**. A district can recognise cultural ties to both countries while belonging politically to one, remaining autonomous, or entering a shared federal unit.

### Forces and economy

Forces use local guards, mining security units under reform, river troops, and regional infantry. Economic play centres on copper and other minerals, railways, local processing, labour conditions, and preventing concession control from replacing sovereignty.

### League relationship

Both countries can become major Central or Great Lakes members. Their cooperation improves regional trust. A host that assigns mining rights without representation can push one or both into a rival bloc.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Luba prioritises internal administration and mining settlement. AI Lunda prioritises cross-border access and claimant legitimacy. Both prefer negotiated separation or federation when confidence is high. The package uses one overlap framework and shared regional projects rather than two isolated large mechanics.

Great Zimbabwe is a southern African restoration centred on heritage, Shona political traditions, trade routes, land, cattle, mining, and the modern societies surrounding the monument. The compact formation uses a viable Shona-majority or monument-centred region. Mutapa and Rozwi can appear as alternate political traditions, associated countries, or constitutional routes. Southern Rhodesian, Mozambican, and neighbouring claims require separate settlements.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is **a powerful symbol contested by colonial rule and rival restorations**. It tracks land access, chiefly and civic representation, urban and rural confidence, heritage custody, mining and farming interests, and relations among Great Zimbabwe, Mutapa, and Rozwi projects.

The lifecycle can produce:

- a Kingdom of Great Zimbabwe
- a Great Zimbabwe republic
- a Shona federation
- a Mutapa or Rozwi constitutional restoration
- a people’s agrarian and mining union

### Focus and decision modules

- establish heritage and community custody
- settle land and cattle rights
- choose Great Zimbabwe, Mutapa, Rozwi, or federal constitutional language
- build regional roads and rail links
- reform mining concessions and local processing
- negotiate with Ndebele, Mozambican, and neighbouring communities

### League relationship

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Great Zimbabwe prioritises land, heritage custody, and a viable transport corridor. It avoids simultaneous conflict with South Africa, Portugal, and settler authorities without strong League backing. Mutapa and Rozwi are handled as constitutional and country-package choices, not three mandatory overlapping full states.

## Merina

### Playable promise

Merina is a Madagascar restoration whose political centre lies in the highlands but whose legitimacy depends on coastal communities, regional identities, land, labour, trade, and the memory of both the precolonial kingdom and colonial conquest. Its campaign asks whether a restored crown can become island-wide, constitutional, and representative.

### Safe formation and territory

Merina begins in the central highlands when released separately. Island-wide Madagascar requires a negotiated settlement with coastal and regional councils. The country can remain one kingdom inside a broader Malagasy federation or transform into an island government.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is **a highland crown with an island-wide legitimacy deficit**. It tracks royal legitimacy, coastal confidence, land and labour reform, religious and civic institutions, port access, and the relationship between Merina identity and Malagasy citizenship.

The lifecycle can produce:

- a constitutional Kingdom of Merina
- a federal Kingdom of Madagascar with a Merina crown
- a Merina republic
- a Malagasy republic with protected royal and regional institutions
- a people’s island union
- an isolationist royal state that can lose the coast

### Focus and decision modules

- define crown and assembly powers
- create coastal and highland representation
- reform land, labour, and tax obligations
- rebuild roads between highland and ports
- protect regional languages and institutions
- choose Merina sovereignty, Malagasy federation, or Indian Ocean association
- settle the capital and customs question

The distinct decision family manages **island confidence**. Highland policies can be accepted, amended, or rejected by regional councils. Coercive island integration raises port strikes, withdrawal pressure, and foreign intervention risk.

### Forces and economy

Forces use highland infantry, coastal guards, gendarmes under reform, and later island logistics and marines. The economy combines agriculture, ports, roads, local industry, mining where appropriate, and Indian Ocean trade.

### League relationship

Merina can be a crowns-route member, a Malagasy federal component, or the leader of an Islands Council. It can resist a mainland host that treats island interests as peripheral. A successful federal settlement makes it a useful model for asymmetric autonomy.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Merina prioritises island confidence and port access before external ambition. It avoids forced island annexation when coastal opposition is strong. The package shares Madagascar’s island systems rather than duplicating them under every regional identity.

## Zulu

### Playable promise

Zulu is a southern African restoration built around monarchy, military memory, land, rural authority, labour migration, and the place of a compact kingdom inside or beside South Africa. It should provide a disciplined military and constitutional challenge without turning nineteenth-century imagery into a timeless stereotype.

### Safe formation and territory

The compact country uses a viable Zululand core. Wider Natal, Swazi, Ndebele, or South African claims begin as cultural, land, or security disputes. They require regional settlement and do not become automatic cores.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is **a royal institution inside a racial and territorial order it did not design**. It tracks royal legitimacy, rural and urban representation, land rights, labour migration, military organisation, relations with South African factions, and confidence among neighbouring communities.

The lifecycle can produce:

- a constitutional Kingdom of Zulu
- a Zulu republic
- a federal state inside a democratic South Africa
- a Southern Council kingdom with protected autonomy
- a people’s land and labour union
- a royal military state that can become a regional rival

### Focus and decision modules

- settle crown, assembly, and local council powers
- restore or redistribute land through lawful processes
- protect migrant workers and urban families
- professionalise royal and territorial forces
- negotiate Natal ports and transport
- choose South African federation, League membership, or sovereign kingdom
- arbitrate Ndebele, Swazi, and neighbouring claims

The distinct decision family manages **crown, land, and labour balance**. Royal authority can mobilise quickly, but durable strength requires urban and rural consent. Land reform that ignores livelihoods or neighbouring claims raises resistance.

### Forces and economy

Forces use territorial infantry, scouts, mounted units, engineers, and later regular formations. Ceremonial military traditions provide identity, while actual battlefield development follows period logistics, firearms, vehicles, and supply. The economy depends on land, livestock, labour, rail and port access, and regional industry.

### League relationship

Zulu can be a loyal crowns-route member, a federal South African state, or a rival southern bloc leader. It opposes a settler-dominated host and can support the continental side in the South African civil war when constitutional guarantees are credible.

### AI and anti-bloat boundary

AI Zulu prioritises land guarantees, constitutional status, and protection from South African coercion. It pursues regional military leadership only when the League is weak or hostile. The package does not create fantasy spear armies as the normal military system. Strange animal or mythic formations belong only to revealed high-chaos content.

## Priority package selection and promotion

These packages are priorities because they create different campaign problems:

- Asante, Oyo, Sokoto, Kanem-Bornu, and Manden create a varied West African political field.
- Kongo, Luba, and Lunda create cross-border and resource-settlement problems in Central Africa.
- Buganda, Aksum, Harar, Kilwa, and Nubia create kingdom, city-state, corridor, and river systems in the east and northeast.
- Great Zimbabwe, Merina, and Zulu create distinct southern and island settlements.

A priority country receives its full package only when it is selected as a major restoration, released as a serious actor, or promoted by a regional congress. Minor cultural recognition, an autonomous province, or a short transition subject uses a compact package instead.

Promotion requires at least three of the following:

- a viable compact territorial base
- local support or a credible claimant or civic institution
- an economic or strategic function beyond symbolic restoration
- a distinct relationship problem with the League
- enough surviving population and infrastructure for meaningful play
- a focus or decision identity not already supplied by a neighbouring priority country

A country should be demoted to an autonomous region, cultural body, or associated council when a full tag would create an enclave without access, duplicate a neighbouring package, or force an unsafe territorial transfer.

## Shared implementation boundaries for priority countries

Every priority package should receive:

- a direct public country name and ideology variants where useful
- a compact starting problem with a staged idea lifecycle
- three to six country-specific focus groups or overlay modules
- one distinct decision family connected to the Charter system
- a starting force concept and reinforcement route
- at least two meaningful League relationships
- refusal, withdrawal, and rival-bloc behavior
- route-aware AI
- a post-settlement activity loop
- flags, a leader or council portrait, and a small icon family

No priority package should receive:

- automatic maximum historic borders
- instant full cores over contested territory
- an isolated 60-focus tree that duplicates the continental tree
- a new resource or influence meter when an existing Charter value can express the same pressure
- unique mechanics that disappear after annexation without contributing to regional or continental play

## Acceptance criteria for the priority package layer

The layer is ready for implementation only when:

- each priority country has a distinct starting dilemma and first strategic objective
- every territory choice distinguishes compact territory, negotiated expansion, and heritage interest
- historic overlap is resolved through local consent, autonomy, association, or congress
- the country remains playable after successful settlement
- military forces have a plausible source and a reinforcement pathway
- AI can refuse unsafe restoration and can choose compact survival over maximal claims
- country-specific content changes shared Charter play instead of duplicating it
- the total tag count is controlled by activation and promotion rules
