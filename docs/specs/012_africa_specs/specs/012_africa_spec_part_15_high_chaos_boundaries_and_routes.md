# 012 Africa spec part 15, high-chaos route boundaries and mechanics

This file expands high-chaos routes with nonhuman and supernatural boundaries, abstract disaster pressure, fictional disease mechanics, blowback, and AI restrictions. It keeps baseline Africa grounded and researched.

## Core boundaries

High-chaos content must follow these rules:
- ordinary baseline Africa remains grounded
- nonhuman actors are explicitly animals, supernatural bodies, living monuments, impossible councils, or abstract disaster actors
- nonhuman actors are not human African ethnic caricatures
- no route should make a real ethnic group into animals or monsters
- fictional disease systems must stay abstract and non-operational
- no real-world pathogen engineering, culturing, or dissemination detail belongs in the spec
- disasters are game pressure and event consequences, not real instructions
- AI should rarely choose high-chaos routes unless the campaign is already extreme
- high-chaos routes should create blowback for the unifier if abused

## High-chaos reveal gates

A high-chaos route can reveal through several paths:

| Gate | Conditions | Route content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sacred Soil anomaly | Sacred Soil route, repeated stewardship missions, high chaos | Forest signs, heritage awakenings, oracle events |
| Deep Green doctrine | Player chooses high-chaos doctrine after signs | Full nonhuman and nature pact branch |
| Disaster spiral | Region has repeated disaster pressure and low League cohesion | Rain Prophet State, weather bargains |
| Heritage rupture | Coercive extraction or desecration in heritage region | Living statues or stone host |
| Forest backlash | Congo basin exploitation, failed forest mediation, high chaos | Virunga Gorillas, Congo Chimpanzees, forest wrath |
| Fictional outbreak spiral | Abstract disease pressure, high chaos, unsafe biowarfare route | Fever Without Name |
| World-collapse threshold | Chaos near terminal levels | Rare convergence of nonhuman and supernatural actors |

Reveal should feel earned. Do not reveal every high-chaos actor in a normal campaign.

## Deep Green Covenant mechanics

The Deep Green Covenant route uses four main values:

| Value | Scope | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Covenant pressure | Unifier global and region-specific | How strongly impossible nature demands action |
| Appeasement | Region-specific | How much the unifier has satisfied nonhuman or supernatural demands |
| Wrath debt | Region-specific | How much punishment is building from broken promises |
| Human settlement strain | Region-specific | How much ordinary administration conflicts with the covenant |

Actions:
- protect habitat
- restrict extraction
- recognize nonhuman actor
- restore old sites
- negotiate passage
- offer grain or water missions
- avert disaster
- unleash disaster pressure against enemies
- contain blowback
- choose whether human members or nonhuman actors receive priority

Tradeoffs:
- protecting habitat may reduce resource extraction
- unleashing wrath raises wrath debt and League fear
- recognizing nonhuman actors lowers colonial extraction but shocks human members
- binding living hosts gives military power but damages legitimacy
- too much covenant pressure can block normal integration

## Abstract disaster pressure

Disaster pressure is a game value. It can represent drought, flood, storm, locust, river change, or impossible weather, without detailed real-world mechanisms.

Disaster pressure should be:
- regional
- visible through decisions or UI
- affected by high-chaos route choices
- reduced by stewardship, relief, rail, and water projects
- increased by coercive extraction, broken bargains, route abuse, and high chaos
- able to hit enemies or the unifier through blowback

Disaster pressure outcomes:
- state modifier for supply strain
- temporary production disruption
- infrastructure damage
- refugee pressure
- local support loss
- member confidence loss
- high-chaos actor spawn chance
- natural disaster event hook where the existing event supports it

The spec should not say in player-facing text that a specific other event will fire. It should say strange disasters may follow, through direction only.

## Fictional disease mechanics

The disease route uses fictional disease pressure. It should never provide real biological details.

Allowed design:
- fictional names such as Fever Without Name as working labels
- abstract pressure values
- containment missions
- hospital and quarantine decisions as broad game abstractions
- state modifiers
- deaths system integration if implemented safely
- air cleanliness or contamination pressure through existing systems
- blowback against the user
- condemnation and member confidence loss when weaponized

Forbidden design:
- real pathogen instructions
- real culturing methods
- dose, delivery, or dispersal details
- operational real-world biowarfare guidance
- final text that jokes about real disease suffering
- using disease as an ethnic marker or caricature

Disease values:
- outbreak pressure
- containment capacity
- weaponization temptation
- blowback risk
- foreign condemnation
- League fear
- hospital capacity

Decisions:
- build emergency clinics
- isolate affected routes
- send medical volunteers
- investigate fictional outbreak
- suppress panic through lawful or coercive means
- weaponize abstract pressure only for high-chaos route with severe costs and blowback
- destroy unsafe stockpiles through a costly mission
- ask foreign or League medical aid

Failure states:
- member exits League
- foreign quarantine
- port closure
- state population loss through deaths system
- high-chaos actor becomes independent
- unifier suffers same pressure it inflicted

## Nonhuman actor packages

### Virunga Gorillas

Role:
- defensive forest actor
- can become protected League anomaly, hostile forest power, or Deep Green ally

Mechanics:
- habitat pressure
- forest passage permission
- ranger protection mission
- anti-extraction demands
- ambush abstraction in forest states
- member shock if recognized without preparation

Military:
- limited forest defense units
- no normal industrial tree
- powerful in forest terrain
- weak outside habitat
- cannot be used as ordinary manpower farm

Blowback:
- overuse raises human settlement strain
- broken promises cause sabotage and regional panic
- enemies can provoke them if unifier ignores habitat

### Congo Chimpanzees

Role:
- chaotic sabotage and intelligence anomaly
- should feel unpredictable and nonhuman

Mechanics:
- forest sabotage
- captured depot disruption
- strange route signs
- hidden paths through rainforest
- communication confusion

Military:
- irregular ambush abstractions
- short-range disruption decisions
- no standard conquest path

Blowback:
- sabotage can hit the unifier
- League members lose confidence if civilians suffer
- containment requires nonviolent forest missions or harsh military route

### Living Statues of Kush

Role:
- supernatural ancient host tied to Nile heritage

Mechanics:
- relic awakening
- heritage respect
- stone host obedience
- river defense
- containment seal

Military:
- elite living-stone units
- very slow
- excellent defense
- vulnerable to containment failure
- limited quantity tied to heritage sites

Blowback:
- heritage disrespect can awaken hostile units
- binding the host lowers legitimacy among human members
- overuse damages Nile integration

### Stone Host of Great Zimbabwe

Role:
- defensive supernatural host tied to plateau heritage

Mechanics:
- stone oath
- site protection
- plateau defense
- resource extraction warning

Military:
- fort and mountain defense
- attrition pressure on invaders
- limited offensive use

Blowback:
- mining abuse raises hostility
- failed containment damages infrastructure
- route can block normal industrial extraction if ignored

### Red Sea Oracle

Role:
- supernatural coastal or maritime power

Mechanics:
- port omen
- convoy blessing or disruption
- storm pressure
- Red Sea access bargains

Military:
- not normal army
- naval and port pressure effects
- convoy risk manipulation

Blowback:
- storms can hit unifier convoys
- port panic can disrupt diaspora lanes
- member confidence loss if used for coercion

### Rain Prophet State

Role:
- disaster-predicting or disaster-triggering actor

Mechanics:
- rain debt
- drought pressure
- flood pressure
- relief demands
- warning missions

Military:
- weak normal units
- strong disaster pressure actions
- local defensive bonuses under active storm or flood state

Blowback:
- ignored demands hit unifier regions
- enemies can suffer, but so can members
- repeated use accelerates world chaos

### Fever Without Name

Role:
- fictional disease pressure and high-chaos outbreak actor

Mechanics:
- outbreak pressure
- containment capacity
- port quarantine
- hospital network
- member fear
- foreign quarantine

Military:
- no normal army by default
- route can create contaminated zones as abstract state effects

Blowback:
- cannot be cleanly controlled
- affects unifier ports and members
- can close diaspora lanes
- can trigger world-threat integration if severe

## High-chaos route decisions

| Decision family | User | Effect direction | Cost and risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recognize nonhuman actor | Deep Green or Sacred Soil reveal | Turns hostile pressure into negotiated anomaly | Member shock, legitimacy cost |
| Protect habitat | Any high-chaos forest route | Lowers wrath debt and forest hostility | Resource extraction reduction |
| Demand forest passage | Unifier at war | Allows movement or local attack bonuses | Wrath debt if forced |
| Bind living host | High-chaos heritage route | Gives supernatural defensive units | Heritage backlash, obedience risk |
| Bargain for rain | Rain Prophet route | Reduces drought or flood pressure | Future debt and possible blowback |
| Unleash weather wrath | Deep Green route at war | Applies disaster pressure to enemy | Severe blowback, condemnation, League fear |
| Contain fictional outbreak | Any route with disease pressure | Lowers outbreak and fear | Equipment, industry, time |
| Weaponize abstract outbreak | Severe high-chaos disease route only | Hurts enemy states through abstract mechanics | Massive blowback, condemnation, member exits |
| Seal the covenant | Deep Green capstone | Makes nonhuman pact permanent | Locks some normal routes and changes integration |

## AI restrictions

AI use should be controlled by route and campaign state.

AI may investigate signs when:
- chaos is high
- it is on Sacred Soil
- it has disaster pressure
- it controls relevant region

AI may recognize nonhuman actors when:
- high-chaos AI routes are enabled
- it has low colonial dependence
- member confidence is not already collapsing
- it is not in a fragile civil war

AI may unleash disaster pressure only when:
- chaos is very high
- it is at war
- it has wrath debt below a safe cap or accepts blowback
- it is not about to lose all member confidence
- rare AI high-chaos behavior is active

AI should almost never weaponize abstract outbreak pressure. It can do so only under terminal chaos, explicit route choice, and world-end preparation.

## Blowback rules

High-chaos powers must have blowback.

| Abuse | Blowback |
| --- | --- |
| Repeated weather wrath | Drought or flood pressure in member regions |
| Broken habitat bargain | Forest sabotage, member panic |
| Overbound living statues | Obedience failure and heritage crisis |
| Disease weaponization | Outbreak in unifier ports, foreign quarantine, condemnation |
| Ignored nonhuman actor | Actor becomes hostile or joins rival |
| Forced integration under Deep Green | Human settlement strain and federal collapse |
| Extraction after covenant | Forest or stone host attack |
| Blocking relief | League confidence collapse |

Blowback should be harsh enough that high-chaos power feels dangerous. The route can be absurd and overpowered, but it should not be clean.

## Interaction with baseline routes

| Baseline route | High-chaos interaction |
| --- | --- |
| Federal Charter | Struggles to legally recognize nonhuman actors, but can build protected anomaly status |
| Revolutionary Congress | Can frame nonhuman actors as anti-colonial allies, but risks coercive misuse |
| Crown | Can bind ancient hosts through regalia, but succession and legitimacy can collapse |
| Continental Command | Can militarize anomalies, strongest short-term route, worst blowback |
| Sacred Soil | Safest reveal path and best containment |
| Black Star Return | Vulnerable to port disruption and fictional outbreak pressure |
| Deep Green Covenant | Full route, highest power and highest risk |

## Presentation and assets

High-chaos presentation should rely on:
- generated nonhuman portraits
- generated supernatural report images
- animated route emblems if asset scope allows
- forest, river, stone, storm, and animal motifs
- abstract disease iconography
- warning frame for covenant pressure
- static fallbacks for every animated element

Do not use final super-event titles, quotes, button text, or audio. Any high-chaos super-event candidate remains research-gated.
