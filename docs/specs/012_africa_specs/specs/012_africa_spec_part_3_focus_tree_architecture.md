# Event 12 Africa specification, part 3

## Large non-linear focus tree architecture

All focus and branch names are working labels, not final localisation. The implementation agent owns final focus count, exact names, coordinates, prerequisites, and clean layout. The tree must preserve the architecture, route interaction, payoffs, AI logic, and visible consequences described here.

## Tree design principles

The Africa tree is a shared continental tree with host-region overlays and route-specific content. It must not play like one generic tree copied onto every possible host. The regional overlay changes opening focuses, state targets, unit traditions, corridor projects, restoration candidates, advisors, and AI priorities.

The tree should feel broad from the start. The player can stabilise the host, begin protection, open development, and prepare the military in parallel. Political choices narrow the constitutional future, but support branches remain available unless a route makes them logically incompatible.

The tree should contain early, middle, and late play:

- early play builds legitimacy, first contacts, and first protective capacity
- middle play creates the League, regional congresses, integration systems, and route identity
- late play settles Africa, survives foreign containment, and enters world-order politics

No major branch may end in a small modifier. Every branch must unlock decisions, missions, characters, units, map work, country identity, faction mechanics, integration tools, or a clear capstone.

## Opening lane: host stabilisation and first proof

This lane is available immediately. It solves the chosen country’s immediate weaknesses and establishes the event mechanic.

### Focus group: define the continental project

Purpose:

- apply the provisional continental identity
- establish the decision category and GUI
- reveal Charter Authority, Reach, Burden, Pressure, and member confidence
- choose the first contact region
- create the first proof mission

The opening focus should not choose the final constitution. It establishes the public ambition and allows route debate.

### Focus group: secure the capital

Purpose:

- protect the capital and key command state
- establish emergency communications
- create a defensive unit or command structure appropriate to the host
- prevent the event from immediately collapsing in a weak country

Reward style:

- forts, anti-air, rail, supply, command, or emergency units tied to actual states
- no generic stability-only reward

### Focus group: map the first corridor

Purpose:

- identify the first transport link to a potential partner
- open a state-targeted construction mission
- raise Reach when completed

Regional variants:

- desert route
- river route
- rail route
- port route
- lake route
- island convoy route

### Focus group: the first guarantee

Purpose:

- unlock protection actions
- select or generate a threatened African partner
- create a timed proof mission

Failure should open a recovery focus rather than block the whole tree.

### Focus group: host legitimacy

Purpose:

- address the selected country’s starting political problem
- set the initial leader or council direction
- open advisors and local coalition choices
- determine whether the host enters the first congress as a government, movement, crown, army, or coalition

Host-specific implementation must use existing politics where possible. It should not erase a meaningful vanilla tree unless the event created the country or the additive branch cannot work.

## Political constitution route family

The constitutional family is the main set of mutually exclusive routes. The player can delay the choice while building the League, but deeper integration requires a route.

### Route A: Charter federalism

Narrative role:

A negotiated federation with strong regional representation and protected member rights.

Mechanical role:

- strongest peaceful integration
- lower Burden
- member votes and constitutional missions
- federal subjects and regional congresses
- stable post-unification state

Key focus groups:

1. **Provisional convention.** Opens constitutional delegates and representation balance.
2. **Member bill of rights.** Creates enforceable autonomy clauses and reduces departure risk.
3. **Regional chambers.** Establishes region-based representation and unlocks federal member conversion.
4. **Common citizenship.** Links diaspora and member integration.
5. **Federal treasury.** Creates shared resource and development contributions.
6. **Continental court.** Improves arbitration and peaceful border settlement.
7. **Responsible common command.** Integrates defence without dissolving every member army.
8. **Federal proclamation.** Final route identity and integration capstone.

Tradeoff:

Strong members can block measures. The route takes time and may be vulnerable during war.

Failure state:

A constitutional deadlock can create a reform crisis, a rival confederation, or an emergency executive branch.

AI:

Favored by democratic hosts, mixed ideological coalitions, medium powers, and hosts with many independent members. Avoided by desperate countries losing a major war unless they have strong allies.

### Route B: Continental republic

Narrative role:

A central republican state built through mass politics, common citizenship, and a continent-wide constitutional campaign.

Mechanical role:

- high Authority
- strong diaspora integration
- faster accession among republics
- elections, parties, and leadership changes
- central institutions with regional safeguards

Key focus groups:

1. **Continental assembly.** Opens party and movement alignment.
2. **Citizens before borders.** Speeds accession and links return programmes.
3. **Republican guard.** Creates a loyal defensive force with constitutional obligations.
4. **Campaign across the regions.** Uses missions to win public support in members.
5. **A common franchise.** Opens integration referenda and representation projects.
6. **The first continental election.** Can change leader and party balance.
7. **A capital for the republic.** Chooses a permanent, rotating, or distributed capital model.
8. **Republic proclaimed.** Final identity and post-unification political branch.

Tradeoff:

Monarchies, sultanates, and traditional authorities may resist. Election defeats can change route leadership.

Failure state:

A disputed election can cause a constitutional crisis, military intervention, or regional secession.

AI:

Favored by democratic, social democratic, and broad nationalist hosts. Stronger when diaspora Trust and urban industry are high.

### Route C: Council of crowns

Narrative role:

A union that preserves kings, sultans, emirs, and restored courts inside a hierarchy of recognised crowns.

Mechanical role:

- strong restoration packages
- dynasty and court diplomacy
- local legitimacy in monarchic states
- elite units and ceremonial institutions
- route-specific regnal flavour

Key focus groups:

1. **Invite the old houses.** Reveals compatible restoration candidates.
2. **Rules of precedence.** Creates a rank and recognition system for crowns.
3. **Guarantee the lesser thrones.** Protects small monarchies from absorption.
4. **Court roads and royal progresses.** Builds regional links and raises Authority.
5. **Council of succession.** Prevents or manages inheritance crises.
6. **Imperial or confederal settlement.** Major fork between first-among-equals and central crown.
7. **The continental regalia.** Final portrait frame, flag, and state identity.
8. **Crown compact.** Final integration and subject rules.

Tradeoff:

Republican and revolutionary states resist. Rival crowns can create succession wars.

Failure state:

A disputed seniority claim can divide the League into royal blocs.

AI:

Favored by monarchies, traditional restorations, Ethiopia-related identities, and hosts with several royal partners. Avoided when most members are communist or strongly republican.

### Route D: People’s union

Narrative role:

A socialist or communal continental project built through anti-concession politics, organised labour, land reform, and liberation movements.

Mechanical role:

- rapid support for colonial revolts
- common production and equipment pools
- strong industry mobilisation
- ideological integration of compatible members
- intense foreign sanctions

Key focus groups:

1. **Congress of workers and peasants.** Defines the route’s internal coalition.
2. **Break the concession system.** Opens resource nationalisation and foreign conflict.
3. **Arms for liberation.** Creates equipment pipelines and volunteer missions.
4. **Continental labour standards.** Raises member support and production coordination.
5. **Land and local power.** Branches between communal autonomy and central planning.
6. **People’s defence committees.** Creates militia and regional reserve systems.
7. **Union planning network.** Links factories, rail, and member contributions.
8. **People’s union.** Final identity and revolutionary world-order branch.

Tradeoff:

Conservative revolt, capital flight, embargoes, and pressure from non-socialist members.

Failure state:

Forced reform can trigger peasant, court, religious, or regional resistance. A bureaucratic centralisation branch can become coercive.

AI:

Favored by communist and radical socialist hosts, high colonial pressure, active independence waves, and strong labour politics.

### Route E: Military continentalism

Narrative role:

A security-first project that unifies command before institutions.

Mechanical role:

- fastest war readiness
- protectorate and occupation tools
- strong common command
- lower confidence and higher Burden
- commanders as political actors

Key focus groups:

1. **Emergency continental command.** Creates the central military body.
2. **Standardise the armies.** Links templates, equipment, and training.
3. **Secure the corridors.** Adds border and rail missions.
4. **Protectorate doctrine.** Unlocks security members and coercive access.
5. **Regional commanders.** Creates powerful characters and command districts.
6. **Civilian supervision or soldiers’ state.** Major internal fork.
7. **Continental mobilisation.** Opens large manpower and equipment systems.
8. **Command of Africa.** Final military identity and war-order branch.

Tradeoff:

High Burden, coups, member exits, and diplomatic isolation.

Failure state:

A defeated regional commander can defect, create a warlord state, or lead a rival bloc.

AI:

Favored by fascist, military, desperate, or heavily threatened hosts. Avoided by weak democracies unless the world threat is active.

### Route F: Confederation of African states

Narrative role:

A permanent union of sovereign states with common defence, diplomacy, and selected economic systems.

Mechanical role:

- alternative success condition
- low Burden
- strong member agency
- shared decisions and world diplomacy
- limited direct manpower and industry

Key focus groups:

1. **Sovereignty clause.** Protects members from annexation.
2. **Common defence treaty.** Builds shared reserves and commands.
3. **Council of governments.** Creates votes and rotating leadership.
4. **Continental market.** Opens trade, transport, and standards.
5. **Right of withdrawal.** Makes exits orderly and raises confidence.
6. **Common foreign policy.** Defines recognition and intervention rules.
7. **Permanent conference.** Establishes the confederal capital system.
8. **Africa in concert.** Final confederal identity and victory state.

Tradeoff:

Members can leave and can block war. Some continent-unifier interactions require deeper centralisation.

Failure state:

Leadership paralysis can split the confederation during crisis.

AI:

Favored by strong independent African powers that will not accept annexation and by hosts with low relative strength.

## Charter League lane

This lane is available to every political route and changes according to the constitution.

### Focus group: provisional secretariat

Unlocks the first League institutions, member registry, and selected-member GUI.

### Focus group: protection protocol

Defines when the League can guarantee, intervene, evacuate, and send aid.

### Focus group: accession clauses

Unlocks the clause packages and scripted negotiation events.

### Focus group: common reserve

Creates equipment, transport, and manpower contributions. Later military focuses improve it.

### Focus group: arbitration chamber

Unlocks border and debt mediation. Constitutional routes modify fairness and enforcement.

### Focus group: regional congresses

Opens the nine congress systems. The branch should fork into early congress priorities based on the host overlay.

### Focus group: member representation

Creates regional seats and lowers Burden. Military routes can weaken it. Federal and confederal routes depend on it.

### Focus group: emergency vote

Allows the League to respond to war, disaster, outbreak, coup, or defection. AI members vote through confidence and threat.

### Focus group: discipline or consent

A mid-tree fork determines whether the host relies on penalties and command or incentives and negotiation. It is not fully mutually exclusive with the constitution, but it changes integration costs and member behavior.

### Focus group: permanent Charter

Capstone that upgrades the faction, opens full shared systems, and gives the League a route-specific emblem.

## Integration and regional congress lane

This lane becomes available after the first functioning region.

### Focus group: survey the regions

Creates readiness values and state groups for all nine regions.

### Focus group: settle the first region

Requires a congress outcome. It unlocks regional subject, integration, or confederal tools.

### Focus group: federal templates

Creates several subject and autonomy models. It should not create one generic puppet type for every route.

### Focus group: local administration corps

Unlocks trained personnel, advisors, and equipment costs for integration projects.

### Focus group: connect the capitals

Requires real transport links. It interacts with the economy lane.

### Focus group: representation before cores

Reduces resistance and unlocks core recognition after political requirements.

### Focus group: absorb or preserve

A route-sensitive fork between direct integration and permanent regional subjects. Confederation cannot choose mass absorption without route change.

### Focus group: second and third congress waves

Opens concurrent but capped regional projects. Burden rises if too many are active.

### Focus group: continental settlement

Final regional integration branch that checks all nine regions and prepares Africa is one.

## Liberation and external war lane

The expansion branch is distinct from politics and industry. It begins with defence and grows into anti-colonial war.

### Focus group: defend the first partner

Unlocks the first intervention and proof mission.

### Focus group: aid without annexation

Improves guarantees, volunteers, and corridors while protecting member confidence.

### Focus group: liberation committees

Creates contacts inside colonies and links to Independence Wave.

### Focus group: ultimatum to a colonial holder

Unlocks a diplomatic demand only when Reach, readiness, and local support exist.

### Focus group: war of liberation

Creates route-aware war goals and postwar settlement choices.

### Focus group: bases and adjacent islands

Addresses foreign bases, Red Sea islands, Indian Ocean islands, Atlantic islands, and African-adjacent holdings. It must use case-specific diplomacy.

### Focus group: postwar choice

For every liberated country, choose independence, League membership, federation, protectorate, or direct integration. Direct integration without local support creates high Burden.

### Focus group: continental defence perimeter

Builds ports, airbases, radar, anti-air, supply, and regional commands in named states.

### Focus group: anti-colonial world policy

After near-unification, opens support for liberation outside Africa and connections to other continent unifiers.

### Focus group: no second partition

Capstone for defending Africa from the Scramble response.

## Economy and corridor lane

This lane should contain visible map work and production systems.

### Focus group: development inventory

Maps industry, resources, transport, ports, and food vulnerability. It creates regional project choices.

### Focus group: regional corridor opener

One focus group per overlay family, with several shared follow-ups. The player chooses a first corridor and can later complete others.

### Focus group: rail and road standardisation

Unlocks construction missions, trains, trucks, and supply improvements.

### Focus group: river and lake transport

Improves Congo, Nile, Niger, Great Lakes, and other eligible waterways through scripted regional projects.

### Focus group: continental ports

Builds dockyards, naval bases, convoys, and coastal infrastructure.

### Focus group: local processing

Adds factories or resource processing in source states and changes concession politics.

### Focus group: continental procurement

Standardises equipment families and creates shared production contracts.

### Focus group: development bank or common fund

The institution name is internal. It creates project pools and member contribution rules.

### Focus group: food security network

Adds infrastructure, storage, agriculture support, and disaster resilience.

### Focus group: resource sovereignty

Major fork between negotiated revision, nationalisation, shared concessions, and host extraction.

### Focus group: continental industrial plan

Late capstone that turns connected regions into a major production network and supports post-unification mobilisation.

## Military and common defence lane

The military lane solves real security problems and provides several doctrine paths.

### Focus group: emergency staff

Creates the first defensive plan and officer pool.

### Focus group: integrate volunteer experience

Improves units after protection wars and creates commanders.

### Focus group: regional commands

One command per settled region. Commands have local templates and AI roles.

### Doctrine path 1: mobile continental defence

- trucks and motorisation
- rapid reinforcement
- desert and savanna movement
- corridor security
- mobile reserves

### Doctrine path 2: people’s defence

- militias and local defence committees
- low equipment cost
- high local support
- strong defence, weaker offensive control

### Doctrine path 3: professional common army

- standard templates
- officer academies
- higher equipment cost
- strong organisation and combined arms

### Doctrine path 4: royal and regional hosts

- elite guard units
- cavalry and ceremonial units with real combat roles
- regional recruitment
- strong local identity, weaker standardisation

### Doctrine path 5: high-chaos formations

Hidden until Evolution II or III. Opens elephant logistics, forest giants, nonhuman units, living statues, river units, and oracle support according to route.

### Focus group: continental air network

Airbases, transport aircraft, fighter coordination, and long-distance support.

### Focus group: two-ocean fleet

Atlantic and Indian Ocean naval branches, plus Mediterranean and Red Sea access when relevant.

### Focus group: common arsenal

Production, stockpiles, licensing, and member contribution.

### Focus group: army of Africa

Capstone that changes command structure, mobilisation, and post-unification readiness. It must not be only a national spirit.

This lane helps the League survive espionage, sanctions, coups, and expedition planning.

### Focus group: map foreign interests

Reveals concessions, bases, guarantees, and likely intervention powers.

### Focus group: protect the Charter

Counterintelligence against member coups and foreign bribery.

### Focus group: liberation intelligence

Supports underground networks in colonies. It increases risk and foreign pressure.

### Focus group: break the blockade

Convoy protection, smuggling, neutral trade, and diplomatic work.

### Focus group: expose the expedition

Reveals foreign preparation and gives members time to mobilise.

### Focus group: divide the coalition

### Focus group: continental cipher and liaison network

Improves member coordination and intelligence sharing.

### Focus group: the world watches Africa

Capstone that improves deterrence and world-order diplomacy.

## Restoration lane

The restoration lane is fed by the host overlay, political constitution, and regional congresses. It does not show every possible polity at once.

### Focus group: survey dormant claims

Reveals candidates in the first region.

### Focus group: legitimacy councils

Measures local support, historical anchor, surviving institutions, and border conflict.

### Focus group: restore under protection

Creates a Tier A or Tier B polity as a League member or subject.

### Focus group: courts, councils, and republics

Lets ideology and route change the restored state’s public form without changing its direct identity.

### Focus group: settle overlapping claims

Required where several identities share territory.

### Focus group: regional federation

Allows several small restored identities to form one federal subject while retaining local characters and flags through autonomy or cosmetic systems.

### Focus group: preserve the old names

Confederal and monarchic route capstone. It rewards unity without direct annexation.

### Focus group: fold the crowns and councils

Central routes convert restored members toward integration.

The restoration matrix in part 4 and the regional polity catalog control which states appear and how much bespoke content they receive.

## High-chaos hidden lane

This lane remains hidden until Evolution III symptoms appear. A normal campaign may never see it.

### Entry conditions

At least one of these must exist:

- extreme chaos and repeated natural disasters
- a supernatural African event connection
- a nonhuman actor in an African region
- repeated successful high-chaos rituals or predictions
- an unresolved biological or ecological catastrophe
- a rare leader, artifact, or regional event chain

### Route family 1: covenant with the land

Mechanics:

- Ecological Wrath
- disaster prediction
- disaster bargaining
- weather retaliation
- conservation and sacrifice choices

### Route family 2: nonhuman congress

Mechanics:

- Gorilla Kingdom, Pan, River, Green, Stoneborn, or other approved actors
- special member clauses
- nonhuman leaders and units
- ordinary member fear

### Route family 3: ancient hosts

Mechanics:

- living statues
- restored legendary armies
- archaeological and ritual sites
- high supply and maintenance constraints

### Route family 4: plague and medicine

Mechanics:

- fictional supernatural diseases
- quarantine, cure, weaponisation, and condemnation
- integration with existing bio warfare systems
- strict abstract design without real-world procedural detail

### Route family 5: oracle state

Mechanics:

- prediction of disasters and wars
- unreliable visions
- pre-emptive missions
- legitimacy when predictions succeed

### High-chaos capstone

The final route may transform the continental identity and world reaction. It must remain clearly supernatural and must not treat a real African community as an animal or monster caricature.

## Branch interaction rules

- Political constitutions are mutually exclusive once a final route lock is taken.
- Economy, military, diaspora, intelligence, Charter, and early liberation branches remain broadly compatible.
- Confederation cannot use forced annexation capstones without a visible route crisis or constitutional change.
- Military continentalism can use peaceful tools, but receives weaker confidence gains.
- People’s union and council of crowns have strong mutual hostility unless a rare compromise branch is designed.
- Federalism and continental republic can converge in a late constitutional settlement.
- High-chaos branches can corrupt or replace normal institutions. They should not be a free extra route stacked on every normal capstone.
- Scramble response emergency powers can temporarily alter the constitution, then require a postwar settlement.
- Continent sponsorship requires Africa is one or a qualifying confederal victory.

## Focus AI architecture

AI route choice must use campaign state.

### Host strength

- weak host favors protection, confederation, external aid, and gradual federalism
- medium host favors federalism, republic, or people’s union according to ideology
- strong host can choose any route and is more likely to consider military continentalism

### Member landscape

- many strong independent states increase confederal and federal weights
- many colonies increase liberation and people’s union weights
- many monarchies and restoration candidates increase council of crowns weights
- repeated member defections increase military or reform crisis weights

### World state

- active world threat increases common defence and emergency centralisation
- high colonial pressure increases military, intelligence, and sanctions branches
- severe industry weakness increases development priority
- active disasters increase relief and high-chaos prediction routes
- tier 6 permits rare high-chaos choices

### Ideology

Ideology influences but does not completely dictate the constitution. A democratic monarchy can pursue a council of crowns. A military republic can pursue federalism after a reform. AI should avoid impossible combinations through route validity rather than flat ideology locks alone.

### High-chaos AI

Ordinary AI should have a very low chance to enter high-chaos routes unless symptoms, leaders, or actors exist. Special chaos countries and high-chaos hosts can prefer them. AI must not sacrifice the League to a supernatural demand when survival conditions are clearly impossible.
