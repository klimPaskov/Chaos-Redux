# Host-country playbooks and country-specific continental openings (block 1)

## Host-country playbooks and country-specific continental openings

All focus names and event labels in this file are working labels, not final localisation. The host playbooks refine the shared continental tree. They do not require a separate full tree for every African start.

## Purpose of the host layer

The continental project should begin from the country that receives it. Ethiopia, Liberia, Egypt, South Africa, Nigeria, Uganda, Madagascar, and the Belgian Congo cannot share the same opening problem without losing their identity. They can share the Charter League, integration ladder, constitutional routes, economic systems, and post-unification content, but the first years of play need different pressures, allies, enemies, and proof of leadership.

Each host therefore receives a compact overlay that changes:

- the opening legitimacy problem
- the first protected partner or liberation target
- the first corridor and supply problem
- the mix of Charter Authority, Reach, Burden, and member confidence at entry
- the first six to ten host focuses or equivalent event-owned branches
- route preferences and route-specific mutations
- the local actors most likely to support or resist the project
- the form of the first rival bloc
- the military and economic proof expected before the first regional congress
- the post-unification institution or regional privilege carried into Africa

The overlay must use the shared systems after the opening. It should not duplicate the full decision category, create a second integration ladder, or build a separate version of every constitutional route.

## Depth classes

### Full host dossier

A full dossier is required for a strategically distinctive start that can plausibly lead a long campaign. It receives:

- one opening crisis chain
- one country-specific national problem with a complete lifecycle
- one proof mission with partial success and failure
- two or three host focus modules that cross into the shared tree
- at least one distinct regional diplomatic problem
- one country-specific route mutation for each plausible constitutional family
- route-aware AI priorities
- a clear anti-bloat boundary

### Compact host signature

A compact signature is used for smaller territories, colonial administrations, protectorates, and rare starts that do not justify a full bespoke opening. It changes the first proof mission, regional corridor, starting values, a small set of focus rewards, and AI preferences. It promotes to a full dossier only when implementation or testing shows that the start lacks a coherent long campaign.

## Shared overlay budget

A host overlay should normally add six to ten focuses, two to four events, one proof mission, and one or two country-specific decisions. Existing meaningful country content should be preserved. The overlay attaches to the shared tree or opens through event-owned branches. A generic or event-created host can receive the shared tree directly.

A host overlay has failed if it only changes flavour text, gives a different flag, or replaces one flat modifier with another. It has also failed if it grows into a second full continental system that repeats the League, congress, integration, and world-order content.

## Full host dossiers

# North Africa and the Nile

## Ethiopia

### Starting tension

Ethiopia begins from an immediate test of survival and international credibility. The continental claim is credible only if the host can keep a sovereign African state alive under invasion, preserve a functioning government, and turn the failure of collective security into an African protection system. The opening should use the existing war state when the Italian invasion is active. If the war has already ended, the same layer becomes restoration, exile return, or liberation of occupied Ethiopian territory.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The host starts with a mixed institution representing an embattled imperial state. It combines weak supply, uneven regional command, and diplomatic isolation with legitimacy drawn from sovereign continuity and resistance. The problem can become:

1. a negotiated imperial federation with regional guarantees
2. a republican constitutional settlement after victory
3. a military emergency state that risks never returning power
4. a socialist reconstruction coalition built around land, soldiers, and anti-concession policy
5. a confederal settlement that lets other crowns and regions retain wide autonomy

The starting problem should be transformed by concrete accomplishments, not removed by one focus. Holding Addis Ababa, maintaining a corridor, winning foreign recognition, protecting civilians, and integrating regional forces should each remove one part of the burden.

### First proof mission

The first proof is to preserve or restore an Ethiopian capital-supply corridor while another African state or movement receives material aid. Full success requires both survival and outward assistance. Partial success preserves the state but postpones continental leadership. Failure can create an exile or guerrilla opening rather than ending the package.

### Host focus modules

- **The unfinished appeal.** Converts diplomatic humiliation into the protection protocol and opens public evidence against colonial guarantees that failed.
- **Roads beyond Shoa.** Builds supply and administrative links through named Ethiopian regions, then connects the first Horn partner.
- **Armies of the provinces.** Chooses between negotiated regional commands, professional centralisation, or emergency levies. Each choice alters later federal and military routes.
- **The Red Sea question.** Opens Eritrean, Djiboutian, Somali, and Sudanese diplomacy without granting automatic claims or cores.
- **A court for African disputes.** Gives Ethiopia an early arbitration advantage if it avoids using restoration claims as excuses for conquest.

### Route mutations

Charter federalism becomes a negotiated imperial-federal settlement where regional autonomy is proven at home before being offered abroad. The continental republic route requires a real constitutional break or abdication process, not a cosmetic name change. The council of crowns route gives Ethiopia strong prestige but also creates disputes over precedence. The people’s union route grows from wartime mobilisation and land settlement, with a serious risk of court and regional revolt. Military continentalism is powerful during active invasion but accumulates emergency-rule debt. Confederation is attractive when the host is weak, encircled, or dependent on autonomous partners.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Somali actors, Eritrean factions, Sudanese interests, and rival Ethiopian regional elites must react to the host’s conduct. A League led through guarantees and negotiated access can win confidence. A League led through automatic historical claims can produce a Horn sovereignty bloc, foreign-backed secession, or a regional war before wider unification begins.

### Military and economy

The host’s early strength should come from mountain defence, irregular networks, regional cavalry or mobile formations, and progressive supply recovery. Industrial expansion should first repair roads, depots, workshops, and communications. It should not grant an immediate heavy-industrial base that ignores the invasion.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Ethiopian host can make Addis Ababa one of several constitutional capitals, establish a continental court, or preserve a ceremonial crown inside another constitutional route. These are institutional legacies rather than mandatory map names.

### High-chaos gate

Aksumite, oracle, relic, or sacred-weather material appears only after normal survival and restoration content has had time to matter. It must not replace the real invasion with fantasy at the opening.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Ethiopia prioritises survival, supply, external aid, and one secure partner before deep integration. It avoids broad Horn claims while losing the home war. The overlay should not reproduce Ethiopia’s entire historical tree. It owns the continental consequence of Ethiopia’s survival and the political settlement created by that consequence.

## Egypt

### Starting tension

Egypt begins with formal sovereignty, a powerful monarchy and party system, British military presence, the Canal, and an unresolved claim over the Nile Valley. Its continental project can become liberation, regional hegemony, constitutional leadership, or a new form of imperial control. The first campaign question is whether Egypt can oppose British power without turning Sudan and the rest of Africa into objects of Egyptian policy.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is divided sovereignty. It tracks British access, royal authority, parliamentary legitimacy, officer confidence, Canal security, and Sudanese consent. It cannot be solved by simply expelling Britain. A government that removes British troops but annexes partners without consent trades foreign domination for regional Burden.

The problem can mature into a treaty-backed republic, a constrained constitutional crown, an officer-led emergency state, a Nile confederation, or a socialist anti-concession coalition. Each form changes the first regional congress and the host’s treatment of Sudan.

### First proof mission

Secure the Canal and Lower Nile supply network while obtaining a negotiated African partner. The preferred target is Sudan when a valid Sudanese actor exists, but the mission requires a bilateral settlement, not a unilateral union. If Sudan is unavailable, the proof can use Libya, Ethiopia, or a Red Sea partner.

### Host focus modules

- **Sovereignty in practice.** Converts treaty clauses, foreign bases, and wartime access into concrete bargaining actions.
- **The Nile is negotiated.** Opens a river-basin commission, irrigation work, flood planning, and a Sudanese consent path.
- **Parliament, palace, and officers.** Establishes the domestic power struggle that determines which continental routes are credible.
- **The Canal as a continental road.** Lets Egypt use Canal income and shipping for African aid, at the cost of foreign pressure and convoy exposure.
- **South of the cataracts.** Requires reconnaissance, diplomatic missions, and local agreements before claims or federation proposals appear.

### Route mutations

Federalism requires a binding Nile autonomy clause and protections against Egyptian vote dominance. The republic route is strong in urban administration and diaspora links but can fracture between parliamentary and officer republicanism. The crowns route can retain the Egyptian monarchy while treating Sudanese and restored Nile crowns as partners. The people’s union route nationalises concessions and Canal interests, creating sanctions and internal property conflict. Military continentalism receives access to an experienced officer corps and strategic chokepoints, but it faces a high coup risk. Confederation can use a Nile and Red Sea compact as its founding model.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Sudanese actors should be able to form an Upper Nile coalition if Egypt treats them as a dynastic possession. Ethiopia can resist Egyptian water pressure. Maghreb hosts can oppose Cairo’s claim to speak for North Africa. Strong Egyptian coercion can unite otherwise incompatible governments against the host.

### Military and economy

Egypt’s advantage is strategic position, population, existing institutions, and access to the Canal. Its weakness is dependence on foreign equipment, vulnerable political legitimacy, and the cost of defending a long Nile axis. The overlay should build river transport, rail, air defence, and officer reform before it grants continental expedition capacity.

### Post-unification signature

Egypt can make the Canal a common continental trust, establish a Nile water compact, or host a permanent external-relations chamber. Keeping the Canal as a host monopoly raises long-term regional distrust.

### High-chaos gate

Ancient hosts, living monuments, or flood prophecy require a separate high-chaos reveal. They must remain clearly supernatural and distinct from modern Egyptian and Sudanese peoples.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Egypt weighs British power, domestic legitimacy, Sudanese confidence, and Canal vulnerability before escalation. It should prefer a negotiated Nile settlement unless an authoritarian route has already accepted the resulting Burden. The overlay should not become a second Middle East event package.

## Sudan

### Starting tension

Sudan begins inside the Anglo-Egyptian condominium and under competing external claims. Its continental project is credible when it establishes Sudanese agency across the Nile Valley, reconciles or protects regional interests, and prevents both London and Cairo from deciding its future without local participation.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem combines dual administration, uneven regional development, river dependence, military recruitment, and the risk that a northern elite speaks for the whole territory. It should contain a visible north-south representation problem without treating either region as uniform.

The institution can evolve into a federal Nile state, a regional confederation, a republican civil service, a military river command, a socialist irrigation and labour programme, or a restoration settlement involving Nubian identities. Every solution must address local representation, transport, and military command.

### First proof mission

Keep the Nile corridor open between at least two major administrative regions while preventing Egypt or Britain from imposing a settlement. Full success adds one independent African partner. Partial success establishes Sudanese autonomy but leaves the host dependent on one external patron.

### Host focus modules

- **No decision without Sudan.** Creates diplomatic refusal tools and an internal constituent conference.
- **The river administration.** Turns rail, river steamers, irrigation, and flood control into the first Reach system.
- **Regiments of the condominium.** Decides whether colonial forces become a national army, regional guards, or a League reserve.
- **Khartoum and the regions.** Establishes representation rules before the host proposes continental institutions.
- **The Nubian settlement.** Opens cultural autonomy, restoration, or federal options without forcing a single ancient identity across the Nile.

### Route mutations

Federalism and confederation are strongest when the host solves regional representation. The republic route can build a professional civil service but risks centralising the capital. The crowns route works through local dynastic and religious authorities only when they accept a constitutional council. The people’s union route focuses on irrigation labour, land, and anti-concession politics. Military continentalism uses river mobility and existing regiments, but command fragmentation is a major failure state.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Egypt can act as sponsor, partner, or claimant. Ethiopia watches water and border policy. Southern and western regional actors can refuse a Nile-centred project. Coercive centralisation can produce a peripheral states coalition that is stronger than any single rebellion.

### Military and economy

Sudan’s army should be mobile along rail and river lines but weak away from them. Development focuses should create corridor depth, irrigation, veterinary services, and depots. A large abstract factory package would erase the country’s central logistical problem.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Sudanese host can establish a continental water and pastoral mobility chamber, or make regional vetoes part of the federal system. This legacy should depend on whether the opening conference represented more than the capital.

### High-chaos gate

Nile or desert prophecy, ancient river powers, and living-statue material require high chaos and explicit fictional framing.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Sudan prioritises autonomy, corridor control, and a domestic settlement. It should not choose continental war while both external condominium powers can still remove its government. The overlay does not attempt to simulate every later Sudanese political movement.

## Morocco

### Starting tension

Morocco begins as a divided protectorate with a surviving monarchy, French and Spanish zones, international interests around Tangier, and an emerging reformist-nationalist movement. The continental project must reconcile palace legitimacy, urban petition politics, rural authority, and anti-colonial action.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is partitioned sovereignty. It records the degree to which the palace, reformists, regional notables, and armed resistance accept a common strategy. It can mature into a constitutional monarchy, a republic, a council of regions and courts, a socialist anti-concession coalition, a military liberation state, or a loose Maghreb confederation.

### First proof mission

Link the French and Spanish zones through clandestine or negotiated access while protecting a neighboring Maghreb movement. Full success requires a settlement for Tangier or another international zone that does not simply transfer foreign privileges to the host.

### Host focus modules

- **Petitions become institutions.** Turns reform demands into an assembly, press network, and legal challenge.
- **The divided protectorate.** Opens separate tools for French, Spanish, and international zones.
- **The sultan and the movement.** Establishes cooperation, constitutional limitation, or a rupture.
- **Roads over the Atlas.** Uses mountain logistics and local agreements as the first corridor proof.
- **A Maghreb conference.** Invites Algeria and Tunisia as distinct partners rather than extensions of Morocco.

### Route mutations

Federalism becomes a protectorate-to-federation transition with strong regional guarantees. The republic route needs a negotiated or revolutionary end to monarchical authority. The crowns route gives Morocco a plausible senior court but requires strict equality clauses for other monarchies and restored states. The people’s union route attacks land and concession structures, creating palace and foreign backlash. Military continentalism relies on resistance networks and mountain forces. Confederation is a natural Maghreb outcome when the host cannot impose a single state.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Algerian and Tunisian movements resist Moroccan seniority. Spanish and French colonial authorities can coordinate despite their rivalry. Saharan actors can oppose coastal centralisation. A coercive host can create a republican Maghreb bloc or a Saharan autonomy alliance.

### Military and economy

The host’s early military identity is mountain warfare, local networks, small coastal forces, and contested access. Economic development should connect ports, Atlas routes, phosphate and agricultural regions, and local processing. Foreign concession cancellation should create immediate budget and sanctions problems.

### Post-unification signature

Morocco can preserve a Maghreb chamber, a constitutional court of crowns, or a protected international-city model for shared continental ports.

### High-chaos gate

Desert oracle, ancient court, or impossible Atlas content remains hidden until the normal liberation and constitutional struggle is resolved or abandoned.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Morocco balances palace survival, foreign control, and Maghreb cooperation. It avoids claiming leadership over Algeria and Tunisia before winning their confidence. The overlay does not create separate full systems for every protectorate zone after they are settled.

## Algeria

### Starting tension

Algeria begins under direct French rule with a large settler population, unequal citizenship, strong regional differences, and several competing political currents. Its continental project is inseparable from the question of who belongs to the new state, what happens to settlers, how land is handled, and whether liberation is political, social, military, or all three.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is unequal incorporation. It tracks indigenous political representation, settler resistance, rural land pressure, urban labour, military recruitment, and the legitimacy of competing nationalist strategies. It can evolve into a plural republic, a central nationalist state, a socialist land settlement, a military liberation government, an Islamic constitutional current, or a Maghreb confederation.

The design must give the player choices about civilian protection, property settlement, language and legal transition, and local government. It must not reduce the route to expelling one population or granting automatic harmony.

### First proof mission

Create a secure inland-to-port corridor and protect one neighbouring African movement while preventing mass reprisals. Full success raises continental trust. Military success paired with uncontrolled reprisals gives Authority but sharply raises Burden and foreign intervention risk.

### Host focus modules

- **Who speaks for Algeria.** Establishes competition or cooperation among reformists, mass organisers, religious figures, labour, and armed networks.
- **Land after liberation.** Chooses restitution, compensated redistribution, communal settlement, or state administration.
- **Cities and the interior.** Prevents an urban coastal leadership from ignoring inland and Saharan regions.
- **The army that returns.** Governs colonial veterans, defectors, resistance forces, and foreign volunteers.
- **A republic of residents or a state of victory.** Defines citizenship and the treatment of non-combatant settler communities.

### Route mutations

Federalism offers the strongest regional and minority settlement but moves slowly during war. The republic route can create mass citizenship and elections, with a risk of factional collapse. The crowns route is weak unless a credible regional traditional settlement emerges. The people’s union route has strong land and labour tools but faces severe foreign sanctions. Military continentalism can win liberation quickly and then struggle to demobilise. Confederation works as a Maghreb order after independence but does not solve Algeria’s internal land question by itself.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Saharan regions, neighbouring Maghreb movements, and strong local military commanders can resist a coastal central state. France and settler forces can support rival authorities. A host that exports armed revolution without consultation can create a defensive bloc among independent African governments.

### Military and economy

Algeria has access to veterans, ports, dense northern infrastructure, and Saharan depth. It also faces urban fighting, divided forces, and foreign naval pressure. The economic branch should focus on land, mines, ports, rail, local processing, and the replacement of concession management.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Algerian host can create a continental citizenship and restitution court, or a liberation-veterans compact that prevents permanent military privilege. Which institution survives depends on how the war was fought.

### High-chaos gate

Saharan ancient hosts or impossible desert warfare require high chaos and cannot be presented as a substitute for real Algerian political traditions.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Algeria prioritises liberation, internal coalition management, and civilian order. It should not launch several external wars while its land and citizenship settlement is unresolved. The overlay focuses on the consequences of liberation rather than simulating every organisation as a separate country.

## Tunisia

### Starting tension

Tunisia begins with a beylical state under French protection, an organised nationalist movement, urban labour, coastal infrastructure, and a smaller territorial base than its neighbours. It can lead through political organisation, diplomacy, and administration, but it cannot assume military supremacy.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is protected sovereignty without effective power. It tracks the bey, nationalist organisation, labour, French administration, and regional confidence. It can become a constitutional monarchy, a disciplined republic, a labour-oriented union, a military emergency government, or a Maghreb confederal secretariat.

### First proof mission

Win control of a port-administration corridor and secure recognition or practical aid for a neighbouring movement. The mission rewards careful organisation, intelligence, and diplomacy more than raw division count.

### Host focus modules

- **The movement and the palace.** Defines cooperation, constitutional limitation, or republican rupture.
- **Cells, unions, and municipalities.** Builds local support and administrative reach.
- **The port campaign.** Uses strikes, legal pressure, and security work to gain practical control.
- **A school for continental organisers.** Creates advisors and liaison teams for other African movements.
- **The small state doctrine.** Converts limited manpower into intelligence, air defence, coastal protection, and diplomacy.

### Route mutations

Federalism and confederation are natural strengths because Tunisia can offer institutions rather than domination. The republic route receives efficient party and municipal tools but risks over-centralisation. The crowns route can preserve the bey inside a limited constitution. The people’s union route is built through labour and anti-concession policy. Military continentalism is available only after severe threat or a successful officer coalition and should remain costly.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Algeria and Morocco may accept Tunisian mediation but reject Tunisian command. France can isolate the host through port and financial pressure. A Tunisian host that relies excessively on foreign patrons can lose the legitimacy to arbitrate Maghreb disputes.

### Military and economy

The host should use coastal forts, ports, rail, light industry, intelligence, and trained cadres. Its expansion depends on partners and access. It should not receive manpower rewards that erase the size difference with Egypt or Algeria.

### Post-unification signature

Tunisia can provide the permanent secretariat, municipal charter, or civil-service academy of the continental state. These are rewards for institution-building, not automatic capital status.

### High-chaos gate

Oracle or ancient coastal material remains a late reveal and should be smaller in scale than the high-chaos routes of vast states.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Tunisia prefers coalition-building, federalism, republican organisation, or confederation. It uses military centralism only under invasion or extreme chaos. The overlay avoids separate subsystems for every urban organisation.

## Libya

### Starting tension

Libya begins under Italian colonial rule after a brutal pacification campaign, with coastal provinces, interior and desert regions, Senussi networks, exile politics, and weak integrated infrastructure. Its continental project grows from survival, return, and control of desert corridors.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is a country suppressed into separate colonial regions. It tracks exile leadership, Senussi legitimacy, tribal and urban cooperation, desert supply, and the cost of Italian repression. It can become a constitutional monarchy, a regional federation, a republic, a military liberation state, a socialist anti-concession coalition, or a Saharan confederation.

### First proof mission

Reopen one desert or coastal supply route, establish a functioning liberated administration, and support a neighbour without abandoning the home front. Failure can leave the host as an exile government with lower Reach but continued decisions.

### Host focus modules

- **The return routes.** Builds exile, smuggling, oasis, and coastal networks.
- **Cyrenaica, Tripolitania, and the interior.** Requires an internal settlement before wider federation proposals.
- **The Senussi question.** Chooses constitutional leadership, religious-political guidance, or separation from the state.
- **Desert war administration.** Creates mobile logistics and local governance rather than free armies.
- **The Mediterranean blockade.** Opens naval, air, and foreign-aid problems that a desert army alone cannot solve.

### Route mutations

Federalism is tied to a real regional compact. The republic route needs a common civic administration across the former colonial provinces. The crowns route can use Senussi legitimacy but must protect non-dynastic regions. The people’s union route targets land, concessions, and foreign property, while balancing religious institutions. Military continentalism is effective in liberation but prone to regional commanders. Confederation can lead a Saharan and Maghreb network without forcing early territorial merger.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Egypt, Tunisia, Algeria, Chad, and Saharan actors have different interests in Libyan corridors. A host that treats the Sahara as empty strategic depth can provoke a trans-Saharan autonomy coalition.

### Military and economy

The early army is mobile, lightly equipped, and dependent on depots, foreign aid, and oasis chains. Economic development should repair ports, roads, water systems, and local processing. Future oil potential should not become a 1936 windfall without a dedicated alternate-history development chain and major investment.

### Post-unification signature

Libya can establish a continental desert service, mobile water and medical corps, or a protected trans-Saharan corridor charter.

### High-chaos gate

Desert oracle, buried army, or impossible drought warfare appears only after the liberation system is established and clearly carries supernatural costs.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Libya prioritises survival, one supply corridor, and internal regional settlement. It avoids direct wars against several Mediterranean powers without outside support. The overlay does not turn every oasis or tribal network into a tag.

# West Africa and the Atlantic

## Liberia

### Starting tension

Liberia begins as a sovereign republic with diplomatic recognition, a small administrative base, deep social divisions, and an economy constrained by foreign concessions and debt. Its continental project carries unusual credibility because it is independent, but that credibility collapses if the host reproduces concession rule or treats neighbouring peoples as subjects of an elite republic.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is sovereignty mortgaged to private power. It tracks concession dependence, public revenue, interior representation, labour abuses, frontier administration, and the legitimacy of the ruling political order. It can mature into a reformed federal republic, a civic continental republic, a traditional-council settlement, a labour and farmer union, a military anti-corruption government, or a confederal port and aid state.

The problem should be reduced through audited contracts, labour protection, interior roads, representative institutions, and an alternative revenue base. Cancelling every contract at once can be a legitimate radical choice, but it creates equipment shortages, diplomatic retaliation, and budget collapse.

### First proof mission

Deliver aid through Monrovia to a neighbouring African movement while keeping the national budget and transport network functioning. Full success requires the shipment to arrive without granting the foreign company or outside sponsor new political privileges. Partial success delivers the aid but deepens dependence. Failure can expose corruption or trigger an internal reform crisis.

### Host focus modules

- **The republic beyond the coast.** Extends representation and administration inland through negotiated local institutions.
- **Contracts under public law.** Audits concessions, labour obligations, taxes, and infrastructure promises.
- **Monrovia as a free African port.** Creates an aid and liaison hub with customs, convoy, and intelligence tradeoffs.
- **A citizenship settlement.** Addresses political rights, local authority, and the relationship between settler-descended elites and indigenous communities.
- **The frontier is not a concession.** Opens border guarantees and local development with Sierra Leone, Guinea, and Côte d’Ivoire.

### Route mutations

Federalism requires a domestic county and local-authority settlement before Liberia can sell federalism abroad. The republic route is powerful but must choose between party continuity, broad franchise, and a new civic movement. The crowns route is weak as a national route, but traditional authorities can receive a protected chamber. The people’s union route attacks concession ownership and labour abuses while risking isolation. Military continentalism can emerge from anti-corruption or frontier crisis, but it has little manpower for reckless expansion. Confederation turns Liberia into a port, court, and aid coordinator rather than a territorial hegemon.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Neighbouring colonial administrations can close borders and sponsor dissidents. Strong West African actors may dismiss Liberia as too small or too tied to foreign capital. Interior communities can refuse a continental project that has not reformed domestic citizenship. A concession-backed rival faction can offer stability in exchange for external control.

### Military and economy

Liberia should rely on a small professional force, frontier constabulary reform, volunteers, port access, and foreign equipment. Its economic path focuses on customs, roads, local agriculture, public finance, and replacement of concession monopolies. It should not become an industrial major through a few focus rewards.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Liberian host can establish a continental anti-concession code, public-contract court, or protected free-port network. These institutions should inherit the domestic choices made during the opening.

### High-chaos gate

Forest covenants or impossible coastal actors require high chaos and must remain visibly fictional. They cannot be coded as distorted versions of real interior communities.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Liberia prioritises budget survival, representation, one aid corridor, and external guarantees. It avoids military continentalism without a severe threat or a strong League. The overlay does not simulate every concession as a separate corporation mechanic.

## Nigeria

### Starting tension

Nigeria begins as a very large colonial construction joining regions with different political institutions, economic systems, religious histories, transport patterns, and experience of elected politics. Its continental project can command manpower and resources, but it risks reproducing the same centralising logic that created the colony.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is an amalgamation without a common mandate. It tracks northern emirate confidence, western urban and traditional politics, eastern local representation, middle-belt security, Lagos legitimacy, rail and port dependence, and the role of the colonial civil service. The host cannot solve this through a single national-unity focus.

The problem can become a negotiated federation, a mass republic, a council of regions and traditional states, a workers and farmers union, a central military command, or a confederation with strong regional sovereignty. Every route must define revenue sharing, civil-service recruitment, language and education policy, and command of regional forces.

### First proof mission

Keep two interregional rail or river corridors functioning while winning consent from at least three major regional political centres. Full success then funds a neighbouring anti-colonial partner. Partial success secures the infrastructure but leaves one region outside the mandate. Failure can create a regional boycott, rival congress, or army intervention.

### Host focus modules

- **The amalgamation conference.** Converts the colonial administrative division into a constituent negotiation.
- **Lagos and the regions.** Prevents the capital and coastal press from claiming automatic national authority.
- **Emirs, councils, and elected wards.** Creates different local-government templates under one constitutional process.
- **Railways of consent.** Links development spending to regional approval and corridor delivery.
- **The army of several traditions.** Integrates regiments and police without letting one region monopolise command.
- **A continental mandate must be earned.** Requires successful domestic federal or confederal practice before rapid external integration unlocks.

### Route mutations

Federalism is Nigeria’s strongest peaceful route, but it must decide revenue, regional veto, and civil-service balance. The republic route can mobilise urban parties, labour, students, and the press, yet it risks becoming a southern or Lagos project. The crowns route becomes a council of emirs, obas, and regional institutions, with a severe precedence problem. The people’s union route links railway labour, farmers, market organisations, and anti-concession policy, but it must avoid treating the north as a passive rural bloc. Military continentalism has major manpower potential and the highest coup and regional-fragmentation risk. Confederation offers a credible solution if the host accepts limited direct control.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

A coercive Nigeria can produce a West African coastal coalition, a Sahel emirate bloc, or internal regional secessions. Strong restored polities such as Sokoto, Oyo, Edo, Kanem-Bornu, and coastal Niger Delta identities should not all become independent at once, but each can appear as a constitutional claimant, autonomous member, or rival when the host mishandles its settlement.

### Military and economy

Nigeria’s strengths are population, agriculture, ports, rail, and regional military traditions. Its weaknesses are distance, uneven infrastructure, local command, and the danger of resource extraction without regional benefit. The economy lane should create north-south and east-west corridors, local processing, ports, public works, and regional development formulas.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Nigerian host can establish a continental revenue-sharing chamber, regional civil-service quota system, or multi-capital administrative model. These institutions should be earned by solving the opening rather than granted for free.

### High-chaos gate

Oyo, Benin, Nok, forest, river, or ancient-host material must remain separated from real political identities and require explicit supernatural reveal conditions.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Nigeria chooses one domestic settlement before external acceleration. It should not form the Charter League and begin coercive integration while major regions are in boycott or revolt. The overlay uses regional blocs and scripted localisation rather than dozens of country-specific duplicate focus nodes.

## Gold Coast

### Starting tension

The Gold Coast begins with a profitable cocoa and mineral economy, coastal political institutions, powerful traditional states, northern territories, and an administrative order that channels African representation through a narrow structure. Its continental claim can grow from finance, transport, and political organisation, but it must settle the relationship among the colony, Asante, the Northern Territories, and Togoland.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is prosperity without sovereign control. It tracks cocoa producer confidence, traditional authority, urban representation, mining concessions, railway and port ownership, northern inclusion, and the treatment of Asante. It can mature into a federal republic, a mass continental republic, an Asante-linked council of crowns, a producer and labour union, a military-commercial state, or a confederation of regional governments.

### First proof mission

Use cocoa revenue, rail, and a port to deliver a complete aid package while avoiding a producer crisis or foreign commercial veto. Full success also secures consent from Asante and one northern authority. Partial success sends the aid but deepens one domestic grievance.

### Host focus modules

- **Cocoa under African terms.** Creates producer boards, price defence, and local processing choices.
- **The colony and Asante.** Establishes federal, royal, republican, or autonomy settlements.
- **Northern roads before northern votes.** Forces the player to build representation and infrastructure together.
- **Takoradi and the continental trade.** Turns the port and rail network into League logistics.
- **Togoland’s choice.** Uses plebiscite, federation, autonomy, or protected membership instead of automatic annexation.

### Route mutations

Federalism combines regional chambers with producer revenue sharing. The republic route uses coastal politics and mass organisation but must win Asante and northern confidence. The crowns route can place Asante at the centre of a plural council without erasing other authorities. The people’s union route links farmers, miners, and railway labour against concession interests. Military continentalism has good logistics but limited justification unless foreign intervention is active. Confederation can use the Gold Coast as a commercial and administrative hub.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Asante can become a loyal anchor, autonomous partner, or major rival. Northern authorities may reject coastal fiscal dominance. French and British neighbouring administrations can close routes. A producer crisis can weaken the host faster than a battlefield defeat.

### Military and economy

The host begins with transport, ports, export revenue, and recruitable colonial forces, but not an independent war machine. The economy path should convert export wealth into local processing, public works, schools, and regional corridors. It should force a choice between rapid military spending and a durable producer settlement.

### Post-unification signature

A successful Gold Coast host can create a continental commodity-stabilisation fund, producer chamber, or shared port and railway authority with enforceable member benefits.

### High-chaos gate

Forest, gold, ancestral, or living-art material requires high chaos and symbolic care. It must never turn Asante or other human identities into supernatural stereotypes.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Gold Coast prioritises revenue stability, Asante settlement, and a complete logistics proof. It favours federalism, republic, or confederation. The overlay does not create a separate commodity minigame for every export.
