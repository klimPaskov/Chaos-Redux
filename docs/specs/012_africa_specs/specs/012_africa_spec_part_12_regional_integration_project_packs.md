# 012 Africa spec part 12, regional integration project packs

This file expands the regional integration system into named requirements, staged coring missions, federal member routes, puppet routes, coercive annexation risks, and resistance outcomes. It continues prior integration philosophy and avoids instant continent-wide cores.

## Integration stages

Every region should move through staged integration. The stage should be tracked per region and, where needed, per member country.

| Stage | Working label | Purpose | Typical unlock |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stage 0 | Claim or invitation | The unifier has a public claim, invitation, or regional interest | Event opening, focus branch, or regional selector |
| Stage 1 | Protection | The unifier can defend or support local actors | League membership, colonial war, or aid mission |
| Stage 2 | Administration | Local institutions begin working with the unifier | Confidence, local support, rail or port access |
| Stage 3 | Integration project | The region runs timed missions and material projects | Focus unlock and target confidence |
| Stage 4 | Settlement | The region chooses federal, subject, or annexation outcome | Successful missions and route policy |
| Stage 5 | Core or permanent member | Region becomes stable core area or durable member | Final mission, vote, or conquest aftermath |
| Stage 6 | Post-integration maintenance | Keeps resistance and rival appeal from returning | Repeatable but capped maintenance missions |

A region should not move from Stage 0 to Stage 5 in one focus. A large region should usually need several missions and at least one visible local requirement.

## Shared requirements

Most regions should require a mix of:
- control or alliance with key capitals
- port, rail, river, or road access
- member confidence or compliance
- local support
- stable supply
- no active rival bloc control
- no active colonial owner in the region
- enough civilian or military capacity to run projects
- decisions to handle restored polities where they exist

The implementation can tune exact values, but the design needs the player to prove more than map painting.

## Federal member routes

Federal members are not instantly annexed. They can remain on the map with rights, shared decisions, and regional projects.

Federal route stages:
1. Member accepts Charter rights.
2. Member joins shared defense or service project.
3. Member confidence reaches high band.
4. Member completes regional integration missions.
5. Member votes for accession, federal membership, or associated state.
6. If accession succeeds, cores or integration benefits arrive in stages.
7. If member remains autonomous, it gains League benefits and stays a subject or faction member.

Federal members should provide:
- shared reserves
- construction slots or regional projects
- volunteer access
- legitimacy
- lower resistance
- achievement hooks

They should cost:
- time
- local investment
- autonomy guarantees
- reduced direct resource extraction
- risk of delay or refusal

## Puppet and associated-state routes

Puppet routes should be viable but less stable than voluntary federation.

Associated state:
- target keeps high autonomy
- unifier gains military access and aid rights
- no instant core
- low resistance
- slower integration

Protectorate:
- target becomes subject under pressure
- unifier gains more control
- resistance and rival appeal rise
- outside powers may react

Resource protectorate:
- applies to mineral or port zones
- should be dangerous if abused
- increases extraction but lowers confidence
- can trigger strikes or forest backlash

## Coercive annexation route

Coercive annexation should be strong but costly. It should be available mostly through Continental Command, absolute Crown, hardline Revolutionary, or high-chaos routes.

Requirements:
- high unifier influence
- low target confidence or target refusal
- military superiority
- route permission
- regional missions prepared
- no active strong outside guarantee unless player accepts war risk

Costs:
- stability
- equipment
- command power within caps
- manpower or occupation burden
- League cohesion
- local support
- member confidence across nearby regions

Risks:
- regional revolt
- rival bloc formation
- outside ultimatum
- sabotage
- delayed cores
- high resistance
- high-chaos blowback in forest, Nile, or heritage regions

Coercive annexation must never grant a clean full region core instantly. It should grant claims, occupation tools, and later coring missions under harsher conditions.

## Resistance outcomes

Resistance outcomes should be varied and tied to what went wrong.

| Cause | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Failed protection | Member exit, rival appeal, trust collapse |
| Coercive annexation | Revolt mission, sabotage, foreign warning |
| Resource extraction abuse | Strike, mine sabotage, forest backlash |
| Port overuse or diaspora overcrowding | Dock strike, housing unrest, local support loss |
| Heritage disrespect | Restoration revolt, Crown legitimacy loss, Sacred Soil backlash |
| Military governors | Underground resistance, international pressure, Federal route lockout |
| Ignored autonomy | Rival bloc vote, federal mission failure |
| High-chaos disaster abuse | Blowback, member panic, weather or disease pressure against unifier |

Resistance should be recoverable through missions unless the player repeatedly escalates.

## Staged coring missions

A large region should normally need four to six mission types before stable cores.

Example mission family:
1. Secure administrative capital.
2. Restore rail or port route.
3. Deliver civil supplies.
4. Settle local autonomy or restored polity.
5. Reduce resistance below threshold.
6. Ratify regional settlement.

Success effects:
- lower resistance
- raise local support
- raise confidence
- unlock partial cores or state modifiers
- reduce autonomy demand

Failure effects:
- raise autonomy demand
- raise rival appeal
- spawn resistance mission
- delay coring
- reduce League cohesion
- create foreign reaction if outside power is involved

## Route-specific integration differences

| Route | Integration style |
| --- | --- |
| Federal Charter | Slow, stable, confidence-heavy, best voluntary coring |
| Revolutionary Congress | Fast against colonizers, ideological pressure against rivals, labour missions |
| Crown of the Continent | Strong restored polity support, succession disputes, oaths |
| Continental Command | Fast conquest and defense, high resistance, military governors |
| Sacred Soil | Strong local support and rural legitimacy, slower industry gains |
| Black Star Return | Strong port, city, and industry integration, housing and convoy risks |
| Deep Green Covenant | Nonstandard integration, habitat bargains, disaster pressure, many normal regions resist |

## Cleanup and display

The integration display should show:
- region name
- current stage
- local support
- resistance or autonomy demand
- member confidence where applicable
- next required mission
- blocked reason if the region cannot advance
- route-specific risk

Cleanup should remove missions and stage flags when:
- region becomes core
- region becomes permanent federal member
- target country dies
- unifier loses the route
- world-end branch begins
- region is controlled by a rival bloc with no active negotiations
