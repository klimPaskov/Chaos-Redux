# 012 Africa spec part 2, Charter League and decision systems

## Charter League concept

The Charter League is the first playable system. It begins as a defensive anti-colonial league and can later become a continental pressure network. The League should not start as an annexation faction. It should protect African countries, support anti-colonial wars, bargain with members, and only later press members toward federation, puppet status, or absorption.

The League should be a faction or faction-like structure with additional mechanics. It needs membership rules, refusal logic, member confidence, leadership contests, and exit routes. A member can support unity, bargain for autonomy, accept protectorate status, create a regional bloc, or leave and become a rival.

## League member states

Every African country should track a relation to the Africa unifier when the event is active.

| State | Meaning | Typical actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outside | No formal connection | diplomacy, propaganda, guarantees, rival influence |
| Protected | The unifier promises defense | defensive aid, border missions, intervention rights |
| League member | Full Charter League participant | shared reserves, votes, influence, cohesion effects |
| League associate | Loose member with opt-outs | aid and trade without integration votes |
| Regional subject | Old polity or local state under unifier protection | local units, autonomy missions, later integration |
| Federated member | High-trust member accepting partial integration | shared focus benefits, staged cores, local autonomy |
| Puppet member | Coercive route result | resistance, low cohesion, faster control but higher panic |
| Rival bloc | Breakaway anti-unifier coalition | diplomatic crisis, possible war, counter-claims |
| Integrated | Fully absorbed after missions | cores or high-compliance states, reduced local resistance |

## Decision category families

### Found the Charter League

This early decision family opens the League, invites nearby African countries, and gives the player public tools. It should cost legitimacy work, political capital, and visible material effort. It should not be a single political power click.

Actions:

- call a founding congress in the capital or a symbolic African city
- send envoys to nearby countries
- offer anti-colonial guarantees
- publish a charter with route-specific ideological language
- create the first shared reserve pool
- choose whether the first charter promises federation, confederation, liberation, empire, socialism, monarchy, or high-chaos destiny

### Defend African countries

This family appears when an African country is attacked by a colonial or outside power. The unifier can spend equipment, convoys, trains, volunteers, army XP, and diplomatic credibility to intervene.

Actions:

- open an arms corridor
- send officer cadres
- send volunteer formations
- guarantee the capital
- create an emergency air bridge if airports or ports exist
- demand that the coloniser withdraw
- join the war after a timed preparation mission

Success raises legitimacy and cohesion. Failure raises colonial panic, lowers member confidence, and may push the target into a rival bloc.

### League aid and influence

This family is the main peaceful route to unity. Aid actions should improve target trust but can create dependence and later integration pressure.

Actions:

- build rail and port links
- fund local factories
- send medical and food convoys
- train local defense forces
- help clear resistance after liberation
- sponsor radio and school networks
- share resource prospecting teams
- offer diaspora settlement grants

Each action should have a target and a visible cost. Strong countries should accept aid less easily if it threatens their independence. AI acceptance should depend on war state, stability, ideology, threat, relation, and whether the unifier has defended the target.

### Integration and federation

Integration opens only after the League has enough legitimacy or the unifier controls large regions. It should use staged missions.

Integration stages:

1. recognition of continental leadership
2. joint customs and rail planning
3. military command coordination
4. local autonomy charter
5. regional federation vote
6. state integration missions
7. final absorption or federated member status

Not every target should follow the same path. A friendly small member can federate quickly. A proud regional power needs a longer path. A recently conquered enemy should need compliance, reconstruction, garrison control, and local legitimacy.

### Regional subject restoration

The unifier can revive old African polities as subjects, League members, or local protectorates. These restored states give regional identity and local army support. They should not all be instantly annexed.

Uses:

- release regional allies from colonial land
- create buffer states around contested borders
- revive old royal or republican names for local legitimacy
- give the player a local partner instead of direct occupation
- create later integration choices

Each restoration should have a direct country name. The restoration decision can use institutional wording, but the map name stays direct.

### Rival bloc pressure

Stronger African countries can resist the unifier. They may create regional blocs if integration pressure rises too fast or if the unifier behaves like a conqueror.

Rival bloc triggers:

- low League cohesion
- high integration pressure
- coercive puppet route
- unifier loses anti-colonial wars
- ideological mismatch
- major country strength above threshold
- outside power sponsorship
- high local legitimacy in the target country

Rival blocs should not always become enemies immediately. They can bargain, demand autonomy, ask for League reforms, leave the faction, create a parallel League, or declare war.

### Colonial panic and outside reaction

Outside powers with African interests should react through escalating tools.

Panic stages:

1. diplomatic complaints and guarantees
2. sanctions and trade disruption
3. covert aid to rival blocs
4. expeditionary plans and port seizures
5. ultimata against League members
6. a Scramble for Africa crisis after continental unification

Panic should be strongest for powers that hold African states, border African waters, rely on African resources, or have colonial allies.

## Decision pacing

The category must hide obsolete actions. Early play shows invitations, protection, and aid. Mid play adds integration, regional restorations, and anti-colonial war planning. Late play adds continent-wide settlement, resource control, external ultimata, and post-unification pressure. High-chaos play adds oracle pacts, impossible weather threats, animal and supernatural actors, and disease or disaster mechanics at game abstraction level.

The player should not see a wall of every African country at once. Use regional selectors, target focus, or phased pools. A selected-target pattern is preferred for human clarity. AI should still evaluate all valid targets through hidden or AI-only visibility.

## Costs and risks

Costs should match the action. Examples:

| Action type | Better costs |
| --- | --- |
| arms corridor | infantry equipment, support equipment, trucks, convoys, rail or port access |
| officer mission | army XP, command power, trained manpower, target approval |
| rail project | civilian factory burden, trains, steel or industry capacity, state control |
| local defense force | infantry equipment, manpower, local support, training time |
| integration vote | legitimacy, cohesion, stability, target trust, high compliance |
| coercive annexation | command power, garrison strain, stability loss, resistance growth |
| diaspora return route | convoys, consumer goods burden, port access, housing projects, time |
| anti-colonial ultimatum | war support, military readiness, border control, diplomatic cost |

Political power can appear where diplomacy or bureaucracy matters, but it should not dominate the category.

## Success, failure, and partial outcomes

Every major League mission needs success and failure logic.

- A successful defense should raise legitimacy, League cohesion, target trust, and volunteer willingness.
- A failed defense should raise colonial panic, lower League cohesion, and make rival blocs more likely.
- A successful integration mission should unlock federation, cores, claims, or subject status depending on route.
- A failed integration mission should create local resistance, regional autonomy demands, or external sponsorship chances.
- A heavy-handed success can integrate land faster while increasing future resistance and panic.

## AI behavior

AI unifiers should prefer peaceful League building early unless they are high-chaos, fascist, desperate, or already at war. Democratic and nonaligned AI should value protectorates, League cohesion, and gradual federation. Communist AI should prefer solidarity committees, worker aid, and revolutionary federation. Fascist AI should lean toward command integration, war planning, and coercive protectorates. High-chaos AI can choose strange routes only when chaos and evolution gates allow it.

AI African targets should accept protection when threatened, accept aid when weak, resist integration when strong, and leave when the unifier becomes predatory. AI colonial powers should react hardest when their African holdings are threatened and less when the unifier only helps local countries defend themselves.
