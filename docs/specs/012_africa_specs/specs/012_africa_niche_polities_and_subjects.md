# Event 012 Africa — Niche polity and subject expansion spec

This file expands the Africa event package with a much deeper subject and regional-authority layer. It is accepted source design for the Event 012 Africa spec pack. It replaces the old idea that Africa only releases a handful of broad regional subjects. The unifier should instead interact with a living continent of restored courts, old trade cities, frontier councils, religious/legal authorities, island compacts, and later high-chaos nonhuman/supernatural actors.

The goal is not to make the unifier instantly annex everything. The goal is to make the continent feel like it is being assembled through promises, bargaining, protection, intimidation, memory, faction pressure, and increasingly absurd chaos.

## How the expanded subject layer works

The selected African unifier receives a **Charter League** system. At first it creates modern anti-colonial leagues and emergency regional administrations. As the event escalates, the unifier can sponsor **legacy authorities**. These authorities are event-created subjects or faction members that claim legitimacy from old courts, kingdoms, sultanates, trade routes, city networks, religious orders, or local confederations.

Legacy authorities are not normal releasables. They have four possible statuses:

| Status | Meaning | Gameplay role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Observer Authority** | The name and council exist as a diplomatic office, but no country tag has been released. | Gives small local support, reveals decisions, and creates route flavour without map fragmentation. |
| **Charter Subject** | The authority is released or transferred as a subject/faction member with limited land. | Defends local territory, provides regional decisions, may help anti-colonial wars, and can later integrate. |
| **Regional Partner** | The authority remains independent or semi-independent inside the Charter League. | Stronger armies, more autonomy, can resist annexation, can threaten to leave if cohesion collapses. |
| **Covenant Actor** | Higher-chaos supernatural or nonhuman version. | Explicitly nonhuman/supernatural, has extreme mechanics, and must be classified outside normal country systems. |

The implementation should not create all tags at once. It should use phased reveal:

1. **Baseline:** broad regional authorities and anti-colonial rescue league.
2. **Evolution I:** legacy authorities become visible as observer councils and subject candidates.
3. **Evolution II:** selected legacy authorities unlock stronger units, unique regional missions, and weird but still mostly grounded military logistics.
4. **Evolution III:** nonhuman and supernatural Covenant actors can appear in relevant terrain and route states.
5. **Evolution IV:** the unifier can bind human authorities, nonhuman actors, and continent-sponsor movements into a world-chaos bloc.

## Legacy authority creation rules

A legacy authority should appear when at least one of these is true:

- The unifier controls or liberates the authority's home region.
- A colonial power is at war with an African country in the authority's region.
- A nearby Charter League member asks for help.
- A focus in the regional authority lane unlocks the authority.
- A decision spends legitimacy, equipment, administrators, and local support to found the authority.
- A high-chaos evolution creates a mythic or nonhuman reflection of the authority.

The player should usually choose between three settlement styles:

| Settlement style | Effect | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Release the old office** | Create/release the authority as a subject or League partner. | More local legitimacy and units, slower direct integration. |
| **Keep as a Charter bureau** | Keep territory under the unifier and create an observer authority only. | Faster central control, lower local trust, more Paper-Core Burden. |
| **Merge into a regional governorate** | Integrate the authority into a broad regional subject such as West Africa, Nile Authority, Great Lakes League, or Swahili Coast. | Less tag clutter, but stronger old courts may later resist. |

Strong authorities should never be free annexation buttons. Their integration should require local support, controlled capitals, connected supply, legitimacy, low resistance, and a cooling period after liberation or war.

## Expanded historical and legacy subject packages

### 1. Jolof-Wolof Federation

**Role:** western Senegal and lower Senegal River legitimacy office.

**Historical anchor:** the Wolof/Jolof empire provides a strong regional federation memory for the western Sahel and Atlantic coast.

**Gameplay package:**

- Can appear as an observer authority after the unifier reaches Senegal/Gambia or liberates the western coast.
- Can become a Charter Subject if colonial pressure is high or if the unifier uses the **Call the River Federations** focus.
- Units: river guards, coastal militia, light infantry with low supply use, cavalry/scout detachments.
- Decisions: mediate Cayor, Walo, Baol, Sine, and Salum memories as legitimacy projects rather than separate mandatory tags.
- Integration problem: coastal elites prefer trade autonomy; inland councils fear being swallowed by the unifier.
- High-chaos variant: **The Griot Mandate**, where oral-history decisions forecast enemy troop movement and reveal false treaties. This is a human cultural/symbolic route, not a supernatural tag unless paired with Ananse at Evolution III.

### 2. Mossi Kingdoms

**Role:** inland Volta defensive federation.

**Gameplay package:**

- Revealed when the unifier controls Upper Volta/Burkina Faso region or a nearby colonial war starts.
- Can become a hardy defensive subject that specializes in forts, anti-encirclement, cavalry, and militia conversion.
- Focus lane: **The Headwater Forts**, **Yatenga Riders**, **Wagadugu Arbitration**, **The Shield Does Not Bend**.
- Decisions require infantry equipment, horses/cavalry template unlocks where possible, local support, and supply control.
- Integration resistance: prefers league federation over direct annexation; high Authority but low League Cohesion can make it leave the faction.
- Evolution II unlock: **Dust Cavalry Columns**, stronger mobile irregulars for Sahel fronts.

### 3. Hausa Bakwai and Market Cities League

**Role:** northern Nigerian/Sahel urban commercial authority.

**Gameplay package:**

- Works best as an observer office unless Nigeria/northern Nigeria is contested.
- Brings market-tax decisions, rail hub repair, convoy-equivalent overland trade, and legitimacy from city councils.
- Can split into a peaceful trade route or militarized emirate route.
- Interacts with Bornu, Sokoto, and Oyo routes through rival influence.
- High-chaos variant: **The Seven Markets That Move**, a scripted-GUI target network where markets appear as movable cards; completing convoy/rail missions causes supply bonuses to jump to the threatened front.

### 4. Kanem-Bornu

**Role:** Lake Chad authority and desert-water route.

**Gameplay package:**

- Appears around Lake Chad, Chad, Niger, northeastern Nigeria, and Cameroon borderlands.
- Gives water-control missions, desert logistics, cavalry detachments, and anti-raider patrols.
- Can mediate or rival Baguirmi and Wadai. If mishandled, the Lake Chad authorities can resist central integration.
- Decisions: **Guard the Lake Roads**, **Recruit the Basin Scouts**, **Survey the Wells**, **Break the Desert Encirclement**.
- Evolution II unlock: desert-fog reconnaissance, better supply grace, reduced attrition.
- Evolution III unlock: can invite **The Oasis Witnesses**, a supernatural non-state adviser network that predicts drought/famine missions.

### 5. Baguirmi-Wadai

**Role:** central Sahel buffer between Lake Chad, Sudan, and equatorial approaches.

**Gameplay package:**

- Can appear as a hard-border subject when the Charter League is stretched between western and Nile campaigns.
- Specializes in defensive buffers, border raids, and caravan escort missions.
- Can be loyal if Bornu and Sennar routes are both respected; can revolt if one side is favoured too strongly.
- Focus groups: **Between Two Empires**, **March Courts**, **Caravan Hostages**, **The Red Sand Oath**.

### 6. Futa Jallon and Futa Toro Reform Councils

**Role:** western Islamic reformist/legalist authority and mountain-river council.

**Gameplay package:**

- Should be implemented carefully with sourced localisation. If final naming is uncertain, use generic **Futa Reform Council** until source review.
- Provides legal legitimacy, mountain defense, local arbitration, and anti-bandit missions.
- Can join democratic/legalist, socialist, or religious-law routes depending unifier politics.
- Decisions should cost legitimacy, infantry equipment, local support, and stability rather than only political power.
- High-chaos variant should remain symbolic/legal, not demonized. Avoid making Islamic identity the “weird” part.

### 7. Segu-Bambara

**Role:** Niger River militarized production and infantry authority.

**Gameplay package:**

- Appears around Mali/Niger river corridor when the unifier needs a military-industrial subject but does not want direct annexation.
- Unlocks river depots, infantry equipment conversion, light artillery stockpile decisions, and river-fort missions.
- Can cooperate with Songhai or compete over old imperial legitimacy.

### 8. Songhai of the Western Roads

**Role:** high-prestige western imperial memory route.

**Gameplay package:**

- Should usually begin as an observer authority rather than a released country unless the unifier controls Gao/Timbuktu/Songhai region.
- Focuses: **Open the Archive**, **Survey the Salt Roads**, **Timbuktu Scholars**, **The Western Ledger**, **Imperial Name Without Imperial Chains**.
- Gives research bonuses, supply mapping, legitimacy, and diplomatic prestige.
- Dangerous branch: if the unifier is authoritarian and Authority outruns League Cohesion, the Archive can justify forced integration and create strong resistance.
- Evolution IV: can become one of the historical pillars for a pan-continental title, not a separate world-conqueror.

### 9. Oyo

**Role:** Yoruba/savanna cavalry and administrative authority.

**Gameplay package:**

- Appears in southwestern Nigeria/Benin region.
- Balances with Benin guilds, Dahomey guard, and Orisha high-chaos routes.
- Units: cavalry/scout detachments, later motorized cavalry if industry route supports it.
- Decisions: **Reopen the Road to the North**, **Summon the Cavalry Houses**, **Negotiate with the Forest Courts**.
- High-chaos bridge: Oyo can unlock the **Thunder Council Liaison** if Shango/Orisha path opens, but it remains human unless the Orisha Court itself appears separately.

### 10. Benin / Edo

**Role:** forest-region artisan, fortification, and court legitimacy package.

**Gameplay package:**

- Focuses on artisans, bronze/guild iconography, forts, urban administration, and propaganda art.
- Decisions can create national-spirit upgrades rather than free factories: artisan workshops improve equipment reliability, fort repairs, and local resistance reduction.
- Integration path should require respecting guild autonomy or paying heavy administrative cost.
- Asset needs: sourced historical-symbol guidance for Benin motifs; generated icons can use abstract guild/bronze motifs without copying sensitive artifacts as generic loot.

### 11. Dahomey

**Role:** disciplined southern Benin guard subject and coastal pressure actor.

**Gameplay package:**

- Can become a military subject if coastal West Africa is under colonial pressure.
- Units: palace guard infantry, coastal fort detachments, female-presenting fictional commander option if sourced/fictional rules are followed correctly.
- Route choices: **Guard the Charter**, **Abomey Command**, **Coastal Reckoning**, **Never Again the Slave Ports**.
- Decisions should confront coastal colonial infrastructure and slavery-memory politics without turning the subject into a flat conquest machine.
- High-chaos branch: **The Palaces Remember**, a symbolic route where old palace murals become warning/event images and unlock defensive prophecy; treat as supernatural only at Evolution III.

### 12. Asante

**Role:** Akan/Gold Coast diplomatic and legitimacy authority.

**Gameplay package:**

- Usually starts as a diplomatic embassy rather than a normal subject.
- Brings gold, diplomacy, legitimacy, and council/king tension.
- Decisions: **Protect the Stool Embassy**, **Gold Coast Arbitration**, **Call the Forest Roads**, **Refuse the Colonial Ledger**.
- Can mediate other West African subjects; if disrespected, reduces League Cohesion sharply.
- High-chaos bridge: may trigger Ananse Web cooperation, but the embassy remains human.

### 13. Kongo

**Role:** western-central Christian/diplomatic/coastal authority.

**Gameplay package:**

- Appears in Angola/Congo regions after liberation or anti-colonial war.
- Gives diplomacy, coastal trade, and missionary/kingdom-memory events.
- Can compete with Ndongo-Matamba and Loango; the unifier must decide whether to create one Kongo authority or several central African subjects.

### 14. Ndongo-Matamba

**Role:** Angolan inland resistance and mobile-war subject.

**Gameplay package:**

- A compact subject that specializes in guerrilla, mobile infantry, and anti-Portuguese/anti-colonial wars.
- Works as Kongo’s rival or ally depending settlement.
- Decisions require arms, training cadres, and safe corridors.
- Can unlock a rare **Queen’s War-Council** fictional leadership route. If a real historical leader inspiration is used, source portrait/name carefully; otherwise use fictional council portrait.

### 15. Loango

**Role:** central-western coast trade/port authority.

**Gameplay package:**

- Coastal convoy, dockyard, and port-control subject.

### 16. Luba

## Tiered subject priority for implementation

The full design can support many authorities, but implementation should not hide shallow placeholder packages behind a large list. If implementation must prioritize, use these tiers:

| Priority | Must include | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — Core coverage** | Jolof/Wolof, Mossi, Songhai, Oyo, Benin, Dahomey, Asante, Kongo, Luba, Lunda, Kush-Meroe, Aksum, Ajuran, Swahili Coast, Buganda, Great Zimbabwe/Stone Cities, Barotse, Merina | Ensures every major region has at least one distinctive legacy authority. |
| **B — Deep regional texture** | Hausa, Bornu, Baguirmi-Wadai, Futa, Segu-Bambara, Ndongo-Matamba, Loango, Kuba, Makuria-Alodia, Sennar, Adal-Harar, Bunyoro, Great Lakes Highlands, Nyamwezi, Maravi, Sakalava/Betsimisaraka, Comorian Passage, Nama-Herero-Damara, Khoe-San Tracks | Adds replayability and local route conflicts. |
| **C — Optional high-density map** | Additional city offices and sub-authorities inside the above packages | Use observer offices or GUI cards instead of tags if performance or readability suffers. |

Do not implement Priority C until Priority A and B packages have real decisions, names, localised descriptions, AI behavior, and assets.

## Integration and resistance behavior

Each legacy authority should have an **Integration Temperature** from -100 to +100. It can be represented as a variable, hidden target value, or dynamic scripted calculation.

Positive factors:

- Unifier liberated the authority’s home region.
- Unifier defended it from a colonizer.
- Unifier spent equipment, administrators, and local support.
- Unifier chose federal or negotiated paths.
- The authority’s rival received fair treatment.
- Supply lines and capitals were protected.
- The unifier has high Legitimacy and League Cohesion.

Negative factors:

- Unifier conquered the region directly.
- Forced integration decisions were used.
- Authority’s home capital is unsupplied or occupied.
- Rival authority was privileged.
- Paper-Core Burden is high.
- Colonial Alarm is high and the unifier cannot defend the region.
- Authority has strong identity and the unifier’s route is highly centralist.

At high positive temperature, the authority offers integration missions, special units, and leader/advisor candidates. At low temperature, it refuses annexation, leaves the faction, invites a rival patron, or declares a defensive war.

## Focus-tree integration hooks

The main Africa focus tree should gain a **Legacy Authority Lane** after early Charter League focuses.

Anchor focus groups:

- **Inventory the Old Thrones:** reveals observer authorities in controlled/liberated regions.
- **Charter the First Legacy Office:** unlocks the first chosen authority as a subject or office.
- **The League Must Not Become a Museum:** keeps modern pan-African legitimacy high and prevents old offices from dominating.
- **The Museum Must Not Become a Prison:** prevents centralist routes from deleting old offices too quickly.
- **Councils of the Western Roads:** unlocks West/Sahel authorities.
- **Nile and Stelae Compacts:** unlocks Nile/Horn authorities.
- **Forest and River Crowns:** unlocks Central/Great Lakes authorities.
- **The Mint Cities and Islands:** unlocks Swahili/Indian Ocean/Madagascar authorities.
- **Southern Memory Courts:** unlocks southern and plateau authorities.
- **The Continental Register:** late focus that lists every authority’s temperature and grants integration/defense decisions.

Political route changes:

| Africa route | Authority behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Federal Congress | Authorities prefer observer/subject status and integrate slowly but peacefully. |
| People’s Liberation | Authorities become local liberation councils; monarchic/court language is toned down or transformed into people’s committees. |
| General Staff | Authorities are militarized as district commands; faster integration but higher resistance and League Cohesion losses. |
| Crown Congress | Court and title authorities become stronger; republican/socialist authorities become suspicious. |
| Green Covenant | Historical authorities coexist with nature/spirit/nonhuman actors at high chaos; normal legitimacy may collapse if Covenant Pressure rises too high. |

## Decision families added by the subject expansion

### Authority recognition decisions

- **Open an Observer:** create an observer authority in a controlled/liberated region. Costs legitimacy, administrators, local support, and sometimes convoys/trains.
- **Release a Charter Subject:** release/transfer limited states to the authority. Costs equipment and cohesion, reduces Paper-Core Burden, increases local trust.
- **Merge into Regional Governorate:** fold the authority into broad subject. Lowers tag count and integration burden but risks future resentment.
- **Dismiss the Claim:** centralist choice; reduces authority clutter but raises resistance and can empower high-chaos distortions later.

### Authority missions

- **Guard the Old Capital:** place supplied divisions in named capital region for 120–180 days.
- **Repair the Charter Road:** build/repair infrastructure/rail/ports in the authority’s region.
- **Hold the Council:** keep stability, legitimacy, and local support above thresholds while not at risk of conquest.
- **Defend the Authority:** join war or send volunteers/equipment to a member under attack.
- **Integrate the Office:** after trust is high, spend administrators/equipment and complete a state-control mission to convert claims/paper cores into real cores.

### Rivalry missions

Some authority pairs should create rivalry events:

- Buganda vs Bunyoro.
- Kongo vs Ndongo-Matamba vs Loango.
- Oyo vs Dahomey vs Benin.
- Aksum vs Adal/Harar vs Ajuran.
- Swahili Coast vs Merina/Sakalava island claims.
- Songhai vs Bornu/Baguirmi-Wadai desert legitimacy.

Rivalry should not always mean war. It should create arbitration, concessions, local autonomy, or prestige contests. War only happens when League Cohion collapses or the unifier abuses centralization.

## AI behavior for expanded subjects

- AI unifier should release fewer, stronger authorities than a player unless chaos is high.
- AI should prefer observer offices when fronts are overloaded or industry is weak.
- AI should release Charter Subjects when colonial wars threaten African states or when Paper-Core Burden is high.
- AI should avoid Green Covenant/nonhuman routes unless chaos tier and route flags allow it.

## Asset needs added by this expansion

Each priority A authority needs:

- one 64x64 idea/national-spirit icon direction;
- one 32x32 decision icon direction;
- one focus icon family direction for the regional lane;
- a flag/cosmetic identity plan if it can become a subject;
- leader/council portrait direction, usually generated fictional/institutional unless a real leader is explicitly sourced;
- a small regional emblem or seal if used in the scripted GUI.

Priority B authorities can share regional icon families but still need distinct names, descriptions, and at least one unique asset motif.

No generated real historical flags or real leader portraits. Historical symbols and flags require source review. Fictional route flags, council portraits, supernatural leaders, and nonhuman actors may use generated art.
