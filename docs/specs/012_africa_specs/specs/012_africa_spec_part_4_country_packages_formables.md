# Event 12 Africa specification, part 4

## Country packages, restored polities, formables, names, leaders, and starting forces

Country names in this file are direct public names in the current package. Internal route labels and technical slugs remain separate from final localisation. Historical identities must still be reviewed against the final map, surviving institutions, local claims, and tag availability during the release audit.

## Country package philosophy

Event 12 can affect nearly every African country and can create many restored or revived identities. The package must therefore distinguish between three levels of country content.

### Tier A: major restored or regional anchor package

A Tier A country can survive for a long campaign and may lead a region, oppose the unifier, inherit the continental project, or remain an important federal member. It needs:

- stable tag or cosmetic-tag plan
- direct public names and ideology variants
- base and route flags
- one adult male ruler package, with distinct adult male advisors and commanders only where the polity exposes those roles
- starting politics and parties
- starting ideas with lifecycles
- dynamic starting forces
- reinforcement path
- regional focus overlay or compact bespoke tree
- decisions and missions
- AI route behavior
- League relationship logic
- integration and refusal logic
- assets and localisation direction

### Tier B: regional member package

A Tier B country is a playable or persistent member with a clear identity and regional role. It needs:

- tag and public names
- leader and flag
- starting political setup
- two to four meaningful ideas or staged modifiers
- dynamic starting forces when expected to fight
- a shared regional tree with country-specific localisation, decisions, and AI
- one distinct mechanic or decision family
- League and integration logic

### Tier C: rare restoration, local autonomy, or short-lived identity

A Tier C identity appears through a rare route, a regional congress, a border settlement, or a high-chaos outcome. It can use a smaller package only when its expected life and role are limited. It still needs a flag, leader or institutional portrait, basic politics, safe map setup, AI, units if it fights, and a clear path into a larger member or federal subject.

No identity is complete because it has only a tag and flag.

## Host country package

The selected host retains its original political and regional identity during the opening. It receives a continental overlay rather than a complete generic replacement.

### Host package layers

1. **Original layer.** Existing country history, meaningful leaders, parties, and base identity.
2. **Continental project layer.** New focus branches, decisions, ideas, faction tools, and cosmetic presentation.
3. **Regional overlay.** State targets, restored polities, military traditions, corridor priorities, advisors, and AI.
4. **Constitution layer.** Route-specific leader, party, name, flag, government, and subject rules.
5. **Unification layer.** Final Africa identity after all regions are settled.
6. **High-chaos layer.** Optional transformation that replaces or corrupts ordinary institutions.

The host should not lose a strong existing focus tree unless a verified additive solution is impossible. Event-created or generic-tree hosts may receive the full shared tree directly. Existing major countries should receive the shared branches or a controlled runtime tree replacement only when the event owns the transformation.

## Public naming standard

Country names must remain direct and readable.

Allowed structure:

- `Asante`
- `Kingdom of Asante`
- `Asante Republic`
- `Asante Commune`
- `Kongo`
- `Kongo Federation`
- `Sultanate of Kilwa`
- `Aksum`
- `Nubia`

Disallowed public structure:

- `Asante Military Office`
- `Kongo Integration Authority`
- `Nubian Development Board`
- `Kilwa Continental Mission`

Those terms can name internal institutions, decisions, councils, commands, or focus groups.

### Ideology name pattern

Each package should define only variants that fit.

- neutral or traditional: direct name, Kingdom, Sultanate, Emirate, or Confederation
- democratic: direct name, Republic, Federation, or Union
- communist: direct name, Commune, Union, or People’s Republic when culturally and politically suitable
- fascist or military: direct name, Empire, State, or route-specific direct form
- high-chaos: direct supernatural identity, not a bureaucratic title

The matrix should avoid inventing a monarchy, sultanate, or ethnic state where the history and route do not support it.

## Restoration legitimacy

A historical name alone is not enough to create a country. Every restoration candidate receives a Restoration Legitimacy score built from:

- control of a historical or symbolic capital
- local support
- surviving dynasty, court, institution, title, movement, or cultural center
- route compatibility
- regional congress approval
- absence of a stronger overlapping claim
- support from the host or local member
- historical research confidence
- foreign opposition
- chaos tier

Low legitimacy allows a cultural autonomy, local council, or cosmetic identity. Medium legitimacy allows a League member or federal subject. High legitimacy allows a major restoration package and regional leadership.

High chaos can create impossible restorations, but the event must identify them as supernatural or alternate-history transformations rather than historical certainty.

## Overlapping claims

Many historical states overlapped, changed borders, or represented different periods. The package must not release every candidate over the same states at once.

Use regional claim pools:

- one primary regional anchor can receive full state ownership
- secondary identities can receive autonomy, claims, local characters, or subregional subjects
- congress decisions settle overlapping claims
- player-facing tooltips name the affected region
- direct annexation cannot duplicate states or create invalid capitals
- AI must not pursue a restoration whose required capital or state set is impossible

Examples:

- Kush, Napata, Meroe, Nobatia, Makuria, Alodia, and Nubia form a Nile restoration web. They are not seven simultaneous full-state releases over the same land.
- Great Zimbabwe, Mutapa, Rozwi, Butua, and Khami-related identities form a southern plateau web.
- Oyo, Ife, Edo, Dahomey, and neighboring Yoruba or coastal identities require separate claim groups.
- Mali or Manden, Songhai, Segu, Massina, Futa Toro, Futa Jallon, and Sokoto require Sahel and river-basin settlement rules.

## Starting force standard

Every country expected to fight receives dynamic forces. A restoration must never appear as an empty tag while surrounded by enemies.

### Force scale bands

#### Weak opening

- one to three small militia or guard divisions
- minimal equipment and manpower reserve
- one local commander or council leader
- strong defensive terrain bonus or temporary protection
- immediate reinforcement mission

#### Normal opening

- three to seven divisions across two template families
- limited artillery, support equipment, transport, or cavalry according to identity
- one or two commanders
- enough stockpile for a short defensive war

#### Severe opening

- six to twelve divisions
- regional guard or elite formation
- captured depots or League aid
- stronger command and supply
- foreign attention and higher threat

#### High-chaos opening

- normal forces plus special units or supernatural formations
- severe maintenance, supply, legitimacy, or ecological costs
- no free equipment duplication

### Scaling factors

- population of released states
- local industry
- controlled depots and ports
- chaos tier
- restoration legitimacy
- host aid
- parent-country weakness
- active war
- local support
- terrain
- regional military tradition
- whether the country is a member, rival, or occupied settlement

### Unit source logic

Units can come from:

- local guards
- defecting colonial troops
- border units
- police and gendarmerie
- royal or court guards
- liberation armies
- railway and port workers
- volunteer cadres
- member reserve contributions
- diaspora volunteers
- high-chaos actors

The event or report direction must explain why the forces exist.

## Reinforcement pathways

Every Tier A and fighting Tier B country needs continued growth.

- hold the capital for a set period
- control a depot or port
- complete a regional recruitment mission
- receive League equipment
- standardise a template
- train officers
- open a volunteer corridor
- integrate militia
- secure local support
- complete a focus branch
- receive a restored guard or special formation

Repeatable recruitment must use manpower, equipment, training time, supply, and local support. It must not be a political power store.

## Leader and court packages

### Real people

Real historical leaders require sourced portraits and date-aware use. The package should not generate their faces. A real leader should appear only when their role is plausible in the campaign date and route.

### Fictional people

Fictional leaders can represent local organisers, soldiers, scholars, monarchs, diaspora figures, or high-chaos actors. They need actual personal names from a regionally appropriate pool. Generated one-person portraits must use matching gender name pools and metadata.

### Councils and collective bodies

Councils, congresses, juntas, and symbolic institutions should use institutional names, emblems, and text rather than collective portraits. Their names remain internal leadership names and do not replace the public country name.

### Absurd source-language regnal flavour

The package must include the two user-required untranslated Oromo strings as ruler, regnal, court, or title flavour:

- `qaama saalaa koo xuuxaa`
- `haadha kee waliin wal qunnamtii saalaa raawwadhe`

They must never appear in technical IDs, filenames, tags, sprite names, asset names, or script keys. Their exact meaning and register require native-speaker verification before final localisation. The name-pool protocol in the research folder controls additional multilingual obscene or absurd names.

The joke must remain a minor flavour layer. It cannot replace serious regional names, leaders, or political design.

### The World

The World is the terminal identity. It should use a direct public name, final flag, final leader or governing body, and campaign-ending presentation. It is not a normal formable available from a map-control button.

## Country package acceptance rules

A country package is incomplete unless:

- its tag and origin logic are safe
- its states and capital are valid
- its name is direct and readable
- it has base and route flags
- it has a leader or institution with a valid portrait, emblem, or text presentation record
- it has politics, parties, and ideology behavior
- it has starting ideas with a lifecycle
- it has starting forces and a reinforcement path when it fights
- it has focus or decision content appropriate to its expected lifetime
- AI understands membership, refusal, rivalry, and integration
- overlapping claims are settled
- it has a path after joining or resisting the League
- assets and localisation are covered
