# 012 Africa spec part 13, diaspora lanes and settlement systems

This file expands diaspora content into shipping lanes, returnee settlement missions, industry effects, cultural diplomacy, failure states, and achievement tracking. It uses route labels only. Final player-facing text belongs to localisation and research.

## Diaspora route purpose

The diaspora route should feel like an active transoceanic logistics, citizenship, and settlement system. It should not be a flat manpower button. The player must open lanes, protect ships, prepare receiving ports, build housing and employment, and keep local support high.

Core route values:
- return momentum
- port capacity
- convoy safety
- settlement capacity
- local reception
- skilled returnee integration
- cultural diplomacy
- foreign surveillance pressure

## Shipping lane families

Shipping lanes are not all active at once. Focuses should reveal groups. Decisions should open individual lanes when ports, convoys, and diplomatic conditions are ready.

| Lane family | Working lane names | Required African ports | External origin direction | Costs and requirements | Risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| North Atlantic | Harlem to Dakar, Harlem to Monrovia, Harlem to Accra | Dakar, Monrovia, Accra, Lagos, or equivalent controlled or allied ports | Afro-American organizations, Black press, unions, churches, civic groups | Convoys, port capacity, political legitimacy, foreign surveillance handling | Surveillance, convoy losses, housing pressure |
| Caribbean | Kingston to Monrovia, Port of Spain to Accra, Bridgetown to Lagos | West African Atlantic ports | Caribbean diaspora networks | Convoys, port safety, cultural mission | Storm loss, port congestion, local labour friction |
| Brazil and South Atlantic | Salvador to Luanda, Rio to Lagos, Recife to Accra | Luanda, Lagos, Accra, or Atlantic ports | Afro-Brazilian and Lusophone networks | Convoys, diplomatic access, port housing | Portugal or Brazil diplomatic pressure, language and housing strain |
| Europe and Mediterranean | London to Freetown, Marseille to Algiers, Lisbon to Luanda | Freetown, Algiers, Luanda, Dakar | African students, seamen, workers, anti-colonial circles | Diplomatic access, surveillance mitigation, convoy safety | Colonial intelligence, refusal if colonizers still control ports |
| Indian Ocean | Zanzibar to Mombasa, Durban to Madagascar, Aden route if route allows | Mombasa, Zanzibar, Durban, Madagascar ports | Indian Ocean African diaspora and seafaring networks | Ports, convoys, Red Sea or Indian Ocean security | Naval war, island autonomy pressure |
| Internal African return | Lagos to Congo ports, Dakar to Sahel rail, Mombasa to Great Lakes | Multiple African ports and rail routes | Displaced Africans and intra-African migration | Rail, trucks, port capacity, member confidence | Regional overcrowding, local unrest |

Lane opening should require meaningful map and logistics conditions. A landlocked unifier can use lanes only through allied ports or after securing port access.

## Lane state model

Each lane has one of these states:

| State | Meaning | Player action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uncontacted | The lane is not yet organized | Complete route focus or diplomacy mission |
| Contacted | Diaspora organizers are willing to talk | Build legitimacy and port capacity |
| Open | Ships can move | Escort convoys and run settlement missions |
| Strained | Port or housing capacity is under pressure | Build housing, reduce pace, mediate disputes |
| Disrupted | Surveillance, blockade, or naval war blocks route | Clear disruption through mission |
| Established | Lane has stable regular traffic | Unlock skilled return and citizenship benefits |
| Suspended | The route failed or is temporarily unsafe | Recover through crisis mission |

## Returnee settlement missions

Returnee missions should target receiving regions, not abstract country-wide pools. Each mission should name a port, city, rail hub, farm belt, or industrial region in final implementation.

| Mission family | Requirement direction | Success | Failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Port reception | Port controlled, port capacity available, convoys assigned | Raises return momentum and local reception | Port congestion, lost momentum |
| Housing works | Civilian factories or construction capacity, local support | Raises settlement capacity and stability | Housing shortage, local reception loss |
| Workshop placement | Skilled returnee pool, military or civilian factories | Adds factory output, research bonus, or production line support | Skilled returnee frustration, lower momentum |
| Farm allotments | Rural region, food or land project, local mediation | Raises local support and manpower integration | Land dispute, rural unrest |
| Medical mission | Clinics, support equipment, stability | Raises health capacity and reduces failure risks | Disease pressure or unrest, using fictional and abstract mechanics only |
| School and press mission | Political legitimacy and cultural diplomacy | Raises long-term legitimacy and route support | Propaganda backlash or rival narrative |
| Veterans and guards | Army XP, infantry equipment, local support | Creates trained returnee guard units | Tension with local forces, equipment loss |
| Port escort | Convoys, navy or air coverage | Reduces convoy loss and surveillance pressure | Convoy losses, route disruption |

Missions should have variable duration. Easy port reception can be around the short mission band. Housing, farms, and skilled industry should take longer. Naval disruption recovery should depend on war state and convoy access.

## Industry effects

The diaspora route should create industry through missions, not instant factories from a single focus.

Industry outputs can include:
- temporary construction speed in receiving ports
- civilian factory construction after housing works
- military factory production after workshop placement
- dockyard repair or convoy production after port missions
- research speed in specific industry or electronics fields after skilled return
- medical or support equipment bonuses after health missions
- building slots in high-success settlement regions

Industry should be tied to capacity. If a player opens too many lanes and fails housing, the industrial benefit should pause or turn into unrest.

## Cultural diplomacy

Cultural diplomacy should build legitimacy and member confidence without turning into final slogans.

Decision families:
- invite diaspora congress delegates
- sponsor writers, musicians, teachers, and journalists as abstract cultural missions
- build exchange schools
- protect manuscripts, archives, churches, mosques, and community halls where relevant
- create radio and press networks
- mediate disputes between returnee and local communities
- coordinate with restored polities and local councils

Effects:
- increase local reception
- increase unifier legitimacy
- improve member confidence
- reduce rival propaganda
- reveal route-specific achievement tracking
- unlock stronger settlement missions

Risks:
- foreign surveillance
- ideological division
- propaganda backlash
- local elite resentment
- overpromising settlement capacity
- route hypocrisy if the unifier is using coercive annexation elsewhere

## Foreign surveillance and lane disruption

Foreign powers can disrupt lanes when:
- they still hold African colonies
- the unifier is at war with them
- the lane originates in their territory or port network
- the unifier has high anti-colonial threat
- the Scramble reaction is active

Disruption types:
- visa obstruction
- shipping insurance crisis
- intelligence arrests
- port denial
- propaganda attack
- naval harassment
- colonial pressure on origin community

The player should counter with:
- neutral transit agreements
- convoy escorts
- alternate ports
- cultural diplomacy
- intelligence exposure reduction
- member-port agreements
- emergency resettlement capacity

## Settlement capacity and local reception

Settlement capacity measures material readiness. Local reception measures political and social acceptance.

Capacity rises through:
- housing works
- port improvements
- farms and workshops
- local mediation
- health missions
- member confidence

Capacity falls through:
- convoy surges
- war damage
- overcrowding
- failed housing
- low stability
- sabotage

Reception rises through:
- cultural diplomacy
- local councils
- fair employment
- protection of local institutions
- federal route behavior
- successful settlement missions

Reception falls through:
- forced settlement
- housing failure
- labour conflict
- coercive integration
- ideological repression
- too many arrivals without work

Low capacity with high momentum creates overcrowding. High capacity with low reception creates political backlash. Both should be playable problems.

## Returnee guard and unit pathway

The route can create special units, but only through settlement and training.

Unit sources:
- veterans returning through lanes
- dock guards
- rail guards
- medical volunteers
- foreign-trained cadres
- port security units

Requirements:
- equipment
- local support
- port safety
- training mission
- route flags
- manpower from settled returnees, not a free infinite pool

Limits:
- active cap based on settlement capacity
- no repeated free unit loops
- units should be stronger if skilled return and training succeeded
- failures should reduce local reception or cause equipment loss

## Failure states

| Failure | Trigger direction | Consequence | Recovery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Convoy tragedy | Lane open during naval danger with poor escort | Return momentum loss, port grief, surveillance rise | Escort mission and memorial direction |
| Housing crisis | Arrivals exceed capacity | Local reception loss and settlement pause | Housing works and local mediation |
| Labour conflict | Skilled placement without local support | Industry benefit reduced | Mediation and fair employment mission |
| Foreign surveillance | Outside power pressure high | Lane disrupted and organizers at risk | Diplomatic counter or alternate origin |
| Ideological split | Route politics clash with origin networks or members | Cultural diplomacy penalty | Congress mediation |
| Port blockade | War or outside reaction closes route | Lane suspended | Military or diplomatic port opening |
| Returnee disappointment | Overpromised citizenship or jobs | Momentum falls, propaganda risk | Citizenship and industry repair missions |

Failures should not automatically kill the route. Repeated failure should make the route expensive and politically dangerous.

## Achievement tracking

The diaspora route should support achievement logic without exposing achievement language in event text.

Tracking variables and flags:
- number of lane families established
- number of safe convoy missions completed
- number of settlement regions stabilized
- skilled return missions completed
- local reception above threshold in several regions
- no convoy tragedies
- no housing crisis, for a hard achievement variant
- diaspora route completed while Federal Charter or Revolutionary Congress remains coherent
- diaspora route completed while Scramble reaction is active
- returnee guard units used to defend a member capital
- all major port regions connected to the route

Potential achievement conditions:
- establish at least five lane families while keeping convoy loss low
- stabilize settlement in at least four regions with high local reception
- complete the route without triggering a housing crisis
- use returnee guard units to defend a League member capital
- complete cultural diplomacy with several restored polities
- complete the route while no African member has left the League

Achievement titles remain direction-only and should be written later.

## Focus and decision integration

Focuses should not complete settlement by themselves. They should:
- reveal lane families
- increase lane capacity
- unlock stronger settlement missions
- reduce surveillance pressure
- open skilled return missions
- unlock citizenship decisions
- add returnee guard training
- improve AI willingness to protect lanes

Decisions and missions do the work:
- open lane
- escort lane
- settle arrivals
- place skilled workers
- mediate local tension
- upgrade port and housing
- respond to disruption
- ratify citizenship

## AI behavior

AI should use diaspora routes only when it has:
- port access
- convoys
- stability
- enough industry or allies to build capacity
- no severe naval blockade unless it has escort options
- route compatibility

AI should not open more lanes than it can handle. It should prefer one stable lane, then expand after capacity improves. AI should pause lanes when at war with a naval major or when housing crisis triggers.
