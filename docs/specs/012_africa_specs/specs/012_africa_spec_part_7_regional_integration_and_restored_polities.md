# 012 Africa spec part 7, regional integration and restored polities

## Integration philosophy

Integration is the main gameplay proof behind `Africa is one`. The unifier can proclaim unity early, but state-level integration should require control, trust, local administration, reconstruction, garrisons, or federal agreement.

The system should support four main paths.

| Path | Best for | Result | Main risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Federation | trusted League members | federated member status, slow core or shared benefits | slow pace, outside sabotage |
| Protectorate | weak or threatened states | subject protection, local armies, later integration | dependency, autonomy disputes |
| Coercive command | occupied or hostile states | puppet, annexation, faster control | resistance, panic, rival blocs |
| Restoration | historical regional subjects | local legitimacy and local army support | autonomy growth, old claims |

## Regional integration stages

Each region should move through stages rather than one country-wide check.

1. Contact and map survey
2. Protection or occupation state
3. Local administration mission
4. Infrastructure and supply project
5. Political settlement
6. Military integration or autonomy guarantee
7. Core, federated member, puppet, or regional subject decision
8. Post-integration maintenance

The order can vary by route. A command route may put military integration early. A federal route may put political settlement first. A restored-polity route may create a subject before full state administration.

## Restored polity roles

Restored polities should have practical uses.

- local legitimacy holder
- buffer subject
- federation member
- regional army contributor
- cultural route anchor
- high-chaos old-capital gate
- postwar settlement partner
- rival bloc candidate if mistreated

Some restorations can be optional, some route-specific, and some hidden. The player should not need to restore every polity to unify Africa. Restorations should be attractive because they solve local problems and create alternate play.

## Resistance and autonomy

Integrated regions should retain memory of how they entered Africa.

- voluntarily federated regions have high trust and lower resistance
- liberated regions have gratitude but need reconstruction
- conquered regions have resistance and outside sponsorship risks
- restored polities have autonomy pressure and old border claims
- high-chaos regions can have fear, prophecy, or supernatural blowback

The same state should not become an ordinary full core immediately after conquest unless the route has earned that through missions.

## Rival bloc formation

A rival bloc can emerge from a strong state or group of states that reject the unifier. It should not be random. It should have a cause.

Causes:

- command route coercion
- failed defense of a member
- low cohesion after repeated integration pressure
- ideological conflict
- restored polities fear absorption
- outside power sponsorship
- major African power sees itself as a better unifier

Rival bloc outcomes:

- negotiated autonomy settlement
- parallel League recognized as equal partner
- federation reform and reintegration
- cold war inside Africa
- open war
- outside-backed counter-unification route

## Peaceful unification versus conquest

The event should be playable through several broad strategies.

- Slow federation with low panic and high trust
- Revolutionary liberation with high anti-colonial war tempo
- Royal or old-polity federation with many subjects
- Military command integration with strong army and high resistance
- Diaspora-backed development route with ports and industry
- High-chaos route with powerful strange actors and deep blowback

Each strategy should reach the same public fantasy in a different way. The end map can say Africa, but the internal state of Africa should remember how it got there.
