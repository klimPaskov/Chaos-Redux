## South Africa

### Starting tension

South Africa is the most dangerous ordinary host because it combines sovereign institutions, substantial industry, a regional military, dominion ties, racial exclusion, land dispossession, settler nationalism, African political organisation, labour, traditional authorities, and control over neighbouring protectorate and mandate relationships. The continental fantasy cannot begin as a painless decision by the existing state. It must pass through the specified civil war when South Africa belongs to the Allies, and it should use a severe constitutional rupture in other situations unless the country has already transformed through compatible gameplay.

### Civil-war entry

When the required RSA-in-Allies condition applies, the opening creates a continental coalition rather than simply changing the existing government’s cosmetic tag. The coalition’s exact political composition depends on prior ideology, laws, leaders, and event state, but it must include African political forces and can include labour, defecting officers, anti-segregation liberals, regional organisations, and external volunteers. Loyalist and continental sides need distinct country identities, leaders or councils, starting forces, supply assumptions, and diplomatic goals.

The continental side’s war aim is control of the state and dismantling of the exclusionary political order. The Allied side can support loyalists, seek mediation, or distance itself. If the continental side wins, the specified peace logic ends the automatic Allied war against Africa and replaces it with a diplomatic crisis, recognition process, sanctions, or a later Scramble response. The victory must not grant instant confidence from the rest of Africa.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is power without a legitimate African mandate. It tracks racial franchise, land, labour, provincial politics, traditional and local authority, mining capital, settler confidence, African political confidence, officer loyalty, and neighbouring-state fear. During civil war it also tracks coalition cohesion and control of mines, ports, rail hubs, and population centres.

The problem can mature into a nonracial federation, a continental republic, a council settlement that recognises traditional authorities under equal citizenship, a workers and farmers union, a military liberation command, or a confederation that gives neighbouring countries strong safeguards against South African domination.

Every route must settle citizenship and land. A focus that declares equality without changing franchise, legal status, land access, labour organisation, or local government is not a solution. Radical land seizure can be available, but it creates food, capital, and military consequences. Negotiated reform takes longer and can fail through obstruction.

### First proof mission

During civil war, the first proof is to protect civilians and keep one rail-port-industrial corridor functioning while winning support from at least two African regional actors outside the host. After civil war, the proof is to guarantee a neighbouring state without annexation and complete a domestic citizenship or land milestone. Full success shows that the coalition can govern and protect. Partial success wins the war but leaves the continental project feared. Failure can produce a partitioned settlement, prolonged insurgency, or a rival African coalition.

### Host focus modules

- **The coalition decides what victory means.** Establishes the balance among mass politics, labour, traditional authorities, officers, and constitutional reformers.
- **Citizenship before command.** Opens franchise, legal equality, and local representation reforms that affect member confidence.
- **Land, farms, and mines.** Creates several settlement methods with food, production, migration, and resistance consequences.
- **The railways belong to the settlement.** Connects industrial control to civilian supply and regional aid.
- **The protectorates are not prizes.** Gives Basutoland, Bechuanaland, Swaziland, and neighbouring states independent relationship choices.
- **South West Africa under scrutiny.** Opens withdrawal, trusteeship, federation, local independence, or contested security options.
- **A continent will not be ruled from Pretoria by default.** Offers distributed capitals, rotating institutions, or a host-capital model with strict checks.

### Route mutations

Federalism becomes a nonracial constitutional convention with powerful provincial and regional safeguards. It can be the most stable route, but it must survive obstruction and coalition fracture. The republic route builds mass citizenship, elections, and a new civil service, with a danger that one party captures the liberation state. The crowns route is a council of recognised traditional authorities inside equal citizenship, not a restoration of settler monarchy. The people’s union route links mineworkers, farm labour, urban movements, land reform, and regional liberation, while facing capital flight and a command struggle between party and unions. Military continentalism can win the civil war and secure corridors quickly, but it carries the greatest risk of reproducing domination. Confederation is attractive to neighbouring states and can preserve a strong South African economy inside a limited continental centre.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

Nearly every neighbouring state has reason to fear South African supremacy. Southern Rhodesia, Portuguese colonies, settler forces, conservative African authorities, and outside powers can support rival blocs for different reasons. A host that annexes the protectorates or imposes military governments can create a Southern African defence coalition even among ideological enemies. A host that offers real sovereignty guarantees can instead gain the strongest regional logistical network in the package.

### Military and economy

South Africa begins with industry, rail, ports, mines, trained forces, and a potential officer corps split. The civil war should divide these assets by geography and political control. The continental side should not receive every factory and formation through a hidden transfer. Its postwar army must integrate volunteers, defectors, militias, and regional forces through staged decisions.

Economic power is also political. Mines and railways can fund continental development, but concentration of investment inside the old industrial core raises sovereignty concern. Land reform, labour rights, housing, food supply, and public services must compete with rapid rearmament.

### Post-unification signature

A successful South African host can create a continental equality court, labour and mining compact, southern development bank, or a distributed executive that prevents one industrial region from dominating the state. A coercive victory can instead leave a command economy and security apparatus that makes later member withdrawal more likely.

### High-chaos gate

Deep-earth, mine, animal, storm, or ancient-host content appears only after the normal civil and regional struggle is established. High-chaos actors must be visibly nonhuman or supernatural and cannot use racist imagery or turn real African peoples into monsters.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI South Africa evaluates civil-war strength, Allied support, coalition cohesion, land pressure, and neighbouring confidence. The continental AI should prioritise war victory, civilian supply, and a domestic settlement before external annexation. Loyalist AI should not be scripted as a generic fascist enemy when its actual ideology and alliance differ. The overlay does not replace all existing South African content. It owns the continental rupture, civil war, postwar settlement, and the legitimacy conditions for African leadership.

## Southern Rhodesia

### Starting tension

Southern Rhodesia begins as a self-governing settler colony with racially allocated land, settler political control, mining and agricultural interests, rail links to South Africa and the ports, African urban and rural political organisation, and a regional position that can draw it toward South Africa or a Central African federation. A continental project must dismantle settler constitutional privilege and settle land before it can claim wider legitimacy.

### Starting problem and idea lifecycle

The starting problem is a parliament without equal citizenship. It tracks land apportionment, settler confidence, African political organisation, mine and farm labour, railway control, relations among Mashonaland, Matabeleland, and other regions, and dependence on South African and Mozambican routes. It can mature into a federal republic, a broad republic, a council settlement with protected regional authorities, a workers and farmers union, a military liberation government, or a confederation tied to Northern Rhodesia and Nyasaland.

### First proof mission

Secure a rail corridor to a port or neighbouring African partner while completing one real land or franchise reform. Full success also prevents a settler military breakaway. Partial success keeps the economy moving but raises internal resistance. Failure can split the territory or trigger outside intervention.

### Host focus modules

- **Land lines become political lines.** Opens restitution, purchase, communal trust, and radical redistribution paths.
- **A franchise that governs.** Replaces token representation with a real constituent process.
- **Railways between regions.** Makes access to Beira, South Africa, and the north a strategic choice.
- **Mashonaland and Matabeleland settlement.** Supports regional autonomy, restored identities, or a common republic without forcing a binary ethnic map.
- **The federation question arrives early.** Allows a negotiated Central African federation, confederation, or rejection before colonial powers impose one.

### Route mutations

Federalism and confederation can resolve regional and northern relationships if land and franchise are addressed. The republic route creates mass citizenship and a national civil service. The crowns route may protect restored Ndebele or other regional authorities, but it cannot preserve settler supremacy. The people’s union route links land, mine labour, farms, and rail. Military continentalism grows from liberation forces and defectors, with a high risk of permanent emergency rule.

### Regional resistance and rival bloc

South Africa, Portugal, settler organisations, Northern Rhodesian copper interests, and local regional authorities can each resist for different reasons. A host that treats Northern Rhodesia and Nyasaland as annexation targets can create a central African sovereignty bloc.

### Military and economy

The host has useful rail, mining, farms, airfields, and a trained security apparatus, but a civil or constitutional conflict should divide them. Military growth must integrate liberation forces and protect food production. The economy path should resolve land and labour alongside industrial development.

### Post-unification signature

A successful host can establish a continental land restitution court, rail-access treaty, or federal protection for regions emerging from settler rule.

### High-chaos gate

Great Zimbabwe, stone, ancestral, or ancient-host content requires high chaos or a researched restoration route. It must not make modern Shona, Ndebele, or other communities into supernatural caricatures.

### AI plan and anti-bloat boundary

AI Southern Rhodesia prioritises franchise, land, rail access, and internal security before continental expansion. It avoids military continentalism without civil conflict or severe threat. The overlay does not turn every later nationalist movement into a separate full branch.

## Promotion rules

A compact signature becomes a full dossier only when at least two of the following are true:

- the host remains independent and playable for a long campaign
- the host leads a regional council or rival bloc
- a shared route produces country-specific contradictions that the generic overlay cannot express
- the host has a distinct post-unification institutional legacy
- testing shows that its opening lacks a real choice or survival loop
- the host receives a Tier A restored identity that changes its political package

Promotion should replace weak shared content rather than add another parallel layer on top.

## Host overlay acceptance rules

The host layer is complete only when:

- every valid start has either a full dossier or a compact signature
- full dossiers change opening play, not only names and modifiers
- the first proof mission uses the host’s actual geography and institutions
- every host can fail without permanently breaking the event package
- domestic legitimacy affects foreign member confidence
- existing meaningful country content is preserved where possible
- country-specific decisions call shared League and integration logic
- route AI understands domestic and external conditions
- no host receives instant authority over neighbouring African states
- compact signatures remain compact until promotion criteria are met
- no country overlay duplicates the full Charter League, congress, or integration system
