# Event 12 Africa specification, part 6

## Presentation, localisation direction, achievements, assets, and documentation

## Presentation purpose

Event 12 needs a presentation layer that explains a living continental system without reducing Africa to a coloured map or a single conquering army.

The player should see:

- countries making choices
- governments asking for protection
- regional congresses arguing over accession
- railways, ports, rivers, and supply corridors changing the practical meaning of unity
- colonial holders preparing sanctions, intervention, or withdrawal
- restored polities negotiating their place in a new order
- diaspora communities deciding whether to travel, invest, serve, or remain abroad
- high-chaos actors appearing only after the grounded system has already established stakes

The normal visual language should remain political, social, military, and infrastructural. Maps can support the interface, but people, institutions, cities, transports, troops, crowds, councils, ports, ruins, and rituals should carry the emotional weight.

## Player-facing text contract

The specification remains the source of direction and dynamic content requirements. The release-candidate text and media records for the four Event 012 super-event roles are installed, while final localisation and event-completion review remain open.

Final text must follow the project writing standard:

- no em dashes
- no semicolons in sentences
- no dialectical contrast formulas
- no staccato chains used for artificial drama
- no empty apocalypse language
- no implementation notes or rework history
- no direct announcement that an incident is a warning
- no generic office-paperwork mystery as the default frame
- no future super-event title, quote, button line, cultural reference, or audio choice without the super-event research workflow

## Core localisation registers

### Continental public register

Used for the unifier, Charter League, public congresses, economic programmes, and post-unification announcements.

Direction:

- confident without sounding inevitable
- direct country and region names
- concrete references to ports, railways, borders, troops, food, work, representation, and protection
- language of shared obligation rather than vague destiny alone
- readable public state names

Avoid:

- administrative country names
- generic globalist slogans
- claims that every African government already agrees
- text that treats all African societies as interchangeable

### Member-government register

Used when an African country considers protection, membership, resistance, departure, or a rival bloc.

Direction:

- state-specific concerns
- visible bargaining over sovereignty, defence, officers, customs, debt, representation, royal status, and local administration
- pride and fear appropriate to the member's strength
- clear differences between a weak threatened state, a strong regional power, a monarchy, a republic, a colonial government, and a restored polity

### Colonial-holder register

Used for foreign powers with territory, bases, guarantees, or adjacent interests.

Direction:

- concrete military and economic stakes
- colonial panic, commercial loss, strategic access, prestige, and domestic political disagreement
- varied response by power and campaign state
- room for negotiated withdrawal, recognition, sanctions, covert action, or war

Avoid making every foreign response identical or automatically genocidal.

### Diaspora register

Used for return, investment, professional missions, veterans, families, artists, workers, and political organisers.

Direction:

- voluntary movement with real choices
- travel, housing, citizenship, work, education, family, safety, and political trust
- recognition that diaspora communities are diverse and do not act as one body
- explicit support for Afro-American participation without treating all Black people outside Africa as interchangeable

The route can be idealistic, opportunistic, radical, cautious, or exploitative depending on policy. The player must be able to build trust or destroy it.

### Restoration register

Used for historical polities, courts, councils, sacred centres, city-states, and regional identities.

Direction:

- a living political claim grounded in people, institutions, place, memory, or surviving authority
- uncertainty where the historical continuity is debated or reconstructed
- distinct local vocabulary and political form
- no museum-display tone

### High-chaos register

Used for nonhuman nations, nature cults, living rivers, stone hosts, prophet states, and weaponised diseases.

Direction:

- strange but readable
- clear separation between fictional nonhuman actors and human African ethnic identities
- physical consequences that make the absurdity playable
- humour through impossible confidence, alien priorities, regnal flavour, and absurd military doctrine
- no human ethnic caricatures

## Event Details direction

The Event Details entry should describe the premise and public situation.

It should convey:

- one African-capital country has announced a continent-scale project
- the project begins with protection, aid, and diplomatic mobilisation
- African governments can join, bargain, resist, depart, or oppose it
- colonial possessions and foreign bases are becoming points of crisis
- unity requires political, military, economic, and constitutional work

It should not list modifiers, core rules, decision costs, evolution thresholds, hidden routes, high-chaos actors, other continent unifiers, achievement conditions, or the terminal world path.

## Entry event direction

The first event should show the host's public declaration and immediate domestic mobilisation.

Visible information:

- who declared the project
- the host capital and government
- the first public promise of African defence and unity
- the first practical commitment the host must prove

Uncertain information:

- whether other states will trust the host
- whether colonial holders will negotiate
- whether the host can afford the programme
- what constitutional form unity might take

The main option should acknowledge the commitment. Route choice belongs in focuses, decisions, and follow-up events rather than a single irreversible opening button.

## Report event families

### First protection request

Show a threatened African government, border force, civilian movement, or port asking for material help.

The report should establish that protection has a cost and that intervention can create dependency or trust.

### First defended partner

Show the practical result of aid or intervention. The image should focus on arriving supplies, volunteers, repaired transport, guarded civilians, or a defended capital.

### First failed guarantee

Show the human and political cost of a promise the unifier could not keep. This should damage authority and encourage rival leadership.

### First regional congress

Show delegates, monarchs, officers, workers, clergy, local organisers, or traditional authorities entering a visible political arena.

### First member departure

Show an actual break in the League, such as removed flags, withdrawn units, closed crossings, or rival mobilisation.

### First restored polity

Show a restored government or regional authority assuming public functions rather than only displaying an old symbol.

### First diaspora arrival

Show travel, families, professionals, veterans, workers, or organisers arriving through a real transport route. Avoid a generic triumphal crowd.

### First continental corridor

Show a railway, river port, convoy route, air link, or repaired road connecting previously separated members.

### First high-chaos manifestation

Show an impossible actor or natural phenomenon that can no longer be dismissed as ordinary politics. Keep the subject clearly fictional or supernatural.

## News event families

### Charter League formation

Global news should explain that the organisation can protect African states and bargain over accession. It should not imply that all members have surrendered sovereignty.

### Major colonial withdrawal

The news should identify the territory, power, and settlement form. Negotiated withdrawal, defeat, local independence, Charter membership, and direct integration need different versions.

### Rival African bloc

The news should establish that continental unity has produced an organised African opponent rather than a random rebellion.

### Africa is one

The news should mark completed or near-completed continental unification, depending on the final trigger chosen by implementation. It should distinguish political proclamation from full administrative integration.

### Scramble response

The news should identify the foreign coalition, sanctions, ultimatum, or intervention plan without revealing hidden AI calculations.

### Other continent unifier

Each continent needs its own news package and identity. Do not reuse Africa's wording with a changed continent name.

### Continental war

The news should explain the belligerents, union names, and stakes. It should not immediately announce the final world-end outcome.

## Super-event roles

### Africa is one super-event role

Role:

- continent-scale political transformation
- public recognition that the unifier has achieved the core fantasy
- possible first super-event for Event 12 if the final implementation treats continental unity as globally decisive

Trigger direction:

- the unifier controls or has constitutionally integrated the defined continental requirement
- unresolved hostile African states are below the accepted threshold
- the proclamation has not fired before

Presentation direction:

- central human or institutional subject
- visible continental breadth through people, flags, routes, or architecture rather than a flat map
- confidence and unresolved burden

Installed research record:

- final title, description, button remark, quote, audio, and image source records are installed
- final playback, localisation, and event-completion review remain open

### Scramble for Africa response super-event role

Role:

- global reaction to unified Africa
- beginning of sanctions, ultimata, expedition planning, or recognition crisis
- shift from continental consolidation to world conflict

Trigger direction:

- Africa is one has fired
- at least one outside great power qualifies for a serious response
- the crisis has reached the coalition or ultimatum phase

Presentation direction:

- foreign fleets, mobilised troops, closed markets, crowded ports, anti-intervention demonstrations, or diplomatic confrontation
- Africa remains the active subject rather than a passive prize

Installed research record:

- final title, description, button remark, quote, audio, and image source records are installed
- final playback, localisation, and event-completion review remain open

### Continental wars super-event role

Role:

- beginning of the rare world-order war among continent-scale powers
- clear signal that normal international politics has ended

Trigger direction:

- required continent unifiers exist
- their own post-unification routes are complete
- world chaos requirement is met
- terminal competition has been deliberately started

Presentation direction:

- multiple continent-scale identities confronting one another
- no generic burning globe
- use distinct military, political, and symbolic identities

The researched title, description, button remark, quote, audio, image, and source records are installed. Final playback, localisation, and event-completion review remain open.

### The World terminal super-event role

Role:

- final campaign identity
- last surviving continent-scale power becomes The World
- terminal world-end state

Trigger direction:

- terminal path active
- only one eligible continent-scale power remains
- no incompatible world-end state already exists

Presentation direction:

- finality without a generic apocalypse image
- the surviving identity visibly absorbing, federating, or ruling the defeated world
- route-sensitive visual treatment when feasible

The researched title, description, button remark, quote, audio, image, and source records are installed. This role cannot use placeholder music, default audio, an invented quote, or an unresearched cultural line. Final playback, localisation, and event-completion review remain open.

## Defeat and aftermath presentation

A defeat super-event is justified only when the unified African project became a sustained global threat or world-order actor.

Smaller defeats should use news and event chains.

Possible aftermath subjects:

- breakup into successor states
- restored member sovereignty
- foreign occupation crisis
- Charter exile network
- memory of continental war
- reconstruction and reparations
- surviving diaspora communities
- high-chaos containment

The aftermath should preserve campaign consequences. It should not return the world to the pre-event state without cost.

## Achievement philosophy

Achievements should reward mastery, difficult political management, unusual coalition states, rare restorations, disciplined protection, high-chaos survival, and terminal victory.

They should not unlock because Event 12 fired or because the player completed an obvious first focus.

The full condition matrix is in `matrices/012_africa_achievement_matrix.csv`.

## Priority achievement families

### Protection achievements

Reward defending multiple African states without annexing them, keeping guarantees, defeating colonial intervention, or rescuing a state already at war.

### League achievements

Reward maintaining a large voluntary League, keeping Member Confidence high, resolving congresses, surviving a major departure crisis, or winning without direct coercive integration.

### Constitutional achievements

Reward completing difficult forms of federation, monarchy, republic, people’s union, military order, confederation, or high-chaos covenant under route-specific constraints.

### Restoration achievements

Reward rare historical polities, difficult overlap settlements, a federation of restored states, or victory as a restored member that resists the unifier.

### Diaspora achievements

Reward trusted voluntary return, safe transport, successful settlement, and a large diaspora contribution without exploitation or forced relocation.

### Economic achievements

Reward full corridor coverage, local processing, resource sovereignty, food security, and high development without crushing Integration Burden.

### Military achievements

Reward winning protection wars, maintaining the common reserve, defeating a great-power expedition, or winning with route-specific strange units.

### High-chaos achievements

Reward nonhuman victory, ecological retaliation, disaster prediction, containment of a created disease, or a supernatural actor joining a constitutional order.

### World-order achievements

Reward sponsoring another continent, creating a dynamic two-continent union, defeating a rival continent, and becoming The World.

## Achievement disqualifier standards

Use explicit tracking for conditions that cannot be inferred from the final map.

Common disqualifiers include:

- using console or debug paths where the achievement system detects them
- forcing the triggerable scenario when the achievement is intended for normal play
- using direct annexation before required protection milestones
- allowing a protected partner to be destroyed
- breaking a voluntary-route coercion limit
- failing the required diaspora trust condition
- using a high-chaos route for a grounded-only achievement
- switching away from the required host or restored polity
- entering another terminal world-end state

## Achievement icon direction

Every achievement needs a completed 64x64 icon designed for its exact condition.

Icon families:

- shield and open gate for protection
- linked seals or hands for voluntary federation
- crown and congress floor for council-of-crowns routes
- railway, river, and port motifs for economic achievements
- ship, train, luggage, tools, and family motifs for diaspora achievements
- region-specific historical symbols for restoration achievements
- clearly fictional animal, forest, river, stone, or weather motifs for high-chaos achievements
- interlocking continent emblems for world-order achievements
- a single final globe-scale identity motif for The World

Grey and not-eligible variants follow the project achievement asset workflow.

## Accessibility and clarity

The mechanic should not rely on colour alone.

Use:

- icons plus text labels
- readable state names
- numeric values with consistent integer formatting where fractional precision is unnecessary
- tooltips that explain gains, losses, and blocked actions
- warning symbols for urgent states
- separate labels for confidence, authority, burden, and pressure
- static fallbacks for all animations

Regional maps and cards should remain usable at common HOI4 interface scales.
