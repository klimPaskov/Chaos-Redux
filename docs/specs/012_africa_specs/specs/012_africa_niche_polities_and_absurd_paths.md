# Event 012 Africa — Niche Polities, Regional Microstates, and Higher-Tier Absurd Paths

This file is part of the source specification for Event 012. It deepens the regional-authority layer so the African unifier does not play as one generic continental tag swallowing identical puppets. The unifier proclaims that Africa is one, but the continent answers through old courts, city-states, river leagues, port republics, caravan polities, island confederations, anti-colonial committees, religious offices, and, at higher chaos, impossible nature signatories and nonhuman forest powers.

The design goal is not to spawn every historical polity in every campaign. It is to give the implementation agent a large, researched menu of authority cards, subject packages, release tags, focus hooks, decisions, missions, assets, AI behaviors, and high-chaos variants. The system should pick what fits the host, route, region, chaos tier, state control, and player choices.

## Polity activation model

A niche polity can exist at five implementation depths. The selected depth depends on available tags, regional importance, route, and performance budget.

| Depth | Name | What it means in play | Use case |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Research motif | Name, focus icon, mission title, or flavour event only. | Very small or duplicate polities; no gameplay actor needed. |
| 1 | Authority card | A card in the Congress Board with trust, demand, contribution, and integration state. | Most old polities and local councils. |
| 2 | Charter subject | A releasable or spawnable subject/tag with a council, flag, small forces, decisions, and integration route. | Regionally important authorities or player-facing subjects. |
| 3 | Route polity | A subject or transformed tag with a mini-branch, distinct units, leaders/council, and route choices. | High-priority historical anchor in a region. |
| 4 | Mythic pact | A high-chaos authority card or subject whose public explanation is supernatural, prophetic, or impossible. | Evolution II–IV route spice and regional absurdity. |
| 5 | Nonhuman / impossible sovereign | A separate nonhuman or supernatural actor registered in shared nonhuman/special-country triggers. | Gorilla, chimpanzee, bonobo, forest, river, storm, or artifact countries; never a metaphor for human groups. |

The implementation should prefer depth 1–2 for breadth and depth 3–5 for selected highlights. A campaign should feel different if the host is in the Horn, the Gulf of Guinea, the Congo, Madagascar, the Maghreb, or South Africa.

### Activation gates

Baseline activation:

- Only historical or political authority cards and charter subjects appear.
- They ask for security, autonomy, religious/cultural respect, restoration of courts, ports, railways, markets, or artifact protection.
- They can join the Charter League and later negotiate federation, puppet status, protectorate status, or integration.

Evolution I activation:

- Nearby authority cards appear earlier and generate news snippets so outsiders understand why wars started.
- Niche authorities can provide volunteers, local claims, or route-specific units after the unifier wins the first ring of wars.
- Strong authorities can resist if they believe the unifier is only another empire.

Evolution II activation:

- Old military institutions and ceremonial forces become stronger: cavalry columns, port guards, lake flotillas, mountain schools, women guard regiments, railway guards, desert scouts, and elephant logistics.
- Several authority cards can be upgraded into charter subjects with mini-branches.
- Mythic rumours begin, but most effects can still be explained as weather, propaganda, ritual politics, or intelligence work.

Evolution III activation:

- Niche authorities become export tools. The unifier can sponsor other continents by sending named delegations: Swahili port envoys to the Indian Ocean, Sahara route missions to the Middle East, Kongo river advisers to Amazon analogues if a South American unifier exists, or diaspora congress envoys to the Americas.
- Absurd paths become public. Nature courts, trickster offices, water-spirit admiralties, desert underground water kingdoms, moving bronze guardians, and forest signatories can appear as high-chaos diplomatic actors.

Evolution IV activation:

- Some mythic pacts can become full impossible sovereigns.
- The unifier can annex or federate successful continental unifiers through dynamic union names, while niche polities become ministries, protectorates, or ceremonial estates inside the union.
- Nonhuman and supernatural subjects must receive special classification so normal migration, plague, ideology spread, and civilian-panic systems do not treat them as ordinary human countries.

World-end activation:

- The World Is One path can call every active polity into a final World Congress ledger.
- Normal historical authority cards become commemorative ministries, archives, ceremonial seats, or reconstruction compacts.
- Impossible sovereigns become final signatories only if the world-end path already has all continental unifiers and the campaign is in terminal chaos.

## Shared rules for niche authorities

A niche authority should never be a free reward button. It should create a local problem and a local advantage.

Every authority card or subject needs:

- a named region or state-group requirement;
- a legitimacy demand;
- a practical contribution;
- at least one non-PP cost or map objective;
- a trust value;
- an integration condition;
- a resistance trigger;
- a route preference;
- AI behavior;
- asset notes.

Common demands:

- protect a court, shrine, port, archive, market, palace, railway, fort, or holy site;
- keep local autonomy above a threshold for a period;
- station supplied divisions in named states;
- spend support equipment, infantry equipment, trucks, trains, convoys, command power, army XP, or civilian factory burden;
- hold a capital, river crossing, mountain pass, rail hub, port, or caravan route for 90–180 days;
- sign an anti-looting or anti-puppet clause;
- resolve a dispute with another authority;
- choose federation, protectorate, or integration path.

Common contributions:

- local trust;
- militia or special-unit templates;
- regional construction decisions;
- route-specific advisors or councils;
- intelligence or reconnaissance;
- state compliance bonuses after missions;
- faction cohesion;
- legitimacy;
- resistance reduction;
- convoy, rail, river, desert, or mountain logistics.

Common resistance states:

- **Quiet refusal:** card remains in Congress but contribution stops.
- **Charter strike:** local factories/ports/railways underperform until a mission succeeds.
- **Autonomy ultimatum:** subject demands protectorate/federation status instead of annexation.
- **League exit:** strong African member leaves the Charter League.
- **Restoration war:** route polity declares war or joins a rival African bloc.
- **Mythic backlash:** high-chaos pact turns disaster powers against the unifier.

## West Africa and Gulf of Guinea polity catalogue

### Ife–Oyo Yoruba

**Historical anchor direction:** Ife as a Yoruba cultural/religious legitimacy centre and Oyo as a major Yoruba state with cavalry and a trained army tradition.

**Authority form:** Route polity when the unifier controls southwestern Nigeria or if the host is Yoruba/Nigerian; otherwise authority card.

**Starting package:** low-width cavalry/motorized abstraction, capital guards, palace officials, road scouts, limited artillery after the army branch.

**Decision and focus content:**

- **Seat the Ife Delegation:** requires control of the Ife/Oyo/Lagos state group and local stability; raises Legitimacy and Yoruba trust.
- **Rebuild the Oyo Road Offices:** spends trucks, infantry equipment, and army XP; unlocks mobile Liberation Columns.
- **Ask the Crossroads for Witnesses:** high-chaos mission that reveals hidden collaborator networks; increases Mythic Charge but risks religious backlash.

**Absurd higher-tier path:** **The Thunder Cavalry Ledger.** At Evolution II, cavalry/motor columns gain storm-road bonuses during rainy seasons or thunder reports. At Evolution III, a mythic crossroads office can misdirect colonial armies, changing mission targets and delaying enemy reinforcements. At Evolution IV, the “Crossroads Ministry” becomes a state-driven animated board element that pulses when a hidden route or trap is available. It must be presented as supernatural bureaucracy, not as a joke about Yoruba religion.

**Assets:** sourced motifs only if real symbols are used; generated congress seal, crossroads decision icon, cavalry focus icon, possible council portrait.

### Benin / Edo / Benin

**Authority form:** authority card or charter subject in southern Nigeria.

**Starting package:** palace guard, urban garrisons, artifact-protection committees, defensive infantry.

**Decision and focus content:**

- **Safeguard the Bronze Stores:** requires control of the Benin City area, infantry equipment, and a garrison mission; raises Legitimacy and blocks foreign looting events.
- **Palace Offices in Congress:** adds a council advisor and unlocks diplomatic demands around cultural property.
- **The Bronzes Remember Faces:** high-chaos intelligence decision that reveals which colonial power has stolen or claimed artifacts; can target embargoes, raids, or restitution ultimatums.

**Absurd higher-tier path:** **The Court That Casts Witnesses.** At Evolution II, bronze-custodian units reduce resistance and improve legitimacy after liberation. At Evolution III, generated report events describe bronze figures appearing in foreign museums overnight with warnings. At Evolution IV, a symbolic “Living Bronze Guard” can become a supernatural support unit or animated leader-overlay motif. It must be explicitly artifact/supernatural and never represented as human caricature.

**Assets:** generated bronze court seal and animated bronze-glow UI fallback; real Benin art references require source notes and museum-license review if used directly.

### Dahomey / Fon Guard Compact

**Authority form:** charter subject if the unifier controls southern Benin; otherwise authority card.

**Starting package:** disciplined guard infantry, women-warrior route template, coastal fort missions.

**Decision and focus content:**

- **Recognise the Guard Compact:** unlocks a limited elite infantry template with high training cost.
- **Settle the Oyo–Dahomey Memory:** prevents Yoruba/Fon authority rivalry from harming Congress Cohesion.
- **Market of No Chains:** anti-slave-trade memory decision; raises Legitimacy and Diaspora Return but costs stability if mishandled.

**Absurd higher-tier path:** **The Vodun Guard of the Red Earth.** At Evolution II, guard units can bind a state to defensive terrain bonuses after a ritual/report event. At Evolution III, earth/thunder warnings can predict disasters in enemy-held coastal states. At Evolution IV, a “Guard of the Earth Court” pact can punish colonisers that ignore ultimatums with supply disruption, flood, or disease-fear missions. All final Vodun terminology must be researched by the super-event/text workflow if it becomes localisation.

**Assets:** generated guard icon; sourced historical research required if using Agojie/Dahomey women-warrior imagery or terms.

### Asante / Akan Golden Stool Registry

**Starting package:** disciplined infantry, ceremonial guard, cocoa/gold/trade economy missions.

**Decision and focus content:**

**Absurd higher-tier path:** **The Stool Refuses the Unworthy.** At Evolution II, a balance-of-power event can remove an abusive Congress governor. At Evolution III, the Golden Stool Registry can make a bad annexation reverse into autonomy. At Evolution IV, the ceremonial seat becomes an animated Congress Board warning seal: if it glows, a forced integration will trigger revolt.

**Assets:** historical symbols must be sourced if used; generated non-literal stool/seal icon preferred for fictional Congress use.

## Decision and mission integration

Create or extend a decision category named **Old Polities Registry** or attach it to the Congress Board. It should be target-managed, not a wall of 70 decisions.

Player flow:

1. Select a region.
2. Reveal up to three relevant authority cards based on controlled states, route, chaos, and current mission count.
3. Choose one action: recognise, arbitrate, arm, restore offices, investigate, integrate, or suppress.
4. A mission starts with a named map objective.
5. Success raises trust and contribution; failure creates strike, autonomy demand, or revolt risk.

Decision families:

- **Recognise an Old Seat:** legitimacy and trust; costs political capital, not just PP: local autonomy, stability, support equipment, or observer mission.
- **Restore Court Offices:** builds local administration; costs support equipment, civilian factory burden, and time.
- **Raise Local Guards:** unit unlock; costs infantry equipment/manpower/army XP and raises Authority or local pressure.
- **Protect Archives and Relics:** prevents looting, raises legitimacy, may trigger foreign restitution disputes.
- **Arbitrate Rival Claims:** solves two-card rivalry; costs Legitimacy and time, success raises Cohesion.
- **Demand Charter Contributions:** military/forced route; gives manpower/equipment/units but lowers trust and can create revolt.
- **Invite Mythic Witnesses:** high-chaos; spends Mythic Charge, risks disaster/backlash, reveals hidden effects.
- **Sign Nonhuman Pact:** requires Green Covenant route, high forest/water/herd trust, and explicit nonhuman classification.

## AI behavior for niche polities

The AI should not try to activate every authority. It should choose based on route and practicality.

Federal AI:

- prefers authority cards with high trust and low military risk;
- recognises regional councils before raising guards;
- avoids forced contribution decisions unless in existential war;
- prioritises arbitration and autonomy guarantees.

Military AI:

- prioritises guard units, roads, arsenals, and frontier offices;
- uses demand-contribution decisions only when legitimacy is above a safe threshold or enemy pressure is high;
- may trigger revolts if overused, but should not spam them.

People's/revolutionary AI:

- favours port unions, labour councils, diaspora offices, anti-colonial military memories, and anti-looting decisions;
- suspicious of crown-route authority unless it can be converted into popular councils.

Crown route AI:

- favours old courts, palace offices, ceremonial legitimacy, and restored congress seats;
- avoids disrespectful integration decisions;
- may create strong but autonomy-heavy federation.

Green Covenant / mythic AI:

- only activates high-chaos pacts when chaos and Mythic Charge support them;
- does not sign nonhuman pacts if the unifier is exploiting forests/rivers/herds;
- can choose absurd paths under Totalen Chaos or World Collapse but should not pick them in grounded baseline.

Colonial/foreign AI:

- targets authority cards that threaten its holdings;
- tries to bribe or split strong authorities;
- uses propaganda if the unifier abuses forced integration;
- escalates Scramble Pressure if too many old polities recognise the unifier.

## Asset and localisation rules for niche polities

Do not invent real historical flags when a sourced flag or symbol is required. For most old polities, use generated **Charter seal** variants rather than pretending to revive an attested flag. If the implementation chooses a real historical symbol, it must go through sourced asset review.

No generated readable text. Script, glyph, archive, charter, and document motifs should use non-readable symbolic marks unless a sourced real script/image is processed and documented.

Real historical leaders are not resurrected through fake portraits. If the route needs a leader, use councils, offices, anonymous fictional officials, or symbolic portraits. A supernatural “empty chair,” “bronze witness,” “forest court,” or “root congress” can be generated because it is fictional.

Every high-chaos animal/nature actor must have:

- explicit nonhuman/supernatural labels;
- shared special-country/nonhuman classification if implemented as a tag;
- generated assets, not real animal photos by default;
- no human ethnicity implied by animal identity;
- normal human regional authorities coexisting in the same region.

## Exploit and clutter controls

- Only 3–5 authority cards should be active in a region at one time.
- A player cannot repeatedly farm legitimacy by recognizing and integrating minor cards; each card has one main recognition reward and later maintenance rewards are smaller.
- Forced contributions have escalating revolt risk.
- Elite units from old polities are capped by local trust, integration level, and route.
- Nonhuman pacts are defensive or special-purpose; they should not become infinite conquest unit factories.
- Mythic disasters must have cooldowns and diplomatic costs.
- Integration of an authority card should not grant full cores instantly unless region integration requirements are already satisfied.
- Strong authorities can exit or fight back if their trust collapses.
