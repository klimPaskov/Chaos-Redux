# Event 033 Acid Rain super-event production prompt

Complete the formation super-event for Chaos Redux Event 33 through the repository's existing super-event framework. Read Part 9, Part 10, the research files, the super-event skill, the asset skill, the frame-animation skill, and current super-event registries before editing. Use `chaosx_super_event_text_researcher`, `chaosx_super_event_audio_researcher`, `chaosx_asset_source_researcher`, and `chaosx_event_completion_auditor`.

## Trigger contract

The super-event fires once after the Event 33 formation transaction has committed its frozen eligible-state registry, first front, first active footprint, global phase, and decision-category availability. It must not fire from a failed or rolled-back initialization. Cluster and independent launches use the same once-only presentation receipt. Save reconciliation must not replay it.

## Image

Use `gfx/event_pictures/super_events/033_acid_rain.dds` at 457 by 328. The image should present a corrosive atmospheric front over a wartime industrial city, damaged stone and metal, and civilians moving toward civil-defense shelter. Leave readable space for the title and quote. Review contrast inside the actual super-event window. Do not use modern protective suits, fluorescent colors, generic nuclear imagery, or copied historical photographs.

## Text research

Verify the exact source and rights status before choosing the final quote. Review the Robert Angus Smith candidate documented in `research/033_acid_rain_science_and_source_research.md`, but do not adopt it from the planning note alone. Check the page image or a reliable transcription, confirm context and punctuation, and confirm public-domain status in the target distribution territories. Keep the displayed quotation short. Record author, work, year, edition, page or section, source URL, access date, and rights basis.

The title should be direct and short. The surrounding text should present an abnormal and immediately lethal weather system. Civilians are burned, poisoned, suffocated, or driven into shelters, and the first affected states suffer real population loss. The source may remain unknown or supernatural. Do not add pH figures, scientific disclaimers, or wording that reduces deaths to manpower shortages.

## Audio

The current unauditioned research candidate is the public-domain Wikimedia Commons recording of Mussorgsky's *Night on Bald Mountain*. Do not accept it from metadata alone. Listen to the complete recording, select an excerpt no longer than two minutes, and confirm that the selected segment fits the formation scene. Check that no other super-event uses the same track or excerpt. Record title, composer, performer or recording credit, file source, license or public-domain basis, exact start and end times, final duration, conversion settings, and output path.

Reject the candidate if the recording rights are unclear, the excerpt begins or ends awkwardly, dialogue or applause intrudes, the mood conflicts with the event, or it duplicates another super-event. Find and document a replacement under the same rules if needed. Use the required audio conversion format and loudness range. Test start, stop, and multiplayer behavior in game.

## Framework wiring

Register the image, title key, quote key, audio asset, stable super-event ID, visibility trigger, once-only receipt, and optional debug trigger in the established files. Use the current framework's standard opening and closing behavior. Do not create a second custom full-screen system.

The super-event must open for all players according to the repository multiplayer rule. It must not advance the weather scheduler, alter exposure, add Chaos, add contamination, or perform any gameplay mutation. Closing it must not stop the audio system incorrectly or leave an invisible overlay.

Test independent launch, Natural Disasters cluster launch, save reload during display, two-player presentation, disabled major-event presentation settings, missing-audio fallback, and the manual debug trigger. Verify image dimensions, text fit, quote attribution, audio uniqueness, rights record, start and end timing, and once-only behavior. Add final evidence to the Event 33 asset and super-event manifests.
