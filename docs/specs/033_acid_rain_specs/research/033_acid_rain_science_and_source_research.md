# Event 033 Acid Rain science and source research

## Research role

Event 33 is fictional, potentially supernatural, and directly lethal. Its mortality model is an accepted gameplay premise rather than a conclusion drawn from atmospheric science. The sources below inform corrosion, contaminated runoff, ecological harm, respiratory exposure, protective equipment, shelter design, and visual direction. They do not set a chemical ceiling on the anomaly and must never be used to weaken its population losses.

The implementation still follows a hard gameplay rule: every applied civilian death removes one person from the affected state's real civilian population through the shared exact-loss helper.

## Primary scientific sources

### United States Environmental Protection Agency, What is Acid Rain?

Source:

https://www.epa.gov/acidrain/what-acid-rain

Useful findings:

- acid deposition includes wet and dry forms
- ordinary environmental acid deposition has measurable acidity, but Event 33 does not use a real-world pH value
- sulfur and nitrogen compounds are central to formation
- deposition can travel beyond its source region

Specification use:

- supports sulfuric and nitric acid language
- supports moving atmospheric transport
- informs long-range atmospheric transport and wet or dry deposition imagery

### United States Environmental Protection Agency, Effects of Acid Rain

Source:

https://www.epa.gov/acidrain/effects-acid-rain

Useful findings:

- acidic water can release aluminum from soil
- ecosystems and young aquatic life are sensitive
- acid deposition harms plants and trees
- acidic particles corrode metal and damage paint and stone
- sulfate, nitrate, and ozone contribute to haze and human health burdens

Specification use:

- supports environmental aftermath
- supports resource, water, forest, visibility, and building effects
- supports corrosion-focused art direction

### United States Geological Survey, Acid Rain and Water

Source:

https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/acid-rain-and-water

Useful findings:

- environmental acid deposition moves through atmospheric moisture and runoff
- sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides can form sulfuric and nitric acids
- acid rain removes nutrients and can mobilize aluminum
- acid deposition can damage forests, limestone, marble, and monuments
- sheltered carbonate stone can develop gypsum crust and later crumble

Specification use:

- supports water and soil aftermath
- supports protected water and food projects
- supports stone, monument, and infrastructure imagery

### National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health, Sulfuric Acid

Source:

https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0577.html

Useful findings:

- concentrated sulfuric acid and mist can irritate or burn eyes, skin, nose, and throat
- serious exposure can harm the respiratory system
- the material is corrosive to metals
- protection needs skin, eye, washing, and respiratory controls

Specification use:

- supports masks, protective clothing, shelters, decontamination, and medical response
- informs corrosive mist, skin, eye, and respiratory injury presentation
- does not define or cap Event 33 mortality

## Accepted fictional premise

Writing and tooltips can describe:

- an unexplained, Chaos-driven, or supernatural atmospheric reaction
- lethal acid precipitation
- concentrated corrosive aerosol
- civilians dying from direct exposure, respiratory injury, and poisoned water
- acidic runoff entering reservoirs and food systems
- rapid corrosion of exposed transport and machinery
- shelters, evacuation, decontamination, and medical response reducing actual population loss

Avoid:

- assigning an exact real-world pH or chemical formula to the anomaly
- adding scientific disclaimers that soften or question the accepted lethality
- using modern climate-policy arguments as the event's main tone
- treating gas masks as complete protection against liquid and water contamination
- describing deaths only as manpower shortages or administrative estimates

## Research-to-design map

| Research finding | Design consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Acidic precipitation and mist can travel with atmospheric systems | Moving fronts, warning regions, wet and dry exposure imagery |
| Corrosive acids damage exposed material | Buildings, rail, airbases, paint, metal, and stone receive corrosion pressure |
| Acid deposition damages soil and water | Water and food protection, decontamination, and aftermath tiers |
| Fine particles and acid mist harm respiratory systems | Medical and protective capacity, shelter protocols, sustained mortality |
| Aluminum and nutrient loss harm ecosystems | Resource and environmental aftermath penalties |
| Corrosion is cumulative | Fractional damage-pressure accumulator instead of one massive daily roll |
| Early acid-rain quote is public domain | Candidate super-event source with full attribution |

## Source handling rules

- Recheck every source during implementation if the final text quotes it.
- Keep exact source pages and rights evidence in the final asset and audio manifests.
- Generated art may use scientific and historical references for composition, but should not copy a protected image.
- Do not use a copyrighted photograph as final event art without a reviewed license.
- Final super-event text and audio need specialist review and in-game fit testing.
