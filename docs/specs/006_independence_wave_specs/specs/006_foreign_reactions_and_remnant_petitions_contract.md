## Candidate and score contract

The target cohort is the frozen country/package/generation table from the committed Event 006 public report.
Event 005 origins, Join conversions, failed allocations, and unrelated pre-existing countries never enter that released-target cohort.
Foreign actors come from bounded live neighbors, verified package contacts, existing patron or diplomatic relationships, the aligned current Independence Network, and exact former-host contacts supported by a bounded engine lookup.
There is no periodic world scan and no universal major-country list.
Unsupported or missing actor-source routes are explicit coverage blockers, not grounds to invent sponsors or silently substitute another pool.

Each actor must be living and sovereign unless a separate diplomatic-capacity contract explicitly authorizes its subject status.
The actor must differ from the target and its former host; actors released in the same committed cohort do not receive the earlier-wave reaction profile.
The former host uses the host-response family for its own losses.
The actor/target pair must retain its recorded provenance at dispatch.

Interest families contribute at most once each: direct stake four, recorded relationship three, strategic fit two, and political fit one.
The same fact belongs only to its strongest family; a shared frontier cannot supply both direct and strategic points by itself.
A qualifying conflict or overextension subtracts three once, and the total cannot be negative.
Unknown historical, minority, trade, resource, or colonial ties contribute zero until an accepted package profile or live fact supports them.
Detailed automatic responses require a score of six or more.
The selected target is the actor's highest-scoring eligible pair; equal scores retain the first frozen committed-report row.
Lower scores and additional targets use the public wave report and optional engagement decisions.

Profile priority is earlier-wave Event 006 country, great power with an actual contact, registered regional stakeholder, then local neighbor.
Secondary interests remain visible in the event's reason text.
Major status alone supplies neither a contact nor score.

## Shared bilateral transactions

Existing paid diplomatic missions must freeze their selected capital-state owner and the released country's generation at selection.
Delayed settlement must use that country receipt, validate it, and never redirect a paid mission to a later owner of the map proxy.
Country-initiated and foreign-initiated recognition use one generation-matched bilateral receipt so the same recognition cannot award Statehood progress twice.
Network recognition records the countries that actually endorsed the settlement, sharing those bilateral receipts.
Guarantees use the real engine relationship and one bilateral reward receipt; an existing guarantee prevents a duplicate promise and duplicate Statehood reward.
If another compatible transaction is already paid and active for that same pair, a foreign offer settles that transaction or defers instead of charging or rewarding the pair twice.

Liaison and ordinary recognition use the existing light diplomatic payment and affordability helpers.
Formal mediation, guarantees, and remnant petitions use their existing standard counterparts.
These are accepted reuse of current tuning, with no new numeric cost baseline.
Each caller validates scope, pays the same quoted cost, and displays blocked requirements before applying a shared transaction.
Patron offers require released-country acceptance, limitation, counteroffer, or refusal before influence/dependency is booked; material support debits its real donor resource channel.
Host, recognition, patron, Network, and League changes use their existing ledgers and charter rules.

## Remnant petition

The host response's outside-guarantee flag opens a 30-day petition opportunity; it does not give protection.
Only a living, generation-matched remnant on the legal-continuity or regional-identity settlement may petition.
It may choose one reachable high-interest recipient from the same bounded contact registry, with deterministic ranking and an explicit chosen counterpart.
The host pays the standard diplomatic cost when it petitions.
The chosen recipient may receive one separate petition popup in that wave, even if it also received its ordinary reaction; no second petition popup is permitted for that actor-wave.
The response timeout is 13 days, with refusal/noncommitment as the default.

The host may petition only once in its actual country identity/origin generation, not once for every report wave.
Use an existing durable country-generation receipt when available; otherwise maintain an explicit host identity epoch with bounded native destruction/release cleanup.
A wave ID must not stand in for that host identity epoch.
No second attempt is permitted in that generation without a later accepted cooldown and cost design.

A guarantee requires valid access/capacity, no hostile relationship, the standard foreign diplomatic cost, and the actual engine guarantee.
The host agrees to contingent military-transit permission as its concession, activated only if the recipient actually guarantees it.
Refusal, invalidation, or a prior guarantee does not activate that concession or manufacture protection.
The `.69` recipient event and `.70` host outcome notification retain both sides of the transaction.
Petition outcomes affect the surviving host and its counterpart and never count as recognition of a released country.
An unchosen opportunity expires without an AI or human guarantee; AI petitions only for a useful threat response with a viable recipient.
