# Independence Wave, Part 6: Formables, league, super-events, and triggerable scenario

All names in this file are working labels, not final localisation.

## Regional formable framework

Independence Wave countries can grow into larger states through a shared regional formation system.

The framework is data-driven. It does not hardcode one route for one invented country and leave every other candidate without ambition.

Every candidate package can reference zero, one, or several formable families. The family defines broad political and territorial logic. The candidate defines its own route, eligible territories, local names, leaders, flags, and integration problems.

## Formable family data

Every formable family records:

- family ID
- public identity direction
- region
- eligible Event 6 origin packages
- required anchor regions
- optional expansion regions
- mutually exclusive families
- government routes that can reveal it
- ideology or leadership restrictions when justified
- league or patron conditions
- territorial control standard
- member consent standard
- subject and ally handling
- formation method
- initial cores and claims policy
- post-formation integration missions
- capital options
- leader and institution transition
- flag and cosmetic identity requirements
- AI willingness
- super-event and achievement eligibility
- hidden or public discovery state

The initial registry is in `matrices/006_formable_family_registry.csv`.

## Formation methods

### Negotiated federation

Several countries or regions enter voluntarily.

Requirements:

- strong Legitimacy
- acceptable Recognition
- member relations
- congress preparation
- minimum league or bilateral trust
- control of agreed capitals or transport links

Effects:

- broad legitimacy
- lower resistance
- more immediate cores for consenting members
- complex federal balance and autonomy decisions

Failure:

- one member withdraws
- capital dispute
- military faction attempts forced centralization
- foreign patron blocks the union

### Dynastic or traditional union

A crown, court, sultanate, confederated stool, customary institution, or recognized traditional authority unites several regions.

Requirements:

- valid traditional route
- accepted succession or selection
- support from regional authorities
- territorial or member control
- recognition or domestic legitimacy threshold

Effects:

- strong symbolic unity
- new court or council system
- possible provincial autonomy
- claimant and succession risks

### Revolutionary union

Popular councils, socialist movements, peasant leagues, or revolutionary republics create a shared state.

Requirements:

- compatible routes
- local movement support
- control of key industrial or agrarian centers
- successful congress or war

Effects:

- mass mobilization
- shared economic institutions
- foreign ideological support
- elite and regional resistance

### Military settlement

One country conquers or coerces the required regions.

Requirements:

- route commitment
- Security
- territorial control
- victory or capitulation conditions

Effects:

- fast formation
- claims rather than broad cores
- high instability
- integration and resistance missions
- foreign condemnation or containment

### League transformation

A regional league converts into a federation, union, or confederation.

Requirements:

- high league cohesion
- agreed charter
- member consent or defined vote rule
- shared defense and economic milestones

Effects:

- preserves member identity through federal or subject structures when appropriate
- gains shared decisions
- creates leadership and autonomy disputes

### Hidden high-chaos proclamation

A rare route reveals an unusual historical, ideological, or alternate identity.

Requirements:

- high chaos
- specific country origin
- route and leadership conditions
- territorial proof
- public reveal event

Effects:

- dramatic identity transformation
- stronger rewards and risks
- unusual flags, leaders, or institutions
- possible danger super-event eligibility

## Formation territory rules

Large formables should not grant all cores instantly.

### Immediate cores

Allowed for:

- the founding country anchor
- voluntarily participating countries with high consent and compatible legitimacy
- regions already treated as core by the candidate package when the design is well supported

### Staged cores

Normal for:

- disputed territories
- conquered members
- culturally mixed regions
- large unions
- territories with a different former host
- regions added through a military settlement

### Claims only

Used for:

- optional historical ambitions
- distant or controversial borders
- territories controlled by a nonparticipant
- high-chaos expansion goals

Integration missions can require:

- local support
- capital and rail connection
- administrative capacity
- equipment and manpower
- infrastructure construction
- autonomy agreements
- plebiscite or congress approval
- resistance reduction
- recognition
- sustained control over time

## Formation discovery

Formables can be visible, revealed, or hidden.

### Visible from the mature tree

Used for obvious regional federations and historic unions.

### Revealed by route

A focus, congress, leader, or government settlement reveals the decision.

### Revealed by map state

The country controls key capitals, river corridors, or historic centers.

### Revealed by league state

A regional caucus, charter, or member vote opens the formation.

### Hidden high-chaos route

The name and requirements remain hidden until an event reveals the identity. Implementation docs and prompts still record the full logic.

## The independence network

All active Event 6 countries belong to an informal network before a faction exists.

The network provides:

- mutual recognition decisions
- aid and civil service exchange
- emergency information about host attacks
- regional conference opportunities
- shared formable and border arbitration contacts
- congress preparation

The network has no automatic military obligations.

## Formal league concept

A formal faction or league may emerge once sudden independence becomes a shared political identity.

Working label: League of New Nations

This label is not final localisation.

The league is not merely a normal faction with a special name. It has membership rules, charter goals, shared values, congresses, votes, reserves, rescue obligations, patron conflicts, and failure states.

## League formation prerequisites

Normal prerequisites:

- at least four living Event 6 origin countries
- at least three willing founding members
- no founder is already fully controlled by a foreign patron unless the charter permits client members
- one member has completed congress preparation
- minimum combined Recognition or network standing
- at least one shared strategic problem, such as host reconquest, recognition blockade, trade isolation, or regional war

High chaos can lower diplomatic preparation and raise military urgency. It does not remove the minimum viable membership rule.

## Charter pillars

Every charter chooses positions across five pillars.

### Sovereign equality

Determines whether all members have equal votes, whether population or contribution matters, and whether large members can lead permanently.

### Mutual defense

Ranges from consultation to automatic defense against former hosts and external aggression.

### Anti-puppetry

Determines limits on foreign bases, patron influence, subject status, and client governments.

### Arbitration

Determines whether members must submit border disputes and whether sanctions apply to refusal.

### Common development

Determines shared reserves, reconstruction funds, trade preferences, infrastructure, and technical missions.

## League values

### League Cohesion

Measures member trust, charter compliance, shared successes, and willingness to act together.

### Common Cause

Measures whether members face a shared strategic goal. It rises during host aggression, recognition campaigns, league wars, or major development projects. It falls when members have no common problem or pursue incompatible territorial agendas.

### Patron Capture

Measures whether one outside power dominates the league through loans, arms, bases, advisers, or client members.

### Shared Reserve

Tracks equipment, convoys, trains, fuel, volunteers, and reconstruction resources contributed by members.

### Member Confidence

Tracked per member. It affects votes, exit risk, leadership challenges, and willingness to answer rescue calls.

## Charter route families

### Defensive congress

Purpose:

- protect members from former-host reconquest
- coordinate guarantees
- keep border disputes contained

Key mechanics:

- mutual defense missions
- mobilization votes
- host sanctions
- joint planning

### Development compact

Purpose:

- pool technical experts, finance, rail, shipping, and reconstruction

Key mechanics:

- league development fund
- cross-border infrastructure
- civil service exchange
- industrial specialization

### Sovereign equality league

Purpose:

- protect small states from hosts and patrons
- promote recognition and anti-puppet rules

Key mechanics:

- influence caps
- member rights charter
- balanced leadership
- collective diplomatic missions

### Armed liberation bloc

Purpose:

- support later breakaways
- challenge former hosts
- intervene in member wars

Key mechanics:

- volunteer corps
- arms reserve
- liberation target votes
- coordinated ultimatums

### Radical revisionist league

Purpose:

- reject existing borders and legal hierarchy
- coordinate high-chaos territorial change

Key mechanics:

- aggressive common goals
- sponsor breakaways
- simultaneous border crises
- secret military planning
- strong danger milestone progress

## Membership rules

A country can join when:

- it has active Event 6 origin
- it meets charter compatibility
- it is independent enough for the chosen anti-puppetry rule
- it accepts arbitration and defense obligations
- existing members approve according to charter vote rules

A non-Event 6 country cannot normally join. A special sponsor, guarantor, or associate status can exist without full membership.

A former host cannot join as a full member while it retains active claims against Event 6 countries. It may become an observer or treaty partner after settlements.

## Refusal and expulsion

Countries can refuse because of:

- isolationism
- patron pressure
- incompatible ideology or government
- border conflict with a member
- fear of military obligations
- preference for a regional league
- high confidence in independent survival

Expulsion grounds can include:

- annexing a member
- refusing binding arbitration
- becoming a host proxy under a strict charter
- abandoning a rescue obligation
- repeated charter violation
- sponsoring a coup in another member
- starting an unauthorized war under a defensive charter

Expulsion lowers Cohesion and can create a rival bloc.

## League leadership

Leadership depends on charter.

Possible systems:

- rotating presidency
- elected secretary-general
- permanent strongest-member leadership
- council of regional representatives
- dual civilian and military leadership
- traditional congress host

Leadership contests use:

- network standing
- Recognition
- contributions
- military strength
- rescue history
- patron dependence
- ideology and charter compatibility

A small country can lead through high standing and diplomacy. A large member can dominate through force, but this may raise Patron Capture or member distrust.

## Shared league goals

### Secure Five Recognitions

The league coordinates diplomatic campaigns for its weakest member.

### Break a Recognition Blockade

Members use trade, guarantees, and diplomatic pressure against a hostile host or patron bloc.

### Build the Emergency Reserve

Members contribute equipment, trains, convoys, fuel, and volunteers.

### Defend a Member Capital

A timed crisis asks members to keep one threatened capital supplied and controlled.

### Arbitrate a Border

Members resolve a dispute without war.

### Complete a Shared Corridor

Members connect ports, railways, rivers, or roads.

### End Patron Capture

Members reduce one outside power's influence below a threshold.

### Support a Regional Congress

The league helps several members form a federation or confederation.

### Confront a Former Host

A charter vote chooses sanctions, guarantees, volunteers, war, or negotiated settlement.

## League failure states

- Cohesion collapse
- leadership coup
- patron capture
- rival regional caucus secedes
- member war
- failed rescue
- economic reserve bankruptcy
- aggressive charter provokes containment
- forced federation dispute

League failure creates a remembered event and alters later congress attempts. It does not erase every bilateral relationship.

## League faction mechanics

The formal league can use a faction when military cooperation becomes real. Its faction behavior must follow charter rules.

- defensive charter does not auto-invite members into unrelated wars
- armed liberation charter allows intervention votes
- radical charter can create common offensive goals
- members can refuse operations at a confidence cost
- leadership changes AI priorities
- faction peace and capitulation behavior should protect small members from accidental annexation by allies
- postwar settlements require member and charter logic

## League formation super-event

A super-event fires when the first durable formal league is proclaimed.

### Trigger meaning

- the congress succeeds
- minimum founding membership is met
- a charter is adopted
- leadership is selected
- the league becomes an active diplomatic or military actor

### Role

Faction formation and world-order announcement.

### Tone direction

The world realizes that the new states are no longer isolated accidents. They have created a common institution and a public doctrine of survival.

The exact tone changes by charter:

- defensive and legalist
- hopeful and developmental
- suspicious and anti-patron
- militant and revolutionary
- frightening and revisionist

## Dangerous milestone super-event

A second super-event is reserved for a globally alarming Independence Wave outcome.

Working role label: coordinated revisionist sovereignty milestone

This is not final localisation.

### Eligible trigger packages

One of the following must occur:

- a radical league adopts an offensive charter and has enough members and military strength
- a high-chaos 20-country wave begins with several armed or radical governments and immediate coordinated claims
- several Event 6 countries launch synchronized wars against former hosts
- a hidden high-chaos formable becomes the center of an aggressive bloc
- the league sponsors enough new breakaways to create a visible cascade

### Exclusions

- normal release wave
- weak consultative congress
- one regional formable without global impact
- routine host war
- ordinary league election

### Tone direction

The event should communicate organized revisionism, military confidence, and fear that the principle of sudden independence has become an engine for further partition.

### Image direction

Generated alternate-history period scene. Possible subjects include a synchronized congress of military delegations, mobilized columns under several new flags, or an imposing league command assembly. Avoid a generic world map as the main subject.

## Triggerable scenario

Proposed scenario ID: `SCN-008`

Working label: Every Flag

The label is not final localisation.

The scenario releases every viable candidate package that can receive a unique anchor without violating host survival.

“All possible countries” means every enabled candidate in the Event 6 registry that is valid on the current map after deterministic anchor reservation. It does not mean overlapping states are duplicated or hosts are deleted.

## Scenario preparation

1. Build the full candidate registry.
2. Remove living tags and invalid transformations.
3. Reserve one protected state for every host.
4. rank candidates by anchor uniqueness, research readiness, and package completeness.
5. Reserve unique anchor states for all candidates.
6. Resolve overlap through anchor priority and optional-state trimming.
7. Remove any candidate that cannot receive a unique valid anchor.
8. Record blocked candidates and reasons for scenario summary.
9. Assign territory level and force package from selected intensity.
10. Apply the selected scenario type.
11. Execute all releases.

## Scenario intensity

Every intensity attempts the same candidate count. Intensity changes starting territory, forces, resources, and route access.

### Low

Territory:

- anchor package only

Forces:

- fragile local defense
- low stockpiles
- severe officer and supply problems

Mechanics:

- high instability
- low recognition
- maximum survival challenge

### Medium

Territory:

- compact package

Forces:

- viable militia and border formations
- normal starting equipment

Mechanics:

- baseline country values
- normal route access

### High

Territory:

- compact plus available extended states

Forces:

- armed package for many candidates
- better depots, officers, and stockpiles

Mechanics:

- stronger ambitions
- lower opening fragility
- faster league formation

### Maximum

Territory:

- full non-overlapping release package
- remaining ambition territory stays as claims or missions

Forces:

- strongest dynamic opening
- rare units and foreign support where package allows

Mechanics:

- high-chaos routes available
- hidden formables can be discovered early
- radical and aggressive options receive higher AI weight
- type-specific war or league effects are strongest

## Scenario types

### Sovereign Scatter

All countries begin independent and outside a faction.

Purpose:

- pure survival sandbox
- maximum diplomatic variety

### Common Congress

All countries begin in the Event 6 league or in regional caucuses that immediately convene a global congress.

Purpose:

- test league scale
- collective survival
- patron and cohesion management

Maximum intensity can begin with a stronger charter and shared reserve.

### Wars of Separation

Every released country begins at war with its former host when a valid host exists.

Purpose:

- military challenge
- test host safety and defensive packages

Countries without a viable host war remain independent and receive regional threat missions.

Wars of Separation evaluates one viable former-host war per released country, so multiple releases may share a surviving host in this type.

### Universal Belligerence

Released countries begin in broad conflict according to a defined scenario rule.

Recommended variants within the type selector:

- war with former hosts only
- war with neighboring released countries
- war with all nearby nonleague countries

The UI must explain the selected rule clearly. The scenario should not create an unbounded every-country war without performance and diplomacy review.

Universal Belligerence: Former Hosts reserves each surviving former-host target at most once per launch. A later release that shares an already reserved host receives the regional-threat result, while failed declarations release the reservation for a later eligible actor. Neighboring Releases and Nearby Nonleague States use the same bounded target-reservation contract.

### Patron Worlds

Released countries are distributed among major patrons according to reach, ideology, and region.

Purpose:

- proxy competition
- dependency management
- rival faction development

### Great Partition

Hosts retain only protected cores or capitals where possible. Released countries receive larger packages.

Purpose:

- extreme map transformation
- formable and regional war sandbox

This type needs the strictest host-capital and overlap validation.

## Source-driven mode cardinality

The implementation retains six numeric scenario families: Sovereign Scatter, Common Congress, Wars of Separation, Universal Belligerence, Patron Worlds, and Great Partition.

Universal Belligerence contains three independently selectable rules: Former Hosts, Neighboring Releases, and Nearby Nonleague States.

The player-facing selector therefore exposes eight selectable modes backed by six numeric families. The runtime acceptance matrix is eight selectable modes by four intensities, or 32 cells.

The three Universal Belligerence rules are separate acceptance rows and must never be collapsed into one family row. This clarification adds no scenario family or intensity beyond the accepted design.

## Scenario AI

AI route seeds depend on type.

- Sovereign Scatter favors survival and local diplomacy
- Common Congress favors league participation and collective defense
- Wars of Separation favors military and host-settlement routes
- Universal Belligerence favors defensive or radical military routes according to strength
- Patron Worlds favors client, balancing, and anti-patron routes
- Great Partition favors regional formables and border settlement

## Scenario super-event use

The scenario does not automatically use the league or danger super-event merely because many countries spawn.

- Common Congress can trigger the league super-event when the formal charter activates
- Maximum intensity with a radical aggressive type can trigger the danger super-event
- Sovereign Scatter at Low intensity should rely on the wave summary, not a global super-event

## Scenario completion and summary

After setup, the scenario summary records:

- candidates released
- blocked candidates and reasons
- hosts retained
- scenario type
- intensity
- league membership
- active wars
- territory package level
- force package level

The player should be able to inspect why a registry candidate could not appear.
