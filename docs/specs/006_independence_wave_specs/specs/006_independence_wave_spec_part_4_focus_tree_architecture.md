# Independence Wave, Part 4: Focus tree architecture

All names in this file are working labels, not final localisation.

## Shared framework, adapted identity

Every Independence Wave country receives a deep common framework. The framework is not one identical generic tree.

It is assembled from four layers:

1. common survival trunk
2. government route family
3. regional overlay
4. country ambition or package archetype

A fifth layer becomes available for high-chaos governments, formables, and league leaders.

The framework must feel coherent for more than one hundred possible countries. It does this by changing route names, institutions, leaders, decisions, AI, military tools, regional goals, and late-game ambitions through package data.

## Tree assignment rule

### Event-created tag without a meaningful tree

The country receives the full Independence Wave tree.

### Existing tag with a meaningful tree

The country keeps its existing tree. It receives an additive Independence Wave overlay, starting ideas, decisions, missions, and origin mechanics.

The overlay must not blind-load a replacement tree over an existing country that was already alive or had its own identity.

### Existing tag released by Event 6 with only a minimal tree

The implementation can use the full Event 6 framework after confirming that the event created the tag and that no more meaningful tree should be preserved.

### Formed country

A formable receives post-formation branches or an additive formable overlay. It should not lose completed survival content, league status, or origin history.

## Early, middle, and late pacing

### Early game

The country secures the capital, chooses provisional authority, integrates armed forces, creates a civil service, and opens first foreign contacts.

### Middle game

The country settles government form, builds route-specific institutions, professionalizes the army, manages patrons, negotiates with the former host, and decides whether to join the league.

### Late game

The country leads a league, forms a larger state, pursues regional expansion, completes a high-chaos transformation, resolves patron dependence, or establishes a durable settlement with the former host.

## Architecture map

The tree uses seven major lane families.

1. Survival and state construction
2. Government and internal power
3. Economy, infrastructure, and administration
4. Army, security, and military identity
5. Diplomacy, recognition, and patrons
6. Former host, borders, and expansion
7. Network, league, formables, and high-chaos ambitions

These lanes interact. They are not isolated columns.

## Lane 1: Survival and state construction

### Narrative role

The country becomes more than a declaration and a flag.

### Opening focus groups

#### Secure the Provisional Capital

Purpose:

- confirms the capital
- opens capital defense mission
- begins administration recovery
- links early military and government branches

Reward style:

- capital state effects
- mission unlock
- command and administration changes
- no flat political power reward as the main effect

#### Name the Provisional Authority

Purpose:

- selects a government archetype pool
- reveals early political options
- changes leader or council presentation

Possible authorities:

- provisional republic
- national council
- traditional court
- revolutionary committee
- emergency military command
- regional congress
- foreign-backed cabinet

#### Inventory the State

Purpose:

- starts population, treasury, property, rail, port, and depot surveys
- improves Government Capacity
- reveals package-specific economic targets

#### Bind the First Oath

Purpose:

- integrates one armed faction or public institution
- moves Legitimacy and Security
- changes the first army mission

### Middle survival group

- establish permanent ministries
- create a treasury and revenue service
- standardize legal authority
- restore communications
- integrate provinces or local councils
- resolve the first leadership challenge

### Survival capstone

The survival capstone requires acceptable Legitimacy, Capacity, capital control, and completion of the first founding mission family.

It removes or upgrades the most severe starting crisis idea and unlocks mature political routes.

Failure route:

If the country cannot complete survival, an emergency branch opens. It can produce military rule, patron capture, provincial fracture, voluntary reunion, or a desperate revolutionary settlement.

## Lane 2: Government and internal power

The framework supports six main government route families. The package can hide routes that do not fit the country.

### Constitutional republic route

#### Narrative role

A provisional government becomes a civilian republic through an assembly, constitutional settlement, electoral law, and permanent institutions.

#### Focus groups

- convene an assembly
- choose unitary or federal structure
- define citizenship and regional rights
- establish courts and elections
- limit emergency powers
- create a professional civil service
- write anti-puppet or neutrality clauses

#### Mechanical identity

- strong Legitimacy growth
- recognition efficiency
- better peaceful host settlements
- lower military political power
- slower emergency mobilization
- access to democratic and legalist league charter options

#### Failure state

A failed assembly empowers military, radical, regional, or patron factions.

### Popular council route

#### Narrative role

Labor organizations, peasant leagues, revolutionary committees, cooperatives, or popular councils claim the new state.

#### Focus groups

- organize local councils
- nationalize or socialize key assets
- create mass political education
- arm worker or peasant formations
- build cooperative administration
- choose centralized party rule or federated councils
- export solidarity or remain nationally focused

#### Mechanical identity

- high mobilization potential
- strong local participation
- ideological foreign support
- greater class conflict and patron rivalry
- special regional land or workplace reforms

#### Failure state

Factional purges, rival councils, food supply breakdown, or foreign party capture.

### Traditional restoration route

#### Narrative role

A dynasty, chieftaincy, sultanate, kingdom, customary council, religious authority, or historical institution gives the new state legitimacy.

#### Focus groups

- confirm succession or selection
- restore court, council, or customary institution
- define relations between tradition and modern administration
- recruit regional notables
- create ceremonial unity
- negotiate recognition guarantees
- choose constitutional tradition, central restoration, or sacred authority where suitable

#### Mechanical identity

- high symbolic Legitimacy
- region-specific diplomacy
- access to restoration formables
- potential administrative weakness
- claimant disputes
- asset-heavy visible identity changes

#### Failure state

Rival claimant, provincial refusal, military guardianship, or loss of recognition.

### Emergency military route

#### Narrative role

Officers, defecting commands, veterans, border guards, or militia leaders argue that survival requires centralized rule.

#### Focus groups

- establish emergency command
- integrate militias by force or bargain
- seize depots and communications
- impose border security
- choose temporary guardianship, permanent junta, or national army state
- build a military economy
- prepare reclamation or defensive doctrine

#### Mechanical identity

- strong Security
- fast unit integration
- lower civilian Legitimacy
- harder recognition
- high coup and faction risk
- aggressive former-host options

#### Failure state

Officer split, militia warlordism, patron-controlled command, or civilian uprising.

### Patron client route

#### Narrative role

The country accepts one sponsor as guarantor, investor, arms supplier, and political model.

#### Focus groups

- select the dominant patron
- accept advisers and loans
- align law and doctrine
- grant transit, bases, or concessions
- rebuild under foreign protection
- choose loyal client, bargaining client, or eventual independence from the patron

#### Mechanical identity

- fast Recognition and Capacity
- strong foreign equipment
- high dependency
- restricted diplomacy
- route-specific leader and flag changes when justified
- possible subject or associated-state outcome

#### Failure state

Public backlash, patron abandonment, rival sponsor coup, or formal puppeting.

### Radical sovereignty route

Earliest normal access: high chaos or Evolution 5

#### Narrative role

The government treats sudden independence as proof that existing borders, legal orders, and host claims can be discarded.

#### Focus groups

- reject inherited borders
- mobilize a founding myth or revolutionary principle
- arm aligned movements abroad
- coordinate claims with radical league members
- transform the constitution or leadership
- pursue hidden formables
- establish a revisionist bloc

#### Mechanical identity

- strong expansion and mobilization
- high instability
- low conventional recognition
- high danger milestone progress
- unusual leaders, councils, symbols, and military units

#### Failure state

Isolation, league split, multi-front war, internal terror, or fragmentation.

## Internal power struggle layer

Countries with two credible power centers should use a balance-of-power style system or equivalent value.

Possible conflicts:

- civilians versus army
- central government versus provinces
- traditional authority versus assembly
- domestic cabinet versus foreign patron
- labor councils versus ministries
- league internationalists versus national isolationists
- restoration court versus military guardians

The regional overlay identifies which conflict is appropriate. The framework should not force the same balance on every country.

Focuses and decisions move the balance. Thresholds unlock leaders, laws, advisers, missions, coups, compromises, and route endings.

## Lane 3: Economy, infrastructure, and administration

### Narrative role

The country must turn a released territory into a functioning economy.

### Opening groups

- emergency revenue
- secure food and fuel supply
- restore one key transport route
- reopen a port, railway, river route, or caravan route
- establish customs and border collection

### Regional construction groups

The regional overlay chooses meaningful geography.

Examples:

- island states develop ports, coastal shipping, and convoy defense
- landlocked states secure rail corridors and border markets
- desert states build wells, roads, caravan protection, and air links
- mountain states improve passes, tunnels, mountain roads, and local depots
- industrial breakaways seize factories, power stations, and worker housing
- agrarian states reform land, irrigation, storage, and rural credit
- river states control navigation, levees, ferries, and river ports

### Route interactions

- republics build accountable revenue and public works
- popular councils build cooperatives and worker administration
- traditional routes use land settlements, court patronage, and local notables
- military routes prioritize arsenals, roads, forts, and controlled industries
- patron routes accept investment corridors and concessions
- radical routes mobilize requisition, forced construction, or ideological production

### Economy capstones

Possible capstones:

- independent currency and treasury
- national transport authority
- regional development board
- cooperative commonwealth
- royal or customary land settlement
- military arsenal state
- patron-backed development corridor
- league reconstruction bank participation

Each capstone unlocks continuing decisions rather than ending in a passive modifier.

## Lane 4: Army, security, and military identity

### Narrative role

The country creates an army from defectors, militias, local guards, police, volunteers, veterans, and limited equipment.

### Opening groups

- integrate militia commands
- secure depots
- recruit officers
- create basic templates
- establish border guards
- form capital defense units

### Military doctrine archetypes

The package assigns one or more suitable archetypes.

#### Territorial defense

- local defense battalions
- fortification and supply
- difficult terrain bonuses
- reserve mobilization
- league mutual defense

#### Mobile frontier forces

- cavalry, motorized scouts, or light mobile troops
- border patrol and deep reconnaissance
- rapid reaction missions
- long-front supply challenges

#### Mountain and highland defense

- mountain detachments
- pass control
- pack supply
- local guide networks
- attritional defense

#### Island and coastal defense

- coastal garrisons
- patrol craft and naval militia
- port denial
- convoy corridors
- marine or raider units where suitable

#### Urban and industrial militia

- factory guards
- railway troops
- armored trains where regionally appropriate
- engineering and anti-air defense
- worker or municipal units

#### Desert and caravan security

- camel or light cavalry concepts where appropriate
- oasis and route control
- low-supply operations
- border intelligence

#### Defector army

- inherited host formations
- officer loyalty crisis
- equipment variety
- professionalization path
- risk of host infiltration

### Army route choices

- civilian control or military autonomy
- mass militia or small professional force
- domestic production or foreign arms
- defensive border doctrine or reclamation doctrine
- league standardization or independent command

### Military capstone

The capstone creates a durable force-generation path through decisions, training, production, league reserves, and route-specific units. It does not rely on repeated free division spawns.

## Lane 5: Diplomacy, recognition, and patrons

### Narrative role

The country gains legal and practical access to the world.

### Opening groups

- establish a foreign office
- send first mission
- seek neighbor recognition
- open trade and postal access
- choose neutrality, patronage, or network solidarity

### Recognition route groups

- bilateral recognition campaign
- regional guarantees
- institutional admission
- treaty registration
- diplomatic corps
- international arbitration
- arms-limit or neutrality agreements

### Patron route groups

- accept arms mission
- accept investment
- accept advisers
- grant concessions
- balance sponsors
- expose interference
- buy out obligations
- choose client or autonomous future

### Diplomatic capstones

- entrenched neutrality
- balanced patronage
- treaty-backed republic
- protected traditional state
- ideological client
- league diplomatic leader
- independent regional power

## Lane 6: Former host, borders, and expansion

### Narrative role

The country decides whether independence means settlement, guarded separation, reunion, revenge, or wider liberation.

### Former-host route families

#### Negotiated separation

- ceasefire line
- debt and property settlement
- citizenship and transit agreement
- recognized border
- mutual guarantee or non-aggression

#### Guarded frontier

- fortify border
- maintain claims
- prepare defensive mobilization
- accept an armed peace

#### Association or reunion

- negotiate federal status
- personal union or traditional association
- autonomous republic arrangement
- economic union
- voluntary merger

This route must be explicit and should cleanly close incompatible Event 6 content if sovereignty ends.

#### Reclamation and revenge

- document host abuses or legal claims
- mobilize border populations
- prepare ultimata
- seek league or patron support
- war plan and postwar settlement

### Expansion lane

Every full tree has a distinct regional ambition lane.

The lane uses candidate package data and formable families.

Focus groups can:

- survey claimed districts
- sponsor local committees
- call a regional congress
- invite related released states
- establish a border commission
- prepare a plebiscite
- unlock a protectorate or federation offer
- reveal a formable decision
- reduce integration cost
- prepare postwar administration

War goals are not the entire lane. The route includes negotiation, local support, league response, resistance, and integration.

## Lane 7: Network, league, formables, and high-chaos ambitions

### Network branch

- recognize fellow released states
- exchange civil servants
- establish an aid corridor
- share officer training
- propose arbitration
- coordinate host defense

### League formation branch

- draft a charter
- gather founding members
- choose secretariat location
- define membership rules
- select defense and economic clauses
- elect leadership
- proclaim the league

### League route families

#### Defensive congress

Mutual recognition, arbitration, and defense against former hosts.

#### Development compact

Shared reconstruction, trade, rail, finance, and industrial aid.

#### Sovereign equality league

Strong anti-puppet rules, balanced patrons, member autonomy, and public rights commitments.

#### Armed liberation bloc

Military cooperation, intervention in host conflicts, support for future breakaways, and coordinated claims.

#### Radical revisionist league

High-chaos route that rejects the existing order and can trigger the danger super-event.

### Formable branch

The country discovers and pursues one or more regional formable families. The branch changes according to route, ideology, territory, members, and league status.

### High-chaos branch

This branch is hidden until public conditions reveal it. It should not be displayed as a secret spoiler from game start.

Possible content:

- sponsor further ruptures
- transform the state identity
- establish unusual government
- adopt aggressive league charter
- unlock hidden formable
- field route-specific special units
- challenge recognized borders beyond the normal ambition package

## Regional overlay integration

Every regional overlay changes at least the following:

- route names and institutions
- one starting problem
- one economic branch theme
- one military archetype
- one diplomacy and patron concern
- one former-host issue
- one regional ambition family
- one or more country-specific focus groups for highlighted packages
- localisation tone
- asset motifs
- AI weighting

The detailed regional matrix is in `matrices/006_regional_overlay_matrix.csv`.

## Country ambition modules

Selected candidates receive one bespoke ambition module. Other candidates use a regional ambition family with package-specific states and names.

A module can include:

- restoration of a historical institution
- formation of a regional federation
- leadership of a cultural or linguistic union
- control of a river, trade route, island chain, mountain corridor, or historic capital
- rivalry with another candidate package
- special league charter proposal
- unique military institution
- unusual government settlement
- hidden high-chaos identity

The module should normally contain several focus groups, decisions, events, and an end state. One focus with a claim is not a country-specific ambition.

## Shared focus tree adaptation rules

- final focus names and descriptions use country and regional scripted localisation where helpful
- route icons share coordinated families but are not all identical
- country ambition modules receive unique icons
- government route availability is package-aware
- AI weights read package archetype, war state, legitimacy, patron pressure, and chaos
- existing countries receive additive branches or decision integration rather than destructive tree replacement
- focus rewards change decisions, missions, ideas, leaders, units, buildings, diplomacy, values, claims, or formable access
- negative starting ideas have visible mitigation and failure paths
- branches end in continuing gameplay or real transformation

## Focus AI

The AI chooses routes in two stages.

### Founding posture

The opening package seeds preferences based on:

- government archetype
- starting leader or institution
- host hostility
- military strength
- legitimacy
- regional tradition
- patron offers
- league presence
- chaos band

### Formal route decision

The AI re-evaluates before locking a mature government route.

It avoids:

- constitutional routes during total government collapse unless recovery is plausible
- military expansion when equipment and supply are inadequate
- patron routes when the patron is dead, hostile, or unable to reach the country
- formables whose required states or members cannot exist
- league routes when no valid members survive
- high-chaos routes outside allowed conditions
- suicidal wars against an overwhelming former host without allies or guarantees

It may choose dangerous routes when desperate, ideologically aligned, well-armed, or operating at high chaos.

## Focus layout direction

The final layout should be broad and readable.

- survival trunk near the center
- government routes fan outward above or to one side
- administration and economy support all political routes
- military and diplomacy lanes remain compatible with most governments
- former-host and expansion lane sits opposite the league and formable lane
- hidden high-chaos content stays out of normal view until revealed
- regional ambition modules attach without crossing the whole tree
- capstones and convergence points are visually clear

The route diagram in `diagrams/006_focus_tree_lane_map.md` is a design guide, not an exact coordinate map.
