## Statehood and recognition

### Montevideo Convention, 1933

Article 1 identifies four classic qualifications for a state in international law:

- permanent population
- defined territory
- government
- capacity to enter relations with other states

The Event 6 survival system adapts these into public gameplay pillars. The spec does not turn the convention into a literal law simulator. It uses the framework because it fits a 1930s event about sudden states proving that they can function.

Design use:

- population and administration feed Founding Legitimacy and Government Capacity
- defensible and administered territory feeds Government Capacity and Security
- functioning government feeds Legitimacy and Capacity
- external relations feed Recognition

The player-facing terms should remain plain and should not require legal knowledge.

### League of Nations Covenant

Article 1 allowed a fully self-governing state, dominion, or colony to seek membership through a two-thirds Assembly vote, subject to guarantees about international obligations and possible arms regulation.

Article 10 described respect for member territorial integrity and political independence against external aggression.

Articles 12 through 16 created dispute settlement, delayed resort to war, sanctions, and collective support concepts.

Article 16 also allowed a member that violated the covenant to be removed through a council vote.

Design use:

- recognition campaigns can culminate in an admission vote
- some recognition offers can demand arms limits or guarantees
- the Event 6 league can include arbitration, collective defense, sanctions, and expulsion
- charter debates can separate legalist, defensive, developmental, and aggressive versions

The Event 6 league remains an alternate institution. It should not simply copy the historical League of Nations.

### League minority treaty background

United Nations legal history notes that League-era minority treaties were applied unevenly, especially to some new states in Central and Eastern Europe, while older states resisted universal obligations.

Design use:

- recognition can come with rights or minority-protection conditions
- new states can resent unequal standards
- league charter debates can include equal obligations for old and new states
- former hosts can use rights concerns sincerely or opportunistically

The event should avoid treating minority protection as a simple cost or punishment. It is a political and institutional issue tied to legitimacy, recognition, local autonomy, and border settlement.

## Assyrian package

Encyclopaedia Iranica describes modern Assyrians in Iran as East Syrian Christian communities and explains the historical complexity around Assyrian, Chaldean, Aramean, and related naming.

Design use:

- the Assyria package must distinguish community, church, language, and secular national leadership
- the public state name, party names, and cultural route labels require source review
- a single generic Christian restoration route is inadequate
- an Assyrian state should begin with recognition and security problems tied to dispersed communities and external guarantees
- a Mesopotamian federation route should preserve community autonomy rather than absorb the package without consequence

Resolution:

The signature dossier fixes the modern council, Mosul anchor, registered `ASY` tag, compact-border rule, community safeguards, and symbol attribution rule. Final portraits and individual asset files use the dedicated asset provenance workflow.

## Mapuche package

A University of Chile-linked recent study emphasizes that Mapuche identity is not monolithic and identifies significant territorial identities including Mapuche, Lafkenche, Williche, and Pehuenche.

Design use:

- the package cannot use one generic council and one undifferentiated territory
- regional autonomy and internal representation should be central
- coastal, southern, and highland interests can shape military, economic, and political branches
- a Wallmapu route should require negotiated internal structure and cross-border diplomacy
- symbols, names, institutions, and leaders need community-specific research

Resolution:

The signature dossier adds the Federación Araucana, Manuel Aburto Panguilef, Herminia Aburto Colihueque, 1931 Indigenous Republic proposal, 1935 congress, 1936 repression, territorial representation, and a compact south-central anchor.

## Asante package

The African Studies Centre Leiden notes that separate Asante chiefdoms were united under Osei Tutu, that the Asante Union developed around 1700, and that the Golden Stool became a central symbol of authority.

Design use:

- the traditional route should use confederated authority rather than a generic absolute monarchy
- the Golden Stool is a legitimacy symbol requiring sourced visual treatment
- Kumasi, neighboring alliances, court authority, and modern administration can form a distinctive government conflict
- a modern constitutional, labor, military, or regional federation route should not erase traditional institutions automatically

Resolution:

The signature dossier identifies Prempeh II and the 1935 restored Asante Confederacy as the period bridge, fixes the Kumasi anchor, and separates sourced royal regalia from modern route flags.

## Sokoto, Oyo, and Benin packages

A Library of Congress Nigeria country study describes the Sokoto Caliphate as a major nineteenth-century Islamic government with broad regional reach. The same study describes Oyo as a constitutional monarchy in which the Oyo Mesi selected the alafin and checked royal authority, with cavalry as a major source of power. It describes Benin as a centralized kingdom led by the Oba, advised by hereditary chiefs, with strong guild and court institutions.

Design use:

### Sokoto

- emirate and caliphal relations can form a federal or religious government mechanic
- cavalry and frontier forces have a grounded military role
- a modern route must address administration, law, neighboring identities, and colonial institutions

### Oyo

- the Oyo Mesi and alafin relationship supports a distinctive internal power balance
- cavalry, city-state relations, trade routes, and military oligarchy create several routes
- a Yoruba federation should not be identical to an Oyo restoration

### Benin

- the Oba and council structure supports traditional and constitutional routes
- guild and urban administration can improve Government Capacity
- court art and symbols require sourced assets
- the package must be distinguished from the modern country named Benin

Resolution:

The signature dossiers identify Akenzua II, Ladigbolu I, Hasan dan Mu'azu Ahmadu, and Siddiq Abubakar III, fix the date-aware Sokoto succession, coarse Nigeria reservation group, and symbol and historical-violence rules.

## Aceh and Minangkabau packages

A Library of Congress Indonesia country study notes Aceh's history of opposition to outside rule and its history as an independent sultanate. The same study describes Minangkabau matrilineal descent and landholding institutions, elected lineage headmen, the interaction of adat and Islamic law, migration traditions, and the importance of Minangkabau intellectuals in Indonesian nationalism.

Design use:

### Aceh

- sultanate legacy, religious law, port trade, and resistance to external rule create several routes
- a modern republic, sultanate, military resistance state, or patron-backed regime can emerge from the same package
- outside support should risk dependency

### Minangkabau

- local land and lineage institutions should affect government and legitimacy
- adat and religious law can form a political balance without becoming a simplistic conflict
- migration networks can support foreign service, finance, and exile-return decisions
- the package should not be represented by one generic tribal government

Resolution:

The Aceh signature dossier fixes the 1936 coalition, Daud Beureueh role, PUSA 1939 date gate, Sumatra reservation group, and source-sensitive flag rule. Minangkabau remains a conditional package with a separate West Sumatra substate requirement.

## Mon package

A Library of Congress Thailand country study describes historical Mon kingdoms, Mon influence on regional religious and cultural life, and Mon communities and institutions in Thailand.

Design use:

- the Mon package can combine cultural institutions, monastic networks, local government, and modern federal politics
- a historical restoration should not copy a medieval kingdom
- the package needs cross-border logic involving Burma and Thailand
- a mainland federation route should preserve Mon identity and autonomy

Resolution:

The Mon signature dossier fixes a 1936 monastic, cultural, municipal, and federal council bridge, excludes post-1947 organizations from the opening, and requires a distinct Lower Burma or Mon state.

## Regional identity and research discipline

The candidate registry intentionally includes packages at different readiness levels.

### Ready after normal repository verification

- registered releasables with existing tags and assets
- clear historical states with well-attested borders and symbols
- countries that can use the shared framework without cultural invention

### Requires focused historical research

- restorations whose modern political bridge is unclear
- packages with several competing leaders or institutions
- identities spanning several hosts
- territories with several overlapping national claims

### Requires sensitive community and territorial research

- indigenous nations
- dispersed peoples
- labels that cover several distinct communities
- packages connected to genocide, forced displacement, slavery, or colonial atrocity
- modern names that may not fit the 1930s

A package assigned a conditional, formable, scenario, or specific-community disposition must stay within that recorded disposition. It must not be implemented with generic flags, invented historical leaders, broad claims, or copied focus text.
