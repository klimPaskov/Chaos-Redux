# Sensitive identity research rules

## Purpose

Independence Wave contains indigenous nations, dispersed communities, historic kingdoms, religious communities, colonial subjects, borderland peoples, and identities connected to mass violence. A large candidate pool creates a risk that implementation will flatten or fictionalize real peoples for speed.

These rules are acceptance criteria for package research.

## Do not infer a state from a name alone

Before implementation, identify:

- which people or political movement the package represents
- which period institutions are relevant
- whether the public name is appropriate to the 1930s
- whether the identity spans several distinct communities
- whether the proposed territory is continuous, dispersed, or overlapping
- whether a one-state anchor can represent the package without claiming a maximal homeland

## Separate community, territory, and government

A people does not imply one government route.

Each package should ask:

- who claims authority
- how local communities participate
- whether a council, monarchy, republic, religious authority, labor movement, military command, or federal body is plausible
- whether several internal territorial identities need guaranteed representation

## Avoid generic labels

Do not use generic labels such as:

- tribal council
- native state
- ancient kingdom
- primitive army
- warrior people
- timeless tradition

Use researched institution names and plain descriptive working labels until final localisation is written.

## Borders

- prefer a unique compact anchor
- leave broad cultural homelands as diplomacy, claims, or formable content
- do not grant maximal maps at release
- use local support, plebiscites, arbitration, and negotiated federalism
- mark mixed and disputed territory explicitly

## Leaders and portraits

- real leaders require sourced portraits and biographies
- fictional leaders require regionally plausible name pools
- do not assign a fictional person to represent a real community when a council is safer and more accurate
- one-person portraits must match name and gender metadata
- collective institutions use institutional names

## Flags and symbols

- source historical and cultural symbols
- verify whether a symbol represents the whole package or one faction
- do not use sacred, funerary, ceremonial, or community-restricted symbols without context
- fictional route flags must be documented as alternate history
- do not create ideology variants by recoloring one design

## Violence and oppression

Packages connected to genocide, slavery, forced displacement, colonial atrocity, or ethnic cleansing require grave treatment.

- no cheap jokes
- no celebratory atrocity mechanics
- no population group treated as a resource token
- no route rewards that trivialize historical suffering
- describe land, rights, survival, institutions, and political conflict concretely

## Resolved disposition

Every sensitive candidate now has one binding outcome in `006_sensitive_package_resolution.md` and `006_package_research_resolution.csv`:

- automatic only when a unique current-map anchor exists
- high-chaos only
- specific named community variant only
- negotiated formable or route only
- scenario variant only

No candidate remains in an open research status. A restrictive outcome is a completed design decision. It prevents a broad label from becoming a generic automatic country. The wave planner selects another automatic-ready candidate when the named state, consent rule, or package condition is unavailable.
