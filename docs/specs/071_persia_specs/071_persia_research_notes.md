# 071 Persia: source basis and research notes

## Evidence boundary

The attached Persia brief is the source for the requested event design. The supplied project skills define the planning and implementation workflow. Historical material below supports a small number of territorial and visual references. It does not establish the fictional modern institutions, numerical balance, eligibility thresholds, or engine capabilities described in the specifications.

All numerical rewards, costs, durations, force compositions, legitimacy bands, and political sequences in the package are authored design proposals. They have not been measured in HOI4. The exact state map, equipment IDs, character roster, and engine routes remain repository and installed-game verification work.

Public sources were checked on 20 September 2026. Only the sources listed below are used as external evidence. Unrelated search results were discarded. No online Paradox Wiki was used. No historical image was acquired as a licensed final game asset.

## H01: Achaemenid territory and administration

Source: Department of Ancient Near Eastern Art, The Metropolitan Museum of Art, The Achaemenid Persian Empire, published 1 October 2004.

URL: `https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-achaemenid-persian-empire-550-330-b-c`

Relevant support: a broad empire extending through Anatolia, Egypt, western Asia, northern India, and Central Asia. The essay describes roads and governors under Darius and major projects at Susa and Persepolis. It supports the broad territorial and administrative inspiration used in parts 3 and 5.

Limit: this does not provide an exact HOI4 state map, a fixed border valid for every reign, or a modern system of constitutional satrapies. The route's tolerance and assembly mechanisms are authored alternate history. Do not convert imperial imagery into a claim of universal ancient political equality.

## H02: Persepolis

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Persepolis, World Heritage property 114.

URL: `https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/114/`

Relevant support: Darius I's foundation, the site's location in Fars, its monumental remains, and its important reception and ceremonial functions. Parts 11 and 16 use that basis for a site-centered restoration with modern facilities kept distinct from the ancient remains.

Limit: UNESCO is describing the actual archaeological site. The assembly, military settlement, diplomatic institution, project durations, and game rewards are fictional. The source is not a license to reuse every photograph linked on the page.

## H03: Sasanian context and Ctesiphon

Source: Department of Ancient Near Eastern Art and Blair Fowlkes-Childs, The Metropolitan Museum of Art, The Sasanian Empire, published October 2003 and revised April 2016.

URL: `https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-sasanian-empire-224-651-a-d`

Relevant support: central government, western and eastern conflicts, the empire's reach toward the Euphrates and Indus under Shapur I, and Ctesiphon's role as a court center near Baghdad. Parts 3, 6, and 11 use these limited references.

Limit: the route's modern frontier commands and armored guard are game design. The page does not validate an exact modern command organization, a 20th-century uniform, or the final claimed-state list. Do not infer that modern neighboring countries have a current political obligation to accept Persian rule.

## H04: qanats

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, The Persian Qanat, World Heritage property 1506.

URL: `https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1506/`

Relevant support: gravity-fed underground water systems supporting settlements and agriculture in arid areas, with communal management. Part 8 uses this as the basis for a local water and settlement project.

Limit: a qanat is not a military railway or a general solution to army supply. The game reward must correspond to a supported local development mechanism. The source does not establish any proposed modifier value.

## H05: the Trans-Iranian Railway

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Trans-Iranian Railway, World Heritage property 1585.

URL: `https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1585/`

Relevant support: construction began in 1927 and finished in 1938, connecting the Caspian and Gulf through difficult terrain. Part 8 therefore requires an actual campaign-map audit before deciding whether the player is completing, expanding, or repairing the route.

Limit: the historical railway's existence does not reveal how the installed HOI4 map represents it. Exact state and province connections remain a local inspection task.

## H06: existing Iranian DLC content

Source: Paradox Interactive, official Hearts of Iron IV: Graveyard of Empires product page.

URL: `https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/hearts-of-iron-iv/add-ons/hearts-of-iron-iv-graveyard-of-empires`

Relevant support: the Iran description includes a new focus tree and a revived modern Persian Empire option. This creates the compatibility requirement in part 18.

Limit: the page does not document restoration flags, tree IDs, scripted effects, current installed versions, or the user's owned DLC. Those must be inspected locally.

## Unfinished research gates

Exact territorial bands require a declared historical scope and a verified installed map. The regional projects in part 3 are not a finished list of state IDs. Ancient city sites require province or state mapping, including the approximation policy when a site is smaller than a game state.

Contemporary leaders, advisers, commanders, and real claimants require roster and chronology research. The package deliberately avoids fabricated biographies. A real figure cannot be added based only on a familiar surname or a remembered role.

Historical and alternate flag designs require a documented source or explicit fictional design statement. New uniforms, equipment art, and 3D inputs require their own modern visual references and rights checks. The historical pages above are not approved 3D inputs.

Super-event titles, quotations, cultural references, and audio remain research tasks. Composition rights and recording rights must be checked separately. No final quote, music track, or licensed audio file has been selected.

The official public DLC page establishes a compatibility concern only. Engine syntax and system behavior must come from the local offline wiki, installed files, project helpers, and required verification tools.
