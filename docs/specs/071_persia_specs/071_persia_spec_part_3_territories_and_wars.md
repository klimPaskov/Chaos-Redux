# 071 Persia: territorial restoration and wars

## Three territorial bands

The territorial system begins with the user's historical bands. They represent the restored government's ambitions. They are not statements that modern countries belong to Iran. Broad historical evidence supports a large and changing Persian imperial geography, but exact game claims require an explicit design decision and a verified map. The broad Achaemenid and Sasanian context is documented in the research notes. [H01](071_persia_research_notes.md#h01-achaemenid-territory-and-administration) [H03](071_persia_research_notes.md#h03-sasanian-context-and-ctesiphon)

**Persian heartland** contains the verified normal Iranian homeland. This includes the actual Iranian core map appropriate to the installed start and any carefully documented correction required by the project. The Iranian plateau as a broad geographical phrase does not justify silently treating all of Afghanistan or neighboring territories as immediate Iranian cores.

**Imperial heartlands** contain selected regions in Mesopotamia, the Caucasus, Afghanistan, Central Asia, the Gulf, eastern Anatolia, and the Levant. These regions form distinct settlement projects. A single historical label must not sweep in an entire modern country when only part belongs in the design.

**Greater imperial claims** contain the wider Achaemenid ambitions toward Egypt, western Anatolia, the eastern Mediterranean, Central Asia, and the Indus. The historical scope changes across time. The implementation must use a declared reference period for each outer project and show the resulting state list before granting claims.

This register is a design map, not an implementation-ready state list. The exact states, provinces, capitals, ports, and route connections remain a required map audit. No numeric state IDs are invented in this package.

## Ownership, control, access, and settlement

A military objective can use current control. A final restoration requires a valid political settlement. Directly owned and controlled territory qualifies when the necessary administration is complete. A satrapy qualifies only when it has accepted a charter, remains subordinate, controls the relevant territory, and provides the required access.

A temporary occupation by a faction ally does not become Persian restoration territory. Allied military access can support a supply route but does not count as sovereignty. An enemy's legal ownership does not make it a useful diplomatic recipient when another country actually controls the disputed territory. The confrontation must identify the controller, owner, and relevant overlord before selecting its target.

A subject with an unresolved rebellion does not satisfy a completed regional charter. A nominal puppet on the far side of an enemy-controlled corridor does not satisfy a connected imperial road. Naval connections can replace a land route only for projects that explicitly allow a maritime connection, with ports and usable access at both ends.

## Immediate homeland confrontation

The opening creates one current principal homeland confrontation. If several foreign countries hold homeland states, the player can choose the first target. Other targets remain listed without automatic simultaneous declarations. One country holding several states receives one demand covering the valid set.

The demand can seek withdrawal, transfer, recognition of Iranian authority, or war. A friendly holder can negotiate. A holder that is already at war with Iran becomes the existing campaign target without receiving a redundant war declaration. A subject holder is addressed through its actual diplomatic authority. Where the relevant engine route cannot support a valid demand, the player must see that limitation and the ordinary diplomatic path.

A refusal permits an immediate conventional war when legal. It does not automatically declare the war for a human player. AI Persia can declare when its survival, force balance, access, and international commitments make the confrontation viable. The opening army should make such a campaign plausible, not guarantee victory against every major faction.

## Wider expansion

Each main route opens regional projects through its focus branches. Unlocking a project reveals its exact state map, strategic target countries, peaceful settlement opportunities, and military option. Claims can be granted at this stage. War goals follow the relevant confrontation and route commitment.

Achaemenid projects can form a western or eastern sequence. Sasanian projects prioritize a secure western base and defensible frontier commands. Modern projects prioritize oil regions, ports, transport, and client agreements. No route unlocks every remote war goal in its first political focus.

Subjects, guarantees, and faction membership remain real. The player cannot assume a local opponent's allies will stay out. Scripted negotiation can conclude a settlement only within supported diplomatic conditions. It must not force an unrelated major war to end or transfer an ally's territory without a valid outcome.

## Peaceful settlement options

A weak regional state may accept a guarantee and access agreement before becoming a satrapy. A state with a hostile overlord may request Persian support for independence. A neighbor sharing an enemy may trade access and mutual defense without accepting subordination. A country holding disputed Iranian territory can choose a transfer settlement where actual ownership rules permit it.

These offers require consent. A human-controlled recipient receives the same meaningful choices as the AI. A treaty is not enforced by taking the recipient's units or changing its government before it responds.

A failed peaceful offer creates a cooldown and a clear reason. Repeatedly sending the same demand cannot build free legitimacy, reveal unlimited information, or indefinitely block the target's decisions.

## Claims, integration, and cores

Original Iranian cores can be restored to practical use after recovery under normal game rules. Outer claims provide a political objective and help define a settlement. They do not become cores on conquest.

Direct integration of an outer region requires peace or a secure local settlement, a funded administration project, a connected supply route, and sustained control for 180 days. The default reward is improved administration and access to local development, not blanket coring. Any exceptional new core requires a separate justification, a bounded state list, and country-package approval. The normal design does not need new outer cores to be powerful.

A satrapy exchanges direct control for obligations and regional agency. The empire can later integrate an eligible subject through a long constitutional settlement, but cannot take a living player's country by an unannounced button. Direct integration forfeits the subject's specialized charter benefit and may increase the center's administrative burden.

## Preventing bad maps

Do not create a satrapy from a single disconnected enclave simply because it has a famous historical name. Each proposed subject needs a viable capital, a territorial base, and a route for its obligations. Existing countries are reused where they fit. A large living country cannot be renamed into a Persian subject and have all unrelated territory treated as one ancient province.

If a required ancient site falls within a large modern state, the project can use that site's presence without claiming the entire state as an ancient city. For example, Ctesiphon must be tied to the verified location near Baghdad, and Persepolis to its actual site in Fars. The territorial puzzle must make such approximations visible.

## After conquest

A regional victory opens a 120-day settlement mission. The player chooses direct administration, a satrapy charter, or a negotiated withdrawal with access. The objective is to complete the chosen settlement, repair its critical connection, and stop active local fighting. A successful settlement grants the relevant one-time legitimacy gain. An incomplete settlement keeps military control but withholds the permanent milestone. A failed settlement can produce a withdrawal, renegotiation, or a local crisis.

The player always knows what remains unfinished. The empire's final borders are the result of campaigns and treaties that occurred in that save, not a static button that annexes everyone inside a historical outline.
