## The restoration

Iran announces the restoration of the Persian Empire and receives the means to act on that announcement immediately. A substantial army arrives with equipment, air support, officers, and a usable opening supply arrangement. Historical territories become strategic objectives. Other governments must decide whether to contain the restoration, bargain with it, or seek its protection.

The first campaign is about recovering a homeland and finding a sustainable base for expansion. The later campaign is about choosing how an empire should govern. A player can build a multinational system of satrapies, a centralized military monarchy, or a modern industrial bloc. Each route has its own political settlement, territorial priorities, internal opposition, army development, and conclusion.

The initial force is deliberately generous. Its strength comes from actual assets. It is not represented by a small combat modifier. The campaign after the opening must develop the factories, trained replacements, fuel, naval access, and regional administration that allow that army to remain effective.

All new labels in this package, including diagram labels, achievement labels, and focus anchors, are working design labels. They are not final player-facing localisation. Route and evolution names supplied in the original brief remain the design terminology. Final wording belongs to the implementation and localisation stages.

## Opening eligibility

A living Iranian country must control at least one valid, inhabited state in its normal homeland. The actual vanilla identity must be verified in the installed game. A cosmetic rename, a released Iran, or a changed government does not require a duplicate country tag.

A remnant with very little territory remains eligible. A government in exile with no controlled homeland does not. An Iranian country that has already completed an equivalent Persian imperial restoration is excluded. The implementation must inspect both Chaos Redux and installed DLC restoration paths before defining equivalence. A generic nationalist focus, a temporary claim, or a cosmetic name alone does not prove that a full restoration has happened.

An independent Iran is the standard receiver. A subject Iran is eligible only when the event can place it in an explicit restoration settlement with its overlord. The opening offers recognition of autonomy, release, or an independence confrontation. It cannot silently give a puppet unrestricted diplomatic powers. The army is still delivered to the Iranian state. The player sees which actions remain restricted until the settlement ends.

An existing war does not invalidate the idea. Iran retains its current enemies, allied commitments, territorial control, deployed units, and research. The event cannot reset a losing war or erase an occupation.

## What happens immediately

The same Iranian country gains the Persian imperial cosmetic identity, event-owned flag variants, the dedicated tree, the restoration category, and a provisional imperial administration. It keeps its existing government and characters until a later political choice changes them. Existing military assets remain in place.

The opening force is granted exactly once, using the highest active Event 071 evolution at the moment the event fires. The event records the package that was delivered. The army, air force, reserves, and supporting logistics are one transaction for repeat prevention, with separately auditable quantities. Saving, reloading, recapturing Tehran, switching governments, and later activating an evolution cannot repeat it.

The player receives the territorial overview and can begin a homeland recovery campaign. A foreign controller of Iranian homeland may be confronted immediately. A wider restoration does not begin with automatic declarations against every holder of an ancient imperial claim.

A major news event announces the restoration and describes its immediate strategic effect. Relevant neighbors receive a response opportunity. Remote countries receive the news without a redundant diplomatic event. The opening is not a super-event.

## The first campaign

The shared opening offers an immediate military review, a provisional constitutional settlement, and a practical supply program. The first focus is short enough to matter during the opening war. Political route selection follows a small common opening, without requiring a long industrial tree first.

Iran with a secure homeland can pursue the provisional settlement and choose a regional objective. Remnant Iran sees recovery and supply as its first priorities. Neither version waits through several years of focuses before it can use the force it has already received.

An early player decision identifies one principal strategic enemy. Multiple disputed regions held by the same country belong to that single confrontation. The interface shows the enemy's overlord, faction, guarantees, and other obvious commitments where the engine exposes them. A war objective is not a promise that the conflict will remain local.

Winning an opening war unlocks settlement choices. Direct recovery of Iranian territory restores the homeland. Conquered territory outside that homeland needs an administrative settlement or a subject arrangement. Temporary occupation is useful for a campaign, but it does not count as a completed imperial settlement.

## The three routes

**The Achaemenid Legacy** pursues the widest historical map. Its strength is a network of regional charters, roads, local elites, and satrapies. A large empire can be held together without directly governing every state. Its weakness is that powerful regional partners can bargain, withhold obligations, and organize against an overextended court.

**The Sasanian Restoration** builds a military center with fortified approaches, elite commands, and strong control of Mesopotamia, the Caucasus, the Gulf, and the eastern frontier. Its weakness is the concentration of political and military power in commands that may become rivals when the center loses authority.

**The New Persian Empire** builds an industrial and strategic bloc. Oil, ports, factories, airfields, mechanized forces, and commercial clients sustain its influence. It supports a republican or other non-monarchical government. Its weakness is dependence on a functioning economy, secure transport, and clients that expect useful investment in exchange for obligations.

All routes can use satrapies, the Immortals, Persepolis, and Gulf expansion. The routes change the terms and purpose of these systems. They do not lock a player out of basic logistics or an entire military service.

## Main player decisions

The central recurring choice is how much the imperial center demands from the territories it controls. Direct rule provides clearer control but requires integration and a garrison. Satrapies reduce direct administration and can provide specialized support, but they retain political agency. A negotiated settlement is slower to exploit than a conquest, yet can remain reliable during a distant war.

The second choice is where to commit the opening military advantage. Recovering the heartland protects the state. A Mesopotamian campaign can gain a strong western base. Gulf investment can protect imports and future naval growth. A distant prestige campaign can raise legitimacy, but also exposes supply and internal weaknesses.

The third choice is how to develop elite troops. The Immortals can remain a small, well-supported formation, expand as a mountain and desert command, or become a mechanized imperial guard. They consume training, equipment, manpower, and a share of the army's institutional capacity. They do not provide a free duplicate army.

## What success looks like

A successful route has a recognized political settlement, a recovered homeland, secure communications, at least one functioning outer administration, and military production that can replace losses. A large territorial silhouette alone is insufficient.

The main route conclusions are distinct. The Achaemenid path concludes with a chartered imperial assembly and a connected imperial network. The Sasanian path concludes with a functioning system of frontier commands under the center. The modern path concludes with an economically useful Persian-led bloc that can defend its own routes and members.

Evolution III adds an outer layer of supremacy. The empire can demand submission and tribute, guarantee subordinate states, and intervene for them. A monarchical route can use the title King of Kings. A non-monarchical route receives equivalent institutional powers without being forced to invent an emperor.

## Defeat and recovery

Major losses can damage legitimacy, encourage satrapy defiance, split military commands, and trigger rival governments. These failures develop from visible conditions. A stable empire does not disintegrate because of an unexplained random roll.

A country that loses a campaign may withdraw from an outer province, settle with a rival, or revise a charter. A government that loses its administrative center must relocate and restore command. A state that loses the homeland and cannot sustain a recognized center can fragment. The recovery route preserves the history of the restoration and never reissues the opening package.

This event does not add a separate world-end scenario. Persia remains an ordinary human country subject to the project's normal civilian and international systems.
