# 071 Persia: evolutions, crises, and collapse

## Evolution contract

Event 071 uses the supplied thresholds: Evolution I at Chaos 200 or above, Evolution II at 400 or above, and Evolution III at 600 or above. The active level is cumulative. Later content includes the earlier level's systems.

Before the root event, the active level determines the one-time opening package. After the root event, a newly active level opens its institutions and development. It does not deploy the corresponding opening army again. Equipment, units, claims, and subjects already created remain governed by their own rules.

Activation and visibility alone add no Chaos. Only concrete actions and consequences from the evolved systems can change Chaos. The level remains unlocked after a later fall in the world meter, subject to the project's shared evolution behavior. Its strongest actions can still require sufficient legitimacy and practical power.

## Evolution I: The Satraps Return

The basic satrapy system becomes a political network. Specialized charters, collective hearings, regional recruitment agreements, and powerful satraps become available. Existing subjects can adopt a specialization through a review instead of being destroyed and recreated.

The Achaemenid route gains a stronger assembly and more meaningful charter negotiations. The Sasanian route gains specialized frontier and military subjects. The modern route gains differentiated clients and imperial provinces with investment obligations.

The level introduces a valuable choice between stronger contributions and greater regional autonomy. It does not simply add a universal subject bonus. A well-supported frontier satrapy can contribute useful defense. A neglected one can seek a separate settlement. A material shortfall caused by occupation is treated differently from deliberate refusal.

When the root fires at this level, the army uses the Evolution I package. Later activation grants no units. It instead opens paid recruitment and development paths.

## Evolution II: The Immortals

The guard develops into a major military institution. The player can create advanced terrain, heavy, or mechanized guard formations and invest in their training, equipment, and command. The strongest variants should be among the best conventional human units on their intended missions.

The route interpretation remains important. Achaemenid recruitment can include chartered regional contingents. Sasanian development emphasizes the central command and operational reserve. Modern development emphasizes standard equipment, mechanization, and production.

Advanced guard capacity is earned through the relevant command program. It does not turn all existing infantry into elite units for free. Training and conversion must reserve capacity, charge the material difference, and preserve personnel accounting.

When the root fires at this level, the army uses the Evolution II package. Later activation opens the program without a second mobilization.

## Evolution III: King of Kings

A successful empire can claim a wider right to order regional affairs. After its route conclusion and legitimacy of at least 80, it can make submission offers, establish tribute agreements, guarantee subordinate states, and intervene in their wars.

The monarchical routes can use the personal title after their crown settlement. The modern route expresses the same power through an imperial institution and bloc leadership. The engine must not replace a republican leader with an invented emperor to make a title fit.

Targets must be reachable and politically relevant. A distant strong country outside Persian military or economic reach is not an eligible submission target merely because the world Chaos meter is high. A refusal can create an opportunity for pressure or war, not a guaranteed annexation.

The level also raises the consequences of failure. A broken imperial guarantee undermines the empire's public claim. Subjects that accepted protection have a reason to reconsider their place in the order. This is a concrete failure, not an automatic penalty for unlocking the level.

## Crisis stages

The empire uses visible situation labels, not another persistent meter. The stages are secure, strained, disputed, and fragmented. Legitimacy remains the single numerical custom value the player manages.

A strained situation starts after a meaningful loss, broken charter, missed obligation, or command dispute. It identifies the cause and offers a bounded remedy. A single failed shipment during a war does not create a national collapse.

A disputed center can begin when legitimacy remains below 20 for 30 days and at least one serious condition persists: control of less than 75 percent of the homeland, an unresolved loss of the administrative center, or active defiance from multiple established satrapies. The active military situation and the actual disputed institutions determine which crisis appears.

A major collapse becomes possible when a disputed center fails its recovery process and also loses its effective political or military base. Low legitimacy by itself is insufficient. Full capitulation or complete loss of the homeland can accelerate the territorial consequences under normal game rules, but still requires clear reporting and asset accounting.

## Crisis one: loss of the administrative center

The government can relocate to a valid controlled homeland center. A 120-day command-restoration mission asks the player to establish communications, retain a supplied field force, and complete an administrative settlement. The mission must contain actions the player can perform.

Success stabilizes the government and recovers only the legitimacy tied to that particular institutional loss. Partial success keeps a smaller functioning state but suspends outer ambitions. Failure can open a command split or a subject secession hearing when the corresponding actors actually exist.

The old capital's buildings remain on the map. Evacuation moves only what an explicitly supported and funded evacuation action allows. It cannot teleport the entire industry to a surviving mountain province.

## Crisis two: a charter coalition

This crisis requires multiple established subjects with a common grievance. It can follow repeated extraordinary levies, an ignored charter, or a failure to provide promised defense. Subjects that are loyal and unaffected do not join automatically.

The center can convene a hearing, repay or revise the disputed obligation, accept a smaller imperial settlement, or attempt enforcement. A peaceful compromise can preserve the wider network. Enforcement can produce coordinated refusal or a real secession conflict.

The hearing lasts 120 days. Individual subjects have their own response and territorial situation. A human subject receives a choice. The outcome must not delete its government before it responds.

## Crisis three: the command split

A command split requires a powerful command institution, a serious defeat or loss of central authority, and an unresolved command dispute. The challenger is an existing commander or a clearly defined institutional group with a real territorial base.

The center can replace the command, negotiate a new appointment settlement, or confront a revolt. Replacing it may require loyal forces and control of its administrative center. Negotiation can preserve the army while conceding regional authority. A revolt is limited to the actual command's base and forces.

There is no default transfer of half the country's army or territory. Command assignments and location guide the split, with explicit treatment for forces on remote fronts. A unit is assigned once. It cannot remain in Persia and also appear in the revolt.

## Crisis four: modern commitments fail

A modern empire can face a combined contract and security crisis when it misses major investments, loses essential transport, and fails to protect clients. The relevant agreements are listed. An unrelated peaceful client does not leave simply because Persia has low fuel for one day.

Recovery can reduce the number of commitments, deliver real equipment, repair a route, or negotiate a smaller bloc. The player can preserve the homeland and domestic industry while accepting the loss of an outer sphere. This is a valid reduced success state.

## Fragmentation and asset accounting

If collapse proceeds, subjects decide their own exit or renewed relationship according to the crisis settlement. Directly governed outer regions can become successor administrations only where a viable territory and legal release route exist. The Iranian homeland is not randomly sliced into equal pieces.

Existing units follow documented command, territory, and allegiance rules. The regular force and guard remain finite. Equipment stockpiles, manpower, aircraft, ships, and convoys must have a declared one-time allocation. Any explicit transition grant must be small, separately justified, and cannot duplicate the opening mobilization.

Subjects keep their own existing assets. A released province does not receive a copy of every Persian technology or fleet. Naval and air assets use actual bases and ownership. A ship cannot be assigned to a landlocked successor simply to satisfy an equal split.

Normal occupation, peace conference, and capitulation rules continue to apply. A collapse event cannot silently end an unrelated global war or steal allied territory.

## Recovery after fragmentation

The surviving Iranian state retains the fact that Event 071 fired. It can recover the homeland, rebuild legitimacy from outstanding losses, and reconstruct a smaller route institution. It cannot activate the root event again.

A restored route resumes its existing history and first-completion records. Rebuilding Persepolis after damage can recover its previous institutional function without another first-restoration reward. Reconquering a former satrapy needs a new political settlement and does not erase the earlier breach.

The final presentation can acknowledge a reduced empire, a negotiated federation, or a renewed center. It must reflect the actual result instead of declaring universal recovery after one successful mission.
