# 071 Persia: The Achaemenid Legacy

## Route purpose

This route builds a large empire through regional settlements and connected administration. It has the greatest territorial reach, but direct annexation is not its default method. Its strongest version combines a secure Iranian center, useful satrapies, a recognized imperial assembly, and routes that carry troops and supplies between them.

The historical references are a basis for imagery and political claims. The modern constitutional settlement, tolerance choices, and exact satrapy powers are alternate-history design. They must not be described as a faithful reconstruction of one ancient constitution.

## Entry and first commitment

The restoration assembly brings the existing Iranian government, military, and regional representatives into a new settlement. A suitable living monarch can retain the crown under the new settlement. A constitutional government can establish an imperial office with legal limits. A government unwilling to recognize any such office should use the modern route.

The initial route choice promises local institutions a place in the empire. That promise affects later action. Regional cooperation becomes easier, but demands that contradict the charter cost legitimacy and can make subjects collectively resist.

## Branch A: the imperial charter

The first anchors define the powers of the center, protect ordinary local administration, and set a standard form for satrapy obligations. Completing this branch unlocks a charter hearing for each eligible regional settlement. It does not automatically create subjects in unconquered countries.

The main fork concerns representation. A consultative assembly leaves the center freer to act during war but gives regional actors fewer reasons to accept unpopular demands. A binding assembly protects agreed obligations and makes negotiated expansion more attractive, while requiring consent for extraordinary levies. These alternatives replace the same imperial settlement spirit.

The branch concludes with a recognized imperial charter after the homeland is recovered, a first satrapy settlement is functioning, and a political hearing has been completed. The reward is a better charter framework and a one-time legitimacy milestone. It is not a global compliance bonus applied to every province in the world.

## Branch B: roads and regional administrations

The road branch selects real communications between the Iranian center, Mesopotamia, the Caucasus, and outer regions. A regional plan begins with surveys and access agreements. Construction then improves actual rail links, local infrastructure, and a useful supply connection.

The player chooses which corridor receives investment first. Western investment supports Mesopotamia and later the Mediterranean. Eastern investment supports Afghanistan, Central Asia, and the Indus approach. Northern investment supports the Caucasus and its difficult terrain. Several can eventually be completed, but simultaneous construction competes for factories and equipment.

A road milestone requires an uninterrupted usable connection, not the possession of scattered states. A naval link is acceptable for the Mediterranean network only when both ports and transit conditions are valid. A missing bridge in the territorial plan must be resolved through construction, access, or a campaign.

A completed regional administration unlocks local recruitment agreements, repair contracts, and a stronger settlement choice. It does not create free manpower every time the player reopens the category.

## Branch C: western restoration

Mesopotamia is the first major western administrative project. It can be governed directly or through a satrapy with a viable capital and western supply obligations. A Ctesiphon restoration can follow, but must use the actual site and a local political settlement.

The next split chooses an Anatolian and Mediterranean approach or a Levantine approach. Both require access and a prepared base. The route should present the next real holders as strategic enemies, including overlords and faction partners. A claim on a region is not permission to bypass diplomacy.

Egypt is a late outer project. Its opening requires a functioning western connection or a supported maritime route, a major regional settlement, and legitimacy of at least 60. The player can accept a regional government with tribute and access or fund direct administration. An isolated occupation without supply does not complete the restoration.

The western conclusion creates a durable western charter. It provides a useful subject obligation, secure access, and a regional development program. It does not require every possible ancient western territory for the basic route ending.

## Branch D: eastern restoration

The eastern branch begins with a prepared frontier corridor and a settlement in the Afghan approaches. The empire can support a willing regional government, negotiate access, or pursue a conventional campaign. Local obligations reflect the region's actual capabilities. A poor frontier state is not asked for the same industrial deliveries as a developed coastal subject.

The route then separates a Central Asian network from an Indus frontier project. The first emphasizes long-distance supply and frontier partners. The second emphasizes a bounded eastern territorial ambition with a well-supported army. The state list must not include all of British India simply because the ancient empire reached the Indus.

An eastern charter requires a completed route, functioning local administration, and a period without unresolved rebellion. Its reward is a stable eastern base and a stronger imperial network. It also gives regional actors more bargaining power during a succession crisis because their forces and territory now matter to the empire.

## Branch E: peoples of the empire

The center can preserve local institutions, languages of administration, and established authorities under a common charter. This reduces the frequency of demands for intervention, but limits how much manpower and equipment the center can extract at once.

An emergency requisition path permits larger temporary contributions during a serious war. It requires a stated purpose and expires after the emergency period. Keeping emergency rules after the war becomes an explicit decision with a political cost. A player cannot obtain the benefits of a binding charter and unlimited arbitrary levies at the same time.

The branch includes a reconciliation hearing after a broken promise. The empire can repay a missed investment, lower an obligation, or accept a local representative with stronger powers. These are concrete settlements. A small political power payment alone cannot erase every breach.

## Branch F: the royal or imperial guard

The Achaemenid interpretation of the Immortals is a professional central guard that represents the imperial office across several regions. Its early specialization is reliable infantry suited to difficult marches. Later development can produce a mountain and desert formation or a mechanized central reserve.

A multicultural recruitment charter expands the eligible recruitment base through consenting satrapies. Contributions are deducted from the contributing country's available manpower through a verified route. Recruitment does not manufacture people from an abstract imperial map.

The guard's command remains tied to the central institution. If the empire fractures, its formations do not become five copies under five claimants. The force-accounting rules in part 12 decide whether each existing formation stays with the recognized center or defects.

## Route conclusion: a chartered imperial network

The conclusion requires the recovered homeland, completed charter, restored Persepolis political project, at least three durable regional charters outside the homeland, and usable connections to those regions. At least one must be a western settlement and one an eastern or northern settlement. Legitimacy must be at least 60, and no constituent charter can be in active secession.

The reward is an improved imperial settlement, access to an assembly-wide development program, and a major presentation milestone. The empire can coordinate a limited common military contribution through its existing agreements. It still has to pay and reinforce those forces.

A separate maximum-restoration ambition requires the verified greater imperial map and its settlements. This is deliberately harder and suited to late campaigns. The route's ordinary conclusion must remain achievable without a world war against every outer holder.

## Evolution interactions and failure

Evolution I adds specialized satrapies and collective charter hearings. Evolution II makes the guard a larger professional institution. Evolution III allows a broader hierarchy of tributary states, guarantees, and submission agreements under the imperial assembly.

The distinctive failure is a charter coalition. Several subjects facing the same broken obligation can negotiate together. The player can honor the charter, revise its terms, or try to enforce central authority. Force is possible but risks coordinated secession. A functioning assembly can mediate a military setback and save the outer empire. An assembly repeatedly ignored by the center becomes a forum for opposition.

The AI favors this route when it has viable prospective subjects, multiple regional opportunities, and enough stability to invest in long connections. It does not choose the widest claim project before it can supply a first regional campaign.
