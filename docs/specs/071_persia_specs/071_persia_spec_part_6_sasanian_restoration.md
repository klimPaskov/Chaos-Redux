# 071 Persia: The Sasanian Restoration

## Route purpose

The Sasanian route builds a centralized military empire with strong western and eastern commands. Its reach is substantial, but it values defensible corridors, reliable armies, fortified approaches, and control of strategic centers more than the widest possible historical outline.

Sasanian history supplies references to kingship, central government, Mesopotamia, and eastern and western rivalry. The 20th-century military district system in this design is fictional. Heavy cavalry traditions influence unit identity and doctrine direction. They do not imply that mounted lancers should defeat modern tanks.

## Entry and political settlement

The military restoration gives senior commands formal responsibilities in building the state. A living, eligible monarch can head the restoration. A temporary military council can administer the transition where a crown settlement has not yet been completed, but the route must explicitly resolve its final executive form.

The early branch defines the relationship between the crown or central executive and the commands. Officers receive resources and responsibilities. They also receive limits, reporting duties, and an eventual process for replacement. The government cannot obtain complete command autonomy and complete central control from the same choice.

## Branch A: the central command

The first anchors establish a general staff, a system of regional commands, and an officer development program. They improve planning through actual training and staff decisions, not a succession of small national spirits.

A command charter assigns each active frontier a responsibility. The western command defends Mesopotamia and the approaches from Anatolia and the Levant. The northern command organizes difficult Caucasus routes. The eastern command manages the Afghan and Central Asian approaches. These are institutional roles. The game should not create a new country for each command during normal operation.

The main fork chooses appointed commands or entrenched frontier houses. Appointed commands are easier to rotate and less likely to become political claimants. Entrenched commands can provide a stronger local military contribution and more flexible frontier administration, but gain political weight during a weak succession or defeat.

## Branch B: Mesopotamia and the western base

The western branch opens a prepared campaign for Mesopotamia, followed by a local settlement and a Ctesiphon project. It requires supply preparation appropriate to the actual front. An already friendly regional government can accept a frontier charter without conquest.

After Mesopotamia, the player chooses between a fortified western boundary and an outward campaign toward eastern Anatolia or the Levant. The defensive option creates actual fortification and supply projects along verified approaches. The outward option requires a second strategic assessment and commits the western command to a broader mission.

The key tradeoff is timing. Defenses and supply make the empire harder to dislodge. A further campaign can gain strategic depth and legitimacy while the enemy is weak. Trying to do both at once uses the same construction and military resources.

The branch concludes with a secured western command. It needs settled territory, a functioning supply base, and a tested command charter. Temporary occupation of Baghdad alone is insufficient.

## Branch C: the northern and eastern frontiers

The northern branch develops mountain warfare, prepared passes, depots, and regional access. A Caucasus settlement may be direct, a frontier satrapy, or a guarantee with access. The route must not require annexing a friendly state when its treaty already meets the military purpose.

The eastern branch organizes long-distance logistics and a viable frontier administration. The player can invest in a strong inner line or extend the command through a regional partner. The outer choice provides room and local support but creates a longer commitment.

A frontier project requires a completed connection and a deployable force suited to the terrain. A five-state line of forts without a supplied army is not a completed frontier command. The exact fort provinces and rail paths remain map-audit work.

## Branch D: cavalry traditions and armored forces

The cavalry tradition branch first improves mounted reconnaissance, mobile staff work, and the transition of existing formations into a modern mobile arm. It preserves useful cavalry where the setting and equipment make sense. It does not force every mobile division to remain horse-based.

The main development fork is between an armored breakthrough command and a more flexible mobile frontier army. The armored choice needs armor production, fuel, maintenance, and concentrated supply. The frontier choice needs motor transport, engineers, mountain preparation, and a wider repair network.

Both can later access ordinary modern equipment. The first institutional choice determines the most developed command and its distinctive guard formation. It is not a permanent ban on researching the other equipment family.

A modernization program converts eligible units by changing their real equipment needs and training, without duplicating their manpower. The player must provide the additional vehicles. The army does not gain tanks simply because a focus description invokes cataphracts.

## Branch E: the Immortal command

The Sasanian interpretation of the Immortals emphasizes central military loyalty and disciplined operational reserves. It can produce a heavy guard suited to breakthrough and a mountain guard for the northern approaches. These are conventional elite troops with costs and vulnerabilities.

The center chooses whether to keep the guard concentrated near its strategic base or distribute detachments to frontier commands. Concentration improves political reliability and provides a reserve. Distribution gives stronger frontier response but exposes guard officers to local command politics.

At Evolution II, advanced guard institutions can increase the force cap and improve its training. The strongest form should outperform ordinary infantry on its intended mission, while still requiring equipment, reinforcement, supply, and air protection. It cannot ignore encirclement or become immune to attrition.

## Branch F: the state and its traditions

The route can support historical sites, court ceremonies, and cultural institutions without replacing the country's religion by decree. A cultural restoration policy is distinct from a compulsory religious policy. This package does not add a religious conversion system.

A court patronage branch invests in Persepolis, Ctesiphon, and a contemporary administrative center. The player chooses whether ceremonies reinforce central oversight or give frontier commands a public place in the imperial order. The first strengthens the center's legal claim. The second can ease command rivalry at the cost of acknowledging local status.

Final names, symbols, quotations, and religious imagery require research. An ancient motif cannot be treated as a documented modern national flag merely because it looks suitable.

## Route conclusion: a functioning command system

The conclusion requires the recovered homeland, a completed western settlement, a northern or eastern frontier settlement, a funded central army institution, and working supply to the active commands. The political oversight branch must be resolved. Legitimacy must be at least 60.

A completed command system upgrades the imperial settlement and unlocks coordinated frontier mobilization. The mobilization draws from the empire's real reserves and consenting or charter-bound subjects. It does not reissue the opening army.

The maximum route ambition secures both eastern and western systems and demonstrates that the center can sustain a serious regional war without losing command cohesion. This can support a route-specific super-event after the durable milestone conditions are met.

## Evolution interactions and failure

Evolution I gives frontier satrapies deeper duties and creates powerful satraps with military interests. Evolution II expands the guard command. Evolution III allows imperial guarantees and interventions, with the commands acting as guarantors of the wider order.

The distinctive failure is a command split. A major defeat, low legitimacy, and an unresolved command dispute can produce an ultimatum from an existing commander or institutional faction. The center can dismiss and replace the command, negotiate a new settlement, or confront a territorial revolt.

A revolt is bounded by the command's actual territorial base and existing forces. It does not receive half the entire country or a duplicate army by default. A loyal command in another region can support the center, but cannot transfer its army across an impossible route instantly.

The AI favors this route when it has a threatened homeland, useful land frontiers, and a government compatible with a central military restoration. It invests in supply and a secure command before opening a second distant war.
