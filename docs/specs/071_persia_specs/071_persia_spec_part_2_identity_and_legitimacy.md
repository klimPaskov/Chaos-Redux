# 071 Persia: government, identity, and legitimacy

## A state that remembers its past

Restoration changes the country's declared identity and its political agenda. It does not replace the country's existing history. The old army remains the old army. A surviving government remains in office. Existing elections, advisers, commanders, research, national spirits, and diplomacy are retained unless a later, explicit choice changes them.

The imperial identity must have appropriate ideology variants. The same state can present a crowned restoration, a constitutional imperial federation, a military restoration, or a modern republican sphere. Names and flag designs must fit the chosen institutions. Calling the country an empire does not require a monarch on the first day.

No ancient ruler is resurrected. Cyrus, Darius, Xerxes, and Sasanian rulers can be invoked as historical references after research, but cannot become contemporary leaders. Real living claimants and officers must come from an audited existing roster or documented research. An interim institutional government is a valid presentation when the setting has no suitable personal claimant. It must not be disguised as a fabricated historical person.

## Provisional settlement

The common opening establishes a provisional administration. The player then chooses the basis of authority before locking a main route.

**Retain the existing constitution.** The government continues to operate under its current basic rules while it claims a wider imperial role. This is the direct entry to the modern route. A democratic country can later choose a constitutional crown through a separate public settlement. It cannot silently become an absolute monarchy.

**Convene a restoration assembly.** Regional elites, military representatives, and the current government negotiate a crown and charter. This supports the Achaemenid route. Accepting limits on central demands improves early cooperation with prospective satrapies. It also makes later emergency requisition more politically expensive.

**Authorize a military restoration.** The center gives military commands a formal role in constructing the new state. This supports the Sasanian route. The choice provides a faster path to frontier administration but creates organized military interests that must later be brought under civilian or royal oversight.

Route selection is a real commitment. A country can recover from political crisis within its chosen route, but cannot collect the final institutions of all three routes by repeatedly changing government. Government change after a route lock adapts titles and characters while preserving the route's administrative settlement. A special constitutional revision can change the form of the executive without resetting the tree.

## Imperial legitimacy

Legitimacy is the single persistent public custom meter, ranging from 0 to 100. It represents whether the restoration has established a credible political order. It is not popularity, combat strength, or a substitute for stability and war support.

The normal starting value is 25. A remnant controlling less than 25 percent of the verified homeland starts at 15. An established Iran that already controls its full homeland still starts at 25 because the larger imperial claim has not yet been established. Existing stability does not manufacture imperial legitimacy.

| Band | Political meaning | Principal effect |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 19 | Disputed restoration | Outer institutions are vulnerable and major new submission demands are unavailable |
| 20 to 39 | Provisional center | Homeland recovery and basic satrapy settlements are available |
| 40 to 59 | Recognized regional empire | Advanced charters and important restoration projects can conclude |
| 60 to 79 | Established imperial order | Route conclusions and stronger external commitments become possible |
| 80 to 100 | Dominant imperial center | Evolution III supremacy content can reach its full form |

These bands are authored tuning proposals. Their purpose is to distinguish stages of political development. They should not create an invisible flat combat bonus on top of every route reward.

## Sources and setbacks

Recovering the full Iranian homeland awards 10 legitimacy once. Settling Mesopotamia awards 10 once. A recognized Caucasus settlement and a secure Gulf settlement each award 5 once. Completing the first durable satrapy charter awards 5. Completing the political restoration at Persepolis awards 10. A main route's institutional conclusion awards 10. A documented victory over the principal regional opponent awards 5 when it produces a lasting settlement, not for each battle.

Each geographical or institutional reward has one first-completion record. Releasing a subject, annexing it, and releasing it again cannot repeat the gain. Losing and regaining the same capital cannot generate unlimited legitimacy.

A serious loss of a previously settled region removes 5 or 10 according to its importance. Loss of the current administrative capital removes 10. A failed imperial guarantee removes 10. A forcibly broken charter removes 5. A completed voluntary constitutional compromise can restore 5 once per distinct crisis. A recaptured region can recover the legitimacy actually lost from that region, with a ceiling equal to the outstanding loss. It never awards the original first-completion gain a second time.

Routine battle outcomes, isolated province changes, and small temporary occupations do not move the meter. A major region loss is confirmed after a 30-day loss period, except a genuine administrative capital evacuation, which is immediate. This avoids a constantly flickering meter.

## Offices and competing institutions

The empire uses existing government and character surfaces where possible. The player appoints a head of regional administration, an army command, and a development authority through meaningful route choices. These offices are design roles, not three mandatory new named characters.

Achaemenid offices manage charters and the imperial assembly. Sasanian offices supervise military districts and elite formations. Modern offices coordinate industrial investment and client agreements. Appointments affect the kinds of settlement that are easier to negotiate. They should not become a large separate council minigame with another set of numerical loyalties.

Internal disputes are presented as concrete issues. A governor may reject an extra equipment levy. A general may demand control of a frontier province. Industrial clients may ask for investment before renewing access. The player sees the disputed obligation, the actor's reasons, and the available settlements. Supporting calculations remain hidden.

## National spirit budget

The tree owns no more than three national spirit slots at once.

| Slot | Purpose | Lifetime |
|---|---|---|
| Imperial settlement | The chosen administrative model and its current health | Provisional version, then route-specific upgrades or crisis replacement |
| Mobilization settlement | The opening military support arrangement and its transition to normal production | Temporary opening stage, then production or demobilization settlement |
| Guard command | The developed Immortals institution | Absent initially until the guard command is formally established, then staged upgrades |

A route changes the existing settlement spirit instead of adding a new one for each reform. A collapse replaces a healthy institution with its damaged version. It does not stack several crisis spirits on top of it. State projects use state-level results where they genuinely concern local administration or construction.

Inherited Iranian spirits are preserved. They are outside the three newly owned slots, but their interactions must be audited. The tree must not obtain excessive bonuses by keeping an obsolete inherited problem and also applying a duplicate cure. A dedicated compatibility settlement resolves the exact inherited situation after the installed content is inspected.

## Political costs with visible alternatives

Emergency rule permits a stronger one-time demand from subjects but weakens the charter settlement. Parliamentary or assembly consent takes longer and can limit immediate returns, but prevents that breach. A military command may be retained after a victory for faster frontier response, or reduced in power to protect the center from a future coup.

The player should be able to explain why the state is stable or unstable from its recent choices. There is no separate hidden random collapse timer. Legitimacy bands constrain the strongest actions, while actual military defeats and broken obligations create specific crises.

## Acceptance

A democratic or communist Iran can complete a coherent modern imperial route without a forced personal monarchy. An existing monarch can remain in office through an Achaemenid or Sasanian settlement when alive and eligible. A lost or replaced leader does not strand a route. Changing flags or political titles does not create a new country or refund earlier costs.

The legitimacy tooltip explains current value, band, recent changes, and important unresolved causes. It does not expose every supporting calculation as a second public meter. All route conclusions require both legitimacy and real institutional achievements.
