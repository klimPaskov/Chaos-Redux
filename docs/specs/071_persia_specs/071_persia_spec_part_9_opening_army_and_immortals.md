# 071 Persia: opening army and the Immortals

## The opening force is real

The event adds deployed formations, the manpower and equipment those formations require, and separately defined reserves. Existing Iranian forces remain untouched. This is the exceptional military grant authorized by the brief. Later recruitment uses ordinary resources and explicit programs.

The figures below are initial balance proposals. They are not measurements from a running game. The implementation must preserve their intended scale while using legal templates, actual equipment requirements, and verified unit-family systems. A hidden downgrade to a few ordinary divisions would fail the design.

## Tier and campaign scaling

The opening tier is the highest active Event 071 evolution at the root firing. The tier remains recorded permanently for the grant. A later evolution does not increase the opening package retrospectively.

The composition table is the minimum package before campaign scaling. It counts new native division objects, including smaller guard formations. It does not assert that every listed formation has the same manpower or combat width.

| Formation family | Baseline | Evolution I | Evolution II | Evolution III |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Conventional infantry | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
| Mountain formations | 5 | 5 | 10 | 10 |
| Motorized or mechanized formations | 0 | 5 | 10 | 15 |
| Armored formations | 0 | 5 | 5 | 15 |
| Immortal guard formations | 5 | 5 | 10 | 15 |
| Total new formations | 20 | 35 | 55 | 80 |

The baseline guards are compact professional formations. They do not equal five full heavy divisions. The opening total must be judged by combat manpower, battalions, equipment, and supply as well as division count.

Apply a campaign factor of 100 percent normally, 125 percent from 1940, and 150 percent from 1943. A country with at least 50 usable factories can move up one factor step, capped at 150 percent. This avoids giving a much later or already industrialized Iran an opening force designed only for 1936. Dates are campaign gates, not a promise of historical equipment availability.

Scale the conventional families and reserve budgets, rounding generated formation totals to a usable five-formation increment. Keep the guard opening within its separate manpower capacity. Added formations should favor the tier's existing composition. Do not use the context factor to introduce a family absent from that tier. The displayed grant review must show the resolved composition before the player continues.

## Equipment quality and training

Infantry and mountain formations use a practical conventional template with artillery and support appropriate to available research. They must arrive trained and equipped enough to conduct an opening campaign. A tier is not fulfilled by deploying empty shells with division names. Mountain formations must also satisfy the actual native special-forces capacity rules. The opening settlement must provide a bounded legal entitlement for its delivered mountain force where required, without an unlimited special-forces exemption or a fourth national spirit. Later mountain recruitment follows the verified ordinary capacity system.

Early armor uses legal early armor. Later armor can use stronger designs when the campaign and research support them. Evolution II and III favor mechanized units when a valid family and a sustainable production route exist. A 1936 evolution must not silently grant mutually incompatible future technologies. If mechanization is unavailable, that tier's mobile component uses a fully equipped contemporary motorized force and the tree offers the explicit mechanization development path. This is a date-dependent composition rule, not permission to replace every elite component with basic infantry.

Any minimal production unlock needed for a granted legal equipment design must be explicit and checked in the technology graph. Do not copy a major country's full research tree. Existing doctrine choices remain intact.

Most conventional units arrive at regular training. Guard formations arrive at the highest supported non-combat training level appropriate to the design. They do not receive fabricated combat histories. Commanders are assigned from an audited living roster or a clearly fictional, properly created officer package. Officers must not duplicate the same person under several identities.

## Deployed equipment versus reserves

The implementation calculates the actual manpower and equipment consumed by the selected templates first. Those quantities belong to the deployed force. The reserve table is additional stock after that deployment. The two accounts must never be merged into one ambiguous grant.

Experience and command power respect their actual caps. Fuel is added only up to real storage capacity, with the resolved amount reported. The support review offers a storage project where capacity is insufficient. It must not claim that inaccessible overflow fuel was delivered. Equipment families with actual maximum storage or other engine constraints receive the same honest treatment.

Trains, trucks, and convoys are military assets, even when a landlocked country cannot yet use all of them. No fleet is spawned in an inland province. A convoy reserve can wait for future port access without creating a fictional naval base.

Aircraft use verified, compatible designs. Valid airfields receive usable wings within capacity. The remainder stays in the actual equipment reserve. The review distinguishes operating aircraft from reserve aircraft. The air development branch provides base expansion and production, so the reserve does not remain unusable indefinitely.

The same campaign factor can scale aircraft and material reserves. Experience and command power remain the listed tier grants. Their function is preparation, not a large later-game currency windfall.

## Placement and remnant Iran

Formations are deployed across controlled, legal homeland provinces, favoring usable supply and defensible assembly areas. The allocation must not put every new division into one mountain province when a better distribution exists. Existing combat positions are not deleted to make room.

A remnant still receives the full tier minimum. Its immediate manpower includes returning personnel and the event's exceptional mobilization. This is fictional event causation. It must not be described as an exact historical demographic calculation. The grant does not also add those deployed people to the free manpower pool.

If the remnant has only one legal deployment area, the army must still exist immediately. The player receives a deployment warning and a focused supply-relief project. The opening mobilization spirit reduces supply consumption by a proposed 15, 25, 35, or 50 percent by tier for 180 days. It does not remove normal supply rules or make the army invulnerable. The implementation must test whether the smallest eligible remnant can actually operate. A failure here requires a proper local logistics solution, not delaying most of the army into an undisclosed future grant.

The event must not invent an overseas port, transfer another country's railway, or teleport a newly raised army into occupied enemy territory.

## Custom Immortals family

The Immortals are a real custom elite subunit family registered through the project's shared unit systems. A renamed ordinary infantry template is insufficient. Its counter, equipment requirements, recruitment, reinforcement, model, sound routing, and documentation belong to the same feature.

The core family is elite infantry. Its later variants are a mountain and desert guard, a heavy central guard, and a mechanized guard. Each variant must retain a conventional weakness. Infantry guard units are vulnerable to armor and air attack without support. Mountain specialists should not also receive universal armor-level breakthrough. Mechanized guards require vehicles, fuel, maintenance, and appropriate terrain.

The proposed core elite advantage is approximately 15 percent better organization and 10 percent better appropriate attack or defense than a comparable ordinary formation, with 25 percent higher equipment or training burden. Advanced Evolution II institutions can improve selected advantages by a further 5 percent. These are design targets for comparative tests, not an instruction to stack every bonus on every variant.

## Capacity and reinforcement

Guard capacity is measured in authorized guard manpower, including deployed and training formations. The opening capacity floors are 10,000, 20,000, 35,000, and 50,000 by tier. Beyond that floor, the ceiling can grow to 15, 20, 25, or 30 percent of the non-guard deployed army's manpower, respectively. Use the greater of the floor and the calculated share. The denominator excludes guard manpower to avoid circular growth.

The initial floor comes from the recorded opening tier. Later floor and share upgrades require the corresponding paid guard institution, even after an evolution unlocks its availability. No capacity upgrade itself grants personnel. Opening guard personnel are distributed across the listed guard formations within that capacity. A larger context-scaled regular army can support a higher later guard ceiling, but does not grant extra free guard manpower beyond the opening allocation.

The cap includes equipment-less shells and training queues. Copying a template, splitting a formation, converting to another guard variant, lending units, or renaming a division cannot increase authorized manpower. A conversion charges the material difference and training cost without duplicating existing people. Disbanding returns only what the normal system returns.

If the regular army later shrinks below the share requirement, existing guard units are not instantly deleted. New recruitment and expansion stop until the force is within capacity. Reinforcing authorized surviving guard units remains possible within the previously committed manpower ceiling. This prevents a battlefield loss from causing a second unexplained deletion.

## Later growth

Basic guard recruitment is unlocked by formalizing guard command. Evolution I permits regional recruitment agreements. Evolution II adds advanced formations and command development. Evolution III permits a larger guard institution and imperial commitments, but every new formation still requires equipment and people.

Later recruitment and conversion decisions use visible costs, a real training period, and a capacity reservation. A canceled or invalid project releases its reservation and returns only the unconsumed part of its payment. There is no second free army hidden in the focus tree.
