# 071 Persia: The New Persian Empire

## Route purpose

The modern route turns historical legitimacy into an industrial, military, and diplomatic bloc. Its influence is built through useful production, oil access, transport, clients, and security agreements. A republican government can complete it without a crown.

The route can become highly coercive, but coercion has practical conditions. A modern imperial demand needs military reach, economic leverage, or an existing treaty. A country outside that reach cannot be subordinated by a generic global decision.

## Entry and constitution

The government confirms a modern constitutional settlement. It can preserve its present executive form or pursue a defined domestic reform. The empire's public language adapts to monarchy, republic, military government, or another supported ideology.

A constitutional continuity path offers a more predictable basis for agreements. An emergency development authority can make faster state-directed commitments during war but creates a later obligation to resolve exceptional powers. The route does not erase existing domestic problems merely by renaming the administration.

## Branch A: domestic industrial foundations

The first anchors survey the actual industrial base, expand practical workshops, establish repair capacity, and create a production program for the opening army's main equipment families. The player sees which shortages matter.

The early industrial choice is between broad domestic repair and a concentrated arsenal. Repair capacity reduces the risk that a generous starting force becomes unusable after its first campaign. The arsenal creates a stronger production base for one chosen family but concentrates investment in fewer states.

Later anchors support heavy industry, research institutions, and a second production center. They open actual construction programs and research support. They do not place unlimited factories in a single full state or ignore building limits. Completed prior construction can satisfy the objective without paying its reward twice.

## Branch B: oil and contracts

The oil branch begins with an audit of actual ownership, infrastructure, contracts, and transport. A country cannot nationalize an industry it does not control. A foreign concession must identify a real partner or existing arrangement from the installed campaign.

The player can pursue negotiated participation, direct state control where legally and practically possible, or a concession-led expansion. Negotiation trades some immediate returns for access to investment and equipment. Direct control requires domestic capacity and can worsen relations with affected powers. Concession-led expansion is faster when a willing partner exists, but creates deliveries or access obligations.

The branch includes an explicit renegotiation path. Once domestic industry and naval protection improve, the empire can seek better terms. The partner can accept, bargain, or retaliate through supported diplomatic means. The result depends on real leverage, not a guaranteed focus outcome.

Oil production is useful only with fuel processing and transport. The branch therefore connects to storage, rail, ports, and escort capacity. It must not provide enormous fuel reserves with no future means to replenish them.

## Branch C: modern armed forces

A standards branch consolidates equipment production and training. The army can choose a mobile combined-arms priority or a more economical regional army with strong artillery and air support. Both require factories and replacement capacity.

Mechanization follows a verified technology and equipment path. New formations consume manpower and equipment. Converting an existing guard or regular formation retains its people and requires only the legitimate additional material and training costs. The player cannot repeatedly convert units for free XP or equipment.

The air force develops airfields, maintenance, fighters, and battlefield support before long-range commitments. A modern empire should be able to coordinate air power across the region. It must still build the bases and maintain the aircraft.

The naval branch provides coastal protection first and a stronger regional fleet later. Trade dependence gives the modern route a reason to invest in escorts and access rather than treating naval focuses as optional decoration.

## Branch D: clients and investment

The empire can offer a development agreement to a viable partner. The first offer may exchange construction support or equipment for access and a defined delivery. It does not immediately turn the partner into a puppet.

A successful agreement opens a deeper client settlement. The partner can join an economic association, accept a defense agreement, or become an imperial subject. Each level has distinct obligations. The player cannot count a friendly trade partner as a satrapy for an achievement or territorial conclusion.

Investment projects need a consenting recipient, an actual destination, and a result that can be checked. A rail investment should improve a useful connection. An arsenal investment should create or expand production within ordinary limits. If war interrupts the project, the agreement records incomplete work and offers a renegotiation or partial settlement.

Clients expect protection and performance. Missing an agreed investment can make a client bargaining or defiant. Abandoning a client during a guaranteed war damages legitimacy. A well-treated client can remain reliable even when the empire loses a border battle.

## Branch E: the imperial market

The market branch coordinates established agreements. It does not introduce a new spendable currency or pretend that HOI4 supports a full customs simulation without verified mechanics.

A market charter can prioritize resources, equipment contracts, transport access, and mutual production projects through existing supported systems. Countries without the relevant DLC use a fully specified supported base-game agreement where possible. A placeholder modifier that merely says an agreement exists is not an acceptable substitute for a core promised function. Any unsupported exchange remains a release blocker until a proper design is chosen.

The main fork concerns dependence. A center-heavy market directs more production toward Persia and provides stronger short-term military support. A reciprocal market invests more in clients and gives them stronger reasons to remain. Both can reach the route conclusion. They have different responses to a Persian downturn.

## Branch F: regional security leadership

The empire creates a Persian-led bloc only when it can legally form or lead one. Existing faction membership is handled through a visible political and diplomatic settlement. A country cannot lead two factions at once.

The initial bloc needs Persia and at least one willing partner. The full institutional conclusion requires several functioning partners and actual defense commitments. Members can refuse expansion into unrelated distant wars when their treaty permits it. The empire must decide whether to respect the agreement or demand a more subordinate relationship.

At Evolution III, the bloc can offer imperial guarantees, mediate client disputes, and intervene in wars affecting its subjects. Non-monarchical presentation uses a central imperial institution. The branch grants the same class of strategic power as the King of Kings concept without creating a fictional personal ruler.

## Route conclusion: an industrial imperial bloc

The conclusion requires the recovered homeland, domestic production capable of replacing the main opening equipment families, a functioning Gulf or other viable maritime outlet, a completed major transport program, and at least three durable client or subject agreements. At least one agreement must include defense and one must include completed investment. Legitimacy must be at least 60.

The reward is an improved modern settlement and a coordinated development program. Members can undertake complementary military and transport projects. The final effect should make the bloc useful in an ongoing campaign, not simply give Persia another flat political power bonus.

A landlocked empire can work toward access through a treaty or campaign. It cannot complete the maritime requirement through a port it neither controls nor has durable use of. The naval sub-branch remains useful once access is gained.

## Distinctive failure

A failed industrial settlement can produce contract disputes, military shortages, and client exits. These failures are tied to missed obligations, lost transport, and defeat. They do not require another public economic crisis bar.

The recovery route can reduce commitments, repay a contract with real goods, diversify production, or negotiate a smaller bloc. A player can preserve a successful modern state after giving up an outer client. The system should not require total collapse whenever a contract fails.

The AI favors this route for non-monarchical governments, stronger domestic industry, useful oil access, and partners that would accept investment. It does not sign deliveries it cannot afford while its own army has severe shortages.
