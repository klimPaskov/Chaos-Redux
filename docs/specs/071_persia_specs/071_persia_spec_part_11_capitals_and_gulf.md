# 071 Persia: imperial centers and the Persian Gulf

## A network of centers

The empire distinguishes its administrative capital, military bases, and symbolic sites. These do not have to be the same place. Tehran can remain the administrative capital while Persepolis hosts the restoration's principal ceremonial institution. Ctesiphon can become an important regional center after a Mesopotamian settlement.

The actual administrative capital uses a valid controlled province and the game's ordinary rules. A symbolic restoration does not create a new city, relocate every factory, or move the capital automatically. Each site has a clear role and a bounded project.

## Tehran: continuity and command

Tehran's project consolidates the provisional government, staff, records, and central administration. If Tehran is held, the player can improve its administrative resilience and connect it to the main military and industrial routes. If it is lost, the equivalent opening function operates from a valid surviving administrative center.

The route should preserve a reason to recover Tehran without making every focus unusable until it returns. A recovered capital can restore legitimacy previously lost from that loss. It cannot award the same opening recognition again.

During a crisis, evacuation preserves institutions at a real cost. The player chooses a valid replacement center and receives a recovery mission. State assets remain where they are unless ordinary supported evacuation mechanics explicitly move a bounded part of them.

## Isfahan: administration and production

Isfahan supports a second major administrative and production center. The player can prioritize training and technical education or a regional arsenal and repair base. This reduces dependence on a single capital state and gives the interior a useful role.

The reward is a funded local program with actual construction or a defined administrative result. It does not turn a historical name into unlimited industrial slots. Existing development counts toward the project where appropriate, with no repeated grant.

Isfahan can serve as a candidate emergency center if the actual map and control permit. Its eligibility is practical, not guaranteed by the focus text.

## Persepolis: a substantial restoration chain

UNESCO identifies Persepolis as a major Achaemenid site founded under Darius I, with monumental and ceremonial importance. [H02](071_persia_research_notes.md#h02-persepolis) The event's restoration program is alternate history. The project should acknowledge an archaeological site and its surroundings instead of treating the ruins as an intact administrative metropolis.

**Stage one, protection and survey.** Persia must control the verified site and its immediate access. The project secures the area, establishes a conservation and survey team, and creates a limited local support program. It takes 120 days. The player assigns support equipment and funds the work. Active fighting at the site pauses the project.

**Stage two, access and conservation.** The empire restores practical access, stabilizes important remains, and creates facilities in the surrounding area. This takes 180 days and uses real construction capacity. The work does not replace the ancient remains with a factory complex. Failure leaves the completed part recorded and permits a revised project.

**Stage three, the imperial institution.** The route chooses the site's contemporary political function. The Achaemenid route hosts an imperial assembly. The Sasanian route holds a central court and command settlement. The modern route establishes a national cultural and diplomatic institution. Each provides a distinct political use without claiming to rebuild an ancient constitution exactly.

**Stage four, public recognition.** The final ceremony requires the previous work, legitimacy of at least 40, and a stable local situation for 90 days. Completion grants the one-time Persepolis legitimacy milestone, a major news event, and the relevant route connection. The project must not be reduced to a single political power button.

The player may choose a more restrained conservation policy or a larger ceremonial development around the site. The larger option costs more construction effort and attracts greater political attention. It does not yield a large global industrial bonus. Final visual work must distinguish ancient remains from newly built ceremonial facilities.

## Ctesiphon: a western seat

Ctesiphon was a Sasanian court center near modern Baghdad. [H03](071_persia_research_notes.md#h03-sasanian-context-and-ctesiphon) The game project uses a verified site location and a settled Mesopotamian region. It must not assume that an ordinary occupation of Baghdad has automatically restored Ctesiphon.

The project can establish a regional court, a military administration, or a charter assembly according to the chosen route. A local satrapy can host it by agreement. Persia does not need to annex the entire region just to preserve or use the site politically.

The main cost is a local administrative and conservation project. Its military value comes from the wider Mesopotamian supply and command system. The ancient site itself does not grant a magical global combat bonus.

## Optional seats

Additional sites may be added only when they create a distinct political or strategic choice and have a researched location. Susa is a possible later reference, but this package does not require an extra chain simply to collect more famous names.

The core campaign already has a contemporary center, a secondary interior center, a major restoration site, and a western seat. Adding more must not dilute those projects or multiply national spirits.

## The Gulf sequence

The Gulf branch has five major stages: a usable outlet, port and support development, protection of coastal traffic, a regional naval force, and durable political access around the Gulf. These are structural stages, not five mandatory identical focuses.

A usable outlet may be an owned and controlled port or a durable access agreement that genuinely permits the intended activity. Building a Persian naval base on foreign territory requires a supported agreement and actual recipient consent. A temporary wartime docking right may support operations but does not meet the final permanent-access milestone.

Port development adds dockyard and maintenance capacity through real projects. The coastal stage develops escorts, patrols, and operating bases. The regional fleet stage supports a fleet that can operate and be replaced. The final political stage settles remaining access and security commitments.

## Gulf control and pressure

A port owner can be offered investment, a guarantee, a lease, or a subject settlement. Each offer depends on real control, diplomatic authority, and a valid implementation route. Refusal can lead to pressure or an ordinary campaign, but does not automatically transfer the port.

Strategic shipping dominance needs an accessible route, naval presence, air support where relevant, and an ability to sustain the force. The exact sea-region and strait conditions must be verified. An event should not promise that a single focus can close an international strait if the installed engine and map do not support it.

A Gulf security settlement can raise legitimacy once when it meets the declared conditions. Losing a small naval battle does not immediately remove the milestone. Losing the essential outlet or durable access for 30 days does.

## Route differences

The Achaemenid route uses Gulf access to connect outer charters and support a maritime imperial network. The Sasanian route uses it to protect the core, supply western and eastern commands, and limit hostile naval pressure. The modern route uses it to sustain exports, imports, client investment, and a regional fleet.

All three need ports and ships. Their political agreements and development priorities differ. A route cannot claim naval success from a different route's completed focus that it never took.

## Acceptance

Persepolis receives a multi-stage restoration with route-specific use. Tehran is not forcibly abandoned. Isfahan has a practical role. Ctesiphon is tied to a verified location and settlement. The Gulf is a sequence of construction, access, and military capability, with no free ocean fleet for a landlocked remnant.
