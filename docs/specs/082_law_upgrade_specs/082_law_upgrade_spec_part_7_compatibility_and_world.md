# Law Upgrade
## Part 7. Compatible systems and world consequences

## One event, many owners

Event 82 advances participating systems through their real wartime progressions. The system that owns a law family also owns its order, prerequisites, and consequences.

The event does not maintain a parallel copy of every country's laws. It does not sort policies alphabetically, infer extremity from a name, or assume that the last item in a menu is the most militarized.

Each participating family must identify its current position, its immediate wartime successor, and whether that successor is structurally possible. A country-specific branch may differ from the ordinary vanilla sequence while retaining the same one-step principle.

## Participation by profile

| Profile | Participating families |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline | Economy and conscription |
| Emergency State | Baseline, trade, and registered military, economic, production, manpower, and wartime-policy families |
| Total War State | Every registered wartime law or policy family |
| Totalen Krieg!!! | All earlier families, with the two new endpoints enabled |

A category can be registered for a later profile when its policy is more sweeping than an ordinary emergency mobilization measure. Its owner must explain that placement.

A category that is already eligible in Evolution I does not advance again because it also qualifies under Evolution II. The most advanced applied profile contains a set of participating categories, not a sequence of repeated passes.

## Real direction rather than arbitrary conversion

A compatible policy must have a meaningful direction toward wartime mobilization. A three-step civilian production priority can qualify. A set of three different naval specializations is not automatically ordered.

A branching system can expose a single wartime branch when its owner has established that branch for the current country. Event 82 does not choose the player's ideology, recruit an advisor, complete a focus, select a doctrine, buy a technology, or build an institution merely to create a branch.

Balances of power, political paranoia, industry organizations, occupation systems, and national resource markets are not all “laws” by default. Only a defined wartime-policy path inside such a system participates.

## Purchase restrictions and structural requirements

The forced advance bypasses ordinary Political Power payment, public-support purchase requirements, peacetime purchase restrictions, and the usual political inconvenience of choosing a harsher law.

It preserves requirements that define whether the new institution or policy can actually exist. Missing DLC, absent institutions, unavailable technologies, incompatible population systems, and non-existent national branches are structural conditions.

A real committed resource requirement can also be structural when the policy's owner defines it that way. The event must not conjure the resource or replace the requirement with a guessed modifier.

The owner must classify these requirements explicitly. A blanket instruction to bypass every condition is insufficient, as is a blanket refusal to advance any law whose ordinary purchase button is greyed out.

## Unknown and incompatible categories

An unknown current law remains unchanged. Other compatible categories still advance, and the country still receives the profile's War Support.

No unrecognized law is replaced with a default beginning law. No country receives Political Power because its category could not advance. A missing adapter is a compatibility limitation, not a successful “maximum reached” result.

The release coverage must enumerate actual supported families and explicitly identify uncovered installed families. “Supports all DLC dynamically” is not an acceptable release claim without that evidence.

## Compatibility with country content

A focus or decision that checks whether a country has reached Total Mobilization or Scraping the Barrel may need to recognize the extreme successor. This must preserve the original condition's meaning.

An exact-law condition used for a deliberate political choice is different from a minimum-mobilization condition. The event cannot treat those two purposes as interchangeable.

Existing country transformations, civil-war inheritance, and forced law resets must retain their owner semantics. When an extreme law is inherited or removed, its current penalties reconcile without replaying the global event or its War Support grant.

## Worldwide trade contraction

The move toward Closed Economy can restrict the resources that other countries were importing. This is an intended consequence of pushing the whole world in the same direction.

Event 82 does not add a second scripted international embargo, cancel every import manually, or award compensation to importers. The trade system determines the actual availability and shortages.

A country with excellent War Support can still fail to supply its factories. Public enthusiasm does not replace oil, steel, or transport.

## Economic and civilian crises

Extreme construction, output, repair, growth, and research penalties should naturally influence other systems that already consume those capacities.

The event does not independently start a famine or mass migration merely because an extreme law is active. A participating crisis owner may observe the resulting conditions through its own established mechanics.

A separate economic collapse remains a separate event. Its effects must not be duplicated as an invisible extra “Law Upgrade collapse” penalty.

The absence of automatic population deaths is deliberate. Any later mortality must have a real cause and use the owning project's death accounting.

## Other Chaos Redux connections

Military-preparation events can make the world's newly mobilized armies more dangerous by providing equipment, formations, or infrastructure through their own effects. Event 82 itself provides none of those assets.

A worldwide tax system can make civilian recovery harder by continuing to claim resources or industrial capacity. This is a natural interaction, not an instruction for Event 82 to recalculate another event's taxes.

A future demobilization event can expose the predecessor direction through the same owning law systems. It can return either extreme law to its normal maximum through an explicit forced change. It does not silently become a manual purchase.

The supplied catalog still calls Event 61 “Half mils into civs”. This package therefore does not claim that a fully implemented “Return to Peacetime” law-reversal owner already exists. Any such connection is future integration until its actual owner is inspected.

## Chaos accounting

Event 82's catalog Chaos level remains 1. Evolution eligibility, evolution activation, profile selection, War Support grants, and capped no-change results do not themselves add Chaos.

The proposed event-owned Chaos awards are small, concrete, globally one-shot milestones.

| First actual outcome in the campaign | Proposed Chaos change |
| --- | ---: |
| An Event 82 firing actually advances at least one law | +5 |
| An Event 82 advance first puts a country into Closed Economy | +5 |
| The Event 82 legal family first puts a country into Totalen Krieg!!! | +5 |
| The Event 82 legal family first puts a country into Totalen Menschen!!! | +5 |

The extreme-entry milestones can be reached by either a forced advance or a valid voluntary entry after the evolution unlock. They are awarded once for the world, not once per country.

The maximum positive award from these four milestones is 20 Chaos for the campaign. Repeated normal advancement, a new holder, re-entry, save reload, and a new evolution cannot farm the same milestone.

Existing generic consequences such as war, annexation, casualties, and contamination remain with their own systems. Event 82 does not add another copy of those awards.

## Durable recovery

For each extreme-law family, award one proposed -5 Chaos recovery when all of the following are true.

- That family's positive extreme-entry milestone was previously awarded.
- A genuine voluntary reversal or an explicit forced demobilization removed its last worldwide holder.
- No country has held that extreme law for 30 consecutive game days afterward.
- That family's recovery credit has never been awarded before.

Annexation or disappearance of the last holder does not count as successful demobilization. If any holder returns before the period finishes, the pending recovery period ends without a credit.

The maximum recovery credit is -10 Chaos for the campaign. A credited recovery never resets to become farmable again.

These are world-history consequences. They are not a public countdown system that the player must manage.

## Event selection and cluster treatment

Military Preparation treats Event 82 as its Low member, while the event remains a Minor Repeatable. The cluster consumes one event selection under the shared cluster rules, not one selection for each affected country.

The existing repeatable-event recovery, cap decay, timer, and member eligibility remain owned by the central event system. This package does not create a second repeatability formula or an independent timer.

A firing with every compatible category already capped can still grant War Support. When support is capped too, it is a genuine no-change outcome, not permission to invent an alternative prize.

## Disabling future firings

Disabling Event 82 or its cluster prevents future selections through the owning event controls. It does not freeze a held extreme law's current War Support penalties or remove its manual reversal actions. Disabling a future wave is not a free demobilization.

The firing's applied profile is fixed before its outcome milestones change Chaos. A milestone earned during a wave cannot raise that same wave into a stronger profile.
