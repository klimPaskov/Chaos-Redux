# New Ore, part 4: International competition and Ore Wars

## Ordinary competition around an extraordinary prize

The ore gives countries a reason to use existing diplomacy and military power differently. Suppliers can seek guarantees, improve relationships, support selected allies, or trade access for equipment. Customers can protect a source, find an alternative, or reduce their dependence. Rivals can improve piercing, plan a campaign around a deposit, or pursue a credible demand after negotiations fail.

The state becomes strategically valuable as soon as the ore appears. Evolution III raises the priority of that objective and introduces the explicit demand route. The event's AI should already defend and value the deposit before that evolution.

Interest is based on actual campaign conditions. Relevant armored forces, armored production, the gap between direct and foreign access, current suppliers, territorial reach, faction relationships, war commitments, and the strength of potential defenders all matter. National tags do not determine whether a country is entitled to care.

## Actor strategy matrix

| Actor situation | Preferred strategy | Reasons to change strategy | Behavior to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small controller with little armor | Protect the state, seek a useful security partner, exchange one place for material support, develop armor through normal systems | Reliable protection exists, equipment needs are met, or a threatening customer becomes a rival | Giving full access to its overlord, selling every place to enemies, or abandoning the state because its old industry score was low |
| Armored major with a defensible deposit | Preserve direct access, defend its source, selectively strengthen useful allies | A second deposit provides resilience or an attractive commercial buyer poses little threat | Treating export income as automatically more important than military monopoly |
| Controller with an exposed deposit | Offer shorter agreements, seek a guarantee or helpful access relationship, reinforce and prepare a retreat plan for other fronts | Source becomes secure or an alternative deposit is captured | Assuming the mineral makes defenders invulnerable or repeatedly resetting a failed defence mission |
| Armored customer with a reliable supplier | Keep useful access, support the supplier through ordinary diplomacy and war, consider one backup | Rival prices fall, source becomes exposed, or the customer gains direct access | Buying several licences for nonexistent stacking or cancelling a useful cheap treaty for a tiny price difference |
| Capable armored rival denied access | Improve offer, seek another source, prepare credible pressure, and consider a deposit objective in a normal war | Defenders become too strong, another war takes priority, or a new source becomes available | Issuing an impossible demand or declaring war without a viable front or invasion route |
| Distant major with naval reach | Negotiate first, evaluate coastal approaches and force commitments, prepare only when naval and supply conditions are credible | New nearer source or safer ally appears | Treating a landlocked inaccessible state as an immediately executable naval invasion |
| Faction leader without direct ore | Encourage allocation to the most useful armored members and protect the actual controller | Controller refuses, leaves, or faces a stronger threat | Counting members' deposits as its own direct holdings |
| Country with little present armor | Value future development, bargaining and denial at a lower military weight | It develops a substantial armored force or becomes a supplier | Paying its entire stockpile for an advantage it cannot use |
| Country controlling several deposits | Protect concentrated supply, use export assignments intelligently, evaluate the first extra Super-Alloy gain especially highly | Fronts overstretch or strategic concentration provokes rivals | Treating the tenth deposit as equal to the second or assuming its armor advantage has no limit |

Ideology changes diplomatic compatibility and the style of demands, but it does not override hard validity or create a fixed recipient list. Democratic, communist, fascist, and non-aligned governments can all negotiate, protect, or covet the ore in circumstances consistent with their normal diplomatic constraints.

## Hidden interest and restraint

AI interest uses a private assessment rather than a new player meter. Begin with the value of the armor improvement to forces that exist or are credibly being produced. Add the value of denying it to an opponent and the value of retaining an endangered current source. Reduce interest when the country already has secure direct access, an adequate reliable agreement, inadequate industry, or overriding military emergencies.

Evaluate military feasibility separately. It includes force balance, active fronts, military access, supply, relevant naval reach, guarantees, faction obligations, truces, non-aggression commitments, and the danger of involving stronger enemies. Strong desire does not prove a viable attack.

At Ore Wars, the interest in access and acquisition increases. Strategic restraint remains active. A high-Chaos country may accept more risk, but a country with no executable route still cannot behave as if it can occupy the state tomorrow.

The AI can pursue different choices against the same supplier. A current enemy may plan seizure. A partner may seek a faction allocation. A neutral industrial buyer may make a premium commercial offer. These choices use the actual current relation and target state.

## The access demand route

A demand asks the controller of one named deposit to grant a normal foreign allocation under specified terms. It never demands the controller's full bonus.

The demanding country must have a useful military interest, no current adequate access, a credible way to pressure the supplier, and a rejected or expired good-faith offer within the previous 90 days. A stronger newly valid offer can replace the negotiation prerequisite, but opening and immediately cancelling a sham proposal does not qualify.

A country may maintain one active ore demand. The named supplier cannot be its faction mate, its subject, or its overlord. The pair must be able to enter the intended ordinary diplomatic and military relationship. Existing truces and applicable non-aggression constraints continue to govern war. A demand does not bypass native restrictions by silently changing ideology or granting an unrestricted declaration.

The action's cost is 75 Political Power and 25 Command Power as anchors. Stronger opposing guarantees, a poor power balance, and distant operations raise the cost. An existing relevant World Claims claim or a previous supplier breach can reduce it. Bound the cost to 50–150 Political Power and 15–50 Command Power. Military reach and credible forces are mandatory nonconsumed requirements beyond those attention costs.

The proposed access terms are stated at issue. A demand can request a commercially compensated allocation or a security allocation for which the recipient can actually fulfil the commitment. The demanding country must remain able to pay the final access price if the controller accepts. It does not take equipment from the controller for accepting.

### The controller's responses

| Response | Immediate consequence | Strategic consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accept the specified limited allocation | Run the normal fresh agreement confirmation and payment | Foreign access begins, direct control remains stronger, the ore-demand episode closes |
| Offer one alternative eligible allocation | The demander receives a final choice within the existing response window | Creates a possible negotiated settlement without endless deadline renewal |
| Refuse | End negotiations and allow a credible demander's next preparation choice | Supplier keeps its monopoly and takes the risk of a normal war |
| Leave unanswered | Treat as refusal when the response deadline arrives | Same preparation opportunity, with no duplicated refusal effects |

The ordinary response window is 21 days, ranging from 14 days under immediate military pressure to 30 days for distant diplomacy. This short window is a diplomatic reply period, not a construction or territorial achievement mission. Both parties see the deadline and the consequence of refusal.

If the supplier accepts but the requester can no longer pay the fresh quote, has filled its agreement limit, or cannot satisfy the promised security relationship, the episode closes as an uncompleted acceptance. The same applies if source capacity becomes unavailable before final confirmation. No access begins and no new refusal or war-preparation credit is awarded. Delivered diplomatic attention remains spent. A technical inability to complete the request must not be converted into evidence that the supplier refused it.

The demander's costs are expended diplomatic and military attention. They are not transferred to the supplier. If the state or country becomes invalid before any demand is delivered, return actual paid attention costs once. A delivered demand that is rejected, becomes strategically obsolete, or receives no answer does not refund spent attention.

## From refusal to military preparation

After refusal, the demanding country can abandon the dispute or prepare an ore objective against that supplier. Preparation uses the normal war-goal and diplomatic framework, with the specific ore state as its strategic purpose. Where the installed native war-goal form supports a limited state demand, use the named state. A broad annexation goal must not be presented to the player as a guaranteed limited war.

The intended preparation period is 90 days, adjusted within 60–180 days by relevant native rules, political constraints, existing claims, military distance, and the strength of the dispute. The resulting authorization expires after 180 days unless the native framework requires a different validated duration. There is one ore-generated authorization for the pair at a time. A refusal does not create permanent cores or a repeating stack of claims.

The human decides whether to declare war. AI decides through its normal political and military restrictions and the additional ore priorities. The event does not directly execute a declaration after a timer expires. Notifications must describe the actual preparation and possible war rather than guarantee that war will occur.

An accepted agreement before declaration closes the event-owned preparation and removes its temporary ore pressure. Removal must preserve independently existing war goals, claims, native orders, and strategic interests from other sources. If exact event-owned removal is not safely supported, the implementation must document and resolve that engine constraint before treating the demand lifecycle as complete.

The ore demand cannot be repeated against the same supplier for 360 days after a resolved dispute. Repeated refusal does not escalate a free war-goal ladder. A genuinely new controller or newly discovered deposit is a new strategic situation, while state-control cycling by the same pair does not reset the old dispute memory.

## Conducting the war

Ore states receive concrete state-level offensive target priority. A country already at war with a supplier may prioritize those states without completing a diplomatic demand. That priority is strongest when capture would remove the enemy's last source, terminate important enemy allocations, or provide the attacker's first direct access.

Defending AI reinforces its sources and the routes that make them defensible. The correct allocation still depends on supply and other fronts. It should not place an entire army on an island while losing its capital and industry.

Country-level hostility, war preparation, front requests, offensive state weighting, and invasion requests have distinct purposes. The implementation must use verified native hooks for each purpose. A generic conquest preference alone is not evidence that the AI will direct forces toward the ore state.

The event does not grant automatic peace after a deposit changes hands. Existing wars can have other participants and goals. The new controller immediately gains ore access while the war continues. The previous controller has a powerful reason to counterattack, seek another supplier, or negotiate the wider conflict.

In a peace settlement, ore increases the strategic value of the actual state being contested. The design does not promise a special peace-conference currency or override unrelated territorial settlements. Puppet creation only grants direct ore to whichever country ends up controlling the state.

## A bounded source-defense objective

A current controller may undertake one source-defense objective for an ore state already threatened by an actual enemy front or credible incoming operation. The mission asks the country to retain continuous control for a declared period. Its anchor is 180 days, with a range of 120–270 days based on whether the threat has reached an active local front or remains a credible preparation, as calibrated in part 9.

Starting it requires real defensive forces with viable supply in or adjacent to the source region and commits 20 Command Power, dynamically bounded to 10–40. The nonconsumed military requirement prevents an empty click from becoming proof of defense. It does not grant free troops or a second armor bonus.

Success automatically records successful defense and awards 25 Army XP, or Navy XP for a validated primarily naval defense, with a 15–40 range reflecting the relevant friendly defended force under part 9's formula. An actual enemy threat remains a prerequisite throughout. It can improve the supplier's reliability in subsequent AI negotiations. The source remains valuable even when the player does not undertake this objective.

Failure occurs on losing control before the due date. The ore changes hands normally, the commitment remains spent, and the new controller receives the direct benefit. Failure does not apply an additional penalty to all armor. Cancellation because the original threat ends is a neutral closure with no success reward. The same state and defending country cannot farm the objective through another attempt for 720 days.

Only actual hostile exposure starts this family. A friendly agreement to hand the state back and forth, a border without war or credible hostile preparation, or a dormant mission with no threat cannot satisfy it. The success period is tied to actual elapsed dates and uninterrupted control.

## Scenario expectations for AI review

The full probability matrix is in part 9. Its essential rankings are:

- An affordable useful allocation outranks an unaffordable one.
- A threatened minor favors meaningful protection over a hostile buyer's larger payment when that buyer is likely to seize the deposit.
- An armored rival with military reach and denied access has higher demand and preparation willingness after Ore Wars than before it.
- An unreachable or diplomatically invalid target has zero actionable demand weight.
- A current supplier's survival matters more to a dependent customer than a remote unrelated deposit of equal nominal grade.
- Capturing a first source matters more than a tenth source, and at the Super-Alloy the second direct source matters more than the third.
- A paid active agreement and a second backup are evaluated as existing commitments, not as two additive armor upgrades.

The audits compare declared circumstances and ranking behavior. They do not invent an exact percentage chance of war from incomplete native AI inputs.
