# Event 067 Research Bibliography

## Core sources

### James T. Quinlivan

**Work**

Coup-proofing: Its Practice and Consequences in the Middle East.

**Publication**

International Security, Fall 1999. Belfer Center publication page.

**Source**

https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/coup-proofing-its-practice-and-consequences-middle-east

**Use in specification**

Supports the tradeoff between regime protection and military effectiveness. Informs loyal reserve commands, parallel command structures, internal-security control, officer loyalty, and the real cost of countermeasures.

**Confidence**

High. The publication page provides the abstract and citation.

### Naunihal Singh

**Work**

Seizing Power: The Strategic Logic of Military Coups.

**Publication**

Johns Hopkins University Press, 2014.

**Related review used**

Barbara Geddes, Political Science Quarterly, 2015.

**Source**

https://academic.oup.com/psq/article/130/3/580/47376826

**Use in specification**

Supports the coordination-game model of coup alignment. Informs Influence, officer uncertainty, failed-removal shock, and the importance of visible command control.

**Confidence**

High for the reviewed claims. The source is an academic review of the book.

### Beth Rabinowitz

**Work**

Reviewing Seizing Power.

**Publication**

The Strategy Bridge, May 29, 2019.

**Source**

https://thestrategybridge.org/the-bridge/2019/5/29/reviewing-seizing-power

**Use in specification**

Supports communication and coordination among security forces as a major factor in tactical coup success. Informs command centers, communications, headquarters, and the distinction between motivation and expected success.

**Confidence**

Medium to high. It is a detailed professional review and is not the primary book.

### Nam Kyu Kim and Alex M. Kroeger

**Work**

Regime and Leader Instability Under Two Forms of Military Rule.

**Publication**

Comparative Political Studies, first published online February 1, 2017.

**Source**

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0010414016688009

**Use in specification**

Supports the distinction between personalist and collegial military rule. Informs Personal Command, Officer Directorate, officer unity, leadership reshuffles, and different failure states.

**Confidence**

High for the abstracted distinction and findings.

### Hicham Bou Nassif

**Work**

Generals and Autocrats: How Coup-Proofing Predetermined the Military Elite's Behavior in the Arab Spring.

**Publication**

Political Science Quarterly, Summer 2015.

**Source**

https://academic.oup.com/psq/article/130/2/245/6849100

**Use in specification**

Supports the importance of intra-officer relationships, institutional position, and different patterns of military unity or splintering. Informs regional commands, loyal officers, service branches, and rival claimant overlays.

**Confidence**

High for the extract and publication details.

### Online Etymology Dictionary

**Entry**

Generalissimo.

**Source**

https://www.etymonline.com/word/generalissimo

**Use in specification**

Supports the broad historical meaning of the title as supreme military commander and its use before the twentieth century.

**Confidence**

High for the etymological entry.

## Source-use limits

- No source is used to claim one universal law of coups.
- No source provides a final game probability.
- No real leader or country is treated as the Generalissimo's direct model.
- Numerical tuning remains a game-design decision that requires probability evaluation.
- Final super-event quotes and audio are not selected in this bibliography.
