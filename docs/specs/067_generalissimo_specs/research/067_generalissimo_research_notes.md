# Event 067 Research Notes

## Research purpose

The research supports the event's political and military structure. It does not turn the Generalissimo into a depiction of any one real ruler or coup.

The strongest useful findings concern:

- how coup participants decide which side is likely to win
- why control of communications and command centers matters
- how regimes try to prevent coups
- the military cost of coup-proofing
- differences between personal military rule and collegial officer rule
- the historical meaning of the title generalissimo

## The title

The term generalissimo historically means a supreme military commander. Its use predates the twentieth century and does not belong to one national tradition.

Design use:

- The title can function as the character's public identity without assigning him a false historical nationality.
- The event should avoid copying a specific real generalissimo, uniform, ideology, or state.
- The title supports the accepted premise that his authority stands above ordinary generals and field marshals.

## Coups as coordination problems

Research on military coups emphasizes that officers often care about which side other units will support. Personal grievance alone does not determine alignment. Officers may remain undecided until they believe one side has established a credible expectation of victory.

Design use:

- Generalissimo Influence represents expected success and military obedience, not personal popularity alone.
- A failed arrest or assassination gives the Generalissimo a coordination advantage because the conflict is public and compromise is over.
- Control of senior commands, communications, capital approaches, and regional headquarters matters more than a random national rebel percentage.
- High Influence can move many units with little initial fighting because officers believe resistance will fail.
- A divided officer corps can produce a civil war even when many officers dislike the incumbent government.

## Communications and visible control

Studies and reviews of coup behavior place strong importance on communication among security forces and on signals that a seizure of power is succeeding.

Design use:

- The revolt strength score includes internal-security reach, regional command support, and headquarters control.
- Capturing the capital and command centers is a strategic objective, not only a symbolic map change.
- The government can separate military intelligence and secure the chain of command before removal.
- The world-end branch uses peaceful coups where one side establishes clear command dominance and civil wars where the signal remains divided.

The design does not treat communications as sufficient by itself. Government legitimacy, loyal units, officer relationships, territory, and war conditions still matter.

## Coup-proofing and military cost

James Quinlivan's work describes coup-proofing methods that include special loyalties, parallel military forces, internal-security organizations, professional regular forces, and sustained funding. It also argues that such measures can reduce the state's military power.

Design use:

- Civilian oversight, loyal reserves, command rotation, separated intelligence, and dispersed arsenals reduce coup risk.
- These measures cost command power, army experience, equipment, civilian capacity, planning, or coordination.
- The player cannot lower Influence through free political-power purchases.
- A government can become safer while becoming less effective at war.
- A wartime player has a real reason to postpone countermeasures and keep the Generalissimo in command.

## Intra-officer relationships

Research on military behavior during political crises shows that senior officers and their subordinates do not always act as one group. Existing command structures, privilege, institutional position, and internal relationships can produce unity or splintering.

Design use:

- The event tracks officer loyalty and regional-command support separately from public Influence.
- Loyal appointments, protected officers, and command rotation have mechanical effects.
- Event 019 claimant commanders can divide the officer corps.
- Navy and air loyalty depend on service-branch conditions instead of following the land army automatically.
- Officer Directorate play centers on maintaining unity across branches and regions.

## Personal and collegial military rule

Comparative research distinguishes personalist military rule from collegial military rule. Collegial military regimes can face distinct leadership and regime instability because officer unity and internal reshuffling matter.

Design use:

- Personal Command gives the highest direct power and the greatest dependence on one ruler.
- Officer Directorate gives senior officers formal roles and stronger long-term cohesion.
- National Emergency Council adds a military-civilian structure without assuming every junta is identical.
- Officer Directorate failure should create reshuffles, command crises, and internal bargaining more often than an automatic second civil war.

These routes are inspired by broad regime types. They are not simulations of a specific country.

## Why the Generalissimo remains tempting

The research supports a central gameplay tradeoff. Governments can reduce coup danger by dividing military authority, building parallel commands, and supervising security institutions. Those choices can make the armed forces slower, less coordinated, and more expensive.

Event 067 therefore gives the player a strong reason to keep the Generalissimo:

- his personal commander package is unmatched
- expanded authority adds national military benefits
- winning campaigns with him can be decisive
- limiting him disrupts planning and consumes resources
- removing him permanently sacrifices a unique asset

The political crisis works only if the military benefit is real.

## Why the revolt strength is dynamic

A fixed civil-war percentage would ignore the event's decisions.

The research supports a model where revolt strength depends on:

- belief that the coup will succeed
- officer coordination
- visible control of command institutions
- loyal formations
- government counterweights
- headquarters and communications
- control of resources and bases

Generalissimo Influence remains the largest public predictor, while hidden components determine the exact army, territory, commander, navy, air, and stockpile split.

## Why the world-end branch is plural

The world-end title refers to a world of military rulers, not clones of the original character.

The research supports several possible military responses:

- personal military rule
- officer councils
- military-civilian emergency governments
- rival commands
- split armed forces
- officers remaining loyal to civilian government

The original Generalissimo is the strongest and most important actor. Other countries use existing commanders or councils and can align with him, oppose him, or create rival blocs.

## Historical restraint

The event should avoid:

- copying Francisco Franco, Joseph Stalin, Chiang Kai-shek, or another real figure
- assigning the Generalissimo a real nationality
- using a real dictatorship's flag as a fictional event flag
- treating military institutions as culturally identical in every country
- presenting a single regional case as a universal rule
- claiming that all coups are bloodless or all military regimes behave the same way

Historical and political research informs mechanics. The event remains fictional.

## Research-derived design requirements

1. Influence must represent coordination and expected success.
2. Failed coercive removal must give an immediate coordination shock and revolt.
3. Capital, headquarters, arsenals, and communications must matter.
4. Coup-proofing must reduce military effectiveness or consume real resources.
5. Officer branches and regional commands must be able to split.
6. Personal Command and Officer Directorate must play differently.
7. World-end countries must have several response outcomes.
8. The original Generalissimo must remain unique.
9. The event must avoid a real-person portrait or copied historical flag.
