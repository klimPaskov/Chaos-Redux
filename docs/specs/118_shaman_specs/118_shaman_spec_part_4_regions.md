# Shaman, Part 4: Regional identity and cultural direction

## The accepted roster

The six profiles below are accepted design targets.
Each still requires an exact installed-map binding and period-appropriate visual references before implementation can enable it.
Cultural research establishes a home and practitioner context.
It does not establish a game state's boundaries or a 1936 costume.

| Profile | Cultural home | Practitioner description | Ordinary campaign controller example | Main local emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Olkhon Island and adjoining western Baikal communities | Buryat shaman, with any native title locally verified | Soviet Union if directly controlling the mapped home | Food stores, husbandry, lakeshore routes and exposure |
| R2 | Podkamennaya Tunguska basin | Evenki saman | Soviet Union if directly controlling the mapped home | Taiga supplies, shelter, crossings and travel |
| R3 | Jeju, anchored in Geonip-dong and northeastern coastal communities | Simbang | Japan if directly controlling the mapped home | Fishing, civilian recovery, island supply and shelter |
| R4 | Lower Kuskokwim coast and adjoining Yukon-Kuskokwim delta | Yup'ik angalkuq | United States if directly controlling the mapped home | Fishing, hunting, preserved food and seasonal routes |
| R5 | Luang Prabang uplands, with Pouxang as the research anchor | Hmong txiv neeb | France if directly controlling the mapped home | Upland food production, carrying routes and protected positions |
| R6 | Tày communities in the Cao Bằng uplands | Then practitioner | France if directly controlling the mapped home | Crop recovery, reconstruction and upland approaches |

Controller examples are conditional campaign contexts.
They do not assert that every installed start date has those control relationships.
A conquering major can become eligible through actual control.
Neither ownership alone nor an overlord's relationship is sufficient.

The same hidden capability distribution, rite weights, costs and outcome strengths apply to all six.
Differences come from actual geography, usable transport, current need and political relationship.
There is no culture-specific fraud rate, supernatural power tier or inherent military superiority.

## R1: Western Baikal

Wierucka's original Olkhon fieldwork supports a geographically specific Buryat context and variation between local and organized practice.
Museum collection interpretation supplies separate material context.
The term böö appears in related Buryat fieldwork from Mongolia, with a dialect qualification.
It is not automatically the final title for an Olkhon visitor.
Sources B1 through B3 in the research register record those distinctions.

The fictional visit begins with a locally credible intermediary and a concern for households or movement close to the shore.
Food language follows the selected settlement and season.
It can concern stores, husbandry or recovery, rather than assuming a cereal harvest everywhere.
Warrior guidance emphasizes exposure, shelter, shore approaches and positions that local people know.

The arrival scene shows a practical meeting among residents and officials.
Use period-supported ordinary clothing and working surroundings.
A contemporary ceremonial organization, generalized Mongolian headdress or an assortment of Buddhist and shamanic objects cannot stand in for research.
Sacred landmarks and objects are not generic magical props.

## R2: Podkamennaya Tunguska

Ssorin-Chaikov's field and archival work supports this particular basin and the term saman.
The profile deliberately follows that geography.
Pitt Rivers Museum material from other Evenki locations provides comparison and collection context, with its own locations retained.
A museum headdress is one provenanced object, not a universal outfit.
Sources E1 through E3 record the evidence.

Food aid concerns suitable hunting, fishing, herding or stored supplies.
Military value comes from a finite network of routes, crossings and shelters in the selected states.
A unit leaving those states loses the local benefit.
A remote target uses the portable-supply quote unless its verified motor-transport condition actually holds.

The scene should show a lived-in working settlement or travel stop.
Avoid presenting a whole people as frozen in an ancient past.
Historical repression can inform the seriousness of a government's treatment, but ideology alone never forces persecution or changes hidden honesty.

## R3: Jeju

The local account published by ICHCAP uses simbang and connects the documented community context with fishing, farming, welfare and safety at sea.
It also records later changes, so the present shrine and festival cannot be copied into 1936 without additional evidence.
Source J1 is the principal anchor.

The fictional government-selected rite is not a reenactment of the named seasonal ceremony in that source.
Use locally appropriate food availability and recovery, with fishing and household work as viable calm-state directions.
Acceptance by a controlling government does not imply community consent to colonial rule.

Warrior guidance is offered only if the general local operational gate actually works for the mapped island area.
A distant naval war does not qualify.
If no supported local-frontier or battle condition exists, the military option remains unavailable while land aid remains fully meaningful.
Do not create a national naval bonus as a replacement.

Art should show people preparing supplies or shelter in a period-supported island setting.
Ritual names, deity names, invocations and current ceremonial clothing do not become decorative shorthand for the fictional bargain.

## R4: Lower Kuskokwim and Yukon delta

Community-authored interpretation uses angalkut, and bilingual oral-history material supports angalkuq and early twentieth century recollections.
ELOKA's work with elders documents geographic knowledge and routes.
These sources do not make every person or account interchangeable.
See Y1 through Y3.

Calm-state aid concerns fishing, hunting, preservation, access to stores or travel recovery.
It does not create farms or impose a grain-harvest story.
A military package can provide temporary supply caches, shelter and safer local movement where its operational gates are met.

The home belongs to the specified southwest Alaskan communities.
An Iñupiaq, Alutiiq, Unangan or Tlingit object is not a substitute visual.
Community accounts of supernatural acts inform the fictional premise as beliefs and stories, not as scientific proof.
No named oral-history participant becomes the visitor.

The scene uses ordinary collective work and period-supported equipment.
Ceremonial masks, songs and spirit figures are not approved assets merely because a source mentions them.
Portable support is the default route until a suitable local road condition is verified.

## R5: Luang Prabang uplands

PhotoForward and TAEC's community research provides the Pouxang and Luang Prabang anchor.
Original Hmong practitioner interviews support the term txiv neeb while retaining the diaspora setting of that terminology evidence.
TAEC and local institutional material emphasize distinctions between communities and material traditions.
Sources H1 through H4 record the exact limits.

The visit concerns particular upland households and routes.
Calm aid can support food stores or crop recovery.
Military guidance emphasizes carrying limits, shelter and local approaches.
The visitor does not speak for every upland people or turn cooperation into automatic imperial recruitment.

The scene uses a researched hillside settlement and a practical collective task.
Do not combine garments from different Hmong traditions into a generalized festival costume.
Modern interview subjects supply research evidence, not faces to copy into a fictional period scene.

## R6: Cao Bằng Tày communities

The official heritage record supports the northern geographic context, while Holm's fieldwork abstract identifies Tày Then practitioners and variation.
The heritage summary emphasizes oral transmission and the research abstract identifies textual repertoires.
The design makes neither statement universal.
Only the abstract and metadata of Holm's article were accessible in this research pass.
Sources T1 through T3 make the evidence boundary explicit.

The fictional request concerns crop recovery, damaged approaches or a threatened local position.
Cultural musical practice remains context.
It is not a combat ability.
Keep Tày distinct from Nùng, Thái and Zhuang identities, and do not confuse Vietnam's Thái designation with Thailand.

A scene can show community recovery with the practitioner among residents.
An instrument or specific ceremonial garment requires its own local and period reference.
The current research does not license reproducing songs, texts, seals or ritual sequences.

## Mapping acceptance

Each regional manifest row must record the cultural anchor, verified state ID and name, current state-file source and hash, relevant province components, optional adjacent working states, supported land needs, transport mode and regional art key.
Store the source of each geographic decision.
A modern community coordinate does not by itself prove which current game state contains it.

The inspected repository has no `history/states` tree.
A targeted 013 geography handoff mentions Jeju as part of state 1031, but that secondary project record is not sufficient to certify the installed current state or the full effect footprint.
It is a follow-up locator.
Do not ship guessed numeric state mappings from memory.

A cultural home may be smaller than an engine state.
The player-facing account must identify the community without calling the whole state culturally homogeneous.
State-level relief can benefit other residents.
Its cultural identity stays narrow.
If the state is so large that military guidance would become a broad regional advantage unrelated to the community, restrict that profile to land aid until a verified narrower scope exists.

The two-state or three-state military extension is allowed only where each state's inclusion has an explicit geographic rationale.
It is not an automatic ring around a sacred site or a continent-wide region.
All chosen states must be directly controlled by the host at commitment and delivery.

A failed map binding disables that profile with an explicit development finding.
It does not select an invented culture.
Full acceptance aims to validate the six accepted rows.
Any necessary replacement must be documented as a design change.
The researched Alutiiq and Tlingit alternatives in the source register are possible replacements, not additional automatic launch content.

## Text and visual boundaries

The practitioner is an anonymous fictional participant in a government encounter.
Their specific supernatural bargain is fictional.
The source record supplies cultural context and prevents interchangeable ethnic imagery.
It does not claim that any documented historical person made this offer.

Prose uses a credible local title where sufficiently supported, otherwise a clear English description.
It avoids invented accents, mystical broken English, generic tribal language, sacred quotations and ethnic punchlines.
Government uncertainty can be conveyed through conflicting reports and official reactions without making a community the joke.

No final sacred ceremony, song or iconography is prescribed.
Ordinary practical scenes, material commitments and observed consequences carry the event.
A separate grounded portrait would trigger the source-first portrait pipeline and a new explicit consumer requirement.
The accepted presentation uses group scenes rather than a character card.

Period clothing, settlement forms, boats, carts, tools and administrative uniforms remain specific asset-source tasks.
Modern ethnography is not a blanket period reference.
Before production, the asset worker must connect each chosen visual detail to evidence or keep it out of the composition.
