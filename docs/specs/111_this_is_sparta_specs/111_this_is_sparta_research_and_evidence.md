# Research and evidence for Event 111

## How to read the evidence

The gameplay mechanics in this package are authored design. Historical and mythological sources explain the choice of institutions, places and figures. They do not prove the proposed combat statistics, modern political program or supernatural reality. Repository observations establish what the inspected source snapshot contains, while installed-map and native-engine facts remain separately gated.

The user brief is the design authority for current Event 111, its original identity 116, immediate independent Laconian founding, five campaigns and Age of Heroes. All supplied project sources were read before substantive research and specification drafting. The full method and receipts are in [Source-reading report](111_this_is_sparta_source_reading_report.md).

## Historical and literary grounding

### Spartan institutions and civic reform

[H1: Xenophon, The Polity of the Lacedaemonians, translated by H. G. Dakyns](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1178/1178-h/1178-h.htm) describes public messes, military education, ephor authority, royal duties and the exchange of obligations between king and state. This informs the joint royal/ephor government, the military institution and the choice about who participates in it. It is an ancient account with its own perspective, not a neutral manual for a modern constitution. The modern adult Agoge, recruitment rules and numerical benefits are authored adaptations.

[H2: Plutarch, Life of Agis, especially chapters 5–11](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Plutarch/Lives/Agis*.html) describes conflict around property, debt, the citizen body and proposed admission of qualified outsiders. This gives the civic branch a specific Spartan connection instead of treating reform as unrelated to the setting. The package does not claim that Agis's program was a modern democratic constitution or reproduce its numbers as game balance. Modern equal citizenship and the new state's social choices remain alternate-history design.

[H3: Pausanias, Description of Greece, Book 3.1–13](https://www.theoi.com/Text/Pausanias3A.html) supplies a Laconian account of royal traditions, places and competing explanations. It supports using a layered Spartan identity rather than one frozen popular image. Exact modern map boundaries still require geographical work and installed province inspection. Pausanias does not supply a valid modern state ID or a historical photographic portrait.

### The Dioscuri and earthborn warriors

[M1: Pseudo-Apollodorus, Library 3.4.1–2 and 3.11.2](https://www.theoi.com/Text/Apollodorus3.html) provides the selected mythic basis. Its Cadmus narrative places the armed earthborn Sparti at Thebes and describes their destructive conflict. Its Dioscuri passage associates Castor with war, Polydeuces with boxing and the pair with shared divine and mortal existence. The package uses this distinction to separate Theban Spartoi from Spartan humans, give Polydeuces a genuine tournament connection, and design a paired-service limitation. The exact service rotation, modern offices and deployment caps are new mechanics.

### Talos

[M2: Pseudo-Apollodorus, Library 1.9.26](https://www.theoi.com/Text/Apollodorus1.html) describes Talos guarding Crete, his bronze nature and alternative accounts of the vulnerability that leads to his death. This supports a unique Cretan guardian with a binding and recovery problem. It does not support producing an unlimited line of identical guardians. Modern maintenance crews, convoy movement, anti-armor balance and custodial treaties are authored systems. The source's alternative versions should remain acknowledged in research notes rather than flattened into a falsely certain historical account.

### Stymphalian birds

[M3: Pseudo-Apollodorus, Library 2.5.6](https://www.theoi.com/Text/Apollodorus2.html) places the Stymphalian birds in Arcadia and describes the use of a bronze noise-making instrument to drive them into the air. It supports an Arcadian encounter with a flock and a containment problem. Their use as a limited modern air-wing consumer is deliberate game fiction. The package does not claim that a particular wing size, anti-aircraft vulnerability or airfield procedure comes from the ancient text.

### Western and maritime campaigns

[H4: Plutarch, Life of Pyrrhus, especially chapters 13–17 and 26–29](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Plutarch/Lives/Pyrrhus*.html) describes the Tarentine invitation and Italian expedition, as well as Pyrrhus's later attack on Sparta. The Western Campaign therefore treats him as an Epirote model selectively appropriated by the new Spartan state. He is not presented as a Spartan king or an uncomplicated ancestral ally. The later conquest of all Italy is the user's alternate-history ambition.

[H5: Museum of Cycladic Art, Greek colonisation](https://cycladic.gr/en/essay/ellinikos-apoikismos/) describes Greek movements and colonies in southern Italy, Sicily, farther west and around the Black Sea. It supports the broad maritime scope of Hellenic Colonies Reborn. The nine anchor requirements are a modern strategic network inspired by this wider history. They are not a claim that every site was founded by Sparta or that one ancient state controlled the full network.

The Alexander route's appropriation of a Macedonian legacy is explicit in the accepted user brief. Its required modern geographic sets and optional Afghan and Pakistani extension are authored campaign boundaries. No map in this package claims to reproduce Alexander's exact historical frontier.

## Research use in assets and text

These sources justify themes and distinguish identities. They are not final super-event quotations, song selections, voice licenses, photographic portrait sources or model references. The super-event specialists must research final wording and audio through their owning workflow. The asset team must inspect actual historical design sources for flags and symbols, preserve source provenance, and produce each final flag as a flat reference-constrained reconstruction.

Grounded Spartan institutions need authentic institutional source material for their native portrait consumers. No photograph of an ancient king is assumed to exist. An actor, statue, reenactor, invented face or generated historical likeness is not an approved fallback. Castor and Polydeuces are explicitly fictional mythic beings, with individual generated portrait consumers only in the supernatural setting.

For 3D production, the current skill requires modern designed-artwork references and separately modeled held items. Historical archaeological photographs and statues are not automatically suitable Meshy inputs. Sound selection needs source and license evidence for the actual recording. Public-domain mythology does not make a modern recording free to redistribute.
