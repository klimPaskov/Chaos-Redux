# 120 Widespread Mutiny, Part 4: Soldiers acquire a country

## The institution and its responsibilities

An armed council inherits responsibility for soldiers, inhabited territory and promises made during the crisis. Its country package asks whether protection from an unwanted military obligation can coexist with civilian government, reliable supplies and a defensible border. The country remains playable after its founding emergency. Recognition, negotiated return and independent survival are meaningful outcomes.

The package serves both an ordinary domestic armed mutiny and the rare joint council in [Part 3](120_widespread_mutiny_spec_part_3_evolution.md). Domestic founders share one military origin. Joint founders retain separate origin records, repatriation duties and civilian constituencies. Their common institutions do not erase different languages, equipment or political beliefs. All additional names in this part are working labels under the package-wide text rule.

Disaffection and Agreement confidence retain their existing meanings. A domestic council inherits its originating incident's values. A joint country begins with the higher relevant Disaffection and the lower relevant Agreement confidence. Neither activation nor a new flag grants a favorable reset. Donor incidents continue for their remaining cohorts, with the departed formations resolved through their actual transfer outcome. The new council owns its subsequent promises. It gains no additional legitimacy, fraternity, dependency or council-currency meter.

Recognition is acknowledgment of specific rights or a settlement by another government. Independent existence is the council's continued territorial government. An unrecognized council can sustain itself, and a recognized council can voluntarily reintegrate. Foreign refusal cannot leave every domestic route inaccessible.

## Founding conditions and country identity

Country activation is a committed outcome of the armed-separation branch. It requires surviving participating land formations, a coherent local footprint, a valid capital, supply, civilian administration and an accepted institutional mandate. Evaluate the established Event 116 recipient option first under [Part 5](120_widespread_mutiny_spec_part_5_connections.md). A compatible recipient must accept the actual obligations. The soldiers' and recipient's acceptance follow that ownership contract. An armed defection can occur against the former government's wishes while conserving the actual people and property.

The founding footprint is selected from territory that the participating force can actually administer and defend. Each included state needs its own justification. A front-sector record alone does not grant its states, population or facilities. Joint founding territory stays within the verified local sector defined in Part 3. The footprint is frozen when the country activates, so later conquests cannot rewrite the original claim.

Failed preparation leaves the original formations and property with their actual owners. Reservations are released without issuing troops, stockpiles or development rewards. An uncertain irreversible outcome instead quarantines the exact disputed set until reconciled under Part 5. It cannot prompt a guessed replacement or an unproved rollback. The surviving incident retains valid negotiation, withdrawal and discharge possibilities. A stateless committee can remain a political institution while its soldiers' practical outcome is settled.

### Choosing to play the council

A human controlling an originating government can explicitly elect to continue as the completed council. The offer appears only after the new country's actual forces, territory, government and obligations have passed admission. It identifies the country being left, the new country's situation and the consequences for control. Decline is the unattended default. Both original human governments keep their control until a player expressly accepts a valid offer.

When both origins have human players, offer the role first to the player whose country contributed the greatest actual founding manpower. Resolve a tie by the earlier founding-cohort admission, then by a stable carrier identifier. That offer lasts 10 days. On decline or expiry, offer it once to the next eligible founding player for 10 days. Exactly one player can accept for the new incarnation. The council acts under its normal AI until acceptance, and these offers do not freeze combat or promises. An accepted transfer cancels all other offers. If every offer expires, the country remains AI-controlled.

Control transfer is the last committed step after recipient setup and any required receiving notification are queued safely. It preserves the eligible originating campaign and achievement lineage, with no duplicate achievement owner. Ordinary government players can remain where they are and interact with the council diplomatically. Exact runtime player-transfer support is a feasibility gate for the playable handoff, as it is for later reintegration.

## Starting property, research and laws

Factories, resources, railways, infrastructure, ports and airbases remain physical state assets in their observed condition. Transfer of a state does not create a duplicate facility reward. Depot contents, transport, land-army fuel and reserve manpower enter the council's possession only through measured accepted transfers. Their donor loses the same property through its single owning transaction. Strategic stockpiles and unrelated military assets remain with their entitled owners.

Event 120 founding handles land formations only. It creates or transfers no fleet, ship, air wing, aircraft reserve or naval personnel package. Contextual sympathy at a port or airbase cannot enlarge that authority. Unrelated forces present in transferred territory retain their ownership and need lawful access, withdrawal or the applicable normal game resolution.

Starting research access follows functioning civilian and military industry. Use one slot below five factories, two from five factories, and three from fifteen factories when at least five are civilian. These are discrete institutional capacities. The council inherits provenance-backed civilian and production knowledge associated with its founding industrial center and the conventional technologies needed to maintain its received land equipment. It does not receive the complete technology union of its founders.

The accepted command tradition supplies the starting doctrine baseline. A joint council chooses a common training standard and bears retraining costs. Later research follows ordinary progression. Advanced weapons in a surviving formation do not automatically convey every related project, production system or secret arsenal.

Production initially prioritizes maintainable infantry equipment, then support equipment and the transport needed by the real supply network. Existing artillery and armor retain their actual crews, equipment condition and fuel needs. New lines require factories, resources and relevant knowledge. Imports, licences and donor shipments need real counterparties and actual deliveries. Resource extraction requires an existing deposit.

The council starts with volunteer-based recruitment. A severe defense emergency can authorize limited conscription through its civilian emergency procedure. Economy law follows actual war and mobilization conditions, with a recovery transition after peace. Trade policy supports necessary inputs without promising resources the country cannot obtain. Civilian courts, accountable policing and review of unjustified detention are part of administration. Forced levies, collective punishment and unauthorized seizure breach the council's commitments and affect the same two event values.

Party names and any existing lawful political organizations follow the admitted regional identity. The new council begins with zero political power, command power and country-level experience reserves, then earns them through ordinary government, training, combat and actual accepted work. A compatible living carrier instead retains its existing lawful resources and politics except for the expressly accepted council transformation. It cannot apply the new-country setup to refill or reset its accounts.

Legal subject relationships are addressed explicitly. A negotiated autonomy or protection agreement states recruitment authority, access, aid obligations, release terms and the powers retained by the civilian government. Faction membership requires a separate accepted invitation compatible with existing front commitments. A sponsor does not automatically obtain the council's troops or draw it into unrelated wars. These core agreements must remain playable without DLC. Relevant DLC interfaces may deepen their presentation and management.

## An army with actual origins

Starting strength is the surviving membership of the committed cohorts. Preserve the required native identity, manpower fill, equipment, organization, experience and ownership properties. A weak opening may contain one depleted formation. A large mutiny can contain several differently equipped formations from up to three cohorts. Chaos changes the circumstances and possible participation. It supplies no free soldiers.

Founding transfers and partial personnel outcomes remain subject to the native capability gates in Parts 5 and 8. An aggregate civil-war fraction, deleted-and-recreated formation or fresh fully equipped unit cannot stand in for the promised preservation. Country activation waits for a valid physical operation.

Inherited formations initially retain their templates. The soldiers remain ordinary humans with conventional subunits, equipment, models and appropriate existing sounds. Council affiliation changes command and institutions. Later template policy offers local infantry defense, a supplied field reserve and support detachments that the industrial base can sustain. Terrain-specialist battalions require the actual technology, recruitment and training. Refitting consumes ordinary equipment and time.

Reinforcement has three accountable routes. Existing formations receive paid replacements. Civilian-authorized volunteers enter ordinary training from actual eligible reserves. A verified foreign shipment transfers measured equipment and can support a training agreement. None of these routes instantly fills a division. Protected discharged personnel from Part 2 remain unavailable until their original terms allow voluntary return.

At most one new formation trains per functioning military factory, with a ceiling of five concurrent council recruitment orders. Training needs paid equipment, eligible manpower, a sustainable supply plan and the required civilian authorization. A country with no military factory can preserve, equip and retrain existing formations through accepted deliveries while developing production. It cannot bypass the recruitment cap through a focus reward.

Commanders follow verified allegiance and lawful reassignment. The same person cannot command for both owners at once. A quartermaster institution and civilian administration role can support logistics and negotiations without fabricated biographies or a padded advisor roster.

Later naval or air development uses ordinary research, production and assets the council actually owns through normal gameplay. A maritime country can invest in convoy protection, ports and repair when it has the relevant capacity. Air development requires appropriate owned aircraft, bases, personnel, fuel and maintenance. The shared tree reveals those support modules only when they become useful. It never backfills a founding fleet or air force. Landlocked and very small councils receive a compact land-defense architecture.

## Focus-tree architecture and immediate action

Founding immediately exposes feasible relief, civilian-protection and settlement actions. A threatened country cannot wait 35 days to discover its survival tools. The focus opening is a small provisional-administration group followed by the three 35-day political anchors below. Substantial institutional, industrial and military programs normally use 70 days. Delivery and recovery missions use 90–180 days where their work warrants it.

A focus completes administrative preparation or a route commitment. Material rewards depend on actual delivery. It cannot complete a compact, extend a ceasefire or restore absent soldiers merely because its calendar ended. The zero-spend discharge and other lawful exits in Part 2 survive political route locks.

## Political route A: Negotiated service charter

This route uses territorial authority to secure enforceable service rights and a lawful status. Its 35-day anchor commissions a civilian delegation and commits the council to defensive conduct while an initial 90-day settlement is pursued. It opens specific offers concerning leave, discharge, accepted officers, fair treatment and repatriation. The actual compact uses Part 2's costs and receipts. A focus does not charge a second fee for an already paid compact.

Middle anchor groups establish a complaints process, agreed command supervision and civilian custody of the settlement. The council publishes a feasible schedule and reserves the real transport or supplies promised. A first completed delivery transforms provisional command into a contractual institution. Further decisions return eligible formations to service, establish an agreed reserve or discharge personnel under their protected terms.

The final settlement can preserve an autonomous country or return the council's jurisdiction to the original country. Recognition specifies the counterpart's actual obligations. It grants no right to the other origin's soldiers. A joint council negotiates each origin's personnel separately while preserving the civilian settlement. One contingent's return leaves the remaining institution viable only if its own land, forces and administration still satisfy the country contract.

The route trades military freedom and time for credible guarantees and continued access. A refused offer leaves domestic government operational. The council can revise terms, sustain provisional independence or make one openly recorded transition toward civilian constitutional independence. Consumed resources, completed construction and accepted physical dispositions remain historical facts through that transition.

A broken signed settlement lowers confidence and worsens disaffection through the shared promise rules. Replacing delegates does not erase the breach. The payoff is a fulfilled service relationship and an agreed political status, with both autonomous survival and complete return designed as real outcomes.

## Political route B: Civilian territorial republic

This route gives civilian inhabitants the authority to decide the durable government. Its 35-day anchor unlocks an explicit 90-day consultation action. The start quote is political power `r5(25q)`, with q from Part 2 applied once to the council's total actual participating manpower. It funds representation and administration, while supply, transport and reconstruction retain their separate actual commitments. Civilian representation covers towns, districts and relevant minorities. Coercive recruitment is suspended during ratification. The process must deliver its actual access and safeguards before its government reward is granted.

Middle anchor groups separate civilian courts from service discipline, establish a budget, choose accountable public reconstruction or a supervised commercial agreement, and place recruitment under civilian authorization. They unlock procurement, local works and voluntary training decisions. Government and party identity follow the resulting coalition and origin context. The tree develops credible political institutions without imposing an ideology quota.

Each founding civilian jurisdiction receives a recorded consultation, access to its representatives, the agreed language access where required and a factual list of promised services. The authored ratification test requires 90 elapsed days of the process, actual civilian access throughout the final 30 days, reliable supply or completed relevant basic-service delivery for those 30 days, no unresolved reprisal or forced-levy breach, Disaffection at most 50 and Agreement confidence at least 60. These conditions are a transparent gameplay abstraction of a valid mandate, not a claim that a native election engine measures consent. No additional public value is introduced. Existing institutions and residents' recorded choices determine the resulting civilian coalition, with final local naming and party mapping researched for the admitted identity.

All qualifying jurisdictions ratify. If only some qualify, they retain their completed consultations while the others receive one disclosed 30-day remedy period for the named missing condition. This extension consumes no second full consultation fee and grants no government or confidence reward in advance. If none qualify, or the extension expires without complete consent, the council remains provisional and chooses a revised mandate or a negotiated jurisdiction settlement. A fresh 90-day process becomes available only after the failed conditions have actually changed. Delivered work and costs survive, and no repeated start bonus is available. Military turnout alone cannot constitute civilian consent. A dissenting district can seek an agreed jurisdiction settlement while every formation retains its separate ownership record.

The route slows emergency mobilization and diverts effort to services. Its payoff is a durable regional republic with regular civilian government, sustainable defense and a reconstruction program. It can pursue recognition while maintaining an independent domestic path. A failed vote produces a revised mandate, negotiated status or continued provisional administration. It never becomes automatic approval after a timer expires.

Exclusion, reprisals or fabricated consent suspend integration rewards and reopen the constitutional settlement. Restitution, protected consultation and a renewed mandate can restore the route. A full civil war requires the actual armed and territorial conditions of the event rather than a political disagreement alone.

## Political route C: Emergency defensive command

This route grants accepted professional command temporary authority against an immediate threat to the inhabited footprint. Its 35-day anchor requires real supplied formations, accepted officers, a civilian emergency resolution and an available reserved commitment of 10 army experience. Completion revalidates the continuing threat, commits that amount once and starts the fixed 90-day command mandate. If the mandate is no longer valid, release the unused reservation without granting temporary powers. It opens coordinated reserve and repair decisions.

The temporary power window gives 25% improved organization recovery to the qualifying council land-defense package and 25% slower civilian construction. It grants no offensive claim, free reinforcement or instant restoration of organization. When a formation also qualifies for Part 2's 10% delivered-compact recovery benefit, only the stronger recovery benefit applies for the overlapping period. The same institution owns the effect and its expiry.

Middle anchor groups reconcile orders with delegates' rights, audit requisitions and prepare civilian handover. Concentrated command can hold the threatened footprint while reconstruction waits. Unauthorized coercion may produce temporary obedience but worsens the existing values and endangers lawful recruitment. Its consequences persist after the military emergency ends.

The civilian audit mission is available immediately when the mandate starts. It takes 60 days and uses the already funded emergency administration. It requires an actual deployment account, recorded property custody, access for the founding civilian representatives and remedy of any unauthorized seizure. Optional focus programs can support the audit concurrently, but are never prerequisites for starting it. A valid audit unlocks the handover decision immediately, with a 15-day completion window, so ordinary completion can occur by mandate day 75. A failed audit identifies the outstanding remedy and leaves the original day-90 deadline intact.

Handover restores authority to the accepted founding civilian body and ends the emergency powers. It can establish the route's professional territorial reserve without requiring a second 90-day constituent process to finish first. A later full constitutional settlement follows its own route and timing. Failure to hand over by day 90 is an actual broken promise, ends the temporary benefit and opens the command-removal and civilian-authorization recovery actions.

A defended civilian center and completed audit allow the mandate to end in a professional territorial reserve under civilian emergency law. This final institution differs from the republic route's broader participatory military structure. A negotiated service settlement remains another valid conclusion.

Further exceptional authority requires a new civilian decision responding to a continuing actual threat, with its resource and construction burden disclosed. It never reapplies the original temporary reward. The command must pursue the handover deadline. Refusing oversight degrades the existing institution and closes new lawful recruitment. Removing the offending command, compensating affected districts and reopening civilian authorization offer recovery. No route permits a focus to extend Part 3's local ceasefire beyond its separate 90-day maximum.

## Development and the three spirit families

Construction, recruitment and institutional development follow separate obligations from founding property transfers. A repair focus opens a 120-day priority program for one selected owned and controlled site. Its choices are repair of an actual damaged civilian or military factory, repair of an existing supply connection, or new construction of one civilian factory. A later workshop choice can build one military factory when at least two functioning civilian factories and the native building slot and inputs are available. A production or training focus opens the paid program and its choices. These rewards cannot repeat through annexation, release, route switching or a reused carrier.

The construction contract commits one to three actually available civilian factories, with the chosen amount and opportunity cost displayed before acceptance. It uses the native construction cost, current speed and actual site conditions. There is no second PP payment merely for placing the same work in a program. An accepted sponsor can provide the measured equivalent under a real aid contract, with donor capacity committed once. If capacity cannot be reserved and observed through a verified mechanism, that implementation remains blocked rather than granting a finished building by timer.

The preview gives the predicted finish and distinguishes a 120-day review from a promise to complete a new factory in 120 days. A small council may finish only part of a large project. At review, completed native work counts once. Unfinished work retains its real progress and can continue in the ordinary queue, or renew the priority commitment after a fresh capacity check. Renewal supplies no new factory, confidence reward or Chaos milestone. Loss of the site cancels future commitment and leaves physical work with the state. A promised completion date is binding only when expressly accepted from a feasible quote, and missing it follows the common breach rules.

Failed delivery cancels unpaid benefits and releases unused commitments under the common refund rules. Completed physical construction remains in its state. A new owner does not receive another copy, and an old owner does not destroy completed civilian work merely because a council ceased to exist.

## A bounded territorial settlement

The territorial branch makes the frozen founding footprint defensible and supplied. Its tools are access, neutral transport, return of occupied founding ground and agreed borders. At most one adjacent state outside that footprint can enter negotiations when indispensable for supply. The selected state's actual geography and value must justify the connection. Access is sufficient where feasible. Annexation requires civilian consent and an accepted settlement. The objective cannot grow after conquest.

A military recovery mandate covers only specified founding territory held by a hostile government. It identifies participants, civilian protections and the relevant war relationship. Guarantees, faction obligations and outside intervention are considered before commitment. No military route creates a general claim to the former owner's country or an excuse to annex the supply neighbor.

An access agreement, return to pre-operation lines or another accepted limited settlement becomes available when the disclosed objective is secured or the attacker is repelled. Outside intervention, attacks beyond the mandate or renewed mass coercion close the limited branch and trigger a disclosed wider-crisis review. Temporary benefits and claims cannot continue under a falsely limited-war label.

Actual national peace uses Part 5's Event 009 ownership contract and its exact eligible context. A local ceasefire remains the Part 3 agreement. Native preservation, enforceable local restraints and a supported limited settlement are explicit feasibility gates. If the required operation is unsupported, the corresponding implementation remains blocked. A national white peace cannot silently replace local restraint.

After settlement, missions restore services, resolve civilian jurisdiction and retire temporary war goals or claims. Durable civilian acceptance and an audited identity basis are required for any core integration. The payoff is a viable local polity or agreed return, with continuing diplomacy and development.

Part 5 measures sustained industrial independence, broader civilian jurisdiction and independent operational reach against the founding snapshot. Its milestones recognize actual outcomes and retain their lineage caps. They grant no additional territorial objective, factory, formation or political right. Completing a focus, changing the institution or renewing a mandate grants no separate Chaos reward. Civilian-backed settlement and reintegration can reverse only the event-owned amounts that Part 5 explicitly permits.

## AI, succession and original-country return

[Part 6](120_widespread_mutiny_spec_part_6_ai_balance.md) owns the common AI framework. Council AI protects supply and the founding population before growth. A small exposed enclave favors deliverable service guarantees. A viable industrial region with civilian participation favors constitutional independence. Immediate armed danger can favor emergency command when officers, supply and a defensible objective exist. Joint councils prioritize reciprocal protection and repatriation, then compare their independent prospects with concrete offers from both governments.

Route choice remains a commitment. Temporary adversity does not trigger a new political choice every review. Invalid sponsors, impossible borders and unsupported transactions cannot receive positive action validity. Recognition refusal changes diplomatic tactics while domestic recovery continues. AI keeps its only effective formation near the actual defensive need and develops only equipment it can sustain.

Original-country return requires a current lawful recipient, accepted civilian status and an exact schedule for surviving property and formations. If the original country no longer exists, an appropriate successor must be identified and separately accepted. The displayed settlement does not invent consent from an extinct government.

The human player receives the full country-ending terms before acceptance, with cancellation as the unattended default. A return of player control needs verified runtime support before that route can be implemented faithfully. The receiving country's meaningful tree, history and unrelated institutions remain intact.

Returning survivors never restores earlier deaths, already discharged soldiers or formations that joined another recipient. Outstanding protected personnel and equipment claims remain accounted for. Finished construction stays with its state. Unused council rewards, reservations, temporary strategies and obsolete missions retire at the lawful transition.

Annexation, release or reuse of a carrier invalidates old proposals and callbacks for the prior incarnation. A fresh identity package cannot inherit another council's acceptance, claims, rewards or personal debts from its tag alone. Supply and capital loss first trigger relocation or a feasible negotiated withdrawal. Defeat resolves actual survivors and occupation. It does not mint a replacement exile army.
