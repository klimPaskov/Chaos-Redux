# 068 ZIN: Rush and the Golden Court

## The kingdom before the succession

The canonical Rush Kingdom is the largest common colony and the political center of the early ZIN arrivals. Its strength comes from a human kingdom, established armies, and recognized allied peoples. It is not a federation of every creature drawn on the Rush map.

The Golden King begins in power. His hoards, sale of offices, court spending, and purchased loyalty can finance an impressive army while weakening public trust. His rule should create useful short-term choices with political costs. A player can prepare institutions that survive him, exploit his court, or allow Afrit to become indispensable. None of these choices prevents the king's death.

The kingdom's internal map follows remembered institutions from Rush, Selen, Hiloron, Kilinti, Huhun, the Great Lake, and the island houses. These are relationships transported to Earth. Earth states do not acquire their names automatically, and a forest in France is not declared to be the original Kilinti Forest.

### Starting package

| Field army | 15 Rush line formations, 5 frontier formations, and 5 Huhun mounted formations |
| Elite access | The homeland dominion branches unlock Selen, Hiloron, and Kilinti formations through their own commitments |

Research slots and factory counts are structural quantities, so they are not forced to multiples of five. All starting equipment is real and sufficient for the specified formations. The package does not grant modern aircraft, tanks, chemical stockpiles, or every Earth technology.

## The two public values

**Rush Unity** ranges from 0 to 100 and describes support for a shared Rush state among the army, recognized dominions, capital, and Earth residents. It is not personal approval of the king. High Unity can support a constitutional coalition against his court after his death.

**Afrit's Influence** ranges from 0 to 100 and describes access to the court, clergy, officers, dominions, and institutions. It is visible as political penetration. The interface does not announce that Afrit has murdered the king before evidence proves it.

| Band | Rush Unity | Afrit's Influence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-20 | Institutions are divided and local defense takes priority. | Afrit has contacts but cannot openly direct the state. |
| 25-45 | Cooperation depends on specific charters and commanders. | Several intermediaries can obstruct reform or offer bargains. |
| 50-70 | A common constitutional or royal settlement can command broad support. | A direct succession claim becomes credible when the relevant footholds exist. |
| 75-100 | A shared political camp can retain most ordinary forces during the succession. | Afrit can contest the capital and begin the civil war with a major advantage. |

Intermediate integer values use the lower completed band. Ordinary changes occur in increments of 5 or 10. Route milestones can apply 15, 20, or 25. Values are clamped and shown as integers.

Unity rises through fulfilled dominion charters, protected Earth communities, delivered military aid, fair arbitration, and restoration of an institution previously damaged by the court. It falls through broken promises, confiscation, abandoned fronts, coerced integration, and civil-war losses that discredit the player's camp.

Influence rises through accepted bargains, appointed agents, corrupted military support, deliberate suppression of evidence, and the king's most extractive court policies. High Chaos increases the opportunity for these incidents, but does not grant an unavoidable daily increase. Influence falls through proven exposure, removal of an identified intermediary, protected independent institutions, and refusal of a concrete bargain.

Repeating a routine administrative decision cannot farm either value. Each charter, exposed network, fulfilled pledge, and repaired harm has an identity and a one-time outcome. A later betrayal can create a new problem, but cannot reset the original reward.

## Pre-succession tree architecture

The tree has seven connected route families before and around the death, then four substantial late political continuations. The following labels are internal focus work items. They describe the content and are not final localisation.

| Family | Sequence and choice | Persistent consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R-Court | Inventory the royal holdings, decide how to finance settlement, appoint the court officers, then choose a protected civil budget or a military patronage system. | Determines which court institutions can later resist or assist Afrit. |
| R-Dominions | Reaffirm the recognized peoples, negotiate individual charters, open genuine outposts, and choose royal centralization or a negotiated federation. | Gives territory, specialist units, local loyalty, and binding constitutional obligations. |
| R-Earth | Preserve residents, establish translators, negotiate recognition, and choose common citizenship, parallel administration, or coercive rule. | Changes human recruitment, aid, resistance, and eligibility for the good succession. |
| R-Army | Standardize Rush equipment, organize frontier defense, integrate specialist commands, and choose a professional core or mass royal levies. | Sets ordinary recruitment, equipment dependence, and the succession's officer alignment. |
| R-Crossing | Stabilize the supply bridge, build an independent supply base, open one related settlement request, and organize refugee passage. | Makes the army sustainable and supports the later dominion outposts. |
| R-LostCause | Establish a defensive council, obtain one external colony partner, convene the founding meeting, and set mutual-defense obligations. | Creates The Lost Cause under the Golden King without automatically absorbing Earth factions. |
| R-CourtAccess | Investigate unexplained appointments or employ the new intermediaries, respond to the first major bargain, and decide who controls court records. | Shapes Afrit's claim, evidence survival, and the strength of corrupted formations. |

Institution-building focuses normally take 70 days. Focuses that choose between two already prepared policies take 35 days. Immediate crisis instructions can use 7 days where delay would make a playable response impossible. This is a pacing plan, not a requirement to pad every branch to the same length.

A minimum playable pre-death route must include several meaningful choices across economy, dominions, Earth policy, military organization, and diplomacy. The player should not be able to finish the Golden Court by taking five identical factory focuses. The implementation blueprint must show dependencies and mutually exclusive commitments, but may choose final focus names and coordinates after the route architecture is approved.

### R-Court: wealth and authority

The first economic choice determines what the royal hoard does. Releasing a civil allocation commits two civilian factories for 90 days to housing and supply repairs, increases Unity by 10 on delivery, and makes later private requisition more costly. Keeping the hoard intact gives 100 political power and a 15 percent military construction advantage for 180 days, but lowers Unity by 10 and opens an Afrit intermediary appointment.

Selling offices is a separate choice, not the same reward under another title. It funds a military equipment shipment and improves the loyalty of ordinary court officers. It also produces one named compromised office that may later obstruct a purge. The player can abolish the sale later, but loses the bought officers' support during the succession.

The king can appoint a civil chancellor, a military patron, or a court intermediary. These are institutional appointments with distinct consequences. A civil chancellor improves the execution of charters. A military patron makes conventional Rush forces more reliable for the royal camp. The intermediary supplies a strong short-term benefit and increases Afrit's Influence by 15. These choices use character or institution data, not three additional permanent national spirits.

The court remains a poor government even on the best preparation route. Its compulsory maintenance incidents should show neglected settlements and private spending. The player can fund repairs and protect a local institution, but cannot transform the Golden King into a generous ruler and bypass the intended succession.

### R-Army: a kingdom able to survive its king

Professionalization improves the quality of ordinary Rush formations and provides field replacement services. Royal levies increase the number of available human formations and retain more ties to court patrons. Neither branch changes the species of its recruits.

Specialist commands require their respective dominion charter. Selen Rangers cannot be unlocked through a generic infantry research focus. A Hiloron giant host requires Hiloron's political cooperation and its own replacement pool. Kilinti ents require a forest-preservation agreement. Huhun riders require remount and supply arrangements. Island forces require an admitted maritime partner.

An integrated command creates combined-arms opportunities without merging all cultures into one template. The army can receive a Ranger reconnaissance detachment, giant siege support, ent forest defense, or fast mounted reinforcement. Each has a real equipment and replacement requirement. The player cannot obtain a new giant by replacing an ordinary infantry battalion with a different template name.

## The recognized dominions

Each dominion has a charter, a responsible institution or leader, a military obligation, and a remembered grievance. Its relationship is displayed qualitatively as cooperative, estranged, or compromised, with the current reason. These are not additional public meters.

The four land dominions can exist as departments inside the founding settlement before they gain their own Earth outposts. A dominion without its own state still has political representatives and unit families. It cannot become an independent country until a legal state is assigned to it. Outposts remain genuine arrivals, not fabricated claims to existing Earth land.

### Selen

Selen's proposed displaced institution is a ranger assembly that records border paths, protects villages, and checks the court's claims about distant threats. Its historical hook should describe forest service and old frontier obligations, without inventing a sourced historical battle from a handwritten place label.

The Selen charter offers two workable settlements. A protected ranger jurisdiction preserves local patrol authority and supports a federal or popular succession. A royal frontier command places the Rangers under central supply and war planning, but does not make them comfortable with Afrit. The coercive third option replaces their officers and can provide temporary obedience while destroying the normal Ranger recruitment route.

Its signature mission requires a real protected settlement or aid corridor to remain safe for 90 days. Delivering that mission grants a five-formation Ranger mustering allotment, Unity +10, and access to an investigation against one compromised court office. Abandoning the protected community makes Selen estranged and can move its formations away from the Court when the king dies.

Selen is the strongest organized anti-Afrit dominion. A compromised Selen commander is a named exceptional event with evidence and a recovery route, not proof that the entire ranger culture routinely supports corruption.

### Hiloron

Hiloron's giants arrive with the memory of giant settlements, stone work, and negotiated service to Rush. Their political importance comes from the fact that a small number of giants can decide a siege or keep a supply route open.

The player chooses between a civil works compact and a permanent siege obligation. Civil works commit giant labor to actual damaged infrastructure for 90 days and improve Unity when repairs are complete. The siege obligation unlocks stronger wartime availability but makes Hiloron oppose careless offensive wars and prolonged unreplaced losses.

Hiloron receives a limited giant mustering allotment and cannot replace casualties with human manpower. A lost giant host is a political event as well as a military loss. Replacement requires the charter, a secure crossing, and the family cooldown. The kingdom can borrow giants from an allied Hiloron country through a recorded service agreement. It cannot duplicate them when that country later joins a federation.

Hiloron chooses sides through its charter and the treatment of its formations. It normally supports a lawful common settlement, and can support Einendil when the good route remains open. Afrit must corrupt, coerce, or conquer it. His victory over the Rush crown alone does not turn every giant leader loyal overnight.

### Kilinti

The Kilinti Forest represents the ents and old forest peoples recognized by Rush. Preserve the user's Kilinti spelling in political and unit identities while recording Klinti as a map alias. Do not silently rewrite the original map.

Its charter protects specified forests and permits a controlled military contribution. The two main choices are a forest sanctuary with strong local defense or a negotiated wartime guardian obligation that permits deployment outside the forest for a limited campaign. A logging or forced-labor route gives immediate timber-related industrial advantages but closes ordinary ent recruitment and creates opposition.

The signature mission protects one real forest state while rebuilding its transport and settlements without destructive extraction. Success allows a small ent host and a durable defensive relationship. A second host requires a later treaty and replacement capacity, not another click on the same mission.

Ents are slow and very strong. Their political memory lasts through annexation and government changes. Afrit can bind a corrupted ent force only after a specific corruption or conquest sequence. That force uses its own visual and reinforcement variant. A green ent icon recolored purple does not satisfy the asset requirement.

### Huhun

Huhun's riders remember the open routes between Rush settlements and the pressure to answer several distant calls for help. Their Earth dilemma is whether to serve the crown's central army or maintain a network of local mounted defense.

The central-service charter supplies the kingdom with a larger reserve of riders and makes royal commanders more influential. The route-defense charter gives Huhun control of remount depots and places its political loyalty with fulfilled local commitments. The player must choose which threatened allied state receives the next rapid intervention when several calls overlap.

Its mission is a real reinforcement assignment with a 90-day commitment. A state that remains held with the promised force earns Unity +10 and a rider replacement allotment. Abandonment preserves surviving units but breaks the pledge. The mission does not generate a second army merely because a front changed.

Huhun can form the operational center of the Free Dominions. It favors a federation that can actually deliver reinforcements over an empty promise of autonomy.

### Rush Island Houses

The Rush Island Houses retain the supplied names Rush, Asgard, Hould, Faber, and Tint as the brief's political labels. The handwriting differs in places and belongs in the alias register. House competition concerns ship ownership, port administration, trade, and the cost of supporting an inland crown.

The mainland kingdom can negotiate a naval service charter, a commercial autonomy charter, or a coercive royal monopoly. Naval service supplies a limited fleet and marine contingent after a functioning port and crews are secured. Commercial autonomy improves trade and allied supply but gives the houses more freedom to refuse continental wars. Monopoly provides short-term revenue and can split the houses during the succession.

A landlocked Rush Kingdom cannot receive ships in a lake or summon a free ocean port. The maritime route requires a real coastal partner or a lawful acquired coast. A separate Rush Island Confederacy can remain independent, join the kingdom, or become the maritime base of a surviving good Rush splinter.

## Opening the dominion outposts

The proposed ordinary schedule opens the first outpost opportunity after 90 days of Rush rule. Further opportunities can occur after 180 and 270 days. Each requires a valid state and is processed through the ordinary crossing allocator. The founding charter chooses whether the new dominion is a subject or an independent treaty partner. The player cannot choose an exact Earth state.

One territorial outpost is mandatory preparation for the succession. Additional outposts broaden the possible war. The death clock is not reset by delaying a focus. When ordinary placement fails, the system retries within the reserved capacity and reports the structural problem. It never quietly replaces the civil war with a loyalty mission.

Before the death is committed, the crisis builder proves at least two legally owned states among the kingdom and admitted dominions. If that proof fails because the world has removed every viable state, the crisis remains explicitly blocked and preserves the king-death request. This is a structural impossibility, not a peaceful completion or a route that saved the king. The manual Empty Throne scenario performs the same proof before launch.

## Founding The Lost Cause

The Golden King forms the faction after Rush has completed its defensive council and obtained one willing external colony partner or two willing recognized dominions. The founding does not require 1000 Chaos or the arrival of Volgan. It is a response to remembered threats and the need to protect scattered settlements.

The founder expects tribute and prestige. Friendly members join because Rush can provide military help. They do not have to approve of the king. The founding terms include mutual defense, protected civilian passage, and a council process for offensive intervention. The king can abuse the tribute clause, creating later constitutional disputes.

A member's obligation is concrete. It can pledge equipment, a formation, naval transport, or civilian construction capacity for a named project. A promise without delivery does not count as successful cooperation. Broken promises affect the same relationship later used in the succession and faction-leadership transfer.

Earth countries can establish a liaison and contribute to a project before full membership is available. They do not automatically leave their existing factions at the first colony arrival. Formal Earth membership opens through the wider policy routes and the Reckoning, with explicit diplomatic consequences.

## Afrit's access to the court

Afrit's agents can arrive with court contacts even when his sovereign Worshipdom has not appeared on Earth. This allows the mandatory baseline succession story to continue at low Chaos. It does not create the Worshipdom early or grant an E1 arrival through an unrelated baseline button.

The five possible institutional footholds are the royal treasury, court appointments, military supply, religious intermediaries, and a dominion liaison. These are finite qualitative footholds. Rush sees suspicious access and political influence, not a complete list of invisible conspirators or the future murder method.

Every foothold has an offer, an immediate benefit, an obligation, an exposure path, and a removal cost. For example, a military supply intermediary can deliver equipment while placing a compromised officer in the replacement service. Removing that officer cancels the ongoing supply benefit and can cost 50 political power, but reduces Influence by 10 and protects part of the regular army during the succession.

The king's death is Afrit's responsibility in every ordinary Rush route. Evidence can change, intermediaries can differ, and the actual means can remain uncertain. The design must not randomly assign another killer or make the responsibility depend on a late dice roll. An earlier defeat of the Worshipdom does not erase orders and agents already placed in the court.

## The end of the Golden Court

The proposed normal death window opens 540 days after the canonical Rush arrival, with a 30-day MTTH and a final scheduled crisis commitment at day 630 when the territorial builder is valid. A sufficiently advanced court route can bring that window forward to day 365. No focus, high Unity, lack of E1, or absence of a sovereign Worshipdom cancels the death.

The death immediately begins the succession war. Preparation determines who holds territory and forces. It does not turn the war into an avoidable failure branch. The next specification part owns the death reports, claimant packages, Einendil's appearance, and the four late outcomes.
