# 068 ZIN: decisions, missions and visible controls

## A small active surface for a large event

The event contains many societies, but one player should see only the actions relevant to the current country and problem. The registry is a design catalog. It is not a request to display every row at once. A country normally has at most six primary choices in one active phase and one to three concurrent missions. The selected partner or target state filters the remaining tools.

Priority is immediate survival, an active treaty or war deadline, a succession commitment, a valid society-specific problem, then optional development. Expired targets and concluded incidents disappear from the active list but remain in the appropriate history. A no-longer-relevant category stays hidden.

The only new persistent public meters are Rush Unity and Afrit's Influence. Founding supply, diplomatic standing, a dominion charter, a binding operation and castle custody use qualitative states plus their actual remaining time. Existing Chaos, Famine, Migration and other systems retain their own values and interfaces.

The four spendable cost families are political power, command power, equipment and committed civilian factories. Convoys and named creature support are equipment. No ZIN mana, souls, prestige, influence currency, food currency or private treasury meter is added. Ordinary CP costs stay at or below 60.

## Cost and outcome contract

Every action checks its target, cost, phase and ownership again at commitment. A button and its normal decision equivalent call the same gameplay outcome. The AI uses that same action path. The visual interface never grants a cheaper or broader version.

PP and CP pay for the committed operation and are not refunded merely because its outcome is unfavorable. Material already delivered, consumed or lost is not refunded. If the operation becomes impossible before dispatch because a reserved target ceases to exist, release unused equipment reservations and future factory capacity. If it becomes impossible after partial work, retain actual delivered construction and losses and cancel only the unfinished portion. A refund cannot manufacture a second copy of a shipment.

A factory commitment uses the exact country's available capacity and records its start, release date and remaining obligation. No action can commit the same capacity twice. The implementation must prove the chosen native consumer or shared project adapter. A text note that two factories are busy is not a paid factory cost.

A contract has an offer, acceptance or refusal, delivery, performance and closure. The recipient can refuse unless a declared war settlement has already imposed the term. An action that sends a request cannot immediately grant the requested army as though agreement were guaranteed.

## Mission lifecycle

A mission has one owner, an exact incident or project identity, a target, a deadline and a success predicate. Beginning it does not resolve its outcome. Its owner is the country responsible for the promise, which may differ from the target state's current controller.

| Phase | Required behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Prepared | Validate target and resources, reserve only what the operation needs, and explain the success condition. |
| Active | Track the actual relevant route, construction, supply, control, delivery or political evidence. Do not use a whole-world daily scan. |
| Successful | Commit the promised outcome once, release unused reservations, record actual delivery and retire the mission. |
| Failed | Apply the named failure once, retain existing material facts and expose a meaningful repair or withdrawal choice. |
| Invalidated | Stop when the target or conflict is no longer coherent. No fictional victory, duplicated refund or indefinite busy-factory effect. |
| Rebound | A negotiated successor can assume an existing obligation only through explicit acceptance. A carrier change alone does not transfer every old promise. |

Repeated local repair is allowed only for new actual damage. Repeated defense is allowed only for a new threat or renewed agreement. Repeating a mission against an unchanged solved problem cannot generate Unity, influence removal, equipment, cores, achievements or Chaos reduction.

## Signature missions for secondary societies

Each of the 34 secondary arrival packages has a distinct signature mission with its own proof, success and failure in its country document. Those records are mirrored in `registries/068_zin_signature_missions.json`. They supplement the shared provisioning mission. They do not all run simultaneously with every possible diplomatic task.

A failed signature mission should alter the society's next choice. Clan defense failures can strengthen central control or force a new agreement. Failed trade and supply obligations can end neutrality or require withdrawal. Failed protection does not automatically turn peaceful elves into a conquering faction. The specific country route owns the consequence.

## Interface proposal

Most countries use ordinary decision categories with one source-linked category picture and the active mission list. The full colonial directory belongs in the event's own optional ledger, not a replacement for diplomacy, Event Details or the settings window.

Rush warrants a compact decision-category panel. It shows the two public meters, five recognized dominion cards, the current court phase and the next actual deadline. A dominion card shows its name, charter state, current pledge and one relevant action. Selecting a card filters ordinary decisions instead of opening a second large interface. The assassination method and unexposed agent identity remain hidden.

The proposed colonial ledger has three views: known colonies, current agreements and the active Reckoning alignment. Each row shows the known public country, homeland, current posture and actual diplomatic relationship. Unknown loyalties and hidden court operations do not appear as developer flags. Clicking a row can open diplomacy or the related ordinary decision category.

Yeldenne uses a compact custody entry showing the castle state, current controller and the source of Glo's current national effect. It does not need a separate artifact shop, attunement meter or transfer inventory. Castle selection must point to the actual province anchor while the tooltip explains that the state controller receives the power.

Every custom interface remains event-owned. Shared settings, the Event Log and Event Details are parent-owned integration surfaces. The UI worker receives only the bounded event-owned layout. Production must inspect and render actual GUI hierarchies, hitboxes, blocked and active states, localization expansion and supported resolutions. This document is a layout brief, not a claim that a working GUI exists.

## AI action ordering

The AI first prevents the imminent loss of its capital or essential supply, then performs an accepted obligation, then advances its chosen political route. Optional expansion comes after those commitments. It does not spend all equipment on diplomacy while its own formations cannot reinforce.

Peaceful societies evaluate defensive actions from actual threat and reachability. Raiders require an accessible port and a legal active target. Heavy creatures need a supplied deployment area before accepting distant contracts. Afrit values leverage but must pay for it. Volgan can pursue conquest, yet still needs actual crossing and command capacity.

The probability auditor must inspect the real implemented option pools, MTTH entries and decision weights before evaluating outcomes. The action labels and priority descriptions here are not measured probabilities. The acceptance document defines the ordering and failure cases to test.
