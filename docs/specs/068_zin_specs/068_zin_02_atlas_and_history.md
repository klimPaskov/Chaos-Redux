# 068 ZIN: atlas, history and identity

## How to read this atlas

The thirteen unchanged images are the primary visual sources. The supplied brief defines the character and political canon. The historical paragraphs marked as proposals add playable reasons for crossing and remembered conflicts. They are invented setting development, not transcriptions from the maps. No new date, ruler name, species assignment or border should be described as proven by a handwritten geographical label.

The First Men called the Ainu are fictional ZIN people in this project. Their name does not authorize an ethnographic connection with the real Ainu. The lineage Aen, Ein and Einendil and the duration of Einendil's freezing come from the brief. The location, mechanism and person responsible for that freezing remain unresolved.

Preserve original filenames. Use the recorded sheet-title mapping for homeland selection. Do not combine the thirteen images into a supposed exact world projection without further source geometry. The scans have different layouts, scales, joins and cropped edges.

## Tornado Islands

Original: `sources/maps/Triton's Islands(3).png`. Stable map key: `tornado_islands`.

**Readable geographical anchors.** Tornado Islands, Adel, Zuman Uplands, Hohen, Amarelo, Windy Seas, Poolho Cold Lands and a large eastern island used as Rafby in the brief.

**Brief-defined identity.** The filename says Triton's Islands, but the sheet title says Tornado Islands. The brief assigns Rafby corsairs and Adel riders to this region.

**Historical expansion.** Proposed histories concern seasonal sea routes, island rivalry, mounted coastal intervention and mercenary fleets. Rafby and Adel have independent origins and cannot receive a random homeland image from another continent.

**Regional material and uncertainty.** Rafby, Santo or Santon, Nondor or Nondondor, Bennan and Poolho require careful spelling review. The source map remains authoritative evidence even when the working country label is inherited from the brief.

**Arrival packages.** Rafby Corsairs, Adel Wind Riders.

## Histories that arrive with the colony

Each arrival report uses three layers. First explain what Earth observers can establish, such as a new border, an unfamiliar army and a declared government. Then present the settlers' own account of their homeland as attributed testimony. Finally give one current argument that changes play. For example, an island house may dispute royal tribute while asking an Earth port for supplies.

A homeland history must identify at least one place, one institution, one remembered conflict and one reason to cross. The country package already supplies the proposed conflict and starting problem. The writer should develop those specifics without adding an unrelated pantheon, global age chronology or another supreme dark power.

Different colonies from the same society can remember the same dispute differently. Their shared origin permits recognition and diplomatic contact. It does not prove that they share a current ruler or that one is entitled to annex the other.

## What remains unfixed

The brief does not place the Worshipdom or Castle of Yeldenne on a specific supplied sheet. Afrit's early Rush activity uses Rush evidence when that is the actual subject, but his homeland arrival image cannot be assigned by guesswork. Yeldenne likewise needs a confirmed homeland reference or a separately approved non-map arrival exception. These two source-image gaps are recorded in the asset plan.

The personal names of most secondary rulers and the exact non-royal title of Einendil are left to the later naming and localisation pass. Leader biographies and visual directions are provided. Those directions must not be presented as pre-existing user canon.
