# 068 ZIN: the Rush succession

## The king dies and the war begins

The Golden King's death is a required middle stage. Afrit caused it, but the public report presents only what the current evidence supports. Officials announce the death, the court seals the treasury and records, dominions question the next command orders, and competing forces claim to protect the kingdom.

A real war begins between at least two territorial camps. The former kingdom's player is offered a political allegiance, not a generic rebel tag with a blank tree. The choices describe the Court, dominion opposition, and an open Afrit claimant when eligible. A popular opposition can later accept Einendil. The normal opening does not reveal him by name before the death.

The crisis preserves existing Earth wars and defenses. A player is warned when choosing a camp will leave an external front weak. The succession does not grant blanket peace with every external enemy. Temporary internal military access can be removed as war begins, while existing civilian evacuation commitments remain recorded.

## Building the territorial war

The minimum normal configuration has the royal capital and a real dominion outpost. With additional outposts, the builder can create three or four independent claimants. A camp needs an owned state, a capital, units, a leader or provisional governing institution, and its limited crisis tree before it becomes an independent belligerent.

When there are fewer states than eligible claimants, compatible claimants share a political coalition with named leadership, unit contingents, and a later constitutional choice. They are not fabricated as countries without territory. The Free Dominions and the popular host can initially share a camp. A weak Golden Court can join an anti-Afrit coalition while retaining its regency option. Afrit never shares a good coalition and is never automatically absorbed by Volgan.

The builder cannot seize an unrelated Earth state at the exact moment of death solely to create a fourth faction. The earlier outpost system supplies real territorial roots. A fully independent four-way war is available in a developed campaign and in a valid manual Empty Throne setup. A two-sided war remains the minimum valid implementation of the mandatory succession.

### Control of the capital

The Court normally begins in the royal capital. At Influence 75 or above, with the treasury and military-supply footholds both compromised, Afrit's camp can begin there instead. The displaced Court requires a surviving loyal state to stand independently. Otherwise its supporters join a named anti-Afrit coalition.

A capital seizure changes the opening position, not the winner. Afrit does not inherit every dominion or all units through one transfer effect. The loyalty assignment runs separately, and committed Selen, Hiloron, Kilinti, and Huhun forces follow their actual charters and leaders.

## Assigning forces

Unit identity survives the split. A veteran formation keeps its experience, equipment, losses, and origin. A state transfer cannot issue the same starting army to every claimant. Named heroes, unique giant hosts, and contracted forces require an explicit single owner before and after the split.

The following table gives proposed target shares for ordinary Rush formations. It does not override specialist charters or promise an exact share when a small army cannot be divided evenly. Allocate whole formations by stable identity, using largest-remainder allocation and a deterministic tie break.

| Political condition, checked in this order | Court | Dominion or popular coalition | Afrit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Influence at least 75, with two required footholds | 20 percent | 20 percent | 60 percent |
| Unity at least 75 and Influence below 25 | 25 percent | 75 percent | 0 percent |
| Influence at least 50 and Unity below 50 | 40 percent | 30 percent | 30 percent |
| Influence at least 50 with stronger Unity | 40 percent | 40 percent | 20 percent |
| All other valid cases | 50 percent | 50 percent | 0 percent |

An independent camp receives a minimum viable defense from its actual local militia and assigned formations, not an unlimited free spawn. If the assignment would leave a claimant with no meaningful military force, it becomes a coalition tendency instead of an empty country. The player's selected viable camp is identified before final allocation, but selection does not secretly award it a stronger army.

Hiloron giants follow their charter and the treatment of their losses. Kilinti ents defend the protected forest agreement. Selen normally opposes Afrit. Huhun follows its central-service or route-defense obligations. Island houses divide by their recorded commercial and naval commitments. A rare allied orc, goblin, troll, or ogre clan participates only when an earlier explicit exception admitted it.

Earth-recruited formations follow citizenship and local-defense commitments. They are not treated as a species-neutral reserve that can be converted into demons. Mercenaries follow the paid contract until its withdrawal terms apply.

## Four claimant packages

| Camp | Political purpose | Initial problem | Crisis tree content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Golden Court | Preserve the kingdom through a regency after the king's death. | Bought officers are useful, but the court's records and finances are compromised. | Secure the treasury, choose a regent, recover loyal units, bargain with dominions, and decide whether to expose Afrit. |
| Free Dominions | Replace central royal control with a negotiated federation. | The members agree to oppose domination but dispute military command and contributions. | Assemble delegates, allocate commands, fulfill a defense pledge, adopt a charter, and decide the crown's future. |
| Afrit's claimant | Capture Rush and combine it with the Worshipdom. | Influence can seize institutions faster than territory, but uncorrupted dominions resist. | Seize the capital, recruit compromised officers, compel oaths, defeat rival claimants, and form the combined crown. |
| Popular host | Build a lawful Rush government around Einendil after his acceptance. | The leader's identity is disputed and the host must prove that its protection extends beyond its own soldiers. | Protect civilians, verify testimony, obtain dominion recognition, defeat the usurpation, and convene a public settlement. |

Each independent claimant needs three practical crisis focuses and at least two incompatible political continuations. A coalition tendency retains its own commitments and can become the postwar governing route. No claimant receives a generic national tree as its completed content.

## Einendil's appearance

After the king's death, reports describe an unknown soldier surviving encounters that should have killed an ordinary warrior. The report chain is anchored to the current succession front and the troops who encountered him. Story reports of skirmishes are fictional narrative events, not a claim that the engine has produced a verified individual combat statistic.

He identifies himself as **Einendil, son of Ein, son of Aen, the Ainu** and says that he was frozen for more than one hundred thousand years. Modern Rush scholars consider the line extinct. The first report does not resolve the place or cause of the freezing. Those remain mysteries unless the user later supplies the answer.

The event appears once, normally between 15 and 45 days after the death. A developed battlefield situation can accelerate it. A prolonged quiet front does not permanently remove the route. The soldier offers service and warns of the coming dark powers. He does not demand the throne.

The player can dismiss him. Dismissal permanently closes the Einendil leadership route for that campaign. It cannot be reversed by reloading a focus tree, changing a cosmetic tag, forming a federation, or using another Rush colony. A later flavor report may mention his absence, but must not quietly hand him back.

Acceptance admits him to the existing popular or dominion camp and creates his unique commander and personal guard. It does not crown him. If the campaign has enough territory and an actual political reason for a separate popular host, that camp can form its own country through the crisis builder. Otherwise he serves within the existing coalition until its postwar settlement.

### Preserving the good route

The proposed leadership requirements are acceptance of his service, no committed support for Afrit's claim, an active civilian-protection charter, and no deliberate destruction of a friendly recognized dominion. Rush Unity must reach at least 50 and Afrit's Influence must be reduced to 50 or below before the postwar popular settlement.

These numerical conditions need not all be met when he first appears. The civil war provides a finite opportunity to protect a community, remove a proven court network, and secure a dominion agreement. The player can repair poor preparation. Irreversible atrocities, deliberate betrayal of a protected dominion, or dismissal of Einendil are not erased by spending political power.

The Golden King's ordinary neglect does not by itself disqualify the player. The route judges the player's explicit commitments and later conduct. A good outcome remains possible in the kingdom that the user described as badly ruled.

### Testing the claim

The identity investigation has three evidence families: an old language or legal formula, recognition by an ancient creature or forest tradition, and a surviving relic or record. The full chain can support the claim without inventing the unresolved freezing location.

A scholar can initially reject the lineage while still supporting the soldier's service. An ancient being can recognize a sign without explaining his entire past. The player can protect the evidence or suppress it for court politics. These choices affect public confidence and the postwar settlement, not his actual ancestry, which is fixed by the supplied canon.

## Einendil in battle

Einendil is the most powerful individual warrior in Event 068. He is not the largest army. His personal guard is one unique formation, and his commander role must share the same character identity as his later country-leader role.

The proposed guard can defeat an ordinary line formation in a direct prepared engagement and contribute heavily against an elite creature host. It cannot hold every province, repair every supply route, or occupy a continent alone. Its extraordinary power is constrained by a single location, limited guard replacement, and the risk of isolation.

The player can assign him to one active front mission at a time. A 30-day intervention can reinforce a threatened allied state, protect an evacuation, or challenge a dark host. The mission costs 25 command power and commits his actual guard. It grants a specific operational effect at that front, not a global army bonus while he is absent.

Loss of the guard triggers a withdrawal and recovery state if a valid friendly route remains. Recovery takes 180 days and replaces only the allowed guard establishment. It does not create another commander. If the political camp has been destroyed and no lawful recovery route exists, the hero is unavailable pending the defeat outcome. The design must not promise automatic immortality in every encirclement.

## Ending the succession

A claimant wins when it controls the required Rush political centers and every rival claimant is defeated, has accepted a lawful settlement, or has joined the winning coalition through a recorded agreement. Capturing one capital while a powerful rival still controls several dominions is not victory.

The proposed minimum proof is the royal capital, a majority of the admitted territorial dominions, and 90 days without an independent rival claimant capable of sustained war. The final 90-day consolidation can be shortened by a complete negotiated settlement, but not by a hidden capitulation shortcut. External Earth wars can continue.

Defeated good camps can be integrated through a constitution or protected autonomy. Afrit's claimant cannot receive a harmless ceremonial role inside a good government. Its network must be dismantled, expelled, or remain an identified unresolved threat. A victorious Afrit can offer submission to ordinary court and dominion institutions, but cannot be treated as a generic regent.

## Route A: Einendil's leadership

The postwar assembly chooses Einendil to lead. The final non-royal office title is a localisation and canon decision. The specification requires popular selection, a public obligation to protect the polity, and refusal of a hereditary crown. No coronation portrait, royal succession law, or focus reward may contradict that identity.

The route has five connected late branches.

| Branch | Required proof | Consequence and continuing play |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A-Evidence | Surviving court records, one exposed intermediary, and a completed investigation. | Publicly establishes Afrit's responsibility for the Golden King's death and weakens his claims over Rush institutions. |
| A-Dominions | A fulfilled charter for each participating recognized people. | Restores their military contributions without erasing autonomy or past harm. |
| A-EarthPeace | A lawful agreement with a former owner or a completed civilian-protection mission. | Opens mutual recognition, release or restoration of agreed states, and Earth membership in the defensive alliance. |
| A-Containment | A real dark crossing, captive population, or hostile command site has been secured. | Removes that specific threat and applies one-time event-owned Chaos reduction. |
| A-LostCause | The founding alliance or its surviving successor recognizes Rush leadership. | Gives Einendil strategic leadership while retaining member autonomy and obligations. |

The route can reduce Chaos by rescuing real civilian cohorts, dismantling a proven hostile crossing, settling a persistent inter-colony war, or defeating a dark ruler's Earth command. Ordinary peace, liberation, and population effects already handled by shared systems are not counted twice. No repeatable political-power decision directly buys global calm.

Victory over Volgan and Afrit produces a settlement that protects surviving Earth states. It can dismantle hostile regimes and remove military institutions responsible for the war. It does not require the conquest of peaceful neutral Earth countries merely to complete an achievement or world-end checklist.

## Route B: the Crown of Afrit

Afrit exploits the empty throne and becomes King of Rush when his claimant wins. He remains the ruler of the Worshipdom in the political fiction, but his unique character must have only one active runtime owner.

If the Worshipdom already exists, the standard single-player route forms one combined sovereign country using the victorious Rush carrier and the Worshipdom's actual territory, forces, institutions, and obligations. The Worshipdom survives as a constituent realm, not a second copy of Afrit. If a separate human player controls it, a governed subject arrangement can retain that player's country under an appointed governor until the players accept merger. The player-controlled governor is not another Afrit.

If Afrit first appears physically through a low-Chaos Rush usurpation, record his unique arrival there. A later E1 Worshipdom arrival can establish its constituent territory and servants, but cannot create another Afrit or reset his army and influence. This is an explicit story route to his person, distinct from ordinary E1 society selection.

The Ashen Throne receives the Rush army and economy that actually survived the civil war. Its advantage is strongest when Afrit preserved rather than destroyed the institutions he captured. The proposed successful-usurpation target is a 25 to 50 percent military advantage over Volgan's ordinary initial expedition in a developed campaign, measured through the surviving armies and reinforcement access. It is a tuning target, not a free national attack multiplier or a guaranteed victory.

Afrit then addresses each dominion separately. Submission grants access to that dominion's ordinary unit family under the new command. Resistance can lead to an invasion, a targeted corruption campaign, or exile. Selen does not automatically become corrupted because the capital changed hands. Kilinti does not produce bound ents without a completed domination sequence.

Orcs, goblins, trolls, and ogres remain outside the inherited Rush settlement. Afrit must bargain with, corrupt, intimidate, or conquer their independent societies. A success adds a particular host with its own terms. It does not unlock an unlimited universal monster recruit pool.

Late branches cover the combined crown, the worship institutions, corrupted specialists, dragon binding, domination of independent monster rulers, and preparation for war with both Volgan and The Lost Cause. Each acquisition should create an obligation or rivalry that survives the focus reward.

## Route C: the Golden Regency

The regency keeps Rush together without Einendil or Afrit as ruler. Its central choice is whether power rests with a restrained civil regency or a military regency. A treasury oligarchy is available when the court hoard and purchased offices survived, but it faces the strongest dominion distrust.

The civil regency preserves the monarchy as an institution while ending the sale of offices and signing a constitutional settlement. The military regency rebuilds a disciplined army and accepts fewer limits on emergency powers. The treasury route supports industry and mercenary contracts while risking a renewed political crisis if it breaks its reconstruction pledges.

This route can remain leader of The Lost Cause and can defeat both dark powers. It does not receive Einendil's personal battlefield intervention or his unique peace-settlement tools. It must rely on institutions, the dominions, Earth agreements, and ordinary military strength.

A regency that suppresses the murder investigation retains an unresolved Afrit network and a later blackmail risk. A regency that exposes him loses compromised officers and short-term supply support. The evidence choice therefore matters after the civil war has ended.

## Route D: the Free Dominions

The Free Dominions replace the crown with a federal settlement. Their first problem is a real distribution of defense, supply, and decision-making among scattered Earth enclaves. The federation cannot be a cosmetic rename that gives every ally free cores.

The player chooses a common military command with constitutional limits or a looser defensive league. Common command permits shared expedition planning and larger specialist deployments, but requires delivered contributions and a clear rule for ending emergency authority. The loose league preserves more independence and has stronger local defense, but slower combined offensives.

Selen can lead its civil institutions, Huhun its rapid defense network, Hiloron reconstruction, Kilinti protected territory, and the island houses overseas supply. The leading member depends on actual survival and commitments. One faction founder does not erase the rest of the society packages.

A federation formed from several sovereign countries retains those countries unless they ratify a merger. A merger counts the actual people and equipment once and preserves local charter rights through state or institution data. An autonomous league does not receive a single blanket core over every member's territory.

The route has continuing neutral and Lost Cause options. A neutral league can defend its members and shelter refugees without entering every global war. A Lost Cause league can fight the dark powers under a council mandate. It cannot become secretly subordinate to Afrit while still displaying the same completed free charter.

## What happens to The Lost Cause

The Lost Cause survives the king's death as a faction relationship, not as an ownership flag on his character. Good or non-Afrit Rush winners can retain leadership when the members accept their settlement.

If Afrit captures Rush, Rush and submitted dominions leave the faction for The Ashen Throne. Before that departure invalidates the leader, the faction selects its strongest surviving eligible good member. Eligible candidates are Sunhot, Horos and Yeldenne, the Magical Forest elves, and a good Rush splinter. Strength considers fielded forces, sustainable supply, controlled industry, and willingness to defend members. Glo is an advantage, not an automatic leadership appointment.

Ties use prior delivered defense obligations, then founding membership seniority. A destroyed or capitulated shell cannot become leader because it has a famous name. If no eligible member survives, the alliance is defeated. A later good coalition can be formed through its own route, but the system must not resurrect a dead founder for convenience.

## Multiplayer and repeated crises

Only the canonical Rush Kingdom owns this assassination and succession. Secondary Rush colonies respond politically and can host exiles, but do not start their own copies of the Golden King's death.

A player may choose a viable unoccupied claimant country. An existing human player's country cannot be reassigned, merged, or stripped of its interface through a hidden event. Where two players support the same constitutional route, their countries can cooperate and resolve a later merger explicitly. Choosing a camp cannot duplicate a unique leader or award starting forces again.

Manual Empty Throne uses the same claimant packages and ownership rules. It can preconfigure the political balance toward one route, but it does not bypass territorial validity, duplicate Afrit, or create a second Einendil after he was dismissed or defeated in the same active story.
