# 068 ZIN: the other central baseline societies

## The Elves of the Magical Forest

The Magical Forest elves begin as a friendly democratic society. Their country is an elected sanctuary with an army, a population, and competing practical priorities. A normal political branch must not turn them into generic conquerors merely to create a second route. Afrit can corrupt individuals and institutions through an explicit hostile chain, but corruption is a distinct crisis with resistance and consequences.

The founding leader is a proposed elected envoy responsible to a forest assembly. The character's personal name and exact appearance remain production decisions. The biography should explain a concrete responsibility, such as organizing the crossing while preserving the right of Earth residents to remain in their homes. It must not claim that this biography was written on the map.

### Political architecture

| Internal workstream | Main decisions | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E-Citizenship | Preserve separate communities or extend common citizenship after language and civil-law work. | Different recruitment and integration patterns, with protection of residents in both normal routes. |
| E-Forest | Establish a protected sanctuary or permit tightly bounded civil construction. | A choice between stronger habitat defense and a more capable mixed civilian economy. |
| E-Assembly | Keep broad approval for foreign deployment or delegate emergency defense authority. | Slower but trusted interventions, or faster responses with a later public review. |
| E-Earth | Seek recognition, exchange defensive knowledge, and negotiate the former owner's claims. | A stable border and real opportunities for joint defense or reconstruction. |
| E-Military | Rangers, mountain service, disciplined elven line troops, and eventually Dragon Mountain traditions. | Small high-quality forces whose replacement depends on the elven population and institutions. |
| E-LostCause | Support the early council, assess Rush's conduct, and join a lawful defensive alliance. | A clear good-aligned late campaign with a leadership option if Afrit takes Rush. |
| E-Containment | Investigate corruption, protect ancient sites, and close a proven hostile crossing. | Removal of a real threat and a limited event-owned Chaos reduction. |

The sanctuary and common-citizenship routes are compatible when the player pays for their institutions. A strict isolation policy and a permanent foreign peacekeeping mandate are incompatible. The tree should expose the practical conflict before the commitment focus, not hide it behind a final reward.

The early forest decision chooses one protected zone in the actual founding state. It cannot assign every forest on Earth to the elves. A 180-day sanctuary mission requires a lawful resident policy, real supply, and a completed housing or protected-construction project. Success creates a stable habitat and allows one additional elven replacement tranche. Failure opens a repair path and postpones foreign deployments. It does not make the country hostile.

### Earth residents and democratic authority

Common citizenship opens human volunteer formations, local administration, and deeper industrial participation. It does not create human recruits for elven battalions. Separate communities retain distinct legal arrangements and lower shared recruitment, but still prohibit arbitrary expulsion in the normal good route.

Emergency military delegation lasts for a named war or protection mission. The assembly reviews the deployment when that commitment ends. A successful review can retain a limited rapid-response authority. A failed review removes that permission until the player resolves the disputed civilian harm, broken pledge, or missing force. This is an institutional state, not a second public approval meter.

If a friendly Earth government is attacked, the elves can provide a finite escort or defense force before joining a full faction. They cannot send their entire army to an unreachable front while their own state is under attack. AI uses the same deployment and home-defense requirements as the player.

### Dragon Mountain

Dragon access is a late military institution, not a routine infantry technology. The route requires an intact elven polity, a completed sanctuary, a secure crossing, and the Dragon Mountain agreement. The elves then undertake a 180-day bonding and preparation mission, commit 100 political power and 500 support equipment, and receive one finite dragon-host allotment.

A dragon host is a planned land-combat creature formation with its own handlers and supply establishment. Its model can use real wing and attack animation, but the design does not pretend that a land formation is an air wing or can teleport across the map. A later dedicated air-domain implementation would be a separate accepted expansion with engine proof for creature counts and wing behavior. The current package preserves strong dragon battlefield units without making an unverified air-system promise.

Dragon losses require a long replacement interval and renewed preparation. Human manpower, captured rifles, or annexation cannot produce another dragon. The elf country can share a dragon host only through an explicit service agreement. The same creatures cannot remain in the original country's stockpile while also appearing in an allied army.

### Corruption and recovery

Afrit can target an identified official, a military supply agreement, or a threatened sanctuary. A bargain must offer something concrete, such as relief from a real siege or a replacement shipment. The player sees the obligation before accepting. A generic high-Chaos tick cannot silently convert a friendly democracy into a hostile demon state.

A compromised institution can be exposed through surviving evidence or a protected witness. Recovery removes the associated benefit and can require a 180-day institutional repair. A fully captured regime becomes an Afrit-aligned political package with distinct leader art and military access. The surviving democratic opposition can retain a state or exile institution if the territorial builder can support it. It does not receive another founding army.

### Neutral and victory continuations

The elves can remain an independent sanctuary that guarantees specific communities, receives refugees through the shared Migration system, and undertakes limited containment. They do not need to conquer a continent to remain relevant.

In The Lost Cause, they contribute elite infantry, Rangers, a possible dragon host, and the institutional capacity to protect a postwar settlement. If Rush falls to Afrit, they can lead the faction when they are the strongest willing good member. Their victory does not require naming them rulers of every Earth state.

## Noris Desert Warriors

Noris is a society built around water, routes, mounted movement, and military service. Its three principal routes produce different states: a caravan polity, a desert protector, or a conquest state. All begin with the same neutral arrival and the same need to sustain a large mounted force.

The proposed leader is a caravan commander who organized the crossing after a dispute over military requisitions. The homeland references are the Noris Desert, its neighboring southern regions, and remembered routes. This is fictional history proposed for the mod, not a claim about a real-world desert culture.

### The route spine

| Internal workstream | Sequence | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N-Wells | Survey the founding supply routes, secure actual transport, and agree on civilian access. | Creates the basis for either trade or war without a new water currency. |
| N-Caravan | Publish contract rules, negotiate several non-exclusive partners, and establish protected passage. | A neutral commercial state with escorts, repair projects, and diplomatic leverage. |
| N-Guardians | Defend one threatened desert community, build a joint frontier network, and accept a limited defense mandate. | A good or neutral protector route with a possible Lost Cause alignment. |
| N-Conquest | Centralize mounted command, prepare a supplied target, and choose tribute or direct occupation. | Regional expansion after activation, with real garrison and resident-policy consequences. |
| N-Military | Develop camel formations, mobile line troops, scouts, remount services, and eligible mercenary auxiliaries. | Strong desert performance and different deployment costs outside the climate. |
| N-Succession | Resolve a dispute between caravan delegates and military chiefs after a major failed pledge or an ambitious expansion. | A constitutional or military leadership change that affects the existing route. |

The tree should not give each political route the same factories and a different flag. The caravan route produces access agreements, escorts, and reliable repair capacity. Guardianship produces obligations and limited foreign intervention. Conquest produces territory and military authority, but weakens the trust needed for neutral transit.

### Water and supply without another meter

Noris uses real supply, infrastructure, equipment, and civilian construction commitments. A well or caravan route is represented by a named state project and its supply effect. The event does not create a public water counter that duplicates the supply map.

The first 180-day mission secures the founding route and completes a civil distribution agreement. It requires actual supply to the capital and a finished construction or repair commitment. Success permits another five mounted formations from the admitted human pool. Failure reduces mounted replacement throughput until the route is repaired. It does not make camels immune to fuel, supply, or equipment requirements that their template actually uses.

The player can choose which of two threatened partners receives an escort. That commits existing riders for 90 days and costs 25 command power plus 100 support equipment. The second partner sees that the force is unavailable. A mission cannot claim successful protection because the country merely owned enough cavalry somewhere else.

### Contracts and foreign service

A contract identifies the client, the named front or protected state, the committed force, payment, duration, and withdrawal terms. The payment can be equipment, a construction commitment, or political recognition. A flat monthly political-power payment without a real service obligation is insufficient.

Noris can serve a dark power, but the relationship must state what it gives up. An Afrit contract can introduce an intermediary and a later demand for permanent service. Volgan may offer protection while expecting obedience to an ancient command. The independent route can reject both and retain a network of smaller clients.

A contract against a current guaranteed partner is incompatible with a lawful caravan charter. The player can break the charter deliberately, but the prior protection and trust do not remain active. The AI evaluates the same conflict and does not accept every available payment.

### Conquest

Regional conquest requires a real supplied land route or a lawful maritime transport plan. Noris does not acquire all desert states as claims at once. It prepares a target corridor whose states form a coherent campaign. After victory, it chooses direct administration, a tributary relationship, or a negotiated withdrawal.

Direct administration has the largest economic potential and the greatest burden of housing, garrisons, and integration. Tribute preserves more local authority and makes revenue depend on continued compliance. Withdrawal can complete a military-service contract or settle the original provocation without building an empire.

Desert goblins or ogres can appear as independent clients or mercenary hosts. They are not automatically part of every Noris army. A recruitment agreement uses their own population and replacement package and remains distinct from the ordinary human mounted army.

### Later alignments

Guardianship can support The Lost Cause through actual protection of civilians and opposition to dark domination. The caravan route can remain neutral or join a defensive alliance after attacks. The conquest route can become independent, Afrit-aligned, or Volgan-aligned depending on its contracts and political choices.

No alignment removes the geographical problem. A desert army sent to a wet, cold, distant front still needs adaptation and supply. A well-run Noris state can solve that problem through logistics and allies, but it does not receive a global terrain exemption.

## Dondor Brutes

Dondor's people are humans. Their military culture values strength, discipline, personal command, and the ability to survive harsh service. Their units can be exceptionally powerful human shock troops without being mislabeled as ogres or demons.

The proposed starting ruler is a war-captain supported by senior company commanders. A military hierarchy is the opening institution. The later tree decides whether that hierarchy becomes a regulated service state, a personal conquest regime, or a subordinate army of a dark ruler.

### Political and military branches

| Internal workstream | Main choice | Lasting consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D-Command | Formal officer law or personal challenges backed by a ruler. | Predictable military administration or stronger personal authority with succession risks. |
| D-Settlement | Regulated requisition, separate civilian administration, or coercive extraction. | Different recognition, workforce, and garrison outcomes. |
| D-Service | Limited mercenary contracts or permanent patron service. | Independence with smaller guarantees, or stronger support and obligations. |
| D-Expansion | A defended frontier, a tributary zone, or direct regional conquest. | Different burdens and diplomatic responses after activation. |
| D-Training | Heavy line troops, shock formations, siege cooperation, and adaptation to Earth equipment. | A distinct human military rather than a generic monster roster. |
| D-Patron | Volgan, Afrit, or rejection of both. | A real third-party choice with internal officer consequences. |

Dondor's challenge events must concern command and law, not repeatable fights for free stats. A chief who has completed a difficult service contract can demand an office or more autonomy. A chief whose force was abandoned can challenge the ruler. The outcome changes the officer roster, a limited force, or the governing structure.

### The service state

A regulated service route establishes written terms for foreign military work, limits the ruler's private claims on equipment, and creates a civilian administration able to keep workshops functioning. It can remain harsh and non-democratic without being automatically hostile to every neighbor.

Its first major mission is a 180-day contract or defense obligation. The player chooses a reachable client, commits existing shock troops, and provides the agreed equipment. Success funds real workshop development and preserves the command structure. Failure can remove an officer's support and leave a damaged force that must be rebuilt.

Neutral service remains a complete late route. Dondor can sell protection to Earth countries, defend a frontier against a dark colony, or coordinate with other independent military societies. It does not need to join a universal good faction to have a reason to survive.

### Personal conquest

The conquest route centralizes command and opens a deliberate external policy. It requires a viable target, supply, and sufficient forces left to hold the home state. Its early objectives are neighboring territory and regional control, not immediate world conquest.

A victory produces a choice between direct rule and a tribute agreement. Direct rule requires settlement and garrison work. Tribute requires continued military credibility and a lawful agreement. Neither creates instant cores across an entire region.

Requisition can support a short campaign, but it damages workforce cooperation and recognition. Forced movement of residents uses the shared Migration system and retains responsibility for harm. A Dondor focus cannot erase a population and replace it with brute soldiers.

### Choosing a dark patron

Volgan offers the strongest initial military protection. His price is command obedience and service against his enemies. Dondor remains an external Dark Lands client, not a region that was always part of the Dead Lands. Its accession is a real event with terms and a loss of independence.

Afrit offers a more political relationship. He can support selected captains, finance a campaign, or promise a privileged role in a captured Rush kingdom. The immediate support may be smaller, but a successful usurpation changes the strategic calculation. Dondor can become a major Ashen ally without being inherited as a Rush dominion.

The ruler can reject both patrons. This opens a difficult independent route with limited anti-demon cooperation and a stronger need for Earth supplies. Dondor's normal late choices are independent rule, service to Volgan, or service to Afrit. A good military agreement can remain limited and does not automatically rewrite the country as a friendly democracy.

### Army and AI

The army uses heavy human infantry, shock formations, and practical equipment adaptation. Captured or imported Earth weapons can improve its reach, but the country must research and produce or receive the actual equipment. Brute strength does not mean immunity to supply failure, air attack, or encirclement.

AI chooses service when the state lacks a secure industrial base, conquest when it has a reachable target and sustainable army, and patronage when a real threat makes independence dangerous. It considers delivered promises, not only nominal faction strength. A patron that repeatedly sacrifices Dondor forces creates a route toward refusal or revolt.

## Shared completion requirements for these three core trees

Each core tree needs a substantial domestic spine, at least three political or external-policy continuations, a distinct military branch, a complete neutral or independent route, and decisions that continue to matter after the final focus. Final node counts follow the approved branch map and should not be inflated by repeated flat rewards.

The implementation must provide an explicit route graph, dependency checks, AI plans, idea replacement rules, a target-state contract for each mission, and an asset crosswalk. A tree with working buttons but no country-specific political consequences is not the completed design.
