# 068 ZIN: greater powers and unique rulers

## Afrit and the Worshipdom

Afrit begins with much less direct military power than Volgan. His advantage is the ability to acquire institutions and armies that belonged to other societies. The Worshipdom's opening should therefore make political capture useful and direct military overreach dangerous.

The initial proposed army is five human-servant formations and five demon formations. The country has three research slots, a target usable economy of three civilian and three military factories, and a supporting civilian settlement of 25,000. Its government is a distinct worship-based absolutism within the non-aligned engine category. That engine category does not make it politically neutral once its expansion route is active.

The Worshipdom's precise location on the supplied maps is not established. Its arrival news needs a verified homeland choice or a clearly framed non-map arrival image until the user fixes that geography. It must not randomly use a dark-looking map and claim it depicts Afrit's capital.

### The institutions route

| Internal workstream | Content | Strategic result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A-Worshipdom | Secure the settlement, organize human servants, establish a defensible command, and choose the balance between direct rule and delegated cult institutions. | A viable small state able to sustain influence work. |
| A-Bargains | Offer relief, equipment, protection, or political support to a specific institution. | Creates a real obligation and a path toward deeper access. |
| A-Rush | Enter the Golden Court, preserve the hidden plot, support a claimant, and seize the crown when the succession permits it. | The main route to surpassing Volgan. |
| A-OtherCourts | Target an eligible independent colony or Earth government through a finite sequence. | Acquires external power without a universal instant puppet button. |
| A-Hosts | Recruit demons, corrupt specific elves or giants, and bind eligible dragon formations. | Expands the military through actual agreements and captured institutions. |
| A-AshenThrone | Build the independent third faction and prepare war against both rivals. | A durable strategic bloc whose identity is not absorbed by Volgan. |

Afrit can manage at most two active external institutional operations at once, plus the canonical Rush plot. This is an operational limit, not a new public influence currency. Each operation has a named target, a stage, a benefit offered, an obligation accepted, a counteraction, and an expiry or failure result.

### Capturing an institution

The proposed five-stage pattern is an offer, a liaison, an exclusive office or contract, a crisis intervention, and a final sovereignty demand. Each stage requires a new action or a real target decision. An early accepted shipment does not automatically surrender the country later.

An offer costs 50 political power and a real material commitment appropriate to the target. A supply bargain uses 500 infantry equipment or 100 support equipment. A civil rescue bargain commits two civilian factories for 90 days. The target can accept, reject, or request a different lawful arrangement. Rejection does not reveal hidden information about another country.

A liaison provides a specific benefit and creates a vulnerable institution. An exclusive office gives Afrit access to a command, court, or supply service and makes removal costly. The crisis intervention requires an actual war, succession dispute, collapse of a promised project, or exposed political conflict. Afrit can exploit a crisis, but the system cannot invent a coup solely because 90 days passed.

The final demand can produce a subject relationship, an Afrit-aligned government, a negotiated limited contract, or a refusal followed by war. A democratic or strongly independent target has a real resistance path. A player is not forced to accept submission because the AI signed a trade agreement months earlier.

Targets counter the operation by removing an identified intermediary, replacing a dependent supply route, publishing surviving evidence, or obtaining an alternative guarantee. A purge without proof can damage the target's own institutions and does not automatically remove every hidden agent. The player sees the known problem and cost, not an omniscient list of all secret actors.

### The Rush opportunity

The Rush route is owned jointly by Afrit's political operation and the mandatory Rush succession. It never creates a second king-death sequence. Afrit can support the plot even if his sovereign country has not yet arrived. Orders and agents placed before the Earth crossing remain the reason for the king's death.

At high influence, Afrit can take a direct claimant position and seize the royal capital at the start of the war. His army remains the one that actually arrived or was recruited. Corrupted Rush officers bring their existing units. Submitted dominions provide their actual unique forces after their own submission decisions.

The reward for a successful usurpation is the combined political and military structure of Rush and the Worshipdom. It is not an arbitrary national attack bonus labeled as a new empire. The surviving economy, Rangers, giants, ents, riders, island fleets, and acquired institutions make the difference. A destructive civil war can leave Afrit with a crown but fewer useful resources than he expected.

### Creature acquisition

Afrit can acquire corrupted elves, giants, bound ents, demons, and dragons only through their named military routes. Orcs, goblins, trolls, and ogres require separate external agreements or conquest. The acquisition record identifies the society, units, remaining replacement allotment, and continuing obligation.

A corrupted unit family needs a distinct political and visual package. It can have different reinforcement conditions and stronger short-term performance, but its benefits must follow the actual bound institution. Defeating or cleansing that institution can stop future replacements without deleting every already fielded unit in an unexplained instant.

Dragon binding is a 180-day late project costing 100 political power and 500 support equipment after Afrit has obtained the relevant access. Its finite dragon host is extremely powerful and difficult to replace. It remains a unit family, never a dragon country. The project cannot use a captured human recruitment pool as dragon stock.

### Afrit's defeat and exile

Country defeat and permanent defeat of the unique ruler are different states. A defeated Worshipdom can leave Afrit displaced if a supported exile or claimant route exists. His unique identity, prior bargains, and losses remain. A later throne is a new seat for the same actor, not another initial spawn.

A permanent defeat outcome closes his future sovereign and claimant creation. Agents already ordered to kill the Golden King can still carry out the established plot, but they cannot create a living Afrit claimant after his permanent removal. No event resets the unique actor because another eligible state appears.

## Shachihata Demons

Shachihata demons are very large, very powerful, and few. Their opening country is a small host with a settlement, not a mass army of ordinary infantry wearing a demon icon. Five demon formations are the proposed starting military establishment, with their exact physical headcount defined by the unit contract.

The society begins neutral and must establish shelter, supply, and a command agreement. A 180-day provisioning mission is its first major obligation. It requires a secure capital route, actual construction capacity, and the military support equipment needed by its small host. Success permits a finite replacement tranche. Failure reduces foreign deployment and invites a patron bargain, but does not automatically hand the country to Afrit.

### Three viable political routes

The independent route creates a host council and a limited regional policy. It can demand a protected frontier, sell one finite military service contract, or remain a defended demon state. Its army can be intimidating without attacking every neighboring country immediately.

The Afrit route exchanges protection and replacement access for a binding military obligation. Afrit must be a living actor able to offer the promised support. The host's final submission gives The Ashen Throne a real elite force and a political institution that can be contested if the bargain is broken.

The Volgan route accepts service to the stronger ancient power. Shachihata remains an external society joining his command, not a region that the brief says was always inside the Dead Lands. Volgan must provide the specific protection or logistical support promised by the accession.

A fourth late outcome can emerge from a broken patron contract: a revolt with help from an independent or good neighbor. That does not make the society automatically friendly. It creates a defended independence route and a reason to cooperate against a particular former patron.

### Army and society

The host has major shock and siege value. Replacements are slow, and the loss of a formation creates a named political consequence. A commander who repeatedly spends the entire host on distant wars can lose the authority to accept another contract.

The player can commit one elite contingent for 180 days at a cost of 25 command power and 100 support equipment. The agreement identifies a reachable target and the return conditions. A human patron cannot receive another free host by renewing the same contract.

Flavor concerns buildings too small for the newcomers, the civil cost of supporting them, their negotiations with humans, and the debate over patron service. It should describe practical consequences of their size without reducing them to a repeated joke.

## Sunhot Elves

Sunhot elves are over three meters tall, the strongest elves, and among the most peaceful societies in the event. Their military power is intentionally far above ordinary infantry. Their restraint creates the strategic problem: they cannot protect every threatened community with a small host.

The proposed opening army contains five Sunhot formations. The colony has a strong but compact civilian base, three research slots, and finite elite reinforcement. Their normal government is a peaceful council with a democratic engine presentation. The exact constitutional terminology and personal names remain later localisation work.

### The protection tree

| Internal workstream | Main choices | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S-Home | Establish a protected settlement, civil construction, and a secure supply base. | A sustainable peaceful colony. |
| S-Protection | Choose which real threatened population or ally receives the next host. | A limited intervention with a visible opportunity cost. |
| S-Relations | Recognize Earth governments, mediate borders, and work with friendly colonies. | Strong civil cooperation and a route into The Lost Cause. |
| S-Military | Prepare elite infantry, guardian cooperation, and a later dragon or fire-drake tradition. | Rare forces with strong performance and slow replacement. |
| S-DarkWar | Identify a proven dark threat, obtain an intervention mandate, and commit decisive force. | A full good-aligned war route at higher Chaos. |
| S-Leadership | Assume faction leadership after a failed Rush state and define postwar obligations. | A credible Lost Cause successor without a claim to rule Earth as an empire. |

Sunhot's initial restraint does not cancel the common defensive-peace rule. If attacked, it offers the neighboring-state settlement defined by the core system. A later good route can return agreed territory in exchange for enforceable protection and recognition. That is a separate diplomatic settlement, not a hidden alteration of the original demand.

### Choosing where to intervene

At most two Sunhot protection missions can be active at once, and at least one viable home force must remain unless the home state itself is the mission. The player chooses a real threatened state, a civilian evacuation route, or a friendly colony under attack. Each mission commits existing formations and cannot be fulfilled by keeping them elsewhere.

A normal protection mission lasts 180 days and costs 25 command power plus 100 support equipment. Success records a fulfilled protection agreement and can unlock another finite replacement tranche. Failure preserves the actual losses and opens a public review of the abandoned commitment. It does not turn peaceful elves into conquerors through a generic instability event.

A decisive military intervention can begin after a verified dark attack, exposed capture attempt, or a Reckoning mandate. The enemy and objective are named. The intervention ends when the protected objective is secure or a lawful settlement is reached. Sunhot does not keep a permanent war goal against every neutral country because it once defended a border.

### Dragons, drakes, and guardians

Sunhot's late military branch can unlock a rare dragon host or a Great Volcano fire-drake tradition. The player chooses the tradition according to the available homeland relationship and military need. It does not receive every dragon variant at once.

A 180-day preparation project, a secure crossing, 100 political power, and 500 support equipment precede the first allotment. The creature unit has a distinct counter, model, attack presentation, and replacement contract. It does not create an independent Great Volcano or dragon tag.

God Forest Keepers and IHUJ islanders can support the logistics or political agreement, but do not themselves become unrestricted dragon recruiters. The eligible elven military owner remains explicit.

### A peaceful late campaign

Sunhot can remain outside the global factions while protecting a small network of settlements. It can also join The Lost Cause and become one of its strongest military members. If Rush is captured, Sunhot can lead when it has the actual strength and willingness to defend the alliance.

The final good settlement preserves Earth autonomy and limits the victorious military mandate. Sunhot's power makes it capable of imposing peace, but the route must require actual protection and restoration work rather than awarding a world victory for maintaining a high army score.

## The Children of Volgan before their master arrives

A Children colony is a herald command of Volgan's established realm. Its forces already serve him. It does not begin with a three-way ideological lottery. Independent Dark Lands societies have that choice, but Yez, Netherborn, Hell Forest, Demonic Mountain, and Mazerin detachments are already parts of the Dead Lands military structure.

The proposed herald army is five herald formations, five grave-host formations, and five Netherborn formations. The country arrives neutral in the formal opening posture, then prepares its declared hostile policy through its own route. Manual Reckoning setup begins it as an active belligerent explicitly.

### Herald routes

The command has three operational specializations, not three different ultimate loyalties. A fortress foothold builds a defended crossing and holds a strategic position. A gathering host recruits admitted monsters and receives Dead Lands reinforcements. An infiltration command targets a specific vulnerable independent dark society or hostile political institution.

The first 180-day mission secures the founding command and supply route. Success admits a later reinforcement request and improves the viability of Volgan's unique arrival. Failure leaves a weakened herald country that can be conquered or forced to retreat. It does not award a fresh army every month until it succeeds.

A herald can request one five-formation reinforcement tranche after a completed command focus and a 180-day cooldown. The troops come from the established realm. The colony cannot obtain a second Volgan, another Glo, or an independent Edvoid through that request.

### Contact with independent dark countries

Blackton, Goldoroth, the Endless, and other Dark Lands rulers may submit, bargain, resist, or join Afrit. The herald's diplomacy therefore includes a real demand and a counteroffer. Rejection can lead to a declared war after activation. It does not retroactively mark the independent country as always owned by Volgan.

An Earth country defeating a herald can dismantle its command, close its specific crossing, and restore or reorganize the affected state. That liberation does not convert a loyal herald into a cheerful democratic Children country. Surviving Earth civilians and independent captives use their own political or population outcomes.

## Volgan the Necromancer

Volgan is an ancient evil who has existed for hundreds of thousands of years. He already has full authority over the Dead Lands. His Earth arrival is the projection of a developed realm with commanders, institutions, and the strongest initial dark army.

The proposed opening field army contains 60 formations: 20 grave hosts, 10 Netherborn, 10 Hell Forest orcs, 5 Demonic Mountain ogres, 10 Mazerin monster formations, and 5 personal-command guards. The usable economy target is five civilian and ten military factories with four research slots. The physical personnel and supply requirements are defined per family, not by treating every formation as a standard human division.

The placement builder must prove a viable deployment and supply plan for that complete army. Existing loyal herald positions can provide legal dispersal and supply. In their absence, the chosen founding state must support the declared temporary bridge and actual starting establishment. The implementation may select another valid state when this proof fails. It must not quietly reduce Volgan's army to match a weak host.

### The realm's military cultures

Yez grave hosts, Netherborn, Hell Forest orcs, Demonic Mountain ogres, and Mazerin monsters are regional forces of Volgan's realm. Their commanders can compete for assignments, but they are not neutral sovereign countries choosing a ruler. Military specialization gives them identity without contradicting the established political control.

A regional command branch chooses which force receives the next major logistical investment. Grave hosts provide numbers and attrition resistance. Netherborn provide elite shock forces. Hell Forest orcs provide conventional organized pressure. Demonic Mountain ogres provide siege strength. Mazerin monsters provide terrain-specific and irregular capabilities. Volgan cannot fund every branch simultaneously without a real economy and secure crossings.

### The invasion tree

| Internal workstream | Required action | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| V-Projection | Secure the founding command and link admitted herald positions. | Sustainable deployment and a bounded reinforcement network. |
| V-RegionalHosts | Invest in one regional military institution, then expand the others through later capacity. | Distinct forces and replacement patterns. |
| V-IndependentDark | Demand service from a named external dark ruler or conquer it. | New clients and armies that were not previously part of the realm. |
| V-Afrit | Assess the Rush succession, interrupt the usurpation, or prepare for the combined Ashen state. | A real strategic response to the rival who can overtake him. |
| V-Glo | Locate Yeldenne, secure a viable approach, and capture the state. | The artifact's immediate controller-bound power. |
| V-Reckoning | Prepare simultaneous war against The Lost Cause and The Ashen Throne. | The terminal campaign without merging the dark factions. |

Volgan's initial strength does not depend on recruiting external dark clients first. They can improve his position, but his own realm is already a major world threat. The AI should not spend several years buying minor alliances while ignoring a collapsing good frontier or an imminent Afrit capture of Rush.

### Deaths and necromantic reinforcement

Deaths can strengthen Volgan through a bounded reinforcement opportunity. The design must not count the same corpse repeatedly or create another population debit when a unit is raised.

The proposed reliable baseline uses newly recorded **civilian deaths of living populations** as a realm-wide necromantic stimulus. Every new five-million-death band since the last consumed threshold can authorize one five-formation grave-host tranche, subject to a 180-day reinforcement cooldown and a secure command. The actual troops arrive from Volgan's realm. This avoids claiming that the engine has identified individual corpses at an exact battlefield when it has not.

The historical Deaths total is never reduced by spending the opportunity. No soul currency is displayed. Raised-unit losses do not advance the civilian-death threshold. Ordinary military casualties are excluded from this reinforcement adapter unless the shared source can prove living status and prevent reuse of already raised forces. This is a deliberate source-accounting boundary.

Other reinforcement routes use captured territory, secure crossings, completed regional institutions, and actual equipment support. A fixed country-local callback can process one admitted request. It must not create troops every day or run a daily all-country loop.

### Volgan's defeat

Losing one Earth state can force a retreat or transfer command to a surviving admitted herald seat. The unique ruler and army history remain. A decisive defeat requires loss of viable Earth command, defeat of the organized herald network, and a completed containment or expulsion settlement. It does not require conquering the off-map Dead Lands, which are not a playable second world map in this event.

A permanent defeat closes his Earth return and the Children faction's normal reinforcement system. Surviving isolated troops can be disarmed, contained, or remain limited holdouts according to the settlement. They cannot respawn the unique ruler through a generic colony arrival.

## Horos, Yeldenne, and Glo

Horos rules the Castle of Yeldenne and protects Glo. The arrival creates one country, one unique castle building in a valid capital province, and one artifact identity tied to the state containing that building. The castle must actually appear on the map.

The proposed garrison is five castle-guard and five warden formations. The country begins neutral or good-aligned with three research slots and a compact economy. Its main problem is choosing protection without giving a stronger ally a permanent right to seize the artifact.

### The castle contract

The castle's state and province are recorded once at creation. Moving the capital does not move the castle. Annexation, occupation, liberation, and a change of ruler do not duplicate or remove it. Generic annexation cleanup must contain an explicit exception for this persistent landmark.

A damaged or ruined visual state can exist, but the artifact anchor remains. The building is not an ordinary constructible object that every country can copy. A hidden anchor without a visible unique castle does not satisfy the requirement. A decorative model without the unique building and state-bound artifact logic does not satisfy it either.

The model uses a dedicated verified map-building spawn and a valid state/province pair. It must not reuse a vanilla special-project spawn that can place the wrong building elsewhere. The exact mesh scale, footprint, shader, and placement coordinates require installed-game inspection and runtime evidence.

### Who receives Glo

**The controller of the state containing the Castle of Yeldenne receives Glo's power.** This is the user's ownership rule. It is not the legal owner of the state, Horos personally, the current capital holder, or whichever army occupies the castle province while the state still has another controller.

The control-change transaction removes the previous possessor's Glo idea and grants it to the new valid state controller. There is no attunement delay, purchase cost, shared bonus, or duplicate artifact. The state can have an artifact even when no valid country currently receives the benefit. The artifact record itself never disappears.

Partial occupation follows the game's actual state-controller result. If the castle province changes hands but the state controller does not, the possessor remains unchanged. The interface must make that rule visible to avoid suggesting that the model alone determines ownership.

### Proposed Glo power

| Effect family | Proposed magnitude |
| --- | ---: |
| Land attack | +50 percent |
| Land defense | +50 percent |
| Army organization | +50 percent |
| Factory output | +100 percent |
| Construction speed | +100 percent |
| Research speed | +50 percent |
| Political power generation | +100 percent |

These are design values to map to verified supported modifiers. The artifact is intentionally exceptional. It does not generate population, duplicate units, undo historical deaths, guarantee victory, or reverse another system's irreversible terminal state.

Glo occupies the third ZIN spirit slot. The country must merge or replace its ordinary event conditions if necessary. It cannot stack a second copy by changing government or by receiving the state through a different transfer path.

### Horos's tree

The guarded-neutrality route fortifies the actual castle state, obtains limited guarantees, and controls access to the artifact. Its late purpose is to survive as an independent protector, not to sit with an empty focus tree.

The Lost Cause route establishes a defense compact, supports friendly colonies, and can assume faction leadership after Rush falls. Horos cannot transfer Glo through a diplomatic gift while keeping the state. A treaty may promise joint defense, inspections, or a future lawful territorial settlement, but the actual power always follows state control.

A desperate-protector route seeks help from a questionable partner when the castle is isolated. It can create dependency, a siege-relief bargain, or an attempted seizure. It does not give Afrit a second artifact or convert Horos into a duplicate dark ruler. If Afrit captures the state, he receives the full ordinary Glo benefit immediately. A later corruption project changes presentation and political consequences, not the basic right of the current controller to its power.

### Loss of the castle

Horos can remain a living exile or allied commander after losing Yeldenne if a valid host accepts him. He no longer receives Glo. A recovery route requires an actual war or lawful transfer that changes control of the castle state.

The final capture, liberation, and recapture reports show the state, its current controller, and the diplomatic consequences. They do not print hidden plot information or claim that the gem traveled to a new capital. The map landmark and artifact record remain tied to the original state throughout.
