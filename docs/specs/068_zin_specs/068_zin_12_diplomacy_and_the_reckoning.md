# 068 ZIN: diplomacy, factions and the Reckoning

## A foreign policy before the global war

Colony diplomacy begins with specific governments and specific agreements. Recognition opens talks. A supply shipment builds an obligation. A border treaty settles named territory. None of those acts makes the recipient a puppet or discloses every hidden political preference.

A relationship remembers recognition, attack, delivered aid, a broken promise, an accepted peace and an exposed betrayal. These are durable incidents attached to the countries and colony instances involved. Changing a flag does not erase them. A new government can repudiate an old policy, but other countries can demand a settlement before restoring trust.

Same-origin colonies recognize a shared homeland through contact. They may exchange officers, accept refugees, federate or compete. Shared origin is not a permission to annex another human player's country or replace its chosen focus route.

## Neutrality and active policy

Every new sovereign colony begins neutral. The initial posture restricts offensive war and immediate membership in ordinary Earth factions. It does not prevent trade, reconnaissance, defensive preparations, guarantees or a response to an actual attack.

The first active policy requires a completed political commitment. The permitted policy depends on the society and evolution. Good societies can protect named partners or counter a proven dark threat. A mercenary society can accept a limited contract. A hostile government can issue demands or prepare conquest. A neutral colony can remain outside the coming blocs and still develop a complete domestic, trade or regional-defense route.

An offensive focus must identify what the war seeks. Suitable goals include a specific neighboring state, release of a captured partner, control of a real crossing, removal of an exposed hostile command or submission to a declared patron. A generic bonus to aggression does not replace a war goal.

The defensive adjacent-state peace rule belongs only to provocation wars. It does not expand every offensive treaty into an ever-growing demand for all neighboring territory. A colony cannot declare an offensive war and then label the defender an aggressor to obtain the defensive reward.

## Earth factions and ZIN blocs

An Earth country can maintain normal diplomatic ties before it chooses a Reckoning bloc. A defensive promise to a colony is bilateral unless the relevant faction accepts it as a shared obligation. The event never silently merges all Earth factions into one coalition.

A country cannot belong to two native factions at once. Joining a Reckoning faction therefore needs a declared transition from its current faction, with the actual diplomatic consequences. A faction leader must settle leadership and member obligations before dissolving or replacing its bloc. The implementation must verify the native war and faction constraints instead of removing membership in an arbitrary effect order.

A human country receives a clear choice. AI countries evaluate actual threats, existing allies, reachable fronts, delivered aid, ideology and the treatment of their population. They do not choose a dark faction merely because its displayed army is largest. A government that has been coerced into service must retain a record of that coercion and a later resistance or liberation outcome.

## The Lost Cause

The canonical Rush Kingdom forms The Lost Cause through its founding diplomatic route. The Golden King leads it first. The alliance needs Rush and at least one willing external colony partner to become a real defensive organization. Its initial structure reflects royal prestige and uneven obligations, while still opposing the two dark powers.

The faction's early promises include defense of member capitals, agreed transport, admission of displaced communities and consultation before offensive operations. These promises give the Golden King's government something concrete to neglect, exploit or fulfill.

The succession does not delete the alliance. A temporary council handles shared obligations while Rush is divided. It cannot force the other members to fight for the Golden Court or Afrit. A member may aid a compatible Rush camp through an explicit commitment while the alliance continues protecting its own territory.

If Einendil wins, Rush remains the natural leader and replaces royal privilege with negotiated obligations. A Regency can retain leadership through a more conventional security policy. A Free Dominion outcome can lead through a federal delegation. If Afrit wins, Rush and submitted dominions leave for The Ashen Throne. Lost Cause leadership passes to the strongest willing surviving good member under the succession rules.

The alliance's late route has three connected tasks: protect surviving communities, disable hostile crossing commands and settle the resulting political order. A protective alliance that only accumulates attack modifiers is incomplete. Its focuses should unlock finite defense missions, transport and reconstruction agreements, negotiated releases and targeted containment.

## The Children of Volgan

Volgan leads the faction when he arrives. Before then, existing Children cooperate through a herald command. That command prepares his arrival and coordinates actual military support without pretending that his full sovereign Earth country already exists.

The Dead Lands hosts are already his subjects. Yez, Nether, Hell Forest, Demonic Mountains and Mazerin contribute regional military commands. Their rivalries can change the order of deployment, prestige within the army or access to a particular reinforcement route. Ordinary regional hosts do not vote whether to join The Lost Cause.

Independent Dark Lands rulers face a different choice. They can submit for material protection, make a temporary bargain, resist or become a conquest target. Volgan begins strong enough to threaten the world without waiting for all those countries to agree.

Volgan's strategic route connects command expansion, supply, local rule and military conquest. Capturing a capital alone cannot create another complete Dead Lands army. Command sites and surviving herald institutions supply the real reinforcement pathway.

## The Ashen Throne

Afrit leads a separate faction whose purpose is his own rule. He fights Volgan as well as The Lost Cause. A temporary tactical agreement does not permit automatic merger into Volgan's faction or a final two-sided replacement for the Reckoning.

The faction begins around the Worshipdom, cult clients, corrupted institutions and willing dark allies. Its early weakness encourages bargains and targeted infiltration. Afrit must provide something useful before a target grants an office, military role or political privilege.

The Rush crown is the main route to superiority. A successful usurpation provides Rush's surviving state, army, industry and recognized dominion obligations. Consolidation missions then determine how much of that inherited power actually serves Afrit. Independent orcs, goblins, trolls and ogres still need their own pact, binding or conquest.

Ashen Throne diplomacy has a visible distinction between a paid ally, a dependent client, a bound host and a directly ruled country. Those states produce different obligations and different chances of resistance. A government cannot receive complete independent benefits while being treated as fully controlled for every army and industry calculation.

## Pledges and war obligations

A joining country chooses one principal contribution that it can deliver. Large members can add a second role after fulfilling the first. This is an agreement, not a spendable influence meter.

| Pledge | Proof of delivery | Consequence of failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capital defense | A real supplied defensive force or completed evacuation protects the named member. | The member can refuse further military obligations until a settlement is made. |
| Transport | Actual convoy capacity and valid port access carry the agreed shipment or people. | Undelivered material remains with its owner and the broken route needs repair or renegotiation. |
| Military supply | The exact agreed equipment reaches the recipient once. | The recipient loses the expected support and gains a breach grievance. |
| Civilian protection | A named cohort is safely received or a threatened settlement remains protected. | Record actual losses and failed reception through their owning systems. |
| Direct campaign | An existing force joins the named reachable operation under agreed command. | Remaining units withdraw where possible and the patron loses the undelivered service. |

A pledge is not successful merely because the country stays in the faction for 90 days. Performance uses the actual operation record and the mission's defined outcome. Pledges cannot be farmed between the same countries by repeatedly leaving and rejoining.

## Regional unions and formable directions

These are optional route payoffs, with final public names to be written later. They are not automatic rewards for owning a collection of Earth provinces with familiar modern borders.

| Reunited Rush | A winning lawful Rush route and settled obligations of the participating recognized dominions. Afrit uses his own crown route. | Current owned and controlled capital plus the actual admitted dominion settlement states in the frozen charter manifest. | A coherent Rush identity and integrated commands, with staged civilian integration. |
| Rush federation | Free Dominion victory or a negotiated later charter accepted by the participating members. | The participating real settlement states are controlled by consenting members and no unresolved claimant controls the royal capital. | Federal institutions and common defense. Member sovereignty follows the accepted constitutional choice. |
| Northern league | At least three compatible northern colony governments accept a common defensive charter and deliver initial pledges. | Their actual founding states remain held and connected by proven access or maritime transport. | A defensive bloc or negotiated union, chosen explicitly. The manifest of required states is built from the real campaign's admitted colonies and then versioned for the formation attempt. Changing a country's cosmetic tag cannot refresh it. A destroyed or released partner needs an explicit revised settlement rather than silent removal from the requirement list.

When exact state control is the central proof, use the project's manifest-driven state puzzle. Its shapes must come from the installed Earth map. The ZIN source maps provide homeland identity, not Earth state boundaries. Every category exposing that formable must attach the same generated control-proof interface when the accepted attachment policy requires it.

## The Reckoning begins

Evolution IV opens only after its ordinary Chaos and evolution conditions and dynamic delay are satisfied. A normal Reckoning requires the three real anchor powers, or the narrowly defined surviving successor that legally carries an anchor's faction. It does not respawn a permanently defeated unique ruler to manufacture a three-way war.

The setup announces a common origin, resolves faction leadership, invites or pressures still-uncommitted colonies, and starts hostilities between all three organized blocs. A country already in a compatible war keeps its real front and losses. Existing peace and faction records are reconciled rather than discarded.

A 90-day alignment period precedes forced bloc war in the normal progression, giving countries time to choose, arrange access and prepare supplies. The three anchors cannot use that period to select permanent peace with one rival and collapse the premise into two factions. Local ceasefires for evacuation or negotiations remain possible but expire under explicit terms.

Manual Reckoning starts the war immediately after valid setup and can skip that preparation period. It still creates viable anchors, legal territories, armies, supply and relationships before declaring the war.

## Defeat and recovery

A dark faction is militarily broken when its Earth anchor is defeated, its effective command network cannot sustain a fielded major host and its remaining organized clients have submitted, defected or been defeated. The specific command and client records provide the proof. A single inaccessible empty tag does not prevent resolution forever.

A good faction is broken when its actual leading government and surviving effective coalition cannot continue organized resistance. Its leadership can transfer according to the established rule while a viable member exists. The loss of Rush alone is never automatic Lost Cause defeat.

A ruler's defeat on Earth does not imply the destruction of their off-map homeland. Volgan can be defeated through the removal of his Earth projection and viable return routes. Einendil does not need to conquer a continent that is not on the playable map.

Unique identities are never reset by ordinary arrivals. Any accepted recovery route must preserve the same ruler, surviving forces, past losses and evidence. The normal pool does not create a fresh Afrit or Volgan with their original army after a defeat.

## Victory proof and isolated holdouts

The proposed winning faction must defeat both rival ZIN blocs and resolve remaining organized hostile Earth opposition. Friendly or peaceful neutral countries are not required to be conquered. A country counts as resolved when it joins the winner, accepts a specific peace or submits under the victorious route.

An unresolved opponent remains significant if it controls a relevant command or castle state, can field a supplied force capable of operating against the winner, can sustain a hostile crossing, or leads an organized hostile coalition. A distant island with no usable command, no meaningful force and no means of projection can be treated as an isolated holdout after 180 days without an effective hostile operation.

Use a documented practical threshold for that last test: at least five supplied conventional-equivalent formations, one major rare-creature host, an active hostile crossing or a functioning strategic base capable of projection keeps the opponent significant. These are design thresholds for a tested effective-force adapter. Raw division count alone cannot classify a dragon or Sunhot force as harmless.

The victory evaluator rechecks all requirements at commitment. No two factions can commit an ending from stale snapshots. The world-end ownership lock belongs to the shared terminal system. Winning a local war does not set it.

## Three endings and the lower-Chaos question

The Lost Cause ending protects surviving Earth autonomy, dismantles or contains hostile commands and settles the Rush dominions according to the winning good route. Einendil's version emphasizes chosen leadership and concrete peace. A Regency or another good leader changes the political form without turning the result into Afrit's order.

Volgan's ending establishes his grave hosts, monster commands and clients as the ruling world order. Glo strengthens that order only when his faction actually controls its state. Afrit's ending uses worship, bargains, corrupted institutions and subordinate cult governments. An Afrit-held Rush crown gives it a distinct political and military center.

The three endings remain independently selectable public terminal branches in Event Details. Disabling one does not disable the event, its evolutions or its siblings. A disabled victory branch can still produce the ordinary political outcome and aftermath, but cannot commit its world-end state automatically.

There is one unresolved project-policy conflict. The brief makes the Reckoning winner produce its ending and gives Einendil active Chaos reduction. The current project terminal rule requires current Chaos at 1000 or more. A long successful peace route could therefore win below that threshold.

**Preferred design proposal:** record a narrow Reckoning terminal-eligibility receipt when the normal war legitimately begins at 1000 or more, or when the explicit manual Reckoning setup is launched. That receipt would permit only this event's eventual winning branch to resolve below 1000. It would not raise Chaos, freeze its decline, enable another event's ending or bypass a disabled ending.

This is an explicit proposed exception to the current project rule. It is not an existing feature and must not be silently implemented. The implementation handoff carries it as a policy decision requiring approval. Until approved, the strict compatible behavior is political victory and nonterminal aftermath below 1000, with terminal commitment unavailable. That strict behavior does not fully satisfy the brief's unconditional winner-to-ending promise, so it must remain a disclosed limitation rather than a hidden fallback.
