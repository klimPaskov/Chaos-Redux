# ZIN

## Catalog entry

- Event ID: `68`
- Event name: ZIN
- Type: Major
- Status: To Be Reworked
- Chaos level: 1
- Cluster: Alien Invasions
- Cluster role: Severe member

## Source boundary

This event uses the user's ZIN universe and the thirteen supplied maps.

The maps contain many small handwritten labels. Clear major names are used directly. New colony countries proposed from those names are gameplay adaptations and can be renamed when the full specification fixes the canon spelling and identity.

The public opening should not call the other universe ZIN. Earth governments initially know only that unrelated settlers and creatures have appeared. The name ZIN becomes public once the colonies establish a common origin or the Reckoning begins.

## Historical foundation

Every colony should arrive with the memory of a real homeland. The maps define regions, seas, forests, kingdoms, mountains, islands, and old frontiers that existed before the crossings to Earth. Colony histories should name those places and explain why that society left, what enemies it remembers, and what political arguments followed it through the crossing.

### The Age of the Ainu

The oldest Rush traditions speak of the **Ainu**, the First Men. They disappeared more than one hundred thousand years ago and survive as myth, religious language, fragments of old names, and disputed relics.

The lineage **Aen the Ainu, his son Ein, and Ein's son Einendil** belongs to that lost age. Modern scholars and priests consider a living member of that line impossible.

Einendil survived because he was frozen for more than one hundred thousand years. The exact place and cause of his freezing remain uncertain at the start of the event. His survival becomes one of the central mysteries of the Rush route.

### The divided ages

After the Ainu vanished, the mapped world divided into regional societies. Rush grew around its central plains, forests, mountains, the Great Lake, and its recognized dominions. Selen developed its ranger tradition. Hiloron became the home of the giants. The Kilinti Forest became the home of ents and old forest peoples. Huhun became known for mounted warriors.

Orcs, goblins, trolls, and ogres also inhabit territories in and around the wider Rush geographical region, but they are not normal members of the Rush Kingdom. Most remain independent, hostile, isolationist, mercenary, or vulnerable to dark influence. Cooperation with Rush is a rare exception.

The Dark Lands developed around Dondor, the Dark Forests, Blackton, the Forests of Nothings, the Giant Forest, The Endless, Goldoroth, and the Dark Gate. Their orcs, goblins, trolls, ogres, giants, demons, and human societies retain separate rulers and old rivalries.

North Earth formed its own highland, forest, river, mountain, and island powers around Gunwaith, Girdon, Buldor, Lothord Fog Mountains, Eye Mountains, HKY, Kradlon, Gorogon, and the northern forests. The Cold Lands developed separate yeti, giant, troll, goblin, and ogre societies around Coldor, Titanos, the Uplands of Ice, the Yeti Lands, North Yeti Lands, Worldond, Meridor, and Horidor.

The Magical Lands preserved elves, dragons, arcanists, mushroom peoples, forest spirits, and cursed societies around the Magical Forest, Magic Mountains, Dragon Mountain, Mushroom Forest, Zhen Hills, Zillin Forests, the Cursed Lands, and the Lost Lands.

### The dark powers

Volgan is an ancient evil who has existed for hundreds of thousands of years. He already holds full power across the Dead Lands. Its grave hosts, demons, orcs, ogres, monster clans, necromantic institutions, and regional commanders are parts of his established realm rather than neutral societies waiting to choose a ruler.

Volgan begins vastly stronger than Afrit. He possesses the largest dark army, ancient command structures, secure territory, and generations of servants before the crossings reach Earth.

Volgan's direct rule does not automatically extend to every hostile society outside the Dead Lands. The Dark Lands, northern monster societies, and other regions can still contain independent rulers that he may intimidate, conquer, or recruit.

Afrit begins much weaker. His initial strength comes from the Worshipdom, human servants, demons, corruption, bargains, cults, and political influence. He builds power by taking control of other institutions from within.

The Golden King's court is Afrit's most valuable opening. If Afrit captures the Rush crown and its dominions, he can rise from the weaker dark power into a force capable of surpassing Volgan.

### The Age of Crossings

The crossings to Earth do not erase this history. A colony arrives with old borders, clan disputes, dynastic claims, religious memories, and opinions about other ZIN societies. News events should explain enough of this background for the colony to feel like a displaced country rather than a random unit package.

## Core idea

A society from another universe takes one random Earth state and establishes a sovereign colony without asking the host country.

Event 68 is publicly framed as **ZIN**, because the arriving colonies ultimately trace back to the same wider world even if Earth does not understand that at first. Early news should still treat each arrival as a separate colony with its own origin, customs, and dangers.

The first colony comes from the common settlement pool. It begins neutral. The event then continues as a large persistent system. More colonies can appear through time, shared focuses, colony actions, Chaos, and Evolutions.

Some settlers are normal humans. Others are elves, giants, ents, demons, monsters, yetis, or other peoples from the mapped world. They do not share one ideology or one goal.

At higher Chaos the colonies become active powers. More dangerous societies appear, larger focus routes unlock, and colonies begin intervening in Earth politics and in conflicts between ZIN societies. The final escalation is the Reckoning of ZIN, a three-way world war between The Lost Cause, The Children of Volgan, and The Ashen Throne.

## Opening settlement

The opening creates one colony from the baseline pool in one random valid state.

The society uses weighted placement, but the result remains random. Rush colonies favor Europe and North America. The Magical Forest elves favor remote mountains and forests. Noris favors deserts. Dondor favors the Middle East and nearby rough terrain. Map-derived societies use comparable terrain and regional weights.

The colony immediately receives the state as its territory and capital. The former owner receives no veto.

The new country receives its own leader, flag, country package, starting army, decisions, military identity, and focus tree. The starting force depends on the society and does not scale down because the host state belonged to a weak country.

After the first settlement, baseline progression can create more common colonies. A campaign may therefore contain several enclaves before Evolution I.

## Neutrality and provocation

New colonies begin neutral.

They can trade, exchange envoys, negotiate borders, and develop their state. They do not join ordinary Earth factions immediately and do not declare offensive wars until a focus route, Evolution, or society-specific behavior activates them.

A colony counts as provoked when another country declares war on it or uses an explicit action intended to remove or seize it.

After being attacked, the colony offers peace. Its demand is every aggressor-owned state directly neighboring any state currently owned by the colony.

If the aggressor accepts, those neighboring states are transferred to the colony and enter its settlement system. If the aggressor refuses, the war continues. A later peace offer recalculates the demand from the colony's current borders.

The adjacent-state demand applies to defensive wars caused by provocation. Once a colony becomes openly expansionist through its focus tree, its offensive wars use its own route goals.

## Colony growth

The Major event continues after the first firing.

New colonies can appear through dynamic follow-up arrivals, shared focuses that call another settlement, society-specific subcolonies, and Evolutions that expand the available society pool.

Unique direct rulers appear only once. Afrit, Volgan, Horos, and the Castle of Yeldenne are unique. Common societies can establish several colonies when valid states and country carriers are available.

Colonies from the same society can ally, merge, federate, compete, or choose different Reckoning factions according to their routes.

## Baseline societies

### The Rush Kingdom

The strongest and canonical Rush colony is the **Rush Kingdom**. Other Rush colonies and dominions can appear, but this is the main Rush country, the largest Rush military power, and the country that forms and initially leads **The Lost Cause**.

Rush colonies favor Europe and North America, with additional weight for temperate forests, plains, hills, major river regions, and large settled states.

#### The Golden King

The Rush Kingdom begins under **The Golden King**.

The title comes from his obsession with wealth. He hoards treasure, rewards court favorites, sells offices, and treats the prosperity of the crown as more important than the condition of his people. He can still build a strong state and a powerful army, but he is not a good ruler.

The Golden King creates The Lost Cause and becomes its first leader. His motive is mixed. He understands that Volgan and Afrit threaten Rush, but he also sees the alliance as a source of prestige, tribute, and protection for his crown.

His death is a mandatory middle stage of the Rush focus tree. Afrit secretly causes it. The public does not know this when the king dies.

The normal Rush political and military sphere is centered on human Rush society and a small set of recognized allied peoples.

Orcs, goblins, trolls, and ogres found in the wider Rush region are not ordinary Rush dominions or recruitable population pools.

Most of their states remain independent, hostile, isolationist, mercenary, or exposed to Afrit and Volgan. A rare clan, individual commander, or isolated settlement can side with Rush through a specific exceptional event or route, but this must never become the normal Rush outcome.

#### Rush public values

The Rush route should use two public values.

**Rush Unity** measures support for the kingdom among the capital, recognized dominions, army, and Earth population. It affects which states and units remain loyal during the succession war.

**Afrit's Influence** measures Afrit's access to the court, treasury, clergy, military, and dominions. It rises through bargains, corruption, use of Afrit's servants, the Golden King's greed, high Chaos, and decisions that trade long-term independence for immediate strength.

The exact identity of the king's killer remains hidden until a route uncovers it. The player can see Afrit's influence without being told that he caused the death.

The Golden King's death is not an optional failure branch. It is the event that opens the late Rush tree.

#### The death of the Golden King

Afrit causes the Golden King's death after gaining access to the court. The exact method can vary with Afrit's current influence, but the responsibility does not change.

The death leaves an empty throne and immediately begins a special Rush succession civil war. Preparation changes the sides and their strength, but it does not remove the civil war.

Possible sides include the Golden Court, the Free Dominions, Afrit's supporters, and Einendil's popular host when his event has been accepted. Giants, ents, Rangers, riders, island forces, regular Rush formations, and Earth-recruited units choose sides according to their relationships and the two Rush values. Orc, goblin, troll, or ogre intervention requires a rare prior exception and is never part of the default civil-war setup.

The country selected by the player represents the route they support. The other sides receive their own leaders, units, territory, and limited focus content rather than becoming generic rebels.

#### Einendil, son of Ein, son of Aen

After the Golden King's death, reports spread about an unknown soldier who has repeatedly survived battles no ordinary warrior could survive.

He identifies himself as **Einendil, son of Ein, son of Aen, the Ainu**. He claims to have been frozen for more than one hundred thousand years. Since the Ainu and the First Men are believed to have died in the distant past, the claim initially appears impossible.

Einendil has already proven himself in battle when the event appears. He warns that darkness is coming and offers service rather than a demand for power.

The player can dismiss him. Doing so closes the Einendil route.

If the event is accepted and the good path has been preserved, soldiers, civilians, Selen Rangers, old forest peoples, and parts of the dominions begin treating him as the chosen one. He does not claim the throne and does not ask to be crowned. The people choose him to lead the Rush state after his side wins the civil war.

The exact non-royal title can be fixed during the full specification. His political identity is leadership by popular choice rather than inheritance.

Einendil is the most powerful individual warrior in the event. He receives a unique commander and country-leader package, a personal guard, and route content built around direct battlefield intervention.

His good route should let Rush expose Afrit's role, restore the dominions, protect friendly colonies, lead The Lost Cause, and reduce Chaos through concrete peace settlements, defeat of dark powers, rescue of civilian populations, and containment of world threats.

#### Afrit takes the Rush crown

Afrit uses the Golden King's greed to enter Rush politics, causes his death, and exploits the succession war.

If Afrit's Influence is high, he becomes a direct claimant. He can present himself as the only ruler capable of holding Rush together, use corrupted court and dominion leaders, and seize the capital with a much stronger opening position.

If Afrit wins, he becomes King of Rush while remaining ruler of the Worshipdom. Rush and the Worshipdom become the core of The Ashen Throne.

Afrit then demands submission from Selen, Hiloron, Kilinti, Huhun, and the Rush Islands. Submitted dominions provide their normal unique units. Refusing dominions are attacked or corrupted through later focuses.

Orc, goblin, troll, and ogre societies are handled separately. They are not inherited as Rush subjects. Afrit can corrupt, bargain with, intimidate, or conquer them through Ashen Throne content.

This route combines Rush industry, manpower, Rangers, giants, ents, riders, and island fleets with Afrit's human servants, demons, corrupted elves, corrupted giants, dragons, and any monster hosts he separately subjugates.

Afrit begins much weaker than Volgan. Capturing Rush is the route that can make him stronger. A successful Rush usurpation should give The Ashen Throne a clear military advantage over Volgan without guaranteeing victory.

If Afrit captures Rush, The Lost Cause loses its founding leader. Leadership passes to the strongest surviving good member, with Sunhot, Horos, the Magical Forest elves, and a surviving good Rush splinter as the main candidates.

### The Elves of The Magical Forest

The Magical Forest elves are friendly and democratic. They favor mountains, remote forests, highlands, and isolated states.

They prefer recognition, protected borders, cultural exchange, and defense of forests. Their military uses elite elven infantry, ranged troops, scouts, mountain movement, and small high-quality armies.

Their high-Chaos routes focus on protecting friendly colonies and resisting Volgan, Afrit, and hostile demon states. They are a natural member of The Lost Cause.

### Noris Desert Warriors

Noris colonies favor desert and dry states. Strong candidate regions include North Africa, Arabia, Iran, Central Asia, the Australian interior, and dry parts of the Americas.

They use camel troops, mobile desert infantry, raiders, and units with major desert advantages.

Noris can remain independent, guard desert routes, offer military service, or pursue regional conquest. Their Reckoning alignment depends on the route taken.

### Dondor Brutes

Dondor colonies favor the Middle East, Anatolia, the Caucasus, the Balkans, North Africa, and rough frontier states.

They are dangerous humans built around brute strength, heavy infantry, shock troops, personal combat, and a harsh military hierarchy.

Dondor can negotiate from strength, sell military support, seize neighboring land after provocation, or become openly expansionist. Different routes can support The Children of Volgan, The Ashen Throne, or an independent brute state.

### Orcs

Orcs should have several cultures rather than one alignment. Orcs living in the wider Rush geographical region are normally independent, hostile, mercenary, or influenced by Afrit or Volgan. Rare exceptional clans can support Einendil or another Rush ruler. Dark Lands orcs are more likely to serve strong warlords, Volgan, or Afrit. Northern orcs can form maritime and mountain clans.

### Goblins

Goblins provide engineers, tunnel troops, scavengers, spies, traders, artillery crews, and sabotage units. Stone Forest, Blackton, the Forests of Nothings, HKY, Worldond, and the island regions can each support different goblin packages.

### Trolls

Trolls are slow and extremely strong. Giant Mountain, The Endless, Lothord Fog Mountains, the Uplands of Ice, and the Magical Lands can produce distinct forest, cave, fog, frost, and magic troll units.

### Ogres

Ogres are heavy shock troops and clan-based political actors. Hiloron, Goldoroth, Eye Mountains, Horidor, Demonic Mountains, and the Noris frontier support different ogre societies.

### Dragons

Dragons come primarily from **Dragon Mountain** in the Magical Lands.

They do **not** form an independent country or colony.

Instead, dragons, drakes, and wyverns are elite creature units that can be trained, recruited, bonded with, or summoned by specific powers.

The main users are the elven societies and Afrit.

**The Elves of The Magical Forest** can unlock dragon units through their magical and military branches. Their dragons should feel ancient, disciplined, difficult to replace, and closely tied to the traditions of Dragon Mountain.

**Sunhot Elves** can also unlock dragon and drake formations through later routes. Their dragons should fit the Sunhot identity and remain extremely powerful but rare.

**Afrit** can gain his own dragon formations through corruption, binding, bargains, or domination. Afrit-aligned dragons can include corrupted dragons, demon-bound dragons, armored dragons, and fire-oriented variants.

The Great Volcano in Sunhot can support a separate fire-drake tradition, but this still belongs to elven or Afrit military systems rather than a new country.

Dragons should be among the strongest nonhuman units in Event 68. Their strength should be controlled through rarity, reinforcement limits, special recruitment requirements, and access to the relevant focus-tree route rather than by making them comparable to ordinary infantry.

## Shared colony focus framework

Core societies receive large custom focus trees. Secondary map-derived colonies can use an adaptable framework, but each still needs unique branches, units, decisions, leaders, names, and AI behavior.

The common structure contains six broad areas.

### Establish the colony

This branch secures the capital, organizes food and housing, creates administration, raises starting units, and decides how the Earth population in the state is treated.

Possible routes include coexistence, separate communities, strict alien rule, forced removal, or full integration. Available routes depend on the society.

### Relations with Earth

This branch covers recognition, embassies, trade, non-aggression arrangements, guarantees, technology exchange, border agreements, and relations with the former state owner.

### Society identity

This is the largest unique branch. It represents the colony's homeland, beliefs, species, internal factions, and political structure.

Rush colonies choose kindness, cruelty, or specialized subcolonies. Magical Forest elves shape a democratic sanctuary. Noris chooses between caravan rule, desert guardianship, and conquest. Dondor decides how brute power is organized.

### Military and creatures

This branch unlocks unique units, reinforcement systems, commanders, equipment, and military laws.

Creature societies should rely on event, focus, and decision spawns where normal recruitment does not fit.

### Open another crossing

This branch can create allied or related colonies in new states. It can also attract volunteers, refugees, monsters, specialists, or military formations from the same ZIN region.

### Earth policy and Reckoning alignment

This branch remains limited at low Chaos.

At higher Chaos it lets the colony abandon passive neutrality, intervene against rival colonies, build its own bloc, or align with The Lost Cause, The Children of Volgan, or The Ashen Throne.

## Baseline Earth response

The former state owner can accept the loss, isolate the border, seek recognition talks, prepare a reconquest, or attack.

Other countries can recognize the colony, trade with it, guarantee it, study it, support the former owner, or prepare containment.

Earth countries should not know the colony's full alignment or future route. Friendly treatment can build trust, while violence can push an undecided colony toward a hostile faction.

AI behavior must use the society's posture. Peaceful elves should not behave like Dondor brutes. A kind Rush colony should not make the same choices as an evil Rush colony.

## Evolution I: Dangerous Crossings

- Chaos requirement: `200+`.

Evolution I increases arrival pressure, unlocks larger shared focus routes, and adds more dangerous societies.

### Worshipdom of Afrit

Afrit is the ruler.

The Worshipdom begins much weaker than Volgan's established realm. Afrit has a limited direct army and builds power through influence, cults, bargains, corruption, and control of other governments.

The Worshipdom includes human servants, demons, corrupted elves, corrupted giants, and other beings loyal to Afrit.

Afrit follows his own ambitions. He is not subordinate to Volgan and does not belong automatically to the good side. His country becomes the anchor of **The Ashen Throne**.

The Worshipdom can spread through cults, bargains, corrupted officers, summoned servants, and direct force. It can recruit humans from Earth as well as beings from ZIN.

### Shachihata Demons

Shachihata demons are very large, very powerful, and less numerous than ordinary troops.

They favor East Asia, Southeast Asia, mountain regions, volcanic states, and other locations that fit the South Earth map region.

Their tree should focus on a small elite demon host, the difficulty of replacing losses, relations with Afrit and Volgan, and the choice between serving another power or building their own demon state.

## Evolution III: The Great Lords

- Chaos requirement: `600+`.

Evolution III unlocks direct-lord powers, removes most limits on active colony policy, and opens the full large focus routes.

Good colonies can launch wars to protect Earth and destroy dark colonies. Evil colonies can expand without waiting to be attacked. Neutral colonies can form pacts, sell military support, or seize regional spheres.

### Volgan the Necromancer

Volgan appears as a unique ruler and country leader.

He is an ancient evil who has existed for hundreds of thousands of years and already has full authority over the Dead Lands.

His country begins vastly stronger than Afrit's Worshipdom. It receives the largest and strongest ZIN army available before the Reckoning, established necromantic institutions, ancient commanders, and direct access to the regional military cultures of the Dead Lands.

The army contains Yez grave hosts, Netherborn, Hell Forest orcs, Demonic Mountain ogres, Mazerin monsters, undead, demons, corrupted beasts, and other forces already subordinate to Volgan. These are detachments and regional formations of his realm, not neutral countries choosing whether to join him.

Further forces arrive through focuses, decisions, deaths, captured territory, and crossings opened by the Children of Volgan.

Volgan becomes the anchor and leader of **The Children of Volgan**.

His focus tree covers projection of Dead Lands power onto Earth, necromancy, monster mustering, command over the Children, conquest of independent dark rulers outside the Dead Lands, war against the good societies, and preparation for the Reckoning.

### Castle of Yeldenne

The Castle of Yeldenne appears in a remote defensible state as both a unique country package and a unique on-map building in the capital province.

Horos is the ruler of Yeldenne and the protector of **Glo**.

The castle must visibly exist on the map when Horos appears. It is not just flavor text or an abstract idea. It is a unique province building or landmark tied to the capital province of the Yeldenne state.

Yeldenne begins neutral or good-aligned. Its main purpose is to keep Glo out of the hands of Volgan and Afrit, though route choices can change how Horos works with Earth and other colonies.

### Glo

Glo is a unique artifact bound to the **Castle of Yeldenne** building rather than to a leader trait alone.

Only one state can hold the castle, and whoever controls the state containing the Castle of Yeldenne building receives the power of Glo.

The controller receives an extremely powerful national idea and related military, political, and economic advantages. The bonuses should be among the strongest artifact effects in the mod.

If control of the Yeldenne state changes, the power of Glo changes hands with control of the state and its castle building. If the state is occupied, annexed, liberated, or retaken, the controller of that state becomes the current possessor of Glo.

Glo must never duplicate or disappear. It is always tied to control of the Castle of Yeldenne.

Possession of Glo affects Reckoning diplomacy. The Lost Cause wants it protected. Volgan wants it captured. Afrit can seek to seize or corrupt its power.

## Colony diplomacy

Colonies need relationships with Earth and with each other.

Possible actions include recognition, border treaties, trade, military access, research exchange, cultural missions, guarantees, mutual defense, creature recruitment, and joint wars against hostile colonies.

Colonies should remember attacks, peace settlements, aid, guarantees, and betrayal.

Same-origin colonies should recognize one another. Friendly societies can form councils before the Reckoning. Hostile societies can fight long before the global alignment war begins.

## Earth response system

Earth countries need a compact decision family while colonies exist.

Useful actions include recognizing a colony, refusing recognition, opening talks, establishing a controlled border, trading for unusual goods, requesting military aid, supporting the former state owner, preparing a reconquest, guaranteeing a friendly colony, investigating common origins, preparing defenses against special units, supporting one side in an inter-colony war, and joining a Reckoning faction.

The state owner that lost territory receives stronger reconquest and negotiation tools.

No universal Earth coalition forms automatically at baseline. Governments can welcome, exploit, fear, or attack the colonies.

## Colony activation at high Chaos

At low Chaos, common colonies can remain neutral for long periods.

At Evolution II and especially Evolution III, shared active routes unlock.

Good colonies can intervene to protect Earth or friendly colonies. Neutral colonies can create pacts, demand spheres, or act as mercenaries. Hostile colonies can conquer nearby states. Afrit's followers can spread cults and corruption. The Children of Volgan can open crossings for Volgan's established Dead Lands armies. Rush colonies can choose good, evil, or independent expansion. Sunhot elves can act as peace enforcers. Horos can build a coalition around Glo.

A colony that stays neutral still needs a complete route and late-game purpose.

## Starting balance of the three powers

The Reckoning begins asymmetrically.

**Volgan** is the strongest dark power at the start. He possesses full control of the Dead Lands, the largest monster army, ancient institutions, and hundreds of thousands of years of experience.

**Afrit** begins far weaker in direct military terms. His advantage is political infiltration. Cults, bargains, corruption, compromised rulers, and the Rush succession crisis let him acquire power that he did not originally possess.

**The Lost Cause** begins around the canonical Rush Kingdom. It has the strongest common colony, powerful allied peoples, the elves, Horos, and potential Earth support, but it is weakened by the Golden King's rule, Afrit's influence, and the mandatory Rush succession crisis.

Afrit can surpass Volgan only by successfully capturing major external power, especially the Rush crown and its recognized dominions.

## Evolution IV: The Reckoning of ZIN

- Chaos requirement: `1000+`.

The final Evolution reveals the common origin of the colonies and begins the Reckoning of ZIN.

The Reckoning is a global war whose victor determines one of three world-end scenarios.

Surviving colonies choose among three factions according to their society, focus routes, relations, wars, and leaders.

Earth countries can join a faction, remain outside, or be attacked by factions that consider neutrality impossible.

### The Lost Cause

The Lost Cause is the main good-aligned faction.

The canonical Rush Kingdom forms it and leads it first under the Golden King. The Golden King is a poor ruler, but the coalition still begins around Rush because Rush is the strongest common colony and the only power capable of organizing several early dominions.

After the Golden King's death, the faction's future depends on the Rush civil war.

If Einendil wins, Rush retains leadership and he turns The Lost Cause into a genuine alliance for peace, defense of Earth, and destruction or containment of Volgan and Afrit.

If a Golden Regency or Free Dominion route wins, Rush can keep leadership with a less idealistic policy.

If Afrit captures Rush, the kingdom and most submitted dominions leave The Lost Cause for The Ashen Throne. Lost Cause leadership then passes to the strongest surviving good member, usually Sunhot, Horos and Yeldenne, the Magical Forest elves, or a surviving good Rush splinter.

Likely members include the Magical Forest elves, Sunhot elves, Goodin Sunfolk, God Forest Keepers, good Rush dominions, Horos and Yeldenne, friendly Gunwaith or Girdon routes, and Earth countries that choose to defend them. Elven members can contribute dragon units once their routes unlock them. Rare individual orc, goblin, troll, or ogre clans may join through exceptional content, but they are not expected members.

### The Children of Volgan

The Children of Volgan is led by Volgan.

Volgan enters the Reckoning as the stronger dark power. His full Dead Lands realm, Yez Grave Hosts, Netherborn, Hell Forest orcs, Demonic Mountain ogres, Mazerin monsters, undead, demons, and ancient commanders already stand under his control.

Independent dark colonies outside the Dead Lands can submit, resist, or be destroyed. Volgan does not need their support to begin as a major world threat.

### The Ashen Throne

The Ashen Throne is led by Afrit.

It fights both The Lost Cause and The Children of Volgan.

Afrit begins with the weaker direct military position. Likely members include the Worshipdom, corrupted elves and giants, Afrit-aligned Shachihata demons, Goldoroth or Stabillo routes, human cult governments, and colonies captured through influence.

Its strength depends heavily on what Afrit has infiltrated or usurped before the Reckoning. If he becomes King of Rush, The Ashen Throne can overtake Volgan as the strongest faction.

Afrit remains a real third power and cannot be automatically absorbed by another side.

## Reckoning victory

One faction wins after both rival ZIN factions have been defeated and the remaining organized Earth opposition has joined, submitted, or been conquered.

The exact victory test should ignore isolated holdouts that cannot affect the war while still requiring a real global victory.

The winner then activates its own world-end scenario.

## World-End Scenario: The Lost Cause Victorious

The Lost Cause defeats Volgan and Afrit.

If Einendil leads Rush, this ending becomes his promised peace. He refuses imperial rule, breaks the power of the dark factions, protects the surviving Earth states, and uses the victory to reduce Chaos through enforced settlements, disarmament of hostile colonies, release of captives, and restoration of the friendly dominions.

If another good ruler leads, the same world-end scenario uses that country's political structure while preserving the Lost Cause victory.

Friendly Earth countries retain meaningful autonomy. Hostile demon and monster regimes are dismantled, contained, or forced into settlements according to the victorious route.

## World-End Scenario: The Children of Volgan

Volgan defeats The Lost Cause and The Ashen Throne.

The Children of Volgan become the ruling world order. Monster armies, Children of Volgan, grave hosts, and dark client states dominate the surviving world.

Glo, if captured, makes this outcome stronger.

## World-End Scenario: The Ashen Throne

Afrit defeats The Lost Cause and The Children of Volgan.

Afrit establishes his own order through demons, corrupted peoples, human servants, bargains, and subordinate cult states.

If Afrit became King of Rush, the world order is built around the combined Rush crown and Worshipdom. Rush dominions, corrupted Rangers, giants, ents, riders, and island fleets become the central military foundation of his victory. Orc, goblin, troll, and ogre hosts appear only where Afrit separately corrupted, bargained with, or conquered them.

This ending remains separate from Volgan's rule. Afrit's order is built around worship, corruption, bargains, captured institutions, and loyalty to Afrit.

## Manual triggerable scenario

Event 68 needs a manual scenario with three setup types.

### First Colonies

The scenario creates several baseline colonies immediately. Intensity controls how many societies appear and how strong their starting armies are.

The scenario creates the canonical Rush Kingdom, advances its dominion and Lost Cause content, kills the Golden King, and begins the Rush succession war.

The scenario immediately creates the three faction anchors, places a broad selection of colonies, assigns initial alignments, and begins the three-way world war.

The manual Reckoning does not require prior Event 68 progression or normal Chaos thresholds.
