# Event 062 research notes

## Research purpose

This research supports design choices for alliance rupture, member weakness, side switching, military coordination, and settlement. Event 62 is a fictional global chaos incident. The historical cases below are analogies and design anchors. The event should not present itself as a simulation of one real alliance or one historical betrayal.

## Alliance cohesion as a gameplay concept

The United States Army Military Review article `An Alliance Divided? Five Factors That Could Fracture NATO` describes cohesion through shared goals, military coordination, converging commitments, mutual trust, common threat perception, institutional structures, political and economic factors, technology, and shared values.

Design use:

- faction pressure should read more than military size
- a common external threat can hold an alliance together
- divergent threat perception and internal disputes can weaken it
- consultative institutions and credible commitments can protect members
- a security charter and war council decisions are mechanically grounded themes

Source:

- Military Review, `An Alliance Divided? Five Factors That Could Fracture NATO`
- https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Journals/Military-Review/English-Edition-Archives/January-February-2018/An-Alliance-Divided-Five-Factors-That-Could-Fracture-NATO/

## Support relationships and outside sponsors

The International Committee of the Red Cross describes a support relationship in armed conflict as support that increases another party's capacity to conduct armed conflict. It also stresses that supporters can influence how a conflict is fought and how its aftermath is managed.

Design use:

- outside powers can supply equipment, fuel, access, guarantees, or mediation
- the event should limit sponsor count and record who supported which side
- support can affect settlement and humanitarian consequences without automatically creating direct war entry
- donor actions require concrete capacity and a paid transfer

Source:

- International Committee of the Red Cross, `Allies, Partners and Proxies: Managing Support Relationships in Armed Conflict to Reduce the Human Cost of War`
- https://www.icrc.org/en/publication/4498-allies-partners-and-proxies-managing-support-relationships-armed-conflict-reduce

## Yugoslavia and the Tripartite Pact

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum records that Yugoslavia joined the Axis on March 25, 1941. A military coup followed on March 27. The new government rejected the prior course, and the Axis invaded on April 6.

Design use:

- an alliance commitment can reverse rapidly after an internal political change
- member loyalty and leader legitimacy can matter as much as formal membership
- a bloc can answer perceived defection with military force
- internal political division can precede an external invasion

The Event 62 mechanic differs from this case. Yugoslavia was not selected through a weakest-member purge, and the event should not copy the historical partition or atrocities.

Source:

- United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, `Axis Invasion of Yugoslavia`
- https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/axis-invasion-of-yugoslavia

## Italy after the 1943 armistice

The National WWII Museum timeline records the rapid military rupture after Italy's armistice in September 1943. German forces occupied Rome, Italian units resisted in some locations, the Italian fleet surrendered to the Allies, and a German-backed Italian Social Republic was established.

Design use:

- former partners can become enemies while forces, ports, fleets, and territory remain intermingled
- military access, expeditionary relationships, unit withdrawal, and legal war-side cleanup need a staged transaction
- one country can fracture internally while its former ally occupies strategic territory
- a crisis can produce competing governments and wider resistance without every event needing to create a new country directly

The Event 62 design uses this as a warning about transaction complexity. It does not reproduce the Italian campaign or create its political actors.

Source:

- The National WWII Museum, `The Allied Campaign in Italy, 1943-45: A Timeline, Part One`
- https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/allied-campaign-italy-1943-45-timeline-part-one

## Postwar legitimacy and recovery

A 1945 United States diplomatic report on Allied policy toward Italy describes a country that had been defeated, occupied by different sides, divided politically, economically disrupted, and affected by civil war. The report also argues that political respectability and economic help were connected to stability and international alignment.

Design use:

- settlement terms should affect legitimacy, recognition, and later alignment
- an expelled government can seek external recognition and support
- punitive victory alone does not create a stable political outcome
- readmission and recognized separation need durable trust memories

Source:

- Office of the Historian, Foreign Relations of the United States, `Future Allied Policy Toward Italy`, June 27, 1945
- https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945Berlinv01/d467

## Design conclusions drawn from the research

The following are design inferences, not direct historical claims:

1. Alliance cohesion should be a single public state supported by several hidden factors.
2. Weakness should include military, industrial, political, and geographic vulnerability.
3. Common enemies can protect a weak member when it remains strategically useful.
4. A purge needs a staged legal transaction because forces and diplomatic relationships can be intermingled.
5. Outside support should change capacity and settlement without guaranteeing direct intervention.
6. Readmission, recognized separation, and successor blocs should leave durable political memory.
7. A leader can become isolated when most allies defect, even when the legal faction leader cannot be directly expelled safely.
8. The strongest event presentation should show physical signs of betrayal, such as removed insignia, closed headquarters, and blocked access.

## Research boundaries

No historical source supports the event's exact random global mechanism, Chaos thresholds, victim-count tables, public cohesion value, or Evolution structure. Those are original gameplay design decisions.

No source was used to justify atrocities, ethnic targeting, protected-class selection, or punitive civilian harm. Event 62 selects governments through strategic and faction factors only.
