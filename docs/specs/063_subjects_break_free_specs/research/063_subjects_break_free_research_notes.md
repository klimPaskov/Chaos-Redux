# Event 063 research notes

## Research purpose

The event is fictional and dynamic, but its settlement and cooperation systems draw from recurring features of twentieth-century decolonization and state formation. The research was used to avoid one generic independence outcome and to give the Liberation Pact a practical institutional role.

## Finding 1: Independence followed several different paths

The United States Office of the Historian describes postwar decolonization as a varied process. Some transfers were peaceful and orderly. Other states gained independence only after long revolutions. Former colonial governments also reacted differently, from accepting a new relationship to military resistance.

### Design use

Event 063 uses four settlement modes:

- negotiated separation
- unilateral separation with recognition
- contested separation without immediate war
- armed refusal

This structure is preferable to making independence automatically peaceful or automatically violent.

### Source

United States Department of State, Office of the Historian. `Decolonization of Asia and Africa, 1945-1960`.

URL: https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/asia-and-africa

Relevant material: the discussion of peaceful transfers, protracted revolutions, military resistance by former powers, Cold War pressure, outside aid, nonalignment, and newly independent states supporting further decolonization.

Source note: the page is an official historical overview. It states that the retired Milestones series is no longer maintained. The historical summary remains useful, but it should not be treated as a current policy source.

## Finding 2: Recognition, self-determination, and territorial integrity can pull in different directions

The United Nations Audiovisual Library of International Law summarizes General Assembly Resolution 1514. It identifies self-determination and transfer of powers as central principles. It also notes the resolution's concern with national unity and territorial integrity. The same overview discusses the later pragmatic acceptance of inherited frontiers in order to reduce border conflicts among new states.

### Design use

Event 063 separates independence from later territorial claims.

- the subject becomes independent first
- existing cores and claims remain
- the event does not grant broad claims against the former overlord
- former-overlord restoration claims are narrow and temporary
- border incidents require a real border and existing dispute
- a peace settlement cleans temporary restoration goals

This keeps independence from becoming a free territorial expansion system.

### Source

United Nations Audiovisual Library of International Law. `Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples`.

URL: https://legal.un.org/avl/ha/dicc/dicc.html

Relevant material: adoption on 14 December 1960, the self-determination principles, cessation of repression, transfer of powers, respect for territorial integrity, and the discussion of inherited post-colonial frontiers.

## Finding 3: Newly independent states created organizations for solidarity, sovereignty, and coordinated liberation support

The African Union's historical overview explains that thirty-two independent African states created the Organisation of African Unity in 1963. Its stated objectives included unity, defense of sovereignty and independence, ending colonialism, and international cooperation. The overview also describes the OAU Coordinating Committee for the Liberation of Africa as a mechanism for coordinated support and common positions.

### Design use

The Liberation Pact has practical functions:

- recognize new breakaways
- coordinate aid
- defend member independence
- mediate disputes
- support later independence movements
- present common positions toward former overlords

The Pact remains a bloc of sovereign states. It is not a single government or automatic annexation route.

### Source

African Union. `About the African Union`.

URL: https://au.int/en/overview

Relevant material: the founding of the OAU in May 1963, its objectives, and the Coordinating Committee for the Liberation of Africa.

## Finding 4: Outside support often combined aid, political influence, and military pressure

The Office of the Historian notes that the United States and Soviet Union used aid, technical assistance, and in some cases military intervention to influence newly independent states. Many of those states resisted alignment and pursued nonalignment.

### Design use

Event 063 uses a graduated support ladder:

1. recognition
2. material aid
3. advisers or volunteers
4. guarantee
5. direct intervention

Material support should be more common than direct war entry. Liberated states can cooperate without being forced into one ideology or major-power bloc.

### Source

United States Department of State, Office of the Historian. `Decolonization of Asia and Africa, 1945-1960`.

URL: https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/asia-and-africa

## Finding 5: Collective defense can require assistance without prescribing one identical response

NATO's official explanation of collective defense notes that assistance can take different forms and does not always require armed force. It also describes collective self-defense arrangements as bilateral or multilateral commitments.

### Design use

The Liberation Pact uses one obligation with several valid forms of support. A member can provide equipment, access, advisers, guarantees, or direct military help according to the threat, Pact cohesion, access, and national capacity. Direct war entry is the strongest response, not the only valid response.

### Source

North Atlantic Treaty Organization. `Collective defence and Article 5`.

URL: https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/collective-defence-and-article-5

Relevant material: assistance may include actions short of armed force, and collective self-defense can be organized through multilateral agreements.

Research note: this is used as a general institutional design reference. The Liberation Pact is not modeled as NATO and should not copy NATO names, geography, or exact treaty rules.

## Finding 6: Statehood and recognition are related but not identical design questions

The Montevideo Convention is a common historical reference for the rights and duties of states. The official Organization of American States page provides the convention scans and ratification material.

### Design use

Event 063 makes independence effective before universal recognition. A country can be independent and still face a contested diplomatic settlement. Recognition affects guarantees, relations, and restoration pressure, but lack of recognition does not silently restore subject status.

### Source

Organization of American States. `Convention on Rights and Duties of States`.

URL: https://www.oas.org/juridico/english/treaties/a-40.html

Research limit: the accessible official page is mainly a scan index. The specification does not rely on a detailed legal interpretation of the convention.

## Research conclusions

The research supports six design rules.

1. Independence needs several settlement paths.
2. New sovereignty should not grant broad territorial claims.
3. Recognition and practical agreements should matter after the legal break.
4. Other liberated states can provide organized support without sharing one ideology.
5. Collective defense should allow several forms of assistance.
6. The formal Pact should remain a cooperation system among sovereign countries.

## Content limits

The event applies these institutional patterns to any valid Hearts of Iron IV subject relationship. It does not claim that every subject in the game represents historical colonial rule, that every independence movement has the same legitimacy, or that one historical decolonization model fits every country.
