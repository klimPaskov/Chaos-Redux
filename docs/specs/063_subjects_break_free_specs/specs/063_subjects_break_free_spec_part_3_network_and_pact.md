# Event 063: Subjects Break Free

## Part 3: Liberation network and Liberation Pact

## Shared liberation origin

Countries freed or created by different Chaos Redux events need a common way to recognize a related history without giving one event control over another event's release mechanics.

The shared liberation-origin contract records provenance and current network eligibility. It does not release countries, assign territory, create country packages, grant formables, or choose governments.

### Required provenance fields

Each registered country should retain:

- first liberation origin
- first liberation date
- latest liberation event
- latest liberation date
- former overlord or former host for the latest liberation
- separation mode
- liberation generation count
- active or suspended network status
- current formal Pact status
- current Pact partner status
- unresolved former-overlord conflict where relevant

The first origin is permanent historical provenance. A country created through Independence Wave can later become a subject and be freed through Event 063. Its first origin remains Independence Wave, while the latest liberation event becomes Event 063.

A Soviet Collapse successor keeps its Soviet successor origin. Event 063 can add a later release record without converting the country into an Event 063 package.

### Owner boundaries

Event 063 owns:

- selection of existing subjects
- removal of subject status
- settlement with the former overlord
- Event 063 cooldowns
- Event 063 recognition and support opportunities
- creation and management of the Event 063 Liberation Pact

Independence Wave owns:

- its country candidates
- its territory and anchor transactions
- its country package identities
- its release ledger
- its formable and congress routes
- its event-specific military, political, and institutional setup

Soviet Collapse owns:

- its republic and splinter release logic
- its authority and crisis systems
- its successor country packages
- the Free Republics' League
- its terminal collapse and world-state routes

The shared contract owns only neutral questions that several events need to ask:

- Was this country liberated through an approved origin?
- Is the country currently independent and active in the network?
- Are two liberated countries compatible enough for recognition or support?
- Can a country be invited as a full Pact member or partner?
- Has this country already received the same one-time cross-event opportunity?

No owner should write another event's private ledger to create compatibility.

## Network lifecycle

### Active member

A country is an active liberation-network state when it:

- exists
- is independent
- has a valid liberation origin
- is not an actual nonhuman or incompatible special Chaos country
- is not fighting another network state over a major core conflict
- has not been expelled from cooperation for attacking or resubjugating other liberated states

Active membership is informal. It does not create a faction, guarantee, or automatic war obligation.

### Suspended member

A country's network status is suspended when it:

- becomes a subject
- loses all valid territory
- enters a protected country-replacement transaction
- is absorbed into another state without remaining as an independent tag
- is temporarily controlled by an owner system that forbids network diplomacy

Suspension preserves provenance. A later valid return to independence can reactivate the country.

### Removed active record

If the country ceases to exist, its active network state is removed. Its historical origin may remain for documentation, event history, and later restoration checks.

### Expelled or censured state

A liberated country can lose cooperation privileges when it:

- attacks another liberated country without a serious territorial or defensive cause
- subjects or annexes another liberated country through an Event 063 dispute
- abandons a formal Pact defense commitment without a valid impossibility
- repeatedly sponsors wars against the network
- becomes an incompatible terminal actor

Censure should block invitations and reduce support. Expulsion from the formal Pact does not erase origin history.

## Informal liberation cooperation

Newly liberated countries do not receive automatic friendships. They receive bounded opportunities based on compatibility, distance, strategic need, and campaign history.

### Recognition

An active liberated country can recognize another newly independent state. Recognition can improve diplomatic standing, increase the chance of guarantees, and make later support cheaper or more likely.

Recognition is favored when:

- the countries share a liberation origin or recent Event 063 firing
- they broke from the same former overlord
- they share a region or border
- they have compatible governments
- they have no severe core conflict
- one is under threat from a former overlord
- either country previously supported the other

Recognition should not create a permanent relation bonus between every liberated country in the world. The network evaluates a bounded set of the most relevant partners.

### Guarantees

A liberated country can guarantee another when it has enough military capacity and access to matter. Small distant countries should not issue meaningless guarantees solely because they share origin status.

Guarantees are favored by:

- a shared border or regional theater
- the same former overlord
- an active contested settlement
- existing recognition and positive relations
- Liberation Pact membership or partner status
- military capacity relative to the threatened country

A guarantee can be withdrawn when the guarantor becomes unable to act, the dispute ends, or the guaranteed country attacks another network state.

### Material support

Countries can provide:

- infantry equipment
- support equipment
- artillery where available
- trucks and trains when supply is the main problem
- fuel
- convoys
- temporary civilian-industry support
- advisers or volunteers

Support must come from the donor's real capacity. The event should not create equipment from nothing because a country has liberation provenance.

The donor evaluates its stockpile, current wars, equipment deficit, distance, access, and relationship. Aid packages are bounded and can use a cooldown.

### Military cooperation

Compatible liberated countries can grant access, exchange volunteers, coordinate an independence war, or join the same faction. Informal cooperation should remain lighter than Pact membership.

Two countries with major mutual claims, opposite war alignments, or active hostility cannot use the origin record to bypass normal conflict.

### Support for later independence movements

Active network states can increase political and diplomatic pressure on a remaining subject. This can:

- improve the subject's Event 063 candidate weight
- improve its autonomy progress through a bounded public support action
- increase recognition readiness after release
- create an aid pledge that activates only if the country becomes independent
- reduce the former overlord's confidence in coercion

The network cannot directly release a subject through a normal decision. Event 063, Event 006, Event 005, or the relevant owner system must still perform the actual release.

## Compatibility model

Compatibility is broader than ideology. The network represents shared vulnerability and a recent struggle for sovereignty. Countries with different governments can cooperate when their practical interests align.

### Strong positive factors

- same former overlord
- same liberation firing or wave
- shared border or region
- prior recognition, aid, guarantee, or joint war
- no claims or cores against each other
- similar strategic alignment
- common threat from former overlords
- both have refrained from subjecting other liberated states

### Moderate positive factors

- similar ideology
- same faction partner network
- mutual rivals or common enemies
- close trade or military access
- a recent successful mediation

### Strong negative factors

- active war against each other
- major core conflict
- one country claims most of the other's territory
- one country has resubjugated another network state
- one is the former overlord of the other and the dispute remains unresolved
- one is an incompatible special Chaos actor
- one is already bound to destroy or absorb the other through another owner system

Ideological distance can reduce compatibility, but it should not override every practical reason for cooperation. A rigid same-ideology filter would make the network too shallow and would misrepresent the event's cross-origin purpose.

## Evolution III: Liberation Pact

At `600+` Chaos, the informal network can become a formal international bloc. The Pact is created through a congress after enough compatible independent states exist.

The Pact is a durable faction with its own purpose. It is not an automatic union, federation, common government, or annexation route.

## Pact formation conditions

A congress becomes possible when all of the following are true:

- Evolution III is active
- no Event 063 Liberation Pact already exists
- at least one valid founder can host the congress
- enough compatible active network states exist
- the candidate group is not already contained inside one unrelated faction
- there is a practical reason for cooperation, such as contested separations, recent independence wars, repeated threats, or a large liberation network

### Membership threshold

The normal threshold is:

- four compatible active states when at least two liberation origins are represented
- five compatible active states when all candidates share one origin

These totals include the founder and count only countries that accept full membership. Partners and observers do not satisfy the threshold.

A six-state threshold may be used when the candidates are distant, politically divided, or face no current threat. The event should not form the Pact from a handful of disconnected countries that have no reason to coordinate.

### Founder selection

The founder should be a credible host, not automatically the largest country.

Positive factors include:

- independence and valid network status
- no membership in another faction
- enough political stability to host the congress
- recognition by several other liberated states
- prior aid, guarantees, or mediation
- military and economic capacity to coordinate support
- central geography or access to several candidates
- victory in an independence war
- cross-origin trust

Negative factors include:

- active major core disputes with candidates
- aggressive resubjugation policy
- severe collapse or imminent capitulation
- an existing faction leadership commitment
- repeated abandonment of other liberated states
- extreme ideological hostility to most candidates

A small country can found the Pact when it has the strongest diplomatic network. A large country with no trust should not win by size alone.

## Congress flow

The founder receives the working-label decision `Call a Liberation Congress`.

The congress should:

1. freeze the invitation pool
2. identify full-member candidates and partner candidates
3. invite the most compatible states first
4. let human countries accept, decline, or seek partner status
5. resolve AI invitations from compatibility, threat, and current obligations
6. form the Pact only if the minimum full membership is reached
7. create the charter, founder, membership roster, and initial Liberation Cohesion
8. record the Pact formation evolution actor as the founder
9. open Pact decisions and the staged charter spirit

Initial Liberation Cohesion should normally begin between `35` and `65`, with `45` as the central anchor. More full founding members, origin diversity, an active shared threat, and fulfilled prior support raise the starting value. Serious disputes, reluctant acceptance, and broken prior pledges lower it. A new Pact must not begin in the Fractured or United band. Centralize the exact factors and clamp them to the accepted range.

A failed congress does not erase the network. The founder receives a cooldown before trying again. Relations and campaign changes can alter the next invitation pool.

## Full members and partners

### Full member

A full member:

- is independent
- has valid liberation provenance
- is not in another faction
- passes compatibility checks with the Pact
- accepts the charter
- participates in collective defense and Pact decisions
- contributes to Liberation Cohesion

### Partner

A partner:

- has valid liberation provenance
- supports the Pact's goals
- remains in another faction or cannot accept full membership
- can recognize states, send aid, mediate, and answer bounded support requests
- does not receive automatic Pact war obligations
- does not count as a full member for dissolution checks

Partner status prevents the event from stealing countries from existing alliances. A partner can become a full member later if it leaves its other faction through ordinary valid rules.

### Observer

A newly independent or politically uncertain state can attend as an observer. Observer status is temporary and grants no defense rights. It provides a route to recognition, compatibility growth, and later invitation.

## Liberation Cohesion

The Pact exposes one persistent public mechanic value: `Liberation Cohesion`.

Range: `0 to 100`.

The number and its current band should appear in the Pact decision category and the Pact charter national spirit. The charter icon changes by band so the player can read the condition without relying only on a number.

| Cohesion band | Pact condition | Available behavior |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 to 24 | Fractured | Consultation and recognition remain. Collective action is weak and dissolution risk begins after a grace period. |
| 25 to 49 | Consultative | Members can coordinate aid, guarantees, mediation, and limited volunteer support. |
| 50 to 74 | Coordinated | Common defense planning, larger aid packages, joint missions, and stronger support for threatened breakaways become available. |
| 75 to 100 | United | Members can answer major independence wars, coordinate pressure, and maintain stronger collective readiness. Direct intervention still requires access and military capacity. |

Cohesion is the only persistent Event 063 value that players must monitor. Candidate scores, settlement tension, invitation weights, support readiness, and former-overlord pressure remain hidden calculations shown through qualitative tooltips and status text.

## Cohesion movement

### Cohesion gains

- a member honors a defense commitment
- members successfully defend a breakaway state
- a material aid pledge is fulfilled
- a serious dispute between members is mediated
- a new compatible full member joins
- a threatened liberated state receives timely support
- a joint mission succeeds
- a former overlord recognizes a contested member after Pact pressure

### Cohesion losses

- a member abandons a defense commitment without a valid impossibility
- two members fight each other
- a member resubjugates another liberated state
- a promised aid package is not delivered
- a member capitulates because agreed support never arrived
- the founder disappears without a valid leadership transfer
- members repeatedly refuse relevant consultations
- a severe internal dispute remains unresolved

Routine monthly drift should be small. Cohesion should move mainly through decisions, wars, guarantees, mediation, admissions, failures, and concrete outcomes.

The same action should not be farmable. Repeated small aid to the same target should have diminishing cohesion value, and a single war should not grant the same defense reward through several duplicate hooks.

## Pact goals and rules

### Goal 1: Defend member independence

The Pact coordinates defense when a member is attacked by a former overlord or faces a restore-subject war goal. Members can provide aid, volunteers, access, guarantees, or direct defense according to cohesion and capacity.

The Pact should not automatically treat every ordinary offensive war by a member as a liberation war.

### Goal 2: Support new breakaways

The Pact can recognize newly independent countries, offer emergency aid, invite observers, and issue guarantees. Full membership requires the ordinary invitation process.

### Goal 3: Coordinate independence wars

When Evolution II is active, the Pact can identify one war leader, organize supply, and limit direct interventions. It should not open separate duplicate wars or give every member a broad war goal against every former overlord.

### Goal 4: Mediate liberated-state disputes

Members with mutual claims or border incidents can seek mediation. Success protects cohesion. Failure can reduce cooperation, suspend guarantees, or lead to censure.

### Goal 5: Pressure remaining subject systems

The Pact can support autonomy, recognition, and preparedness in valid subjects. It cannot release them through a direct faction button.

## Pact decision families

The Pact category should normally show three to five actions at once.

### Charter and membership

- call the founding congress
- invite a full member
- offer partner status
- admit an observer
- resolve a membership objection
- transfer Pact leadership

### Defense and support

- coordinate a threatened member's defense
- open a supply corridor
- pool a bounded equipment package
- organize volunteers
- issue a collective guarantee
- prepare mediation

### Independence support

- recognize a new breakaway
- sponsor a subject's autonomy campaign
- prepare an aid pledge
- support a later independence movement
- pressure a former overlord to recognize a settlement

### Discipline and cohesion

- mediate a member dispute
- censure an aggressor member
- suspend a member that became a subject
- restore a re-independent member
- dissolve the Pact when it can no longer function

Obsolete actions should disappear. The player should see decisions tied to current members, threatened states, pending invitations, and active wars.

## Leadership

The founder begins as Pact leader. Leadership transfers when the leader:

- ceases to exist
- becomes a subject
- leaves the Pact
- remains capitulated for a long period
- loses a formal confidence process under severe cohesion collapse

The replacement should be selected from full members using trust, capacity, recognition, and centrality. Raw factory count alone should not determine leadership.

A human leader can voluntarily call a leadership conference. It cannot appoint an ineligible country.

## Admission and exit

### Admission

A candidate must be independent, active in the network, compatible, and free of an unrelated faction commitment. Invitation weight rises with threat, recognition, shared support, and proximity.

Human candidates choose. AI candidates evaluate the Pact's cohesion, likely defense value, ideological fit, active wars, and current faction opportunities.

### Voluntary exit

A member can leave when it is not in a Pact defensive war and has no unresolved obligation. Exit reduces cohesion and can create a temporary trust penalty. The Pact does not force permanent membership.

### Expulsion

Expulsion is available for:

- aggression against another member
- resubjugation of a network state
- repeated refusal of valid collective defense obligations
- becoming an incompatible terminal actor
- acting as the former overlord in an unresolved member restoration war

Expulsion requires a strong case and enough Pact support. It should not be a routine way for the leader to remove rivals.

## Dissolution and fallback

The formal Pact dissolves when:

- fewer than two full members remain for a sustained grace period
- Liberation Cohesion remains at zero for a sustained grace period
- the faction has no valid leader and no eligible replacement
- all members agree to dissolve
- another accepted owner system legally replaces it through a later route

Dissolution removes the formal faction, Pact decisions, and charter spirits. It does not erase liberation origin or the informal network. Former members remain able to recognize and support each other.

## Relationship with Event 006 congresses

An Independence Wave congress remains under Event 006 ownership.

- Event 063 can recognize its member states as liberation-network countries
- an Event 006 faction can become a Pact partner institution
- countries in that faction are not forcibly transferred
- shared aid or mediation can occur through neutral adapters
- Event 063 does not rename, dissolve, merge, or reconfigure the Event 006 congress
- a later explicit design can negotiate a merger, but this specification does not assume one

## Relationship with the Free Republics' League

The Soviet Collapse Free Republics' League remains under Event 005 ownership.

Compatible successors can:

- join the informal network
- attend a Pact congress as observers or partners
- provide aid to an independence war
- join the Liberation Pact when they are not bound to the League and pass ordinary membership rules

The Event 005 League is never silently replaced by the Event 063 Pact.

## Pact success and failure states

### Stable defensive bloc

The Pact maintains at least four members, cohesion above 50, and a record of fulfilled commitments. New breakaways can rely on real support, but member states remain sovereign.

### Broad liberation coalition

The Pact includes several liberation origins, maintains high cohesion, and supports later breakaways across more than one region. This state enables the hardest Pact achievement and stronger recognition pressure.

### Hollow charter

The Pact exists but remains below 25 cohesion. Decisions focus on mediation, fulfilled pledges, and leadership. Failure to recover leads to dissolution.

### Captured by one power

A very strong leader tries to use the Pact as its own sphere. Other members can resist through a confidence dispute, partner withdrawal, or leadership conference. The Pact must not become a free puppet network.

### War between members

Members fighting each other suspend common guarantees and sharply reduce cohesion. Mediation can restore cooperation. Continued war can trigger expulsion or dissolution.

### Member resubjugation

A member that becomes a subject is suspended immediately. Other members can support its return through ordinary event and war systems. They do not receive a free liberation effect.

## Network and Pact acceptance

The cooperation system is complete when:

- first origin and latest liberation can coexist
- Event 063 never overwrites Event 006 or Event 005 ownership data
- network membership suspends and restores cleanly
- recognition and aid use bounded candidate sets
- countries with severe conflicts cannot exploit shared provenance
- factioned countries become partners instead of being stolen
- the Pact forms only through a viable congress
- the one public value is visible and consequential
- cohesion changes through concrete actions
- leadership, admission, exit, expulsion, and dissolution work
- the Pact can support later breakaways without directly releasing them
- dissolved Pact members retain the informal network
