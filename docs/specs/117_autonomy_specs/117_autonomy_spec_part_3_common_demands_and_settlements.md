# 117 Autonomy: Common Demands

Common Demands turns a shared improvement in autonomy into a coordinated political opportunity inside each master's sphere.
The subjects approach their master at the same moment, while each retains authority over its own settlement.
A master can offer a durable cooperative arrangement, support lawful changes of status, or spend political effort trying to preserve the present relationship.
Negotiation can succeed, divide the petitioners, or produce unrest.

## Entry and participants

The proposed entry tier is Chaos level 3.
This evolution changes the next firing and can operate alone or alongside Self-Government Momentum.
It is a genuine mutation of that firing, not a name for the ordinary act of receiving points.

Group the firing's recipients by their actual immediate master after the baseline grant has been committed.
Each group begins one common petition with a **120-day negotiation window**.
A master with one subject receives the same process with one petitioner.
A subject that also has subjects participates as a petitioner toward its own master and as a master toward its direct subjects.
Its two roles do not confer authority over one another's consent.

The petition records the actual countries, their relationship generations, the current autonomy tiers, and the issues they can legally negotiate.
It does not recreate absent countries or enlist non-subject states.
Territories without a separate country do not become petitioners merely because they have cores or a liberation movement.

## A common petition with individual outcomes

The master sees the whole group, which makes the cost of refusing everyone politically clear.
The common process does not impose majority rule on sovereign governments.
No subject can accept a concession, choose independence, pledge resources, or start a war on behalf of another.

A public master policy can establish a default offer for the group.
Each subject then receives its own concrete terms, including its applicable legal status route, the effort each side must undertake, and what continued cooperation means.
The master may tailor a settlement to an individual subject's actual position without making the entire petition fail.

The opening choices have three broad purposes.

| Working policy direction | Master commitment | Subject choice | Result if carried through |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recognize a lawful higher status | Endorse the existing legal route and respect its costs and consent requirements | Use or defer that route through its owner | A real status change when all requirements are met, with existing cooperation preserved where the relationship allows it |
| Negotiate a cooperation compact | Deliver the finite political commitment in Part 4 and refrain from Event 117 coercion against the member during the compact | Accept a finite civil program and suspend Event 117 escalation while it is honored | A settled petition, continued relationship, and a limited earned autonomy installment |
| Maintain the current arrangement | Commit political effort to explaining and sustaining the existing relationship | Accept the explanation, withdraw, continue negotiation, or remain unresolved | Current status remains, while the member-specific risk reflects the actual dispute |

The master cannot buy a blanket cancellation of the unconditional grant.
Retention does not secretly remove points or force a lower status.
Native political and economic methods of influence remain available under their existing systems, with an explicit compact-breach consequence where a promised restraint is violated.

## Lawful status settlements

A higher-status offer is valid only if the existing relationship owner exposes a legal route.
The offer displays every remaining native cost and requirement.
The subject supplies the required affirmative action, and the normal owner executes the transition.
Agreement does not bypass a score threshold, consent rule, focus requirement, war gate, or native political price.

If the native route cannot be executed safely through an event adapter, the offer directs the player to the existing action and recognizes its completed result.
Recognition is based on the actual new status and the matching relationship, not on pressing an offer button.
For AI countries, the adapter must respect the actual ladder's native AI restrictions or obtain a reviewed owner-specific behavior rule.

A legal independence settlement closes the subject's petition and transfers the country's next opportunities to the appropriate independence owner.
It preserves existing territory, units, leaders, and focus content unless that owner has a separate accepted reason to change them.
It does not instantiate the country again.
Its already-earned negotiated-settlement aftermath remains one domestic assessment for the same living country at the original due time.
That assessment carries no former-master authority or continuing claim over the independent country's status.

## Cooperation compacts

A compact offers a meaningful settlement to a subject that values security or is not ready to take a higher status immediately.
Its **90-day implementation period** begins only after both parties accept the same current terms.
The master delivers its aggregate political commitment under Part 4.
Separately accepted targeted administrative assistance is optional, has its own cost and recipient limit, and alone supplies the master-assistance risk adjustment and additional recovery benefit.
The subject explicitly begins its civil implementation program.
Both refrain from Event 117 coercion or confrontation against one another while the compact remains valid.

After 90 days of proven implementation, the subject earns an additional **10 percent of its entry interval W in native autonomy points**.
This installment is awarded once per member and petition, with native clipping and ordinary legal gates intact.
It does not refresh Self-Government Momentum, refund the opening grant, or automatically promote the country.
There is no recurring dividend after the compact ends.

For one firing using the same frozen member-entry W and uninterrupted valid work, the nominal target total from the baseline and a completed compact is 35 percent of that interval.
With a complete momentum window it becomes 55 percent, before ordinary native changes and any cap behavior.
This makes cooperation a meaningful way to advance while preserving the continuing relationship.

Accepting a compact closes that member's demand in the petition immediately, while the finite implementation commitment remains visible.
Failure to deliver an agreed concession can break the compact and remove the unearned installment.
Already granted points remain with the country.
Delivered support and elapsed opportunity costs cannot be refunded as political power or equipment that was never paid.

For a locked relationship, stabilization assistance can still be politically useful, but it is not proof that the universal autonomy promise has been fulfilled.
The compatibility requirement in Part 1 remains open until a lawful native-score use is established.
Do not silently substitute a stabilization-only compact for the specified native autonomy installment.

## Retention, refusal, and nonparticipation

A master retaining the present arrangement commits a 90-day political-effort burden.
The burden is a five-percent reduction in political-power gain for one or two active member commitments and ten percent for three or more, under Part 4's aggregate rule.
Military pressure affects the master's willingness to accept that burden, rather than silently changing its price.
This is a finite rate burden, not an upfront fee that makes a depleted master unable to choose.
It creates no negative stockpile and supplies no points to another actor.

A subject can accept the current arrangement, continue asking for workable terms, or stand down from the special petition.
Standing down retains its initial points and all ordinary legal routes.
It accepts that this petition produces no compact installment or forced concession.
Quiet withdrawal before an agreement or actual public breakdown creates no petition aftermath assessment.
This is an ineligible assessment, not a zero chance raised by the probability floor.

An explicit blanket refusal increases the risk of organized opposition.
Silence at the negotiation deadline closes unresolved demands as unanswered, without committing either side to a war or a purchase.
Unanswered demands use the ordinary unresolved-risk branch.
A popup timing out is not consent to a compact, a status change, or military escalation.

## Partial settlements and fair late entry

The petition can end with different results for different countries.
Some members may change status, others may complete a compact, and others may leave unresolved.
One member's refusal does not undo another's completed settlement.
The master receives an aggregate outcome that names unresolved relationships without treating every subject as a single bloc.

A later firing gives all current subjects another baseline grant.
An already open petition keeps its original deadline and settled-member receipts.
It does not reopen a completed member, restart costs, or reroll that member's aftermath.
New current subjects may join the open petition against the same master.

If a new member has fewer than 30 days left before the original deadline, it receives a short-notice protection.
It can accept a current valid offer, negotiate within the remaining time, or withdraw.
Unanswered expiry alone produces no adverse roll for that late member, and the interface states the remaining opportunity clearly.
An actual coercive act or explicit rejection remains a real political choice, with its normal disclosed consequences.
The original deadline is not extended, and no deferred petition is silently queued.

When no petition is open, a later firing can create a new one from the current world.
A subject honoring its own D08 stand-down in that exact relationship is recorded as withdrawn from a fresh petition.
It receives the new baseline and the stated momentum treatment, but no fresh demand, offer acceptance, or II aftermath arises during that stand-down.
Expiry does not queue automatic participation, which requires a genuinely later firing after the stand-down ends.
Its previously earned assessments keep their original due times and finite-tail rules.
A master's own stand-down cannot exclude its subjects or settle their claims.
It restricts that master's coercive and retention pressure, while leaving voluntary concessions available to subjects that have not withdrawn themselves.
An already active compact remains the one compact for its exact relationship, even if the former group has closed administratively.
A new petition recognizes that existing agreement without new terms, another harmful draw, or another installment for the same work.
After that compact actually finishes, a genuinely later firing may support a new compact with a fresh full service period.
Historical completed settlements remain evidence for achievements and prior-conflict factors, but do not give a former master authority over a new relationship.

## A real chance that demands become unrest

Each member receives at most one aftermath assessment for a petition.
The assessment occurs 30 days after its agreement, explicit refusal, withdrawal following a public breakdown, or unresolved deadline.
If an apparent agreement visibly breaks before that assessment, use the broken-agreement state.
Later breaches can end the remaining compact and damage relations, but cannot manufacture another random draw in the same petition.
An assessment already earned by agreement or genuine public breakdown survives voluntary stand-down, with the shorter harm tail in Part 4.
A canceled confirmation, expired unsigned offer, or neutral loss of authority is not itself a public breakdown.
Quiet withdrawal does not cancel an otherwise valid momentum assessment.

The proposed starting chances are **10 percent after a negotiated agreement**, **20 percent after an unanswered or unsuccessful negotiation**, and **30 percent after an explicit blanket rejection or a broken compact**.
Apply current member conditions at assessment and clamp the result between **5 and 50 percent**.

| Condition | Adjustment in percentage points |
| --- | --- |
| Subject stability below 40 percent | +10 |
| A separate suppression or broken political promise in this exact relationship, within the rolling 180 days at assessment including the exact boundary, and before this petition began | +10 |
| At least three current same-master members retain an unresolved disposition from this petition at assessment | +5 |
| Active Autonomy Shock or an existing independently owned domestic crisis | +10, with overlap handling below |
| A valid civil program is underway | −10 |
| Separately accepted targeted master assistance is being delivered | −10 |
| Subject stability at least 70 percent and the agreement has been honored | −5 |

Acceptance can provoke hardline loyalists, separatists, or rival movements.
Rejection can produce quiet disappointment or a stronger organized opposition.
Neither response guarantees peace or guarantees violence.
The outcome is grounded in the specific subject and relationship rather than a uniform penalty to every country in the group.

The current broken compact supplies the 30 percent starting chance and cannot also supply the previous-conflict adjustment.
The unresolved-member count survives formal closure of the negotiation window, but a later real settlement, independence, transfer, or invalid relationship removes that member from the count.
An active shock and the country's current low stability may both matter because one represents an ongoing administrative burden and the other measures the present political condition.
Consequently the two evolutions' harmful results are conditionally linked, and their chances must not be multiplied as if independent.

After lawful negotiated independence, the surviving agreement assessment keeps its original due time and ten-percent starting chance.
Use the independent country's current stability, valid civil work, current domestic crisis, and eligible factual conflict history from before the petition.
Keep the honored-agreement stability adjustment where the terms were honored up to their lawful termination.
The old unresolved-group count and former-master assistance do not apply.
A newly accepted independent-country aid contract may qualify only through its actual owner.
Lawful termination of subject-only duties is not a broken compact.
Other unused relationship assessments cancel, and this country-owned aftermath cannot reopen demands, restore subject status, or authorize confrontation with the former master.
If another crisis owner accepts the same consequence, transfer or retire the assessment through its acknowledged contract once.

The main harmful result is a **90-day political unrest episode** with a temporary stability burden and one context-appropriate output or administrative burden.
Use the same ranges and recovery logic as Part 2.
The description may identify strikes, mutinous sentiment, factional challenges, or contested authority, but it cannot claim casualties, destroyed equipment, or defected divisions unless an owning system actually produced them.

## When both evolutions contribute to the same crisis

One country can receive both opportunities, but it has only one active Event 117 domestic recovery episode at a time.
If petition unrest lands during Autonomy Shock, it changes the political cause and may replace the current burden with the more severe applicable variant.
Do not add two identical stability penalties or two construction penalties.
Any extension is limited to 30 days beyond the existing recovery deadline, with a maximum continuous Event 117 recovery duration of 120 days from its first onset.
If one disruption ends at the exact boundary where another starts, preserve that first-onset clock because no unaffected interval elapsed.
Only an actual interval without Event 117 domestic disruption starts a new continuous-harm clock.
An outcome at the continuous deadline records its factual result without extending the expired burden or adding a new disorder increment for harm that was not applied.
The two evolution receipts retain their distinct histories, while the disorder and Chaos contribution remain a single owned disturbance.

If another event owns the domestic crisis, Autonomy offers support or political choices through that owner where an accepted interface exists.
It does not create a parallel civil war, mutate another event's flags, or claim a second independent cleanup authority.

## Local independence confrontation

Unrest creates an opportunity for a deliberate local confrontation only when an actual unresolved political dispute remains and the existing independence or rebellion owner can support it.
The opening is never automatic on a failed roll.
The subject must choose to escalate, and the master receives a final 30-day opportunity to offer a workable settlement or stand down from coercion.
The subject retains a unilateral civilian withdrawal route throughout this stage.

The local owner must validate the subject's actual existence, relationship, territorial and military viability, current wars, consent carriers, and its ability to produce a bounded settlement.
A country with no viable military position can bargain or withdraw.
It is not forced into a newly fabricated army or a doomed automatic war.

If both parties continue resisting a settlement and the owning system's conditions are satisfied, that owner may begin its limited independence confrontation.
The intended political objective is the named subject's status toward the named master.
The owner defines any necessary faction participation and makes that involvement visible before commitment.
Event 117 does not promise that a native war can be isolated if the actual faction rules would expand it.

A usable owner contract must provide an initial settlement review by 90 days of fighting and a bounded decision by 180 days.
It must cover recognized independence, a negotiated higher-autonomy relationship, a return to the existing relationship with a finite settlement, and interruption by a larger war or political collapse.
An exact bilateral settlement cannot silently end unrelated wars or erase a separate civil war.

**That reusable local confrontation contract is not established by the inspected repository.** The current Event 063 implementation is a legacy direct release with a former-master response, rather than a safe general petition-to-war API.
The relevant newer plans also distinguish intended behavior from implemented capability.
The full local branch is specified here, but its implementation remains blocked until the named owner exposes and proves the required contract.
If it is unavailable, do not present a working escalation button or substitute IMRO's worldwide uprising.
Retain civilian negotiation and recovery, and report the local military branch as incomplete.

## Resolution

A peaceful agreement ends the relevant petition and leaves the agreed legal relationship in place.
An unaccepted offer expires without payment or involuntary consent.
An unresolved petition closes with its limited aftermath and leaves normal autonomy routes available.
A local confrontation hands authority to its owner and receives a terminal result from that owner.
Independence resolves every outstanding claim based on the former subject status.
An already-earned negotiated-settlement domestic assessment is an aftermath of that country's decision, not an outstanding former-master claim.
Nothing in this evolution activates IMRO's shared worldwide crisis.
