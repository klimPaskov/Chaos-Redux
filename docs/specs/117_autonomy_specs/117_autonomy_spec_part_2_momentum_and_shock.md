# 117 Autonomy: Self-Government Momentum

Self-Government Momentum gives the initial award a short period in which it can develop into sustained political progress.
Subjects have time to organize their administration and decide whether to pursue a higher status.
Masters can support a stable adjustment or spend their attention preserving an arrangement they value.
The opportunity carries a real possibility that institutions fail to keep pace.

## Entry and duration

The proposed entry tier is Chaos level 2.
Eligibility and settings are evaluated for the next firing.
Once that firing begins, its recipients and evolution state are fixed.
Enabling the evolution later does not reach backward into an earlier baseline grant.
Disabling it prevents later activations and leaves an already committed episode to resolve normally through its finite course.

Each newly affected subject receives a **120-day momentum window**.
The additional native autonomy growth targets **20 percent of the entry interval W across the full window**, above whatever ordinary growth or loss the relationship already produces.
The daily design rate is W ÷ 600, subject to verification of a compatible native growth route.
Freeze W at entry so an ordinary promotion does not cause the event to recalculate a larger reward from its own success.
Fractional delivery must preserve the intended cumulative total at native precision.
For example, W of 500 means 100 additional points across 120 days, not 120 points from rounding each day's contribution up to one.
A private rounding remainder, if required, serves only exact delivery and cannot become a public currency or an overflow reserve.

This is an additive positive contribution, not a promise that the net autonomy total rises every day.
An existing lawful downward influence can still offset it.
Tooltips separate the event's contribution from the country's net progression.
The bonus cannot unlock a prohibited status or replace a native point gate.

The full opening opportunity therefore targets 45 percent of one entry interval, combining the immediate 25 percent grant with 20 percent over time.
Ordinary native activities remain relevant to the result.
A subject near a threshold may act early, while one far from it still gains a substantial foundation.

## Repeated firings during momentum

A later firing always gives its new baseline award.
If the recipient already has an active momentum window, that window keeps its original growth rate, end date, and single shock assessment.
The new firing does not stack a second rate, reset the deadline, queue another window, or create another shock roll.
The effect summary tells the player that the existing momentum continues for its remaining duration.

A recipient with no active window starts a new 120-day window when a later firing includes this evolution.
This rule makes each active episode finite and keeps repeated grants from preventing recovery indefinitely.
The balance review must include a repeat on the last day, since the continued window will then be brief.
The full point award remains the repeat's useful immediate component.

## The opening choices

The subject may continue ordinary politics without taking a special action.
It may instead begin a civil consolidation program, which protects against institutional shock at a visible economic opportunity cost.
Its master may offer temporary administrative assistance, which the subject can accept without surrendering autonomy points.
Neither actor must pay a stockpile or political-power fee merely to keep a safe response available.

These choices are commitments to use existing capacity.
They do not create a new currency or a public institutional-strength meter.
The player sees native autonomy, the remaining time, the assessed risk band, and the specific factors behind it.

## One assessment, based on the actual country

Assess Autonomy Shock once on **day 30** of a newly started momentum window.
The opening period gives both governments time to make a meaningful response before the risk is resolved.
The roll uses current conditions at that assessment, with entry history retained only for facts that genuinely need it, such as earlier conflict.
The game records the result once.
Reloading, reopening a report, or receiving another grant cannot reroll it.

The proposed probability starts at **10 percent**, then uses the following additive percentage-point adjustments.
Clamp the final probability between **5 and 40 percent**.

| Condition at the assessment | Adjustment | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stability below 50 percent | +10 | Government has less room to absorb the change |
| Stability below 30 percent | +10 more | Severe fragility adds to the ordinary instability factor |
| Active defensive war, or recent capitulation and recovery still in progress | +10 | Administrative capacity is already under exceptional strain |
| A real prior suppression, broken agreement, or independence conflict involving this relationship in the preceding 180 days, including the exact 180-day boundary | +10 | The political dispute has an established history |
| Civil consolidation began before the assessment and remains valid | −10 | The government has committed capacity to the transition |
| Separately accepted targeted master assistance is being delivered | −10 | Additional support reduces the burden |
| Stability at least 70 percent and no current relationship conflict | −5 | A settled administration is better placed to manage progress |

Do not use culture, ethnicity, country tag, continent, or a generic special-country label as a risk proxy.
Recent conflict must refer to a real recorded outcome, rather than mere receipt of another Autonomy event.
One fact cannot be charged twice under differently named modifiers.
The current defensive war does not also qualify as a prior conflict merely because it began recently.
The master's ordinary compact-wide political effort alone does not qualify as targeted assistance.
The floor preserves a real risk even for well-managed cases, and the ceiling leaves success possible in a fragile country.

Risk is dynamic before the draw and fixed afterward.
Display an exact percentage where the implementation can faithfully expose it, with a plain explanation of its factors.
Do not show a smooth probability meter whose apparent precision exceeds the actual calculation.

## Successful momentum

If no shock occurs, growth continues to the original deadline.
There is no extra success payout for simply waiting out the timer.
The useful reward is the autonomy progress and the legal choices it enables.
Any native status change retains its normal costs, rules, and owner.

Successful consolidation completes its stated 90-day service without minting a second autonomy reward.
Delivered targeted assistance can end the recipient's harm after 60 days, while the master's accepted 90-day service continues under Part 4.
The country can preserve cooperation or seek greater independence without being scored as a failed participant for choosing either outcome.

## Autonomy Shock

A shock creates a **90-day domestic recovery episode**.
Its presentation follows the country's actual situation and selects one main disruption, rather than stacking every possible calamity.
The growth window keeps running until its original end.
The initial award is never confiscated.

| Working disruption label | Suitable context | Proposed temporary consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Administrative overload | A functioning but overstretched civilian administration | −5 percentage points of stability and −10 percent construction speed |
| Industrial dispute | A country with meaningful active production and labor conflict | −5 percentage points of stability and −10 percent factory output |
| Government fracture | Recent political confrontation or a very weak government | −10 percentage points of stability, with no additional production penalty |

Treat stability effects as temporary modifier burdens so recovery removes the burden rather than granting an unrelated stability windfall.
Use one appropriate variant and one event-owned disruption binding.
If the industrial variant has no practical consumer, use the administrative or political variant.
A stateless government, exiled government, or country already under a more important crisis needs an owner-aware disposition instead of a meaningless factory penalty.

The source, magnitude, remaining duration, and recovery options must be visible.
The event does not directly kill population, destroy factories, delete divisions, or start a civil war to dramatize this roll.
If another system later produces such an outcome, that system owns it.

## Recovery and tradeoffs

Civil consolidation is the default active recovery route.
It commits 90 days of civilian attention under the exact cost model in Part 4.
For recovery, it immediately reduces the event's stability burden by five percentage points and its ten-percent economic disruption to five percent.
That smaller economic burden is the recovery program's price, with a five-percent political-output burden used for political-only fracture.
Preventive and compact work uses a five-percent construction burden, or its meaningful political-output alternative.
One civil program applies across these roles, without a second penalty for the same work.
The player starts the program explicitly, and completion is proved by that program's uninterrupted valid service.
Merely beginning with high stability does not count as having completed it.

A master can offer assistance to a named subject.
The master accepts a temporary 5 percent construction-speed burden for 90 days, and the subject receives the specified reduction in event-owned disruption or earlier recovery after 60 days of delivered help.
Assistance has a concrete benefit, with no fictitious equipment shipment and no automatic debit from a resource the master does not possess.
Where the construction burden has no meaningful consumer, use an explicit political-effort burden with the same finite service window and disclose it before commitment.
The implementation must prove the chosen modifier route before the action is considered available.

An emergency stand-down remains free in spendable resources.
The subject withdraws its Event 117 pressure actions for 90 days and accepts disruption until the earlier of its original expiry or a 30-day tail, while keeping all native points and ordinary legal status rights.
This prevents a poor or defeated country from being trapped by an unaffordable response.
It does not promise immediate removal of all harm.
Part 4 defines the exact program burden, immediate containment, support limits, and early relief so recovery does not require an unproductive full wait.

Suppression remains an action of the actual domestic or relationship owner that exposes it.
That owner defines its legal capacity, relief, costs, relations consequences, and risks.
Event 117 observes a genuine suppression outcome as factual conflict history under its dated risk rules, without inventing a separate suppression button or promising earlier relief.
The ordinary Event 117 hardline choice is the explicit retention or rejection policy in Parts 3 and 4.
An observed suppression does not create a new shock roll within the same momentum episode.

## Freedom, transfer, and other interruptions

Independence ends subject-specific growth immediately and closes pending demands against the former master.
A domestic shock already underway may continue for its remaining term because the administrative problem does not disappear with the legal relationship.
The independent country keeps access to its local recovery action.
The former master loses unilateral authority to change its episode.
Any assistance that continues must use an explicitly accepted independent-country relationship through its owner.

A master transfer ends the old master's offer and pressure authority.
It does not restart the country's momentum timer or create a second draw.
The new relationship is reflected in later petitions and later firings.

Annexation ends active country effects for that incarnation.
If the tag later reappears, it does not inherit stale commitments or unpaid rewards from the annexed government.
A current civil war or an IMRO-owned worldwide crisis hands political authority to that owner as specified in Part 6.

## Containment and Chaos

The points, growth window, assessment, and successful management add no direct Chaos.
An actual Autonomy Shock can create a small owned disorder contribution under the budget in Part 5.
It cannot be counted a second time when Common Demands describes the same disturbance.
Recovery may remove only the event's own outstanding contribution after a real completed recovery.
It cannot reverse a war, independence, or other outcome counted by a shared system.
