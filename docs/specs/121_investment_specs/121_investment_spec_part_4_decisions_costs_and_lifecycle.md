# Investment specification, part 4: Decisions, costs and lifecycle

## 1. The agreement the player makes

Investment is a sequence of paid construction choices. The government chooses a sector and legal sites, authorizes one phase at a time, and receives the work that phase has actually earned. A larger promise does not pay for itself. Inspection consumes political authority and industrial time. Acceleration commits more civilian capacity. Reducing scope gives up named future outputs. Wind-down stops the remaining burden while preserving work already delivered.

This part controls decisions, funding, timers, settlement and lifecycle integrity. [Part 1](121_investment_spec_part_1_core.md) controls identity and the main loop. [Part 2](121_investment_spec_part_2_sector_projects.md) controls output packages and their assigned work. [Part 3](121_investment_spec_part_3_evolutions_and_consequences.md) controls evolution incidents, operating hazards and consequences. Labels here are working labels, not final localisation.

One country owns one active charter, including any remaining anomalous operation. Baseline scope has one main site and at most one supporting site. Megaproject scope has two to four sites. Finished construction does not free the charter slot while its bounded first-service authorization window or anomalous operating license remains active. Ordinary decisions, formatted summaries, a static category picture and native missions present the system.

Work Complete measures verified construction work against the accepted scope. Exposure measures unresolved procurement, supervision and technical promises. Exposure is not the probability of an incident. Held capacity, deadlines, phase names and delivered buildings are ordinary status facts. They do not become additional meters.

## 2. Quote the real industrial commitment

### Work and capacity

The funding unit is a civilian-factory-day. Holding three genuinely usable civilian factories for 20 eligible days pays 60 civilian-factory-days. A reservation quote, an intended future hold and a timer reaching zero do not prove that payment occurred.

| Ordinary envelope | Normal integer hold | Principal units | Nominal days | Nominal work |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Small | 1 | 1 | 300 | 300 |
| Standard | 3 | 2 | 360 | 1,080 |
| Large | 5 | 3 | 420 | 2,100 |

Special industry multiplies nominal work and duration by 1.5. Apply the sector and delivery-policy factors first. Then multiply each site's preceding nominal work share once by one plus the sum of its contextual additions: 10% for a non-core site, 10% when its infrastructure is below half the installed native maximum, and 10% while the payer is at war. The additions cap at 30%. Freeze these factors on acceptance. There is no further supply or geography multiplier. Real supply and legal control remain eligibility requirements, and later interruptions use the declared pause or incident rules.

Megaproject envelopes and anomalous additions use part 3's separately costed scope. These are proposed subsidized Investment prices, not native construction costs. Implementation must compare their economic value with installed building costs without quietly replacing the accepted design.

Before purchase, assign work to each site, phase and promised output. Nominal site shares are 25% mobilization, 50% works and 25% commissioning. For each phase, divide its proposed work by the selected integer hold, round the duration upward to five days, then set payable work to that hold multiplied by the rounded duration. Freeze the sum. The nominal shares guide allocation rather than promising an exact ratio after rounding.

For example, Special Small starts with 450 nominal days at one held factory. Its phase quotes are 115, 225 and 115 days, totaling 455 payable days. Display the accepted 455-day sum and its actual cost. Do not advertise 450 and charge 455 invisibly. At an amendment, round only prospective remaining work. Preserve the actual paid record and existing deadlines that the amendment does not legitimately change.

Freeze actor, charter, sector, sites, native or no-DLC branch, promised outputs, output work shares, contextual factors, initial usable civilian capacity, capacity allocation and accepted work. Site selection and policy preview can refresh an unaccepted quote. A material changed quote at the purchase click returns to a clear revised proposal before reserving capacity. It cannot silently buy a larger obligation.

### Policies and amendments

Verified tender starts Exposure at 10 and adds 20% work at the normal hold. Matched capital starts at 25 with normal work. Accelerated delivery starts at 40, requires the upward-rounded integer equivalent of 150% of the normal hold, and adds at least 10% prospective work. Its hold cannot exceed twice the original normal capacity envelope. A Small charter therefore needs two civilians to accelerate. Higher capacity divided into the larger work requirement determines speed. There is no additional hidden time discount.

Megaproject Fever adds 15 Exposure. Impossible-method contributions follow part 3. A policy upgrade adds only the increase above the highest policy risk previously accepted. Choosing a safer future policy does not automatically remove prior Exposure. Work amendments affect unfinished work only. Inspection is the paid route for reducing existing procurement risk.

Each of three contextual conditions contributes 10 Exposure once per charter when first included in accepted scope: any non-core site, any selected land site adjacent to hostile-controlled land, and peak accepted total hold at least 50% of frozen initial usable civilian capacity. Context contributions cap at 30. An amendment can introduce a previously absent condition once. Removal, recapture, callback or reacceptance neither repeats nor automatically subtracts it. These Exposure conditions are distinct from the work multipliers.

At a phase boundary the government may authorize a smaller integer hold, with the same outstanding work and a longer accepted completion time. Capacity shortage during an already funded phase pauses the affected allocation until a revised or restored hold is authorized. The original promise does not become cheaper because fewer factories are available. Expected funded duration targets 180–720 days for ordinary non-special work, 270–1080 for ordinary Special and 360–1440 for Fever. A prospective quote outside its normal envelope offers a smaller viable scope or an explicitly documented exceptional balance choice before purchase. Never shorten accepted work invisibly.

The country adapter must measure genuinely usable civilian capacity after consumer goods and other commitments. A new hold needs an inclusive check against that remainder. Replacing an existing Investment hold checks the final total while crediting only the exact hold being replaced. Neither double-subtract the same reservation nor count another site's, another system's or an anomalous service hold as free capacity.

The proposal clock starts when the selected actor receives its offer. Returning to sectors, changing sites or changing policy never restarts its 90 days. The offer reserves the country's single pending charter slot without charging capacity. Decline and unfunded expiry close it at zero charge with a 180-day cooldown.

Selecting one focuses its ordinary controls. Do not repeat four action rows beneath every site. This selector is normally the fifth row, with a genuine extension allowing the exceptional sixth.

Inspection's political-power charge uses the exact universal ordinary-cost quote, including valid discounts. Its industrial service time does not. The quoted fee buys the inspection service once. Its fee becomes nonrefundable when the accepted service installs its actual hold. If execution rejects before that commitment, restore a proven debit once if one occurred. Consumed service time is lost. Do not create a second fee when interrupted service resumes under the same receipt.

An audit allowance belongs to the original site-phase identity. Investigation of that phase's incident consumes or replaces the same allowance. Relocation, subdivision, a new generation, a policy change or an amendment does not create a fresh allowance for the same work. A newly added, genuinely separate future phase may have its own allowance. Existing evidence cannot be sold again under another response label. Reuse completed relevant audit proof at no second fee or Exposure reduction, or explain that the exhausted allowance cannot buy another audit of the same work.

## 4. Incidents replace ordinary controls

Ordinary checkpoints occur once at 30% and 70% of the charter's work. Megaproject checkpoints belong to the charter as specified in part 3, never to every site separately. Record each consumed checkpoint before resolving its outcome. A revised denominator, duplicate callback or recapture cannot recreate a roll. At most one incident is live. Reconcile other due checkpoints once in their preserved order after resolution, without dropping or repeatedly rolling them.

Exposure bands use the authored probabilities of 5%, 15%, 30% and 45% for 0–24, 25–49, 50–74 and 75–100. The initial conditional mix is 40 delay, 35 overrun and 25 corruption. These are design probabilities requiring implementation validation. Conditions can reweight applicable issue types, with genuine corruption fixed for that case.

Each ordinary incident adds its own unresolved Exposure once: delay adds 5, overrun adds 10 and a corruption case adds 20 before its hidden truth is known. Remove only that case's remaining contribution after its prescribed corrective work is paid and verified, or investigation and an actually funded viable settlement resolve it. Quote acceptance is insufficient. If failure closes the case, evaluate its preserved critical warning and actual material loss before retiring the contribution.

| Incident response | Availability and cost | Outcome and lockout | AI intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fund revised completion | Exact current incident and legal remaining work. Reserve the revised affordable hold for the revised work. | Accept delay work, overrun or justified rework. Ordinary anchors are 15–45 additional funded days, 15–30% remaining work or 20–40% affected unfinished rework. | Preserve valuable scope when the revised price is still worthwhile. |
| Investigate and replace | Unused phase audit, or continuation of its already paid investigation. Same inspection fee and funded-service basis. | Resolve frozen evidence, remove only an unjustified future surcharge and recognize proven reusable work. No refund of consumed capacity. | Prefer it when fraudulent claims or unresolved supervision make blind payment wasteful. |
| Reduce unfinished scope | A smaller legal useful result exists. No preview fee, followed by its own accepted remaining-work hold. | Drop named undelivered extras first. Minimal completion may sacrifice support or dividend. No full-scope success credit. | Keep a feasible principal result when extras are no longer affordable. |
| Wind down | Always available for the true owner. Zero exit charge. | Settle paid and delivered work, release holds and close affected unfinished scope. | Use when continued expenditure or danger exceeds the remaining value. |

A financing incident requiring a new quote pauses affected construction and releases that allocation. Other lawful funded sites may continue. It has a 90-day response window and one 30-day extension only for a real shortage, both inside that site's remaining cumulative dormant allowance. The shorter applicable deadline controls. Expiry automatically winds down affected unfinished work. Investigation and another menu do not reset the clock.

At the first unresolved Exposure of at least 75, disclose the 90-calendar-day critical financing warning. Accumulate only days at 75 or above with an unresolved material obligation. A pause in that condition preserves elapsed critical days. Genuine resolution ends that case. National shock also requires part 3's material-loss threshold. At simultaneous deadlines, settle actual scope loss and evaluate the preserved warning before cleanup removes its evidence.

For example, a National Fever charter with 20 initial usable civilians has accelerated policy 40 plus Fever 15, totaling 55 before incidents. Its accepted accelerated hold of nine is below half of 20, so it adds no capacity context risk. With no other contextual additions and at least 2,400 factory-days already paid, a corruption case adds 20 to reach 75. After 90 unresolved critical days, wind-down of two genuinely funded linked sites with at least 2,400 actual written-off factory-days can satisfy part 3's first national-loss threshold. Earlier successful settlement, inspection or wind-down prevents that timed conjunction when its conditions are not met.

An anomalous safety warning instead has part 3's justified 30-day emergency response deadline. Suspend its abnormal output and release its service hold during that safety pause. Containment or restart needs fresh paid authorization. An unanswered hazardous warning cannot leave benefits operating for free.

## 5. One owner controls time and paid work

Maintain one scheduled owner callback for the active charter and at most four registered sites. It may run daily to reconcile real capacity, eligible funded intervals and due settlement. Do not introduce a recurring all-country or all-state scan. Stop the loop when construction, anomalous service and bounded settlement are all finished.

A later aftermath-recovery job belongs to its residual distress receipt and runs only for its bounded authorization. It does not reopen a charter or keep the construction callback alive indefinitely.

Store absolute dates, the last accounted boundary and relevant control-change cutoffs. Accrue work only for a proven interval during which the authorized allocation existed, the site was eligible and the payer could actually fund it. Callback count does not represent elapsed time. A delayed callback must stop at intervening capture, capacity loss, an incident or phase completion. It cannot retroactively fund an unaffordable interval.

The native capacity adapter must detect real loss of usable industry. It cannot infer solvency from a nominal hold that exceeds the surviving pool, or add a hold back to a clamped free-capacity number and call the result available. Reconcile whole authorized allocations in a stable order, prioritizing service safety and near-complete viable work. Pause allocations that cannot be covered, preserving their paid work. Resumption or a smaller hold is a new authorization, not automatic unpaid progress.

Use native missions as visible deadlines and objectives, synchronized with the owner records. Normally show one construction or funding timer and one incident or operation timer. Three is the exceptional maximum across the entire charter, not per site.

It survives repeated pauses and later phases. Time under genuine funded construction or accepted inspection service does not consume dormant grace. Expiry closes the affected unfinished scope, not unrelated completed assets. An already expired deadline cannot be revived by a late callback or same-day menu change.

## 6. Delivery, ownership and partial success

Deliver only the output named by a paid work share. Preassigned output shares cannot charge the same factory-day twice. An enabling improvement or permitted early principal unit becomes permanent only after its full share and phase conditions are satisfied. The remaining work continues to owe its own share. Native facilities, the exclusive no-DLC bundle and research grants use their correct separate delivery receipts.

Immediately before a grant, recheck state identity, ownership, control, supply and all exact building constraints, including coastal province, slot, cap and facility-group restrictions where applicable. Do not silently truncate a multi-unit grant at a cap. Complete valid individual output transactions and leave blocked outputs visibly unsettled. A requested level is not an observed added level.

New slot loss pauses the undelivered output and releases its unnecessary hold. Offer a prequoted legal support substitution, a smaller explicit settlement or relocation of unfinished scope. Use the existing finite funding and dormant deadlines. A replacement site inherits the original work, audit and checkpoint history. It does not create another charter or refresh the 180-day allowance for that work.

Temporary occupation pauses affected uncommissioned work and releases its allocation. The same payer can resume after recovering control through new authorization. Permanent ownership transfer retires that site's unearned original obligations. The successor receives physical buildings through ordinary state custody but no purchase, refund, brownfield or achievement receipt. Annexation retires the payer's charter, cleans its registered sites and invalidates delayed callbacks. A surviving ROOT or annexer must never substitute for the vanished payer. A cosmetic identity change in the same country preserves valid proof. A new civil-war or released country cannot inherit authority from copied flags or a copied receipt pointer.

An ordinary completion dividend requires continuing control of its qualifying site and keeps its original 180-day expiry. Recapture restores only remaining entitlement. Fever has one program-level dividend receipt. Full mandatory-network completion grants the chosen route's part 2 amplitude for 180 days. Agreed reduced terminal completion with at least one usable commissioned principal site grants that amplitude for 90 days. Early sites grant physical assets only. Failure or abandonment without agreed reduced commissioning grants no dividend.

At Fever terminal commissioning, freeze the retained qualifying site set. The dividend requires control of every site in that set, pauses on lost control and never restarts expiry. No early-site timer is copied or renewed at network completion. Precision's temporary production-efficiency benefit belongs to the consolidated commissioning-dividend family. Refresh or replace applicable same-type contributions without stacking a fourth visible reward family.

Classify endings as original full completion, agreed reduced completion, partial wind-down or failure. Preserve that distinction in history and achievements. Reaching 100 after cutting the denominator cannot imply completion of the original scope. Full-network achievement credit requires full original scope. A03 checks this single charter's Exposure of at most 24 at final full-program commissioning, not each site's separate completion. Ordinary construction and these bookkeeping transitions add zero direct event Chaos.

## 7. Finite exit, refunds and brownfield work

Wind-down immediately stops future construction authority and releases the relevant Investment reservations. Record delivered outputs and actual paid work, settle any genuinely refundable independent payment, assess reusable unfinished work, record the outcome and apply the terminal cooldown. Construction exit requires no further payment and never waits for an external crisis service to release its factories. An active anomaly must separately reach safe expiry, paid containment or emergency closure. Construction wind-down cannot erase that service case, bypass its emergency quote or claim final charter closure while it remains active.

A wholly unfunded decline or expiry receives 180 days. Every charter that accepted a positive phase commitment receives 540 days on funded terminal closure, including immediate abandonment, reduced completion and failure. An anomalous license or its bounded first-service authorization window keeps the charter active until it settles. A background acknowledgment retry cannot postpone an otherwise completed economic release or restart the cooldown.

Consumed civilian-factory-days and inspection-service days are never reimbursed. Releasing a future hold is not a refund. For any separately consumed payment explicitly eligible for refund, use its original actual paid amount and discount, exact original payer and unused refund receipt. Record refund success once. A missing payer, nonpositive amount or mismatched record cannot pay a successor or create a guessed quote. Failed preflight makes no debit and therefore needs no fictitious refund.

### Brownfield credit

A settled incomplete site may retain one source-owned brownfield credit for physically reusable unfinished work. It belongs to the original payer and that exact state. The payer must again own and control the site, outside its cooldown, under a genuinely new eligible charter. A transfer to another owner does not transfer the credit. Annexation cannot cash it for the annexer.

At settlement, identify the particular unfinished preparation or construction component that survives. Its reusable amount cannot exceed the verified actual paid work attributable to it. Exclude inspection, delay overhead, service operation, disproven procurement, delivered output shares, compensated work and credit inherited from an earlier charter. If reuse cannot be established, issue zero.

For the later matching work component, usable credit is the lesser of the saved reusable amount and 25% of that component's new precredit work. Apply it once to reduce the new prospective work quote, then perform normal phase rounding. Freeze the resulting payment and output promise. It does not count as new paid work, fund a larger principal package or satisfy a new cash/payment receipt.

Reserve the exact credit to one proposed successor charter. On that charter's first positive actual funded work, guard and consume the credit and its one permitted claim together. A proposal that never funds releases only the claim reservation. Any unused remainder is retired after the claim, rather than split across repeated contracts. Failure of the successor can credit only its own newly paid reusable work, never reissue the inherited amount.

For example, 80 proven reusable factory-days applied to 200 compatible new work can credit at most 50. The remaining 30 cannot become a second claim. The new project still performs its accepted postcredit work. Neither project earns a completion achievement from this settlement.

## 8. Operating licenses and national consequences

The method and operating mode follow part 3's exact inspected and full-risk packages. An inspected prototype adds 20% to remaining relevant work for validation and removes only its method's remaining uncertainty contribution after that work succeeds. Its nominal addition is 20 points, subject to the residual rules below. Uncertified full-risk operation retains an effective Exposure floor of 50. Ordinary procurement inspections cannot remove that floor.

Engineering throughput has a continuing licensed research-speed contribution of 10% ordinary or 15% mega when inspected, and 20% ordinary or 30% mega at full risk. Use the strongest valid same-type contribution in the existing dividend family, never an additive stack with the ordinary 5% engineering dividend. Both native and no-DLC routes require service, custody and an unexpired license. Pause or closure removes the operating contribution. The separate two extra one-use 50% industry research bonuses arrive once at verified anomaly commissioning, with their own receipt. They do not replace continuing research speed or repeat on renewal or validation.

Self-assembly counts frozen ordinary principal units or mega principal allocations identically across native and no-DLC representations, using part 3's ratios and caps. Its extra type is civilian for civilian and Special routes, military for military and dockyard for naval. Freeze legal placement among existing charter sites before purchase, within the same site ceiling. It grants no extra facility, refinery, research coupon or support improvement.

These extra units are not a commissioning grant. They require 30 actual funded operating days and the successful original day-30 safety case. A malfunction leaves that one coupon pending until its paid corrective case succeeds. It does not roll another hazard or issue another coupon. Disappearance or cascade retires it. One-time research rewards and inherited-service exclusions retain their separate receipt rules.

Licensed operation begins only after the whole currently agreed conventional scope supporting the method, and any prototype validation, are paid and commissioned. A reduced agreement decreases an unearned self-assembly basis before its amended quote is accepted. It cannot increase that basis or let an unfunded network determine extra units. At conventional commissioning, freeze the first-service window as the smaller of 90 calendar days and the flagship site's retained unused portion of its cumulative 180-day dormant allowance. Waiting consumes that same allowance. Zero remainder causes immediate unstarted retirement. Authorize the first real service hold before the earlier deadline, with no extra extension, or retire the method without an operating Chaos premium or coupon. Reuse the funding action and mission, with no extra simultaneous row or asset.

The initial license lasts 180 calendar days from activation, with one civilian held for service ordinary or three for a mega flagship. Benefits require legal custody and a valid actual service commitment. Pauses do not stop its absolute expiry. One extension bought before expiry may reach 360 days from original activation, with a fresh service receipt and +10 Exposure. It never renews one-time outputs or checkpoints. Without extension, safe expiry precedes a day-180 hazard roll.

The operating view offers extension, conversion, containment and emergency closure as applicable. A malfunction replaces extension with repair. While awaiting authorization, its output stays off and its ordinary service hold is released. Absolute license expiry still limits the wait. A critical warning instead offers containment, validated restart, explicit hazardous continuation and emergency closure. These replace ordinary construction controls for the selected flagship, within the same row budget.
