# 097 Collaboration
## Part 2: Repeats, escalation, and campaign timing

## The repeating world change

Every legitimate later firing repeats the same global operation against the current eligible country snapshot. Existing networks deepen. Previously unaffected new countries receive their first layer. Countries at the native cap remain at the cap.

There is no separate lifetime Collaboration meter. The history can count how often the event appeared, but that count is not a substitute for the actual value of any directed native relationship.

The event's danger develops in two ways. Repetition strengthens the network values themselves. Evolutions change what a sufficiently established network can accomplish. A campaign can therefore have many modest networks after an early evolved firing, or extremely deep networks after several ordinary firings.

## Grant progression

The table describes the normal cumulative progression with earlier stages enabled. The individual-toggle rules below determine the grant when a stage is deliberately disabled.

| Active capability | Grant on each normal firing | Additional behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | +10 percentage points | Native collaboration benefits |
| Evolution I, Deep Networks | +20 percentage points | Larger grants across the world |
| Evolution II, Administrations in Waiting | +25 percentage points | Early administrative handover in qualifying occupied territory |
| Evolution III, The Fifth Column | +25 percentage points | A losing country can suffer two additional native network breakthroughs |
| Evolution IV, Collaboration Governments | +25 percentage points | A qualifying victor receives a stronger route to native government readiness |

The strongest enabled, actually activated grant capability determines the normal grant. Do not add 10, 20, and 25 together. A firing with Evolution IV active grants 25, not 80.

A normal grant and a Fifth Column breakthrough are different transactions. They must not trigger each other's global effects. A breakthrough against one country never gives everyone else another global layer.

## Evolution requirements

The four Chaos requirements remain exactly those in the brief: 200, 400, 600, and 800. They are availability requirements for the shared evolution framework. Crossing a threshold does not itself mean that the evolution has already occurred. Once an evolution has activated, a later fall in Chaos does not by itself erase that capability. The Chaos requirement governs activation. Explicit configuration changes follow the settings rules below.

Register the evolutions with the existing framework's timing, history, configuration, and pre-evolved first-appearance handling. Use its normal evolution timing contract, including the project's baseline timing where applicable. Do not add an independent calendar roll beside it.

The default campaign develops in order. The individual evolution toggles must still mean what the shared settings promise. Disabling an earlier behavior must not secretly disable every enabled later behavior. Each evolution therefore has a self-contained contribution:

- I changes the normal grant to 20.
- II changes it to 25 and enables early administration.
- III enables the two losing-war breakthroughs. It does not require II's compliance handover to function.
- IV changes the normal grant to 25 and enables government readiness after a qualifying defeat. It can perform that readiness step even when II is disabled.

When an earlier stage is deliberately disabled, a later enabled stage may progress through the shared framework without granting the disabled behavior. For example, a campaign with only III enabled retains the 10-point normal grant and gains the Fifth Column behavior at its proper threshold and activation.

## A late first appearance

A first appearance at high Chaos may arrive with eligible evolutions already activated through the existing pre-evolution process. It still performs **one** global firing. It does not replay a baseline grant and then three or four historical grants to catch up.

The report describes the actual state of the event on that appearance. The history must distinguish a first manifestation with advanced networks from several successive firings that never happened.

If a first firing occurs with II and IV active, it can grant 25 and enable both kinds of administration. There is no retroactive government installation throughout the existing world. Current occupations and already defeated targets receive the bounded eligibility review defined in Parts 3 and 5.

## Saturation is a real endpoint

The design deliberately accepts saturation. A capped network cannot become stronger merely because another evolution needs to feel dramatic. Additional firings may still affect new countries or relationships that lost native collaboration, but they cannot invent uncapped surrender bonuses.

When every valid pair is at the native maximum, the event has no material global grant to make. It should be unavailable to automatic selection until at least one supported pair has room to improve. The implementation must maintain that knowledge through the actual change paths or a bounded selection-time check. It must not perform a permanent daily world scan.

If the native backend does not expose a reliable capped-state check, the implementation must record that limitation rather than pretend every no-op firing mattered. A manually triggered diagnostic no-op can report that no network changed, but it must not create a fresh Chaos award, economic effect, or achievement credit.

## Repeat weighting and cluster membership

Use the shared Minor Repeatable lifecycle. The current mechanics guide describes an initial ordinary weight of 1,000, recovery by 20 per month, and a recovery maximum that halves after each occurrence. Event 097 must use that shared recovery path rather than its own monthly scheduler.

Its Intelligence cluster membership is a real change to the supplied catalog configuration. The supplied cluster is currently marked fire-once. The rework must permit repeatable selection of 097 without making every Intelligence member repeatable or consuming the cluster permanently after the first Collaboration event.

Treat the event as one global occurrence. Do not multiply its event weight changes, timer consequences, history count, or Chaos impact by the number of participating countries. Multiple human countries acknowledging their reports must not count as multiple firings.

## Networks between firings

Native operations continue to increase collaboration under their normal rules. Native changes that legitimately reduce or consume collaboration remain meaningful. Event 097 does not continually restore a hidden minimum between appearances.

War, peace, faction changes, liberation, and government formation do not cause a retrospective global grant. The event's receipts protect against duplicate payment, not against the ordinary lifecycle of the native mechanic.

A country that changes ideology keeps whatever the native system actually preserves. A cosmetic name change does not constitute a new country eligible for historical rewards. A released or newly created political identity receives only benefits valid for its present identity and later firings.

## Switching settings during a campaign

Disabling future automatic appearances stops new normal grants. Disabling an evolution stops new effects owned by that evolution according to the shared configuration's runtime semantics. Neither setting should retroactively reduce real native collaboration already granted, strip earned compliance, dissolve an existing government, or recreate an earlier border.

Pending consequences must revalidate settings before changing the world. A stale message can remain readable, but acknowledging it cannot execute an effect that has since become disabled.

These settings are configuration controls, not a player strategy for gaining and removing bonuses on demand. Re-enabling an evolution must not reset its anti-repeat history or award the same administrative handover again.
