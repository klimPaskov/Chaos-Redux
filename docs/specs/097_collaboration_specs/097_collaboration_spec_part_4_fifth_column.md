# 097 Collaboration
## Part 4: The Fifth Column

## A state begins to come apart

At Evolution III, a deeply established network can become active while its host country is losing a real war. Officials cease carrying out some orders, local authorities prepare for an incoming administration, and people with access to the state begin acting on their foreign commitments.

The effect is expressed through additional **native prepared collaboration against that particular country**. It does not create a universal national surrender penalty, delete divisions, damage every factory, or spawn a separate rebel army.

This preserves the event's identity. The same network that will help administer a defeated country is now helping make that defeat more likely.

## Who can benefit

A sponsor A can activate its network inside B only when all of the following are true. A and B are current enemies. B is not already capitulated. A has at least 50 native prepared collaboration inside B. A actually controls at least one of B's applicable homeland states containing victory points through the current war. B has lost enough territory to be approaching its current native capitulation threshold.

A distant belligerent that has never reached B's homeland cannot activate its network merely because another faction is doing the fighting. A friendly faction member has no Fifth Column effect while the countries remain allies, even though its prepared network exists.

A's contribution need not be the largest in its coalition. The relevant test is genuine participation in the occupation of B's homeland, not a share of an invented Collaboration war score. An enemy that takes only an empty border state has not met the victory-point condition.

## Measuring proximity to defeat

Use the target's **current native capitulation threshold** together with its current surrender progress. The threshold must include the native effects that actually apply to that target. Do not assume every country capitulates at the same fixed percentage of territory lost.

For design purposes, let P be current surrender progress and T be the current native threshold, both expressed in the same units. Proximity to defeat is P divided by T, provided T is positive and B has not already capitulated. Evaluate eligibility against one pre-breakthrough snapshot.

Require at least 25% absolute surrender progress as an additional safety condition. A peculiar combination of native modifiers that makes T extremely small must not allow an untouched country to collapse through this event.

The design cannot be implemented faithfully by comparing ordinary surrender progress to two arbitrary fixed percentages and calling them proximity to the current threshold. Reliable access to T is a required capability.

## Two breakthroughs

| Stage | Additional requirement | Native collaboration awarded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First breakdown | Proximity reaches at least 75% of the current threshold | +10 percentage points for A inside B |
| Final realignment | Proximity reaches at least 90% of the current threshold and the first breakdown occurred at least 5 days earlier | +15 percentage points for A inside B |

Both awards remain subject to the native maximum. Each stage can pay once for a particular sponsor and target political identity over the campaign. A new war, a change of faction, a release, or a setting toggle does not automatically replenish those two opportunities.

A second sponsor can have its own qualifying breakthrough against B because it possesses a different native network. Its award does not copy the first sponsor's collaboration or grant a benefit to its entire faction.

## No instantaneous cascade

Award no more than one breakthrough to a directed pair in one evaluation. The second stage always waits at least 5 days after the first. It must then satisfy the live conditions again.

Suppose a first breakthrough reduces the target's native resistance to capitulation enough that the country is now extremely close to defeat. That can matter through the native system, but it must not immediately chain the second award in the same effect. If the native system capitulates B, the second award is no longer payable.

Likewise, evaluate all participating sponsors against the same target-state snapshot for a single world-changing incident before committing that incident's awards. Country iteration order must not determine which sponsor passes a threshold solely because another sponsor was processed first.

Native rules still decide whether B capitulates after the collaboration increase. Event 097 does not directly force a capitulation, choose a victor, transfer the entire homeland, or finish a war.

## Recovery interrupts the danger

A country that regains important territory can move below a breakthrough threshold. A delayed second-stage evaluation then does nothing. It does not punish the country because it was losing five days earlier.

If the country later becomes vulnerable again, the still-unused second stage may become eligible. The first stage remains spent. The country is not charged a new first breakdown every time the front moves back and forth.

If A loses all qualifying victory-point states, makes peace, becomes B's ally, ceases to exist, or no longer has a deep enough network, A's pending opportunity becomes inactive. Nothing should be awarded to an old actor reference after its country has disappeared.

## At the native cap

A network at the native maximum receives no overflow benefit. Its country may already face the strongest capitulation effect the native collaboration system allows. The Fifth Column does not add another national spirit or an uncapped surrender modifier to make the evolution visible at any cost.

A wholly saturated stage can be recorded as resolved without a grant, but it must not be described as a fresh collapse, generate Chaos, or earn an achievement that requires an actual breakthrough. If native collaboration later legitimately falls, the same resolved stage is not a renewable refill.

When a first stage cannot add anything because the network is capped, the later stage may still be evaluated after the timing separation, but it too can only add native headroom. There is no bypass of the shared cap.

## Player information

The affected country should receive a concise notice when a material breakthrough actually occurs. It should explain that an enemy's established collaborators are strengthening as defeat approaches. It must not pretend every official has defected or that surrender is already inevitable.

The sponsor can receive a corresponding intelligence report that identifies the actual target. Other enemies do not receive a copied reward report. Foreign observers should not gain full secret information about every country's network merely because one target received a warning.

No extra popup is needed for a failed recheck, a country already at the cap, or a duplicate callback. A player should not have to acknowledge five warnings to continue a battle.

## Defensive strategy

The player can reduce the immediate danger by defending or recovering important homeland territory and preventing a sponsor from retaining a meaningful foothold. A comeback changes the live conditions and can stop the second breakthrough.

These responses have ordinary military costs: divisions, equipment, supply, operational time, and the risk of concentrating a defense around important territory. There is no compulsory political-power payment that magically restores loyalty everywhere.

The country can still lose despite defeating one sponsor's local opportunity. Another enemy may possess its own deep network and a real occupation foothold. That is part of the event's global and reciprocal character.
