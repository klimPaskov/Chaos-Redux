# 097 Collaboration
## Part 6: Connections, strategy, and the shape of a campaign

## Occupation revolt

Event 095 represents the national opposition that Collaboration cannot eliminate by definition. A prepared administration may govern effectively while a displaced national movement still considers it illegitimate.

The connection must work in both directions. High compliance and a functioning administration can make an uprising harder to organize where Event 095's own design uses those conditions. A successful restoration can overthrow the client regime and end its sponsor's actual control. Neither event should simply disable the other whenever it fires.

A collaboration government remains a valid potential target when Event 095 finds a genuine surviving national claimant and sufficient opposition under its own rules. Event 097 must not remove the claimant's cores, erase its national identity, or set an immunity flag as a side effect of creating the government.

If an uprising restores the original country, the old sponsor loses access to administrative effects in territory it no longer controls. A delayed government-creation offer must close if its target area has been restored or its actor relationship is no longer valid.

The restored country does not inherit an automatic world-sized network from the client government it displaced. The native country lifecycle determines what collaboration survives for that political identity. Its next universal opportunity is the next legitimate firing of Event 097.

## Collaboration does not grant a free counter-revolt

A former occupier may still have a native network inside the restored country if the native system preserves it. That network does not automatically return the country to puppet status or trigger an immediate second civil war.

The former occupier must again acquire lawful hostile occupation and meet the current conditions for any unused effect. Spent early-administration and government-readiness opportunities do not reset merely because the same regime has been removed.

A restoration that creates a genuinely different political country needs explicit identity handling. It must not become a means to farm repeated readiness awards through renaming, civil-war cycling, or releasing and annexing the same area.

The Event 095 rework owns the revolt's army, opening territory, leadership, and success conditions. Event 097 supplies only the collaboration relationship and relevant occupation or government history. It does not duplicate the revolt system inside a second event chain.

## Treaty of Tordesillas Restoration

The companion concept is the user's restored Iberian partition project, identified as Event 093 in the surrounding project brief. Its current catalog identity is the Galicia dispute. Integration must use its accepted rework rather than assuming an already implemented global partition hook.

Spain and Portugal can benefit from their own networks in countries they actually conquer. Their respective administrations can consolidate territory through collaboration governments rather than requiring direct occupation everywhere.

The partition agreement does not merge Spanish and Portuguese collaboration. If Spain has 80 inside a target and Portugal has 20, Portugal cannot use Spain's network as its own simply because the two powers signed the treaty.

Control and sponsor identity follow the agreed settlement that actually occurs. If Spain conquers territory and then peacefully transfers it to Portugal, that transfer does not count as a fresh hostile capture for an unused Portuguese early-handover reward. Portugal's own later native occupation and government eligibility still matter.

A proposed partition line is not enough to grant a government. There must be lawful territory and a native route. Disputed states remain disputed rather than silently changing hands when a report is acknowledged.

The resulting world can have a Spanish sphere and a Portuguese sphere of local collaborator regimes. Their international alignment follows the real sponsor and subject relationships. Event 097 does not create an additional treaty faction, worldwide truce, or separate partition map.

## Existing intelligence operations

Ordinary collaboration operations remain useful while there is native headroom. A country that starts with deeper networks can reach the early-administration, Fifth Column, or government-readiness thresholds sooner than its rivals.

The universal grant does not require an agency. This does not mean agency mechanics become free for every country. Operatives, intelligence networks, decryption, sabotage, and other operations retain their own requirements.

An agency action should not be renamed into an Event 097 purchase of collaboration points. The player already has native ways to pursue intelligence goals. This event changes the world's starting conditions and periodically strengthens them.

## Factions, subjects, and diplomacy

Allies can possess dormant networks inside each other without immediately breaking their alliance. The network is not a declaration of war, an opinion penalty, or proof that a named leader has personally committed treason.

Subjects and overlords participate where the native backend supports their identities. A subject's prepared network inside its overlord does not itself grant independence or permission to attack. If their actual relationship later changes, the native network can become strategically relevant.

Collaboration governments should use normal faction and overlord constraints. The event does not make every installed regime a separate rival diplomatic actor with a bespoke foreign policy tree.

## A small country's opportunity

A minor country can gain the same nominal layer as a major. This makes a successful intervention more meaningful, but it does not give the minor the divisions, supply, naval access, or industry required to reach a distant target.

The minor's reward follows its actual conquest. A small country that secures and holds a qualifying part of a larger enemy's homeland may activate its own deep network. A minor that merely joins a distant war receives no territory through the collaboration event.

This is the intended asymmetry between political preparation and military capacity. Equal access to networks does not make every country equally capable of exploiting them.

## An invader's decisions

The invader chooses which existing network to exploit through ordinary strategic planning. It must weigh the target's armed forces, geography, allies, supply, and important territory. Deep networks can make a difficult campaign shorter, but cannot justify ignoring those constraints.

At Evolution II, the choice of advance can affect how quickly a stable occupation forms. Homeland states with genuine administrative continuity are more valuable than irrelevant colonial holdings for this event.

At Evolution III, holding a meaningful foothold while the enemy approaches defeat may produce a breakthrough. At Evolution IV, the victor chooses how much direct administration to retain and whether to use the native collaborator route.

These are connected consequences of the same network. They do not require four unrelated side systems.

## A defender's decisions

The defender benefits from its own networks abroad while worrying about enemy networks at home. Protecting important homeland territory and recovering from a threatened defeat can stop an unused Fifth Column stage.

The event should produce tension without encouraging repetitive loyalty purges. The player can still make ordinary political and military decisions, preserve supply, choose defensive lines, and pursue peace. Any actual native counterintelligence response retains its proper cost and effect.

The defender may also survive as an exile, return through national restoration, or later benefit from a collaborator regime's weakness. Those outcomes belong to the native and connected event systems rather than a special guaranteed rescue from Event 097.

## Campaign examples

### Mutual preparation

Two allied democracies each receive collaboration inside the other. Their diplomacy remains unchanged. Years later, if they become enemies through real political developments, their existing native networks can matter. The event has prepared an opportunity without inventing the war.

### A coalition invasion

Three allies invade one enemy. Only two hold qualifying victory-point homeland states. Their own networks can activate if the target approaches defeat. The third ally does not receive a breakthrough simply because it is in the faction. Territory and postwar government options follow actual native settlement.

### A prepared occupation that unravels

An invader obtains an early administrative handover and then loses the state to a counterattack. On returning, it finds lower compliance. The old offer does not restore everything. Deeper later networks may provide only a limited new improvement, while native capitulation effects retain their ordinary role.

### A restored homeland

A collaborator regime is overthrown through Event 095. The restored country controls its homeland again. Its former sponsor retains no remote compliance power and cannot revive the regime by opening an old notification. A future conquest would have to satisfy the real current conditions.
