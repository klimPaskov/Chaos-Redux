# 097 Collaboration
## Part 3: Deep Networks and Administrations in Waiting

## Evolution I: Deep Networks

Deep Networks changes the scale of each new global layer from 10 to 20 percentage points. The collaborators are no longer a few isolated sympathizers. They include connected groups within municipal government, industrial management, police, political organizations, and the military bureaucracy.

This evolution does not distribute a separate bonus for each institution. Those institutions explain why the native network is stronger. There is no ministry inventory to fill or list of five offices to capture.

The immediate strategic consequence is that fewer repeated manifestations are required to reach powerful collaboration. A state that seemed difficult to subdue can become administratively vulnerable before the player has changed their invasion plan. The same shift happens inside the player's own country.

The evolution's report explains the new per-firing value. It must not claim that a network has already conquered a country, changed its ideology, or handed over its industry.

## Evolution II: Administrations in Waiting

Administrations in Waiting makes deeply prepared networks useful before the entire target has capitulated. Once an invader captures eligible territory, local collaborators can begin running it while the wider war continues.

The normal global grant rises to 25 percentage points. Separately, a network with **at least 50 prepared collaboration** can offer an early administrative handover in newly occupied target homeland states.

This is an explicit evolved addition to native occupation timing. It must not be described as an ordinary baseline benefit that happens on every border crossing.

## A qualifying handover

A handover belongs to a particular sponsor A, target B, and state S. A must actually control the state as hostile occupation of B's homeland. A's own network inside B is the relevant network.

S must be a non-core occupation for A and part of B's applicable national homeland. A foreign colony that B happens to control does not automatically become a B-network reward. An allied state merely passing through B's control is not eligible. A's own recovered core is not an occupation to reward.

Where several countries have cores on S, the event must identify the defeated or displaced political country whose territory A is actually taking in the present conflict. It does not choose whichever claimant happens to give the highest collaboration value. Overlapping cores do not permit several handovers on the same capture.

The actual local controller receives the handover. A faction leader with a network in B cannot lend that network to every ally. A country without control cannot remotely set compliance in an ally's occupation.

## The initial administrative benefit

For a qualifying network C of at least 50, the first administrative offer has a target compliance of:

`F = min(80, C + 20)`

All values here use percentage points. Thus a 50-point network can initially support 70 compliance, while a network of 60 or more can support 80. The 80-point ceiling is an event balance choice for early occupation. It is not a claim about the engine's current government-creation requirement.

At the first qualifying handover, raise actual compliance to F only when it is lower. Higher existing compliance is preserved. Compliance is never reduced to make the event's number appear exact.

This is a transfer of administrative continuity. It does not erase an existing resistance organization, remove garrison requirements by fiat, add cores, or grant a permanent state modifier on top of compliance. Native occupation laws and local conditions continue to matter after the handover.

## Harsh rule withholds the handover

Local officials serve an occupier who lets them govern, not one who is starving or interning the people they administer.
While A holds S under an occupation rule that the [famine system](../../systems/famine_system.md) classifies as harsher than standard, or runs a camp in S, the handover is deferred rather than paid.
A deferred offer is not spent: it becomes payable at the first evaluation after A relaxes the rule or closes the camp while still holding S, using the network value at that moment.
This makes the occupation-law choice a real trade: extraction and collective punishment feed the famine, camp and condemnation systems that own those consequences and forgo the collaborator dividend, while standard rule collects it.
The deferral adds no value to track; the existing occupation-law and camp interfaces already show the player what is blocking the offer, and the handover report names the reason once.
A rule changed back to a harsh one after payment does not claw back compliance; native compliance decay and resistance handle that.

## Recapture must not manufacture compliance

Once the administration has been offered, that offer is spent. Losing the state and taking it back does not restore the same compliance floor. Neither does transferring its control to a faction member and asking for it back.

For the same sponsor, target identity, and state, remember the highest administrative offer already made. If a later genuine improvement in collaboration raises F, only the newly available difference can be added to current compliance, up to the new F. The old benefit is not paid again.

Example: A first captures a state with 20 compliance and a 50-point network. The first offer raises it to 70. Later the state is lost, the local administration breaks down, and it returns to A at 25 compliance. Another capture with the same network pays nothing. If a later firing raises A's network enough for an 80-point offer, the newly earned improvement is 10, so compliance can rise from 25 to 35. It does not jump back to 80.

Record the offer even when existing compliance was already higher and no actual increase was necessary. Otherwise a player could deliberately lower compliance and claim an unused old offer later.

This memory is an internal anti-repeat receipt. It is not an administrative currency, a player-visible meter, or a second representation of native collaboration.

## Genuine rival administrations

A different sponsor with its own qualifying network may make its own first administrative offer after genuinely conquering the state from an enemy. That is a new political administration, not a transfer of A's network.

A peaceful transfer between allies or subjects does not create a new first-capture opportunity. A release and immediate reintegration under the same controlling power also does not clear the old history. Countries that change their cosmetic presentation remain the same sponsor for this purpose.

A true successor identity created by a civil war must be resolved using the native country lifecycle. The event cannot declare every civil-war fragment a fresh sponsor simply to duplicate grants.

## Existing occupations when the evolution activates

Activation can evaluate states A already occupies from B in an ongoing hostile occupation. This is a one-time opportunity for established networks to organize the area now that the evolution exists. It uses the same eligibility rules and the same spent-offer receipt as a new capture.

Do not perform a worldwide monthly compliance top-up. After the activation review, only actual occupation changes, relevant network improvements, and other bounded lifecycle transitions require another evaluation.

A new normal firing can improve the organization of current occupations. It pays only a higher offer's newly available difference. It does not restart every occupation at the best possible compliance value.

## Capitulation and native conversion

When B capitulates, native collaboration may apply its normal occupation effects. Preserve those effects. The evolved handover must read the real result before applying any further improvement and must never lower the native value.

Event 097 does not claim that its early handover caused every subsequent gain in compliance. Native conversion, occupation laws, focuses, decisions, and other events retain their own attribution.

The native post-capitulation conversion is allowed to have its normal behavior even if it is stronger than the anti-repeat handover. The guard prevents repeated payment by Event 097. It does not rewrite the base game's settlement mechanics.

## Occupation still has consequences

Administrative collaboration can coexist with substantial national opposition. High compliance makes governing easier, but it does not erase a claimant's cores, establish moral legitimacy, or forbid Event 095 from finding surviving opposition.

An occupier who chooses destructive policies can undermine the administration it inherited. Event 097 does not permanently protect that occupier from the consequences. Its benefit is a better starting position and a limited improvement from deeper networks, not an unconditional promise of peaceful occupation forever.
