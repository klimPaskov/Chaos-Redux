# 097 Collaboration
## Part 8: Presentation, reports, and visual identity

## The visual idea

Collaboration should look like the same country beginning to prepare for several possible victors. Use restrained, period-appropriate scenes of officials, industrial managers, police, and local authorities behaving with calculated readiness.

Avoid supernatural imagery, modern computers, giant world-control maps, or a benevolent international cooperation convention. The event is absurd because the networks are universal. Its individual collaborators can look entirely ordinary.

Report imagery can depict fictional, alternate-history situations. It must not falsely present a generated scene as an archival photograph or identify a real person as a collaborator without a researched basis.

## Five report states

| Surface | Image direction | What the report must explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline manifestation | Two discreet meetings on opposite sides of a municipal interior, suggesting reciprocal foreign contacts | Actual global grant, reciprocity, and no acceptance choice |
| Deep Networks | A group spanning municipal authority, police, and industrial management in an understated period interior | Larger future layers and preservation of existing native investment |
| Administrations in Waiting | Local officials meeting an arriving occupation authority, with a town visible beyond them | Qualifying early compliance, actual controller, and spent handover |
| The Fifth Column | A command office losing the cooperation of its civilian and administrative staff while a retreat is visible outside | Actual sponsor-target breakthrough and continued possibility of recovery |
| Collaboration Governments | A local government taking office under the unmistakable patronage of a foreign power | Native government readiness, sponsor relationship, and the choice to retain direct occupation |

Do not put readable document text, modern logos, invented historical insignia, or explanatory labels inside the image. The surrounding interface communicates exact values and identities.

## Reports are information, not effect buttons

The global report is delivered after the native grant. Its acknowledgment is cosmetic. The same is true of an evolution announcement and a breakthrough report. Their buttons cannot repeat a mechanical award.

An actual government-creation action is different. It must use the native choice surface and revalidate current eligibility. A stale readiness notice cannot install a government after the land has been restored, transferred away, or assigned to another subject.

Failed conditions and capped networks should not create a sequence of empty reports. The player needs notification of meaningful changes, not every technical evaluation.

## Text direction

Final player-facing copy is to be written during implementation from these directions. The supplied event and evolution names remain the named design identities. Other labels in this package are working labels rather than finished localization.

The baseline report should lead with the disturbing discovery that foreign collaborators now exist throughout the world. It should then state the actual native grant and explain that the player's country is also affected. Avoid language implying that every government signed a cooperation agreement.

Evolution I should emphasize the breadth of social and administrative involvement. Evolution II should describe a local authority that is ready to work for an incoming ruler before the old government has fully fallen. Evolution III should describe a real weakening of support as defeat approaches without declaring that the enemy is already victorious. Evolution IV should describe government readiness and the actual sponsor relationship without claiming compulsory installation.

Keep irony in the situation and acknowledgment direction. Do not replace useful information with an obscure quotation or make every line a joke. Do not add real quotations, cultural references, or historical accusations without a separate source check.

## Dynamic identity and scope

Every sponsor-specific report must name the real sponsor and target using their current identities. Global reports must not accidentally attribute the manifestation to the country whose timer triggered it.

A report about A's collaborators inside B must never reverse those identities. The effect summary should distinguish prepared collaboration from current occupied-state compliance.

Country names, state names, percentages, and current stage must remain readable in the surface where they appear. Light parchment body text and dark tooltips need separately appropriate formatting. Do not reuse color control sequences blindly across both.

## Existing UI surfaces

Use the native collaboration and occupation interfaces for the main values and government route. The Chaos Redux Event Details page should explain baseline behavior, active and possible evolutions, repeatability, and why the event is unavailable when relevant.

Event history shows actual manifestations and actual evolution activations. It must not list every directed country-pair grant as a separate global event. A material Fifth Column incident can identify its sponsor and target without expanding into a world-sized network table.

The theoretical Event Details description must remain separate from the history of what actually happened in the current campaign. A player inspecting the event at low Chaos should not see text implying that its fourth evolution has already occurred.

## Notification volume

A normal manifestation produces at most one relevant report for each receiving human country. Country count does not multiply the global event entry. AI acknowledgment is silent where the shared presentation pattern supports that behavior.

Coalesce a target's same-incident breakthrough notice when several sponsors qualify together, while preserving correct private sponsor information. Do not reveal all secret network values to the whole world through a public message.

A government readiness message is sent only when a materially new, valid opportunity exists. Revisiting a menu, saving and loading, or changing country control must not produce the same readiness celebration repeatedly.

## Asset production requirements

The five report compositions use the project's report-event pipeline and final report format. Achievement art uses the established achievement templates and separate state treatment described in Part 9 and the asset prompt.

Reference review, generation or source acquisition, final processing, actual game consumer wiring, and visual inspection are separate production steps. A prompt is not an image, an image is not a finished DDS, and an unwired DDS is not a completed event asset.

Use original generated fictional report scenes unless a clearly appropriate, lawfully usable source is selected and documented. Preserve a provenance record and the final processing manifest. No real portrait or country flag should be fabricated merely to complete this event's scene.

## Government presentation

Native generated governments retain the identity and flag behavior supplied by their actual creation route. Their sponsor connection should remain clear in the native subject and country interfaces.

Do not add an event-specific national spirit merely to repeat the word Collaboration. The meaningful information is the native network, compliance, and government relationship. The presentation should make those existing mechanics understandable without adding more values to watch.
