# Historical research notes

## Research purpose

These notes constrain the event's historical geography and naming. They are not a claim that every alternate-history route is historically inevitable.

## San Stefano and the Bulgarian national question

The preliminary Treaty of San Stefano of 1878 is the key historical anchor for the broad Greater Bulgaria idea. Its settlement placed a large autonomous Bulgarian principality across much of Macedonia and Thrace. The later Treaty of Berlin sharply reduced that settlement. For gameplay, this supports a San Stefano route centered on Macedonia, Thrace, and the wider Bulgarian national question, while the exact HOI4 state set still requires installed-map binding.

Southern Dobruja belongs to the interwar Bulgarian territorial question and was returned to Bulgaria by the Treaty of Craiova in 1940. It is therefore a strong baseline claim region.

Western border districts with Yugoslavia require careful state binding because historic claims and modern HOI4 state borders do not line up perfectly.

## First Bulgarian Empire and Simeon

Simeon I provides the strongest medieval anchor for the imperial branch. His reign is associated with major Bulgarian power in the Balkans and the use of imperial title traditions. This supports a Crown of Simeon route, but the event should treat medieval boundaries as an alternate-history ambition rather than precise twentieth-century ethnic proof.

## Old Great Bulgaria and Kubrat

Old Great Bulgaria, associated with Khan Kubrat and the Onogur Bulgars in the seventh century, is generally located in the Pontic-Caspian steppe north of the Black Sea and Sea of Azov. Scholarship differs over exact limits. The most defensible gameplay core is therefore kept around the Azov approaches, Taman, Kuban, and lower Don, with Crimea and southern Ukraine treated as broader strategic or disputed outer groups unless stronger source evidence supports a precise requirement.

The event should not claim a single exact historical polygon where the historical record does not provide one.

## Source classes used in the original planning pass

- Treaty of San Stefano text and later Berlin settlement material for the nineteenth-century Bulgarian territorial framework.
- Treaty of Craiova material for Southern Dobruja.
- United States Library of Congress country-study material for modern Bulgarian historical context.
- Academic work on interwar Balkan territorial politics.
- Standard scholarly secondary references for Simeon I, Kubrat, and Old Great Bulgaria.
