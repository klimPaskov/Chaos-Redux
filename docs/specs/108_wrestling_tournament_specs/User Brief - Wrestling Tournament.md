## Core idea

> Countries choose, fund, and train champions for a major international wrestling competition. The entrants are placed into a real, seeded knockout bracket that the player can inspect as the tournament unfolds. Established stars enter as favorites, but an unknown contender can still become champion.

Each firing starts a fresh tournament with its own participating countries, wrestlers, preparation period, bracket, results, scandals, champion, and history. The event may run repeatedly, with previous victories, rivalries, injuries, and reputations helping to shape later editions.

The tournament is a living competition rather than a sequence of disconnected random popups. Countries commit to a real contender, decide how much to invest in their preparation, and watch the tournament develop through a dedicated bracket window. Wrestlers have identifiable faces and careers. Preparations, bad decisions, unexpected matchups, cheating investigations, and upsets all affect their prospects.

The ordinary tournament can already contain cheating scandals, suspect performance drugs, unsafe training regimens, experimental medical treatments, questionable technology, and sponsorship controversies. These incidents are part of baseline competition and are **not locked behind evolutions**. Higher-Chaos evolutions expand their scale and consequences.

## Tournament phases

A complete tournament follows six recognizable phases:

1. **Announcement and entry.** A new edition is announced and suitable countries decide whether to participate. A participating country chooses one wrestler from its available candidates.
2. **Preparation.** An announced preparation period gives every participating country time to train, fund, rest, or otherwise influence its chosen champion. Scandals and injuries can already emerge.
3. **Roster lock and seeding.** Entries close. Final entrants are assigned visible seeds and placed into a bracket. The competition dates and first opponents are announced.
4. **Knockout rounds.** Matches resolve in bracket order. Entrants can advance, be defeated, be disqualified, withdraw, or receive a legitimate bye. The bracket updates after every result.
5. **Final and award.** The last two remaining contenders wrestle for the title. One winner is recorded and receives a reward appropriate to the field and the opponents defeated.
6. **History and return.** Results stay available for review. Champions, major upset victories, rivalries, and scandals influence later tournaments without carrying an unfinished bracket into a new edition.

The preparation period is substantial enough for several strategic choices and responses, rather than one instant training click. A roughly **90-day** initial duration is a possible planning anchor, with exact dates and adjustments settled during full planning. It should be clear when registration closes and when the first round begins. No training expenditure made after the roster lock can retroactively change the seeds or an already played match.

Several tournaments must not create conflicting active brackets. Repeatable firings can establish the next edition only when the active edition has resolved, or be safely held until it does. Players can inspect finished tournaments while preparing for the next one.

## Countries and wrestler selection

Participating countries may include majors and minors from around the world. The roster should be internationally diverse rather than dominated by a few large powers. The available field should adjust to which countries are alive, independent enough to participate, able to send a representative, and not prevented by a genuinely relevant campaign condition.

Each country selects one contender from a small but meaningful candidate pool. The pool varies between campaigns and editions, allowing the same country to appoint a veteran champion, an ambitious challenger, a technical specialist, a particularly powerful athlete, or a surprise local talent.

Wrestlers have persistent identities and distinguishable attributes:

- **Strength:** capacity to overpower and control an opponent.
- **Technique:** grappling skill, counters, and efficient scoring or submissions.
- **Endurance:** ability to last through long matches and several rounds.
- **Speed:** quick reactions, evasive movement, and sudden attacks.
- **Toughness:** resistance to injury, pain, and fatigue.
- **Adaptability:** ability to change style against unfamiliar or unusual opponents.

A contender's reputation, recent record, current condition, known injuries, preparation, and past matches matter alongside natural attributes. The player should have enough information to make an informed selection, while exact future match rolls and hidden scandals remain uncertain. Choosing the biggest wrestler should not automatically be the best strategy.

A returning champion may be nominated again if still eligible. Experienced rivals can meet in a rematch. National wrestling reputations should be allowed to develop, while new challengers and underdogs keep each edition fresh.

## Preparation, training, and funding

During preparation, each country develops its chosen wrestler through several choices rather than one flat preparation bonus. Countries decide what to emphasize and how much political or national capacity to devote. Investment is useful, but unlimited expenditure should not guarantee victory.

### Training directions

- **Strength camp:** improve physical dominance and close-range control, with heavier fatigue or injury risk if pushed too hard.
- **Technique camp:** improve holds, escapes, counters, and consistency against powerful opponents.
- **Conditioning camp:** prepare for long bouts and late-round exhaustion.
- **Speed and reaction work:** build explosive starts and evasive tactics, with potential vulnerability to sustained grappling.
- **Opponent study:** spend time on scouting likely styles, especially previous champions or a known rival.
- **Rest and rehabilitation:** preserve condition, heal manageable injuries, or sacrifice some possible improvement to avoid burnout.
- **Balanced camp:** make smaller advances in several areas without specializing.

Countries can fund coaching staffs, gym facilities, travel, support teams, medical preparation, or specialized training. Funding levels should affect the quality and reliability of preparation in ways that fit current industry, government capacity, and competing wartime priorities. Use existing political, industrial, logistical, or experience resources where fitting. Do not invent a standalone wrestling currency or a giant training shop.

A small nation that commits intelligently can develop a serious contender. A major country's resources offer more options, but weak choices, injury, poor conditioning, or a bad matchup can still ruin its campaign.

### Ordinary tournament controversies

These incidents may happen **before any evolution is active**, usually as uncommon but meaningful disruptions:

- **Cheating allegations:** bribed judges, compromised officials, illegal holds, match-fixing rumors, concealed assistance, or a suspicious result. An investigation may clear the accused, punish an offender, delay a match, or expose a wider problem.
- **Enhancement drugs:** a wrestler or support team may use banned stimulants, dubious tonics, or other period-appropriate performance-enhancing treatments. They can offer short-lived performance benefits while risking health, disqualification, and public disgrace.
- **Experimental technology:** unusual training devices, advanced medical techniques, or scientific preparations can promise improvement at real expense or physical risk. Ordinary technology must fit the setting. Exceptional equipment is possible only if the relevant Chaos Redux scientific capability already exists.
- **Sponsorship and political pressure:** a government or private patron may demand reckless training, publicity appearances, special treatment, or a politically important victory.
- **Injuries and recovery:** a fighter can strain muscles, arrive exhausted, suffer a bad training accident, or recover in time through sensible preparation.
- **Rules and eligibility disputes:** an unexpected weigh-in, contested referee decision, travel disruption, or complaint about a competitor can create a difficult choice.

A country can accept, investigate, conceal, reject, or address a controversy when that response makes sense. Wrongdoing may help temporarily, but discovery can cause genuine penalties. A clean wrestler remains competitive. The event should not make doping mandatory for high-level success.

The game should distinguish proven cheating from a mere accusation. An innocent contender should not always be ruined because a rival produces rumors. Different countries can react based on their investment, ideology, and reputation, and an AI country should not blindly choose the option with the largest short-term strength bonus.

## Seeding and bracket formation

### A real, inspectable knockout bracket

At the end of preparation, the tournament generates a **literal, dynamic seeded elimination bracket** in a separate event-owned window. The window shows actual participants, their seed numbers, flags, portraits, match pairings, winners, eliminated contestants, and future progression through the rounds. It is not a decorative picture or a static scripted list that pretends matches took place.

A typical international edition may use a **16- or 32-contender main draw**, depending on the number of suitable participants. If the eligible field is smaller, choose the closest supported structure with fair byes. If there are more applicants than spaces, conduct a recorded qualifying stage or use a published admission rule, rather than silently ignoring countries. An edition with a very small eligible field may use a smaller valid draw. Exact field sizes and qualification eligibility belong to full planning.

Seeding is based on an established combination of prior tournament record, recent form, recognized contender quality, reputation, and preparation evidence. The strongest established contenders are separated across the draw:

- Seed 1 and seed 2 begin in opposite halves.
- The other leading seeds are allocated to different quarters or appropriate bracket sections.
- Strong favorites do not normally meet another top seed in the first round.
- The remaining draw positions contain a mix of seeded entrants, ordinary contenders, qualifiers, and lower-ranked challengers.
- First-round byes, when needed, follow a clear and fair seeding rule.

**Seeding does not determine winners.** An unseeded wrestler may beat a favorite through skill, matchup, preparation, an injury, a clever match plan, or a rare upset roll. An unknown country can eventually claim the championship. Higher seeds should have a credible advantage in ordinary circumstances, without removing drama from early rounds.

Once the draw is published, bracket positions and advancement paths remain stable for that edition. Do not reseed the survivors after each round or retroactively alter a pairing to force a famous rivalry. If a competitor withdraws, is disqualified, or cannot fight, the opponent advances through a recorded walkover or another consistent competition rule. Any replacement must happen before the roster lock unless a specifically announced tournament rule provides for one.

### Bracket window behavior

The window can be opened from the tournament announcement, tournament-related decisions, and subsequent match notifications. It remains available to participants after their wrestler loses, and ideally to interested countries observing the tournament. It should be possible to review a completed bracket later.

The interface should clearly show:

- a readable view of the complete bracket, using scrolling or round selection when the field is too large for one screen
- the opening seed, country flag, wrestler name, distinctive portrait, and current tournament status for each entrant
- which matches have already happened, including winners, losers, and the decisive result
- which match is next, its contestants, and when it is expected to happen
- the pathway from the first round through the semifinal and final
- genuine byes, withdrawals, disqualifications, and upsets, with distinct explanations
- clickable or selectable wrestler and match details revealing the known record, preparation, condition, and match history
- the player's own champion highlighted when the country has one in the competition
- previous editions' champions and major results through a compact historical view

The bracket updates immediately after every result. The national flags, wrestler identities, advancing names, and match details must all come from the real tournament state. Player and AI results use the same state, and save/reload or multiplayer observers must not produce different draws.

The player should not need to remember who beat whom across a long series of popups. Match events can still provide atmosphere and choices, while the bracket is the trusted source for standings. Exact layout, buttons, artwork, and engine feasibility require live native GUI inspection and rendering during implementation.

## Matches and underdogs

Before a meaningful match, the player can choose how their champion approaches the opponent. A country's preparation and injuries should inform these decisions. Useful styles include overpowering early, technical counter-wrestling, conserving energy, and taking a balanced approach.

Match outcomes depend on relevant attributes, fatigue, readiness, opponent style, preparation, and a controlled random element. Conditions change over rounds. A strong fighter may overpower several opponents yet struggle against an agile technician, while an endurance specialist can excel after grueling earlier matches.

A clear favorite should win more often against a weak, unprepared challenger, but **even the strongest champion can lose**. Upsets should be exciting because they are uncommon and rooted in plausible tournament circumstances. A particularly strong underdog victory can affect their reputation and influence future seeding. Prior reputation must never make a new champion permanently untouchable.

Ordinary results can be a convincing win, a narrow decision, a surprise pin or submission, a referee decision after a long contest, a disqualification, or a genuine walkover. Scandals may be investigated and adjudicated without rolling back legitimate unrelated matches. The bracket must show which result was officially recorded, even when a later inquiry changes its status under tournament rules.

A player knocked out in the first round should still receive match reporting, able to inspect the rest of the event, and able to follow an unexpected underdog run to the title. Do not terminate the global tournament just because the selected player champion loses.

## Scandals during competition

Cheating and experimental preparation can spill into live matches. A suspected illegal hold, an official secretly favoring a sponsor, a rival accused of using a banned drug, or an unsafe scientific treatment can trigger an event with meaningful consequences. A complaint can succeed, fail, or remain unproven. Investigation has a cost in time, reputation, diplomatic goodwill, or the wrestler's immediate ability to compete.

A caught offender may lose a bout, be disqualified, be suspended for later editions, or have a title called into question. A false accusation may expose the accusing country's politics or embarrass a sponsor. Some controversy can become a public spectacle that increases interest in the competition and encourages future cheating, without automatically awarding a win to the loudest claimant.

Ordinary tournaments can therefore be messy and political even in a relatively calm world. Higher-Chaos evolutions increase the scale, organization, and danger of existing possibilities.

## Champion and tournament history

The champion becomes a recognizable international wrestling figure. Their nation receives a meaningful political and national-prestige reward, with the size and kind of recognition influenced by the tournament's field, defeated opponents, rivalry history, and wider campaign state.

A returning titleholder can receive additional recognition for defending the championship. A first-time minor-country victor who beats several major favorites should gain a distinct reputation and appropriate recognition. A champion whose victory is later tainted by proven cheating can face scandal or loss of status without rewriting every prior bracket result.

Preserve a short but useful record of champions, editions, leading rivals, historic upset victories, and major sanctions. The archive should keep the wrestler portrait identity and national affiliation recorded at the time, even if the country changes later.

# Evolutions

These evolutions are separate mutations of the tournament, not the normal registration, preparation, bracket, round, or championship stages. They become eligible through Chaos and relevant campaign conditions, and each uses the existing evolution pacing rules. They may occur in different orders or not occur at all. Baseline scandals and small-scale dubious enhancements already exist without them.

## Evolution I: National Obsession

- Chaos requirement: `200+`.
- Becomes more likely as major powers, historical rivals, or returning champions place increasingly heavy political importance on tournament results.

Wrestling becomes a point of national obsession. Governments devote more publicity, stronger sponsorship, and greater political capital to their representative. Rivalries intensify, while an unexpected result can trigger public celebrations or national humiliation. Countries can deliberately promote a champion or stay cautious to avoid placing an entire government's prestige on one match.

**Adverse possibilities:** political backlash after a heavily promoted loss, excessive training demands, financial overcommitment, angry supporters, and international friction over referee decisions. These consequences should matter without making every defeat a national disaster.

## Evolution II: The Fixed Match

- Chaos requirement: `400+`.
- Becomes more likely when tournaments have developed high prestige, major political stakes, and influential sponsors.

The occasional baseline cheating attempt grows into **organized tournament corruption**. Networks of promoters, fixers, corrupt officials, and government patrons coordinate favorable seeds, illegal assistance, and paid match outcomes. A country may be approached to participate, refuse, expose the network, or investigate a suspicious contest.

**Adverse possibilities:** a scandal involving several countries, match disqualifications, compromised rankings, loss of reputation, and bitter diplomatic consequences. Organized fixing can be uncovered, and honest wrestlers can still overcome an arrangement intended to defeat them. The published bracket itself stays visible, while adjudicated results and sanctions follow consistent rules.

## Evolution III: Enhanced Athletes

- Chaos requirement: `600+`.
- Becomes more likely when advanced medical, biological, industrial, or scientific programs already exist in the campaign.

Limited baseline experimentation develops into systematic and extreme enhancement. Some teams push unusual therapies, invasive treatments, advanced training machinery, or existing Chaos Redux technologies to create exceptional competitors. Enhancements should build on capabilities that the sponsoring country actually possesses. An isolated minor without access to a scientific program does not suddenly gain one through a wrestling decision.

**Adverse possibilities:** injuries, unstable physical performance, long recoveries, exposure of unethical experimentation, disqualifications, and national outrage. Enhanced contenders may gain a strong advantage in one kind of matchup while retaining real weaknesses and recovery limits. Normal well-trained humans must remain capable of winning.

## Evolution IV: Open Division

- Chaos requirement: `800+`.
- Requires suitable existing nonhuman beings or special unit families in the current campaign.

The championship admits eligible nonhuman and extraordinary representatives. Countries may nominate **zombies**, suitable monsters, clones, aliens, golems, or other actual Chaos Redux creatures when their originating event or system has produced a valid entrant and the entrant can meaningfully compete. Returned human Spartans can enter ordinary divisions at any time, while genuinely mythological figures from Sparta's Age of Heroes can join this evolved division if they actually exist.

Monster entries should feel extraordinary. Zombie wrestlers can be hard to tire but clumsy or easy to exploit technically. A powerful golem may lack agility. Other beings should have genuine strengths and limitations derived from their identity. Nonhumans should not always defeat humans, and an underdog human can still win with skill, clever training, and favorable matchup conditions.

**Adverse possibilities:** a dangerous bout, injuries, frightened spectators, an eligibility dispute, a scandal involving a monstrous entrant, or a competition disrupted by a being that cannot be controlled. The tournament should manage these through clear participation and safety outcomes rather than automatically triggering an unrelated world-end scenario.

Open Division creates special entrants only by selecting **existing valid actors or beings**. It does not summon a fresh zombie invasion, clone army, alien force, or scientific institution merely to populate the bracket. Every extraordinary entrant needs its own meaningful portrait and identity.

## Cross-event connections

Relevant prior events should change the eligible contenders, methods of preparation, and public consequences of the tournament when they actually exist:

- **002 Zombie Outbreak:** when a valid zombie-controlled population or eligible representative exists, the Open Division can include a zombie competitor. The tournament does not create a new outbreak or force a zombie government into existence.
- **019 Soldiers from Nowhere and special unit families:** suitable existing unusual soldiers or creatures may enter the Open Division when the owning system supports them.
- **Clones and alien populations:** an actual compatible human or nonhuman candidate can participate, with the correct origin and eligibility rules. The wrestling event does not duplicate their unit programs.
- **Advanced science and Kruger's programs:** applicable science can influence baseline experimental training and later Enhanced Athletes if the participating country genuinely possesses the needed program.
- **111 This is SPARTA!!!:** returned human Spartans can enter as ordinary wrestlers. Suitable mythological beings from Age of Heroes can enter Open Division after its evolution.

The tournament may create real national pride, propaganda, embarrassment, injury, or public controversy, but it must not rewrite the separate owner events, invent their associated characters, or count the same consequence twice. Any genuine extra harm to civilians or units that should enter a shared subsystem must follow that subsystem's accounting rules.

## Player and AI direction

The player chooses their country's candidate, funding level, preparation focus, and important match tactics. They can respond to cheating accusations and injury. The dynamic bracket gives immediate feedback on whom they face and which potential opponent may await in later rounds.

AI countries choose legitimate candidates, balance spending against war or economic needs, select training based on their candidate's profile, make match approaches according to opponent strengths, and react sensibly to scandals. Powerful AI countries should not always spend the most or always nominate a single wrestler. AI-generated tournament results have the same seeding, tie handling, eligibility, and upset rules as player matches.

Tournament matches should advance at a predictable campaign pace and remain stable through save/reload and multiplayer synchronization. Each edition has one authoritative field, one immutable seeded draw, one result record per match, and one champion. Duplicate awards, repeated match resolution, stale wrestler identities, and simultaneous conflicting active tournaments must be prevented in full implementation.

## Visual and writing direction

Player-facing match and news writing should reflect the wrestlers, the stakes, and what spectators have witnessed. A hard-fought technical win should sound different from a shocking upset, blatant corruption, or a frightened crowd watching a monster enter the arena. Rivalry history and the actual countries, wrestler names, and results should inform the writing. Serious injury and violent misconduct call for serious consequences rather than cheap jokes.

The dedicated bracket must look like an actual organized tournament with clean rounds, visible connecting progression, recognizable flags and portraits, readable seed numbers, and a highlighted championship route. Wrestler portraits, match illustration assets, important scandal reports, and exceptional contender reveals must have clear roles. The design remains playable without opening a separate character-management screen for every small action. Preparation decisions and the bracket window should cooperate rather than duplicate one another.

The tournament bracket GUI, many wrestler portraits, live seeding and match logic, artwork, and event-owned sound or animation packages require a dedicated implementation handoff. See `05 - Wrestling Tournament Bracket and Portrait Handoff.md` for future agent ownership, evidence needs, and native verification requirements.

## Design identity

A national champion is chosen and backed before the bell rings. The player can train or fund them, make choices when a scandal occurs, and follow each round in a genuinely changing seeded bracket. Favorites have meaningful advantages, while preparation, human error, and remarkable underdogs create stories that no fixed list of results can guarantee. Later Chaos can turn a recognizable sporting competition into a contest against impossible opponents.
