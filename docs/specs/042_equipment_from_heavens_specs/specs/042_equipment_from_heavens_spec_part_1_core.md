# Equipment from Heavens

## Catalog entry

- Event ID: `42`
- Event name: Equipment from Heavens
- Type: Minor Repeatable
- Chaos level: 1
- Cluster: Various Anomalies
- Member severity: Low

## The incident

A military arsenal falls out of an empty sky and lands inside one ordinary country. The delivery has no sender, flight path, shipping record, diplomatic explanation, or reliable manufacturing origin. Crates cover roads and fields. Vehicles descend under parachutes too large to have been packed inside any known aircraft. Trains appear on sidings that were empty during the previous inspection. Aircraft are found intact on improvised landing strips or scattered across open ground with no sign of a ferry crew.

The event is generous by design. It is also careless. The sky does not inspect the recipient's industry, manpower, fuel, logistics, doctrine, research, ports, airbases, coastline, or military plan. The selected country receives an arsenal whose scale would matter to a major power even when the country is a one-state minor.

The event does not reveal a sender. Every later report should add evidence that weakens ordinary explanations without confirming aliens, time travel, divine intervention, secret stockpiles, dimensional accidents, or a hidden government program.

## Eligible recipients

Every eligible country receives the same base selection weight. The event must not quietly favor majors, countries at war, countries with low stockpiles, players, AI countries, countries with a large army, or countries that can use the result efficiently.

An eligible recipient is an existing ordinary country that can own and hold stockpiled equipment and has at least one state that it both owns and controls. Player-controlled countries remain eligible. Subjects, civil-war participants, and small minors remain eligible when they meet the same ordinary-country rules.

Shared country classifiers define the main exclusions. Special Chaos countries, actual nonhuman countries, system actors, helper identities, stateless governments, and countries without a valid owned and controlled landing state are not ordinary recipients. A government in exile with no land cannot receive a physical skyfall until it again owns and controls a valid state.

The same country may be selected by later firings. Repeatability does not create anti-repeat protection. A country that already received an arsenal remains in the ordinary uniform recipient pool unless another active Event 42 delivery chain has not finished its landing reports.

If no eligible recipient exists, Event 42 is unavailable. Event Details should show `N/A` weight through the normal event-system behavior, and manual non-force firing should fail cleanly without selecting a substitute special country.

## Event opening

The entry event selects and persists one valid recipient before any equipment or report is created. The selected recipient becomes the Event Log actor for that firing.

The recipient receives one main popup describing the scale of the skyfall, the broad manifest, and the first visible landing zone. The option tone should express stunned military acceptance, strained administrative practicality, or dry disbelief. Final wording should mention the recipient and one actual landing state. It should not explain the source, list hidden weights, or spoil later evolutions.

The stockpile grant occurs once through the main delivery transaction. Follow-up reports never repeat the grant. This one-transaction rule is central because several delayed reports can fire for one package.

The event takes a manifest snapshot before the grant is applied. That snapshot records the delivery magnitude, selected equipment families, selected technology levels or variants, quantities, nuclear quantity when present, special-equipment family when present, report-state list, and compatibility receipts. The snapshot supports follow-up text, Event Details, achievement tracking, save and reload continuity, debugging, and duplicate-grant prevention.

## Delivery magnitude

Every firing receives one magnitude result. Working design labels are Enormous, Colossal, and Impossible. Final localisation may use different names, but the three roles must remain distinct.

- Enormous is already large enough to change a war.
- Colossal can sustain a major expansion or equip an army that the recipient does not yet possess.
- Impossible creates a stockpile that even a major power would struggle to distribute, crew, fuel, or store.

Magnitude is never reduced by recipient size. Higher evolutions and wider global war can shift the roll toward larger packages, but recipient capacity cannot move it downward.

## Physical landing sequence

Each package creates three to seven landing-zone reports. The number rises with magnitude and can rise modestly at later evolutions. Reports are distributed across random owned and controlled states. When the recipient has fewer valid states than the report count, the chain may revisit a state with a different local site and report theme.

Reports arrive over one to fourteen days. Their order is randomized within these roles:

1. First discovery and military cordon
2. Crate or vehicle recovery
3. Transport and depot congestion
4. Provenance anomaly
5. Civilian disruption or local rumor
6. Advanced cargo discovery when present
7. Final manifest reconciliation

Landing reports are presentation and local consequence events. They do not count as separate random events, pacing events, Event 42 firings, repeatable-cap reductions, cluster members, or Event Log history rows. They do not grant additional equipment.

## Landing-zone consequences

The physical event should leave short local effects in the states where material landed. These effects are temporary and should communicate the burden of recovering an absurd arsenal without weakening the main reward into a trap.

Possible state conditions include blocked roads, crowded depots, improvised airframe recovery, unexploded cargo hazards, fuel-spill cleanup, rail-yard congestion, and civilian scavenging. They can briefly reduce local supply flow, infrastructure repair speed, movement, or construction efficiency. Their strength and duration follow the report theme and package magnitude. A one-state minor may receive several overlapping reports, but local penalties must merge or refresh rather than stack without limit.

A landing can also create a small temporary local benefit when the cargo itself helps recovery, such as trucks improving transport, engineering equipment repairing access routes, or intact trains clearing a rail backlog. The event should prefer clear combined state modifiers over many tiny modifiers.

No landing report permanently destroys buildings or population during ordinary operation. Rare accidents may damage a small amount of infrastructure or equipment if the package contains hazardous evolved cargo, but those accidents need visible cause, bounded severity, and a separate report.

## Provenance signatures

Every firing selects one hidden provenance signature for consistent follow-up text. The signature affects reports and art selection but does not become a player meter.

Possible signatures include:

- all manufacturer marks removed without grinding or repainting
- duplicate serial numbers across different weapon families
- factory names that never existed
- production dates from an impossible year
- foreign national markings denied by the named government
- vehicles arriving fueled, lubricated, and ready to start
- every crate containing the same handwritten inventory in different languages
- identical wear on equipment that appears to come from several decades
- replacement parts packed for models that do not share components
- ammunition lots that fit weapons from mutually hostile countries
- sealed instructions written in technically correct but culturally wrong military language

The same firing should not mix mutually exclusive source clues. Later firings may use a different signature, supporting several explanations across a campaign without settling one.

## The recipient's real choice

Event 42 does not need a separate currency or recovery minigame. The military problem begins after the stockpile arrives.

The recipient chooses through ordinary gameplay whether to expand the army, redesign divisions, lend equipment to allies, sell or transfer material where the engine permits, replace production lines, create an air force, accept fuel strain, build supply capacity, retain a strategic reserve, or ignore unusable cargo.

This preserves the event's central joke and strategic effect. The sky gives a country far more hardware than planning capacity. The player's decisions determine whether the arsenal becomes an army, a diplomatic resource, a logistics crisis, or a warehouse full of machines that no one can operate.

## Repeat firings

Each firing is a complete new package with a new recipient roll, magnitude, manifest, provenance signature, landing-state list, and report chain.

A country with an unresolved landing chain is temporarily excluded only to prevent report-state and manifest collisions. It returns to the pool as soon as the chain closes. This is not protection against receiving Event 42 again during the campaign.

Later firings do not upgrade, replace, or retroactively transform earlier equipment. Equipment already granted remains ordinary stockpile content. When an evolution activates between firings, only later manifests use the expanded pool.

## Relationship with nearby event concepts

Event 42 is equipment-only. It does not create divisions, commanders, volunteers, claimants, military governments, or mysterious populations. Those belong to personnel and unit-spawn events such as Soldiers from Nowhere.

Event 42 has no donor relationship. It does not create opinion, debt, faction pressure, ideological influence, or diplomatic gratitude. Ordinary equipment aid events retain the identity of a known sender and a strategic recipient. Event 42 has neither.

The event does not copy a country's technology tree. It grants physical equipment. Technology theft, research breakthroughs, alien reverse engineering, and scientific patronage remain separate event identities.

## Multiplayer behavior

The recipient player receives the main popup and its landing reports. Other players can inspect the Event Log entry and Event Details actor context through the shared global log. The package and reports are resolved once globally, so multiplayer ghost copies cannot duplicate the stockpile.

When the recipient is AI-controlled, the event resolves without forcing all human players through repeated popups. The shared Event Log remains the stable record. Cluster presentation follows the normal cluster-history rules.

## Player-facing information

The main popup and Event Details should communicate:

- recipient country
- delivery magnitude
- broad equipment families
- whether the package includes aircraft, armor, logistics stock, nuclear weapons, or sealed anomalous cargo
- number of known landing zones
- one or two provenance clues

Exact item quantities can appear in the immediate effect tooltip or a concise manifest tooltip. Hidden probability weights, compatibility internals, source-event flags, and special-family safety checks stay out of player-facing text.

## Writing direction

The tone should be factual, incredulous, and concrete. Describe fields filled with crates, engines starting without keys, roads blocked by artillery, parachutes caught on roofs, railway workers finding new trains, and quartermasters discovering impossible inventories.

Avoid generic official-denial framing, long diplomatic cables, stock phrases about a changed world, and staged contrasts between witnesses and governments. The mystery should come from physical details and inconsistent evidence.

Minor landing reports may use dry military humor and administrative absurdity. Reports involving nuclear or biological payloads should become restrained and severe. The event should never turn mass-casualty weapons into a cheap joke.
