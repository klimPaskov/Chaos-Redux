# 09. The ending and the challenge setup

## Chaos Always Wins

The ending marks a failed continental containment. It commits only when the Directorate is alive, directly controls every required state on its origin continent, and the shared world-ending rules accept this branch.

| Gate | Required current fact |
| --- | --- |
| Episode | A successful Event 103 or The Final Boss episode exists and has not been destroyed |
| Actor | The actual Directorate country is alive in its verified owner context |
| Territory | Every ownable land state in the origin-continent set is directly controlled now |
| Chaos | The current value is strictly greater than 1000 |
| Branch setting | Chaos Always Wins is enabled |
| Global setting | World ends are enabled |
| Existing ending | No world-end has already committed |
| Shared arbitration | The request is valid under the current ending coordinator and its normal conflict controls |

The same predicate feeds the internal request and the branch's availability status. Historical continental completion, a completed focus, a queued cinematic or maximum scenario intensity cannot replace a current gate. The ordinary setting for disabling super-event presentation affects presentation only unless the verified shared framework explicitly defines another behavior. Muting audio must not grant or deny military victory.

Recheck all gates when the shared coordinator commits. If a rival recaptures one island between the initial request and the commit, this branch does not begin. If Chaos falls to 1000, it does not begin. If another ending wins arbitration, it does not begin. Do not consume a branch-specific fired flag before the coordinator confirms success.

The first-completion reward and the ending are separate. A continent completed at low Chaos can produce its one-time material victory reward and history, then remain below the terminal threshold for years. If Chaos later rises, live possession is checked again. If the ending was disabled and is re-enabled, the same live check applies without a second conquest reward.

## What the terminal transition does

On a successful commit, set the normal global ending state and the branch's own persistent identity, update the shared public row, and play its researched super-event presentation once. The military changes then strengthen the existing country.

The initial tuning objective is approximately 50 percent more sustainable military output and replacement capacity than its immediately preceding mature state, together with a roughly 25 percent expansion of operational fleet, air and special-delivery budgets. Exact quantity follows current holdings, military losses, surviving sovereign strength, industrial depth and active theaters. These are deliberate terminal escalation anchors, not fixed shipments of obsolete equipment.

The transition makes a one-time reinforcement and reserve package tied to those budgets. Subsequent strength comes from its enhanced production, existing manufactured-body systems, capture and actual operations. It does not refill every stockpile every day. Already completed doctrine or research receipts do not complete again for a second reward.

Replace the relevant staged national spirits within the three-slot tree budget. Show the new state through the same institutional identity, stronger command imagery and the global war. A new flag, new ruler, extra political path or second country is unnecessary.

## Global war continues through the ending

Every remaining sovereign country becomes a target, including neutral countries without a current Directorate border. Subjects participate through the correct sovereign or direct valid war route. Countries created after the transition are discovered through creation or independence hooks and join the same hostility contract. A bounded active-target queue handles missed hooks.

The expansion planner groups targets by reachable coast, staging base, air route or verified special transport. It first secures lodgments, then enlarges them through ordinary combat. Transport protection, replacement flows and operational range remain real problems, now backed by terminal resources.

The normal random-event scheduler stops when a world end begins. Directorate conquest, production priorities, registered operations, war-target maintenance, absorption and cleanup therefore belong to its own persistent active-episode lifecycle. They must not depend on another ordinary major or minor event firing.

The ending does not annex remaining countries, kill all opposing armies or replace the map in one effect. World conquest is complete only when no independent sovereign country remains outside direct Directorate control and every required ownable land state is held. Statistically empty, landless administrative shells are resolved according to the installed country-existence rules rather than keeping a false final target forever.

There is no additional second world-end transition on full map control. Record the military outcome and retire unnecessary target work while preserving the ending and campaign history.

## If the world still wins a battle

After the ending, a resisting country can retake territory, destroy fleets, liberate countries or destroy the Directorate. Those actions remain playable. The historical result Chaos Always Wins is not cleared from the global record, and the ordinary random-event schedule does not restart as if the ending never occurred.

The distinction is intentional. The ending records the world's failure to contain the Directorate before the first continent fell. The later military campaign records whether any sovereign resistance survives that order.

If a different ending begins before this one, Event 103 neither replaces nor erases it. The Directorate continues as an already active hostile country unless that other ending's explicit verified world rules physically destroy it. Its unrestricted use of in-game special weapons is allowed to contribute to another ending. AI must not suppress its arsenal merely to protect the eligibility of Chaos Always Wins.

## The Final Boss

The manual scenario presents one fixed setup identity with the existing four intensity stops. It creates the same Directorate in one uniformly chosen valid non-island state. The launching player remains in their own country. There is no automatic tag switch or hidden preference that spawns the boss next to the requester.

| Intensity | Land and production multiplier | Opening replacement horizon | Immediate arsenal emphasis | Initial event-owned Chaos anchor |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Low | 0.75 | 90 days | Full access with one coherent minimum roster and limited duplicate specialist formations | +20 |
| Medium | 1.00 | 120 days | Natural-arrival equivalent depth, multiple useful special operations | +30 |
| High | 1.50 | 180 days | More simultaneous specialist formations, stronger delivery and transport reserves | +40 |
| Maximum | 2.00 | 240 days | Deep full-spectrum reserves, several concurrent operational packages and strong opening industrial headroom | +50 |

The minimum valid family roster is never removed to meet a lower numerical multiplier. All technologies, units and capabilities remain available at every intensity. Higher levels change immediate quantities, readiness, supported concurrent operations and the depth of fielded variants. This reconciles the brief's intensity language with its overriding universal-access requirement.

The initial Chaos addition is the scenario's concrete boss-arrival shock. It replaces the normal arrival premium instead of adding a second copy. Scale within the selected band only when the actual opening footprint or committed military force differs materially. Use the shared Chaos history path and respect the user's Chaos Meter setting. The launch never sets the meter to 1001, sets a minimum terminal Chaos, or changes the world-end setting. A launch near 1000 may cross the threshold because of its actual gain and ordinary war consequences, but continental control is still required.

### Relaunch after confirmed defeat

The Final Boss may be launched again after the previous Directorate was actually destroyed and its destructive cleanup completed. A clean dormant CXT carrier and no incompatible terminal state remain required. An active boss, a provisionally committing request, unconfirmed capitulation, residual owned or controlled territory, or unfinished actor-mutating cleanup blocks the new launch. This never refills or resurrects an active boss.

Each successful manual attempt receives a fresh immutable episode identity and generation, new frozen arrival and scaling snapshots, and its own receipt namespace. Old callbacks, shipments, pending reports, operation results, refunds and grant receipts cannot mutate or pay the new CXT actor. Retain prior history, completed achievements and finalized provenance. Delayed post-defeat score checks keep their old frozen episode and cannot resolve the replacement CXT as the destroyed actor. Receipts from separate attempts cannot be combined to satisfy any achievement.

Automatic Event 103 remains fire-once. A manual relaunch neither restarts its grace nor clears or resets its prior fired, defeat or arrival history. It bypasses ordinary prior-event state only for the fresh manual setup. Existing terminal history remains intact, so a prior ending may still block launch through the actual shared terminal-conflict rule.

**Disposition: promoted into an accepted spec.** The acceptance basis is the user's 10 October 2026 selection, **Allow relaunch after defeat**, reported by the parent during import. It supersedes the original planning parent's lifetime manual-launch lock. Parts 02, 11, 12, 13 and 16 consume this episode-isolation contract. The original archive and its JSON receipts preserve the earlier policy as history. Failed provisional requests still consume no successful attempt.

The same setup preflight controls the button, its visual disabled state and final confirmation. Confirmation is consequence-free until accepted. The first native option is Cancel, and acceptance revalidates the frozen request, selected intensity, carrier, state candidate and terminal conflict. A duplicate pending request cannot overwrite the first. If the shared window intentionally uses live selectors, freeze them on confirmation and keep that exact accepted request through commit.
