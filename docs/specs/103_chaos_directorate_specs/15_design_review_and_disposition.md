## Prioritized findings and integrated fixes

### F01. Required impassable territory lacked an exact conquest route

**Priority:** high. **Affected Parts:** 05, 07, 09, 13 and 16. **Disposition:** resolved in the accepted design.

The exact origin-continent set correctly included ownable fully impassable administrative states. The original wording required actual war and supported settlement, but did not specify how such a state could legally change direct control when physical occupation was impossible. Leaving that gap would permit either an unreachable ending or an unreviewed annex shortcut.

Part 05 now requires a verified military-defeat receipt for the actual wartime legal owner, direct Directorate control of all that authority's combat-occupiable states on the same continent, eligibility of the exact impassable holding under that real settlement, and no separate resisting controller. The supported exact ownership and control change must actually succeed before absorption or objective credit. Queue acceptance, a lost capital, a private defeat flag or nominal alliance leadership grants no territory. An unsupported result remains a missing state and an implementation blocker. No state is removed from the denominator.

The first revision reused the same condition for Directorate defeat, which would fail when several real enemies held the former CXT states. The integrated victor branch resolves that asymmetry. It requires real wartime military defeat of CXT, actual loss of its combat-occupiable holdings to its wartime conquerors or lawful recipients from that same settlement, and no remaining CXT military control. Each residual impassable state goes only to its proved legitimate participating victor or settlement recipient. Other victors retain their actual holdings. No uninvolved land or unrelated controller is transferred, and no final-attacker award replaces the real settlement. Native country destruction remains an additional required defeat gate.

The concrete follow-through is Part 13 W09 together with E07 and the Part 05 defeat contract. Native settlement effects, actual state control, callback scope and country destruction still need installed reference and engine evidence.

### F02. A tuning band did not prove that the huge army could operate in one state

**Priority:** high. **Affected Parts:** 02, 03, 06, 13 and 16. **Disposition:** resolved in the accepted design.

A starting supply reduction of 70 to 90 percent can leave an extremely large world-scaled army unsupplied. The brief promises real immediate military power, so an arbitrary upper tuning value cannot replace a demand calculation or justify reducing the force.

Part 03 now measures complete committed unmodified demand `D` and verified usable delivered throughput `T`. For positive values, the reduction must satisfy `r >= 1 - T / (1.10 × D)`, with rounding toward sufficient support and a verified native range. The resulting engine-applied demand is checked against actual delivery. The force also requires full planned personnel and equipment, real reinforcement and fuel, legal placement and the proposed organization target. Zero delivered throughput fails logistics admission.

The 70 to 90 percent band is explicitly an initial tuning band. Legal support above it must be proved if needed. If the engine cannot support the complete committed force, provisional setup rolls back and the configuration remains blocked. No family omission, reduced world-scaled army, extra territory or unusable hidden reserve is accepted. Formation compression must preserve the serviceable force and still pass supply proof.

Relaxation is also conditional. It removes at most five percentage points within thirty days, following fourteen days of proved usable capacity that would cover the complete active force at the proposed next value. New deficits pause relaxation. It cannot expire merely because a date or focus passes. Part 16 adopts this preflight, and Part 13 C04 and C11 exercise sparse-state and excessive-demand cases.

Native modifier limits, actual delivery, organization, basing and reinforcement remain implementation evidence. The calculation is a required acceptance contract, not an observed successful setup.

### F03. Industry and focus percentages needed one accounting target

**Priority:** high. **Affected Parts:** 03, 05, 06, 09, 13 and 16. **Disposition:** resolved in the accepted design.

The original opening industrial allowance and later focus percentages left room for the same reward to multiply both real and virtual capacity. A simple minimum-output floor would create the opposite problem, where conquered factories merely replace virtual industry and fail to produce the brief's real snowball.

Part 03 now freezes the absolute opening allowance once as `A0 = max(0, opening target - legal native output at commit)`. The live base is `Qbase = current usable native output + A0`. New real usable factories therefore add net capacity. The adapter's percentage may change to express the same allowance, but `A0` is not recalculated from a later world snapshot or multiplied anew for every factory. With zero native production capacity, it cannot divide by zero or create free stockpiles.

The single output target is `Qtarget = phase factor × Qbase × (1 + sum of approved additive output improvements)`. Terminal output uses the one committed 1.50 phase factor. Each focus, spirit or operation owns its actual dimension once. Role allocations redistribute the envelope. Preparation, transport and repair affect their named consumers. A surge defers comparable work rather than adding permanent output to every line. No implied future production is also awarded as an instant equipment shipment.

Part 06 applies this contract to all anchor groups. Spirit refreshes render the current ledger instead of compounding prior stages. Part 13 C12 and P11 cover growth, damage to zero capacity, restored capacity, focus effects, recapture and terminal amplification. Native modifier arithmetic and the adapter's ability to reach the target require measured implementation evidence.

### F04. Combat missions needed quantified work and exact hold semantics

**Priority:** medium. **Affected Parts:** 03, 07, 08 and 13. **Disposition:** resolved in the accepted design.

Campaign concentration and counterattack required capture and sustained defense without an exact hold duration. Defense required a suitable supplied force without a frozen serviceable requirement. That left room for an empty formation, already-true control or a minimal token detachment to satisfy substantial strategic work.

All missions now freeze and disclose their actual target, owner-defined quantified work, duration and absolute deadline. Combat requirements are explicitly scoped to concentration, counterattack, defense and equivalent combat variants. Their force anchor covers one verified objective frontage, with one complete serviceable formation as the absolute minimum, and the initial delivered-supply anchor is 75 percent of the frozen work requirement. Actual native frontage and serviceability must be measured. Disbanding, equipment removal or a priority change cannot shrink the accepted requirements.

Concentration and counterattack require a genuine hostile capture after acceptance and thirty consecutive days of control and work coverage inside the original 120-day deadline. A break resets the hold without extending the deadline or replaying a premium. Defense performs the full ninety-day interval, or the justified thirty-day emergency interval, from acceptance. Its initial force and supply must already exist to accept that continuous task. A broken defense interval fails that attempt. Reinforcement and rotation may satisfy the same frozen requirement.

The first wording revision imposed combat frontage on every mission. Root corrected that final scope issue. Evacuation, aid, reconnaissance, staging and reconstruction retain their real owner transfer, route, project or operation requirements, with escort or force requirements only when those actual operations need them. No unrelated combat gate was added to a material shipment. Existing owner operation timing remains authoritative. Part 13 P12 and P13 now provide the combat near-miss cases.

### F05. The holdout achievement's anchor selection needed a frozen rule

**Priority:** medium. **Affected Parts:** 05, 11 and 13. **Disposition:** resolved in the accepted design.

R3 required a named enrolled core-state anchor but did not specify when or how that anchor was selected. Delayed selection could choose a more convenient island or restart the ninety-day challenge.

Part 11 now freezes the anchor and due date at the first actor-specific qualifying crisis check. At that instant, the actor is at war, the boss directly controls at least 90 percent of the required continent, Chaos is strictly above 1000, both ending settings are enabled, and the actor directly controls an eligible initially owned core state. The eligible frozen arrival-era capital is preferred. Otherwise the smallest stable native state ID wins. The result is recorded and disclosed immediately. UI opening, later choices, control changes and save/load cannot replace the anchor or restart the clock.

The historical crisis condition remains distinct from the continuous hold. Successful resistance may reduce boss territorial share without invalidating that entry receipt, while anchor loss, Chaos interruption or setting interruption invalidate the required hold. This preserves meaningful counterattack and closes late-choice ambiguity without adding another interface or currency.
