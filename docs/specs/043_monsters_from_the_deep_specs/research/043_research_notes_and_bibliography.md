# Event 043 research notes and bibliography

## Research purpose

Research supports regional placement, creature identity, mechanical themes, and cultural limits. It does not determine final localisation, flag symbols, portrait anatomy, or 3D design without a separate asset review.

The event uses broad mythic inspiration. It should not present one modern retelling as the sole or uncontested source.

## Source-quality approach

Preferred order:

1. primary text or historical object
2. museum, archive, university, or cultural institution
3. peer-reviewed scholarship
4. reputable encyclopedic reference
5. specialist folklore summary with clear sources
6. general summary for discovery only

A weak source can identify a lead. It cannot settle a cultural claim by itself.

## Kraken

### Useful sources

- Natural History Museum, “Sea monsters and their inspiration: Serpents, mermaids, the kraken and more”
  - https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/sea-monsters-inspiration-serpents-mermaids-the-kraken.html
- Early modern Scandinavian descriptions should be checked during asset research, including Erik Pontoppidan's *Natural History of Norway*.

### Supported design use

The Scandinavian and Norwegian maritime anchor is strong. A cephalopod identity is familiar, but the event should recognize that historical kraken descriptions shifted across periods.

### Design caution

Do not copy a modern film or game kraken. Port strangulation and tentacle siege are gameplay interpretations.

## Scylla

### Useful sources

- Theoi, “Skylla”
  - https://www.theoi.com/Pontios/Skylla.html
- National Archaeological Museum and Getty object records should be used for classical visual research where rights allow.
- Homer, *Odyssey*, Book 12.

### Supported design use

The Strait of Messina anchor is direct. Classical descriptions support multiple rapid attack heads, narrow-strait danger, and the loss of sailors from passing ships.

### Design caution

Classical art varies. The final model can use a coherent fish-tailed composite with multiple attacking forms. It should not claim one reconstruction is canonical.

## Leviathan

### Useful sources

- Hebrew Bible, Job 41.
- Hebrew Bible, Isaiah 27:1.
- Psalms 74 and 104 for wider textual context.
- Academic biblical commentaries should be consulted before final localisation.

### Supported design use

A Levantine and eastern Mediterranean anchor is suitable for the event. Armor, endurance, vast size, and destructive force fit the textual tradition.

### Design caution

Leviathan is a religious and literary figure with several interpretations. Avoid presenting the Event 043 creature as a statement about one living faith.

## Cetus

### Useful sources

- Theoi, “Ketos Aithiopios”
  - https://www.theoi.com/Ther/KetosAithiopios.html
- Classical sources collected there include Apollodorus, Pausanias, Hyginus, and Ovid.

### Supported design use

The tradition supports Aithiopia, a destructive coastal monster, inundation, and a secondary Joppa association. This justifies the Red Sea or Horn pool with Joppa as an emergency regional fallback.

### Design caution

Ancient Aithiopia does not map cleanly onto one modern country. The event should use regional language.

## Hafgufa

### Useful sources

- John McCarthy and Erin Sebo, “Parallels for cetacean trap feeding and tread-water feeding in the historical record across two millennia”
  - https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/mms.13009
- Old Norse sources discussed in that paper include *Konungs skuggsjá* and *Örvar-Odds saga*.

### Supported design use

The strongest older descriptions support a whale-like creature that waits at the surface with its mouth open and uses bait. This creates a distinct trap-feeding, stationary coastal-control design.

### Design caution

Do not depict Hafgufa as another kraken. Later conflation with squid and mermaid traditions is specifically noted in the scholarship.

## Jormungandr

### Useful sources

- Snorri Sturluson, *Prose Edda*.
- Poetic Edda material and early skaldic references.
- World History Encyclopedia overview
  - https://www.worldhistory.org/Jormungandr/

### Supported design use

The World Serpent encircles Midgard, lives in the sea, and rises during Ragnarök. Long connected coastal reach, venom, and endgame ambition fit the source.

### Design caution

Avoid one modern franchise silhouette or color scheme. The design should remain an original Event 043 serpent.

## Iku-Turso

### Useful sources

- *Kalevala*, Runes II and XLII.
- Finnish Literature Society material and scholarship by Anna-Leena Siikala should guide deeper research.
- Discovery summary:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iku-Turso_%28creature%29

### Supported design use

The figure rises from the sea in the *Kalevala* and appears in a hostile role during the Sampo episode. Baltic placement, fog, cold water, and sudden attack are compatible event interpretations.

### Uncertainty

The names and identities around Tursas, Turisas, Meri-Tursas, and Iku-Turso have mixed traditions. Visual appearance is not settled.

### Design caution

Use a deliberately original and non-canonical model. Do not state that tentacles, horns, or one animal form are historically certain.

## Akkorokamui

### Useful sources

- Upopoy National Ainu Museum and Park for Ainu cultural context:
  - https://www.ainu-upopoy.jp/en/
- UNESCO article on contemporary Ainu knowledge:
  - https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/contemporary-ainu-knowledge-shaping-community-and-landscape-toya-usu-unesco-global-geopark
- Creature-specific discovery sources commonly link the figure to Uchiura Bay and a red octopoid form. These sources need stronger verification before final localisation.

### Supported design use

The southern Hokkaido and Uchiura Bay anchor is suitable. Red body, tentacles, ink, and regeneration are established modern presentation themes.

### Uncertainty

Accessible authoritative creature-specific material is weaker than for Scylla, Hafgufa, or Te Wheke.

### Design caution

Do not use Ainu motifs, clothing patterns, ritual objects, or invented script as decoration. Final cultural review should involve reliable Ainu-facing material.

## Jiaolong

### Useful sources

- Duke Kunshan University Language and Culture Center, “Jiaolong 蛟龍”
  - https://sites.duke.edu/destinyofrebirth/jiaolong-%E5%B3%A7%E9%BE%8D-flood-dragon/
- Classical Chinese dictionaries and texts should be reviewed before final wording.

### Supported design use

The jiao is linked to rain, floods, serpentine form, and a transitional relationship with the long dragon. A southeastern Chinese coastal anchor and river-corridor mechanic fit.

### Design caution

Do not use a generic imperial dragon model or claim that jiao and long are the same being.

## Timingila

### Useful sources

- University of Edinburgh, Jataka Stories database:
  - https://jatakastories.div.ed.ac.uk/characters/timingilatimitimingila
- Sanskrit and Buddhist source texts should be identified during final text research.

### Supported design use

The name is associated with an immense ocean creature able to swallow large sea animals. A heavy devouring apex and Bay of Bengal or Indian Ocean anchor fit the event.

### Design caution

Keep Timingila distinct from Hafgufa. Timingila hunts and consumes on a vast scale. Hafgufa uses patient trap feeding.

## Lusca

### Useful sources

- George Mason University College of Science, “What really lives in King Kong Cavern?”
  - https://science.gmu.edu/news/what-really-lives-king-kong-cavern
- Bahamian local-history and blue-hole sources should be checked during final writing.

### Supported design use

Andros and Bahamian blue holes provide the strongest anchor. The common shark and octopus hybrid description supports ambush and convoy predation.

### Uncertainty

The institutional source records the folklore as a local claim and investigation context. It does not prove one fixed anatomy.

### Design caution

Keep the opening small and island-focused. Do not turn a blue-hole creature into a free continental empire.

## Cipactli

### Useful sources

- Karl A. Taube, “Cipactli” in *The Oxford Encyclopedia of Mesoamerican Cultures*.
- Mexicolore discussion of the giant earth creature:
  - https://www.mexicolore.co.uk/aztecs/aztefacts/see-you-later-alligator
- Codex and museum object research should guide final visual work.

### Supported design use

Primeval water and earth identity, crocodilian form, hunger, and multiple mouths support Yucatán, Gulf lowlands, marsh adaptation, and the strongest Hunger route.

### Design caution

Cipactli is cosmological. The event should not reduce it to a large crocodile or copy one codex page as final flag art.

## Ipupiara

### Useful sources

- Pêro de Magalhães de Gândavo, *História da Província Santa Cruz*, 1576.
- Public-domain engraving record:
  - https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3APero_de_Magalh%C3%A3es_Gandavo%2C_Ipupiara.jpg
- São Vicente local-history summary:
  - https://www.cidadeecultura.com/lenda-do-ipupiara-sao-vicente/

### Supported design use

The account is tied to São Vicente and describes a coastal monster encountered on land. This supports fast shoreline terror, amphibious raiding, and a southeastern Brazilian landing.

### Source boundary

The best surviving record is a Portuguese colonial chronicle and print. It does not provide direct complete Tupi testimony.

### Design caution

Final text should state the colonial-source boundary when discussing the historical anchor. Do not present the print as a neutral ethnographic record.

## Cthulhu

### Research role

Cthulhu is the terminal union leader. Final use needs a copyright and trademark review of exact text, art direction, names, and quotations in the target jurisdictions.

The event can use the public name under the user's accepted concept, but final art must be original. No commercial illustration, game model, or film design may be copied.

### Quote caution

Do not invent a Lovecraft-style quote. Research exact public-domain status and source text before using a direct quotation.

## Excluded addition: Sisiutl

### Sources reviewed

- National Museum of the American Indian object record:
  - https://americanindian.si.edu/collections-search/object/NMAI_160711
- U'mista Cultural Centre and Kwakwaka'wakw collections should guide any future study.

### Decision

Sisiutl is not added to the Event 043 roster.

The figure remains important in living Kwakwaka'wakw culture and is represented in culturally specific masks and art. Turning it into one more generic hostile invasion country would require a separate community-aware design basis. Yucatán already provides a continental North American monster anchor.

## Research tasks before final implementation

- verify each final public name and transliteration
- identify primary or institutional sources for every final localisation claim
- perform deeper Ainu-facing research for Akkorokamui
- perform regional Philippine research for Bakunawa
- perform scholarly Mesoamerican review for Cipactli
- review colonial-source framing for Ipupiara
- verify Māori place names and diacritics
- verify Finnish names and distinctions around Tursas
- select final original visual anatomy for ambiguous creatures
- research final quotes and cultural remarks separately
- record rights and attribution for every source image and sound
