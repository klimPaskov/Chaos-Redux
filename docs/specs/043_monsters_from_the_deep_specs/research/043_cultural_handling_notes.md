# Event 043 cultural handling notes

## Purpose

Several Event 043 creatures come from living Indigenous traditions, religious texts, national epics, or culturally specific regional histories. The event can use them as fictional Chaos actors only with clear source boundaries and original art.

## General rules

- Use regional and cultural names accurately.
- Keep diacritics where the game font and localisation support them.
- Do not invent pseudo-language.
- Do not place fake sacred writing on flags, units, or UI.
- Do not use traditional body art, weaving, carving, ceremonial clothing, or ritual objects as generic visual texture.
- Do not claim one anatomy is canonical when sources vary.
- Do not make a living tradition sound extinct.
- Distinguish a colonial record from direct Indigenous testimony.
- Keep final flags fictional and anatomy-driven.
- Record source and review decisions in each asset manifest.
- Mark uncertain claims as uncertain in research notes, not as fact in player-facing text.

## Ainu-associated Akkorokamui

Use Uchiura Bay and southern Hokkaido as geographic anchors.

Permitted original motifs:

- red octopoid anatomy
- bay, volcanic coast, cold water
- ink, regeneration, tentacle movement
- ordinary maritime geometry

Avoid:

- copied Ainu textile patterns
- ritual implements
- invented kamuy symbols
- pseudo-Ainu lettering
- human Ainu costume on the monster
- claims about worship or ritual without a reliable source

A cultural-source review is required before final leader, flag, focus-icon, or event-text approval.

## Māori Te Wheke-o-Muturangi

Use Cook Strait, Marlborough, Kupe's pursuit, and named place traditions as the geographic foundation.

Permitted original motifs:

- octopus anatomy
- pursuit
- coast, net, spear, current, rock
- island-chain movement

Avoid:

- copied whakairo
- copied tā moko
- generic koru decoration
- invented sacred symbols
- flattening Kupe's story into one universal Māori version
- using a museum object as a direct final flag design

Verify place names and macrons.

## Cipactli

Treat Cipactli as a primeval earth and water creature with crocodilian, fish, amphibian, and multiple-mouth features.

Permitted original motifs:

- many mouths
- marsh, mud, gulf water
- primeval body geometry
- feeding and terrain transformation

Avoid:

- copying a codex panel
- generic Aztec calendar decoration
- pseudo-Nahuatl text
- assigning a modern nationalist identity
- presenting the creature as an ordinary crocodile

Scholarly Mesoamerican review is required for final descriptions.

## Ipupiara

State clearly in permanent research documentation that the main surviving account comes from a Portuguese colonial chronicle.

Permitted original motifs:

- coastal amphibious humanoid anatomy
- wet hair or body covering described in the print tradition
- São Vicente shore
- fast night attack

Avoid:

- claiming the print is direct Tupi testimony
- inventing tribal symbols
- copying the engraving as the final leader portrait
- using modern Brazilian Indigenous imagery without direct relevance
- presenting one colonial account as settled zoology or belief

## Iku-Turso

Use ambiguity as a design feature.

Permitted original motifs:

- Baltic fog
- sea growths
- horns, appendages, or armor-like ridges as original design choices
- cold water and sudden emergence

Avoid:

- stating that one animal form is canonical
- confusing Iku-Turso, Tursas, Turisas, and every related name without source support
- using the Finnish air-force symbol or unrelated modern extremist associations
- relying on a modern game design

## Bakunawa

Use eclipse, sea serpent, islands, wind, rain, and night.

Avoid:

- presenting one regional version as universal
- invented baybayin text
- copied tattoo or weaving patterns
- permanent global darkness
- modern fantasy-dragon armor that erases the sea-serpent identity

Final text should use broad Philippine or specifically researched regional wording.

## Umibozu

Use historical yōkai sources as references.

Avoid:

- copying a museum image into final art
- treating all yōkai traditions as one fixed canon
- using Buddhist clerical imagery carelessly as horror decoration
- fake Japanese writing

The final creature should remain an original dark sea apparition.

## Biblical Leviathan

Use literary and religious source language carefully.

Avoid:

- declaring one theological interpretation correct
- making a living religion into a monster faction joke
- invented scripture quotations
- copied modern occult symbols

Final super-event and focus text should use verified sources.

## Greek Scylla and Cetus

Classical sources support broad design freedom. Keep the Strait of Messina for Scylla and regional ancient Aithiopia or Joppa context for Cetus.

Avoid:

- treating ancient Aithiopia as identical to one modern state
- copying one vase or fresco as a final flag
- replacing the creatures with modern franchise versions

## Norse Kraken, Hafgufa, and Jormungandr

Keep the three visually and mechanically distinct.

- Kraken uses cephalopod siege and ports.
- Hafgufa uses whale-like trap feeding and prepared lairs.
- Jormungandr uses serpent reach, venom, and endgame ambition.

Avoid merging them into one generic Nordic tentacle style.

## Cthulhu

Create original art. Do not copy a commercial design.

Research direct quotation rights before using Lovecraft text. A short sourced public-domain passage may be considered only after legal and attribution review.

## Review gate

Before final asset approval, each culturally sensitive row needs:

- source list
- design summary
- prohibited motif check
- reviewer name or role
- date
- approved, revise, blocked, or needs user review
- final asset checksum
- runtime consumer
