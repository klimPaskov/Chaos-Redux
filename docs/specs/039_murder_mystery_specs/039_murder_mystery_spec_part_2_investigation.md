# Murder Mystery Specification Part 2: Investigation and Counterintelligence

## Playable investigation promise

The investigation is a compact national security campaign. The player protects offices, commits intelligence resources, preserves evidence, selects investigative methods, follows timed objectives, and decides when enough of the network has been mapped to risk a final operation.

The category should not become a store of small bonuses. Every action must either move Case Progress, reduce Network Reach, protect a real target group, reveal a route, start a timed objective, commit an operative or security force, or change the final capture risk.

## Intelligence capability model

Event 39 uses a derived National Security Capacity score. It gives the intelligence agency major importance while preserving a complete non-DLC route.

The score should consider:

- agency existence and upgrade depth when La Résistance is active
- available operatives, operative experience, and whether an operative is committed to the case
- counterintelligence and defense upgrades
- decrypted or captured intelligence relevant to an affected foreign country
- number and quality of completed Event 39 operations
- stability, war support, and government legitimacy
- local police or security investment represented by ideas, decisions, and assigned resources
- active war, occupation, exile, and capital disruption
- investigator casualties, compromised ministries, and infiltrated security offices
- allied evidence sharing and prior foreign cell captures

Without La Résistance, the system derives equivalent capacity from stability, political control, command structure, encryption technology, radar and radio capabilities, military intelligence proxies, completed security decisions, and committed civilian industry. The non-DLC route must be viable, but it should require more visible domestic sacrifice than a developed agency.

Agency strength affects progress gain, mission duration, target protection, false lead risk, raid quality, foreign cell discovery, operative exposure, and final capture requirements. It does not grant a flat automatic victory.

## Investigation phases

### Phase 1: Secure the succession

The player responds to the murdered leader and keeps government functions intact. The phase uses a short public mission with objectives such as maintaining minimum stability, keeping the capital controlled, retaining a functioning command staff, and committing emergency protection.

The player chooses one opening posture:

- constitutional continuity, which protects legitimacy and evidence but develops slowly
- security emergency, which raises protection and immediate progress at a stability and civil capacity cost
- military cordon, which commits command resources and formations but risks politicizing the case

The choice changes starting Case Progress, target protection, public strain, and future route costs. No option should dominate every host.

### Phase 2: Preserve the scene

The category offers actions that protect witnesses, secure records, audit travel and communications, and isolate compromised offices. The player cannot reach the final capture stage if evidence integrity falls below the public minimum.

A timed mission can ask the country to protect the capital and one or two dynamically selected investigative states while the evidence team works. War, occupation, and supply conditions may change the state list. The targets must be named in tooltips.

### Phase 3: Map the network

The player selects investigative methods. Methods should include human intelligence, communications analysis, financial and supply tracing, interrogation of captured supporters, and international liaison. The exact set depends on DLC, agency strength, and whether the cult has evolved.

Each method has its own cost pattern, target, exposure, and adaptation. Repeating one method without variation yields lower progress. Combining two compatible methods creates a synergy bonus. Combining a public crackdown with covert infiltration raises a tradeoff between immediate safety and evidence quality.

### Phase 4: Protect likely targets

The player assigns a protection priority to one office group at a time. Groups include national leadership, military command, naval command, cabinet and advisors, intelligence staff, judiciary and administration, and investigators.

Protection is not a free global shield. Prioritizing one group improves its survival and may leave another group exposed. Expanded protection requires equipment, manpower, transport, intelligence capacity, or civilian industry. The player can change priority after a cooldown.

### Phase 5: Break the cell

Once an operational cell is identified, the player receives one to three active missions selected from a bounded pool. Missions ask for actions such as securing a rail node, protecting a witness transfer, holding a port against escape, deploying supplied divisions to a named region, maintaining agency coverage, stopping a border crossing, or intercepting equipment.

Mission success raises Case Progress and reduces local cell maturity. Failure can destroy evidence, expose an investigator, increase Network Reach, or move the cell. Mission durations should normally range from 90 to 180 days according to difficulty and current war conditions.

### Phase 6: Final operation

At Case Progress 85 or higher, the country may prepare the final capture operation. Preparation requires a real action, not one political power click. The player selects one route:

- silent arrest, which protects evidence and reduces public damage but needs strong intelligence and witness safety
- coordinated raids, which uses equipment and security forces and is more robust against a mature cult
- baited target, which risks one protected office group to draw out the murderer and can produce the fastest resolution
- foreign interception, available when the core network is preparing to flee through a known foreign route

The operation uses the public preparation state, current Case Progress, Network Reach, evidence integrity, witness safety, selected route, intelligence capacity, cell maturity, and protection posture. The result can be full capture, partial capture, escape with heavy losses, or a revealed wider network.

## Decision family

The normal government category should expose three to five primary decisions in each phase and no more than six. Obsolete actions disappear when the phase changes. Active missions remain between one and three.

### Core action families

| Family | Public purpose | Typical costs | Main result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Protect the succession | Keep government and command intact | civilian factory commitment, command power, support equipment | target protection, succession stability |
| Preserve evidence | Secure records, witnesses, and sites | civilian factories, manpower commitment, trains or trucks | evidence integrity, Case Progress |
| Commit intelligence assets | Assign agency or national security capacity | operative commitment, agency capacity, political attention | progress gain, route discovery |
| Trace movement support | Follow money, equipment, travel, or communications | intelligence assets, convoys, fuel, civilian burden | cell identification, Network Reach reduction |
| Protect an office group | Select one priority target family | support equipment, manpower, transport, command power | role-specific protection |
| Launch a cell mission | Start a named objective | equipment, unit presence, route access, time | cell damage, evidence, capture candidate |
| Share intelligence | Exchange verified evidence with affected countries | agency capacity, diplomatic access, convoys or civilian capacity | secondary defense, shared route discovery |
| Prepare final operation | Lock one capture plan | up to four route-specific costs | final resolution event |

No action may use more than four spendable cost types. Place control, unit presence, agency existence, and relations are requirements, not costs.

## Representative missions

### Guard the witness transfer

A key witness must move from one named state to the capital or a secured investigative center. The player must control the route, maintain rail or motor transport, and place a supplied force in the origin and destination. Success improves witness safety and Case Progress. Failure may kill the witness, reduce evidence integrity, or reveal an infiltrated office.

### Seal the border crossing

Investigators identify a likely escape corridor through a named border region. The player must hold the states, maintain minimum divisions, and prevent enemy control or supply collapse for the mission duration. Success captures couriers and reveals foreign links. Failure raises Network Reach and may seed a cell in the neighboring country.

### Hold the port watch

A cell plans to move people or records through a named port. The country commits naval or security resources, convoys, and intelligence coverage. Success intercepts the route. Failure can move the core murderer abroad if Evolution II is available.

### Audit the officer corps

Military files indicate an insider. The country must retain command readiness while temporarily restricting part of its officer corps. Success reveals an infiltrated cell and protects senior officers. Failure creates command disruption and raises the chance of a military target murder.

### Break the safe-house chain

The player raids a sequence of safe houses across a compact state group. A high-risk fast raid may preserve fewer documents. A slower coordinated action needs stronger control and more resources but offers better evidence. Success reduces local cell maturity. Failure moves the network and increases adaptation against the chosen method.

### Protect the funeral

The murdered leader's funeral or public memorial creates a concentrated target opportunity. The player chooses a public ceremony, restricted ceremony, or decoy arrangement. The mission tests protection, intelligence, and public legitimacy. It can expose the attacker, strengthen the cult's public mythology, or stabilize the succession.

## Evidence integrity and witness safety

Evidence integrity and witness safety are hidden support values with public stage summaries. The player sees concise states such as compromised, fragile, protected, or secure. Exact intermediate numbers are unnecessary.

Evidence integrity affects how much Case Progress can be converted into a final operation. Witness safety affects access to silent arrest and international testimony. A government may reach high Case Progress through raids while losing the evidence needed to prove a wider network. That route remains playable through coordinated raids, but it gives weaker foreign cooperation and a larger chance that surviving cells escape.

## Protection system

Each vulnerable role receives one of four Event 39 protection states:

- normal exposure
- monitored
- protected
- hardened

Protection comes from the selected office priority, agency capacity, security actions, active missions, route-specific ideas, and temporary crisis events. It decays after a serious incident or when the player changes priorities. Hardened protection should be expensive and temporary.

A target roll must first select an eligible role pool, then an eligible safe character or generic office casualty, then resolve protection. It must never pick a protected system owner and attempt to repair the damage after removal.

## Capture logic

A full capture is possible at baseline and Evolution I. Evolution II adds a global condition. The original murderer and core movement may be eliminated, but Event 39 remains active if an independent mature foreign cell has already become a valid movement heir. Full global resolution then requires all mature cells to be dismantled or reduced below the inheritance threshold.

At Evolution III or later, capturing the original murderer does not automatically remove the Assassin State. It creates a succession crisis inside the movement, lowers Cohesion, and may open surrender, fragmentation, or replacement-leader outcomes. The country can still be defeated militarily.

## Government response philosophies

The original host should develop one of three response profiles from player decisions and AI behavior:

- rule-bound investigation, which preserves legitimacy, evidence, and foreign trust but needs time and capacity
- integrated security campaign, which balances protection, raids, and intelligence at moderate institutional cost
- emergency repression, which gives immediate target safety and raid power but damages stability, evidence quality, and foreign cooperation while helping cult propaganda

These are behavior profiles, not full political routes. They influence costs, event options, AI preferences, aftermath, and the public description of the case.

## Secondary-country investigation

Foreign affected countries begin with partial knowledge. Their starting Local Case Progress reflects evidence already shared, global Network Reach, agency capacity, relations with the original host, and whether another country has captured a cell.

Secondary investigations are easier in three ways. Their likely target classes are known, countermeasures exist, and captured evidence can be shared. They are still vulnerable when their agency is weak, their government is unstable, or the cell has deep local maturity.

A secondary country can fully dismantle its own cell. That prevents local murders and revolt while the wider movement remains. It may continue sharing intelligence and protecting against reseeding. Repeated reseeding is blocked by a durable local immunity window and requires a concrete new network route.

## AI behavior

AI governments choose actions from the same public phase and cost logic. They prioritize succession and evidence first, then protection if a role group is exposed, then missions that fit available forces and logistics. They avoid a final operation below the safe public threshold unless a target crisis or imminent escape makes delay worse.

AI uses repression more often under low stability, war, authoritarian government, recent murders, or weak agency capacity. It uses rule-bound investigation more often with high stability, strong agencies, democratic legitimacy, and allied intelligence. The probability spec defines named scenarios and dominance expectations.

## Resolution benefits and limits

Successful governments receive a bounded counterintelligence legacy, temporary stability recovery, protected official transition, and improved defense against future Event 39 cells. The reward should change future play through agency, operative, and security effects. It must not create a permanent universal assassination immunity or an excessive flat modifier.

Failed or abusive investigations may leave a damaged judiciary, politicized officer corps, civil rights strain, distrust between allies, or institutional infiltration even after the killer is gone. These aftermaths should remain resolvable through normal government action and should not become permanent punishment.
