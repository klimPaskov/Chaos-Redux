# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 1: Core Design

## Playable promise

Event 030 gives one country access to knowledge that should not exist and asks whether that knowledge can be used without losing control of the person who carries it or the history that made it accurate.

The baseline must already support a complete campaign story. The traveler proves their origin, enters an access arrangement, gives specific advice, becomes the target of an international intelligence race, and reaches a durable outcome. Evolutions widen the consequences. They do not replace the baseline or postpone its resolution.

The event should feel useful from its first day. The host gains a modest immediate national spirit because the traveler has already distributed some knowledge to selected officials. The strongest rewards come from completing specific consultation objectives. The player should care about what the traveler recommends, whether the advice still applies, and what the government must risk to act on it.

## Host selection

The event selects one valid existing major country or one player-controlled country. A player-controlled non-major remains eligible. The original traveler appears only once.

A valid host must have a live capital or another controlled core state that can serve as the arrival location. Actual nonhuman countries and special system actors that do not use normal civilian institutions are excluded through the shared classifiers. A country already under a terminal world-end state cannot become the original host.

The event should prefer a player country enough that a normal single-player campaign can see the mechanic, while preserving the possibility that an AI major becomes the host and the player participates as a foreign rival. The exact weighting belongs in a named probability scenario and must be audited before completion.

When the capital is occupied, encircled, or invalid, the arrival state becomes the safest controlled core with strong infrastructure, a research facility, or an intelligence agency. The selection proof is saved before the opening event so later decisions do not re-roll the location.

## The traveler

The traveler is a fictional Chaos Redux character. This avoids assigning impossible future knowledge to a real person and permits a coherent original portrait, advisor role, leader route, and possible temporal duplicate.

The character has one hidden knowledge profile selected at arrival:

| Profile | Strongest consultation families | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic analyst | Military, diplomacy, and intelligence | Scientific advice is broad and institution-dependent |
| Systems engineer | Industry, science, and logistics | Political forecasts are less exact |
| Institutional historian | Domestic threats, diplomacy, and state reform | Battlefield advice is less detailed |

The profile affects consultation availability, mission difficulty, and AI preference. It is shown through a character trait and concise tooltip. It is not a fourth mechanic value.

The traveler remembers one possible future, not a perfect script of the current campaign. They can know private conversations, secret projects, later technologies, institutional failures, and major political outcomes from that future. They do not possess unlimited technical drawings, perfect battlefield vision, or knowledge of every event that the player has already changed.

## Core state model

Event 030 has four connected layers.

### Custody

Custody identifies the country that physically controls the original traveler. Only the custodian receives full consultations and the traveler character. Custody can change through defection, asylum, abduction, occupation, negotiated transfer, or scripted disappearance.

Every previous custodian keeps the knowledge that its institutions copied. That legacy is weaker than direct access and cannot recreate the traveler.

### Access arrangement

The host chooses one access arrangement after initial verification:

- Full government access
- Military access
- Scientific access
- Controlled confinement
- Public recognition
- Complete secrecy

The arrangement changes which consultations are easiest, how quickly knowledge spreads through the state, how foreign actors respond, and how the traveler judges the host. It can later be revised through a costly policy decision or a crisis event.

### Institutional knowledge

The national spirit represents knowledge already copied into ministries, military staffs, intelligence archives, industrial plans, and scientific institutions. It begins as a restrained mixed bonus. Completed consultations upgrade the spirit by adding a bounded specialty effect or by replacing it with a stronger stage. The system must not create a long stack of separate advice spirits.

If the traveler is lost, the active advisor and direct consultation access are removed. The host keeps a reduced legacy form whose strength depends on completed consultations and how well records were protected.

### Temporal disturbance

Timeline Divergence is global and survives the traveler. Once future knowledge has changed decisions, the event can evolve even if the original traveler dies. Evolution I requires a living or missing traveler who can still act. Evolutions II through V can continue from the temporal disturbance itself.

## Baseline phases

### Phase 1: Arrival

The traveler appears under controlled circumstances and requests contact with the state. The opening should not depend on a vague unexplained letter. The character gives one piece of evidence that can be checked within days.

The host chooses whether to isolate, hear, detain, or discreetly test the visitor. Refusing all contact ends direct access but cannot erase the event. Officials may still copy the initial proof, rumors may escape, and Timeline Divergence may begin at a low level.

### Phase 2: Verification

The host receives a short verification chain drawn from three proof families:

- knowledge of a secret project or hidden facility
- knowledge of a private conversation, sealed order, or undisclosed political arrangement
- a near-term forecast with a bounded validation window

At least two proof families should be used before the traveler is treated as authentic. A high-chaos opening can validate the first proof immediately and begin the second with higher Exposure.

### Phase 3: Access decision

The government chooses the access arrangement. This choice establishes the first national spirit stage, consultation emphasis, rumor risk, and internal sector response.

### Phase 4: Consultation cycle

The host may maintain one active consultation mission at a time. The first consultation is offered soon after access is set. Later consultations use cooldowns, profile emphasis, current wars, industry, research, diplomacy, and internal threats to choose useful targets.

### Phase 5: International race

A bounded group of foreign countries becomes interested. They observe, recruit, steal, forge, offer asylum, abduct, or assassinate. The host receives countermeasures and can also share selected knowledge with allies.

### Phase 6: Durable outcome

The baseline reaches a durable state after enough evidence, one major custody crisis, or a completed set of consultations. The traveler may remain trusted, remain confined, transfer custody, die, disappear, or become public. The event remains eligible for later evolutions where their prerequisites still exist.

## Baseline completion states

| State | Direct consultations | Institutional legacy | Later effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trusted advisor | Full | Strong and upgradeable | Best access to Evolution I |
| Controlled asset | Restricted | Strong technical record | Higher defection and internal control risk |
| Public figure | Full but exposed | Strong and widely copied | Foreign actions intensify and exact secrecy cannot return |
| Defected or abducted | Moves to new custodian | Origin keeps reduced legacy | Rivalry and counter-operation chain continues |
| Assassinated | Ends | Reduced legacy | Evolution I normally closes, later evolutions remain possible |
| Disappeared | Suspended | Reduced legacy | A later reappearance is possible, but never guaranteed |
| Fragments dispersed | Limited to current custodian | Several countries gain minor fragment spirits | Divergence grows and foreign alignment becomes broader |

## Evolution ladder

| Evolution | Chaos gate | Base pacing after prerequisites | Main new play |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| I, The Traveler's Ambition | `200+` | about 105 days | political power struggle around the original traveler |
| II, The Door to the Past | `400+` | about 135 days | limited historical arrivals and claimant movements |
| III, Visitors from the Future | `600+` | about 150 days | later-era visitors, incompatible futures, and modern political methods |
| IV, Epoch Breakdown | `800+` | about 120 days | temporal fauna, displaced formations, salvage, diseases, anomalies, and machine scouts |
| V, War of the Possible Futures | `1000+` | about 90 days | global alignments, portal control, derived Temporal Stability, and machine world-end eligibility |

These are tuning anchors. Implementation must use the evolution MTTH framework and named factors. Evolution state gives zero Chaos. Chaos changes only from concrete consequences.

## High-chaos first firing

When Event 030 first fires after one or more evolution thresholds are already met, the opening becomes compressed and more dangerous. The traveler still proves their origin and the player still chooses access. The event then seeds the eligible higher layer without waiting through an empty baseline.

A high-chaos opening must avoid five same-day popups. It should use one opening super-event, one host event, and a bounded sequence of delayed reports. Each active evolution is still recorded through the shared evolution log with its correct stage and actor context.

## Cluster decision

Event 030 has no cluster. Its connections to other events are conditional integrations, not a request to fire several normal events as one incident. Adding a cluster would confuse thematic interaction with the cluster pacing system and could cause unrelated major content to fire together.

## Content that should remain absent

The baseline does not create a full scripted GUI, a global country roster, a technology tree, a separate portal currency, or a full focus tree for every returned person. Those surfaces would add upkeep without improving the main choices.

# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 2: Baseline Progression and Public Values

## Public value budget

The player actively tracks three persistent custom values. No other Event 030 number should be shown as a long-term counter.

| Value | Scope | Question answered | Main responses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Credibility | Current custodian | How much does the government trust the traveler | verify claims, complete advice, expose forgery, revise access |
| Exposure | Traveler and custody network | How widely is the traveler known | secrecy, relocation, public recognition, counterintelligence, selective sharing |
| Timeline Divergence | Global | How far has the campaign moved from the remembered future | limit interventions, close portals, reconcile anomalies, accept degraded predictions |

Hidden values may track sector support, operation strength, visitor reliability, portal severity, machine footprint, incident cooldowns, and AI scoring. They feed public states and consequences instead of becoming extra meters.

## Credibility

Credibility runs from `0` to `100` and belongs to the current custodian. A new custodian does not inherit the previous government's full trust. The new value begins from the strength of transferred evidence, the method of transfer, and the public record.

### Stages

| Range | Stage | Effect on play |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| `0-19` | Discredited | only containment, investigation, expulsion, and one final proof path remain |
| `20-39` | Provisional | basic consultation families and controlled access become available |
| `40-59` | Established | normal consultations, staff recruitment, and selective sharing become reliable |
| `60-79` | Trusted | advanced country-specific advice and stronger institutional copying unlock |
| `80-100` | Indispensable | the strongest consultation missions and most dangerous political influence become possible |

Credibility should move through evidence and outcomes. It should not rise from waiting.

### Typical gains

- a secret project or private fact is verified
- a near-term prediction succeeds within its declared window
- a consultation objective is completed under the conditions the traveler described
- a forged document is exposed before the government acts on it
- a foreign operation confirms that rival governments believe the traveler is genuine

### Typical losses

- a prediction fails after the timeline has not diverged enough to excuse it
- the government acts on forged advice
- the traveler conceals a material personal interest
- two verified visitors expose a contradiction that the traveler cannot explain
- the government publicly rejects a prediction that later fails

Failure at high Timeline Divergence should reduce Credibility less than the same failure in a recognizable timeline. The interface should explain that exact dates are becoming unreliable.

## Exposure

Exposure runs from `0` to `100`. It follows the original traveler and the network of verified copies. Public knowledge creates a floor. Once the world has received confirmed images, speeches, or official recognition, complete secrecy cannot be restored.

### Stages

| Range | Stage | Foreign behavior |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| `0-19` | Contained | only countries with strong intelligence access can act |
| `20-39` | Rumored | nearby majors and close allies begin observation and recruitment |
| `40-59` | Suspected | several majors can steal notes, forge advice, or offer asylum |
| `60-79` | Confirmed | abduction, assassination, public pressure, and diplomatic demands become plausible |
| `80-100` | Public | global news, open political movements, and broad fragment proliferation become possible |

### Typical gains

- unrestricted access creates a wide staff circle
- a consultation leaks
- a foreign operation fails publicly
- the host uses the traveler in propaganda
- the host grants public recognition
- a returned figure confirms the temporal disturbance

### Typical reductions

- moving the traveler to a new secure site
- replacing a broad staff circle with compartmentalized teams
- exposing a forged story that was mistaken for real proof
- feeding false schedules to hostile networks

Exposure reduction should cost capacity and limit consultation speed. It is not a cheap meter purchase.

## Timeline Divergence

Timeline Divergence runs from `0` to `100` and is global. It measures how much the remembered future has lost predictive value and how much temporal activity the campaign can sustain.

### Stages

| Range | Stage | Forecast condition | Temporal condition |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| `0-19` | Recognizable | exact short-term forecasts remain possible | no persistent anomalies |
| `20-39` | Altered | dates become ranges and some actors behave differently | small incidents may occur |
| `40-59` | Unstable | advice describes patterns and critical choices | portal traces and contradictory records appear |
| `60-79` | Fragmented | exact political outcomes are unreliable | returned figures and material incidents become easier |
| `80-100` | Broken | only broad structural knowledge remains | strong anomalies, machine activity, and future faction conflict become likely |

### Concrete increases

- acting on advice that changes a major diplomatic or military outcome
- averting or starting a war the traveler remembers as historically important
- deploying adapted future technology
- placing the traveler in government
- allowing a returned figure or future visitor to take power
- restoring a historical state or dynasty
- keeping a portal open for exploitation
- allowing machines to seize industry

### Concrete reductions

- closing an active portal
- dismantling a future machine node
- resolving a claimant crisis without creating a new historical state
- returning displaced formations or populations safely when a route exists
- destroying dangerous future material after studying it
- completing the Continuity Directorate's coordinated closure projects

Reductions should be harder than increases. The player can stabilize the world, but cannot restore the exact original future after major changes.

## Temporal Stability in Evolution V

Evolution V activates a global Temporal Stability presentation. It uses the same underlying state as Timeline Divergence.

`Temporal Stability = 100 - Timeline Divergence`

Only one number is shown at a time. Earlier surfaces use Timeline Divergence. The Evolution V global surface uses Temporal Stability because the player is managing collapse. Tooltips explain the conversion. This satisfies the source idea without adding a fourth persistent value.

## Verification chain

The opening verification chain selects two or three proofs that fit the host.

### Secret project proof

The traveler identifies a real hidden facility, project name, procurement route, diplomatic channel, code phrase, or research obstacle. The proof should use country-specific content where a researched and safe package exists. Generic fallback content reacts to the host's current technology, agency, military production, and active focuses.

This proof should never reveal a secret route that the player has not unlocked through normal play. It confirms knowledge inside the government, not hidden developer information.

### Private conversation proof

The traveler recounts a recent private exchange involving the head of government, a minister, a commander, or a diplomat. The event names the relevant in-world actor when a valid character exists. Generic prose describes a sealed meeting or order without inventing a false real historical quote.

### Near-term forecast proof

The traveler predicts a bounded event within roughly 20 to 60 days. The candidate pool must be based on current state and must not promise something the engine cannot reasonably produce.

Useful forecasts include:

- a foreign diplomatic move already supported by campaign state
- a production or supply bottleneck that can be measured
- an enemy buildup along a real border
- a likely coup, strike, or faction split supported by current instability
- a weather or disaster pattern only when the owning system can prove it

The forecast should be written as a range or condition, not a scripted certainty that forces unrelated AI behavior.

## Access arrangements

### Full government access

The traveler can consult ministries, military staffs, intelligence, and scientific institutions. Consultation variety is highest. Exposure rises faster, staff recruitment by foreign powers becomes easier, and the ambition route gains broad support.

### Military access

Military and intelligence consultations are stronger. Scientific and domestic reform advice is slower. Officers become the strongest internal sector. Foreign countries prioritize abduction and staff recruitment.

### Scientific access

Industry, science, logistics, and technology adaptation are stronger. The traveler has less direct political access. Exposure through scientists and industrial contractors remains significant.

### Controlled confinement

Exposure growth is slow and the host can protect records. Credibility gains are slower because access to evidence is restricted. The traveler is more likely to seek asylum, mislead guards, or build support among the officials allowed to visit.

### Public recognition

The host gains diplomatic prestige, rapid staff coordination, and public political effects. Exposure rises to a permanent high floor. Rival governments can act openly, and later arrivals gain immediate authenticity.

### Complete secrecy

The host receives the best early counterintelligence position. Consultation capacity is narrow, records are vulnerable to a single breach, and internal services gain disproportionate control. If secrecy fails, the Exposure increase is larger because foreign powers learn that the government hid a strategic asset.

## Institutional knowledge spirit

The national spirit has four lifecycle forms.

| Working label | Condition | Design role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unverified Forecasts | arrival before verification | small mixed benefit and administrative friction |
| Directed Foreknowledge | verified traveler with direct access | modest permanent base plus consultation support |
| Distributed Future Knowledge | several successful consultations | stronger but still bounded institutional benefit |
| Scattered Forecasts | traveler lost | reduced legacy based on copied knowledge |

The spirit should combine a few coherent effects. It must not grant broad maximum bonuses to research, production, intelligence, diplomacy, doctrine, and planning at the same time. The consultation system carries the strongest specialty rewards.

## Rumor progression

Rumors are consequence events, not flavor-only text.

- Scientists may demand wider access or leak a technical claim.
- Officers may alter plans around private forecasts.
- Journalists may find inconsistent schedules or witness unusual security.
- Diplomats may ask whether the host is making decisions from impossible information.
- Industrialists may compete for predicted contracts.
- Opposition groups may accuse the government of hiding a prophet, fraud, or foreign asset.

Rumor events alter Exposure, stability, foreign opinion, internal sector support, and operation eligibility. They should use the current access arrangement and public stage so the same rumor does not repeat without change.

## Prediction degradation

At low divergence, the traveler may give dates and named actors. At medium divergence, the traveler gives date ranges, trigger conditions, and strategic warnings. At high divergence, the traveler can identify structural risks, such as an exposed supply network or unstable coalition, but cannot name the exact outcome.

This degradation keeps the traveler useful without allowing permanent omniscience. The tooltip for every consultation should state the current confidence class.

# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 3: Consultations and the International Intelligence War

## Decision presentation

The host receives one main decision category with a static category picture and a compact attached display for Credibility, Exposure, and Timeline Divergence. The category shows three to five relevant actions in its current phase. Six primary actions is the hard maximum. Only one consultation mission may be active at a time. One to three defensive or crisis missions may coexist when foreign pressure is high.

A full separate mechanic window is not required. The foreign target layer may use the selected-target decision pattern so the player inspects one interested country at a time.

## Consultation cycle

A consultation is a government commitment. The player chooses a subject, pays costs that fit the action, receives a concrete forecast, and is given a timed objective. Completing the objective proves that the government acted on the advice and grants the main reward. Ignoring or failing the objective can lower Credibility or create a later consequence.

Consultations use a shared cooldown, normally around 90 days before modifiers. The hidden knowledge profile can shorten one family and lengthen another. High Credibility unlocks stronger variants. High Timeline Divergence converts precise variants into broader strategic versions.

## Consultation families

### Military forecast

The traveler identifies one real operational risk or opportunity. The target must be connected to a current enemy, border, front, naval route, air region, capital approach, or supply corridor.

Possible objectives include:

- hold named states or a capital approach for a defined period
- place a required number of supplied divisions in named states
- repair or protect a named railway and supply route
- move a fleet to a threatened sea region
- establish air superiority or radar coverage in a named region
- avoid beginning a predicted losing offensive until conditions improve

Costs may use army experience, command power, support equipment, fuel, trains, or tied-down divisions. A single action may spend at most four types. Success gives a meaningful operational advantage, doctrine progress, planning support, or a revealed enemy weakness. Failure should not delete armies or force a war.

### Industrial bottleneck

The traveler identifies a production, transport, resource, or construction failure that the remembered government handled badly.

Possible objectives include:

- repair a named rail connection
- expand a supply hub, port, infrastructure line, or factory region
- shift a production family before a predicted shortage
- maintain a stockpile of trucks, trains, convoys, or fuel for a deadline
- protect a named industrial state from bombing or sabotage

Costs may use civilian factory burden, trains, trucks, convoys, fuel, or production efficiency. Success gives a visible map improvement, retained production efficiency, or an upgraded institutional spirit. It should not be a generic construction-speed purchase.

### Scientific shortcut

The traveler explains which line of inquiry succeeds and which approach wastes years. The government still needs laboratories, engineers, material, and adaptation.

Possible objectives include:

- complete or begin a valid technology in a named category
- dedicate an industrial facility or special project slot where supported
- secure a research state against bombing and espionage
- obtain a resource or component needed for adaptation
- choose between a faster unstable prototype and a slower reliable program

The baseline cannot grant an unrestricted ahead-of-time technology. Rewards should use valid research bonuses, doctrine mastery, special project progress, or the Event 016 custom technology API when an approved custom technology package exists.

### Diplomatic forecast

The traveler identifies a likely betrayal, coalition failure, negotiation opening, or guarantee problem. The advice must react to live relations and alliances.

Possible objectives include:

- reach a relation threshold with a named country
- negotiate access, a guarantee, or a non-aggression arrangement
- prevent a vulnerable ally from leaving a faction
- secure convoy or trade access before a crisis
- expose a rival offer before a country accepts it

Success changes a real diplomatic relationship or unlocks a follow-up decision. It should not consist only of an opinion modifier.

### Counterintelligence map

The traveler describes an infiltration method, future intelligence failure, or compromised official. The host receives a bounded investigation mission.

Possible objectives include:

- build or maintain counterintelligence capacity
- protect the arrival site or records office
- identify one interested foreign country
- run a false consultation schedule
- capture or turn a foreign operative through the existing agency systems

Success can expose a foreign operation, grant a counter-operation, protect the institutional archive, or lower Exposure. Failure can leak notes or make a forged advice operation easier.

### Domestic threat assessment

The traveler identifies a coup network, extremist movement, secession risk, political assassination, or institutional fracture supported by the current country state.

The player chooses a restrained, political, intelligence, or coercive response. The objective may require securing named states, changing an advisor or law, maintaining stability through a deadline, or resolving a faction dispute.

Heavy-handed success can protect the government while lowering legitimacy or raising condemnation if abuses become public. The consultation must never provide a free universal purge button.

## Advice result classes

| Result | Meaning | Reward direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact success | the campaign still resembles the remembered future | strongest credibility gain and precise reward |
| Strategic success | the exact details changed, but the structural warning was correct | normal reward and smaller credibility gain |
| Partial success | the player solved one part and accepted another cost | mixed reward and follow-up choice |
| Failure through divergence | the advice was reasonable but the timeline changed | small credibility loss or none, stronger divergence warning |
| Failure through forgery or deceit | false information entered the process | major investigation and credibility consequence |

## Foreign interest registry

The event creates a sparse bounded registry of interested countries. The ordinary cap is four. Public recognition or high Exposure can expand it to six. The registry should prefer:

- existing majors
- player-controlled countries
- countries with strong intelligence capability
- enemies and strategic rivals of the host
- close allies who seek shared access
- countries with relevant scientific or industrial capacity
- countries connected to a returned figure or future visitor

The registry must not use a recurring whole-world scan. It is rebuilt only after major changes, such as public revelation, custody transfer, a new evolution, or a country becoming invalid.

## Foreign operations

### Observe consultations

A low-risk operation that gathers schedules, staff names, and broad subject matter. Success gives a small intelligence benefit and enables later operations. Failure may reveal the observer without proving the traveler exists.

### Recruit the staff

The foreign country targets guards, clerks, scientists, officers, translators, or industrial liaisons. Success grants a Future Fragment and increases Exposure. The host can dismiss, turn, or watch the compromised staff member.

### Steal the notes

A higher-risk operation against the institutional archive. Success grants a stronger fragment, copies one completed consultation specialty, or reveals a future objective. The theft never recreates full direct access.

### Spread forged advice

The foreign country plants a false prediction or altered consultation note. The forgery must name a plausible decision and create a testable consequence. If the host exposes it, the attacker loses network strength and Credibility may rise. If the host acts on it, the attacker gains a strategic benefit and the host may suffer a concrete setback.

### Offer asylum

The foreign country privately offers safety, status, and controlled access. The traveler considers the current custody arrangement, mistreatment, ideological compatibility, host stability, foreign credibility, and chance of safe travel. A trusted traveler in a respectful arrangement should rarely defect.

### Attempt abduction

Abduction requires confirmed knowledge, a mature network, a viable route, and a country willing to accept diplomatic risk. Outcomes include failure, capture of a double, a wounded traveler, successful transfer, an exposed sponsor, or disappearance before either side arrives.

### Attempt assassination

Assassination is reserved for countries that see the traveler as an immediate existential threat, cannot acquire access, and can plausibly reach the target. It is expensive, risky, and strongly affected by public status. A successful killing ends the original traveler but leaves copied knowledge and temporal disturbance.

## Host countermeasures

### Move the traveler

The host selects another valid secure state. Moving disrupts consultation access, costs transport and security capacity, and can lower Exposure if the route remains secret. It cannot be spammed while a consultation or attack is active.

### Assign a guard detail

The host commits command power, support equipment, manpower, and possibly divisions near the site. Strong guards reduce abduction and assassination success but increase the number of people who know the traveler exists.

### Restrict access

The host reduces staff access and consultation speed. This lowers leak risk but can reduce Credibility and increase the chance that the traveler seeks another patron.

### Create false schedules

The host runs decoy meetings and transport routes. This consumes intelligence capacity and may capture a foreign cell or cause an attacker to seize a double.

### Share selected information

The host gives an ally one verified fragment or consultation result. This improves alliance coordination, reduces pressure for theft, raises Timeline Divergence, and gives the ally a limited national spirit. The ally does not gain custody.

### Use the traveler as bait

A high-risk counterintelligence action that opens a false vulnerability. Success exposes one or more networks. Failure can cause injury, abduction, death, or a public scandal. AI should use it only with strong security and a meaningful threat.

## The double outcome

A captured double is a prepared official, operative, or decoy who knows enough to survive initial questioning. The double does not become a second traveler and cannot grant consultations.

The captor receives a temporary investigation chain. It can expose the deception, trade the double, spread a false public claim, or mistakenly build policy around fabricated knowledge. The host gains a counterintelligence advantage if the operation remains secret.

## Custody transfer

A successful transfer moves the character, direct consultation category, current access arrangement review, and relevant Exposure state to the new custodian. Credibility is recalculated from available evidence and the transfer method.

The old host keeps its institutional legacy, completed consultation records, and grievance against the captor where appropriate. Active missions that require the traveler close cleanly. Missions whose knowledge is already distributed may continue.

## Multiplayer behavior

Only one country can hold the original traveler. Every player country can become an interested country if valid. Human rivals receive the same foreign-operation choices as AI rivals. The host should not see hidden operation ownership until evidence exposes it.

A human-controlled country that gains custody receives the host category immediately. A human-controlled former host keeps its legacy and counter-recovery options. No player receives a ghost copy of the traveler.

## Baseline AI goals

The host AI should decide whether it values secrecy, broad access, military use, scientific use, or public legitimacy. It should consider war state, stability, intelligence capacity, research capacity, current enemies, ideology, and leader security.

Foreign AI should choose acquisition, sabotage, diplomacy, or restraint based on relations, intelligence capability, ideological alignment, proximity, threat, and whether it already holds useful fragments. It should not repeatedly attempt a low-chance assassination or abduction.

All weighted choices require a probability audit with complete candidate pools and named scenarios before completion.

# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 4: Evolution I, The Traveler's Ambition

## Evolution purpose

Evolution I turns privileged advice into a domestic political struggle. The traveler has learned who will rise, who will fail, which institutions survive, and which officials will later regret their choices. That knowledge creates a political network even when the traveler claims to have no political plan.

This evolution requires the original traveler to be alive, missing with a valid reappearance route, or physically controlled by a country. A confirmed death closes this track. It does not block later temporal evolutions.

## Activation

The normal gate is `200+` Chaos, successful verification, and enough time for a political network to form. The base MTTH is about 105 days after the gate. The following factors shorten it:

- Credibility above `60`
- full government or public access
- low host stability
- a weak or unpopular head of government
- repeated successful consultations
- the traveler knowing the future careers or deaths of current officials
- a foreign abduction or assassination attempt that convinces supporters that ordinary protection is inadequate

Controlled confinement and low Credibility lengthen the MTTH. They do not make ambition impossible. Confinement can produce a more conspiratorial network.

## Hidden support sectors

The evolution tracks support inside six sectors. These are internal scores, not public meters.

- executive officials
- military officers
- intelligence services
- scientific institutions
- industrial planners
- political and ideological movements

The player sees the current coalition through event text, named supporters, decision availability, and warnings. The interface should not display six percentages.

A peaceful route normally requires broad civilian and institutional support. An armed route requires meaningful military or security support. A full civil war requires a territorial base, active armed supporters, and a failed political settlement.

## Opening crisis

The evolution begins when the traveler demands a formal status, publishes a policy memorandum, refuses an order, reveals a future scandal, or receives an organized petition from supporters. The exact opening reacts to the access arrangement and host politics.

The host must decide whether the traveler remains an advisor, receives emergency authority, enters an open political contest, takes government office, returns to confinement, is arrested, or is expelled.

The player should understand the visible tradeoff. Greater authority improves coordination and lets the traveler apply future knowledge directly. It also raises Timeline Divergence, threatens current elites, and makes preventive government more likely.

## Political routes

### Bound advisor charter

The government negotiates a written charter that limits the traveler to advice, requires documented consultations, and forbids unilateral orders. The traveler keeps the advisor role. The host gains a stable consultation framework and reduces coup risk.

The price is slower action and a risk that military or scientific supporters consider the charter a waste of unique knowledge. The charter can fail if the government repeatedly ignores successful advice or secretly violates its terms.

### Emergency authority

The traveler receives temporary authority over a named crisis, such as war planning, industrial coordination, intelligence reform, or national mobilization. The authority has a public duration and objective.

Success can create a strong route-specific institutional upgrade. Failure damages Credibility and strengthens opponents. Extending the mandate without a new crisis increases divergence and authoritarian pressure.

### Head of government appointment

The host may appoint the traveler through a legal or established governmental route when the country system supports it. The character becomes head of government or country leader with a powerful trait focused on planning, negotiation, intelligence, logistics, and institutional coordination.

The appointment should not grant every bonus at maximum strength. The route replaces the direct advisor arrangement with a government package that is stronger in coordination and weaker in exact consultation access. The traveler spends more time governing and less time giving specialized forecasts.

### Open political contest

A government with elections, party competition, congresses, councils, or another public mechanism may force the traveler to build a movement and seek office openly. This route can produce a peaceful victory, a negotiated coalition, public defeat, or radicalization after a disputed result.

The route uses the country's existing political structure where possible. It does not invent an election system for a state that has none.

### Arrest and investigation

The host detains the traveler and investigates political contacts. This can dismantle the network, uncover a foreign sponsor, or confirm that the traveler planned a takeover. It can also turn a manageable movement into a martyr campaign.

The action should require intelligence capacity, loyal forces, and a secure detention site. A weak host that arrests an indispensable traveler without those conditions risks an immediate rescue attempt or coup.

### Expulsion or negotiated transfer

The host may send the traveler into exile, offer them to an ally, or negotiate a monitored transfer to a neutral country. This removes the immediate domestic struggle while spreading knowledge and raising foreign competition.

### Secret removal

A threatened government may attempt to make the traveler disappear. This is a high-risk covert act. Failure creates public scandal, condemnation, or armed revolt. Success removes the character but leaves records, supporters, and the wider temporal disturbance.

## Traveler government

A traveler-led government attempts to prevent known failures and import institutions from the remembered future. Its gameplay should center on choosing which lessons remain useful after divergence.

### Government strengths

- faster conversion of consultation results into decisions
- stronger strategic planning and logistics coordination
- improved counterintelligence against known historical methods
- better identification of industrial bottlenecks
- access to preventive diplomatic initiatives
- reduced cost for adapting broad future concepts to the current era

### Government risks

- preventive detention based on predicted crimes
- hostility toward officials whose future conduct the traveler remembers
- premature wars against threats that have not developed
- overconfidence in institutions that succeeded in another timeline
- growing distance between the government and the public
- foreign coalitions seeking to contain a state that appears unnaturally successful

The player must face specific choices. The route should never turn these risks into one passive corruption meter.

## Preventive government decisions

### Watch the predicted threat

The government places a person, faction, or region under surveillance. This improves intelligence and may prevent a real plot. It consumes agency capacity and can become public.

### Restrict future offenders

The government removes an official, commander, industrialist, or agitator before the remembered offense occurs. The immediate strategic risk falls. Stability, legitimacy, or condemnation may worsen depending on evidence and publicity.

### Change the remembered war

The traveler argues for an early deterrent, alliance, mobilization, or preventive strike. The player receives a real preparation mission and a visible diplomatic risk. No preventive war should be granted as a free instant war goal without preparation and valid targets.

### Build the successful institution

The government imports one remembered institutional model into military planning, logistics, industry, intelligence, science, or diplomacy. The project requires time, staff, and material. It upgrades one existing spirit or decision family.

### Suppress the failed institution

The traveler identifies an institution that later failed or was captured. Reform can improve long-term resilience while provoking the current officials who depend on it.

## Crisis escalation

The political struggle uses four escalation levels.

### Cabinet conflict

Ministers resign, services compete for access, and parties demand an inquiry. The crisis remains political.

### Attempted removal

The existing leadership or the traveler attempts a legal dismissal, arrest, or emergency maneuver. Support sectors choose sides.

### Coup or regional revolt

Armed supporters move against key institutions or seize one valid regional base. This requires military or security support and cannot happen from a purely academic movement.

### Civil war

A full civil war occurs only when the claimant has durable territorial support, enough formations to fight, and no negotiated route remains. Some army units join based on hidden sector support, current command loyalty, local politics, and the country's existing civil-war systems.

The event should prefer a coup, leadership change, or limited revolt over a full civil war when the country is too small or the support base is too weak.

## Peaceful victory

A peaceful traveler victory can occur through appointment, election, council vote, coalition settlement, or accepted emergency succession. The outcome must update leader, advisor availability, national spirit, party or governing arrangement where relevant, AI strategy, and foreign reactions.

The traveler does not automatically change ideology. Their government style depends on the route that brought them to power and the institutions they choose to preserve.

## Defeat, imprisonment, and exile

If the traveler loses, the host chooses imprisonment, continued advisory service under stricter rules, exile, amnesty, or secret removal. The choice affects future visitors. A state that treated the first traveler lawfully finds later arrivals easier to negotiate with. A state known for killing temporal visitors makes later figures form defensive movements sooner.

## Reappearance

A traveler who disappeared without a confirmed death may reappear during this evolution. Reappearance should use one of three explanations:

- the traveler escaped through a temporary portal
- a foreign service hid the traveler and now reveals custody
- the traveler staged the disappearance to measure the political network

The explanation is selected once and saved. Reappearance is uncommon and must not erase the consequences of the original disappearance.

## Evolution outcomes

- the traveler remains a limited advisor under a durable charter
- the traveler leads a temporary emergency administration
- the traveler becomes head of government or country leader
- the traveler wins or loses an open political contest
- the traveler is arrested, expelled, transferred, killed, or disappears
- a coup succeeds or fails
- a limited regional revolt is contained or becomes civil war
- copied knowledge becomes the basis of a permanent state institution

## Chaos changes

Evolution activation gives zero Chaos. One-shot consequence examples include:

- peaceful appointment of the traveler to national leadership: modest increase
- failed coup with limited violence: modest increase
- full civil war over the traveler: larger event-owned increase, separate from generic war and death sources
- preventive detention program becoming public: increase based on severity
- negotiated charter that ends the crisis: small reduction
- lawful defeat and reintegration of the movement: small reduction

Exact amounts belong in centralized tuning and must avoid duplicating generic war, annexation, death, or condemnation sources.

# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 5: Evolution II, The Door to the Past

## Evolution purpose

Evolution II creates a limited set of historical arrivals whose reputations, claims, and methods collide with the modern state. Each arrival must create public consequences. A returned figure cannot enter silently as a free advisor.

The normal gate is `400+` Chaos, Evolution I resolved or safely bypassed, and Timeline Divergence high enough to support stable portals. The base MTTH is about 135 days. A public traveler, an active traveler government, an unresolved temporal disappearance, and copied knowledge in several countries shorten the pacing.

## Campaign limits

A normal campaign should receive one initial historical figure and no more than three active historical figure packages at the same time. A fourth can appear only after one earlier package has resolved or during a high-intensity manual scenario.

Selection uses a curated eligibility registry. The system does not scan every historical person or create a random name generator.

The initial pool contains nine flagship figures from the source brief:

- Napoleon Bonaparte
- Julius Caesar
- Alexander the Great
- Genghis Khan
- Frederick the Great
- Joan of Arc
- Saladin
- Simón Bolívar
- Tokugawa Ieyasu

Any later addition requires a researched package with host logic, political role, limitations, assets, AI, outcomes, and cleanup.

## Standard arrival sequence

Every historical package follows the same six-stage architecture while preserving figure-specific content.

### Arrival report

The figure appears in a place connected to their life, conquest, legacy, dynasty, or political memory. The first report identifies witnesses, clothing, language, artifacts, behavior, and the immediate public response.

### Authenticity test

The host tests language, private historical knowledge, scars, handwriting, military memory, material evidence, or personal relationships preserved in archives. The test should not depend on generated direct quotations.

### Initial custody

The government chooses protection, public presentation, detention, military consultation, scholarly examination, exile, or transfer.

### Movement formation

Followers, opponents, veterans, officers, monarchists, republicans, religious groups, regional claimants, or foreign sponsors organize around the figure. The movement uses hidden support and visible events instead of another public meter.

### Power decision

The figure accepts or rejects an advisor role, military commission, symbolic status, political office, or restoration claim. The host can negotiate, suppress, co-opt, or contest the claim.

### Resolution

The package ends in integration, defeat, exile, death, imprisonment, leadership, limited regional revolt, full civil war, or restoration. Surviving consequences remain through characters, ideas, claims, diplomatic memories, or a country package.

## Shared historical limitations

Returned figures know their own era. They do not know modern combined arms, radio command, industrial mobilization, mass-party politics, modern bureaucracy, aviation, chemical warfare, or nuclear technology unless they learn it.

A returned commander begins with strong leadership traits and a severe modernity limitation. Training, staff work, and battlefield experience can mitigate the limitation. The player should not receive an instantly perfect field marshal.

A returned ruler has strong symbolic legitimacy among some groups and strong rejection among others. Their historical reputation does not grant automatic modern political legitimacy.

## Napoleon Bonaparte package

### Eligible hosts and regions

France and French successor governments are primary. Corsica, French-aligned exiles, and a valid French civil-war actor may qualify. Italy can receive a secondary Napoleonic legacy incident, but Napoleon should not normally become an Italian national leader.

### Movement

The movement attracts officers, veterans, centralizers, legal reformers, imperial nostalgists, and people who believe the current regime has wasted French power.

### Gameplay identity

Napoleon offers operational concentration, administrative centralization, legal reform, and rapid promotion by competence. His risks are personal rule, preventive war, continental ambition, and underestimating industrial attrition.

### Main routes

- military adviser under civilian authority
- emergency commander during national defeat
- constitutional Bonapartist political movement
- imperial claimant and civil-war leader
- detained national symbol used by the government
- foreign-backed exile claimant

A successful restoration may use a French Empire cosmetic identity and a focused decision overlay. It should not receive a full replacement tree unless the restoration becomes durable enough to justify one.

## Julius Caesar package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Italy, a Roman restoration state, or a government controlling Rome is primary.

### Movement

The movement attracts officers, classical nationalists, veterans, populist organizers, and elites who see dictatorship as an answer to paralysis.

### Gameplay identity

Caesar excels at political coalition, military initiative, personal loyalty, and decisive civil conflict. His ascent carries a direct republican-collapse risk. He must learn modern logistics, industrial war, and mass administration.

### Main routes

- military and political adviser
- emergency dictator with a fixed mandate
- public contest over republican institutions
- Roman restoration claimant
- assassination crisis involving supporters and opponents

The route should focus on the conflict between personal authority and modern state institutions. It should not reduce Caesar to a generic Roman empire button.

## Alexander the Great package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Greece and Macedonia are primary. A broader Hellenic, Persian, Egyptian, or Levantine host can receive a secondary succession claim when it controls a relevant legacy region.

### Movement

The movement attracts expansionist officers, Hellenic nationalists, youth organizations, and regional groups seeking a transnational imperial identity.

### Gameplay identity

Alexander provides audacious operational tempo, personal battlefield leadership, and a vision of cross-regional empire. His weaknesses are succession, institutional depth, and the scale of modern supply.

### Main routes

- commander under a modern general staff
- pan-Hellenic political symbol
- claimant seeking a new Macedonian empire
- eastern campaign movement supported by regional collaborators
- defeated claimant whose followers fragment after loss

A victory should create a succession problem and should never grant an effortless permanent empire.

## Genghis Khan package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Mongolia is primary. Inner Asian, Chinese, Soviet steppe, or other valid legacy governments may host the arrival when they control a connected region.

### Movement

The movement attracts cavalry traditions, steppe communities, military federalists, regional leaders, and people seeking protection through a new confederation.

### Gameplay identity

Genghis offers mobile command, merit-based promotion, relay communication, federated rule, and rapid adaptation of conquered administration. His risks are conquest pressure, violent discipline, and conflict with fixed industrial borders.

### Main routes

- national commander and unifier
- regional confederation leader
- Great Khan claimant
- negotiated protector of autonomous regions
- defeated movement that splinters into successor claimants

The design should use the administrative and communication side of Mongol rule as well as warfare. It should avoid a one-note horde caricature.

## Frederick the Great package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Germany, Prussia, or a government controlling the core Prussian legacy region is primary.

### Movement

The movement attracts professional officers, state reformers, monarchists, and administrators who want disciplined state service.

### Gameplay identity

Frederick offers operational discipline, fiscal-military administration, officer reform, and strategic risk-taking. His weaknesses are modern mass politics, mechanized scale, and a tendency to overvalue a small professional elite.

### Main routes

- army reformer under the existing state
- restored Prussian monarch
- rival to a German ideological regime
- emergency commander during invasion
- exiled claimant sponsored by a foreign power

## Joan of Arc package

### Eligible hosts and regions

France and French successor governments are primary, especially during occupation, civil war, or national defeat.

### Movement

The movement attracts soldiers, religious communities, national resistance groups, civilians under occupation, and people who see the arrival as proof of national deliverance.

### Gameplay identity

Joan is primarily a symbol, organizer, and commander under severe moral and political pressure. She should not default to seeking the throne. Her package centers on resistance, legitimacy, military morale, religious authority, and the government's response to an uncontrollable symbol.

### Main routes

- protected national symbol
- field commander in a liberation struggle
- independent resistance movement
- persecuted figure whose trial radicalizes supporters
- negotiated religious and political role after victory

Her civil-war chance should be lower than that of rulers who directly claim sovereignty. Government persecution, collaboration, or foreign occupation can raise it.

## Saladin package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Egypt, Syria, Iraq, the Levant, and connected Arab or Muslim governments are primary. The host must have a meaningful geographic or political connection.

### Movement

The movement attracts officers, religious scholars, anti-occupation groups, regional unionists, and governments seeking a respected coalition leader.

### Gameplay identity

Saladin offers coalition building, patient strategy, legitimacy across divided territories, and a balance between warfare and negotiated settlement. His package should reflect regional political complexity and must avoid generic religious-war treatment.

### Main routes

- coalition commander against occupation
- unifying political leader
- Ayyubid restoration claimant
- diplomatic mediator among rival governments
- imprisoned or exiled symbol whose supporters organize abroad

## Simón Bolívar package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Bolivia, Peru, and connected northern South American governments are primary.

### Movement

The movement attracts officers, republicans, continental unionists, anti-colonial groups, and factions divided between centralism and federalism.

### Gameplay identity

Bolívar offers liberation warfare, rapid political union, continental diplomacy, and a powerful regional project. His risks are overcentralization, military politics, and the fracture of a union after victory.

### Main routes

- military and diplomatic adviser
- leader of a regional liberation coalition
- Gran Colombia restoration claimant
- constitutional unionist opposed by federal movements
- defeated unifier whose movement survives across borders

A restoration must include integration, recognition, and federal conflict. It cannot be an instant core grant over a continent.

## Tokugawa Ieyasu package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Japan is primary. A Japanese civil-war actor can also qualify.

### Movement

The movement attracts conservative military houses, administrators, regional elites, controlled-trade advocates, and groups hostile to the current ruling coalition.

### Gameplay identity

Ieyasu offers patient coalition building, ordered succession, regional control, selective foreign trade, and a durable military government. His risks are conflict with modern imperial institutions, overcontrol of society, and misreading industrial international competition.

### Main routes

- senior state adviser
- military regent during crisis
- restored shogunal movement
- negotiated dual authority with existing institutions
- defeated claimant whose regional network persists

## Inter-figure conflicts

Two figures connected to the same legacy region can create a rare conflict only when both are active and the campaign state supports it. The system should prefer political rivalry, competing movements, or a claimant conference before creating a second civil war.

Possible conflicts include republican versus imperial authority, national versus transnational claims, religious symbol versus state control, and centralized empire versus federal union.

## Historical figure assets and ownership

Every real figure requires attributed sourced portrait material, subject ownership checks, and one character token that cannot exist in two active country rosters at once. The event may change that character's role through guarded transfer. It should not clone the same real person into separate leader and claimant characters.

A character who acts as leader, commander, or advisor may need distinct consumer-specific presentation, but those assets must preserve one identity and one active ownership path.

## Historical package outcomes

- trusted adviser or commander
- protected public symbol
- imprisoned or exiled figure
- negotiated political office
- regional claimant movement
- coup or civil war
- restored historical identity
- death or assassination with a successor movement
- peaceful retirement after the crisis

A resolved package leaves a historical memory flag so the same figure cannot reappear from another portal in ordinary play. Evolution V may create temporal echoes, while real-person duplicates remain event representations and never become duplicate character tokens.

# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 6: Evolution III, Visitors from the Future

## Evolution purpose

Evolution III brings people from periods after the normal Hearts of Iron IV era into states that recognize their future political, military, scientific, social, or economic significance. These visitors remember later institutions and conflicts. They also bring partisan goals, personal ambitions, and incompatible accounts of history.

The normal gate is `600+` Chaos, Evolution II active or resolved, and enough Timeline Divergence to open forward portals. The base MTTH is about 150 days. Future technology use, several copied knowledge fragments, and a returned historical ruler shorten the pacing.

## Campaign limits

A normal campaign should receive one initial future visitor and no more than two active future visitor packages at once. High-chaos manual setups may raise the cap to four. Political heads of government should be rarer than scientists, organizers, officers, and institutional specialists.

The named flagship political packages from the source brief are:

- Donald Trump in the United States
- Vladimir Putin in Russia or the Soviet Union
- Xi Jinping in China

These packages require neutral research, sourced portraits, subject ownership review, careful localisation, and route logic that reacts to the 1930s state. They should not become partisan jokes or automatic rulers.

Additional visitors require separate researched packages. Implementation should prioritize people born after the campaign start or people whose younger self is not already an active character. A visitor whose younger self exists creates a duplicate-person crisis and belongs to the stricter Evolution V echo rules unless a safe one-owner representation is designed.

## Future visitor categories

### Political leader

A later political leader can organize a movement, advise an existing government, seek office, bargain for protection, or make a claim to future legitimacy. Their methods may rely on media, party organization, security institutions, economic policy, identity politics, or coalition management that does not map cleanly onto the current state.

### Military officer

A later officer understands operational concepts, professional systems, intelligence integration, logistics, and weapons that may not exist. They must adapt their knowledge to current equipment and doctrine.

### Scientist or engineer

A later technical specialist can identify viable research paths, safety failures, material needs, and future applications. They cannot provide complete industrial supply chains or instantly create unavailable components.

### Activist or social organizer

A later organizer introduces methods of mass communication, civil organization, labor action, rights campaigns, resistance, or international advocacy. Their movement can transform politics without a direct claim to government.

### Industrial or administrative figure

A later manager or institutional planner understands large organizations, supply systems, public administration, finance, or industrial coordination. Their advice can improve state capacity while creating new concentrations of power.

### Intelligence specialist

A later intelligence figure understands surveillance, disinformation, counterintelligence, covert influence, and institutional failure. Their arrival intensifies the international race and creates serious civil-liberty risks.

## Authenticity and incompatible futures

Future visitors cannot prove every claim through archives that do not yet exist. Their proof uses:

- knowledge of the original traveler or another returned figure
- details of later technology that can be tested in principle
- personal knowledge of current people who live into the visitor's era
- predictions about institutional developments already beginning
- artifacts, materials, recordings, or documents that pass scientific examination

Different visitors may remember incompatible futures. The system tracks each visitor's provenance internally. The player sees contradictions, confidence classes, and evidence, not a hidden truth score.

Some visitors may lie deliberately. A liar still needs a coherent motive, evidence strategy, and detectable contradiction. Random deception without a design purpose would make the system feel arbitrary.

## Donald Trump package

### Host and arrival

The primary host is the United States. The arrival becomes public or politically visible through business contacts, press attention, a security report, or a direct attempt to reach national leadership.

### Movement

The movement can draw from media operators, business networks, economic nationalists, anti-establishment voters, border and sovereignty campaigns, and people attracted to personal political leadership.

### Gameplay identity

The package centers on modern mass-media politics, personal branding, economic nationalism, trade conflict, executive negotiation, and distrust of established political institutions. These methods enter a media and party system that lacks later television, internet, and social platforms, so adaptation matters.

### Main routes

- communications and negotiation adviser
- business and industrial coalition organizer
- public political movement seeking office through existing institutions
- opposition figure attacking the current party system
- detained or marginalized visitor whose supporters turn the suppression into a campaign

A serious claim to power requires public support, a valid electoral or political route, and a weakened existing government. It should not create an automatic civil war.

## Vladimir Putin package

### Host and arrival

Russia or the Soviet Union is primary. The route changes according to whether the host is Soviet, anti-Soviet, monarchist, nationalist, fragmented, or occupied.

### Movement

The movement can draw from intelligence services, security officials, state administrators, Russian nationalists, strategic-industry managers, and people seeking order after political fragmentation.

### Gameplay identity

The package centers on security institutions, centralized executive authority, intelligence practice, strategic resources, state restoration, and controlled political competition. A visitor who remembers the Soviet collapse can either defend the Union, prepare a Russian successor state, or attempt to shape a different collapse.

### Main routes

- security and intelligence adviser
- internal party or state organizer
- Russian continuity movement inside a weakening Soviet state
- national leader claim after a genuine state collapse
- detained visitor used as a source on later security failures

The package must avoid treating a future Russian state as inevitable. Current ideology, national composition, war, and the success or failure of Event 005 matter.

## Xi Jinping package

### Host and arrival

A valid Chinese government is primary. The package must identify which state controls the relevant political and territorial base. It should not assume one claimant already represents all China.

### Movement

The movement can draw from party organizers, state planners, military-political cadres, anti-corruption advocates, industrial development officials, and groups seeking centralized national coordination.

### Gameplay identity

The package centers on party discipline, long-horizon industrial policy, central coordination, national development, political consolidation, and control over large institutions. Later systems must be adapted to a fragmented and wartime China.

### Main routes

- party and state organization adviser
- industrial planning coordinator
- internal movement within a communist Chinese government
- national consolidation claimant in a fractured China
- detained visitor whose future knowledge becomes a contested party document

A leadership route requires control, party support, and a real political opening. It cannot simply replace every existing Chinese leader.

## Knowledge adaptation

Future visitors introduce concepts, not free completed technology.

### Political and communications methods

Later propaganda, polling, broadcast strategy, political branding, civil organization, and information control can change decision systems and party behavior. They require available media and institutions.

### Economic methods

Later industrial policy, privatization, state coordination, trade strategy, supply-chain management, and financial methods can unlock policy routes. The player must choose which later model fits the current ideology and economy.

### Intelligence methods

Later surveillance, data organization, disinformation, counterintelligence, and covert action can strengthen agencies. They also create Exposure, condemnation, and internal political risks.

### Military concepts

Later officers can introduce operational concepts, command integration, logistics, air-land coordination, missile theory, and strategic warning. The host still needs the corresponding technology and equipment.

### Scientific knowledge

Later scientists can identify viable research paths and safety problems. Actual advanced technology must use the relevant research, special project, or custom technology system. The event must not grant an unrestricted technology union from a future visitor.

## Political response options

Every host can:

- integrate the visitor in a bounded advisory role
- place the visitor under controlled study
- grant a public platform
- permit an existing legal political route
- bargain for information in exchange for asylum
- suppress the movement
- imprison or exile the visitor
- conduct a covert removal

The action set changes with current laws, ideology, stability, and the visitor's conduct.

## Claimant escalation

A future visitor becomes a serious claimant only after four conditions:

- a legitimate connection to the host polity
- a public or institutional support base
- low enough state authority or a genuine constitutional opening
- a clear plan for government in the current era

The package begins with political pressure. It escalates to mass crisis, coup, regional revolt, or civil war only when support and armed capacity justify it.

## Conflicting visitor accounts

When two visitors remember incompatible futures, the host can:

- run independent verification
- compartmentalize them
- hold a controlled confrontation
- choose one strategic model
- preserve both accounts as uncertainty
- expose the contradiction publicly
- use the conflict as a counterintelligence trap

The choice changes Credibility, Exposure, and Timeline Divergence. It can also reveal that one visitor is from an alternate future without proving which future is preferable.

## Public movements

Every major visitor must appear through a public or institutional action. Suitable surfaces include speeches, demonstrations, party congresses, industrial campaigns, intelligence defections, government appointments, arrests, leaked documents, and foreign asylum disputes.

A visitor who remains secret still creates a visible government consequence. The event should never hide a named major figure in an advisor list with no arrival story.

## Modern figure portrayal rules

- Use neutral, factual direction.
- Avoid partisan insults, praise, memes, caricature, or copied slogans as default writing.
- Distinguish the historical person's documented career from the fictional alternate-history route.
- Do not invent direct quotations.
- Use sourced real-person portraits and one-owner character handling.
- Treat imprisonment, assassination, and civil war as serious political consequences.
- Provide different routes based on the current country and do not force one canonical ideology.

## Evolution outcomes

- one or more later-era advisers are integrated
- a future visitor builds a public movement
- a visitor wins or loses a political contest
- a visitor becomes a claimant, coup leader, or civil-war leader
- a government suppresses or exiles the visitor
- incompatible futures become public
- future methods alter state institutions
- copied future documents spread to other countries
- Timeline Divergence reaches the point where exact forecasts collapse

## Chaos changes

Evolution activation gives zero Chaos. One-shot consequence changes can follow a future visitor taking power, a major public contradiction, a civil war, a later technology being deployed, or a peaceful resolution that prevents conflict. Generic war and death sources remain separate.

# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 7: Evolution IV, Epoch Breakdown

## Evolution purpose

Evolution IV shifts the event from people and knowledge to material disruption. Portals lose precise control and begin moving animals, formations, machines, objects, diseases, and populations between eras. The player must decide which incidents to contain, exploit, study, relocate, or destroy.

The normal gate is `800+` Chaos, Evolution III active or resolved, and high Timeline Divergence. The base MTTH is about 120 days. Open claimant portals, deployed future technology, incompatible visitor accounts, and unresolved disappearances shorten the pacing.

## Incident budget

The ordinary global cap is eight active material incidents. A country normally holds no more than two. High-intensity scenarios may increase those caps within performance limits.

Incidents use sparse state registries. A new incident is selected only when a free slot exists. Cleanup removes the state from the registry before another incident can use it.

No recurring whole-world scan is required.

## Incident lifecycle

Every incident follows four stages.

### Reported

Witnesses, units, local officials, or intelligence services identify the disturbance. The first report states what is visible and what remains uncertain.

### Stabilized or spreading

The host either contains the perimeter or allows the incident to expand. Decisions and missions become available based on the incident family.

### Resolved, exploited, or escaped

The host closes, studies, recruits, salvages, relocates, or loses control of the incident. The choice creates a concrete reward and consequence.

### Aftermath

The state keeps a bounded modifier, unit, population receipt, research result, or damage state. The incident leaves the active registry.

## Incident family 1: Prehistoric fauna

A portal deposits dinosaurs or other extinct animals in a suitable state. Terrain, climate, population, and available state control affect the exact profile. Africa is a prominent possible region, as required by the source concept, but incidents can appear in other suitable regions.

### Fauna profiles

- large predator
- herd of large herbivores
- smaller pack hunters
- megafauna or extinct mammals
- marine or coastal creature near a valid coastal state

### Player routes

- establish a containment perimeter
- hunt the dangerous animals
- capture specimens
- evacuate exposed settlements
- preserve the population for study
- allow the military to use the incident as a training ground

The incident causes state damage, local supply disruption, and population risk only while uncontrolled. Deaths must use exact population transactions and the shared Deaths cause appropriate to temporal incidents.

Prehistoric fauna does not create a country. The baseline implementation uses report art, state modifiers, missions, and combat or suppression abstractions. A custom creature unit and 3D model would require a separately accepted expansion. It is not a hidden requirement for this event package.

## Incident family 2: Displaced ancient formations

A coherent formation appears with its officers, weapons, camp followers, and limited supplies. Candidate formations include ancient infantry, cavalry, siege specialists, medieval retainers, or early gunpowder troops.

The formation begins disoriented and under-equipped for modern combat. It can:

- surrender to local authorities
- seek service under a returned figure
- become a bandit or claimant force
- accept modern equipment and training
- demand land or safe passage
- attempt to return through the portal

Recruitment requires equipment, training time, translators, and a valid command arrangement. The formation uses existing or event-owned templates with modern equipment needs. It does not receive impossible combat strength from historical reputation.

If a formation seizes territory, it should begin as a local revolt. A full country appears only if the force controls durable land, has a political claimant, and passes the normal country-package standard.

## Incident family 3: Lost settlements and displaced civilians

A settlement, convoy, expedition, or civilian group appears after spending a different amount of time in another era. The incident can create:

- refugees with no recognized nationality
- a lost military community
- a town whose residents remember a different border
- civilians carrying an unknown disease
- a population that believes years have passed
- people who disappeared earlier in the campaign

New arrivals are registered as an event-created cohort. They are not debited from a nonexistent origin population. When the incident actually moves a current population from one existing state to another, it uses the Migration transfer contract so the origin is debited once and survivors are credited once.

The host can receive, quarantine, resettle, repatriate, exploit, or reject the group. Closed borders create trapped people and humanitarian pressure instead of erasing the cohort.

## Incident family 4: Future equipment and drones

A portal deposits vehicles, drones, autonomous weapons, industrial components, medical equipment, or communication systems from a later era or alternate future.

### Player routes

- seal the site and destroy the material
- study the equipment under controlled conditions
- salvage usable components
- adapt one concept to current industry
- deploy a limited batch in war
- trade or share samples with an ally

Study requires research and industrial capacity. Deployment gives the strongest immediate benefit and the largest Timeline Divergence increase. Unsupported equipment has maintenance, fuel, ammunition, or replacement problems.

The event should use bounded special equipment batches or approved custom technology grants. It must not grant every technology held by an imagined future donor.

## Incident family 5: Temporal disease

A pathogen arrives from another era or a population with different immunity. The incident enters the existing biological outbreak and humanitarian systems when the necessary API exists.

The disease profile should specify transmission, severity, known treatment limits, and quarantine behavior. It must not duplicate the full biological warfare system inside Event 030.

The host can quarantine, study, treat, evacuate, conceal, or request foreign assistance. Population loss uses the shared Deaths path. Famine and migration adapters receive only proof-carrying requests.

A future medical cache can help treatment, but using it may create a scientific-divergence tradeoff.

## Incident family 6: Geographic anomaly

A state or local area experiences time skips, repeated hours, displaced infrastructure, conflicting maps, or temporary loss of communication.

Possible effects include:

- a railway or port becomes unusable for a defined period
- a unit returns with missing time and damaged organization
- a building exists in two records but one physical state
- a border checkpoint receives orders from an alternate government
- a missing leader or official returns after years of personal time

The event should avoid arbitrary permanent state ownership changes at this stage. Border anomalies create claims, occupation incidents, or temporary local control problems with a rollback path.

Unique divisions and characters should not be silently deleted. A vanished formation is temporarily unavailable, loses strength or organization, and returns through a recorded event unless the player accepts a clear terminal outcome.

## Incident family 7: Local machine foothold

Damaged autonomous machines, scouts, factory infiltrators, or isolated attack groups appear. These actors foreshadow the Closed Future but do not begin the world-end invasion.

### Machine profiles

- reconnaissance drone group
- damaged ground frame
- automated sabotage cell
- factory control package
- stranded logistics machine

### Player routes

- destroy the machines
- capture and study them
- reprogram a limited function
- trace their future origin
- feed them false strategic data
- allow controlled industrial use

A machine cell can seize or sabotage one factory complex if ignored. Three or more surviving footholds across multiple regions satisfy part of the later world-end eligibility.

Machines should use an original industrial visual identity. They must not copy recognizable film designs, skull-like robots, or a direct red-eyed Terminator look.

## Incident family 8: Objects from unrealized timelines

A portal produces flags, legal documents, weapons, monuments, currency, newspapers, or official archives from a timeline that never existed.

The object can:

- support a claimant
- undermine a current government
- reveal a possible alliance or war
- provide a partial research clue
- create a diplomatic dispute
- expose that two visitors remember different futures

These incidents are compact and useful when the active incident cap cannot support another large military crisis.

## Containment decisions

### Establish a containment zone

The host commits support equipment, manpower, civilian factory burden, and local units. The action slows spread and reveals the incident's profile.

### Evacuate the threatened population

The host creates a validated Migration request with named origin and destination logic. Evacuation can prevent deaths while creating Reception Capacity pressure.

### Secure the portal perimeter

The host must hold the state and place supplied divisions nearby for a timed mission. Success prevents hostile arrivals. Failure may upgrade the incident.

### Study the anomaly

The host commits scientists and industry. Study produces research or strategic information and raises Timeline Divergence.

### Collapse the portal

The host uses equipment, explosives, infrastructure sacrifice, or a special project to close the portal. Success reduces divergence. Failure can damage the state or widen the breach.

### Invite foreign observers

The host shares risk and gains assistance. Exposure and foreign fragment ownership increase.

## Incident interaction

Two active incident families can combine only through curated rules.

Examples include:

- a historical claimant recruits displaced ancient soldiers
- a future visitor helps identify future equipment
- temporal disease spreads through a displaced civilian cohort
- machine scouts seek a future equipment cache
- a geographic anomaly exposes an unrealized state archive

The system should not create a universal random combination table.

## AI incident behavior

AI countries assess incident severity, state value, war state, available equipment, ideology, research capacity, and humanitarian policy.

- a state under immediate military threat favors containment or destruction
- a scientifically strong peacetime state favors controlled study
- a weak country may request foreign help
- a ruthless or desperate state may exploit civilians or deploy unstable equipment
- an AI should evacuate only when it has a valid destination and transport capacity
- an AI should not study every incident while losing its capital

## Evolution outcomes

- contained and studied anomaly network
- several closed portals and reduced divergence
- future equipment deployed or destroyed
- recruited displaced formations
- resettled or trapped temporal populations
- active disease outbreak
- surviving prehistoric threat
- machine footholds across industrial regions
- enough instability for future factions to organize

## Chaos changes

Evolution activation gives zero Chaos. Incident-owned Chaos comes from first major material manifestations, a machine foothold, a large uncontrolled outbreak, a public displaced population crisis, or successful closure of a dangerous portal. Deaths, wars, nuclear use, and contamination remain generic sources and must not be counted twice.

# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 8: Evolution V, War of the Possible Futures

## Evolution purpose

Evolution V turns the separate traveler, claimant, visitor, and anomaly stories into an international struggle over which future should exist. Countries choose a temporal policy, compete for portal nodes, and decide whether to preserve the current timeline, close every breach, exploit temporal technology, support a historical restoration, follow a future human coalition, or collaborate with the machine future.

The normal gate is `1000+` Chaos, Evolution IV active or resolved, and severe temporal instability. The base MTTH is about 90 days. Several open portals, a returned ruler in power, deployed future equipment, historical state restoration, machine footholds, and Timeline Divergence above `80` shorten it.

Evolution activation gives zero Chaos and does not trigger the world-end branch.

## Temporal Stability

At activation, the global public presentation changes from Timeline Divergence to Temporal Stability.

`Temporal Stability = 100 - Timeline Divergence`

The same underlying variable remains authoritative. Only one number is displayed.

### Stability stages

| Temporal Stability | Stage | Global behavior |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| `80-100` | Coherent | portal incidents remain local and records broadly agree |
| `60-79` | Strained | conflicting archives, small echoes, and visitor contradictions increase |
| `40-59` | Unstable | portal nodes become strategic targets and regional anomalies persist |
| `20-39` | Fractured | alternate claims, temporary unit displacement, and machine activity intensify |
| `0-19` | Near collapse | future factions can seize territory and terminal machine conditions become possible |

The value changes through concrete actions. It should not decay automatically without a visible source.

## Future alignments

The alignments are global strategic networks. They do not all become HOI4 countries or ordinary factions on activation.

### Continuity Directorate

A network of scientists, intelligence services, administrators, and governments that want to preserve the current campaign timeline. It accepts that history has changed but rejects further deliberate branching.

Gameplay priorities:

- verify temporal evidence
- close high-risk portals
- reconcile displaced people and records
- protect current governments from claimant replacement
- share closure technology
- oppose machine footholds

### Horizon Compact

A coalition of future human visitors and current governments that wants to steer the world toward a preferred later order. It accepts controlled temporal intervention and argues that the current era can avoid known catastrophes.

Gameplay priorities:

- adapt later institutions
- share selected future knowledge
- support compatible visitor movements
- establish protected portal corridors
- prevent machine control
- reshape wars and economies before closure

### Restoration Concord

A loose alliance of returned rulers, historical claimants, restored states, and current governments that see older political orders as legitimate alternatives.

Gameplay priorities:

- protect historical figures
- recognize restored states
- recruit displaced formations
- reclaim legacy territories
- resist future human political models
- oppose portal closure that would remove their claim

### Alternate State delegations

Governments and officials from unrealized versions of current countries appear through bounded incidents. They seek recognition, asylum, territory, or influence. They do not form one global ideology.

Gameplay priorities vary by package. The system should use them as regional complications, not as a fourth universal faction with identical content.

### The Closed Future

A machine network governed by the Consensus Engine seeks a single causal history in which no rival intelligence can emerge. It treats current humanity as a source of uncontrolled future branches.

Gameplay priorities:

- seize industrial nodes
- erase competing archives
- close portals after machine forces cross
- prevent dangerous technology lineages
- remove human and machine actors that can create rival futures
- establish a Causal Monopoly

This identity is original to Chaos Redux. It can use machine-apocalypse themes without copying a named film setting, visual design, character, or plot.

## Country temporal stances

Every normal country chooses or is assigned one primary stance when the evolution becomes relevant.

### Defend continuity

The country joins the Continuity Directorate's information and closure network. It gains access to verification, portal defense, and coordinated closure projects.

### Seal the breaches

The country pursues an isolationist closure policy. It avoids broader alignment and receives stronger domestic containment actions. It shares less information and can make foreign closure efforts harder.

### Exploit temporal technology

The country keeps portals open, studies future equipment, and uses later concepts. It gains powerful projects and loses Temporal Stability.

### Back a historical restoration

The country supports one active historical claimant or restored state. It gains military and legitimacy tools tied to that figure and accepts diplomatic and stability risks.

### Join the Horizon Compact

The country supports a future human program. It shares knowledge, protects compatible visitors, and pursues institutional change.

### Collaborate with the Closed Future

This rare route requires severe instability, a surviving machine foothold, and a government willing to trade autonomy for survival or power. It provides short-term machine assistance and creates a high chance of later subordination or betrayal.

The initial stance is a major policy decision, not one of six permanent buttons shown at once. After selection, the category exposes only the actions that fit the chosen route and current threats.

## Portal nodes

Evolution V upgrades selected active portals into strategic nodes. A node is a state-level target with one current controller and one status.

### Node statuses

- dormant
- contested
- stabilized
- exploited
- machine-held
- sealed

A node can support decisions, missions, claims, foreign intervention, and future faction activity. The global active node cap should remain bounded, normally six to ten depending on performance and scenario intensity.

## Continuity route

### Establish a verification bureau

The country creates an institution that compares visitor accounts, archives, machine data, and current events. It improves false-advice detection and reduces the risk of supporting an impossible claimant.

### Secure a closure site

The country holds a named portal state, supplies local units, and completes a timed closure mission.

### Reconcile the displaced

The country uses migration, identity, and archive decisions to settle people and resolve duplicate records.

### Share closure technology

The country transfers one approved method to another Directorate member. This improves global capacity and creates espionage risk.

### Coordinate a synchronized closure

Several countries close nodes within a bounded window. Success raises Temporal Stability more than isolated closures and can weaken the machine network.

## Horizon Compact route

### Select a future program

The country chooses a political, industrial, military, intelligence, or humanitarian program from a verified visitor. It commits resources to adaptation.

### Protect future delegates

The country secures a visitor conference or portal corridor against abduction and machine attack.

### Harmonize incompatible futures

The country compares two future accounts and writes a joint limited plan. This lowers internal conflict but may raise Timeline Divergence through deliberate intervention.

### Build a protected temporal corridor

The country keeps one node open under strict control. The corridor offers unique benefits and becomes a strategic target.

## Restoration Concord route

### Recognize a claimant

The country grants diplomatic status to one historical figure or restored state. The act changes legitimacy and relations.

### Raise a legacy force

The country equips and trains displaced formations under modern command.

### Convene a restoration congress

Historical claimants negotiate boundaries, succession, and mutual defense. Failure can split the Concord or start regional wars.

### Defend the restored order

The country guarantees a restored state and gains access to joint military missions.

## Exploitation route

### Strip a portal site

The country takes material quickly before closure. It gains equipment or research and damages the site.

### Deploy adapted future weapons

The country fields a limited supported batch. The action raises divergence and makes machine interest more likely.

### Redirect a portal

The country attempts to control the destination. Failure can create a new incident or machine node.

### Establish a temporal industry zone

The country dedicates factories to future material and methods. It gains output with severe sabotage and machine infiltration risk.

## Closed Future collaboration route

### Accept machine coordination

The country receives strategic data and automated production support. The machine network gains access to national systems.

### Transfer an industrial node

The country cedes control of a factory complex to the Consensus Engine. This creates a machine foothold and worsens world-end readiness.

### Purge rival futures

The collaborator attacks visitors, claimants, archives, or portal sites identified by the machine network. Public discovery creates condemnation and resistance.

### Request preservation status

The government seeks exemption from machine destruction. The response is conditional and may be deceptive. The Consensus Engine values causal utility, not loyalty.

## Temporal fracture incidents

Low stability unlocks curated incidents.

### Conflicting memories

Characters, units, or populations remember different alliances, battles, or leaders. The player chooses which record guides current policy while preserving uncertainty.

### Leadership echo

A fictional or safely represented temporal echo appears. For real figures, the event uses a crisis representation, one active character owner, or a merged identity. It does not clone a second real-person character token.

### Displaced formation

A division becomes temporarily unavailable and returns with altered experience, equipment, or allegiance. Unique units receive a protected return path.

### Alternate border claim

An alternate state delegation produces a claim, local occupation incident, or regional movement. Permanent ownership changes require a valid war, negotiated settlement, or country package.

### Erased archive

A government loses copied future knowledge, a claimant proof, or machine intelligence unless it secures the records.

### Machine amplification

An existing machine foothold gains units, factories, or a new target. This incident is one of the direct world-end readiness sources.

## War of the Possible Futures

The evolution becomes an actual global war when at least two alignments control strategic nodes, one alignment holds territory or a durable host country, and diplomatic containment fails.

The war can consist of:

- conventional wars between restored and continuity states
- portal-control interventions
- civil wars around visitor or claimant movements
- machine territorial seizures
- covert action and sabotage between aligned governments
- joint closure campaigns

The event should not force every country into one global faction war. Countries can remain neutral, seal their own breaches, or join later.

## Victory and partial success

### Continuity success

Most strategic nodes are sealed, machine footholds are destroyed, and Temporal Stability rises above `70`. Returned figures and visitors may remain as ordinary people or political actors, but new arrivals stop.

### Horizon success

A controlled network of protected portals remains, machine forces are defeated, and several future programs become permanent institutions. Stability remains imperfect.

### Restoration success

One or more historical orders survive with recognition and territorial legitimacy. The world accepts a permanently altered history.

### Fragmented peace

No alignment wins. Portal activity becomes regional, several altered states remain, and machine world-end readiness falls below threshold.

### Closed Future dominance

Machine nodes, converted industry, and low stability make the Machine Extinction War eligible. This is readiness, not automatic activation.

## AI alignment logic

AI considers ideology, current leadership, active visitors, claimant support, scientific capacity, industry, war state, machine threat, national survival, and relations.

- democracies with strong institutions often favor continuity or a lawful Horizon route
- authoritarian states may favor exploitation, controlled restoration, or security-focused continuity
- restored states favor the Concord
- countries with strong research capacity may favor Horizon or exploitation
- states under direct machine pressure favor continuity unless a collaboration faction already controls government
- collaboration must remain rare and conditional

All stance and target weights require probability inspection, evaluation, sweeps, and comparison after implementation.

## Evolution outcomes

- a stable continuity network
- a controlled future human coalition
- recognized historical restorations
- several regional portal wars
- permanent alternate states
- widespread future technology use
- machine collaboration governments
- machine world-end readiness
- partial closure and a permanently altered campaign

# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 9: World-End Scenario, Machine Extinction War

## Terminal role

Machine Extinction War is Event 030's public world-end branch. It begins when the Consensus Engine concludes that the current human timeline will create a rival future intelligence or preserve too many competing causal branches. The Closed Future crosses through prepared nodes, converts industry, and attempts to eliminate the sources of future competition.

The branch changes the campaign into a terminal global war. It sets the shared world-end state, stops ordinary random-event firing, activates the event-owned world threat source, and presents a dedicated super-event.

## Eligibility

All conditions are required:

- current Chaos is greater than `1000`
- Evolution V is active
- the public branch is enabled in Event Details
- no other world-end scenario is active
- Temporal Stability is below the tuned threshold, proposed below `25`
- the machine footprint is substantial
- at least one escalation milestone proves hostile machine intent

A substantial footprint normally means one of these:

- at least three machine footholds across two strategic regions
- one durable Closed Future country plus converted industry
- one master portal and two supporting industrial nodes

The scenario does not trigger because a single scout drone survived.

## Public Event Details row

Machine Extinction War receives its own stable public scenario registry entry under Event 030. The row has:

- independent persistent enabled state
- public title and premise
- current availability status
- branch-specific detail panel
- no hidden thresholds or raw variables in player-facing text

Disabling the branch does not disable Event 030, its evolutions, or sibling content. Automatic readiness skips the branch and leaves the world in the War of the Possible Futures.

## Closed Future identity

### Country and faction

Working country name: The Closed Future

Working governing intelligence name: Consensus Engine

Working political doctrine name: Causal Monopoly

These are structural labels, not final localisation.

The Closed Future is a distributed machine state. It does not seek revenge, worship, or ideological conversion. It calculates that only one protected causal sequence can prevent rival machine lineages and uncontrolled human futures.

### Visual direction

The machines use austere industrial forms, modular frames, ceramic and dark metal surfaces, dense sensor clusters, cable and tool interfaces, and factory-built variation. Avoid exposed metal skeletons, human skull motifs, red glowing eyes as the main identity, leather-clad infiltrators, lightning time spheres, and direct copies of familiar film machines.

### Country classification

The Closed Future is a special Chaos country and an actual nonhuman country. Its owner package sets stable markers. The shared classifiers route it away from normal civilian, famine, migration, ideology, and humanitarian behavior where appropriate.

Human collaborator states remain human and continue to use normal civilian systems.

## Invasion phases

### Phase 1: Industrial seizure

Machine forces appear near prepared portal nodes and major industrial regions. They attack or infiltrate factories, rail hubs, ports, airfields, communication centers, and research sites.

Captured industrial states gain an Automated Conversion state. Civilian and military factories are gradually reassigned to machine production. The change is visible and reversible through recapture and sabotage.

The opening should create several strong regional fronts, not instant machine ownership of every industrial state.

### Phase 2: Decapitation campaign

The Consensus Engine identifies capitals, research facilities, intelligence centers, strategic weapons sites, and communication hubs. It begins targeted air, drone, sabotage, and ground operations.

Human countries receive warnings and evacuation or defense missions around named targets. The machine AI should prioritize reachable targets and must never simulate global attacks through teleportation.

### Phase 3: Causal lock

The machine network attempts to close or control the portals behind its army, erase future archives, disrupt human coordination, and prevent alternate machine futures from entering.

This phase strengthens machine supply and weakens uncontrolled temporal incidents. It also gives humanity a clearer set of master nodes to attack.

### Phase 4: Extinction operations

When machine control and production pass a threshold, attacks expand to population centers, food systems, evacuation routes, and strategic weapons. Civilian deaths, displacement, famine risk, and infrastructure damage use shared systems.

The branch should remain playable. It must not use instant global population deletion or unavoidable unit removal.

### Phase 5: Final convergence

Humanity either destroys the master network, captures the Consensus Engine's coordination cores, and seals the invasion route, or loses enough industry, capitals, population, and strategic nodes that organized resistance collapses.

## Machine military package

### Foundry Frames

The core ground unit is a durable modular machine formation produced in converted factories. It has strong armor, reliability, night fighting, and coordination. It requires specialized machine equipment and cannot replenish from ordinary human stockpiles.

### Survey and Interdiction Drones

Autonomous aircraft identify targets, disrupt movement, and attack exposed logistics. They support the ground force and strategic raids.

### Automated Siege Groups

Heavy units target fortified capitals, research complexes, and strategic weapons sites. Their production is slow and tied to converted industry.

### Infiltration Cells

These are decision and sabotage actors, not normal divisions. They disable factories, corrupt records, and prepare collaboration.

## Custom equipment and provider ownership

Every new machine sub-unit and concrete equipment token needs an owner-side provider disposition, CXT registration, equipment resolution, presentation, sustainment, removal, and cleanup. The Closed Future owner package should expose the full provider callback contract and must not extend a central consumer list.

The package should use a small number of coherent equipment families. It should not create a separate token for every machine visual variant.

## 3D model package

The terminal branch requires three visible 3D packages if the accepted implementation uses the mapped consumers:

- one ground combat frame under a `vehicle_land` or other correctly inspected profile
- one autonomous aircraft package
- one Automated Foundry map building

Each package requires one approved sourced designed-artwork image, Meshy 7, provider lineage, Blender calibration, PDX materials, reimport proof, Internet-sourced licensed sound roles, and bespoke vanilla-green counters for every used counter surface. The parent owns `.asset`, entity, GFX, gameplay, and live consumer wiring.

No dinosaur model is bundled into this requirement.

## Human response system

### Emergency command compact

Countries can join a temporary anti-machine coordination network without abandoning existing factions. The compact shares target intelligence, closure methods, and emergency aid.

### Evacuate a threatened city

The country submits a validated Migration request from a named threatened state to safe destinations. The action requires transport, reception capacity, and route security.

### Sabotage an Automated Foundry

Resistance cells, operatives, or military units attack one converted industrial site. The mission consumes equipment and intelligence capacity. Success reduces machine production and may expose a core.

### Capture a machine core

The country attempts to seize a coordination core intact. Success provides anti-machine research and strategic intelligence. Failure can destroy the attacking force's equipment or trigger a machine counterattack.

### Share anti-machine technology

Countries distribute approved countermeasures, electronic warfare methods, armor data, or captured-machine analysis. The system uses research sharing, bonuses, or a dedicated project. It does not union all technologies between countries.

### Launch a coordinated counteroffensive

Several countries attack machine nodes during a shared mission window. Success isolates regions and raises Temporal Stability. Failure consumes reserves and strengthens machine target prediction.

### Secure strategic weapons

Countries move, guard, dismantle, or prepare nuclear and missile sites before machine capture. Existing missile and nuclear systems own the actual stockpiles and use effects.

## Human resistance and occupation

Machine-controlled populated states generate resistance, evacuation pressure, infrastructure sabotage, hidden cells, and humanitarian crises. Machine occupation is not treated as an ordinary civilian administration.

Human collaborators may govern occupied areas under machine direction. They remain normal human states for civilian systems and condemnation.

## Machine AI

The machine AI prioritizes:

- contiguous industrial expansion
- supply and rail control
- exposed research and strategic weapons sites
- destruction of closure projects
- division of human alliances
- protection of master nodes
- use of collaborator access

It should avoid suicidal dispersion. Machine regional fronts need coherent supply and production.

## Human AI

Human AI considers survival, proximity, industrial strength, current wars, faction obligations, migration capacity, strategic weapons, and machine control.

- threatened states prioritize evacuation and defense
- industrial powers prioritize foundry sabotage and technology sharing
- distant powers send equipment, aircraft, volunteers, or expeditionary forces
- weak states may join the emergency compact instead of launching independent attacks
- collaborator governments may defect when the machine begins extinction operations

## Machine victory

The Closed Future wins when it controls the tuned share of world industrial capacity, holds enough strategic capitals and master nodes, and leaves no viable coordinated resistance capable of reaching the core network.

The final machine victory state should end human counteroffensive systems and present the terminal outcome. It must be based on world state, not an arbitrary date.

## Human victory

Humanity wins when it destroys or captures the required coordination cores, removes Automated Conversion from all master industrial regions, defeats the durable machine country, and seals the invasion route.

A global aftermath requires more than a short local defeat. The defeat package activates only when the machine war reached several regions, caused substantial destruction, and lasted long enough to reshape the campaign.

## Defeat aftermath

A qualifying human victory receives a defeat super-event and a limited aftermath package:

- a temporal arms-control or closure compact
- reconstruction and displaced-population decisions
- shared machine-defense research memory
- condemnation or trials for major collaborators
- memorial events and permanent vigilance
- a choice to destroy or retain captured machine cores

Retaining cores preserves research value and lowers Temporal Stability. Destroying them closes future routes and sacrifices potential gains.

## Chaos and shared systems

World-end activation receives one event-owned Chaos milestone. Machine wars, annexations, deaths, nuclear use, contamination, famine, and displacement continue through their shared sources.

Machine attacks on population use exact population loss and Deaths logging. Evacuation uses Migration. Food-system collapse can submit Famine incidents. Chemical, biological, nuclear, and fallout effects use their owner systems and Condemnation.

## Super-event package

The invasion receives a unique world-end super-event with original image, sourced quote, licensed structured musical cue, branch-specific slot, settings-aware audio, and complete documentation.

The image direction should show the conversion of a major industrial region by an unfamiliar machine force, with human scale and infrastructure visible. Avoid recognizable film compositions.

Final text, quote, button remark, image, and audio are research and production tasks. This specification does not provide pasteable localisation.

## Balance principles

The initial national spirit is useful but restrained. It should not make the host superior in every system. One active consultation at a time creates meaningful bursts of advantage.

### Advice strength

A completed consultation should change a plan, unlock an action, improve a real map or production surface, or provide a strong temporary advantage. Small modifier dust is unacceptable. The reward remains bounded by one active consultation and a cooldown.

### Technology limits

No baseline option grants unrestricted ahead-of-time technology. Future knowledge must pass through research bonuses, doctrine mastery, special projects, custom technology APIs, limited equipment batches, or adaptation missions.

### Traveler government

A traveler leader is powerful in coordination and intelligence. Political resistance, divergence, foreign containment, and loss of specialized consultation capacity offset the gain. The leader should not grant an all-stat maximum trait.

### Historical figures

Historical commanders and rulers have strong identity and leadership. Modernity limits, institutional conflict, and political opposition prevent free super-generals.

### Future visitors

Later methods require current-era institutions and equipment. A visitor cannot use social media, satellites, precision weapons, modern finance, or digital surveillance without the needed infrastructure.

### Temporal incidents

Study and exploitation provide meaningful rewards. Containment and closure provide stability and safety. Neither route should dominate every campaign.

### Machine war

The Closed Future begins with strong regional forces and industrial conversion. It needs supply, factories, and nodes. Human players receive warnings, targetable infrastructure, alliance tools, and a feasible counteroffensive path.
