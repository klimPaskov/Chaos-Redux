# Historical and Contemporary Research Notes

Retrieved for planning on 2026-08-30. These notes provide design anchors. They do not replace full figure research, portrait sourcing, ownership checks, or final localisation review during implementation.

## Napoleon Bonaparte

The Fondation Napoléon discussion of Napoleonic state power emphasizes legal order, executive concentration, and the institutional inheritance of the Revolution. That supports a package built around administrative reform, law, centralized execution, and the danger of personal rule. It does not support reducing the figure to a battlefield modifier.

Source: [Was Napoleonic France a state based on law](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/napoleonic-france-state-based-law/)

Design use:

- legal and administrative institution route
- executive concentration as a tradeoff
- military reputation paired with a modern industrial-war limit
- imperial restoration as one route among several

## Julius Caesar

The Getty program description identifies Caesar as lawyer, politician, general, author, and dictator, and connects his rise through intrigue, violence, and civil war to the destruction of the Roman Republic. That supports a political package centered on personal authority, emergency office, republican breakdown, and assassination risk.

Source: [From the Consulship to the Ides](https://www.getty.edu/visit/cal/events/ev_3079.html)

Design use:

- emergency dictatorship route
- coalition and personal loyalty mechanics
- republican institutional crisis
- assassination and succession consequences

## Alexander the Great

The British Museum material notes that Alexander's conquests transformed the political world and that his empire broke into separate kingdoms ruled by generals and successors after his death. This supports an ambitious conquest route with an explicit succession and fragmentation problem.

Sources:

- [Luxury and power Persia to Greece resources](https://www.britishmuseum.org/exhibitions/luxury-and-power-persia-greece/lp-resources)
- [Exhibition main texts PDF](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2023-05/Luxury_and_power_Persia_to_Greece_Exhibition_map_and_main_texts.pdf)

Design use:

- battlefield charisma and transregional ambition
- cultural and imperial synthesis as a possible policy
- weak durable succession
- fragmentation after victory

## Genghis Khan

The Metropolitan Museum describes the Mongol transformation from nomadic conquerors into rulers of cities and states, the use of existing local administration, continent-wide communications, and the Pax Mongolica. This supports a package that includes administration, relay networks, trade, and federated rule alongside warfare.

Source: [The Legacy of Genghis Khan](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-legacy-of-genghis-khan)

Design use:

- mobile command and merit-based military organization
- relay communications and logistics
- adaptive use of local administration
- confederation and successor-fragment risk

## Frederick the Great

Oxford's Frederick the Great project records the military, political, philosophical, and cultural scope of Frederick's rule. The project is suitable as an entry point for deeper implementation research. The planning package uses Frederick as a professional-army and fiscal-military state figure with clear limits in mass politics and mechanized war.

Sources:

- [Frederick the Great project history](https://frederick.mml.ox.ac.uk/history)
- [Symposium abstracts](https://frederick.mml.ox.ac.uk/symposium-abstracts)

Design use:

- officer corps reform
- disciplined state administration
- strategic risk and military professionalism
- elite-state legitimacy conflict

## Joan of Arc

The Fordham Medieval Sourcebook preserves a translation of the trial record and explains its documentary transmission. The record shows how custody, jurisdiction, political pressure, religious authority, and public reputation shaped the case. This supports a package centered on national resistance, religious and political legitimacy, persecution, trial, and martyrdom risk.

Source: [The Trial of Joan of Arc](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/basis/joanofarc-trial.asp)

Design use:

- liberation and resistance command
- symbolic authority stronger than a direct throne claim
- government custody and trial choices
- persecution creating a larger movement

## Saladin

The British Academy discussion by Carole Hillenbrand emphasizes viewing the Crusades through Muslim perspectives and the regional experience of invasion. The source supports a package grounded in coalition, regional legitimacy, diplomacy, anti-occupation politics, and careful treatment of religious context.

Source: [Saladin and the Crusades, medieval and modern perspectives](https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/podcasts/10-minute-talks-saladin-crusades-medieval-modern-perspectives/)

Design use:

- coalition building across divided territories
- anti-occupation legitimacy
- strategic patience and diplomacy
- avoidance of generic religious-war treatment

## Simón Bolívar

The Library of Congress holds the Colombian constitutional material and Bolívar proclamations that can support deeper research into liberation, union, constitutional design, centralism, and the instability of Gran Colombia.

Sources:

- [Constitución de la República de Colombia](https://www.loc.gov/item/78341644)
- [Proclamas de Simón Bolívar](https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdcmassbookdig.proclamasdesimon00bol/)

Design use:

- liberation war and regional union
- constitutional settlement
- centralist and federal conflict
- post-victory fragmentation risk

## Tokugawa Ieyasu

The National Diet Library records Ieyasu's use of red-seal trade passes and the opening of Dutch trade at Hirado. This supports a package that combines controlled foreign exchange, regional authority, patient coalition building, and state consolidation.

Source: [Beginning of Exchange between Japan and the Netherlands](https://www.ndl.go.jp/nichiran/e/s1/s1_1.html)

Design use:

- controlled trade and diplomatic selection
- ordered regional governance
- long-term succession and stability
- conflict with modern imperial and industrial institutions
