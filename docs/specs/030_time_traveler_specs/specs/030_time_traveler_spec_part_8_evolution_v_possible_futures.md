# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 8: Evolution V, War of the Possible Futures

## Evolution purpose

Evolution V turns the separate traveler, claimant, visitor, and anomaly stories into an international struggle over which future should exist. Countries choose a temporal policy, compete for portal nodes, and decide whether to preserve the current timeline, close every breach, exploit temporal technology, support a historical restoration, follow a future human coalition, or collaborate with the machine future.

The normal gate is `1000+` Chaos, Evolution IV active or resolved, and severe temporal instability. The base MTTH is about 90 days. Several open portals, a returned ruler in power, deployed future equipment, historical state restoration, machine footholds, and Timeline Divergence above `80` shorten it.

Evolution activation gives zero Chaos and does not trigger the world-end branch.

## Temporal Stability

At activation, the global public presentation changes from Timeline Divergence to Temporal Stability.

`Temporal Stability = 100 - Timeline Divergence`

The same underlying variable remains authoritative. Only one number is displayed.

### Stability stages

| Temporal Stability | Stage | Global behavior |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| `80-100` | Coherent | portal incidents remain local and records broadly agree |
| `60-79` | Strained | conflicting archives, small echoes, and visitor contradictions increase |
| `40-59` | Unstable | portal nodes become strategic targets and regional anomalies persist |
| `20-39` | Fractured | alternate claims, temporary unit displacement, and machine activity intensify |
| `0-19` | Near collapse | future factions can seize territory and terminal machine conditions become possible |

The value changes through concrete actions. It should not decay automatically without a visible source.

## Future alignments

The alignments are global strategic networks. They do not all become HOI4 countries or ordinary factions on activation.

### Continuity Directorate

A network of scientists, intelligence services, administrators, and governments that want to preserve the current campaign timeline. It accepts that history has changed but rejects further deliberate branching.

Gameplay priorities:

- verify temporal evidence
- close high-risk portals
- reconcile displaced people and records
- protect current governments from claimant replacement
- share closure technology
- oppose machine footholds

### Horizon Compact

A coalition of future human visitors and current governments that wants to steer the world toward a preferred later order. It accepts controlled temporal intervention and argues that the current era can avoid known catastrophes.

Gameplay priorities:

- adapt later institutions
- share selected future knowledge
- support compatible visitor movements
- establish protected portal corridors
- prevent machine control
- reshape wars and economies before closure

### Restoration Concord

A loose alliance of returned rulers, historical claimants, restored states, and current governments that see older political orders as legitimate alternatives.

Gameplay priorities:

- protect historical figures
- recognize restored states
- recruit displaced formations
- reclaim legacy territories
- resist future human political models
- oppose portal closure that would remove their claim

### Alternate State delegations

Governments and officials from unrealized versions of current countries appear through bounded incidents. They seek recognition, asylum, territory, or influence. They do not form one global ideology.

Gameplay priorities vary by package. The system should use them as regional complications, not as a fourth universal faction with identical content.

### The Closed Future

A machine network governed by the Consensus Engine seeks a single causal history in which no rival intelligence can emerge. It treats current humanity as a source of uncontrolled future branches.

Gameplay priorities:

- seize industrial nodes
- erase competing archives
- close portals after machine forces cross
- prevent dangerous technology lineages
- remove human and machine actors that can create rival futures
- establish a Causal Monopoly

This identity is original to Chaos Redux. It can use machine-apocalypse themes without copying a named film setting, visual design, character, or plot.

## Country temporal stances

Every normal country chooses or is assigned one primary stance when the evolution becomes relevant.

### Defend continuity

The country joins the Continuity Directorate's information and closure network. It gains access to verification, portal defense, and coordinated closure projects.

### Seal the breaches

The country pursues an isolationist closure policy. It avoids broader alignment and receives stronger domestic containment actions. It shares less information and can make foreign closure efforts harder.

### Exploit temporal technology

The country keeps portals open, studies future equipment, and uses later concepts. It gains powerful projects and loses Temporal Stability.

### Back a historical restoration

The country supports one active historical claimant or restored state. It gains military and legitimacy tools tied to that figure and accepts diplomatic and stability risks.

### Join the Horizon Compact

The country supports a future human program. It shares knowledge, protects compatible visitors, and pursues institutional change.

### Collaborate with the Closed Future

This rare route requires severe instability, a surviving machine foothold, and a government willing to trade autonomy for survival or power. It provides short-term machine assistance and creates a high chance of later subordination or betrayal.

The initial stance is a major policy decision, not one of six permanent buttons shown at once. After selection, the category exposes only the actions that fit the chosen route and current threats.

## Portal nodes

Evolution V upgrades selected active portals into strategic nodes. A node is a state-level target with one current controller and one status.

### Node statuses

- dormant
- contested
- stabilized
- exploited
- machine-held
- sealed

A node can support decisions, missions, claims, foreign intervention, and future faction activity. The global active node cap should remain bounded, normally six to ten depending on performance and scenario intensity.

## Continuity route

### Establish a verification bureau

The country creates an institution that compares visitor accounts, archives, machine data, and current events. It improves false-advice detection and reduces the risk of supporting an impossible claimant.

### Secure a closure site

The country holds a named portal state, supplies local units, and completes a timed closure mission.

### Reconcile the displaced

The country uses migration, identity, and archive decisions to settle people and resolve duplicate records.

### Share closure technology

The country transfers one approved method to another Directorate member. This improves global capacity and creates espionage risk.

### Coordinate a synchronized closure

Several countries close nodes within a bounded window. Success raises Temporal Stability more than isolated closures and can weaken the machine network.

## Horizon Compact route

### Select a future program

The country chooses a political, industrial, military, intelligence, or humanitarian program from a verified visitor. It commits resources to adaptation.

### Protect future delegates

The country secures a visitor conference or portal corridor against abduction and machine attack.

### Harmonize incompatible futures

The country compares two future accounts and writes a joint limited plan. This lowers internal conflict but may raise Timeline Divergence through deliberate intervention.

### Build a protected temporal corridor

The country keeps one node open under strict control. The corridor offers unique benefits and becomes a strategic target.

## Restoration Concord route

### Recognize a claimant

The country grants diplomatic status to one historical figure or restored state. The act changes legitimacy and relations.

### Raise a legacy force

The country equips and trains displaced formations under modern command.

### Convene a restoration congress

Historical claimants negotiate boundaries, succession, and mutual defense. Failure can split the Concord or start regional wars.

### Defend the restored order

The country guarantees a restored state and gains access to joint military missions.

## Exploitation route

### Strip a portal site

The country takes material quickly before closure. It gains equipment or research and damages the site.

### Deploy adapted future weapons

The country fields a limited supported batch. The action raises divergence and makes machine interest more likely.

### Redirect a portal

The country attempts to control the destination. Failure can create a new incident or machine node.

### Establish a temporal industry zone

The country dedicates factories to future material and methods. It gains output with severe sabotage and machine infiltration risk.

## Closed Future collaboration route

### Accept machine coordination

The country receives strategic data and automated production support. The machine network gains access to national systems.

### Transfer an industrial node

The country cedes control of a factory complex to the Consensus Engine. This creates a machine foothold and worsens world-end readiness.

### Purge rival futures

The collaborator attacks visitors, claimants, archives, or portal sites identified by the machine network. Public discovery creates condemnation and resistance.

### Request preservation status

The government seeks exemption from machine destruction. The response is conditional and may be deceptive. The Consensus Engine values causal utility, not loyalty.

## Temporal fracture incidents

Low stability unlocks curated incidents.

### Conflicting memories

Characters, units, or populations remember different alliances, battles, or leaders. The player chooses which record guides current policy while preserving uncertainty.

### Leadership echo

A fictional or safely represented temporal echo appears. For real figures, the event uses a crisis representation, one active character owner, or a merged identity. It does not clone a second real-person character token.

### Displaced formation

A division becomes temporarily unavailable and returns with altered experience, equipment, or allegiance. Unique units receive a protected return path.

### Alternate border claim

An alternate state delegation produces a claim, local occupation incident, or regional movement. Permanent ownership changes require a valid war, negotiated settlement, or country package.

### Erased archive

A government loses copied future knowledge, a claimant proof, or machine intelligence unless it secures the records.

### Machine amplification

An existing machine foothold gains units, factories, or a new target. This incident is one of the direct world-end readiness sources.

## War of the Possible Futures

The evolution becomes an actual global war when at least two alignments control strategic nodes, one alignment holds territory or a durable host country, and diplomatic containment fails.

The war can consist of:

- conventional wars between restored and continuity states
- portal-control interventions
- civil wars around visitor or claimant movements
- machine territorial seizures
- covert action and sabotage between aligned governments
- joint closure campaigns

The event should not force every country into one global faction war. Countries can remain neutral, seal their own breaches, or join later.

## Victory and partial success

### Continuity success

Most strategic nodes are sealed, machine footholds are destroyed, and Temporal Stability rises above `70`. Returned figures and visitors may remain as ordinary people or political actors, but new arrivals stop.

### Horizon success

A controlled network of protected portals remains, machine forces are defeated, and several future programs become permanent institutions. Stability remains imperfect.

### Restoration success

One or more historical orders survive with recognition and territorial legitimacy. The world accepts a permanently altered history.

### Fragmented peace

No alignment wins. Portal activity becomes regional, several altered states remain, and machine world-end readiness falls below threshold.

### Closed Future dominance

Machine nodes, converted industry, and low stability make the Machine Extinction War eligible. This is readiness, not automatic activation.

## AI alignment logic

AI considers ideology, current leadership, active visitors, claimant support, scientific capacity, industry, war state, machine threat, national survival, and relations.

- democracies with strong institutions often favor continuity or a lawful Horizon route
- authoritarian states may favor exploitation, controlled restoration, or security-focused continuity
- restored states favor the Concord
- countries with strong research capacity may favor Horizon or exploitation
- states under direct machine pressure favor continuity unless a collaboration faction already controls government
- collaboration must remain rare and conditional

All stance and target weights require probability inspection, evaluation, sweeps, and comparison after implementation.

## Evolution outcomes

- a stable continuity network
- a controlled future human coalition
- recognized historical restorations
- several regional portal wars
- permanent alternate states
- widespread future technology use
- machine collaboration governments
- machine world-end readiness
- partial closure and a permanently altered campaign
