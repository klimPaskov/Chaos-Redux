# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 7: Evolution IV, Epoch Breakdown

Acceptance status: prehistoric animals with Africa as a prominent region come from the user's Event 030 brief.
Families, caps, and actions are proposed design, disposition `unresolved`.

## Evolution purpose

Evolution IV shifts the event from people and knowledge to material disruption.
Portals lose precise control and move animals, formations, machines, objects, diseases, and populations between eras.
The player decides which incidents to contain, exploit, or close.

The normal gate is `800+` Chaos and Evolution III active or resolved.
The base MTTH is about 120 days, scaled by the Divergence band as Part 1 describes.
Open claimant portals, deployed future technology, incompatible visitor accounts, and unresolved disappearances shorten the pacing.

## Incident budget

The ordinary global cap is eight active incidents and a country normally holds no more than two.
High-intensity scenarios may raise those caps within declared performance limits.

Incidents use a sparse state registry.
A new incident is selected only when a free slot exists, and cleanup removes the state from the registry before another incident can use it.
No recurring whole-world scan is used.

## Incident lifecycle

Every incident passes through four stages.

1. Reported: witnesses, units, officials, or intelligence identify the disturbance, and the report states what is visible and what is uncertain.
2. Spreading or contained: the owner's choice decides whether the incident grows or its perimeter holds.
3. Resolved: the owner closes, exploits, or loses control of the incident, which creates a concrete reward and consequence.
4. Aftermath: the state keeps a bounded modifier, unit, population record, research result, or damage state, and the incident leaves the registry.

## Incident actions

Each incident shows exactly three actions in the owning country's category, so seven families never become a wall of decisions.

| Slot | Purpose | Shared rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contain or close | stop spread, then collapse the portal | commits support equipment, manpower, and local supplied divisions through a timed mission, lowers Timeline Divergence on closure |
| Study or exploit | turn the incident into research, equipment, recruits, or intelligence | commits scientists or industry, raises Timeline Divergence |
| Family action | the one response that only this family needs | listed per family below |

A fourth option, inviting foreign observers, appears only as an event option when the owner lacks capacity, and it trades Exposure and a Future Fragment for assistance.

## Incident families

### 1. Prehistoric fauna

A portal deposits dinosaurs or extinct megafauna in a suitable state.
Terrain, climate, population, and control decide the profile: large predators, herbivore herds, pack hunters, extinct mammals, or a coastal creature.
Africa is a prominent region, and other suitable regions remain possible.
Family action: evacuate exposed settlements through a migration adapter request.
Exploiting the incident captures specimens or turns the area into a training ground for army experience.

While uncontrolled, the incident damages the state, disrupts supply, and kills civilians through exact population transactions recorded in the Deaths ledger under a temporal-incident cause.
Prehistoric fauna never creates a country and needs no unit model.

### 2. Displaced formations

A coherent ancient, medieval, or early gunpowder formation appears with officers, weapons, camp followers, and little supply.
Family action: negotiate with the formation, which can surrender, seek service under a returned figure, demand land or passage, or attempt to return through the portal.
Exploiting it recruits the formation, which needs equipment, training time, translators, and a valid command arrangement and gains no combat strength from reputation.
A formation that seizes territory begins as a local revolt.
Formation spawning reuses compatible Event 019 provider patterns while Event 030 keeps temporal provenance and cleanup.

### 3. Lost settlements and displaced civilians

A settlement, convoy, expedition, or civilian group arrives after spending a different amount of time in another era.
Family action: receive, quarantine, resettle, repatriate, or reject the group.
New arrivals register as an event-created cohort and are never debited from a nonexistent origin.
Moving an existing population between states uses a migration adapter request, so the origin is debited once and survivors are credited once.
Closed borders create trapped people and reception pressure instead of erasing the cohort.

### 4. Future equipment

A portal deposits vehicles, drones, components, medical stores, or communication systems from a later era.
Family action: share samples with an ally, which grants a Future Fragment and raises Divergence.
Exploiting it either reverse-engineers one concept through research or fields one bounded equipment batch that suffers maintenance and replacement problems.
Deployment gives the strongest immediate benefit and the largest Divergence increase, and it never grants every technology of an imagined future donor.

### 5. Temporal disease

A pathogen arrives from another era or with a population of different immunity.
Family action: quarantine and treat through the biological outbreak owner.
The disease profile defines transmission, severity, treatment limits, and quarantine behavior, and the biological lifecycle owner runs the outbreak, its deaths, and its Deaths records.
Event 030 does not duplicate that system.
Concealing the outbreak is an event option that creates condemnation when it is discovered.
A future medical cache from the equipment family can help treatment at a Divergence cost.

### 6. Timeline rift

A state experiences time skips, repeated hours, conflicting maps, or objects from a timeline that never existed, such as flags, laws, archives, currency, or monuments.
Family action: authenticate and archive the evidence, which can support a claimant, reveal a possible alliance or war, give a research clue, or expose that two visitors remember different futures.
Physical effects stay temporary: a railway or port unusable for a defined period, a unit that returns with missing time and damaged organization, or a checkpoint taking orders from an alternate government.
Border effects create claims or temporary local control problems with a rollback path, never a silent permanent ownership change.
A vanished unique division or character always returns through a recorded event unless the player accepts a clear terminal outcome.
This family is compact and fills a slot when the cap cannot support another large military crisis.

### 7. Local machine foothold

Damaged autonomous machines, scouts, factory infiltrators, or stranded logistics machines appear.
They foreshadow the Closed Future but do not begin the world-end invasion.
Family action: trace the machines' origin or feed them false strategic data.
Exploiting them reprograms one limited function for industry or intelligence.
An ignored cell seizes or sabotages one factory complex.
Three or more surviving footholds across two strategic regions satisfy one world-end footprint condition in Part 9.
Machines use the original industrial visual identity from Part 9.

## Incident interaction

Two active families combine only through curated rules:

- a historical claimant recruits a displaced formation
- a future visitor identifies future equipment
- temporal disease spreads through a displaced civilian cohort
- machine scouts seek a future equipment cache
- a timeline rift exposes an archive that supports a claimant

There is no universal random combination table.

## Shared-system routing

Civilian deaths use exact population transactions and the Deaths ledger.
Evacuation and resettlement use migration adapter requests with proven state, people, actor, and cause.
Food disruption from an isolated rift zone or a blocked cohort reaches famine only as a famine adapter request with state, actor, source, amount, and generation proof, and the famine owner decides whether a real food problem exists.
Temporal activity does not raise air contamination by itself.

## AI incident behavior

- A state under immediate military threat favors containment.
- A scientifically strong state at peace favors study.
- A weak country invites foreign observers.
- A ruthless or desperate state exploits civilians or fields unstable equipment.
- AI evacuates only with a valid destination and transport capacity.
- AI never studies an incident while losing its capital.

## Evolution outcomes

- contained and studied anomaly network
- several closed portals and reduced divergence
- future equipment deployed or destroyed
- recruited displaced formations
- resettled or trapped temporal populations
- active disease outbreak
- surviving prehistoric threat
- machine footholds across industrial regions
- enough instability for temporal stances to form

## Chaos changes

Evolution activation gives zero Chaos.
Incident-owned Chaos comes from the first material manifestation of each family, a durable machine foothold, a large uncontrolled outbreak, a public displaced-population crisis, or closure of a dangerous portal.
Deaths, wars, nuclear use, and contamination remain generic sources and are never counted twice.
