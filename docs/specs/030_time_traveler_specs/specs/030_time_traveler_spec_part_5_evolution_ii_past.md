# Event 030 Time Traveler Specification Part 5: Evolution II, The Door to the Past

## Evolution purpose

Evolution II creates a limited set of historical arrivals whose reputations, claims, and methods collide with the modern state. Each arrival must create public consequences. A returned figure cannot enter silently as a free advisor.

The normal gate is `400+` Chaos, Evolution I resolved or safely bypassed, and Timeline Divergence high enough to support stable portals. The base MTTH is about 135 days. A public traveler, an active traveler government, an unresolved temporal disappearance, and copied knowledge in several countries shorten the pacing.

## Campaign limits

A normal campaign should receive one initial historical figure and no more than three active historical figure packages at the same time. A fourth can appear only after one earlier package has resolved or during a high-intensity manual scenario.

Selection uses a curated eligibility registry. The system does not scan every historical person or create a random name generator.

The initial pool contains nine flagship figures from the source brief:

- Napoleon Bonaparte
- Julius Caesar
- Alexander the Great
- Genghis Khan
- Frederick the Great
- Joan of Arc
- Saladin
- Simón Bolívar
- Tokugawa Ieyasu

Any later addition requires a researched package with host logic, political role, limitations, assets, AI, outcomes, and cleanup.

## Standard arrival sequence

Every historical package follows the same six-stage architecture while preserving figure-specific content.

### Arrival report

The figure appears in a place connected to their life, conquest, legacy, dynasty, or political memory. The first report identifies witnesses, clothing, language, artifacts, behavior, and the immediate public response.

### Authenticity test

The host tests language, private historical knowledge, scars, handwriting, military memory, material evidence, or personal relationships preserved in archives. The test should not depend on generated direct quotations.

### Initial custody

The government chooses protection, public presentation, detention, military consultation, scholarly examination, exile, or transfer.

### Movement formation

Followers, opponents, veterans, officers, monarchists, republicans, religious groups, regional claimants, or foreign sponsors organize around the figure. The movement uses hidden support and visible events instead of another public meter.

### Power decision

The figure accepts or rejects an advisor role, military commission, symbolic status, political office, or restoration claim. The host can negotiate, suppress, co-opt, or contest the claim.

### Resolution

The package ends in integration, defeat, exile, death, imprisonment, leadership, limited regional revolt, full civil war, or restoration. Surviving consequences remain through characters, ideas, claims, diplomatic memories, or a country package.

## Shared historical limitations

Returned figures know their own era. They do not know modern combined arms, radio command, industrial mobilization, mass-party politics, modern bureaucracy, aviation, chemical warfare, or nuclear technology unless they learn it.

A returned commander begins with strong leadership traits and a severe modernity limitation. Training, staff work, and battlefield experience can mitigate the limitation. The player should not receive an instantly perfect field marshal.

A returned ruler has strong symbolic legitimacy among some groups and strong rejection among others. Their historical reputation does not grant automatic modern political legitimacy.

## Napoleon Bonaparte package

### Eligible hosts and regions

France and French successor governments are primary. Corsica, French-aligned exiles, and a valid French civil-war actor may qualify. Italy can receive a secondary Napoleonic legacy incident, but Napoleon should not normally become an Italian national leader.

### Movement

The movement attracts officers, veterans, centralizers, legal reformers, imperial nostalgists, and people who believe the current regime has wasted French power.

### Gameplay identity

Napoleon offers operational concentration, administrative centralization, legal reform, and rapid promotion by competence. His risks are personal rule, preventive war, continental ambition, and underestimating industrial attrition.

### Main routes

- military adviser under civilian authority
- emergency commander during national defeat
- constitutional Bonapartist political movement
- imperial claimant and civil-war leader
- detained national symbol used by the government
- foreign-backed exile claimant

A successful restoration may use a French Empire cosmetic identity and a focused decision overlay. It should not receive a full replacement tree unless the restoration becomes durable enough to justify one.

## Julius Caesar package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Italy, a Roman restoration state, or a government controlling Rome is primary.

### Movement

The movement attracts officers, classical nationalists, veterans, populist organizers, and elites who see dictatorship as an answer to paralysis.

### Gameplay identity

Caesar excels at political coalition, military initiative, personal loyalty, and decisive civil conflict. His ascent carries a direct republican-collapse risk. He must learn modern logistics, industrial war, and mass administration.

### Main routes

- military and political adviser
- emergency dictator with a fixed mandate
- public contest over republican institutions
- Roman restoration claimant
- assassination crisis involving supporters and opponents

The route should focus on the conflict between personal authority and modern state institutions. It should not reduce Caesar to a generic Roman empire button.

## Alexander the Great package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Greece and Macedonia are primary. A broader Hellenic, Persian, Egyptian, or Levantine host can receive a secondary succession claim when it controls a relevant legacy region.

### Movement

The movement attracts expansionist officers, Hellenic nationalists, youth organizations, and regional groups seeking a transnational imperial identity.

### Gameplay identity

Alexander provides audacious operational tempo, personal battlefield leadership, and a vision of cross-regional empire. His weaknesses are succession, institutional depth, and the scale of modern supply.

### Main routes

- commander under a modern general staff
- pan-Hellenic political symbol
- claimant seeking a new Macedonian empire
- eastern campaign movement supported by regional collaborators
- defeated claimant whose followers fragment after loss

A victory should create a succession problem and should never grant an effortless permanent empire.

## Genghis Khan package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Mongolia is primary. Inner Asian, Chinese, Soviet steppe, or other valid legacy governments may host the arrival when they control a connected region.

### Movement

The movement attracts cavalry traditions, steppe communities, military federalists, regional leaders, and people seeking protection through a new confederation.

### Gameplay identity

Genghis offers mobile command, merit-based promotion, relay communication, federated rule, and rapid adaptation of conquered administration. His risks are conquest pressure, violent discipline, and conflict with fixed industrial borders.

### Main routes

- national commander and unifier
- regional confederation leader
- Great Khan claimant
- negotiated protector of autonomous regions
- defeated movement that splinters into successor claimants

The design should use the administrative and communication side of Mongol rule as well as warfare. It should avoid a one-note horde caricature.

## Frederick the Great package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Germany, Prussia, or a government controlling the core Prussian legacy region is primary.

### Movement

The movement attracts professional officers, state reformers, monarchists, and administrators who want disciplined state service.

### Gameplay identity

Frederick offers operational discipline, fiscal-military administration, officer reform, and strategic risk-taking. His weaknesses are modern mass politics, mechanized scale, and a tendency to overvalue a small professional elite.

### Main routes

- army reformer under the existing state
- restored Prussian monarch
- rival to a German ideological regime
- emergency commander during invasion
- exiled claimant sponsored by a foreign power

## Joan of Arc package

### Eligible hosts and regions

France and French successor governments are primary, especially during occupation, civil war, or national defeat.

### Movement

The movement attracts soldiers, religious communities, national resistance groups, civilians under occupation, and people who see the arrival as proof of national deliverance.

### Gameplay identity

Joan is primarily a symbol, organizer, and commander under severe moral and political pressure. She should not default to seeking the throne. Her package centers on resistance, legitimacy, military morale, religious authority, and the government's response to an uncontrollable symbol.

### Main routes

- protected national symbol
- field commander in a liberation struggle
- independent resistance movement
- persecuted figure whose trial radicalizes supporters
- negotiated religious and political role after victory

Her civil-war chance should be lower than that of rulers who directly claim sovereignty. Government persecution, collaboration, or foreign occupation can raise it.

## Saladin package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Egypt, Syria, Iraq, the Levant, and connected Arab or Muslim governments are primary. The host must have a meaningful geographic or political connection.

### Movement

The movement attracts officers, religious scholars, anti-occupation groups, regional unionists, and governments seeking a respected coalition leader.

### Gameplay identity

Saladin offers coalition building, patient strategy, legitimacy across divided territories, and a balance between warfare and negotiated settlement. His package should reflect regional political complexity and must avoid generic religious-war treatment.

### Main routes

- coalition commander against occupation
- unifying political leader
- Ayyubid restoration claimant
- diplomatic mediator among rival governments
- imprisoned or exiled symbol whose supporters organize abroad

## Simón Bolívar package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Bolivia, Peru, and connected northern South American governments are primary.

### Movement

The movement attracts officers, republicans, continental unionists, anti-colonial groups, and factions divided between centralism and federalism.

### Gameplay identity

Bolívar offers liberation warfare, rapid political union, continental diplomacy, and a powerful regional project. His risks are overcentralization, military politics, and the fracture of a union after victory.

### Main routes

- military and diplomatic adviser
- leader of a regional liberation coalition
- Gran Colombia restoration claimant
- constitutional unionist opposed by federal movements
- defeated unifier whose movement survives across borders

A restoration must include integration, recognition, and federal conflict. It cannot be an instant core grant over a continent.

## Tokugawa Ieyasu package

### Eligible hosts and regions

Japan is primary. A Japanese civil-war actor can also qualify.

### Movement

The movement attracts conservative military houses, administrators, regional elites, controlled-trade advocates, and groups hostile to the current ruling coalition.

### Gameplay identity

Ieyasu offers patient coalition building, ordered succession, regional control, selective foreign trade, and a durable military government. His risks are conflict with modern imperial institutions, overcontrol of society, and misreading industrial international competition.

### Main routes

- senior state adviser
- military regent during crisis
- restored shogunal movement
- negotiated dual authority with existing institutions
- defeated claimant whose regional network persists

## Inter-figure conflicts

Two figures connected to the same legacy region can create a rare conflict only when both are active and the campaign state supports it. The system should prefer political rivalry, competing movements, or a claimant conference before creating a second civil war.

Possible conflicts include republican versus imperial authority, national versus transnational claims, religious symbol versus state control, and centralized empire versus federal union.

## Historical figure assets and ownership

Every real figure requires attributed sourced portrait material, subject ownership checks, and one character token that cannot exist in two active country rosters at once. The event may change that character's role through guarded transfer. It should not clone the same real person into separate leader and claimant characters.

A character who acts as leader, commander, or advisor may need distinct consumer-specific presentation, but those assets must preserve one identity and one active ownership path.

## Historical package outcomes

- trusted adviser or commander
- protected public symbol
- imprisoned or exiled figure
- negotiated political office
- regional claimant movement
- coup or civil war
- restored historical identity
- death or assassination with a successor movement
- peaceful retirement after the crisis

A resolved package leaves a historical memory flag so the same figure cannot reappear from another portal in ordinary play. Evolution V may create temporal echoes, while real-person duplicates remain event representations and never become duplicate character tokens.
