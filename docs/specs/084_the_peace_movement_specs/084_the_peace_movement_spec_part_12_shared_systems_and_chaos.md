## Part 12 · Shared systems, Chaos impact, and event connections

### Event identity and cluster accounting

Event 84 is a Major and is globally fire-once as an episode origin.
Its internal spread, decisions, evolutions, wars, and later government transfers are continuations of that episode.
They are not additional root firings.
The uploaded catalog identity replaces the supplied legacy row and old Political Power implementation through the proper catalog ownership workflow.

The event belongs to Peace as a High member and Domestic Unrest as a High member.
A selection through either cluster must mark the same Event 84 root identity consumed.
Membership in both clusters does not create two copies, two major resets, or two independent evolution histories.
A cluster activation counts as one development for timer and weight accounting under the shared cluster rules.
Event 84's Major identity must remain visible even when a cluster owns that activation's shared pacing transaction.

The complete selection and cluster implementation must be inspected before coding the precise major-versus-cluster accounting call.
The supplied source documents establish the design rule, but the actual dispatchers were not fully provided.
A root that starts no valid country must not consume rewards or partially reset the global system.

### Chaos impact ownership

The following values are authored event-specific contributions for concrete political escalation.
They are distinct from the shared effects of war, annexation, civilian loss, and other crisis systems.
The user's Chaos level 1 catalog field is preserved and does not automatically mean each later incident adds 1 Chaos.

| Development | Event-specific Chaos | Ownership and recurrence |
|---|---:|---|
| Successful first public manifestation | +5 | Once for the episode, unless the validated root framework already owns an equivalent opening award |
| First durable spread to five distinct countries | +10 | Once globally, counts actual surviving organizations rather than five repeated contacts |
| First durable spread to fifteen distinct countries | +15 | Once globally, separate scale milestone |
| Peaceful legal reform or voluntary coming out | 0 | Never treated as disorder merely because it protects queer people |
| Evolution stage becomes available or is recorded | 0 | Its later concrete consequences own their own effects |
| Founding a formal rival International that establishes a polarized two-bloc order | +20 | Once when both blocs actually exist, not once per congress delegate |
| Failed eradication creates an actual institutional rupture | +15 | Once per country for that distinct campaign, with a 360-day same-cause guard |
| Civil war begins | 0 additional here | The shared war and actual casualty systems own their normal contributions |
| Foreign intervention begins a new war | 0 additional here | The shared war owner records it once |
| A verified distinct political assassination destabilizes an established national authority | +10 | Once per genuine case, with actual deaths passed separately to the death system |
| Durable negotiated domestic settlement | −5 | Once per country after 180 days of maintained terms, no repeated posture cycling |
| Durable international settlement dissolves the active restoration conflict | −20 | Once for the actual international settlement, not for each signing member |
| Terminal activation or cinematic playback | 0 | World-end state change is not a second escalation charge |
| Manual setup | +200/+400/+600/+1,000 | The selected setup addition owns all seeded escalation, with no retrospective root or congress bonuses |

If a generic helper already assigns Chaos to the same manifestation, choose one owner and preserve the intended total.
Do not combine a generic opening award with this +5 by accident.
Subsequent religious debates, family-policy decisions, festivals, and character disclosures usually have no direct Chaos award.
Their importance is domestic political change rather than global catastrophe points.

The project records one Chaos per million actual deaths.
Event 84 must not add that contribution a second time.
A failed policy that reduces future growth records no deaths.
A copied incident report records no new victims.
A war's military casualties and a separate civilian atrocity remain different factual events with their own proper accounting.

### Deaths, repression, famine, and migration

All affected countries remain ordinary human civilian polities.
Commune life, spirituality, sexuality, or unusual uniforms do not exempt them from food, migration, civilian protection, or demography.
Use the shared civilian-system eligibility predicate and its actual inverse exclusions.
Do not add the movement to nonhuman exemptions.

Suppression of an organization does not imply deaths, and political arrests do not delete a state's population.
Actual civilian deaths from a separately defined violent incident go to the Deaths system and its Chaos conversion.

| Shared system | How Event 84 uses it | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [Migration](../../systems/migration_system.md) | A failed or abusive suppression and an active eradication campaign submit owner-proven persecution pressure for the affected states, so supporters flee and neighbours decide whether to receive them. Movement-led governments answer through migration's own Request Passage, Open Emergency Reception, Screen Incoming Civilians, and Resettle Abroad decisions; the asylum instrument in Part 9 starts or funds one of those transactions rather than moving people itself | Migration owns cohorts, routes, reception, and settlement. Event 84 never writes population |
| [Famine](../../systems/famine_system.md) | A communal kitchen or welfare charter in a state with a visible food problem counts as delivered relief only through Restore Relief, Import Food, or Release Food Stores. A movement government's welfare charter can pay for one of those decisions instead of a separate Event 84 project | No Event 84 food value. Requisition Food and Conceal Hunger under a traditionalist government are ordinary famine choices, not repression incidents, unless they target a named movement organization |
| Camps | Arrests during eradication may be routed into an existing camp administration (Part 3) | Detention, mortality, exposure, and atrocity condemnation belong to the camp and condemnation owners |
| [Condemnation](../../systems/cbrn_warfare/condemnation/condemnation_sanctions.md) and CBRN | A country at condemnation tier 3 (100) or higher, or recently struck by a recorded CBRN attack, receives the single war-burden Influence source in Part 2 | Event 84 adds no condemnation of its own and never recreates weapon or contamination effects |

Event 84's two public values remain its only new public country meters; famine, migration, and condemnation keep their own established values.

### Timing and performance contract

One root selection initializes one country.
Affected-country callbacks drive their own 30-day political reviews, with shorter bounded checks only for active urgent missions.
Foreign contacts are bounded.
A newly affected country registers its own callback once.
A dormant country removes unnecessary work until a concrete activation signal returns.

Do not add a new all-country daily, weekly, or monthly loop without the user's explicit approval.
Reuse an existing shared service only after confirming its scope, schedule, and ownership.
Full-world work is limited to necessary bounded initialization, an authoritative terminal recount, or an already established shared cache update.
Queued callbacks must not multiply after reload, government transfer, or tag replacement.

### Data and lifecycle boundaries

Country values, organization state, character cases, policy terms, route identity, and mission commitments survive save and reload.
Temporary candidates, preview selections, and display scratch values do not become persistent authoritative state.
A transfer from a dead controller to a successor must preserve the episode's actual organization and case history without copying its effects twice.

A country ceasing to exist ends or transfers its institutions according to the actual territorial and political outcome.
An exile committee can survive with a sponsor.
A nonexistent country cannot receive delayed material rewards that suddenly pay out when it is released years later.
A case tied to a dead character closes or becomes historical evidence, not an active appointment bonus.
