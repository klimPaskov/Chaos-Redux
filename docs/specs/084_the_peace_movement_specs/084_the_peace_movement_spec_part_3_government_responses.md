# 84 · The Peace Movement
## Part 3 · Government responses and playable commitments

### Choosing a response

The government can tolerate, negotiate, co-opt, suppress, or attempt to eradicate the movement.
The opening report explains these alternatives and their immediate consequences.
Later changes use the same response family rather than adding five new permanent buttons for every evolution.
A response remains in force for at least 60 days unless a civil war, government transfer, or explicit emergency breaks it.

An emergency change cancels unfinished benefits, preserves spent resources and documented abuses, and adds 5 Reaction for the visible policy reversal.
A peaceful change at the end of the minimum period has no automatic Chaos cost.
A country cannot collect the rewards of several incompatible responses during one review period.

### Tolerate

Open organizing is permitted.
The government pays 25 political power to formalize assembly rules, and the movement receives its ordinary plus 5 monthly open-organizing contribution.
A credible nonviolence agreement applies a minus 5 monthly Reaction contribution while both sides respect it.
This does not erase separate Reaction from draft refusal, production disruption, or an actual attack on a place of worship.

The player gains civic-project and cultural-exchange opportunities.
These use factories, equipment, and actual construction objectives.
They can raise Influence and improve civilian output without automatically changing the ruling party.
The tradeoff becomes serious during war, when the government must negotiate service alternatives or accept slower mobilization.

Toleration fails politically when the government promises assembly rights and then repeatedly intervenes without a new mandate.
That creates a broken-agreement incident, not an invisible random penalty.
A movement that breaks an agreed nonviolence condition also faces a public dispute.
Investigating the responsible organization can preserve the broader settlement.

### Negotiate

Negotiation opens a 90-day agreement process.
The government chooses a concrete package and must deliver it.
The initial cost is 50 political power.
A small, medium, or large administration also commits 1, 3, or 5 civilian factories during the active process.
Those factories are a temporary capacity commitment, not destroyed buildings.

The selectable packages concern civilian service, assembly and censorship, anti-discrimination and relationship recognition, demobilization, or a local communal-welfare charter.
The package is chosen among the options of the event that opens the negotiation, not through a second decision.
The government can negotiate packages in successive rounds, with only one national agreement active at a time.
The package records precisely which law or institutional concession is promised.

A full agreement requires enactment plus its relevant objective.
Civilian service must supply an actual reconstruction or transport contribution.
Demobilization must lower an applicable law or release a promised contingent when military conditions allow.
A legal-rights agreement must be maintained through the deadline rather than enacted and immediately revoked.
A welfare charter must deliver a building or repair quota in the selected anchor.

Full success reduces Reaction by 15 and confrontational Influence by 5, preserves the granted reform, and creates a 180-day settlement commitment.
Partial success reduces Reaction by 5 and allows one 60-day extension without a second completion reward.
Failure adds 10 Influence and 10 Reaction because the promise was broken.
Losing the named state to a third-party invasion cancels the local objective without calling it bad faith.
Spent political power stays spent and unelapsed factory commitment is released.

A compulsory Law Upgrade wave can make a promised demobilization result undelivered without the government choosing to break it.
Preserve completed civic work and the existing legal protections, then use the same one-time 60-day extension already available to an incomplete negotiation to repair that service term.
The government may perform the valid law change or negotiate an explicitly accepted defensive-service amendment; neither receives full settlement credit until delivered.
Ordinary law-owner costs still apply, including the doubled extreme-law reversal price.
The outside wave alone does not apply the broken-agreement +10 Influence/+10 Reaction result.
Deliberate repudiation or expiry without an agreed repair follows the existing failure rule.
The held conscription law can still contribute its ordinary grievance at the next review, without an additional per-wave grievance or a new response mission.

### Co-opt

Co-option invites a movement representative into an existing institution, sponsors a public project, and attempts to separate moderate organizers from more radical demands.
It costs 25 political power and one capacity-scaled civic delivery package.
Its active period is 120 days.

The government chooses whether the representative receives real authority over the promised project.
Delivering the project reduces Influence by 10 as an independent oppositional force and adds 5 Stability points within the social-settlement family for 180 days.
The project itself remains.
A co-opted official can stay as a normal political actor with a disclosed movement alignment.

A purely rhetorical offer provides no permanent mechanical reward.
If it is exposed or the project is abandoned, Influence rises by 15 and Reaction by 10.
The official can resign, become an independent movement patron, or remain and defend the government.
A repeated co-option attempt with the same organization within 180 days is unavailable.

This route cannot covertly enact every movement reform at zero political cost.
Its purpose is institutional accommodation under the existing regime, with a credibility problem that the player must manage through delivery.

### Suppress

Suppression is a 90-day campaign to restrict visible political organization.
Its first commitment costs 50 political power and a country-capacity-scaled support-equipment package.
It also ties up 1, 3, or 5 civilian factories for administration.
It does not grant the government intelligence-agency capabilities that it does not possess.

The player must identify a named active organization, maintain control of its anchor, and keep an accountable security command in place.
Ordinary security capacity and political negotiations remain available without intelligence DLC.
An agency can add investigation context where its actual capabilities exist, but the core response does not depend on an operative UI.

The campaign has three possible resolved states.
A contained organization loses 15 visible Influence and becomes dormant or underground rather than erasing its supporters.
An incomplete campaign loses 5 Influence but gains a surviving underground node.
A failed or abusive campaign raises Influence by 15 and Reaction by 15, creates a verified repression aftermath, submits persecution pressure for the anchor state to the migration system, and can expose a military-loyalty dispute.

Suppression success is a contest over organization, territorial control, and command discipline.
It is not calculated from religious affiliation, LGBTQ identity, or a universal national stereotype.
Exact outcome weights remain subject to the probability scenarios.
Once the player has fulfilled a guaranteed containment agreement, the game does not secretly reroll it into a massacre.

### Eradicate

Eradication attempts to dismantle the movement's political infrastructure nationwide.
It is available only when the government controls its capital, retains a loyal chain of command, and has not already entered an unresolved civil war.
It costs 100 political power, 50 command power, a capacity-scaled support-equipment package, and a 180-day civilian-factory commitment.
Those four spendable cost types are the complete cost.
There is no hidden fifth manpower or fuel debit.

The player must close the identified organizational anchors, prevent an armed breakaway from controlling an alternative capital, and resolve the principal leadership case.
Completing all objectives reduces visible Influence by 30, disbands the event's public political organizations, and leaves a 180-day aftermath of surveillance costs, legal disputes, and damaged foreign relations.
Individual identities, ordinary religious practice, and the living population are not deleted.

Partial completion lowers Influence by 10 and creates an exiled or underground successor organization.
Failure raises Influence by 20 and Reaction by 20 and opens a rupture incident.
If the civil-war prerequisites are present, that incident can begin an uprising.
If they are not, the consequence is mass noncooperation, a leadership crisis, or armed local unrest rather than an invalid civil war.

The response is deliberately dangerous and politically costly.
While the campaign runs, the country counts as actively persecuting for the migration system, so supporters can flee and neighbours can open reception for them.
If the government already operates the camp system, it may route arrests into camp custody; detention, deaths, exposure, and condemnation then belong to the camp and condemnation owners, and Event 84 records only the repression incident.
An achievement never rewards a count of persecuted people or a successful outing.
Any real civilian deaths caused by a separately defined violent incident belong to the shared casualty system.
Eradication itself has no fictional demographic-deletion effect.

### Administrative scale and affordability

For planning, classify usable civilian capacity below 10 as small, 10–29 as medium, and 30 or more as large.
A small support package is 50 support equipment.
A medium package is 150.
A large package is 300.
A civic transport package uses 5, 15, or 30 motorized equipment where the project actually requires vehicles.
A sea corridor uses 5, 10, or 20 convoys instead of motorized equipment, never in addition merely to lengthen the cost list.

Freeze the displayed band and amount when a commitment is accepted.
Later territorial gains do not secretly increase its bill.
A country that cannot afford a large commitment may select a smaller objective with a smaller payoff, but cannot complete the full-size objective for the smaller price.
Factory commitment cannot exceed the country's available civilian capacity.
A zero-industry country uses political negotiation and local repair objectives until it has capacity, rather than receiving fictional factories to pay a fee.

### Phased decision surface

The visible surface contains no more than six primary actions and no more than three active missions across the whole event.
The phase matrix in the decisions file specifies the complete simultaneous action sets.
Changing phase replaces finished or irrelevant options.
It does not leave old categories full of inactive choices.

The opening policy phase exposes the five response alternatives and the current local dispute.
Once a mandate is chosen, only its relevant action remains alongside the civic objective, character case, foreign contact, political settlement, and eligible emergency response.
In a civil war, battlefield supply, command, foreign assistance, ceasefire, and settlement replace peacetime cultural management.
The player never has to scan every decision in the catalog at once.

Each commitment shows its owner, target, non-consumed requirements, exact spend, deadline, success outcome, partial outcome, and cancellation rule.
Resource icons belong in the actual game interface.
The Markdown cost names describe the design and do not authorize literal text-only cost strings in-game.

### Fair deadlines and aftermath

A local civic program lasts at least 90 days.
A national agreement generally lasts 120–180 days.
International relief and reconstruction use 180 days.
The 30-day command emergency is exceptional because it is a response to a named imminent rupture, not an ordinary development mission disguised as a crisis.

Success happens automatically when the objective is achieved.
The player does not click a second confirmation to receive the same reward.
Partial success is evaluated against actual completed work.
Cancellation does not turn a failure into a full refund.
Territory or target loss caused by another event receives a factual cancellation outcome, and an implementation handoff must distinguish that from the player's deliberate abandonment.

Reconstruction missions make the country function after the political choice.
They restore supply, reduce the disruption component of the service penalty, reconcile local institutions, and replace temporary crisis effects with permanent route institutions.
Their rewards are not unlimited political power, factories, or equipment for repeatedly selecting the same agreement.
