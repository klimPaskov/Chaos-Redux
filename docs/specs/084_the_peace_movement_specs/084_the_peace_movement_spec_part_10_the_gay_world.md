# 84 · The Peace Movement
## Part 10 · World-end scenario: The Gay World

### The terminal threshold

The Gay World is the movement's celebratory identity for a new global social order. It does not mean that everyone changes sexual orientation, joins a commune, abandons religion, or becomes childless. The event's central political demand is a protected right to live freely within a durable international peace settlement.

All four supplied requirements must be true at the same committed activation check. Global Chaos must be strictly above 1,000. Movement-led or movement-dominated countries must control strictly more than 50% of the living world population. The Rainbow International must exist. Organized traditionalist opposition must still control meaningful territory. The branch must also be enabled in Event Details and no incompatible terminal scenario may already own world-end progression.

Exactly 1,000 Chaos fails the first requirement. Exactly 50% fails the second. A floating-point display rounded to 50% must not decide the comparison. Use the actual shared population units, compare twice the qualifying population with the world total, and retain the precise internal result. A zero living-world denominator is invalid for this ending.

### Living population accounting

Count each inhabited state once under its current actual controller, using the shared authoritative current living population. Do not count both owner and controller. Do not add a country's population to the population of its subjects when the same states are already included. Displacement removes people from an origin and adds surviving arrivals to a destination through the migration system, not through another Event 84 copy of those totals.

The shared world denominator also includes living people held outside resident states in a supported travel, missing-person, or reception balance, exactly once. In [Airship's population-backed voyage](../080_airship_specs/080_airship_spec_part_3_passengers_and_hosts.md#population-conservation), boarding removes people from their origin state but does not remove them from the living world. Such people contribute to the denominator while alive and unsettled, but not to a controlled-state numerator until a valid destination settlement places them there. Passenger nationality, the USA's expedition control, or a last-known host does not establish control of an inhabited state. Returning, settling, birth, and confirmed death use the shared population owner's result, with no second Event 84 population write. This shared total is an implementation proof gate; a state-only recount cannot be labelled complete while live custody exists. Without live off-state balances, the ordinary state calculation is unchanged.

The qualifying controller must be movement-led or movement-dominated under Part 6. Popular organizations alone do not qualify. An occupied state under a qualifying controller contributes its actual living population because the supplied requirement is control, not unanimous ideological agreement. Its inhabitants remain politically diverse. Resistance and humanitarian obligations still apply.

Civilian deaths already removed by the shared system do not remain in the denominator. Foregone births caused by a growth policy never enter the living total in the first place. They are not deaths. Undead or nonhuman systems must be reconciled with the project's authoritative living-population definition rather than guessed from a tag or cosmetic name. This dependency is an implementation gate because the full consumer code was not available in the source archive.

Use an event-triggered dirty cache or an existing shared authoritative cache, with a full recount when preparing the terminal commit. Population, control, government authority, and bloc changes mark the relevant totals dirty. Do not implement a new daily full-world population scan for this event.

### Meaningful organized opposition

A qualifying opponent is a functioning traditionalist government or territorial authority participating in an organized rival bloc or active coordinated restoration coalition. It must control inhabited territory and retain a command structure. Ordinary conservative voters, peaceful religious institutions, or a legal opposition party do not satisfy the enemy requirement.

For initial tuning, meaningful territory is at least 5% of the living world population under organized rival control, or a surviving current major power's capital plus at least 25 usable military factories under that rival's control. These are authored operational thresholds for the user's qualitative requirement. They prevent a one-island remnant with no strategic capacity from manufacturing a global finale while allowing an industrial great power with a smaller population to remain a meaningful opponent.

If the movement already peacefully established the order and no organized territorial opponent remains, resolve a nonterminal diplomatic consolidation instead. Do not spawn a traditionalist country merely to satisfy a cinematic gate.

### Activation sequence

Commit terminal ownership once, record the qualifying political and population snapshot, stop ordinary automatic random-event firing through the shared world-end mechanism, and preserve Event 84's own war, diplomacy, recruitment, and settlement callbacks. Existing wars and unrelated active humanitarian systems continue. Opening The Gay World does not freeze famine, migration, casualties, or the clock.

The super-event announces global social dominance, not military victory. Its presentation must distinguish the movement's celebration from the fact that an organized opponent remains. The title requested by the user is preserved as the scenario identity, while the final description, quotation, button wording, audio, and cultural allusion remain subject to researched localisation.

The event creates a final congress mandate. Members decide whether to prioritize negotiated accession, political support inside remaining powers, a defensive strategy that seeks rival collapse, or an explicitly authorized liberation war. These are approaches to the same world-order objectives, with different diplomatic and military costs. There is no immediate annexation or forced peaceful transfer of every nonaligned country.

### Final campaign objectives

The Rainbow International must protect established member states, bring remaining major centers of power into the settlement, defeat or lawfully dissolve the rival restoration command, implement legal protection against persecution, and establish a workable postwar service and economic order. It may achieve accession through treaty, local constitutional change, civil-war settlement, or military victory. Every accession must have an actual sovereign authority able to honor it.

A negotiated accession needs a governing authority, a legal-equality and religious-freedom commitment, a civilian command guarantee, and a functioning settlement relationship. A government cannot count twice by signing a treaty and later formally joining the bloc. A conquered state needs a completed local authority settlement before it counts as a stable member of the final order.

The movement can support legal opposition and civic organization abroad. Internal collapse behind enemy lines requires actual local Influence, grievances, and institutions. A global button does not assign high Influence to all enemy states or destroy all opposing armies.

### Major centers of world power

At terminal activation, record the functioning capitals of all currently valid major powers and a population-and-industry summary. Reconcile an annexed or relocated capital with its legitimate current successor, rather than keeping an unreachable deleted-state objective. Do not move the target merely because a player temporarily relocates a capital to avoid the campaign.

For victory, all surviving major-power centers in that snapshot must be under the control of a member or an authority that has ratified the final settlement and accepted its congress's limited security jurisdiction. Mere good relations, a guarantee, or military access is insufficient. A consenting nonmember can retain domestic sovereignty under the settlement's explicit international security authority. This is a negotiated political outcome, not a claim that the Rainbow International owns its population or territory.

In addition, the settlement authorities must control at least 75% of usable military factories worldwide and still control more than 50% of living world population. The industry requirement prevents declaring world military order while another industrial system remains dominant. These are authored victory operationalizations. The supplied activation threshold remains unchanged.

### Expanded defense and its limits

After the international defense integration program is funded and completed, double the initial recruitment ceilings for Rainbow Militias and Liberation Brigades, raise Commune Guard ceilings by 50%, and raise Lotus ceilings from 2/5/10 to 5/10/20 battalion-equivalents. Actual available personnel, equipment, command, anchors, and training still limit the result. Round fractional ceilings down. No existing formation is created a second time.

The program can adopt the terminal wartime replacement modifiers from Part 8, coordinate real volunteer armies, integrate consenting commanders, organize fighter defense and convoy protection, and improve the movement's logistics. It cannot produce armor, ships, aircraft, or advanced technology from ideological enthusiasm alone.

An aggressive campaign can strain the movement's charter. Offensive conscription, indiscriminate repression, and the permanent suppression of lawful opposition undermine its own settlement commitments. Members can demand a congress, refuse optional expeditionary aid, or replace leadership. These consequences come from specific charter violations, not a new hidden purity score.

### Organized opposition's defeat

The rival command is defeated when it no longer operates a coordinated territorial war against the settlement and every surviving signatory has surrendered, dissolved the restoration pact, or signed an enforceable peaceful settlement. Conservative governments and lawful traditionalist parties may survive. Their continued existence does not prevent victory after they renounce the organized coercive restoration campaign and accept the settlement's legal guarantees.

A government cannot evade defeat by changing a cosmetic identity while keeping the same active restoration war, command, and institutions. Conversely, a real negotiated transition can satisfy the objective without annexation or extermination. The world-order victory does not require killing or expelling traditionalists, religious communities, or political opponents.

### The final 180-day settlement mission

Once the territorial, industrial, population, and opposition conditions are met, begin a 180-day ratification mission. Maintain civilian command, protect legal equality and religious freedom, enact the agreed demobilization terms, open reconstruction, and resolve outstanding member obligations. No active member may be conducting an unresolved charter-violating persecution policy while the coalition claims a universal protection settlement.

Full completion establishes victory and its postwar institutions. Partial completion preserves military victory but delays the social-order ending until the specific unresolved term is delivered. A renewed major war or collapse of the governing majority suspends the mission and updates the objectives. It does not erase actual treaties, casualties, or completed reconstruction.

### Victory order

The final order establishes universal LGBTQ legal equality within the settlement, freedom of religion and spiritual practice, guarantees against persecution, civilian political authority, local autonomy, and reduced or abolished offensive conscription. Members choose communal, mixed, or other compatible civilian economic institutions through their own constitutions. No compulsory orientation, family policy, or religion is imposed.

Demobilization is a real process. It returns surviving service personnel where the engine supports that transition, changes military production commitments, and leaves enough defensive capacity to honor the peace guarantees. The movement's celebratory super-event belongs here only after the maintenance mission succeeds.

### Defeat, stalemate, and reversal

The Rainbow International can lose. A collapse of its governing institutions, loss of the population majority, dissolution of the bloc, or military defeat creates a terminal reversal crisis. A temporary fluctuation in the living-population share does not instantly end the scenario. Allow 90 days for a constitutional or military recovery unless the bloc has already irreversibly dissolved or surrendered.

If a traditionalist coalition decisively wins, resolve a restoration-order aftermath with constitutional, coercive, or negotiated outcomes according to its actual political path. Do not label persecution as an automatic successful peace. A compromise world order can preserve legal rights and autonomy under a less movement-dominated settlement. An unresolved stalemate remains a playable divided world with mediation objectives.

Terminal ownership remains latched until the shared scenario framework explicitly supports a safe transition. Do not resume the ordinary random picker on defeat by guessing how other endings clean up. A verified project transition can later return the campaign to an approved ongoing mode, but that behavior requires the shared world-end audit.
