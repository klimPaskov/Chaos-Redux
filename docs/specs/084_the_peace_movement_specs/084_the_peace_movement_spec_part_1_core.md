Numeric tuning, detailed institutions, route conditions, and consequences not stated in that brief are design selections made for this package, rather than claims that the user already supplied those details.

### The central promise

A spiritual, pacifist, queer-liberation counterculture appears in one country and begins changing how its population lives, works, worships, and responds to war. Its first organizers run discussion rooms, concerts, communal kitchens, legal-aid circles, and conscientious-objection campaigns. The government must decide how much public space to concede to people whose demands directly weaken its mobilization plans.

The movement can become a durable part of ordinary society, enter a coalition, replace a government, or be pushed underground. Its supporters disagree about parliamentary politics, sexual freedom, family policy, religion, economic organization, and armed defense. Traditionalist opponents also disagree about negotiation, national independence, religious authority, military rule, and political violence. Those disagreements create choices and recognizable local stories without adding more public meters.

The dramatic arc runs from a local counterculture to a struggle over the world order. That arc is possible rather than compulsory. A player can build a viable civilian republic, prevent a civil war through negotiated reform, preserve a conservative government through a credible settlement, or lead either international bloc into the final conflict. The game must support the quieter outcomes with real institutions, military arrangements, and aftermath objectives.

### The opening

Choose exactly one country from the deduplicated union of eligible major countries and eligible player-controlled countries. A player-controlled major appears once in that pool. Each eligible country begins with equal origin weight. War weariness and urban opportunity affect what happens after selection rather than silently removing peaceful or small player countries from the requested origin pool.

An eligible origin exists, controls inhabited land, has a functioning government, and participates in ordinary human civilian politics. A capitulated government with no controlled inhabited territory cannot host the first public gathering. A player minor remains eligible even if it has only one state. An active incompatible terminal scenario blocks the opening rather than creating an unreachable episode. Subjects may be origins. Their sovereign powers remain limited until an independence route actually changes their status.

Within the selected country, choose an inhabited controlled urban or transport anchor. Prefer a major victory-point settlement, then a port with a civilian hinterland, then a well-connected populous state. University and cultural-hub names require researched local evidence. Victory points and infrastructure are usable gameplay proxies, not proof that a particular university exists. A one-state country uses its existing capital rather than receiving an invented campus or extra territory.

The baseline starts at Peace Movement Influence 15 and Traditionalist Reaction 10. Place one open local cell at the anchor. The first report introduces a specific dispute over public assembly and military service, identifies the government and local organizers, and offers the five response families in Part 3. It does not announce all later evolutions or the world-end route. No army, annexation, government swap, character orientation change, or immediate country-wide social revolution accompanies this opening.

### The two public values

Peace Movement Influence and Traditionalist Reaction both run from 0 to 100. They are political capacity measures, not demographic shares. Influence 70 does not mean that 70 percent of people are LGBTQ, pacifist, anti-natalist, or movement members. Reaction 70 does not mean that 70 percent belong to a particular religion.

Both values remain visible in the active decision category with a concise trend explanation and the next consequential threshold. Neither is calculated as the inverse of the other. A country can have strong support for the movement and strong organized opposition at the same time. Ordinary Stability, War Support, manpower, equipment, factories, and diplomatic relations retain their existing meanings.

Local cells, coalition agreements, military pledges, treaty signatures, scandals, and reform records are discrete situations. They do not become extra public currencies. International cooperation is governed by votes and fulfilled commitments rather than a third cohesion meter. The world-end population condition appears in its eligibility explanation, not as another permanent management bar.

### Campaign phases

| Phase, working label | Entry | What the player does | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| First circles | Opening or a first foreign cell | Choose a government response and deal with the first public dispute | Influence reaches 20, the cell is contained, or a negotiated local agreement succeeds |
| Public movement | Influence 20 or higher | Manage assembly, service alternatives, civic projects, officials, and foreign contacts | A political settlement, a takeover bid, underground suppression, or a polarization crisis |
| National contender | Influence 60 or higher with a functioning organization | Seek a mandate, support or resist strikes, negotiate institutions, decide military loyalty | Coalition, movement government, traditionalist government, or constitutional crisis |
| Divided state | Influence at least 60 and Reaction at least 70 | Resolve a concrete rupture through negotiation, command arrangements, or force | Settlement, government change, or civil war |
| International contest | Eligible governments establish the blocs | Fund commitments, protect borders and refugees, contest foreign influence, prevent or wage bloc wars | Durable coexistence, bloc defeat, or the world-end threshold |
| World-order campaign | The exact world-end requirements are met | Resolve organized opposition, secure major centers, and implement a settlement | Rainbow victory, traditionalist reversal, negotiated global settlement, or movement fragmentation |

These are ordinary campaign states. They do not substitute for the three Chaos-gated evolutions. Elections, civilian construction, legal equality, coalition government, and ordinary military reform remain possible below 200 Chaos.

### A broad coalition with real disagreements

The movement contains parliamentary reformers, cultural radicals, communal organizers, spiritual practitioners, labor pacifists, and defensive militants once armed defense develops. These are route actors, not collectible subfactions with separate support bars. A reform can satisfy one demand and leave another unresolved. Legal equality does not require anti-natalism. A communal government can support parents. A spiritual council can protect people who do not share its beliefs.

Traditionalist opposition can include constitutional conservatives, nationalist officers, religious associations, family-policy campaigners, and authoritarian security organizations. Their cooperation is contingent. A church may oppose the movement's social program while helping prevent police violence. A secular military government can lead the harshest suppression. A religious pacifist can support conscientious objection without joining the Lotus Ascendants. Regional variants must preserve these possibilities.

### Player incentives

Early toleration trades military readiness for civilian advantages and a lower chance of violent backlash. Negotiation costs resources and political flexibility but can remove grievances without removing civil rights. Co-option is cheaper initially and carries a credibility problem when the government breaks promises. Suppression can reduce visible organizing, but a country that cannot enforce its policy accumulates underground opposition and damaging failures. Eradication is an attempt to dismantle the political organization, with high costs and a serious uprising risk, rather than a free button that deletes a population.

Movement rule must solve production, defense, housing, and foreign threats. It cannot live indefinitely on festival rewards. Traditionalist rule must restore functioning institutions, maintain its coalition, and address the grievances that created the movement. Raising Reaction to 100 is not a victory condition. A country with no remaining administrative capacity has not achieved order.

### Resolution belongs to the player loop

A negotiated settlement can end the country's active crisis while retaining legal equality, alternative service, and cultural freedoms. A contained underground organization can remain dormant and return after a genuinely new shock. A movement-led country can finish its founding program and keep its permanent institutions without retaining every temporary crisis penalty. Civil-war victory opens reconstruction rather than instantly removing all social conflict.

The global episode stops spreading when it has no active organizations, eligible sponsors, or unresolved crises for 180 days. That closure does not reverse permanent laws or resurrect dissolved parties. The root event is consumed once for the campaign. Later unrest inside this episode is an internal resurgence, never another timer pick, major-weight reset, or repeatable opening reward.

### Scope of the expansion

The package adds a social and political event system, phased decisions, character incidents, country transformations, dedicated trees for actual event-created governments, additive content for existing countries, four custom military families, international blocs, civil wars, a terminal world-order route, a four-intensity manual scenario, achievements, and the associated asset and research handoffs.

Its identity remains human political conflict. It does not add a literal sexuality contagion, a supernatural reproductive disease, mandatory conversion to one religion, biological transformation, magical resurrection, or an automatic right to annex every foreign state. Lotus belief shapes resolve and organization. It does not make its members invulnerable.
