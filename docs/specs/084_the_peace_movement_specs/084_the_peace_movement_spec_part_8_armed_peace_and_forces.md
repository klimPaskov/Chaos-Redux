# 84 · The Peace Movement
## Part 8 · Armed Peace, formations, equipment, and force development

### Military premise

The movement begins with no special army.
Armed Peace creates institutions capable of training and supporting one.
Units need adults who are available for service, actual weapons, supplies, officers, and time.
Their strengths come from cohesion, local support, training, and a political reason to hold territory.
Their limitations remain visible in heavy equipment, combined arms, distant logistics, and the tension between civilian priorities and prolonged war.

The four requested formations are distinct military families with separate mechanics and complete asset requirements.
Do not implement all four as renamed ordinary infantry.
Conversely, do not create four new equipment technologies merely because their uniforms and organizations differ.
They can consume the country's existing infantry and support equipment while receiving distinct subunit definitions, templates, training rules, counters, models, and supported tactical roles.

All figures below are proposed design values.
Compare them against the installed game's actual baseline subunits, technologies, and unit modifiers before producing code.
Positive organization and recovery are relative to the verified comparable infantry baseline.
The word morale describes willingness to remain in the fight, represented through supported organization, recovery, and contextual effects.
It is not a new public unit meter.

### Formation design table

| Family | Tactical identity | Relative strengths | Relative weaknesses | Typical role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rainbow Militias | Mass volunteers with strong local support | Organization +10%, recovery +15%, defense +15% in movement-held home territory | Breakthrough −25%, hardness and armor unchanged, initial supply discipline below regular cadres | Hold threatened cities and connected fronts, reinforce popular uprisings |
| Commune Guards | Small, accountable territorial formations | Home-territory defense +25%, supply use −10%, local recovery +10% | Breakthrough −35%, movement speed −10%, poor suitability for distant deployment | Defend relief centers, communes, civilian districts, supply nodes, and negotiated neutral zones |
| Lotus Ascendants | Limited disciplined spiritual volunteers | Organization +35%, recovery +30%, suppression resistance through supported territorial systems | Recruitment and training are slow, breakthrough −15%, no supernatural armor or immunity | Hold critical defensive positions and provide a reliable reserve during collapse |
| Liberation Brigades | Professionally trained movement forces | Organization +10%, defense +10%, ordinary support-company access | Initial training time +25%, equipment and logistics requirements of a real field formation | Sustained fronts, combined-arms integration, limited expeditions authorized by the civilian government |

Contextual home-territory bonuses must use a supported scope and a defined territorial predicate.
If the engine cannot target a proposed subunit-context combination directly, the implementation must present an equivalent supported design for approval.
Do not apply a promised local militia bonus to every tank and foreign volunteer in the country as a silent workaround.

Low surrender willingness is expressed through formation organization, recovery, local support, and country surrender behavior only where supported and justified.
Units still suffer casualties, lose equipment, run out of supply, retreat, and can be destroyed.
Their political commitment is not permission to ignore combat mechanics.

### Recruitment and material requirements

| Family | Proposed training block | Personnel and minimum equipment per block | Training time | Initial capacity ceiling |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| Rainbow Militias | One 1,000-person battalion-equivalent | 1,000 available manpower, 100 infantry equipment, 10 support equipment | 60 days | 10, 25, or 50 battalion-equivalents in small, medium, or large recruitment economies |
| Commune Guards | One 500-person territorial battalion-equivalent | 500 manpower, 50 infantry equipment, 10 support equipment | 45 days | 5, 15, or 30, additionally limited by eligible defended anchors |
| Lotus Ascendants | One 500-person order battalion-equivalent | 500 manpower, 75 infantry equipment, 20 support equipment | 120 days | 2, 5, or 10, with only one cohort training at a time |
| Liberation Brigades | One 1,000-person professional battalion-equivalent | 1,000 manpower, 100 infantry equipment, 20 support equipment | 90 days | 10, 25, or 50, constrained by actual officer and logistics capacity |

Use the civilian-capacity bands in Part 3 to select a provisional ceiling, then respect actual manpower and equipment availability.
A state with an inadequate population cannot reach the numerical ceiling merely because it owns factories.
Capacity is an upper bound, not a grant.
The implementing technology and unit audit must reconcile the battalion equivalents with actual engine equipment ratios and training definitions.
Do not reserve material in a decision and then charge it again when the same formation enters the normal deployment queue.

Prefer the native training queue and one validated source of equipment consumption.
When an event cohort needs explicit equipment reservation, its entire reserve, deployment, cancellation, and refund lifecycle must be owned by that cohort.
Refund only material that was never delivered or consumed.
Actual lost or captured equipment remains lost.
Consumed training resources and elapsed commitments are not restored on a failed political mission.

Early templates are modest: a Rainbow formation has six militia battalion-equivalents, a local Guard two or three, and a Lotus contingent two or three.
Liberation Brigades grow toward a nine-battalion-equivalent field division with supported engineering, medical, logistics, reconnaissance, or artillery elements only after the country has the appropriate equipment and knowledge.
A formation name must not imply a full-strength division when the deployment contains only a small detachment.

### Political recruitment conditions

All four special formation families require Armed Peace, except when the manual scenario explicitly initializes their mature institutions.
An earlier emergency mandate supports the existing regular military without unlocking these special families.
Rainbow training additionally requires Influence at least 40, a valid defense mandate, and an open or established clandestine organization with territorial access.
Guards require a real defended anchor and a local charter.
Lotus recruitment requires an actual order relationship, Armed Peace, and an available training cohort.
Brigades require a functioning authority, an officer agreement, and a logistics program.

Militia recruitment can be faster in high-Influence states.
At Influence at least 80, reduce Rainbow training time by 25%, rounded through supported training mechanics.
The personnel and equipment costs remain unchanged.
Do not convert the country's entire population into supporters or deduct volunteers twice from both a private recruitment pool and native manpower.

A government can prefer nonviolent organizing and refuse these recruitment programs.
It then relies on its existing military, foreign guarantees, diplomatic concessions, and lawful resistance.
That path has a harder defensive problem but retains political benefits from consistent nonviolence.
The event does not force a pacifist leader to recruit Lotus formations for a mandatory focus prerequisite.

### From local defense to a professional army

An officer agreement can transfer experienced cadres into movement command without duplicating their previous units.
Converting an existing infantry formation to a Liberation Brigade preserves its actual strength, equipment, experience within supported limits, casualties, and deployment position.
It must complete the professional mandate and any retraining requirement rather than gaining both its old and new full organization bonuses at once.

Captured equipment can equip formations through the game's actual capture and stockpile mechanisms.
Foreign aid is delivered equipment, not a hidden production multiplier.
A sponsor must own the donated equipment and have a valid transport or diplomatic route.
The receiving government chooses between relief, militia expansion, professionalization, and logistics when resources are scarce.

### Wartime economy variants

A valid movement defense mandate and a completed coordination program replace the most severe peacetime service and economy penalties with a wartime defensive variant.
At Influence at least 80, this variant sets military-output penalty to −15%, training-time penalty to +15%, recruitable-factor penalty to −20%, and War Support contribution to −20 percentage points.
Consumer-goods and civilian-construction policy remain based on the chosen economy route and actual wartime restrictions.

At terminal scale, completed international defense integration may further replace those totals with military output −5%, training time +5%, recruitable factor −10%, and War Support −10 percentage points.
These figures are totals, not bonuses added to previous penalties.
The movement remains less naturally suited to total war than a fully militarized opponent.
Its defensive capacity becomes credible enough for the world-order campaign.

Where the republic's completed service totals, this wartime variant, and terminal integration overlap, the [modifier precedence](084_the_peace_movement_modifier_precedence.md) applies the strongest valid recovery once per field.

### Air forces

A movement government can retain an existing air force after an actual command agreement.
Defensive air policy prioritizes fighter interception, air superiority over threatened territory, reconnaissance where supported, airfield defense, and transport for civilian or military supply.
It does not make aircraft mechanically incapable of crossing a border or pretend that the game has a universal defensive-only aircraft flag.

New fighter wings require real aircraft and personnel.
Existing technology is preserved.
An air-defense focus may offer a bounded research bonus to a validated relevant technology, but cannot grant an advanced airframe outside the intended tech progression.
Bomber use is a cabinet and military-policy decision with diplomatic consequences, not a universal ban forced on every movement state.

### Navies and maritime logistics

A coastline, functioning port, and existing or producible ships are prerequisites for a naval force.
Priorities are convoy escort, protected sea passage, evacuation, reconnaissance, and defense of nearby sea zones.
A landlocked movement government can negotiate port access and escort assistance without receiving ships or a coastal state for free.

Fleet allegiance during a civil war is resolved by whole actual ships, valid basing, command loyalty, and a possible negotiated neutrality arrangement.
Do not copy the fleet to both claimants.
A fleet held in a neutral port cannot simultaneously patrol for both sides.
Naval aid and expeditionary forces must respect the installed game's supported mechanics and the donor's current wars.

### Traditionalist military development

Traditionalist states keep their existing military and may recover the event's War Support and production burdens through their chosen political program.
Constitutional conservation emphasizes dependable service agreements and professional readiness.
National discipline restores capacity faster at the cost of coercive institutions and underground opposition.
Religious-national or secular civic compacts can support morale, relief, and command legitimacy without becoming the same military doctrine everywhere.

Their special value is coherent combined arms and established state machinery.
Avoid giving them an identical renamed version of all four movement formations simply to mirror the content count.
Local traditionalist auxiliaries may exist during civil wars, using actual local equipment and the country's ordinary infantry assets unless a separately justified unit family is approved.

### Attrition, casualties, and demobilization

Combat casualties pass through the existing warfare and death-accounting systems.
Do not add a second fictional civilian death for every militia casualty.
A reported massacre is a distinct incident with its own concrete civilian victims, not a multiplier on the unit's battlefield losses.
Demographic policy never removes living recruits retroactively.

After a settlement, Guards can become a limited lawful territorial reserve.
Rainbow formations can demobilize or volunteer for Brigade retraining.
Lotus units can disarm, integrate, or leave under a negotiated agreement.
Return surviving eligible personnel and recoverable equipment only through verified engine mechanisms.
Disbanding the same formation twice must be impossible.
