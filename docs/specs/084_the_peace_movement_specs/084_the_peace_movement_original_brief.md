## Core idea

> A spiritual, pacifist, LGBTQ-liberation movement emerges in a random major country or player-controlled country and begins spreading across borders.

The movement combines antiwar politics, draft refusal, communal living, personal and sexual liberation, LGBTQ rights and public queer culture, freedom of the soul, meditation and spiritual practice, fictional syncretic beliefs drawing inspiration from Hindu and Buddhist imagery, rejection of nationalism and militarism, reduced emphasis on traditional family formation, voluntary childlessness and anti-natalist currents, and civilian welfare over military production.

At first it is a peaceful counterculture movement.

If it grows large enough, it becomes a political force capable of changing governments, weakening war efforts, provoking a powerful traditionalist reaction, and eventually splitting the world into two opposing ideological camps.

## Initial target

The event begins in:

- one random valid major country, or
- one player-controlled country

The first cells prefer large cities, university centers, cultural hubs, ports, and other places where a counterculture can spread quickly.

## Two public values

Each affected country tracks:

- **Peace Movement Influence**
- **Traditionalist Reaction**

### Peace Movement Influence

Influence rises through:

- high casualties
- long wars
- low War Support
- conscription
- repression
- universities and youth movements
- public festivals
- cultural exchange
- neighboring movement countries
- sympathetic officials
- LGBTQ organizations
- spiritual organizations
- legalization and tolerance
- antiwar victories
- demobilization

### Traditionalist Reaction

Reaction rises through:

- rapid movement growth
- queer-liberation policies
- open rejection of traditional family structures
- anti-nationalist agitation
- draft refusal
- weakening military production
- attacks on religious or national symbols
- movement control of government institutions
- armed movement organizations
- foreign movement interference

A country can have both values high at once.

That is the main route toward violent polarization.

## Baseline effects

The movement weakens a country's ability and willingness to wage war while increasing domestic stability and civilian satisfaction.

Effects scale with Peace Movement Influence.

Possible national effects include:

- lower War Support
- lower mobilization speed
- reduced military factory output
- slower division training
- reduced recruitable population factor
- increased consumer-goods demand
- increased Stability
- civilian construction bonuses
- reduced resistance and repression
- improved political tolerance
- reduced willingness to enter offensive wars

The demographic penalty comes from movement policies and social behavior such as:

- voluntary childlessness
- anti-natalist politics
- reduced emphasis on traditional marriage and family formation
- communal lifestyles
- delayed parenthood
- rejection of state population-growth campaigns

LGBTQ liberation is a core part of the movement, but sexual orientation itself is not treated as an inherent statistical penalty.

## Spread

The movement spreads dynamically through:

- shared borders
- migration
- trade
- faction membership
- international media
- universities
- musicians and cultural figures
- spiritual teachers
- LGBTQ organizations
- peace organizations
- foreign volunteers
- refugees
- common wars
- high casualties
- low War Support

A tolerant country spreads the movement faster.

A repressive country can suppress visible activity but can create stronger underground cells.

## Government responses

### Tolerate

The movement can organize openly.

Influence grows faster, but Reaction grows more slowly than under violent repression.

### Negotiate

The government grants selected reforms such as:

- conscientious objection
- partial demobilization
- assembly rights
- anti-discrimination law
- legal recognition of same-sex relationships
- reduced censorship

### Co-opt

The government adopts some movement rhetoric while trying to keep political control.

### Suppress

Police, military, intelligence services, censorship, and arrests are used against organizers.

Successful suppression lowers visible Influence.

Failed suppression creates martyrs and increases polarization.

### Eradicate

A high-cost campaign attempts to destroy the movement entirely.

Failure can trigger an uprising.

## Evolution I: Love and Liberation

- Chaos requirement: `200+`.

LGBTQ liberation and free-love politics become central to the movement.

The movement expands toward:

- same-sex legal equality
- public queer culture
- sexual freedom
- communal living
- voluntary childlessness
- anti-natalism
- nontraditional family structures
- broader spiritual experimentation
- stronger rejection of hierarchy

Movement-dominated countries can adopt new political identities such as:

- Free Soul Republic
- Rainbow Commonwealth
- Gay Republic
- Communal State
- Spiritual Liberation Council

The exact identity depends on the country and route.

Population growth can decline substantially in movement-dominated states because of anti-natalism, voluntary childlessness, and reduced traditional-family formation.

## Evolution II: The Reaction

- Chaos requirement: `400+`.

A powerful international opposition begins forming.

The reaction can include:

- nationalism
- traditional family politics
- military discipline
- religious conservatism
- opposition to LGBTQ liberation
- opposition to pacifism
- fear of demographic decline
- resistance to foreign cultural influence

The form depends on the country.

Christian traditionalists can organize around Christianity, family, nation, and military duty.

Muslim traditionalists can organize around religious law, family structure, public morality, and resistance to foreign social influence.

Other religious and secular traditionalist movements use their own national or cultural language.

Governments can cooperate internationally through:

- intelligence sharing
- activist lists
- border controls
- funding restrictions
- extradition
- propaganda
- joint police operations
- support for traditionalist parties
- military aid during uprisings

Movement-led countries can answer with asylum, funding, propaganda, volunteers, and sanctions.

## Evolution III: Armed Peace

- Chaos requirement: `600+`.

Repeated repression causes parts of the movement to organize armed defense.

### Rainbow Militias

Mass volunteer formations recruited from movement supporters.

They have:

- high morale
- strong defense of movement-controlled territory
- low surrender willingness
- rapid recruitment in high-Influence states
- weak heavy equipment at first

### Commune Guards

Local units defending communes, movement offices, queer districts, temples, spiritual centers, and civilian populations.

### Lotus Ascendants

A fictional syncretic spiritual order influenced by Hindu and Buddhist imagery, reincarnation beliefs, meditation, and spiritual ascension.

They believe that dying while defending the movement can lead to transcendence.

They receive:

- very high organization
- high recovery
- very high morale
- strong resistance to suppression
- limited recruitment

They do not represent Hinduism as a whole.

### Liberation Brigades

More conventional formations created by movement governments.

These allow movement-led states to survive against organized armies.

## Political takeover

The movement can take control of a country through:

- elections
- parliamentary takeover
- leader conversion
- mass strikes
- resignation of the old government
- military refusal to defend the government
- revolution
- civil-war victory

Movement governments are broadly left-aligned, liberationist, pacifist, and socially radical.

They do not need to use one identical ideology label in every country.

## International blocs

Once global polarization becomes strong enough, two major blocs can form.

### Rainbow International

The movement-aligned bloc.

Its goals include:

- protecting movement organizations
- ending persecution
- supporting LGBTQ liberation
- reducing militarization
- spreading liberationist governments
- defending spiritual and communal organizations
- protecting movement-led countries

### Traditionalist International

The opposing bloc.

Its goals include:

- suppressing movement organizations
- restoring military mobilization
- defending traditional family institutions
- defending religious and national institutions
- preventing foreign movement interference
- replacing movement-led governments
- supporting traditionalist uprisings

## Civil wars

A civil war can begin when both Peace Movement Influence and Traditionalist Reaction are high.

Typical triggers include:

- major purge attempts
- government collapse
- military splits
- movement seizure of institutions
- foreign intervention
- assassination of major movement figures
- mass traditionalist uprisings

Movement forces scale from Influence.

Traditionalist forces scale from Reaction, military loyalty, religious institutions, and foreign support.

## World-End Scenario: The Gay World

Requirements:

- global Chaos is above `1000`
- movement-led or movement-dominated countries control more than **50% of the living world population**
- the Rainbow International exists
- organized traditionalist opposition still controls meaningful territory

A super-event announces that the Peace Movement has become the dominant global social order.

The Rainbow International unlocks a final world-order route.

Its objectives include:

- spreading movement governments to remaining major powers
- dismantling the Traditionalist International
- protecting established liberationist states
- ending persecution
- reducing offensive conscription
- establishing a global peace and liberation order

## World-end military development

At world-end scale, the movement overcomes its initial military weakness.

It can unlock:

- larger Rainbow Militias
- trained Liberation Brigades
- expanded Lotus Ascendants
- international volunteer armies
- converted military commanders
- defensive air forces
- defensive navies
- stronger logistics
- movement-controlled military production

The movement remains less naturally suited to conquest than a fully militarized nationalist bloc.

Its strengths are morale, supporter recruitment, resistance, political spread, and internal collapse behind enemy lines.

## World-end victory

The scenario ends when the Rainbow International defeats organized traditionalist opposition and controls the major centers of world power.

The resulting order can establish:

- universal LGBTQ legal equality
- protected freedom of religion and spiritual practice
- large-scale demobilization
- reduced or abolished offensive conscription
- international guarantees against persecution
- widespread communal and civilian economic policy
- continued local autonomy for member states

The title **The Gay World** is the movement's celebratory world-end identity. It does not mean every person in the world changes sexual orientation.

## Manual scenario: War for the Soul

The manual scenario starts the mature ideological conflict immediately.

Setup includes:

- several movement-dominated countries
- several high-Reaction traditionalist countries
- Rainbow International
- Traditionalist International
- movement cells in neutral countries
- divided officials and military leaders
- Rainbow Militias
- Lotus Ascendants
- an immediate international crisis or war

Scenario intensity controls the number of aligned countries, movement Influence, Traditionalist Reaction, militia strength, foreign intervention, and proximity to world-end conditions.
