# 84 · The Peace Movement
## Part 9 · International blocs, civil wars, and settlements

### From cooperation to rival institutions

The Rainbow International and Traditionalist International begin as negotiated congresses, then become formal blocs when eligible countries ratify a charter. A cultural contact is not faction membership. A joint police memorandum is not a declaration of war. Keep these intermediate relationships playable so the world can polarize without instantly replacing every existing alliance.

A founding congress needs three eligible governments including at least one major power or one player country, a willing host, and valid sovereign authority to sign. The Rainbow congress needs movement-led governments or an enforceable movement governing-majority agreement. The traditionalist congress needs The Reaction and governments that have chosen a traditionalist international mandate. High Reaction alone is insufficient.

If a map cannot support three countries, a bilateral compact may operate without claiming to be the full International. It can provide refuge, aid, and guarantees, but does not satisfy the named-bloc requirement for The Gay World. The manual scenario uses a map-valid founding configuration and must disclose when the full setup is structurally impossible.

### Founding charters

| Institution | Core commitments | Debatable commitment | Founding failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rainbow International | Protect member states, provide refuge against persecution, recognize civilian political authority | Armed liberation abroad, joint conscription rules, and the degree of communal economic policy | A refuge-and-guarantee compact survives among consenting members, without full bloc powers |
| Traditionalist International | Protect member governments, oppose coercive foreign political interference, coordinate agreed security commitments | Extradition, domestic repression, moral legislation, and offensive regime-change war | A limited security consultation survives, without automatic military obligations |

The movement bloc does not require every member to adopt anti-natalism or the Lotus order. The traditionalist bloc does not require identical religions or identical family law. Contested treaty clauses are explicit political choices. A member may decline an extradition clause while remaining in a defensive compact, provided the charter permits that reservation.

### Leadership and cohesion without a third meter

Use discrete commitments: ratified charter, paid or unpaid contribution, accepted or declined crisis aid, active reservation, leadership mandate, and notice of withdrawal. Present them as treaty and mission status, not an accumulating public cohesion currency.

A leadership congress compares actual contributions, military protection, diplomatic support, government legitimacy, and ability to host institutions. The founding origin receives no permanent entitlement. A country can decline leadership. A player cannot repeatedly pass leadership between allies to reset contributions or duplicate national rewards.

Two unresolved serious charter violations within 180 days trigger a congress dispute. Members may accept corrective terms, impose a defined suspension of treaty benefits, replace the leader, or leave through a valid diplomatic process. Two honest disagreements over optional policy do not count as treaty violations. A member at war cannot be expelled into an invalid diplomatic state by a flavour event.

### Foreign instruments

| Instrument | Material or political commitment | Effect and risk |
|---|---|---|
| Asylum | 50 PP and a capacity-scaled civilian commitment for 120 days, plus actual transport where needed | Protect a valid threatened organization or character and establish one exile contact, while creating a diplomatic dispute with the requesting state |
| Cultural or legal aid | 25 PP and actual civilian capacity for 90 days | Support one recipient project and one bounded foreign-network contribution, without instantly changing government |
| Equipment aid | A declared stockpile shipment and valid route | Strengthen the recipient's actual training or relief objective, with donor equipment deducted once |
| Volunteer assistance | Existing supported volunteer rules, available units, and a valid conflict | Provide real formations and casualties, never an extra cloned army |
| Funding restriction | A concrete foreign channel and a sovereign authority | Close or weaken that channel, possibly triggering a treaty or economic dispute |
| Intelligence cooperation | An eligible intelligence system or a supported abstract government cooperation route | Improve one case's evidence or reveal a real sponsorship link, not all private movement members |
| Extradition request | A specific adult fictional case or properly grounded public legal case and a valid treaty | A sovereign acceptance, refusal, legal appeal, or asylum dispute, with no automatic mass deportation |
| Sanctions | Existing supported economic or diplomatic restrictions and a declared political demand | Apply a bounded cost to both policy and trade relationships, with an exit when the demand is resolved |
| Mediation | Two willing authorities and a credible guarantor | Open a 180-day settlement mission with real ceasefire, rights, and command terms |
| Regime-change ultimatum | A government with the appropriate charter mandate and an actual strategic conflict | Offer terms, refusal, negotiated compromise, or an explicitly authorized war path through supported diplomacy |

Use no more than three active foreign contact projects per source country. The overall event mission cap remains three visible active missions per country, so a country cannot simultaneously operate three foreign missions and three domestic missions. A new emergency takes precedence by pausing or explicitly canceling a lower-priority project, with its resource treatment shown.

### Civil-war eligibility

A normal civil-war crisis requires Influence at least 60 and Reaction at least 70, a concrete rupture, and a viable opposing authority. Qualifying ruptures include a failed eradication campaign, a major unlawful purge, a real command split, extralegal seizure of government institutions, an assassination of an established leader, or a foreign intervention that fractures authority. Merely reaching the two thresholds opens the danger and bargaining opportunities.

The crisis first offers a 30-day command-and-authority mission where negotiation is still plausible. The player chooses a valid side, mediates, or remains the existing government according to their actual authority. The outcome follows completed objectives and recorded allegiance. It is not a hidden unconditional civil-war timer disguised as a choice.

A one-state country may lack enough valid territory for a meaningful civil war. Use a capital crisis, military refusal, negotiated transfer, failed uprising, or external intervention instead. Do not invent extra states or split an unpartitionable state by unsupported province scripting. A very small country should still have a meaningful political emergency.

### Territorial and force partition

State allegiance draws on established local organizations, actual control, command loyalty, capital and supply geography, and foreign access. Influence and Reaction help determine support but do not alone assign an exact national army percentage. A high-Reaction army may remain loyal to a constitutional government that has settled with a popular movement.

Construct two viable authorities with connected or supply-capable territory where the map permits. A regional stronghold can be separated if it has a valid port or supply arrangement. Avoid arbitrary distant single-state fragments with no political or logistical explanation. Protect actual ongoing foreign fronts from being copied or instantaneously erased.

Allocate each existing formation once. Allocate each ship once. Allocate air equipment and deployed wings without duplication. Split stockpiles, national manpower, command appointments, and current military obligations through a verified native or project-supported method. The civil-war setup report must reconcile the pre-split total with the two post-split totals plus explicitly declared losses, neutral assets, and any separately funded reinforcement.

Insurgents may receive small initial formations only from actual allocated manpower and equipment. Manual scenarios may declare balanced starting grants separately. The ordinary event cannot call a scripted gift an inherited army.

### Foreign involvement and war aims

A sponsor can send aid, volunteers, guarantees, or enter war through valid diplomatic rules. Its support does not automatically annex the beneficiary into its faction or make every neutral country an enemy. A movement government can defend an invaded traditionalist state against an unrelated aggressor while opposing its domestic policies. A traditionalist constitutional government can mediate protection for movement civilians.

War aims include replacing a coercive government, enforcing a ceasefire, protecting territory, restoring an agreed authority, or gaining independence from an overlord. The event should state which aim is in force. A defensive intervention does not secretly grant every intervening state unrestricted annexation goals.

### Civil-war settlement menu

| Settlement | Necessary authority and military position | Political outcome | Material aftermath |
|---|---|---|---|
| Movement victory | Movement side defeats the rival authority | Constitutional convention with a choice of movement institutions | Reunification of actual assets, reconstruction, demobilization or professional integration |
| Traditionalist victory | Traditionalist side defeats the movement authority | Its chosen constitutional, discipline, or institutional compact route | Surviving organizations may become legal opposition, underground cells, or exile networks according to explicit policy |
| Negotiated reunification | Both authorities and relevant guarantors consent | Shared transition, legal guarantees, and an agreed command | Actual armies merge or demobilize once, with disputes over appointments and reconstruction |
| Ceasefire and divided state | Neither side can force a settlement, and a territorial agreement is viable | Two actual authorities with a defined frontier and diplomatic status | No duplication of territory or population, limited border incidents, and a future mediation route |
| Independence settlement | A subject or region has a valid independence basis and consent or victory | A sovereign or autonomous polity with a complete country package | Real territory, institutions, treaty obligations, and a funded reconstruction problem |
| Exile and amnesty | A defeated organization has a sponsor or a willing victor | Exile committee, legal return, or disarmament under supervision | Only surviving people and portable allocated assets move, with actual transport constraints |

### The 180-day aftermath

The first six months after a civil war are an institutional mission, not a victory popup followed by silence. Reopen civilian production, establish one lawful command, decide the status of armed organizations, resolve protected-person cases, and implement the agreed legal settlement. Successful delivery removes temporary civil-war polarization penalties. Partial delivery retains one outstanding issue. Failed delivery can produce strikes, desertion, local resistance, or a leadership crisis, but it does not automatically restart the identical civil war.

A country-level civil war has a 360-day same-cause relapse lock after settlement. A genuinely new external invasion, distinct command coup, or separate catastrophic rupture may create a different conflict, with an explicit causal record. The lock cannot make the state invulnerable, and its expiry does not itself create another uprising.

### Player and multiplayer agency

Present an eligible human player with a clear political-side choice before an avoidable authority transfer or faction commitment. A defeated government may have fewer choices, but the result must follow the actual war rather than a random event selecting an unplayable tag. Preserve the chosen player's country continuity through the project's validated civil-war pattern.

In multiplayer, each affected country owns its domestic choices. A bloc leader cannot click away another human's constitution, sign its extradition policy, or spend its equipment. World broadcasts and global terminal activation occur once, while each player receives the relevant local decision and can act independently within the treaty they actually accepted.
