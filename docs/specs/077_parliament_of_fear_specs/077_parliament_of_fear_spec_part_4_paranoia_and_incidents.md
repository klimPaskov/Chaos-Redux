# Parliament of Fear
## Part 4. Paranoia, incidents, and political pressure

### The public value

Paranoia ranges from 0 to 100. It measures the political pressure to treat uncertain claims as threats and to demand action against suspected people. It does not measure the actual number of conspirators, the population's ideology, or the intelligence service's knowledge.

The opening anchor is 25. Existing instability, a serious military reverse, a recent credible leak, or a badly divided governing establishment can raise the starting value. An ordinary opening is capped at 55. An evolved opening can reach 65 when justified by actual conditions, but the opening protection period still applies.

The tooltip shows the current reasons for pressure and the last meaningful changes. It distinguishes continuing conditions from one-time shocks. A player should be able to see why pressure rose without learning hidden guilt.

### Pressure bands

| Paranoia | Political condition | Expected behavior |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 19 | Recovering | Accusations lose momentum when institutions function and cases are settled |
| 20 to 39 | Uneasy | Ordinary case handling and occasional accusations |
| 40 to 59 | Distrustful | More demands for investigations and stronger reactions to protection |
| 60 to 79 | Accusatory | Broader allegations, pressure on institutional figures, riskier hearings |
| 80 to 89 | Acute | Credible plots and removal demands can dominate the chamber |
| 90 to 99 | Purge warning | A visible warning names the conditions that could produce a Great Purge |
| 100 | Extreme | A Great Purge can begin if its crisis and warning conditions are satisfied |

The bands change available incident types and political reactions. They do not directly reveal the hidden plot state. A government can have high Paranoia with few real enemies, or low Paranoia while a well-organized plot remains hidden.

### Continuing sources

Large blocs that actively obstruct essential crisis business can generate pressure. Merely disagreeing with the government does not. Low Stability matters through visible administrative weakness, disorder, or the inability to settle public disputes. War contributes through recent setbacks, loss of important territory, or command failures, not through the existence of war alone.

Repeatedly protecting the same figure can create pressure when there is new credible evidence or when the government has prevented an agreed inquiry. Repeating an already disproved accusation without new evidence cannot keep producing its full initial effect.

Institutional damage can slow investigations and appointments. Its effect on Paranoia depends on what actually fails. A research penalty is not automatically a weekly fear increase unless the political dispute concerns the research administration or an associated scandal.

### One-time shocks

A verified intelligence leak can produce an immediate shock. A failed arrest, attempted coup, discovered forgery, exposed coercion, or confirmed assassination can also change the situation sharply. The direction depends on the outcome. Exposing a forged plot can calm the country if the government accepts the finding. Suppressing that finding can increase pressure and strengthen the people who fabricated it.

Nearby civil wars can produce a limited regional scare when the affected country has a relevant political connection or a directly exposed border. The same foreign civil war is not counted repeatedly each assessment. Distant unrelated conflicts do not create automatic pressure.

### Assessment and relief

The active country receives a periodic assessment of relevant conditions. There is no unconditional increase. A calm, functioning country with no unresolved credible threat can experience a small decline. A country whose active cases have reached sound findings can decline faster for a limited period.

Ordinary continuing pressure is bounded so that one poor week does not leap across several bands. Major shocks are separate and explicitly reported. The reference tuning in the supporting material gives a weekly continuing range of minus 5 to plus 10, with named one-time shocks outside that range.

The event does not repeatedly roll for disaster while nothing meaningful changes. Incidents use a growing pressure-sensitive hazard, a minimum interval, and an outstanding-case limit. Calm periods remain possible.

### Incident families

| Family | What the player learns | Possible underlying state | Meaningful response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anonymous accusation | A specific person or bloc is named, with a source-quality warning | Honest report, rumor, rival fabrication | Investigate the source, offer provisional protection, or act with acknowledged uncertainty |
| Forged documents | Documents appear to link a target to a threat | Fabrication with a traceable origin | Test provenance and protect evidence from the interested investigator |
| Minister missing | A linked official is unavailable and their whereabouts are uncertain | Detention, flight, concealment, or a precommitted fatal outcome | Trace the person, preserve their post temporarily, or appoint a replacement |
| Officers denounce one another | Competing claims concern actual commands or responsibilities | Rivalry, genuine misconduct, or a real military plot | Separate witnesses and avoid removing both command networks without replacement |
| Secret meeting | A meeting occurred, but its purpose is unclear | Routine coordination, lawful opposition, self-protection, or plot preparation | Seek corroboration before treating attendance as guilt |
| Intercepted correspondence | Communication exists, with limited context | Genuine contact, altered material, mistaken recipient | Verify provenance and distinguish political contact from an operational plot |
| Assassination rumor | A threat is reported against a named or abstract office | Rumor, staged warning, genuine plan | Protect the office while investigating the threat |
| Rival inquiries | Two investigating bodies produce conflicting conclusions | Different evidence, incompetence, or deliberate interference | Authorize an independent review with a real time and political cost |
| Bloc-wide denunciation | An informant accuses an entire group | Some genuine involvement, guilt by association, or mass fabrication | Narrow the case or accept the cost of a broad removal |
| Cleared person accused again | A settled case returns with new claims | Recycled rumor or independently new evidence | Reject duplication, reopen on new grounds, or exploit the claim at a political cost |
| Investigator implicated | An investigator becomes part of a case | Fabricated retaliation, abusive conduct, or genuine participation | Transfer the inquiry without letting the accused investigator adjudicate it |
| Replacement accused | A recent appointee comes under suspicion | New misconduct, an inherited dispute, or Evolution III self-consumption | Apply the same evidence rules used for the original occupant |

These families are reusable content structures. The selected people, blocs, documents, affected offices, and consequences change with the country's situation. The generator must not reuse the same narrative while attaching an unrelated mechanical outcome.

### Frequency without popup overload

At low pressure, routine developments are delivered through the chamber and grouped updates. Important choices use events. At higher pressure, reports become more frequent, but the system does not issue several indistinguishable accusations on the same day.

At most one ordinary actionable incident is presented within the configured minimum interval. Emergency coup or arrest outcomes can interrupt because they resolve a material threat. Cosmetic incident text never changes hidden truth or creates a new timer by itself.

The first 21 days protect against involuntary purge waves and extreme outcomes caused only by initial values. A genuine external attack or an already active native civil war is not erased by that protection. A new Event 077 plot must still meet its own organization and warning requirements.

### Visible warnings

An acute warning names what is at risk, which part of the government is demanding action, and what would reduce the danger. It does not say merely that a random disaster might happen. Before an automatic purge, the player sees the threatened institution and the political demand driving it.

A warning is not a promise that any single button will solve the problem. The player may need to preserve a majority while closing an inquiry and restoring vacancies. The warning should identify those concrete conditions without giving away hidden conspirators.

### Fair uncertainty

The system can mislead through in-world evidence. It cannot mislead through false tooltips about known mechanical consequences. A purge may state that guilt is uncertain, but it must accurately show the office at risk, the expected vacancy range, the immediate Paranoia change, and any known threat of resistance.

Known costs are exact when the player commits. Uncertain outcomes use clearly bounded descriptions and, where the interface supports it, correctly calculated probabilities. Reloading or reopening the interface must not improve the result by rerolling the underlying truth.
