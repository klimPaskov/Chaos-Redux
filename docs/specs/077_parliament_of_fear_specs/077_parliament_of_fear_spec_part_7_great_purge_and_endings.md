# Parliament of Fear
## Part 7. Great Purge, leadership crises, and recovery

### When a Great Purge begins

Paranoia reaching 100 creates eligibility for a Great Purge. The crisis must also have passed its opening protection period and issued an acute warning with an opportunity to respond. The extreme value does not silently kill characters on the same day as the opening event.

The warning identifies the institutions under pressure and the removal demands behind the purge. The player can reduce fear, resolve the relevant case, rebuild support, or accept a political concession. Reducing Paranoia below the extreme threshold before commitment prevents the initial purge wave. After the first wave, subsequent waves have their own containment conditions.

A Great Purge can occur once per crisis instance. Later extreme Paranoia can maintain severe pressure and provoke other prepared threats, but it cannot restart the same multi-wave package indefinitely.

### A bounded multi-institution crisis

The Great Purge has up to three waves over approximately 40 to 60 days. Each wave targets a meaningful combination of political or institutional groups. The targets are drawn from the crisis's actual cases, removal demands, available figures, and governing structure. A country without a relevant institution is not given one solely to provide another victim category.

The overall reference ceiling is 40 influence seats removed by these automatic waves and six named figures lost from relevant roles across the Great Purge. These are maximum safeguards, not guaranteed casualty quotas. Voluntary removals already committed remain in the history and affect which targets and institutions are still available.

Before Evolution I, the purge primarily reaches the political establishment and people linked to it. With Evolution I, external institutions can be included. Evolution II changes the resistance that survives or forms. Evolution III allows the process to turn on loyalists, investigators, and replacements.

Each completed wave lowers immediate Paranoia, usually by about 15, while creating vacancies and institutional damage. That relief can create a window for containment. If the government reaches workable support and reduces Paranoia below the acute range, remaining waves stop. Already committed removals remain real.

### Why previous choices matter

A government that protected competent officials may retain enough administrative and military capacity to contain the purge. A government that filled vacancies with hurried loyalists can have numerical support but weak institutions. A government that left a prepared military network untouched may face resistance during arrests. A government that removed every opposing network may avoid a coup while still suffering severe institutional collapse.

These outcomes arise from the chamber and institutions. The Great Purge does not choose a completely unrelated ending from a universal random list.

### Institutional damage

Damage follows the people and institutions actually affected. Removing a naval adviser does not automatically reduce civilian research. Removing an abstract economic delegation does not delete a specific industrial organization unless a verified character or organization link supports that result.

| Institution | Meaningful damage | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Political administration | Reduced political coordination, slower appointments, weaker crisis action authority | Restore occupied seats, settle cases, and appoint qualified staff |
| Army or naval command | Actual retirement or loss of available commanders, plus bounded temporary command disruption | Reassign surviving officers and complete a qualified staff restoration |
| Research administration | Bounded research-speed impairment, interrupted specialist coordination | Preserve or replace relevant staff and settle the institutional inquiry |
| Industry | Bounded output or efficiency-related impairment appropriate to the actual institution | Restore management and protect working production networks |
| Intelligence | Loss of verified available figures or bounded institutional effectiveness | Rebuild the relevant organization without resetting all intelligence progress |
| Senior civil service | Broad administrative delay and lower Stability from a weakened state | A slower, partly independent restoration process |

Visible impairment is consolidated into a small number of coherent effects. The event uses no more than three simultaneous national-spirit surfaces for crisis administration, institutional damage, and settlement aftermath. Institutional differences appear inside descriptions and contextual case information. They do not become six new public meters.

The reference severe institutional penalties are meaningful 10 to 20 percent effects, with tightly limited combinations and durations. Exact modifier names and supported combinations must be verified before implementation. The package does not claim that every desired concept has a native modifier.

### A government loses its majority

Below 51 dependable seats, actions that require broad crisis authority become harder to authorize. The government must negotiate support, accept a lawful leadership process, restore vacancies, or operate under a clearly explained emergency arrangement.

The event applies this pressure to relevant crisis choices and political consequences. It does not arbitrarily disable every national focus, research project, or military order. A minority government remains able to perform the recovery actions intended to rebuild its authority.

A parliamentary profile can face a confidence motion and a change of cabinet. A presidential profile faces obstruction and its own verified removal rules. A military council can transfer command to another valid leader. A personal dictatorship can face elite withdrawal or an attempted replacement. The profile determines what actually happens.

### Coup attempt

A coup requires an organized plot and practical backing inside the relevant security, military, or governing institution. Its prospects depend on that backing, the government's remaining support, preparation, and the availability of a valid alternative leader or administration.

A successful coup can change leadership or government without creating a new tag. The new leadership inherits the country's wars, damaged institutions, surviving characters, vacancies, and unresolved cases. It does not receive a repaired economy or a new free army.

A failed coup removes or disables actual participants according to the established outcome. It can reduce immediate fear after the threat is shown to be contained, but indiscriminate retaliation can increase later pressure. A claim of a coup that was fabricated remains a fabricated case, even if the government used it to remove opponents.

### Civil war

Civil war requires an organization with a viable territorial network and forces that can actually resist. It can arise from a prepared conspiracy surviving a failed arrest, a failed coup that retains regional control, or a relevant handoff from Random Civil War.

The civil-war side receives an identity derived from the real dispute and participating groups. Its leadership, party identity, flags, controlled states, available technology, military assets, and inherited political content must be coherent. It cannot be a blank country used only to host rebels.

The split conserves existing assets where transfer is intended. A commander, ship, aircraft, equipment stock, or division cannot be copied to both sides. Any separately raised emergency force requires a stated recruitment and equipment source with a bounded country-scaled size.

The parent's crisis closes into an armed-outcome handoff once the split has been committed. Its delayed investigation and appointment callbacks cannot continue acting on people or states that now belong to the other side. Political damage persists on the appropriate successor. A short aftermath record remains available without occupying the original active crisis slot indefinitely.

### Normal stable resolution

The crisis can end when Paranoia is at most 20, governing support is at least 55, at least 80 seats are filled and functioning, and there is no prepared plot, active purge wave, or unresolved essential case. These conditions must remain true for 21 consecutive days.

An essential case is one tied to an active removal demand, a prepared threat, a protected person whose agreed inquiry remains unfinished, or a major contradiction blocking the settlement. A minor uncorroborated lead does not prevent resolution forever. It can be closed as unsubstantiated, with the government accepting that result and its political consequences.

A genuine plot does not need every sympathizer in the country to be arrested. It must lose the organization and practical backing required for its planned action, or enter a binding settlement that the relevant participants can actually carry out.

### Settlement forms

| Settlement | What the player achieved | Persistent result |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence-led settlement | Resolved major claims and preserved a working chamber | Surviving officials remain available, confirmed findings limit recycled accusations |
| Negotiated majority | Secured dependable support through concrete concessions | The agreement and any remaining obligations persist for their stated terms |
| Controlled removal | Disrupted an actual threat with limited targeted losses | Removed participants stay removed, unaffected institutions remain functional |
| Hardline consolidation | Rebuilt control through extensive removal and patronage | Vacancies are repaired, but lost expertise and justified distrust remain |
| Hollow-state recovery | Survived a Great Purge and restored basic function | Substantial recovery work remains after Paranoia falls |
| Peaceful leadership change | Replaced the administration through the relevant institution | The successor continues with the same case history and material damage |
| Armed handoff | A coup or civil-war outcome has been committed | The political crisis ends into the actual government or territorial conflict |

### Recovery after closure

Temporary impairment can recover through qualified replacement and an orderly settlement. The recovery is bounded and visible. It does not require an endless new daily management system after the main chamber closes.

Permanent character losses remain permanent. Living removed figures may be restored only through explicit rehabilitation and valid country support. Institutional penalties cannot stack without limit across repeated Event 077 firings. Repeated crises use the existing damage record and a capped aggregate, with remaining recovery time extended only when new material damage occurs.

### A later Parliament of Fear

After closure, the country can be selected again by a later firing. The new crisis uses the current government, current available people, surviving institutional damage, and a limited memory of earlier findings. It does not reset the country to its first chamber or restore purged people.

A disproved forgery remains disproved. A previously protected survivor may become influential. A hurried appointment network may now be established. These facts change the starting situation without making another crisis inevitable or permanently unwinnable.
