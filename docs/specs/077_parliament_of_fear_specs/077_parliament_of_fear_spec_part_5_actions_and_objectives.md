# Parliament of Fear
## Part 5. Actions, costs, and crisis objectives

### Four action families

The chamber has four primary controls: Investigate a Bloc, Purge the Accused, Protect the Accused, and Pack the Chamber. Selecting a bloc or case changes their available modes. The ordinary view never exposes a separate permanent button for every variant.

Each family has a standard action and a small number of contextual alternatives. A player sees the target, the relevant evidence, the cost, the expected duration, and the known consequences before committing. Irreversible character loss requires a confirmation that names the person and affected role.

Political power represents political effort where appropriate. It is not the only sacrifice. Time, delayed appointments, lost governing support, withdrawal of an office, reduced administrative competence, and concrete policy concessions are central costs. Routine paperwork does not consume arbitrary artillery, manpower, or fuel to make a cost list more varied.

### Investigate a Bloc

**Purpose.** Resolve a specific allegation or inspect a currently obstructive institution. Investigating a bloc without an allegation is possible when a visible political disturbance provides a reason, but repeated empty searches are not a source of free calm.

**Availability.** The selected target exists, belongs to the current crisis, and has a valid case or documented cause for inquiry. The sole investigation commission must be free. A target already under inquiry cannot be investigated again in parallel.

**Standard inquiry.** The government assigns an ordinary commission. The reference anchor is 50 political power and 30 days. Costs and time vary with bloc size, evidence access, institutional damage, and the independence of the available investigators. A standard inquiry may find useful evidence quickly, but it is vulnerable to existing investigative bias.

**Independent review.** A disputed case, conflicting inquiry, or allegation involving investigators can justify a more independent commission. The anchor is 75 political power and 45 days. The government gives up the ability to dictate the finding and accepts the political cost of a published adverse conclusion. It reduces the risk of recycled or coerced evidence being treated as corroboration. It does not guarantee an innocent result.

**Preliminary review.** A country unable to afford the standard inquiry can devote its commission to a slower preliminary review. The anchor is 60 days without an upfront political-power debit. It is limited to a named open case, occupies the commission, and cannot uncover an entire hidden network in one step. Its opportunity cost and slower relief prevent it from replacing every normal inquiry.

**Resolution.** The result can clear seats, support a charge, identify unrelated misconduct, expose fabrication, discover an organized plot, or leave a narrowed unresolved case. A failed commission can increase Paranoia if its failure becomes a public dispute. No useful result is created from an invalid or disappeared target.

**AI intent.** Prefer investigation when evidence is weak, a valuable figure is at risk, or a threatened bloc has significant support. Prefer an independent review when investigators are implicated. Use the preliminary route when resources are low and there is no immediate armed threat.

**Cancellation.** A change of government does not automatically cancel a valid inquiry. If the target or relevant institution ceases to exist, the commission closes with an explicit record. A partial refund applies only to genuinely unspent political effort, according to the common cost contract. Canceling voluntarily after information has been gained cannot recover the full cost and farm evidence.

### Purge the Accused

**Purpose.** Remove a charged or suspected political threat before it can act, while accepting uncertainty and institutional loss. The action is never a generic button to delete any disliked character.

**Availability.** A valid current case must name the target. The country's profile must permit the selected form of removal, or the player must explicitly choose an emergency extra-constitutional route with its additional penalties. Known protected figures require ending their protection first or accepting the stated breach.

**Targeted removal.** The government acts against the implicated seat group and its linked officeholders. The reference political-power anchor is 25. The normal group is about 5 influence seats, adjusted to the case. Immediate Paranoia usually falls by 10, within a limited context-sensitive band. The seats become vacant, useful offices may be lost, and the remaining bloc can withdraw support.

**Institution-wide purge.** An acute bloc-wide case or an established emergency can allow a broader removal. The anchor is 50 political power and approximately 10 to 20 influence seats. Immediate fear falls more sharply, but vacancy, competence, and resistance costs are substantially greater. The action names which actual individuals are included. Abstract seats do not imply that every member has been executed.

**Urgent arrest.** A discovered operational plot can justify immediate action without a long administrative removal process. This mode is available only for a prepared threat, not for a vague rumor. Success disables specific participants. Failure can accelerate a coup or civil war only if that organization already has the required backing.

**Resolution.** Dismissal, suspension, retirement, detention, disappearance, and confirmed death are distinct recorded results. The legal and political process depends on the profile. A purge does not automatically reveal that the people removed were guilty. Later evidence may expose the removal as a mistake.

**AI intent.** Target verified organized threats, preserve essential command during war, and reject broad purges that make the chamber nonfunctional unless an immediate material threat makes the sacrifice necessary. Hardline behavior can accept more risk, but it does not grant the AI hidden knowledge.

**Cancellation.** Confirmation is the last ordinary cancellation point. Once a removal has been committed, reopening the interface cannot reverse it. If a linked person becomes unavailable before commitment, the action is repriced or canceled without substituting another person.

### Protect the Accused

**Purpose.** Preserve a useful or wrongly accused person, a legal opposition bloc, or the credibility of an inquiry. Protection is a political commitment with consequences.

**Availability.** The target has an open allegation or is facing a stated removal demand. A protection cannot be stacked repeatedly on the same case to gain rewards. It can be renewed only when its term ends or materially new evidence changes the case.

**Provisional guarantee.** The government protects the target for a limited period while allowing an inquiry. The reference anchor is 25 political power and an immediate 5 Paranoia increase. The normal term is 45 days. Stronger new evidence, a breached inquiry agreement, or acute fear can increase the pressure cost. The guarantee prevents ordinary removal by Event 077 during its term, but it does not erase a genuine plot or confer immunity from every other game system.

**Public vindication.** A credible finding can justify clearing the target and accepting the report. The government restores eligible office or reputation where possible, closes the allegation, and receives a one-time reduction in pressure. It must acknowledge a wrongful removal when that is what the inquiry established. A dead person receives a corrected record, not a restored character.

**Negotiated protection and support.** A minority government can open a confidence-settlement case even when the prospective partner is not personally accused. Protecting that bloc's lawful political role supplies a valid target for this mode. This ensures that a fully occupied chamber with minority support has a recovery route. A bloc can offer dependable support in exchange for a concrete concession, such as an independent inquiry, restoration of a wrongfully removed officeholder, cabinet representation, or a time-limited limit on emergency removals. The proposal must identify the concession and the seats covered. It is unavailable when the bloc is merely a disguised duplicate of the government or has no capacity to provide the promised support.

**Resolution.** A protected figure may be vindicated, remain uncertain, breach the agreement, or participate in a newly established conspiracy. The case truth is not rerolled. A genuine plot can proceed behind protection if the government ignores credible developments, but the player receives the appropriate clues and warning.

**AI intent.** Protect competent figures with weak charges, constitutional opposition, and blocs needed for a defensible majority. Reconsider protection when independent evidence establishes an operational threat. Do not protect a target merely because a scripted leader personality always selects the same option.

**Cancellation.** Breaking a guarantee has a support and credibility cost. It is explicitly recorded, especially when it provokes resistance. A guarantee ends cleanly when the target is cleared or the relevant office ceases to exist. Its end cannot trigger several identical expiry incidents.

### Pack the Chamber

**Purpose.** Rebuild political influence and administrative capacity after vacancies, suspensions, or a loss of governing support. The common action family uses the lawful or coercive appointment powers available in the current profile.

**Availability.** There are eligible vacancies or an expired support arrangement that can be replaced through a valid institutional process. There is one appointment queue. The action cannot dismiss an occupied opposition seat simply to create a place for a supporter.

**Loyal appointments.** Where appointment is permitted, the government fills approximately 5 to 10 vacant influence seats with reliable supporters. The anchor is 50 political power and 20 days. The resulting political support is real, but repeated rushed patronage increases administrative impairment and leaves the new network vulnerable to later suspicion.

**Qualified replacements.** A slower selection seeks people able to carry out the missing work. The anchor is 50 political power and 40 days. About 5 to 10 seats are restored, with some initially dependable or uncertain rather than permanently loyal. Institutional damage can recover faster. The new officials do not replace lost unique character abilities unless actual suitable characters are available.

**Institutional restoration.** A depleted country can rebuild through a broader appointment, representative, or negotiated process. The reference duration is 60 to 90 days. It has no mandatory upfront political-power payment, but it occupies the appointment queue and concedes control over part of the resulting representation. The player sees the expected support range. A government using this route cannot guarantee that all restored seats will be loyal.

**Resolution.** Seats are filled only through powers the institution actually has. An elected legislature uses its verified replacement or representative process. The event can model the political time required, but it cannot present that process as executive appointment. A closed party or military council may appoint directly, with different legitimacy and competence costs.

**AI intent.** Restore function before chasing an unnecessary supermajority. Prefer qualified replacement during prolonged war or serious institutional damage. Use loyal appointments to stabilize a narrow majority when there is room to absorb the competence cost. Use institutional restoration when resource poverty or legal restrictions make other routes impractical.

**Cancellation.** Unseated candidates do not become real characters. Committed political costs follow the common refund rule. Vacancies filled by an external event are reconciled before the queue resolves, preventing an overfilled chamber or the displacement of a legitimate new holder.

### Objectives and missions

The chamber can show up to two ordinary active objectives and one short emergency warning. Objectives are attached to concrete problems and end as soon as their conditions are met. They are not rewards for waiting out a timer.

| Objective | Typical window | Completion | Failure or expiry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restore working support | 120 days | Secure at least 55 dependable seats without a currently breached agreement | Confidence pressure advances through the government profile |
| Rebuild an underfilled chamber | 180 days | Reach at least 80 functioning filled seats | Continued impairment and a stronger restoration demand, not automatic civil war |
| Resolve a disputed major case | 120 days | Reach a credible finding, execute a valid arrest, or settle the charge with its consequences | The unresolved dispute gains pressure and may change the case's political support |
| Respond to a prepared coup warning | About 20 to 30 days, adjusted to actual readiness | Disrupt the prepared organization, reach a binding settlement, or remove its material backing | The already prepared attempt proceeds |
| Complete a stable recovery | 21 consecutive days | Meet all ending conditions throughout the period | The recovery window resets when a material condition fails |

The final recovery window is a confirmation of already achieved political stability. It is not a substitute for solving the case or rebuilding the chamber. Emergency windows are shorter than ordinary missions because the plot has already reached an operational stage.

### Resource-poor recovery

A country with little political power and impaired support must retain a slow but genuine route to recovery. Preliminary review, negotiated protection, and institutional restoration provide it. They sacrifice time, control of appointments, and political concessions instead of requiring an unavailable currency.

These routes cannot instantly create a majority. They also cannot be blocked solely because the government lacks the majority they are intended to rebuild. Only the more coercive or expansionary actions require that authority.
