# Parliament of Fear
## Part 8. Connections and global Chaos

### Intel Leaked

When the affected country is the target of an actual Intel Leaked development, the chamber receives a one-time shock and a relevant case lead. The lead reflects what was disclosed. It does not automatically identify every named person as a conspirator.

A genuine disclosure can expose an existing plot or official misconduct. Misinterpreted or altered material can support a false accusation. The leak's original truth and known targets remain authoritative. Event 077 adds the political reaction, not a second contradictory version of the leak.

The reference Paranoia shock is 15, reduced for a minor or stale disclosure and increased only for an unusually important new one. The same leak instance cannot keep adding pressure on every country assessment or reopening the interface. Intel Leaked owns its own global Chaos source.

### Random Civil War

A fractured chamber can increase the danger of a Random Civil War development when the same country is selected. The connection uses the actual opposition organization, regional backing, and government weakness already recorded.

Only one system commits the civil-war split. If Event 077 has a prepared armed organization and Event 021 selects the country, they negotiate one outcome through a single handoff. The result cannot create two rebel countries, split the same assets twice, or charge two independent event-specific premiums for the same armed rupture.

A foreign civil war can create a bounded scare only when there is a relevant connection. The domestic system does not create repeated global Chaos simply because a nearby country remains at war.

### Generalissimo

A Generalissimo present in the affected country is represented only through the real character or established event-owned role. The event cannot fabricate a second copy of the figure.

Before Evolution I, the figure can matter as a political representative or governing supporter. Once military institutions become eligible targets, accusations can affect the figure's actual command network. Protection preserves those abilities but may increase pressure when the case remains credible. A purge can weaken command and provoke organized supporters where their backing genuinely exists.

The character's owner controls retirement, death, and transfer. Both systems must agree on one irreversible outcome. A Generalissimo outside the affected country is not automatically imported into the chamber.

### The Offensive

An active military offensive can increase the practical cost of investigating or removing senior commanders. Current military setbacks and a disrupted command structure can create pressure. Starting an offensive is not itself proof that a general is disloyal.

The connection should make postponement, narrow investigation, and provisional protection useful wartime choices. It must not make every military inquiry fail during war or guarantee success when a commander is protected.

The supplied catalog does not identify a confirmed implementation ID for this named connection. The interaction is part of the design, but the exact event identity must be resolved in the live repository before wiring. No numeric ID is assigned by guesswork.

### Native politics and other crises

A native purge, character-removal event, or government transition owns its existing commitments. Event 077 needs a cooperation adapter or a temporary eligibility deferral for the conflicting period. It must not clear another system's state to create room for itself.

A country with several other crises receives the shared burden treatment. Parliament of Fear occupies one active crisis slot, not a separate slot for every investigation or institution. Aftermath-only damage is not an unresolved chamber crisis.

Human political states with unusual Chaos Redux content can participate if their governing establishment remains meaningful. A special tag is not automatically excluded. Countries without an applicable human political establishment require a deliberate profile rather than an invented parliament.

### Global Chaos impact map

Paranoia and global Chaos are separate. Routine fear adjustments, appointments, and political arguments generally change only the domestic crisis. Global Chaos changes when the event causes a concrete destabilizing development or demonstrably contains its own abnormal consequences.

The magnitudes below are starting design bands. Shared framework scale, burden rules, and source-overlap handling remain authoritative. The event-specific ledger tracks attributable positive and negative contributions so that peaceful recovery cannot become a repeatable source of free global calm.

| Milestone | Cause and direction | Starting magnitude | Dynamic factors | Repeat guard and overlap | Containment counterpart |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First meaningful manifestation | A functioning state's political establishment becomes caught in a sustained accusation crisis | +2 | Importance and actual opening disruption can justify a bounded increase | Once per accepted crisis instance, not once per popup or selected target candidate | Stable evidence-led or negotiated closure can reverse part of the attributable amount |
| First sustained governing deadlock | A chamber loses working authority and fails to restore it through a meaningful settlement window | +2 to +5 | Country importance and duration of actual obstruction | Once per instance, not every week below 51 support | Restoring dependable support and function earns a bounded reversal |
| First major external institution disabled | Evolution-enabled removals materially damage military, intelligence, research, or industrial administration | +2 to +5 | Actual severity and strategic importance, not the number of abstract seats | Once per institution group, with a combined instance cap | Qualified restoration reverses only the relevant outstanding event contribution |
| New coercion-created operational plot | Government conduct creates a new prepared organization capable of material action | +2 | Requires the plot to reach preparation with real backing | Once per new organization, with a small total cap, no reward for repeated grievance fluctuations | A binding settlement or disruption of its practical backing can reverse that contribution |
| Great Purge becomes material | The first automatic multi-group purge wave actually removes people or disables institutions | +5 to +10 | Country importance, breadth, and actual impairment | Once per Great Purge, later waves do not repeat the headline source | Surviving and restoring a functioning state can reverse only a bounded share |
| Leadership changes through ordinary legal means | A valid confidence or constitutional process replaces an administration | 0 event premium | Apply other established sources only where they actually trigger | Do not add a second ideology or government-change source | A stable settlement can still close earlier Event 077 contributions |
| Coup or civil war commits | A prepared organization takes control or divides the country | 0 additional generic-war premium | Shared systems own war, ideology, deaths, and other ordinary consequences | One owner for the split, no double count with Event 021 | Event 077 can close its own outstanding political ledger, not reverse the war's generic effects |

A single institutional loss counted in the Great Purge headline must not also claim a full independent institution premium for that same transaction. The ledger chooses the distinct cause or applies only the documented incremental part. It does not add every matching row indiscriminately.

### Catalog coordination

The event keeps its numeric identity and repeatable status. It becomes a Medium member of Domestic Unrest. The supplied catalog also contains an unavailable separate Paranoia concept and other unused conspiracy-related ideas. This design does not silently enable, merge, or reassign those entries.

Future coordination can reuse evidence and institutional concepts only after those entries receive their own design decisions. The present package defines Event 077's behavior on its own terms.
