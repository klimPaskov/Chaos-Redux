# Parliament of Fear
## Part 2. Seats, blocs, characters, and evidence

### What a seat represents

Each of the 100 seats is a unit of political influence. A seat can belong to a party bloc, a coalition partner, a governing faction, an officer group, a regional delegation, or an administrative institution. The same person is never represented as several removable people merely because they hold several offices.

The initial distribution is generated from present conditions, including ruling ideology, opposition popularity, the form of government, known internal factions, military influence, and existing political problems. Ideology popularity is an input. It is not treated as a literal electoral result or as a complete list of factions.

There are normally six to eight visible blocs, with a hard limit of eight. Large parties can contain several factions when current country content supports that distinction. Small opposition groups can share a mixed bloc. A government with few recognized factions still receives meaningful institutional interests, but those interests use neutral descriptions until a country-specific identity has been verified.

Every bloc has a reason to exist, a source of influence, a current relationship to the government, and a possible source of conflict. The generator must be able to explain each displayed bloc from the country's actual situation or from an explicitly abstract institutional category.

### Three separate dimensions

Support, evidence, and occupancy are separate. Combining them into a single loyalty score would produce misleading outcomes.

| Dimension | Possible states | Political meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Occupancy | Filled, temporarily suspended, vacant | Whether the influence seat has a functioning holder |
| Support | Loyal, dependable by agreement, uncertain, opposed | Whether a filled and eligible seat currently supports the administration |
| Case status | None, suspected, accused, cleared | What the public case record says about that seat or its linked representative |

Purged is a removal result shown in the visual transition and case history. Once removal is complete, the seat becomes vacant. A cleared seat can still oppose the government. An accused seat can still support it. A suspected official keeps their office until a specific suspension or removal changes that status.

A dependable seat counts toward governing support while its agreement is valid. It stops counting when the agreement expires or the government breaches a stated concession. Secret sympathy does not count as a public support pledge. Hidden conspirators can initially appear dependable, but their exposed withdrawal or action must have a concrete political effect.

### Majority and chamber function

The denominator remains 100 throughout the crisis. A government needs 51 dependable seats for ordinary control. Vacancies do not lower that threshold. This prevents a government from gaining a stable majority by removing everyone who might vote against it.

A chamber with fewer than 80 filled, functioning seats is visibly underfilled. It can continue operating under strain, but it cannot qualify for a normal stable resolution. The distinction between bare control and safe recovery matters. A government with 51 supporters and many empty seats has bought time, not finished rebuilding.

The stable-resolution target is at least 55 dependable seats and at least 80 filled seats. Support between 51 and 54 gives normal control but leaves little margin for defection. A temporary suspension removes a seat from the filled-and-functioning count until the suspension ends or a replacement is validly seated.

### Dynamic bloc generation

The generator first identifies the current institutional profile. It then assembles the relevant political and institutional groups. Represented opposition receives a greater direct role in pluralist institutions. Closed-party systems place more influence inside the ruling party's factions, administration, security structure, and selected economic or regional interests.

Known country factions take priority over invented institutional labels. The generator must not assume that every country has an independent officer faction, a powerful secret police, or a royal household. A monarchy label requires a verified monarchy. A military council requires evidence of military rule or a suitable explicit country adapter.

Initial support reflects current political authority and stability. A stable government normally starts above the safe recovery threshold. A coalition under strain can begin near the majority threshold. Severe existing instability can produce a minority opening, provided the opening also exposes a real negotiation or restoration route.

The total always remains 100. Reweighting after a major constitutional change preserves vacancies, existing cases, and known character losses. It does not refresh the chamber into an untouched starting distribution.

### Individual figures

Up to 12 notable figures can be linked to the most important seats or case groups. Most seats remain abstract. Named figures are drawn from actual available country characters and verified relevant roles. The presentation may include ministers, advisers, political leaders, generals, admirals, and other supported figures.

A character's visibility in the chamber does not grant permission to remove every role that character holds. The case must state what is at risk. Dismissal from a ministerial post, retirement from active command, detention, disappearance, and confirmed death are different outcomes.

A living dismissed official can later be rehabilitated where the country's systems support it. A confirmed dead character cannot return. A disappeared official can reappear only through a previously established survival result, never through a fresh random roll created at the moment of return.

A person's removal is committed once even when the person belongs to several institutions. The chamber can show multiple consequences, but it cannot count multiple deaths or repeatedly apply the same retirement.

### Truth is persistent

Every serious allegation receives a hidden truth category when it is created. The possible categories are a genuine organized plot, misconduct unrelated to a takeover, a mistaken inference, a deliberately fabricated allegation, or an unresolved report without enough evidence to establish a charge.

Investigation does not reroll that truth. Protection does not turn an innocent person into a pre-existing conspirator. A threatened innocent bloc can later form a new conspiracy under Evolution II, but the new plot has its own start, motives, records, and evidence. The original false accusation remains false.

For an unresolved report, the uncertainty concerns the missing underlying event or incomplete evidence. A later material development can create a new case. It cannot silently revise a settled earlier case to justify the government's choice.

### Evidence and uncertainty

The player sees an evidence description, source quality, corroboration status, and relevant contradictions. These appear inside the selected case, not as additional permanent meters. A single anonymous letter, a confession obtained under pressure, or several reports copied from one informant do not count as independent corroboration.

Good investigations improve the evidence picture. They can establish that a plot exists, clear a figure, expose fabrication, or narrow an unresolved allegation. Failed investigations can leave the case open, expose investigators' bias, or create a misleading public accusation. A false accusation should be traceable to a weak source, fabricated material, coercion, or institutional bias.

The player is allowed to remain uncertain. The system must nevertheless be internally consistent enough that a later disclosure can explain what happened.

### Case capacity and follow-through

The ordinary chamber carries at most three active case files, with one active investigation commission. Additional low-level accusations are grouped into the existing relevant case or kept as an uncommitted lead. They do not create an unlimited stack of timers and popups.

A serious imminent plot can enter as an emergency case even when the ordinary case list is full. That exception must be visible and short-lived. It does not generate an additional investigation slot or a free completed arrest.

Closed cases remain in a short historical record. Recent outcomes influence repeated accusations and prevent the same disproved document from repeatedly generating full Paranoia increases. A genuinely new independent source can reopen a case, with the new reason stated.

### Vacancy and competence

Vacancies weaken governing support and administrative reach. Loyal replacements restore influence, but hurried patronage can reduce competence. Qualified replacements take longer or require compromise with other blocs. Reinstating a living person cleared by a credible inquiry restores their actual available role, not an invented duplicate.

A replacement inherits the responsibilities of a seat, not the innocence or guilt of the previous holder. Evolution III can later target appointees through a new allegation. Appointments made specifically to repair damage are therefore useful without becoming permanently immune.
