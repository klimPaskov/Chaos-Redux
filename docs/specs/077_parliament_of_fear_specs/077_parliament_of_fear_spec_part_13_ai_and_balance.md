# Parliament of Fear
## Part 13. AI behavior and balance targets

### The AI sees the same case

The AI chooses from the evidence and political information available to the player. It cannot query the hidden truth category to select the perfect response. Difficulty settings may alter bounded crisis pressure through the shared framework, but they do not give the AI guilt detection or free appointments.

The AI must use the same target validity, costs, cooldowns, warnings, and irreversible-resolution path as the player. It cannot remove a protected person through a separate event route that ignores the guarantee.

### Decision priorities

First preserve the ability to recover. An underfilled chamber and a lost majority favor restoration or negotiation unless a prepared plot poses an immediate material threat. Weak accusations against essential figures favor investigation or provisional protection. Credible evidence of an operational plot favors narrow disruption and a prepared arrest.

The AI compares the opportunity cost of its investigation commission and appointment queue. It should not start a slow low-value inquiry when a prepared threat requires the same commission. It should not fill every vacancy with hurried loyalists while severe institutional damage is the main cause of failure.

### Government tendencies

| Profile | Typical preference | Accepted risk | Hard constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parliamentary democracy | Credible inquiry, negotiated support, lawful restoration | Slower progress and concessions | No executive deletion of elected opposition through an ordinary purge |
| Presidential democracy | Evidence review, appointment authority, issue-specific cooperation | Continued legislative opposition | Loss of simple support does not itself remove the president |
| One-party congress | Targeted party inquiry and internal support restoration | Narrow personnel removal | Opposition within the party is not automatically a real plot |
| Royal council | Protection, mediation, ministerial replacement | Court rivalry and limited patronage | Constitutional restrictions follow the actual monarchy profile |
| Military council | Preserve active command, isolate prepared networks | Rapid action against verified operational threats | No coup success without practical command or security backing |
| Personal dictatorship | Faster appointments and greater tolerance for coercion | Competence loss and threatened networks | No unlimited purge loop that destroys all recovery paths |
| Transitional council | Coalition settlement and institutional restoration | Shared authority and slower decisions | No invented factions or forces to fill missing political content |

These are preferences, not deterministic personality scripts. A hardline government can choose an independent inquiry when command preservation makes it useful. A democracy can take urgent lawful action against a verified prepared plot.

### Decision scenario matrix

| Situation | Preferred consideration | Choice to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Low Paranoia, strong support, weak accusation | A standard or preliminary inquiry with no escalation | Broad purge for a trivial immediate reduction |
| High Paranoia, narrow majority, accused dependable bloc | Protection plus evidence review or negotiated support | Removing the bloc without calculating the lost majority |
| Underfilled chamber, sufficient cash, serious impairment | Qualified replacements | Endless loyal appointments that intensify the main weakness |
| Underfilled chamber, little political power | Institutional restoration and limited concession | Taking no action because the standard appointment is unaffordable |
| Prepared military plot, credible evidence | Disrupt backing or prepare a targeted arrest | Waiting for a low-priority inquiry to finish without evaluating the warning |
| Valuable general accused during a losing war | Preserve command while testing evidence | Treating every military setback as proof of treason |
| Investigator accused by a corroborated source | Independent review or transfer | Letting the accused investigator decide their own guilt |
| Protected target receives genuinely new evidence | Reassess the guarantee and target's practical backing | Repeated protection without regard to the new case |
| Great Purge wave completed, containment is possible | Restore support and lower acute pressure | Voluntary extra purges solely for a momentary drop |
| Cleared allegation returns without new evidence | Reject duplication and preserve the finding | Paying repeatedly to re-clear the same case |

### Randomness and uncertainty

Randomness belongs in incidents, available case circumstances, imperfect evidence gathering, and contingent political response. It does not replace the country's known facts. The source of a character, a constitutional power, or a military network cannot be chosen randomly when the repository already establishes it.

Investigation success and false-positive risk must be distinct concepts. An inquiry that fails to prove a case does not automatically prove innocence. A government can receive accurate evidence that still leaves an important question unresolved.

The implementation probability audit must test entire sequences, including investigation, protection, accusation renewal, removal, appointment, and closure. Correct percentages on individual buttons do not prove that the complete crisis is fair or recoverable.

### Balance goals

A player who checks evidence, preserves a workable majority, and repairs vacancies should usually be able to contain a baseline crisis without losing major institutions. Poor choices and severe external circumstances should produce harder outcomes for understandable reasons.

Repeated broad purges should be a weak general strategy because they destroy governing support and competence, while creating resistance under the appropriate evolution. Endless protection should also fail when it prevents credible inquiries or shields an operational organization. The intended strong strategy changes with evidence and the state of the chamber.

Low-resource recovery should succeed more slowly and with concessions. It should not be strictly better than well-funded action, and it should not be impossible. An ordinary minority opening must contain at least one reachable route back to workable support.

### Repetition and performance

Several countries can experience separate crises across later firings. The event must not become more expensive to simulate simply because a chamber has many abstract seats. Political changes are resolved by bounded bloc and case groups, while the 100-seat presentation reflects the result.

Ordinary incident cadence remains limited. High pressure can produce more meaningful choices without requiring the player to dismiss repetitive popups every day. The main panel remains the place where the situation is understood.

### What must be demonstrated

The completed implementation needs evidence that a baseline crisis can be contained, that purging everyone does not solve majority control, that resource-poor recovery works, that prepared threats have meaningful counterplay, and that repeated crises do not duplicate characters or stack penalties indefinitely.

The supporting validation matrix defines concrete scenarios. The planning package's arithmetic tests check only its abstract seat and recovery rules. They do not certify Hearts of Iron IV scripting, MCP probability results, GUI rendering, or live gameplay.
