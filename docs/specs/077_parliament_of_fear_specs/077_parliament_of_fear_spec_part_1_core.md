# Parliament of Fear
## Part 1. Core experience and opening

A major country or player-controlled country becomes caught in a political crisis in which accusations begin deciding who can govern. The player faces a chamber whose members have political interests, reputations, and varying reasons to cooperate. Some accusations uncover genuine threats. Others remove people whose competence the country needs.

The central challenge is keeping a working government while deciding which claims deserve action. Purging a dangerous conspirator can save the government. Purging a dependable critic can weaken it. Filling every vacancy with loyal supporters can restore control while leaving the administration unable to carry out its work.

### The player's recurring question

Each case asks whether the government should spend time finding evidence, remove the accused, offer protection, or rebuild the chamber. These choices use one compact interface and four recurring action families. A player should be able to understand the immediate situation without opening several separate national systems.

The chamber is the main visual. It contains 100 abstract seats representing political influence inside the governing establishment. This is a common scale for comparison between countries, not a claim that their historical institutions had 100 members. Parties, committees, ministers, regional interests, and military representatives can all occupy part of that influence.

The three persistent readouts are Paranoia, governing support, and filled seats. Cases, protections, pressure explanations, and institutional damage appear as contextual information. They do not become extra meters that the player must maintain.

### Selection and arrival

Each firing chooses one country from the union of valid major countries and player-controlled countries. A human-controlled major appears once in that pool. It receives no extra chance simply by satisfying both conditions. Countries already in an unresolved Parliament of Fear crisis are excluded. The shared crisis-burden rules still apply to the remaining pool.

The opening is tailored to the country's present political situation. An orderly country begins with a credible but limited accusation and enough functioning support to answer it. An unstable country can begin with several uncertain blocs or an existing confidence problem. High Chaos can make more dangerous forms available, but it does not justify deleting characters before the player has received a warning and a chance to respond.

The first report identifies the institution, the first disputed bloc, the immediate concern, and the four ways of responding. It does not reveal whether the allegation is true. An introductory protected period prevents an opening incident from going straight into an involuntary Great Purge. Ordinary investigations and political consequences remain active during that period.

### A complete play cycle

A case enters the chamber through an accusation, a political scandal, a credible leak, or an institution already under pressure. The player selects the relevant bloc and reviews its visible position. An investigation can distinguish useful evidence from a weak claim. Protection can preserve the bloc while an inquiry continues. Removal reduces immediate fear but creates vacancies and may damage an institution. Appointments restore seats according to the country's actual rules.

Paranoia changes because of these developments and the country's circumstances. It has no unconditional positive drift. A country with dependable support, credible investigations, and no active disturbance can become calmer. A government protecting the same person against new evidence while losing a war can face mounting pressure even if it has a nominal majority.

The crisis resolves when fear is low, governing support is dependable, the chamber can function, and outstanding threats have been dealt with. The surviving government may be the original administration, a negotiated successor, or a government that has survived a failed coup. Ending the crisis does not undo deaths, dismissal records, lost officers, or accumulated institutional damage.

### Government authority and political disagreement

Opposition membership is not evidence of conspiracy. A democratic opposition bloc can vote against policy, demand a minister's resignation, or refuse a confidence agreement without plotting an armed takeover. An officer council can reject a commander without automatically starting a civil war. A genuine conspiracy requires separate evidence and organization.

The chamber adapts its meaning to the government. A parliamentary cabinet depends on legislative confidence. A presidential administration faces committee obstruction, appropriation disputes, and constitutional removal procedures. A royal council controls access, appointments, and elite support. A military council depends on actual command relationships. Part 3 defines these distinctions.

### Pressure, scale, and pacing

The normal crisis is a medium domestic development. Its usual outcome should be a difficult political settlement, an impaired administration, or a contained investigation. Coups, civil wars, and a Great Purge are conditional escalations whose causes the player can identify.

A contained crisis should normally offer several meaningful decisions across roughly four to nine months. This is a balance target, not a forced expiry. A country dealing with a war, a hollow chamber, or a real conspiracy may need longer. A credible early settlement may end sooner. The event does not punish success by inventing a new scandal every time Paranoia falls.

### Outcomes worth pursuing

The player can preserve experienced officials through a credible inquiry, build a workable agreement with political opponents, remove a genuine conspiracy without a broad purge, or rebuild institutions after damaging choices. These outcomes differ in who survives, which blocs support the government, and how much damage remains.

A hardline settlement can also end the immediate crisis. It should carry the staffing losses, patronage, and distrust produced along the way. It does not receive a compensating package of large permanent bonuses simply for having removed many people.

### Direction for presentation

The opening uses an official administrative viewpoint, with the selected institution and country's current leadership made clear. Low-level incidents can expose bureaucratic absurdity through restrained irony. Arrests, disappearances, killings, and civil conflict use factual, serious language. The interface gradually shows records crossing out names, empty seats, restricted files, and investigators turning on one another.

The design labels in this package identify content. Final event prose, options, tooltips, achievement names, and super-event text are written during implementation from the directions in Part 11.
