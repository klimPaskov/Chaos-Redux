# Parliament of Fear
## Part 6. Evolutions and organized resistance

### Evolution timing

The three evolutions unlock at global Chaos 200, 400, and 600. Crossing a threshold makes an evolution eligible. During an ongoing crisis, activation follows the project's evolution timing rules, using the normal 90-day mean-time anchor with justified dynamic adjustments. Activation itself adds no global Chaos.

An event selected in an already evolved world may begin with the relevant behavior available. Its opening still allows a response before an involuntary extreme outcome. Evolutions add persistent capabilities for that crisis. A later fall in global Chaos does not reset investigations, restore victims, or make an active plot disappear.

Each evolution can be independently disabled through the existing framework. Later enabled evolutions retain a coherent chamber-level version even when an earlier optional module is unavailable. Core recovery never depends on enabling an evolution.

### Evolution I. The Purge Spreads

The crisis expands beyond the governing chamber into military command, intelligence, research administration, industry, and the senior civil service. These institutions become eligible targets when an accusation or political disturbance connects them to the chamber. They are not all accused immediately on activation.

Before this evolution, a general or security official who is already a political representative can be involved through that political role. Evolution I permits institution-wide investigations and removals affecting people whose main role lies outside the chamber.

A military inquiry can expose unauthorized contacts, command rivalry, or a genuine officer network. Removing implicated commanders can protect the government while weakening current operations. A scientific inquiry can interrupt research administration without inventing a named scientist or deleting unrelated research slots. An industrial inquiry can disrupt production coordination, appointments, and verified industrial leadership. An intelligence inquiry can compromise an agency or reveal fabricated reporting.

The affected institution must have an available, grounded gameplay consequence. A missing DLC system uses an abstract institutional case and appropriate ordinary modifiers. It does not claim to remove a non-existent operative, agency upgrade, or industrial character.

**Player leverage.** Protect essential specialists under a credible inquiry, remove a narrow group instead of the whole institution, replace staff with qualified people, or accept broader damage to stop a verified threat.

**Ending consequence.** A contained investigation can leave the institution functioning and improve the credibility of the settlement. A broad purge leaves staffing and competence damage that requires recovery after the political crisis ends.

### Evolution II. Conspiracies Within Conspiracies

Threatened blocs can begin organizing actual resistance. The conditions are causal: credible fear of removal, a breached guarantee, repeated wrongful arrests, exclusion from any lawful settlement, or a demonstrated threat to the bloc's essential interests. Ordinary opposition and low popularity are insufficient by themselves.

Each new conspiracy begins with participants, motives, a target, and a source of practical backing. It may seek to stop a purge, remove a minister, replace the leader, release detained members, or control a region. Not every conspiracy aims to seize the whole country.

The formation of a new plot creates new clues. These might include coordinated withdrawal of support, unusual contact between previously separate blocs, a protective military assignment, or a regional network refusing an instruction. Clues can remain ambiguous, but the organization cannot be retroactively invented at the moment an arrest fails.

### Stages of a genuine plot

| Stage | What exists | Player-facing signs | Available political response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grievance network | Shared fear or a dispute, without an operational plan | Coordinated complaints or demands | Negotiate, protect lawful interests, or investigate specific misconduct |
| Organization | Participants agree on a material action | Repeated coordination, resources, or credible testimony | Targeted inquiry, settlement, removal of organizational backing |
| Preparation | A plan has a timetable and practical support | A serious warning with an identifiable threatened institution | Arrest, disrupt the backing, accept concessions, or prepare to resist |
| Attempt | Participants act using available assets | A coup, assassination attempt, mutiny, or regional challenge | Resolve the actual attempt, with state and character consequences |
| Aftermath | Survivors, evidence, and losses are known | Public findings and unresolved participants | Settlement, trials or removals, reconstruction, or continuing civil conflict |

Not all plots pass through every stage. A grievance can dissolve through a credible settlement. An organization can lose the officers or regional support needed to act. Failed concealment can expose preparation early.

### Arrest and escalation

An arrest succeeds or fails against the actual organization and the government's current ability to act. The relevant factors include evidence quality, the target's warning, support inside the security or military institution, and the government's preparation. The outcome is not decided by Paranoia alone.

A failed arrest of a vague suspected bloc cannot spawn a civil war. A failed arrest of an already prepared military and regional organization can bring its attempt forward immediately. The warning must explain that the target has the backing to resist. The player does not need certainty about guilt to be informed of that practical risk.

### Evolution III. Everyone Is Suspect

Accusations can now turn on loyal party factions, trusted ministers, security leaders, previous investigators, and appointees selected to replace earlier victims. Loyalty gives no permanent immunity. It remains politically valuable because it sustains the government, but it no longer guarantees safety from accusation.

The crisis becomes self-consuming when repeated removals weaken the institutions responsible for evaluating the next claim. Rival investigators can denounce each other. The government can face incompatible demands to protect an indispensable official and to prove its resolve by removing that official.

At acute Paranoia, influential actors can demand an automatic removal. At extreme pressure, bounded purge waves can occur without direct approval. The player sees the threatened institution and a response window before the first wave. The system never runs an unlimited character-removal loop.

**Player leverage.** Transfer an inquiry away from an implicated investigator, accept a credible adverse report, preserve a functioning appointment process, or break a harmful removal demand through a political concession. Simply appointing more loyalists cannot make the system permanently safe.

### Rare development. Fabrication network exposed

**Unlock.** At least two related cases contain evidence that can be traced to the same fabricated source or coercive investigation. This development does not require every allegation to be false.

**Clue.** Case records reveal repeated phrasing, a common source, impossible dates, or a contradiction available to the current investigators. These details must be generated from the case's actual evidence state.

**Action.** An independent review can examine the shared source. Protecting the implicated targets during the review carries the normal pressure cost. Removing the fabricator without establishing the wider record can stop new claims but leave existing accusations unresolved.

**Ending.** A published finding clears the affected false cases, permits eligible living figures to return, and reduces fear once. Concealing the finding preserves a useful political weapon while increasing future distrust. It does not retroactively turn the forged cases into genuine plots.

### Rare development. The protected plotter

**Unlock.** A genuinely involved figure receives protection while retaining practical access to their organization.

**Clue.** Independently new evidence or visible coordination appears during the guarantee. The previous protection decision remains reasonable or reckless according to the information available at the time.

**Action.** The government can terminate the guarantee with stated costs, demand a restricted role, proceed with an inquiry, or accept the risk of continued protection. A justified arrest can still fail if the network is prepared.

**Ending.** The plot is disrupted, the figure escapes into a prepared opposition network, or the attempt proceeds. The game does not manufacture guilt solely to punish the player for using protection.

### Rare development. Investigators become the target

**Unlock.** Evolution III is active and an investigating body has accumulated influence, used coercive methods, or become involved in conflicting accusations.

**Clue.** Independent contradictions, a protected witness, or incompatible reports challenge the investigators' own account.

**Action.** Transfer the case to a qualified alternative, accept a temporary loss of investigative capacity, or allow the accused body to retain control at a clearly stated credibility cost.

**Ending.** A limited correction preserves a functioning investigative institution. A broad counter-purge can disable the institution and produce a self-consuming cycle. The next case receives its own evidence, rather than an automatic assumption that every former investigator was guilty.
