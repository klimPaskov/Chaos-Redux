# Parliament of Fear
## Part 3. Government-specific political behavior

### Profile selection

The institution is selected from verified country rules and the current government, with ideology used as a secondary input. A country-specific political system takes priority over a generic profile. A change in government can replace the profile while preserving the crisis history.

The design uses one chamber display across profiles, but the seats represent different kinds of authority. The same four action families receive different legal or political forms. A generic fallback describes a governing establishment without claiming a constitution the country does not have.

### Parliamentary democracy

The chamber represents governing parties, coalition partners, legislative opposition, and relevant institutional interests. Governing support means sufficient confidence to sustain the cabinet. Dependable opposition may support a temporary confidence agreement without joining the ruling party or changing ideology.

Investigation normally uses a committee, authorized inquiry, or judicial referral. Purge the Accused means removal from relevant government offices, party discipline, or a lawful process for a legislative member. The executive cannot instantly dismiss elected opposition members merely by paying political power. An unlawful removal is a visibly separate emergency action with constitutional penalties and resistance risk.

Pack the Chamber fills seats through the applicable institutional process. Cabinet appointments, party replacements, and negotiated changes may be available. Elected legislative vacancies require the appropriate lawful process. The planning abstraction may represent that process through a restoration timetable, but it must not imply that the leader simply appoints new members to a real elected legislature.

Below a majority, the cabinet faces negotiation, a confidence challenge, or resignation. A peaceful successor government can preserve the state and continue the crisis. Civil war requires a separate organized armed faction.

### Presidential democracy

The chamber represents legislative blocs and the wider governing coalition relevant to the crisis. Support governs the administration's ability to secure cooperation. Falling below 51 does not remove the president automatically.

Opposition can obstruct relevant measures, refuse appointments where appropriate, demand hearings, or pursue a constitutionally grounded removal process. A cabinet scandal and a charge against the head of state are distinct cases. A country's impeachment or removal requirements must be verified before being represented as a valid leadership-removal route.

Investigation and protection emphasize inquiries, legal safeguards, public findings, and executive or legislative disputes. Appointment powers follow the actual office. Elected opposition cannot be purged through an ordinary personnel action.

At low support, Event 077 actions requiring broad cooperation become slower or require concessions. Essential inquiry, provisional protection, and lawful restoration remain possible. The event does not shut off the country's entire research, war, or national focus interface because the legislature is divided.

### One-party congress or central committee

The chamber represents factions inside the ruling party, senior administrators, security officials, military representatives, and recognized economic or regional interests. Opposing policy within the ruling party does not automatically imply a rival ideology or foreign conspiracy.

Governing support measures the leadership's control over the governing organization. Investigations use party commissions, inspectorates, or security structures. Purges can remove posts rapidly, but a broad purge disrupts the networks needed to administer the country. Accusations against investigators are especially dangerous once later evolutions become active.

Appointments are easier to authorize than in an elected legislature, but hurried patronage cannot replace lost experience. A party congress can replace leadership without creating a new country. A military or regional conspiracy requires its own support and organization before an armed outcome becomes available.

### Monarchy and royal council

The profile is used only for a verified monarchy. Seats represent court interests, ministers, senior officers, regional elites, and any recognized representative institution relevant to the present government. The ruler's personal supporters and the government's working supporters may overlap without being identical.

Investigations concern access to the ruler, ministerial conduct, succession rumors, court alliances, and administrative authority. Protection can take the form of a public guarantee, continued office, or a mediated hearing. It can preserve a useful minister while increasing suspicion of favoritism.

Appointments depend on the ruler's actual powers. A constitutional monarchy still follows its parliamentary or legal restrictions. A royal council cannot replace an elected chamber's authority merely because a king is present.

An elite split may produce a change of ministry, an attempted regency, a succession dispute, or a coup. A succession dispute requires a valid country-specific basis. The generic adapter must not invent royal relatives or name a pretender without evidence.

### Military regime or officer council

The chamber represents command groups, staff networks, service branches, administrative officers, and civilian partners with meaningful influence. Support is a measure of reliable command and council backing, not a vote by every soldier.

Investigations can involve command responsibility, unauthorized contact, misuse of authority, and suspected coup preparation. Removing an officer who actually holds a command has a stronger consequence than removing an abstract council seat. The system shows that consequence before confirmation.

A council split can remove a leader without immediately dividing the country. A coup needs participants with a credible ability to act. A civil-war faction needs regional organization, controlled forces, and a viable territory. A high-ranking name with no available backing cannot create a free army.

### Personal dictatorship

The chamber represents appointed officials, party organizers, security leaders, ministers, and selected interests. Formal support can be high while officials privately fear removal. This allows a superficially loyal chamber to become dangerous under Evolution III without treating every loyal seat as secretly hostile from the start.

The leader can remove many offices directly. The political cost appears in competence, vacancies, administrative obstruction, threatened networks, and loss of reliable information. The authority to remove a person does not make the removal a good choice.

Protection can preserve useful rivals or encourage favoritism accusations. Fast appointments provide loyal support, but the replacement network can become an object of suspicion. A functioning administration must eventually tolerate verified innocence and resolve contradictory claims.

### Revolutionary or transitional council

The chamber represents the actual coalition that brought the government to power, recognized revolutionary institutions, local delegates, military supporters, and administrative specialists. A recent revolution does not justify assuming the existence of any specific movement, party, or leader.

Disputes focus on mandates, loyalty to the settlement, control of armed formations, and the division between political leadership and administration. A negotiated charter or distribution of responsibilities can create dependable support. It must be represented as a concrete concession, not an instant ideological conversion.

### Regime change during a crisis

A peaceful leadership change keeps the same crisis instance. The chamber is reinterpreted around the successor's legitimate support, with surviving figures, vacancies, confirmed plots, and case records retained. Paranoia can fall when the source of a dispute leaves office, but an organized plot does not vanish simply because the leader changes.

A constitutional transformation requires a valid country rule or a clearly defined crisis outcome. Event 077 does not rewrite constitutions as a generic reward for a majority. A transition can end a confidence problem while leaving a staffing or security problem to resolve.

### Existing national political mechanics

A country already running a native purge or equivalent character-removal system needs a specific ownership agreement. Both systems cannot independently schedule removals of the same figures. The initial integration must either provide a verified cooperation adapter or defer a new Event 077 crisis while the conflicting native phase is active.

Deferral is explained as a compatibility condition and ends when the conflict ends. It is not a permanent ban on that country. Event 077 must not disable a national focus branch, clear native Paranoia, or suppress an existing political event to make room for itself.

### Historical research basis

The difference between a parliamentary confidence crisis and a presidential removal procedure is grounded in institutional rules, not in ideology alone. The UK Parliament's explanation of confidence motions and the US Constitution's separation of impeachment and trial support this distinction. They do not establish the correct adapter for every country or every year. Country-specific details require additional verified references before implementation.
