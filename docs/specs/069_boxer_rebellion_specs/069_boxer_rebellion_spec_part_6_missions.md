# Missions with observable objectives

A mission asks the player to achieve a condition through the map, diplomacy, production, or sustained compliance.
It does not become complete merely because its activation decision was affordable.
Success is evaluated automatically and does not require a second paid completion click.

The ordinary duration anchors are 90 days for a limited local task, 180 days for a military or reconstruction program, and 365 days for a large political settlement.
Emergency relief can use a shorter 30- or 60-day window when the opening situation explains the urgency.
The affected player must see the objective and deadline before the relevant commitment becomes irreversible.

## Mission capacity and ownership

A country normally has no more than three active Event 069 missions.
The coalition cannot assign an invisible fourth mission and allow its deadline to expire off-screen.
Additional proposals remain unaccepted tasks until a participant has capacity or explicitly replaces a commitment.
An active emergency can displace a proposed project, but it cannot silently cancel a paid ongoing project.

Every mission has one owner and one objective record.
Other actors can contribute to that record.
They do not receive independent copies of the same threatened population, depot inventory, repair reward, or settlement receipt.
A parent and child objective may coexist only when they measure genuinely different achievements, such as opening a corridor and subsequently evacuating a population through it.

## Mission catalog

### M01 Safe departure from a threatened site

The owner must evacuate the registered civilian group or isolated force to a valid safe destination, or secure a binding local guarantee that removes the immediate threat.
The route, destination, affected people, and negotiating parties are explicit.
The emergency deadline is normally 30 or 60 days.

Full success records all intended departures or the verified guarantee.
Partial success records the actual evacuated portion and leaves the remainder in the same site record.
Failure records the unresolved siege and any separately established losses through the shared systems.
It does not automatically kill everyone at the site.
Pressure falls only for the threat actually removed.
D25 normally starts this mission, and D04 can support a negotiated guarantee.

### M02 Protect an agreed district

Maintain security and access at a named site for 90 days.
The owner needs the real capacity to protect the site, not simply a stance flag.
Permitted local authorities and protected communities are named in the agreement.

A temporary route interruption pauses the verification period when protection still exists.
Loss of the site or a confirmed breach fails the undertaking.
The result changes the credibility of the responsible government and the specific foreign demand.
It does not improve relations with every country indiscriminately.
D04, D12, D17, D26, and D38 can lead here, but one site has one active protection objective.

### M03 Restore and maintain a corridor

Restore the selected port, railway, or land connection and keep its essential sections usable for 90 days.
Use actual connectivity and access evidence.
The mission can be fulfilled by an agreed alternate route when the recipient and purpose remain the same.

Partial completion restores the working sections without claiming the full route reward.
Failure retains repaired infrastructure and accounts for consumed resources, while ending the promised delivery effect.
A later attempt resumes the unfinished task instead of creating another copy of completed construction.
D08 and D34 are the main entry points.

### M04 End a local armed campaign

Within 180 days, dismantle or disarm the named armed networks and maintain basic civilian protection in the region.
The target list is set when the mission begins and updates only for genuine successor networks, not unrelated uprisings added to make the objective impossible.

Full success requires the strongholds to surrender, lose their ability to operate, or enter an enforceable disarmament agreement.
Partial success reduces local activity and leaves a smaller unresolved network.
A government that wins battles but continues uncontrolled attacks on civilians receives a military success with a political failure, not the full suppression settlement.
D05 and D06 can support this mission.

### M05 Regularize or demobilize auxiliaries

Complete the selected compact within 90 days.
Each participating formation either becomes an agreed regular unit, enters a legal local militia arrangement, surrenders its equipment, or leaves the compact under a documented separation.

The mission records actual unit and equipment ownership.
A government cannot gain the surrendered stockpile while the same force remains fully equipped under another owner.
Partial success leaves identified holdouts.
Failure can create a local mutiny or loss of a recruitment district rather than a compulsory national civil war.
D11, D16, and D19 are principal entry points.

### M06 Sustain an equipped military core

For 180 days, maintain a selected core of formations with adequate equipment, supply, and a functioning source of replacements.
The required force size scales with the Boxer country's actual capacity and is locked at the commitment date.
A tiny starting state is not asked to field a major power's army.

Success unlocks the relevant professional command or arsenal branch milestone.
Partial success can complete the industrial work while leaving the army training milestone unfinished.
Failure leaves existing factories, units, and equipment intact but prevents the institutional upgrade.
D14, D16, and D22 support this mission.

### M07 Remove a named stronghold threat

Within 180 days, remove the specified stronghold's ability to threaten the coalition or government objective.
This can happen through actual capture, an enforced withdrawal, or a verified surrender agreement.
The briefing states which of those solutions is acceptable.

The mission does not require annexing all territory held by the same country.
A stronghold that changes owner remains the same objective until its military role is resolved.
If another actor resolves it, contributors receive only their documented contribution outcome.
No participant receives a duplicate depot or territorial award.
D15 and D35 normally start it.

### M08 Establish a viable expedition

Within 180 days, deploy the committed force to a legal theater position and maintain a working supply route.
An expedition is established only when the engine's actual unit ownership, location, access, and supply conditions support it.

A diplomatic signature or a paid transport allocation is insufficient.
Partial success can establish a smaller operation with a narrower mandate.
Failure returns only unused allocations and creates a withdrawal, revision, or loss-of-confidence decision.
D28 starts this mission.

### M09 Complete a joint Chinese operation

Within 180 days, participating Chinese actors fulfill distinct tasks toward one anti-intervention objective.
One may secure the approach, another maintain the supply route, and another provide the main combat force.
The tasks must be useful to the same objective and legally possible under their current relationships.

Full success requires the objective and the accepted contributions.
A government cannot claim credit for merely joining after the result is decided.
Partial success can keep the pact alive while renegotiating a failed contribution.
Failure creates a concrete dispute over command, supplies, or territorial control.
It does not automatically restart every former Chinese war.
D23 and Evolution III open this mission.

### M10 Build a functioning regional council

Within 90 days, assemble the required participating societies, secure contact among them, and agree on command and civilian policy.
The delegates are institutions or properly sourced characters, not invented historical people.

Success changes the movement's actual administration, recruitment permissions, and dispute-resolution actions.
A congress held while every route is blocked or every society refuses the agreement does not succeed.
Partial success creates a narrower regional council.
Failure leaves the local organizations alive and makes centralization more difficult until the relevant disputes are addressed.
D18 starts this mission.

### M11 Carry out a negotiated protocol

Meet the signatories' accepted security, payment, withdrawal, and privilege terms over a period normally between 180 and 365 days.
The mission displays current obligations and due dates using normal task and resource information.
It does not introduce a new Treaty Compliance meter.

Terms are evaluated individually.
A failed payment is not automatically equivalent to a renewed attack on civilians.
Partial compliance can lead to a moratorium, amendment, narrowed guarantee, or dispute.
The mission closes only the event-owned obligations that have actually ended.
D39, D40, D41, D42, and D44 interact with this mission.

### M12 Keep an intervention mandate limited

For 90 days, keep the agreed objective and prevent unauthorized expansion by the members who accepted the revised mandate.
The mission begins only when the relevant parties have committed to the revision.

Success is measured by actual conduct and withdrawal planning.
A member that refuses the restriction must leave or be excluded from the limited agreement.
The mission cannot magically prevent that country from pursuing its own war outside the coalition.
Failure exposes the breach and gives other members a clear exit or opposition choice.
D32 is the normal entry point.

### M13 Complete an orderly withdrawal

Within 90 days, remove the relevant forces and event-owned allocations from the agreed locations, or lawfully transfer them under the settlement.
The destination and access route must be valid.

Partial withdrawal reduces the outstanding commitment and preserves the remaining force record.
A blocked route can justify an agreed extension.
The mission cannot remove units from an unrelated front or transfer a navy simply to make the counter reach zero.
D31 and D37 use this mission.

### M14 Restore a damaged community

Over 180 days, repair identified transport and service damage and support recovery through the existing famine and migration systems.
The objective uses the actual damage and crisis records at activation.

Success requires restored function, not a generic stability purchase.
Partial success preserves completed repairs and delivered relief.
Failure leaves documented unmet needs for later shared recovery work.
The project cannot generate new factories by repeatedly rebuilding a state that was never damaged.
D10, D22, and D43 can contribute according to their specific roles.

### M15 Integrate a cooperating region

Over 180 days, implement the agreed transfer or union, establish administration, resolve the participating force arrangements, and maintain the required local cooperation.
The region must meet the reviewed homeland and political eligibility rules.

A military occupation alone does not satisfy negotiated integration.
A subject or partner counts only under the accepted agreement, not because it belongs to the same faction.
Partial success can preserve an autonomous association.
Failure leaves a dispute or a separate government rather than granting permanent cores anyway.
D45 starts this mission.

### M16 Test and contain a claimed anomaly

Over 90 days, investigate a specific Evolution II phenomenon through repeated observations and controlled operational use within the game's abstraction.
The mission is about whether the recorded effect is reproducible and manageable.
It does not provide technical instructions for real-world rituals or weapons.

Success reveals the supported school behavior and its limits.
A psychological result can still improve discipline without establishing supernatural protection.
A genuine anomaly remains bounded by the observed conditions.
Failure or contradictory evidence prevents stronger claims and can create an internal political dispute.
No outcome restores dead people or grants general chemical immunity.

### M17 Preserve a postwar compact

For 365 days, maintain the agreed civil and military settlement without renewed event-owned attacks, unlawful reoccupation, or broken incorporation terms.
The relevant obligations must be within the owner's actual control.
A third party's unrelated war cannot automatically invalidate a compliant actor's record.

Success establishes a durable political outcome and can complete a difficult achievement.
A limited breach triggers a repair opportunity when credible.
A deliberate or repeated breach ends the compact and can activate the bounded aftermath resurgence.
D17, D39, and the political focus routes can start it.

### M18 Secure recognition without dependence

Within 180 days, obtain recognition or a settlement from the relevant counterpart while preserving independent control over the institutions named in the route.
The route supplies tools for negotiation, guarantees, and limited trade.
It does not require one predetermined foreign country to choose one particular focus.

Success unlocks the relevant independent diplomacy capstone.
An alternative valid counterpart or a changed campaign-state settlement can satisfy the same purpose.
Failure can leave continued isolation or a separately chosen dependence agreement.
It cannot silently impose a puppet relationship as a generic fallback.

## Objective integrity

Every mission records its starting target, public goal, required evidence, current owner, contributors, reward receipt, cancellation behavior, and cleanup owner.
Reopening a category or changing the displayed target must not reset the deadline.
Retaking the same stronghold does not create another first-victory reward.
A mission that is no longer meaningful closes with an honest obsolete or superseded result, not a fabricated success.
