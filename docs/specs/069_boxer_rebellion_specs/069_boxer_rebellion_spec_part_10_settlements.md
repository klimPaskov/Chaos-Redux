# Victory, settlement, and lasting consequences

The crisis ends through particular political and military results.
The settlement should make sense for the actors that actually fought, protected people, occupied territory, or negotiated.
The same generic treaty must not appear after a small local compromise and a major intervention campaign.

## Outcome families

| Outcome | Required proof | Immediate result | Continuing play |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local containment | Armed networks dismantled or integrated and local protection maintained | The region leaves active crisis status | Reconstruction and a limited verification period |
| Negotiated accommodation | A government and participating societies honor a compact | Legal local institutions or auxiliaries survive | Command, civilian protection, and later regularization choices |
| Limited foreign success | The participant completes its declared rescue or protection task | That mandate ends | Orderly withdrawal or a new explicitly accepted agreement |
| Foreign suppression victory | The targeted armed organization loses the ability to continue and the responsible government accepts enforceable terms | Disarmament and a tailored protocol | Payments, inspections, temporary occupation, and treaty revision |
| Boxer survival | The movement preserves functioning institutions despite the intervention | A legal auxiliary institution or independent government remains | Military and political consolidation |
| Foreign withdrawal | The relevant powers remove the forces or privileges at issue | The named foreign presence ends | Disputes over recovered territory and the postwar Chinese order |
| Boxer political dominance | A viable Boxer government earns broad authority and sustains it | A durable national political route | Reunification, modernization, diplomacy, and institutional reform |
| Mixed or fragmented settlement | Different regions and participants reach different enforceable results | Some mandates close while others continue | Local disputes, separate treaties, and limited unresolved operations |

A country may have achieved its objective without winning the entire theater.
A coalition can complete an evacuation while failing to impose reparations.
A Chinese government can suppress attacks and still refuse new foreign privileges.
The outcome record must preserve those distinctions.

## Negotiation inputs

A settlement evaluates control of the relevant sites, the ability to continue the operation, completed mandates, actual losses, civilian protection, government compliance, supply, and the preferences of each participant.
An arbitrary national score must not replace the named issues.

The conference starts with the claims and commitments already made during the crisis.
Members cannot invent an unlimited list of new demands after the last stronghold falls.
A demand for a stronger outcome requires credible leverage and creates a new disagreement when it exceeds the accepted mandate.

The Chinese recipient can accept the whole package, accept separable terms, propose a narrower package, or continue the conflict where that is a real option.
The implementation must not present acceptance as optional while applying the terms in every response branch.

## Settlement terms

### Suppression and disarmament

The term names the organizations or districts concerned and the required form of disarmament, regularization, or separation.
It can preserve peaceful local societies while ending their independent armed role.
It does not require deleting an entire culture, religion, or civilian population.
M04 and M05 provide verification.

### Civilian and institutional protection

The term identifies protected people and sites, the responsible authorities, and the duration of the guarantee.
It may involve Chinese protection, agreed observers, or a limited foreign security presence.
The agreement grants only the access actually required by that role.
M02 and M17 provide verification.

### Reparations

A reparation claim has a verified basis, a fixed principal or bounded resource obligation, named recipients, a capacity-limited payment schedule, and a final expiry or resolution date.
It is not an infinite drain with compounding interest.

The proposed ordinary term lasts 180 days, with a larger punitive term up to 365 days.
The due payment is limited by actual available civilian capacity or a supported resource-transfer system.
The implementation must distinguish an engine-supported transfer from a domestic economic penalty.
A consumer-goods burden alone does not prove that the foreign recipient received payment.

A moratorium can defer payments when actual capacity collapses.
Extensions are limited, with a proposed maximum total extension of 180 days and no repeated reset of the entire treaty.
Unpaid obligations after that limit become a negotiated amendment, a documented default, or a new political dispute.
They do not silently continue forever.

The player sees current due resources, the next deadline, and the remaining term as ordinary contract information.
There is no separate Reparation Pressure or Debt meter.
D40 and D41 use one receipt per installment.

### Temporary occupation

Occupation is limited to named sites, states, or corridors justified by the operation.
The agreement defines which power controls each place, the purpose of that control, what permits departure, and the outer term.
It does not transfer ownership merely because a temporary garrison remains.

A normal occupation review occurs after 180 days, with longer terms requiring a specific settlement reason.
The return condition follows the actual original owner, controller, and accepted political arrangement.
A new Chinese government is not automatically required to return territory to a country that no longer exists.
Succession must be resolved through the treaty and current country state.

### Demilitarized areas and inspections

A demilitarized term names the relevant area, permitted local security, excluded military presence, and an observer process.
Its enforcement must use a verified current engine representation.
Do not mark a flag as a successful demilitarized zone while the prohibited units remain freely deployed there without any consequence.
If the necessary behavior cannot be implemented safely, the term remains blocked rather than being replaced with unrelated national defense penalties.

### Concessions and privileges

Restoration returns a specific previously existing privilege when the actual agreement calls for it.
Expansion creates a separately negotiated or imposed new privilege.
Neither grants a broad right to annex Chinese territory.

A substate district, investment right, mission, or railway privilege must use the corresponding supported institutional representation.
Do not transfer an entire large state to represent a small foreign district.
Historical treaty-port names do not prove current foreign ownership.

### Military access

Access is tied to a named purpose, recipient, theater, and expiry.
It ends when the relevant commitment ends unless the parties sign a separate continuing agreement.
The implementation must verify whether the engine can express the intended limit.
A global unlimited access grant is not an acceptable substitute for a narrowly described corridor arrangement.

## Boxer victory and the postwar order

Driving foreign forces out does not automatically reunify China.
The participant controlling a recovered site must honor the prior territorial agreement or openly contest it.
A coalition of Chinese governments can preserve a limited defense compact, negotiate integration, or return to separate administrations.

A surviving Boxer movement can remain an auxiliary institution.
A Boxer government can pursue the full country tree.
A Chinese government that used Boxer support can retain its own permanent anti-foreign route through control of bases, concession revision, limited trade, and the regulation of its auxiliaries.
Those routes do not require automatic war with every foreign country.

A victory achieved while protecting civilians and honoring agreements creates stronger prospects for a stable political settlement.
A victory accompanied by uncontrolled armed societies leaves a serious command problem.
The game should not award the same postwar legitimacy and diplomatic benefits to both cases.

## Why harsh terms can worsen the problem

Heavy occupation, new territorial privileges, abusive conduct, and excessive demands can reinforce the grievances that sustained the movement.
This connection follows the actual affected regions and responsible parties.
It does not apply a permanent national penalty to every Chinese government regardless of its role.

Hostility can appear through refusal to cooperate, new recruitment opportunities, attacks on an unresolved occupation network, political pressure on a signatory, or renewed competition among societies.
The responsible government receives options to revise a term, protect communities, replace an abusive administration, or enforce the agreement.
The foreign signatories can narrow their demands or maintain them at a growing political and military cost.

Do not make cruelty an efficient route to farming a stronger friendly Boxer army.
Civilian harm and abusive occupation have real political, military, and shared humanitarian consequences.
Any temporary recruitment benefit is bounded and cannot outweigh a repeated cycle of deliberate harm and free rewards.

## Bounded resurgence

The aftermath can produce one coordinated Event 069 resurgence when the original settlement creates a continuing, concrete grievance and a surviving network can exploit it.
This is a continuation of the same crisis record, not a second natural source firing.
It uses remaining institutions and real resources.
It does not recreate the founding army, reset all cooldowns, or pay the opening rewards again.

Later unrelated unrest remains available to the project's shared domestic crisis systems.
Event 069 can leave a dated regional hostility or unresolved-treaty record for those systems to consider.
It must not run an endless private cycle of identical rebellions under a fire-once catalog entry.

## Reconstruction and reconciliation

Reconstruction targets actual damage and current needs.
Relief, migration, disease, contamination, and casualties remain under their shared owners.
A government can rebuild a district, regularize a surviving militia, restore a trade route, and negotiate a more workable foreign agreement.
M14 and M17 provide meaningful postwar objectives.

Political reconciliation does not require a scripted declaration that everyone now agrees.
A functioning compact can tolerate continued disagreement while preventing renewed armed conflict and protecting the accepted rights of its participants.
Its success is shown by institutions, services, and completed obligations.

## Closing the crisis

The active crisis closes when no event-owned armed or diplomatic emergency remains and the remaining obligations have independent, bounded lifecycles.
The event releases temporary allocations, removes obsolete decisions, resolves actor references, and preserves the historical outcome.
A country and focus route that legitimately survived remain active.

When an unrelated terminal world condition occurs, the event stops creating incompatible new incidents and safely closes or suspends its remaining temporary tasks according to the shared terminal-state contract.
It does not erase the terminal condition or launch a fresh intervention through an old scheduled event.
