# The Boxer movement in play

The movement grows through local organization, material support, political opportunity, and the results of actual confrontations.
Its members do not all obey one national commander at the opening.
A government that sponsors one group gains a relationship with that group and its connected network, not ownership of every Boxer in China.

## Local development

A participating state can contain an unarmed circle, an armed society, a contested local authority, a stronghold, or a dismantled network.
These are qualitative states with specific consequences.
They are not additional public meters.

An unarmed circle can organize meetings, distribute propaganda, recruit supporters, and shelter activists.
It creates a warning and a political problem, but does not immediately disable the state's industry.
A government can investigate, negotiate, or protect threatened institutions before armed conflict begins.

An armed society needs a plausible source of weapons and recruits.
Sources include diverted local stocks, sponsored deliveries, captured equipment that actually changes hands, and a compatible Black Market transaction.
A failed delivery must not count as both lost equipment for the sender and a successful grant to the recipient.

A contested local authority appears when officials cannot enforce their decisions and societies can maintain an armed presence.
Its effects should concentrate on security, transport reliability, recruitment, and selected facilities.
It should not apply a flat penalty to every industry in the country because one district is contested.

A stronghold needs actual control or a supported local seizure mechanic, an available population, and a supply source.
It becomes a map objective.
Destroying or negotiating the surrender of that stronghold removes its sustained contribution to Boxer Strength.

## Organization and government control

The government stance defines permissions and obligations.
It does not erase local differences.

Suppression orders security operations against armed organizations and offers a route to negotiated dispersal.
Foreign governments can recognize a credible suppression effort without demanding immediate eradication of every sympathizer.
A government that arrests leaders while allowing attacks to continue does not automatically satisfy its undertaking.

Toleration lets local societies recruit and organize without formal recognition.
The government can issue private restrictions, deny responsibility, or selectively protect certain sites.
Those restrictions require credible enforcement.
Repeated violations expose the gap between official denials and local behavior.

Open support creates a public compact.
The sponsor commits equipment, training, command liaison, and a policy toward civilians and foreign institutions.
Affiliated formations can cooperate with the government through a verified military relationship.
Independent societies can reject that compact and remain a separate problem.

Policy reversal has consequences tied to prior commitments.
A government which has supplied rifles cannot make them disappear by changing its stance.
Withdrawal of support opens a disarmament or regularization process, with surrendered equipment accounted for once.

## Recruitment and forces

Recruitment draws from eligible local manpower and an explicit equipment budget.
Use the installed template definitions to calculate the real bill for each formation.
The initial militia template should be small and readable, with a proposed five infantry-battalion structure and no automatic support-company stack.
Final manpower and equipment quantities must come from the verified installed definitions.

The movement can raise a formation only when it has the required local recruits, equipment, training time, and a safe deployment location.
Lack of any requirement produces a blocked action or a delayed training project, not a partially hidden free unit.
Strength affects which organizations can coordinate recruitment and the scale they can administer.
It does not replace the material bill.

Use normal infantry, cavalry, and other existing battalion types when their role fits.
Militia weakness can come from training, equipment shortages, command arrangements, and logistics.
A new battalion type is not required merely to put a Boxer name on a formation.
A separate unit-art and technology review becomes mandatory if implementation proposes a genuinely new subunit.

Each recruitment site has a recoverable local capacity based on population, prior recruitment, losses, and supply.
A state cannot be conquered, released, and recruited from repeatedly to create additional manpower.
Recruits transferred between a sponsor and a Boxer government are removed from one accounting pool before entering another.

## Incident families

Every incident needs a valid location, an actor or unresolved responsibility state, a threatened object, a public warning where intervention is possible, and a resolution record.
An incident does not need a separate popup for every participating country.

| Incident family | What creates it | Player response | Possible resolution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Threatened mission or civilian district | An armed society and a verified threatened institution | Protection, evacuation, negotiation, or disarmament | Safe departure, local guarantee, continuing siege, or recorded civilian harm |
| Railway disruption | A real transport segment inside an active region | Repair, patrol, alternate route, or agreement | Reopened route, rerouted traffic, or prolonged isolation |
| Isolated garrison | A real or explicitly registered force separated from support | Relief, negotiated withdrawal, reinforcement, or surrender | Evacuation, restored access, captured stores, or combat losses |
| Attacks on collaborating officials | A disputed Chinese authority with local opponents | Guard officials, investigate abuses, replace compromised administrators | Restored authority, negotiated local government, or a stronger rival administration |
| Rival societies | Competing sponsors, incompatible ritual schools, or disputed command | Mediation, common command, selective recognition, or separation | A stable compact, a limited mutiny, or a fragmented network |
| Seized depot | A contested site with actual stored or allocated equipment | Defend, recover, evacuate stocks, or negotiate surrender | A finite equipment transfer or loss |
| Rumored foreign atrocity | An attributable incident or a report not yet verified | Investigation, access for observers, protection, or denial | Confirmed responsibility, corrected report, or unresolved distrust |
| Public challenge to an official | A society with political support and a specific demand | Hear the petition, refuse, bargain, or enforce the law | A political settlement or escalation into armed defiance |

Do not create a repeatable player action whose main reward is killing civilians.
Violence against civilians can occur as an adverse development of the movement or occupation, but it should damage political control, increase international pressure, and create protection and accountability decisions.
Its costs cannot be washed away by gaining a small amount of Strength.

## Sieges and relief

A siege is a site-based mission involving a registered threatened population or garrison, a surrounding hostile presence, and an actual route problem.
A low-supply province alone is insufficient.
The initial briefing identifies the site, who can relieve it, who can negotiate, and the immediate deadline.

An emergency relief window can be 30 or 60 days when the situation genuinely warrants a short deadline.
Longer military and reconstruction objectives use the mission durations in Part 6.
If the campaign already contains a siege at opening, its remaining time must leave an actionable response unless the scenario explicitly starts with a nearly lost position.

Relief can succeed through secure evacuation, a negotiated guarantee, or physical restoration of a route.
It does not always require taking the surrounding state.
Partial success records the population or force actually removed and leaves only the unresolved remainder at risk.

The shared famine, relief, migration, contamination, and casualty systems own their respective consequences.
The Boxer system supplies the cause, affected site, responsible actors, and duration.
It must not maintain duplicate food, refugee, or death meters.
A person moved by evacuation is not also counted as killed or newly recruited.

## Logistics and sabotage

Damage should affect the targeted asset or connection.
A railway incident can interrupt a route, delay a delivery, or damage the relevant building where the installed engine supports the effect.
It does not delete every train in the country.
A depot seizure transfers only the stock assigned to that site or the verified share permitted by the project's material accounting.

Use explicit capability checks for effects that appear geographically precise.
If the engine cannot assign a portion of a national stockpile to a site, the implementation must establish a real event-owned allocation before that stock can be seized.
It must not advertise a depot inventory that does not exist.

Repair projects occupy real construction capacity or consume the relevant stock.
A completed repair restores the asset's prior condition up to the supported repair amount.
Repeated damage and repair cannot create additional building levels or accumulate permanent positive modifiers.

## Control and discipline

Affiliated societies can receive orders to protect civilians, avoid a named foreign partner, defend a corridor, or submit to joint command.
Each order needs a plausible enforcement mechanism, such as training, supplied equipment, a respected liaison, or an accepted local agreement.
A government can gain more reliable auxiliaries by accepting local autonomy or paying the cost of regularization.

Violations lead to a concrete dispute.
The sponsor can punish a responsible commander, renegotiate the compact, suspend aid, or accept responsibility.
Do not resolve every disagreement with a full civil war.
Localized mutiny, loss of access to one recruitment district, departure from the compact, or a leadership change are meaningful alternatives.

## Movement defeat and survival

Organizational defeat requires the important armed centers to be dismantled or integrated, supply and recruitment to be interrupted, and no sponsor able to maintain a continuing military organization.
A 90-day verification period is the normal starting target for a durable local settlement.
It can proceed alongside unfinished treaty obligations.

Survival with government support requires an accepted compact, functioning command arrangements, and continued material support.
The movement can survive as a legal militia institution without an independent country.
Its surviving institutions remain politically relevant after the foreign crisis ends.

A defeated original organization can leave lasting hostility in affected communities.
Aftermath unrest uses the settlement rules and the shared domestic crisis systems.
It does not clear the fire-once record or restart the source event's opening rewards.
