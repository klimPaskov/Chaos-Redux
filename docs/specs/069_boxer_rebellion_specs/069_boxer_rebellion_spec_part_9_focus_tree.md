# The Boxer country focus architecture

The dedicated tree begins with an unstable government, a locally raised army, and a choice over the kind of authority the societies will accept.
Politics, military organization, industrial recovery, diplomacy, and reunification remain separate strategic branches that influence one another.
The tree must support continued play after the immediate foreign crisis ends.

This is a path-level specification.
The implementation owner must turn the branch groups into individual focuses, review each reward, and produce the final layout and route coverage table.
It must not inflate the tree with repeated Political Power rewards to reach an arbitrary node count.

## Opening and pacing

The opening offers a small set of useful priorities: secure the founding districts, keep the core formations supplied, and establish a provisional governing compact.
There should be two to four meaningful initial choices.
A long chain of generic founding focuses must not postpone every practical action while the new country is already fighting.

Use roughly 35-day focuses for important early programs and 70-day focuses for larger institutional or industrial commitments.
A brief 7-day handover focus is justified only when it performs a real already-earned political transition.
A focus can open a project immediately while the project itself takes longer to complete.
It must not imply that a 35-day focus has already built an arsenal that still needs 180 days of work.

The first stage addresses survival and provisional authority.
The middle stage establishes a political route, a sustainable military structure, and an economic strategy.
The later stage resolves recognition, reunification, enduring foreign relations, and the place of local societies in the state.
Those stages respond to progress and world conditions rather than a compulsory calendar date.

## Branch map

| Branch | Early groups | Middle groups | Later payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B01 Founding government | Secure the administrative center, recognize participating societies, establish emergency supply | Provisional charter and division of responsibilities | A functioning state ready to choose a durable political structure |
| B02 Political authority | Debate local autonomy, national command, and religious authority | One of the three political routes below | A distinct government, political institution, law direction, and visual identity |
| B03 Army and command | Local training, equipment accounting, command liaison | Regional coordination or a professional core, with controlled regularization | A sustainable military system suited to the chosen political route |
| B04 Production and workshops | Repair existing workshops and identify usable industrial sites | Distributed production or concentrated arsenals | A funded industrial network with real construction and production effects |
| B05 Transport and coastal access | Restore local routes and the founding supply connection | Rail administration, port recovery where possible, protected supply districts | Reliable internal movement and a practical answer to foreign blockade pressure |
| B06 Diplomacy | Negotiate protection, request recognition, state limits on foreign privileges | Controlled cooperation, strict bargaining, or negotiated independence | An enduring foreign policy with active agreement and revision tools |
| B07 Reunification and regional settlement | Establish a policy toward neighboring Chinese governments | Negotiated association, staged integration, or supported military claims | A postwar Chinese order with explicit administrative and military settlement |
| B08 Knowledge, weapons, and ritual | Establish basic technical education and assess ritual claims | Controlled foreign learning, domestic research, or regulated ritual schools | A coherent policy on modern weapons and, when enabled, bounded anomalies |
| B09 Postwar institutions | Demobilization planning and reconstruction | Revise obligations, restore services, settle command disputes | A stable governing compact that can survive the end of the emergency |

Each major group needs more than a stat reward.
It should unlock an action family, a mission, a visible institution, an actual production or map change, a legal diplomatic option, or a meaningful change to an existing system.

## Political route A: A federation of societies

The route begins by binding local organizations to a common charter and a national civilian procedure for disputes.
Delegates retain regional authority while accepting shared defense, civilian protections, and transparent rules for contributions.
The central government gains predictable cooperation by accepting limits on its own direct control.

Early groups establish representation, a common protection compact, and procedures for replacing delegates who breach it.
Middle groups create a shared budget, regional contribution agreements, and a national assembly with real authority over the army's political role.
The final group can establish a civilian constitutional government when those institutions are functioning.
A democratic ideological transition belongs to that completed constitutional choice, not to the mere presence of a council.

The route changes D18, D21, D23, and D45.
Negotiated association and integration become easier when partners receive credible autonomy and representation.
Rapid unilateral mobilization and imposed territorial absorption become harder.
Military contributions take preparation and bargaining, but successful agreements are more durable.

The industrial branch favors several workable regional production centers with repair and transport links.
The army branch preserves local forces alongside a professional national core.
Diplomacy favors agreements that end foreign privileges without replacing them with a new patron.

Failure produces a regional withdrawal, a failed contribution, or an amended charter process.
A national civil war requires an actual competing military authority and incompatible territorial claims.
The route must not collapse automatically because one project missed a deadline.

Its late payoff is a durable federal political institution, a negotiated integration system, and a military compact that remains useful after the rebellion.
The AI favors this route when several strong regional societies or Chinese partners must be kept together and no single command can dominate them safely.

## Political route B: Central military government

The route establishes a national staff, standardizes the chain of command, and brings local armed organizations under a central military authority.
Its purpose is to concentrate resources for sustained war and rapid decisions.
It trades local independence for more reliable operational control.

Early groups establish the command's mandate and identify a core force willing to accept it.
Middle groups standardize supply, regularize formations through M05 and M06, and replace private military commitments with national orders.
The final group establishes a durable military government with a clear succession or accountability mechanism.
Do not reduce the endpoint to an unnamed permanent emergency leader.

The route's default ideological direction is non-aligned military rule.
A different ideology requires an explicit, coherent political development supported by the actual roster and party system.
Nationalism alone does not automatically switch the country to fascism.

The industrial branch favors concentrated arsenals and protected strategic corridors.
The diplomatic branch can make firmer commitments, but partners may demand proof that those commitments survive a change of command.
The expansion branch offers stronger coercive tools while making postwar administration and resistance more costly.

Failure can create a command dispute, a regional refusal to transfer equipment, or a limited military breakaway when the map and force ownership support it.
The implementation must not use a fixed percentage of the whole country as a generic punishment.

Its late payoff is a professional central army and a state able to sustain an independent regional policy.
The AI favors it when the immediate military threat is severe, a viable central army already exists, and the country can supply concentrated forces.

## Political route C: Religious governing council

The route makes religious and traditionalist institutions a formal part of state authority.
It must decide which local practices are accepted, who can authorize military claims, and whether command follows evidence from the battlefield or the authority of a school.
The route can remain entirely conventional when supernatural evolution is disabled.

Early groups define the council's civil responsibilities and rules for protecting communities under its authority.
Middle groups regulate schools, reconcile regional traditions, and decide how weapons, education, and technical specialists fit within the governing order.
The final group establishes a durable council with a defined relationship to military and local civilian institutions.

The route does not automatically reject rifles, medicine, railways, or industrial production.
A restrictive policy is a deliberate choice with visible economic and military costs.
A controlled-learning policy can preserve religious authority while accepting useful knowledge and equipment.
Neither choice grants free advanced technology.

Evolution II adds the specific school operations in Part 7.
Those effects require their own conditions and evidence.
A political focus cannot grant every ritual benefit simultaneously or turn religious authority into permanent invulnerability.

Failure can split a school from the council, expose a false military claim, or force a reform of command procedures.
It should change the country's institutions and available operations instead of applying an unexplained generic stability penalty.

The AI favors this route when religious schools and traditional institutions provide substantial existing authority and a council can coordinate them.
It should regulate failed claims when battlefield evidence undermines them.

## Army branch detail

The branch begins with equipment accounting, local training, and a working liaison system.
Its next groups develop regional command, protected supply, support services, and a sustainable replacement pipeline.
The later groups regularize a professional core and define the continuing role of local militia.

A decentralized army keeps local recruitment and defense value but needs accepted contributions for distant operations.
A centralized army can concentrate forces more reliably but consumes more transport and requires stronger administration.
Both can field a professional core.
Neither is a disguised route to an unlimited number of free divisions.

Focuses open D14, D16, D21, M05, and M06 or improve their real capacity and cost relationships.
A reward that reduces a training period must change the actual training operation and its displayed duration.
A command reward should affect the relevant verified military consumer, not every foreign ally.

Air and naval development remains conditional on real facilities, research, and production.
A landlocked Boxer enclave does not receive an irrelevant fleet branch at the top of its tree.
After obtaining a valid port, the country can develop coastal protection, convoy production, and appropriate naval research through the relevant industrial and transport groups.

## Production and transport branch detail

The first choice is where the state can maintain working production.
Distributed workshops are easier to sustain across a federation and reduce dependence on one vulnerable city.
Concentrated arsenals support stronger standardization but need protected transport, resources, and a larger civilian investment.

The branch opens D22 and D43, repairs specific damaged facilities, and enables funded expansion at suitable sites.
New factory or infrastructure levels are indivisible construction outcomes, with the exact number tied to the project and map capacity.
Do not scatter several free factories across states merely to make each focus have a reward.

Transport groups restore a founding corridor, create a practical rail administration, and resolve port access where geographically possible.
Their payoff is reliable supply and greater freedom to operate away from the founding districts.
A transport focus cannot guarantee foreign military access without that country's agreement.

Loss of the target state redirects the unfinished project to a valid alternative only when the investment can plausibly move.
Completed buildings stay in their actual state.
The same investment cannot be paid out twice after relocation.

## Diplomacy branch detail

The branch starts with protection guarantees and recognition attempts.
It later distinguishes limited foreign cooperation under Chinese control, strict negotiation to end privileges, and a prolonged independent position with fewer external partners.
These policies change which agreements are available and what they cost.

Controlled learning and trade can coexist with opposition to foreign territorial control.
A country does not have to choose between complete isolation and puppet status.
Dependence is a separately accepted political outcome when the actual bargain creates it.

M18 provides the recognition objective.
No capstone depends on one hardcoded foreign country choosing a specific option.
An alternate valid counterpart, a completed limited settlement, or a changed military position can satisfy the same strategic purpose.
Unresolved external conditions must have a visible alternate, timeout, or clean closure.

## Reunification and regional settlement branch detail

The branch has distinct negotiated and coercive tools.
Negotiated association starts with protection, representation, and military coordination agreements.
Successful partners can enter M15 and a staged transfer or autonomous union.
The route must preserve their assets and agreed political status.

Coercive unification requires valid claims, a real military ability to pursue them, and actual war or ultimatum mechanics.
It grants no immediate ownership.
After victory, the country must handle administration, forces, local cooperation, and any prior foreign obligations.

Neither route automatically grants cores on all territory owned by a Chinese country.
The reviewed homeland manifest and integration policy decide which states can become cores.
Distant conquests and unrelated foreign territory remain outside that rule.

A successful common anti-intervention campaign can create a conference opportunity, but it does not award one member the territory of every partner.
The political route changes the available terms and the credibility of their enforcement.

## Knowledge and ritual branch detail

The branch begins with basic technical education and the ability to maintain existing equipment.
Later groups create controlled technical exchanges, domestic training, industrial research, and a policy toward ritual claims.
Research rewards should be appropriate to the nation's facilities and current technology.
Use research support, access, or funded capacity where an instant technology grant would be implausible.

The military government tends to subordinate schools to operational command.
The federal route allows regional practice under common civilian and military rules.
The religious council defines the authority of schools more directly.
All three routes can modernize, and all three must pay the real cost of doing so.

## Postwar branch detail

The branch converts wartime commitments into enduring institutions.
It handles legal militia status, demobilization, reconstruction, treaty revision, protection of communities, and the allocation of recovered territory.
Its main rewards are stable institutions and useful continuing decisions.

M11, M14, M15, and M17 provide the proof for the major capstones.
A country which merely wins the last battle should not automatically receive the entire reconstruction and reconciliation outcome.
Conversely, a contained crisis should not be forced to endure a long punitive treaty that was never imposed.

## Layout, AI, and completion

The final tree needs clear branch identities at normal zoom, compact groups, short connectors, and an opening with a useful choice.
Use mutual exclusions for the main political structure and genuinely incompatible commitments.
Do not make every support program mutually exclusive with its neighboring program.

Use real current focus filter keys and navigation behavior from the installed game.
A special inlay or custom focus GUI is not required by this design.
The normal tree and linked decision category must communicate the route without a new interface project.

For every route, the implementation report must identify its focuses, requirements, rewards, decisions, missions, idea lifecycle, character and flag changes, AI direction, failure path, and late payoff.
The focus and probability auditors must inspect the final graph and named scenarios.
A source-only list of nodes does not prove a valid layout, complete route, or correct AI selection behavior.
