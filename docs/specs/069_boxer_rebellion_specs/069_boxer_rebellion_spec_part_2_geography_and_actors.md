# China, local authority, and foreign interests

The event begins from a theater definition and a set of actual relationships.
A Chinese name, a familiar country tag, or a historical treaty port is not sufficient evidence that the same political relationship exists in the current campaign.

## The Chinese theater

Build a reviewed state manifest from the installed map and the project's Chinese country-family definitions.
The manifest identifies the Chinese homeland theater, regional groupings, important cities, ports, railway connections, and valid alternate names.
It excludes overseas conquests and newly added distant cores unless the reviewed map policy explicitly places them in the theater.

The following are geographic research directions, not verified runtime state IDs: the north China plain, Shandong, the lower Yangtze, central railway corridors, the southeast coast, the southern treaty-port region, and Chinese northeastern borderlands where the installed map and country packages support them.
Beijing or Beiping, Tianjin, major Yangtze ports, Hong Kong, Macau, and Guangzhouwan may be relevant anchors when they exist in the installed map and have the corresponding campaign role.
Their historical names do not make them permanently foreign-controlled targets.

Manchurian and other contested border regions need explicit inclusion decisions in the map manifest.
Use the game's geography and country history consistently.
Do not treat a modern sovereignty claim or an assumed ethnicity as a substitute for the installed data.

The manifest must distinguish whole states from substate institutions.
A foreign district inside a large state can be a registered interest and a siege objective without becoming a separately owned state.
Restoring that district's former privileges must not transfer the entire surrounding state.

## Five relationships for every affected location

| Relationship | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| State owner | Holds the formal territorial title used by the game |
| State controller | Currently exercises military control and normally handles immediate security |
| Local Chinese claimant or government | May retain political support, cores, or a displaced administration |
| Foreign interest holder | Owns a concession, station, mission, investment, or other verified stake |
| Deployed force owner | Supplies and commands actual units present in the theater |

These relationships can belong to different countries.
The event must retain them separately through an occupation, civil war, release, annexation, or peace settlement.
An occupation authority should not be treated as the local population's government merely because it controls the province.
A government in exile should not receive a decision that requires spending another country's stockpile.

## Candidate centers

Select centers using a weighted pool after hard validity checks.
The intended order of importance is a nearby active occupation or foreign possession, a contested transport or urban node, weak authority, recent military defeat, and an existing network of sympathetic societies.
A state can qualify through more than one factor, but overlapping descriptions of the same foreign presence must not be counted repeatedly.

| Local situation | Selection direction | Likely first problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foreign military occupation | High priority when local organization can survive | Sabotage, competing administrations, reprisals, and displacement |
| Foreign possession beside Chinese-controlled land | High priority when routes connect the two | Border incidents and attacks on privileges |
| Divided Chinese authority | High priority if no government can provide security | Rival sponsorship and autonomous armed societies |
| Recent defeat by a foreign power | Increased priority in the affected region | Recruitment, anger at the government, and attacks on collaborators |
| Secure interior with little foreign contact | Low priority unless instability is severe | Political agitation that may remain an internal dispute |
| Destroyed or uninhabitable territory | Excluded unless an established shared system still supports civilian activity | No invented settlements or manpower |

The pool should favor a coherent opening cluster.
An additional distant center is appropriate when there is a real connection, such as a shared railway, a sponsor network, or the same foreign authority.
Do not scatter the initial movement uniformly across the entire map.

## Chinese government roles

Every valid Chinese government chooses its own relationship with the movement.
The government controlling the largest part of China does not automatically decide for warlords, rival governments, subjects, or occupied districts.

A unified government receives one national stance and region-specific actions.
A fragmented China receives separate stance records and a shared view of Boxer Strength and Intervention Pressure.
Those values describe the crisis as a whole, while tooltips identify which governments and regions are contributing to it.

A foreign-backed Chinese subject remains a Chinese political actor when its country package supports that identity.
Its freedom to sponsor an uprising against its overlord depends on autonomy, existing war rules, and a deliberate break with that relationship.
The event must not quietly give a puppet unrestricted diplomacy.
It can offer covert tolerance, civilian protection, negotiated autonomy, or a openly declared break with the patron where supported.

A displaced Chinese government can negotiate, provide material support through a valid route, or make a political commitment.
It cannot directly order arrests in territory it does not control.
A government which loses its last practical connection to the theater becomes a political observer until it regains a valid role.

## Foreign interest registry

Each foreign interest needs an origin and evidence type.
The registry should record the holder, location, host or controller, relationship type, current validity, and the last meaningful change.
A remembered historical grievance can influence support, but it is not equivalent to an active foreign asset.

| Interest type | Admission evidence | Main event uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Territorial possession | Actual ownership of an in-theater state or verified territorial enclave | Defense, restoration, evacuation, and settlement |
| Military occupation | Actual control of territory without the relevant local title | Insurgency, withdrawal demands, and occupation consequences |
| Stationed force | Verified unit presence or an established supported basing record | Relief, reinforcement, and evacuation |
| Concession or institutional privilege | A current project or vanilla record with a named location and beneficiary | Demands, siege incidents, and privilege revision |
| Political patronage | A real subject, sponsor, or dependence relationship | Chinese stance constraints and foreign response |
| Economic interest | A supported record of exceptional investment, control, or privileged access | Negotiated compensation and protection demands |
| Current war against Chinese actors | An actual war relationship and operational theater connection | Counterinsurgency or an intervention objective overlay |

Normal trade by itself does not establish colonial control.
Positive relations, ordinary military access, or an invited ally's troops can produce a Boxer grievance without making that ally an occupying power in neutral event narration.
Use the relationship's actual legal and military status.

Do not assign foreign nationality to all Christians, missionaries, merchants, or employees of a foreign-owned enterprise.
Chinese civilians are an explicit protection constituency.
Foreign nationals, Chinese converts, local workers, and combatants require different incident and evacuation handling.

## Changed borders and missing actors

Refresh an interest after a relevant state transfer, ownership change, unit withdrawal, annexation, or government change.
A bounded periodic refresh may repair missed notifications, but it must not rescan every country every day.

A foreign country that loses its only valid interest can remain involved through an existing unresolved commitment.
It should not receive new unrelated incident targets.
A country that ceases to exist loses its active vote and operational decisions.
Its assets, obligations, and civilians must be reassigned only when the actual succession rules support that transfer.

If several actors claim the same site, retain one site record with multiple relationships.
Do not create several copies of the same besieged population or several repair projects for one railway segment.

## Worked campaign shapes

In a unified China fighting an invader, the invader is an occupation target while invited allies remain separate partners.
The Chinese government can support Boxers behind enemy lines while suppressing attacks on civilians in its own territory.
Its ability to control affiliated societies depends on its agreements and actual local reach.

In a divided China, one warlord can fund societies that another government is trying to disarm.
The movement may benefit from that competition, but a sponsor cannot spend the rival government's manpower or automatically claim the rival's territory.
A durable Boxer government draws its initial territory from genuine local seizures and negotiated defections.

In a fully occupied China, the event centers on local networks and surviving Chinese political actors.
Foreign powers with compatible rescue interests may still oppose the occupier's territorial ambitions.
A relief mandate is not automatically an alliance with the occupation authority.

In a China without surviving foreign possessions, political patronage or an active invading army can still create a valid foreign crisis.
If neither exists, the event may remain an internal uprising or be ineligible for natural selection, depending on the actual instability and recorded grievance.
It must not recreate the historical Eight-Nation Alliance by inventing eight interested countries.
