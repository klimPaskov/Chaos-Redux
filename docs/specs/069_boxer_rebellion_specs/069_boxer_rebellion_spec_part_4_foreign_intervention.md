# Foreign intervention and the temporary coalition

Foreign intervention is a negotiated commitment with a military purpose.
Its participants do not become interchangeable simply because they oppose the same uprising.
A power protecting an existing possession may disagree with a power seeking evacuation, and both may reject a third member's demand for new territory.

## From an incident to a commitment

A directly affected power first chooses a response suited to the incident.
It can request Chinese protection, fund evacuation, reinforce an existing position, send supplies, propose observers, issue an ultimatum, or prepare an expedition.
These choices remain available according to need at different Pressure levels.
The thresholds organize the normal escalation sequence without making diplomacy mechanically impossible outside its usual band.

An ultimatum must name its recipients, demands, deadline, evidence, and consequence of refusal.
It must be possible to comply through an actual action.
A demand to suppress Boxers cannot be satisfied by changing a stance flag while leaving the threatened site unprotected.
A demand for an impossible territorial transfer must not become a forced automatic war trigger.

The recipient can accept, reject, propose a narrower agreement, or demonstrate that the demand concerns a site it does not control.
Counterproposals are evaluated against the issuer's declared interests and ability to enforce its threat.

## Coalition structure

The temporary intervention coalition is an event-owned agreement among actual participants.
It is not automatically a new engine faction.
Members keep their existing faction membership, ideologies, guarantees, and unrelated wars.
The coalition can coordinate separate operations, supplies, mandates, and settlements within those constraints.

A joint coalition requires at least two independent participating powers.
A single interested power can conduct a unilateral intervention without being presented as an eight-country alliance.
Subjects can contribute forces through valid autonomy rules, but an overlord and its subjects do not receive several independent votes for the same commitment.

No fixed country list defines eligibility.
The historical comparison concerns the role of the coalition, not its membership count.
The campaign may produce a coalition of three, eight, twelve, or another number of valid powers.
The interface displays the real members.

## Membership and authority

| Question | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Who may join? | A power with a valid affected interest, an accepted invitation to assist, and a credible permitted contribution |
| Who leads? | A participant able to coordinate the declared operation, normally one with a supplied theater presence and support from the other members |
| What does leadership grant? | The ability to propose objectives and conferences, not ownership of allied armies or automatic territorial claims |
| What binds a member? | Its accepted mandate, contribution, and withdrawal commitment |
| Can a member leave? | Yes, subject to the orderly withdrawal of its own commitments and the political consequences of abandoning an accepted task |
| Can a member be excluded? | Yes, for incompatible war status, breach of the mandate, refusal to honor a settlement, or loss of a viable role |
| Can enemies cooperate? | Only through a supported, explicit deconfliction arrangement. They do not automatically make peace or become allies |

Coalition confidence is expressed through named positions and disputes, not an additional public cohesion meter.
A member can support the current mandate, demand revision, prepare to leave, or refuse a specific operation.
The next available conference action follows from those positions.

## Mandates

The founding agreement chooses a principal mandate and permitted supporting tasks.
The mandate determines what counts as victory, what forces are needed, and which settlement demands are legitimate inside the agreement.

| Mandate | Core objective | Normal completion | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evacuation | Remove threatened civilians or an isolated force | The registered population or force reaches a valid safe destination | Mission expansion after the rescue is complete |
| Protection | Secure an existing possession, district, or agreed facility | The named site remains safe for the agreed verification period | Permanent occupation disguised as temporary defense |
| Suppression | End organized attacks by specified armed networks | Strongholds dismantled or a verifiable disarmament compact completed | Driving a wider population toward the movement |
| Coercive settlement | Compel an enforceable agreement from the responsible government | Accepted terms and a workable withdrawal plan | Demands that exceed military leverage |
| Expanded privileges | Obtain new political, economic, or territorial advantages | A separately accepted or militarily imposed term | Member refusal, Chinese resistance, and condemnation |

Expanded privileges cannot silently replace an evacuation mandate.
A member proposing escalation must state the new commitment and give others a genuine choice to accept or withdraw.
The same rule applies to a shift from suppressing an autonomous Boxer force to overthrowing a Chinese government.

## Real military capacity

An expedition needs a deployment route, supply, suitable forces, and permission or an actual legal war relationship.
A naval power needs the relevant transport and fuel capacity.
A neighboring power can use a land route when the border and access rules permit it.
An actor with no viable route can contribute diplomacy, money through an established system, or equipment through a supported delivery path.
It cannot create a free army inside a Chinese state.

Use the existing force owner's units whenever possible.
The event should prepare and coordinate an operation, then evaluate actual deployment and progress.
Do not charge a complete manpower and rifle bill for a new army while also reusing an existing formation as if it had been newly raised.
No unit, aircraft, ship, commander, or stockpile may exist simultaneously for the sender and the recipient after a transfer.

## Four operational situations

**Permitted assistance** occurs when a Chinese government accepts protection or relief.
The operation uses the actual access, aid, or unit-transfer mechanisms verified for the installed game.
A foreign government cannot treat permission to evacuate civilians as permission to occupy every nearby city.

**An independent intervention war** is possible when a valid war relationship can be created without violating faction and subject constraints.
The war starts through a documented engine path.
The event records the intended limited objectives, but does not assume that labeling a war limited changes the engine's peace rules.

**An existing war** receives an objective overlay.
An invader or defender already fighting in China does not declare the same war again.
The overlay tracks the Boxer-related task while preserving the wider conflict.

**An incompatible relationship** blocks the military action concerned.
Countries in an unresolved hostile relationship cannot share command as if they were allies.
Participants can pursue separate relief, bargaining, or parallel objectives where the engine supports them.
If no safe military representation exists, that operation remains an explicit implementation blocker.
A decorative ceasefire flag is not an acceptable substitute for a real ceasefire.

## Joint operations

The coalition identifies a small number of objectives based on current geography.
Examples include relieving a site, securing a port-to-interior corridor, protecting an existing possession, or removing a stronghold that threatens a major route.
The selected objective explains which member has committed forces and which member is supplying or negotiating.

A coalition member earns political standing through completed commitments.
Do not award a second completion reward for arriving after the objective is already secure.
Do not make arbitrary casualties the main measure of contribution.
A country that supplied the only usable relief route can matter even if another country won the final battle.

Failure opens a conference choice.
Members can reinforce, narrow the mandate, negotiate, or withdraw.
A failed relief attempt does not automatically annex another Chinese state or summon additional major powers.

## Chinese cooperation and resistance

Chinese governments can negotiate with individual coalition members as well as with the conference.
A government that protects civilians and suppresses attacks can ask a limited-mandate power to leave even while another power continues to demand territory.
A supporter of the Boxers can offer guarantees without accepting every foreign demand.

Evolution III adds a broader Chinese coordination compact.
It does not retroactively create a unified government or force current rivals into one faction.
The pact's limits and postwar disputes are described in Part 7.

## Peace and withdrawal

Every completed mandate must specify what happens to foreign troops, allocated equipment, access rights, occupied sites, and temporary modifiers.
A rescue ends with a withdrawal or a newly negotiated protection agreement.
A punitive settlement ends with particular enforceable terms and a schedule for reducing the foreign commitment.

A Boxer-related settlement must not terminate an unrelated world war.
When engine wars have merged or a separate peace cannot be safely executed, conclude the event-owned objectives and obligations while leaving the actual war intact.
The interface must describe that distinction honestly.
The implementation must prove any automatic peace operation against the current war structure before using it.
