# Event 095 research notes

These sources inform design choices.
They do not establish a universal historical model of national resistance and do not determine the event's numerical balance.
No historical quote, leader list, licensed recording or final visual asset has been approved through this research.
Public-source retrieval was performed on 5 October 2026.

## H1. Underground links and surviving organization

The UK National Archives' account of Virginia Hall describes resistance contacts, communication with London, weapons and supply coordination, safe houses and escapes.
It also describes how the loss or exposure of contacts changed the organization that remained available.
The cited institutional account is based on collection material including HS 9/647/4 and related SOE records.

Design application: distinguish surviving contacts, delivered material and national command rather than treating native resistance as a complete army.
Local network loss should change which operations remain possible.
A successful escape should preserve only the people and resources that actually escaped.
This is a game-design inference, not a claim that every movement used the same structure.

Source: UK National Archives, “Virginia Hall”.

```text
https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/stories/virginia-hall/
```

## H2. Cooperation without identical political identity

The National Archives' East River Column account describes guerrilla assistance to Allied prisoners and escapees around Hong Kong, alongside cooperation with British intelligence personnel.
The account links local knowledge, shelter, transport and assistance rather than reducing the relationship to identical political affiliation.

Design application: a sponsor can have a current strategic reason to support a claimant without sharing its ideology.
Rescue, returning personnel and military supply are distinct contributions.
This does not make the East River Column a proposed new Event 095 tag or imply that the event should reproduce that historical organization as a national claimant.

Source: UK National Archives, “The East River Column: the rebels who helped Second World War prisoners of war”.
```text
https://live-www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/stories/the-east-river-column-the-rebels-who-helped-second-world-war-prisoners-of-war/
```

## H3. Dispatched aid is not received aid

The Warsaw Rising Museum's account of the Warsaw airlift describes aircraft carrying weapons, medicines, food and other supplies to the uprising through dangerous routes.
Its Frantic VII material distinguishes the supplies dropped from those that reached insurgent positions.
Different museum accounts give different numerical summaries of some totals, so this package does not adopt a precise historical delivery percentage.

Design application: track departure, arrival, interception and loss separately.
A patron's decision to assist must not equal an immediate full-stockpile gain for the claimant.
A port, airfield or access agreement matters through the route it makes possible.
The game's actual supported route mechanics and evidence must determine its losses, not a copied historical percentage.

Sources: Warsaw Rising Museum, “Warsaw Airlift 1944: Brotherhood-in-arms between the Western Allies and Poles”, and its archival note on Frantic VII documents.

## H4. Dispersal can preserve a later struggle

The National Archives' account of Nancy Wake describes liaison between separate Maquis groups and the communication of withdrawal and dispersal after a military defeat.
The account supports a distinction between losing a particular engagement and losing all organizational capacity.

Design application: a failed revolt can leave actual surviving networks and leadership for a later attempt.
Dispersal has a cost and does not preserve destroyed units or consumed equipment.
A later uprising benefits from real survival rather than a stacking bonus for repeated failure.
No named Nancy Wake character or portrait is introduced by this planning package.

Source: UK National Archives, “Nancy Wake”.
```text
https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/stories/nancy-wake/
```
