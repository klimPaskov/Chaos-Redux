# Occupation revolt: a country returns from occupation

## The opening promise

A country whose homeland has been conquered rises inside that homeland and forces its occupier to fight for it again.
The restored country carries its own identity, surviving institutions and unfinished political arguments into the revolt.
Its initial position depends on the land, people, weapons and organization that the movement can actually bring together.
A revolt can become a liberation war, survive as an isolated regional government, negotiate a partial restoration, or lose its territory and return underground.

Event 095 is a Minor Repeatable event with Chaos level 1, assigned to the Liberations cluster as a Medium member.
Its catalog status remains To Be Reworked until implementation and the required completion process are finished.
The four evolutions are Prepared Underground at Chaos 200+, Uprising on Every Front at 400+, The Army Breaks at 600+, and Liberation Cascade at 800+.
These are the user's supplied names and thresholds.
All other labels in this package identify design concepts and are not finished localisation.

The usual target is a displaced national government or identity.
A government in exile is one candidate, not the only candidate.
A surviving country based in colonies, islands or a remnant region can recover its homeland through the same national tag.
An annexed country can return through its original identity.
Only when the primary pool has no suitable candidate may the event prepare an authentic dormant claimant inside another country.
There is no generic separatist tag created to guarantee a firing.

## What the player is deciding

The claimant must decide where to concentrate its first strength, whether to expose additional underground networks, how much dependence on foreign assistance to accept, and how to reconcile returning officials with leaders who stayed in the homeland.
The occupier must decide whether to contain the revolt, divert serious military resources, negotiate a settlement, or abandon an unsustainable possession.
Foreign powers must decide whether a restored country is worth the equipment, access arrangements and diplomatic risks involved in supporting it.

A large map opening is not automatically a strong army.
A movement may control several poor mountain states while lacking enough weapons to defend their approaches.
A small coastal revolt may be more viable because it has a port, a functioning workshop and an ally able to deliver supplies.
A returning government may have trained soldiers abroad while its local political authority is disputed.
These differences should change useful choices rather than produce a single optimal checklist.

## A compact visible system

The event uses two custom public values, both on a 0–100 scale.
Readiness measures how effectively the active movement can coordinate preparations and convert available resources into an uprising.
Cohesion measures whether the national government, local resistance commands and participating communities can act under an agreed political settlement.
Neither is a spendable currency.
An action can change either value as a consequence, but cannot debit points as though they were money.
Each tooltip shows the current value, the next threshold that changes play (for Cohesion 25, 50 and 75; for Readiness the formation bands at 40 and 70 and, for a dormant movement, the opening gate at 50) and at most three largest current causes.

State-level support, exposure, supply access and occupation weakness remain local evidence and qualitative assessments.
Army strength, manpower, equipment stocks, buildings, stability, war support, resistance and compliance continue to use their ordinary game systems.
A patron's obligations are agreements with stated terms rather than a third influence meter.
The occupier sees the quality of its intelligence and observable threats, not the claimant's hidden exact preparation values.

A phase exposes at most six primary event actions.
The normal target is three to five actions and one to three missions.
A contextual target selector changes the target of an action, not the number of parallel action systems.
Changing tabs must not evade this budget.

## The campaign sequence

Selection identifies the real claimant and a viable homeland component.
A displaced claimant with sufficient existing support can enter the armed phase immediately.
A dormant claimant enters an underground phase first and must develop local organization before it can seize territory.
The first armed opening establishes a capital or provisional seat, a territorial base, an equipped force and a specific conflict with the relevant occupier.
It does not transfer every national core or instantly restore all former institutions.

The first campaign objective is survival with a functioning route for supply and command.
Once a base is secure, the claimant can expand through ordinary combat and locally prepared breakaways.
Military success then raises a political question about who will govern the restored country.
Government settlement opens recovery and reconstruction paths without erasing the war or replacing a meaningful existing focus tree.
After territorial defeat, surviving networks and exiled institutions can influence a later attempt.

Selection, underground preparation, armed opening, contested restoration, settlement, recovery and dormant survival are distinct phases of one incident.
They are not separate rolls in the global minor-event pool.
A secondary uprising caused by Liberation Cascade remains connected to its own claimant and to its originating cascade.

## Baseline military limits

The opening army is assembled from local volunteers, accumulated underground weapons, surviving national stocks, actual returning formations and delivered foreign assistance.
Its scale is constrained by population, native recruitable manpower, available equipment, command capacity and supply.
No fixed number of divisions is assigned to every revolt.
No division is created in every owned state.
A country that still owns overseas territory does not receive an additional army there merely because its homeland revolted.

Ordinary human infantry, security troops, cavalry and support formations use established unit systems.
A lack of DLC-specific irregular units cannot prevent the baseline uprising.
Heavy equipment, air forces and naval forces require a real source.
The event does not grant a modern combined-arms army because the opposing country is a major.
Occupier weakness can improve opportunity without creating the resources needed to exploit it.

## Baseline political limits

The claimant retains surviving characters, political history, technologies and valid diplomatic relationships.
Restoration does not automatically revive dead leaders, reverse previous political choices, recreate a dissolved faction or impose a new ideology.
Where national institutions are missing, the country package supplies a provisional administration with country-appropriate identity and a meaningful route to settled government.
Local resistance leadership must matter without requiring an invented named historical person.

The revolt concerns an actual national homeland.
The event does not create ethnic expulsion, compulsory population exchange, free worldwide cores or a mandatory world-conquest branch.
Other populations in a restored state remain civilians with interests and rights within the shared civilian systems.
Mixed claims require political handling rather than an automatic assertion that every resident supports the selected claimant.

## Victory and failure

A secure national foothold is an important success even when full restoration remains impossible.
The claimant can secure a defensible border, accept a partial settlement or continue the struggle for occupied homeland states.
A settlement must describe exactly what territory, sovereignty, military restrictions and diplomatic obligations it changes.
Recognition alone does not end a war.
A peace operation must not terminate unrelated participants' wars.

Territorial defeat ends the active military package and clears its temporary UI, missions and unrealized rewards.
Surviving troops that really escaped, equipment that was really evacuated and leadership that remains alive can support a later attempt.
Destroyed formations, consumed stores and dead characters do not reappear because the event is repeatable.
Repeated uprisings must reflect a renewed opportunity and rebuilt capacity, rather than provide an equipment-farming cycle.

## The escalation boundary

Evolutions deepen the available methods.
They do not replace the national restoration premise.
Prepared Underground improves preparation and conversion efficiency.
Uprising on Every Front allows several genuine local centers.
The Army Breaks brings finite defections from demonstrably local occupation structures.
Liberation Cascade links the success of one national movement to the preparation of others.

Chaos 800 alone does not dissolve an empire.
A cascade must grow from military and political evidence of successful restoration.
There is no required world-end route for Event 095.
A sufficiently large collapse may receive a single major presentation event, while the actual wars and settlements remain national and regional.
