# Occupation revolt: foreign assistance and the restoration settlement

## Why another country participates

A supporter needs a current diplomatic reason and a useful way to assist.
Former allies may seek the return of a partner.
Enemies of the occupier may want another front.
Neighbors may seek a stable border, transit access or influence over a nearby settlement.
Ideological partners may favor a compatible government.
Regional rivals may oppose an occupier's growing power without sharing the claimant's ideology.
These motives affect preferred terms, not the authenticity of the national claimant.

A sponsor can recognize a claimant without supplying it.
It can deliver material without guaranteeing independence.
It can offer a guarantee without joining the war immediately.
Each commitment is separate and must use the actual diplomatic or resource mechanism involved.
A favorable opinion value is not a receipt for rifles, transport or military protection.

## A small support menu

The active sponsor view has up to five principal actions: establish liaison, deliver military stores, arrange returning personnel, offer a political agreement, and organize humanitarian access where real civilian need exists.
Only applicable actions are shown.
One action can target several eligible claimant incidents through a contextual selector, but the sponsor must commit to an exact recipient and route before spending anything.

Establishing liaison normally costs 25 political power and takes 35 days.
It requires actual diplomatic contact with a surviving government or a supported connection to a local movement.
It provides better information and enables verified material coordination.
It does not reveal every clandestine region or make a dormant country suddenly playable.
A sponsor that loses access can preserve a political relationship while losing its delivery route.

## Military stores

A shipment is sized against the claimant's current missing equipment and the donor's usable surplus.
The draft offer is the smaller of 25% of the recipient's current supported infantry-equipment deficit and 10% of the donor's compatible uncommitted infantry-equipment stock.
Round the equipment quantity down to a practical lot of 50 where that does not exclude a genuinely useful smaller-state transfer.
The system must permit an exact smaller final lot when the recipient's complete remaining need is below 50.
A donor with no surplus cannot purchase a free shipment with political power alone.

The standard action uses equipment plus 25 political power.
Sea or air delivery may require supported native transport commitments, fuel or command power, but no single variant may exceed four spendable resource types.
Prefer a route that uses native delivery systems when they preserve the exact resource and risk semantics.
A route which cannot be supported by the installed game must remain unavailable rather than silently delivering instant supplies.

A dispatch receipt records donor, claimant, equipment variants, departure amount, route, expected arrival, and commitment terms.
The final outcome records delivered, returned, captured and destroyed amounts.
The sum must reconcile with the actual departure.
Only delivered equipment becomes usable by the claimant.
The donor cannot issue the same reserved equipment to a second recipient before the first transaction resolves.

The supplied source history of the Warsaw airlift illustrates why dispatched aid and received aid must remain distinct.
It does not provide a universal delivery-loss percentage for this event.
Loss and delay must follow the actual game route and validated supported mechanics.
See research note H3 for the historical anchor.

## Personnel, volunteers and exiles

A supporter can return the claimant's existing forces, permit native volunteers, provide a finite training mission or release available national personnel.
These are different contributions.
Returning formations retain their actual manpower, equipment, experience, ownership provenance and losses.
Foreign volunteers remain subject to their native ownership and return rules.
A training mission improves organization of recruits over time and does not itself create recruits or rifles.

A training mission normally takes 70 days and costs 25 political power plus 25 command power for the sponsor.
It requires a usable liaison and a claimant force that can actually train.
It can enable conversion from local defense formations to the organized national profile when the material bill is paid.
The same cohort cannot repeatedly earn training credit from several sponsors for a free maximum-experience result.
The contribution is linked to the actual cohort or finite training capacity.

Access and transport are political commitments with military consequences.
A neutral neighboring country can permit a transit arrangement only through a supported diplomatic relationship.
The event does not move armies through a neutral state while pretending no border was crossed.
An exile host can refuse to release forces that it genuinely controls under the existing arrangement.
The claimant can seek a political settlement of that dispute rather than receive duplicate replacements.

## Terms and dependence

Support can be unconditional, tied to a limited reciprocal agreement, or attached to a major postwar political demand.
A limited agreement might concern defined transit, a time-limited basing arrangement or a documented supply obligation.
A major demand might require a subject relationship or a government commitment that the claimant must explicitly accept.
The exact benefits, duration, exit and breach consequences must be visible before acceptance.

The baseline does not require every aid delivery to create a new contract.
Use a single existing support agreement for routine shipments where appropriate.
The claimant can receive aid from several compatible sponsors without accumulating a new public influence meter for each.
Conflicting commitments create a political issue that must be resolved, rather than silently allowing both patrons to obtain exclusive rights.

A claimant's refusal of intrusive terms must remain playable.
It may mean a smaller army, a harder route or slower recovery.
It cannot delete the only essential survival action or force the country into an unannounced puppet outcome.
An occupier's rival can support a national liberation for self-interested reasons without controlling its eventual ideology.

## Recognition and sovereignty

Recognition identifies which government a foreign power accepts as representing the national claimant.
It can improve diplomatic access and support an existing government in exile.
It does not transfer territory or automatically choose between rival movements that belong to another event.
The restored country remains one identity while its internal government settlement develops.

A returning government and local resistance command may disagree over a patron's proposal.
Accepting a major commitment without satisfying the current governing agreement removes 10 Cohesion and opens a specific reconciliation obligation.
Fulfilling a previously accepted limited agreement can grant 10 Cohesion once if it resolves an actual domestic dispute.
Ordinary repeated deliveries do not farm Cohesion.

## Negotiating the war's end

Settlement eligibility requires a real military or political basis.
A claimant might hold a defensible homeland base, recover the national center, obtain decisive allied backing, or face a stalemate in which the occupier prefers a bounded concession.
An occupier might offer withdrawal to concentrate on another front.
An isolated claimant close to defeat may accept autonomy or evacuation.
The AI weighs actual battlefield position and the price of continuing the occupation.

The settlement defines recognized borders, any remaining occupation, sovereignty or subject status, military access, return of eligible displaced civilians, treatment of surviving formations and outstanding support agreements.
It must distinguish an immediate transfer from a dated promise.
Each dated obligation has a completion condition and a breach consequence.
A truce or withdrawal cannot be counted as full independence when the claimant remains a subject.

The design supports full restoration, negotiated partial independence, supported autonomy, continued war from a secure foothold, and defeat with surviving exile.
It does not promise engine-isolated limited peace without a verified native precedent.
Implementation must prove the exact war and participant behavior for every settlement path.
If a desired peace operation would end unrelated wars, that operation is blocked until a safe implementation is found.
The country can continue its ordinary war while other supported political terms are processed.

## Recovery obligations

An end to fighting opens recovery objectives appropriate to actual damage.
A country with intact administration should not be forced through a fictional destroyed-capital reconstruction mission.
A state with ruined infrastructure, disrupted food access or recorded displacement should have a material recovery path.
Repair uses real buildings and construction progress.
Relief uses actual supplies and shared humanitarian receipts.
Civilian return uses exact origin, route, destination and reception evidence.

Demobilization applies only to temporary forces that actually exist and to agreements the government accepted.
It must preserve veteran identity and equipment accounting where the supported native route permits it.
It does not silently destroy the player's regular army when the event concludes.
A country that retains a threatened border can negotiate a smaller demobilization obligation instead of being required to disarm completely.

Foreign support ends through completed terms, explicit withdrawal, route loss or a defined breach.
Unused reservations are released.
Completed donations are not magically reclaimed from units already fighting.
The surviving country leaves the incident with its actual army, debts, institutions and diplomatic position, ready to continue as a normal participant in the game.
