# Occupation revolt: spread and the occupier's response

## Spread follows a moving front

A state can join through ordinary military conquest or through a locally prepared breakaway.
The former uses normal warfare.
The latter requires an authentic home-state basis, local organization, a viable relationship to the revolt and sufficiently weak occupation control.
The existence of a neighboring rebel state alone is not enough.

A locally prepared breakaway has three necessary conditions.
Its network is ready to support open action.
The claimant can sustain the resulting territory through a land connection, a supported separate center or a real supply route.
The occupier cannot maintain a decisive local military position that would make immediate transfer implausible.
Native resistance, administrative collapse and nearby claimant victories improve the opportunity.
High compliance, exposed networks, intact security administration and strong local forces reduce it.

There is no chance roll that can simply steal any home state regardless of these conditions.
Weighted variation is used only between eligible opportunities and for uncertain local participation within a bounded operation.
The probability auditor must evaluate those weighted surfaces before implementation is called balanced.

## Evidence of success

Success is measured from real territorial gains, the survival of a supply route, the return of a national institution, the completion of the initial survival objective and the occupier's actual loss of control.
Do not count repeated captures of the same state as new strategic victories.
Each state has one first-liberation receipt per occupation episode and a separate current-control state.
The first receipt supports achievements and national morale history.
Current control determines whether the state can still support the revolt.

A claimant that is losing its base or suffering unsustainable equipment shortages cannot keep triggering distant breakaways at the same rate as a winning claimant.
The draft opportunity assessment is refreshed through meaningful incident changes and a bounded active-incident check.
Only tracked states and claimant records participate.
Do not scan every country and every state on a daily pulse.

## Organizing the next region

After the opening, the claimant prepares the next eligible home region with the same Organize Region action it used for its first base ([Part 10](095_occupation_revolt_spec_part_10_decisions_and_missions.md#claimant-preparation-actions)).
It requires a surviving local network and a usable command link.
It costs 25 political power for a living claimant and takes 35 days under normal conditions.
The operation improves the target's organization by 15 and can make a local breakaway eligible.
It does not guarantee transfer or create a free army there.
Only one ordinary organization operation is active at baseline.
Evolution II can coordinate several centers through a shared operation once their material budgets are separated.

If the target is captured normally while the operation is underway, complete its political integration objective without issuing a second territory or unit reward.
If the network is exposed and destroyed, close the operation with its actual losses and no automatic full refund.
If a third party restores the region peacefully, re-evaluate the command objective rather than declaring war to preserve the mission.
If the region becomes unrelated to the claimant through a valid identity transformation, invalidate the target and release any unspent reservation.

## The occupier's strategic choices

The occupier receives a bounded decision category for the active homeland incident.
It sees the affected national claimant, confirmed opening states, known prepared regions and current settlement opportunities.
A multi-occupier homeland does not give each occupier control over the other's states or negotiations.
The incident coordinates shared facts while each country acts only on its own authority and resources.

Military containment commits real forces to threatened positions and preserves transport, supply and key administration.
A decision may establish the operational priority and a finite mission, but it cannot conjure divisions or delete rebel supply by narrative command.
The success condition checks actual control and the ability to maintain the position.
A mission to secure a port fails when the port is lost even if the button was paid for.

Local political settlement offers concrete concessions to the claimant or participating regional institutions.
Terms can include a recognized provisional administration, protected local governance, a defined autonomy arrangement or an agreed evacuation and sovereignty transfer.
A settlement that changes subject status must use a supported subject relationship and has the diplomatic consequences of that status.
A promise of a future concession has a dated obligation and a breach condition.

Feeding and protecting the population is the occupier's cheapest counterinsurgency, and it uses the famine and migration categories rather than an Event 095 button.
A home state the occupier starves, requisitions from, conceals hunger in or holds a camp in is a grievance state, as defined in [Part 11](095_occupation_revolt_spec_part_11_connections_and_consequences.md#grievance-states); relieving it removes that local advantage.
Relief cannot erase national identity or convert political opposition into loyalty, but it does take away the easiest breakaways.

Strategic withdrawal permits an occupier to abandon an unsustainable possession under stated terms.
It remains available when political power is low.
It does not require paying an artificial fee merely to stop defending territory.
Military units and civilians can leave only through supported routes and their own legal movement rules.
The withdrawal does not teleport every soldier to the occupier's capital or move the local population along with a state transfer.

## Local security operations

An operation against an exposed network requires an actual confirmed target and sufficient committed local security capacity.
The draft organizational cost is 25 political power and up to 25 command power, with no command-power cost above the project's 60-point limit.
The operation takes 35 days under ordinary conditions.
Its success reduces the specific network's organization by 15 and can seize a verified exposed cache.
It does not remove cores, reset all native resistance or grant the occupier a permanent anti-revolt modifier.

The result distinguishes organizers dispersed, stores seized and political effects.
No generic operation inserts a fabricated civilian death total.
Where another system records actual abuse, deaths or displacement, the responsible actor and public evidence must remain attached to those facts.
The national movement can react to proven abuses through its local support assessment.
No brutality option is required to make this event deep.

The occupier's action can fail when troops are withdrawn, the state changes hands or the target network relocates through a completed route.
Unspent reservations are returned exactly.
Resources actually committed and consumed are not refunded as though the action never happened.
The success report must state the actual outcome rather than a prewritten victory claim.

## Armistices and partial settlements

A local political agreement is not automatically a military armistice.
A valid armistice identifies the parties, territory and conflict instance it affects.
Where the engine cannot isolate the relevant war relationship safely, preserve the wider war and implement only the supported political or administrative agreement.
Do not call a broad white peace that releases unrelated allies or erases an ongoing world war.

A partial restoration can secure the claimant's independent seat and some home territory while leaving other national claims unresolved.
It closes the active incident when the parties reach an actual settlement and the essential survival conditions are met.
The remaining occupied homeland stays visible as a national issue in the country's ordinary development path.
A later qualifying national displacement can trigger a new Event 095 incident.
A stable partially restored state cannot repeatedly claim an entirely new opening army for the same unresolved border.

## Collapse and containment outcomes

A successful occupier can defeat the armed revolt without eliminating the national identity.
It can capture the provisional seat, destroy the claimant's supply route or force an accepted settlement.
A containment result records which networks survived and whether any forces escaped.
It does not secretly award a permanent immunity from future revolt.

A successful claimant can break the occupation structure region by region.
When the occupier loses its meaningful local control and the claimant has a coherent government and army, remaining prepared home regions become much more likely to join.
This is an accelerated collapse of a real occupation.
It is not a transfer of every country the occupier has ever conquered.
Other national movements require their own identities and eligibility under Liberation Cascade.
