# Event 22 Concentration Camps Research Notes

## Research date and purpose

Research reviewed on 22 August 2026.

The purpose was to establish accurate distinctions between camp types, forced labour, extermination, genocide, lawful wartime internment, evidence discovery, liberation, displacement, and postwar accountability. The research also corrected the rough concept where it treated genocide as a generic economic policy and where it implied that nerve agents were the historical killing method used in Nazi camps.

This file guides design and future localisation. It is not a substitute for country-specific research when Germany, Japan, the Soviet Union, a colonial system, a civil war, or another named historical actor receives dedicated content.

## Source hierarchy

Highest-priority sources used:

- United States Holocaust Memorial Museum
- United Nations Office on Genocide Prevention
- International Committee of the Red Cross
- International Labour Organization
- United States Library of Congress
- United Kingdom National Archives

These institutions were preferred over summaries, forums, popular-history sites, and unsourced lists.

## Concentration camp definition

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum describes concentration camps as detention sites outside ordinary judicial sentence and review. In the Nazi system, they served several functions, including detention, repression, forced labour, and murder.

Design conclusion:

- Event 22 requires arbitrary mass detention, persecution, forced labour, severe deprivation, extermination, or comparable abuse.
- An ordinary prison, lawful prisoner-of-war camp, or lawful civilian internment site does not qualify by name alone.
- A camp can change function over time, which supports the baseline conversion and policy system.

## Scale and diversity of the Nazi camp system

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum has documented more than 44,000 ghettos, camps, and other incarceration sites created by Nazi Germany and its allies between 1933 and 1945. The total includes many kinds of sites, not 44,000 extermination camps.

Design conclusion:

- The event should represent a network with different site roles. One generic building effect is insufficient.
- Country-specific German content needs detention, forced labour, transit, experiment, concentration, and killing-site distinctions.
- Public wording must not imply that every Nazi site had the same purpose.

## Forced labour

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum describes forced labour as crucial to the German war effort. Prisoners and civilian forced labourers worked under brutal conditions, and many died from ill-treatment, disease, starvation, overwork, and killing.

The International Labour Organization defines forced labour around work exacted under threat or penalty without voluntary consent.

Design conclusion:

- Forced labour can produce a real short-term economic gain.
- The gain needs a finite detained workforce, guard and transport costs, mortality, disease, sabotage, and declining output.
- A strong assignment can be tempting without becoming an unlimited permanent bonus.
- The system should not imply that camp exploitation was economically clean or costless.

## Extermination camps and killing centers

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum distinguishes killing centers from the broader concentration-camp system. Auschwitz-Birkenau functioned as both a concentration-camp complex and a killing center.

Design conclusion:

- Evolution I conversion changes the site's primary purpose.
- Extermination sites should focus on deaths, terror, resistance, evidence, transport, and political control.
- They should not retain the strongest forced-labour production bonuses.
- A country can have a mixed network, which supports the required half concentration and half extermination split.

## Genocide definition

The United Nations Genocide Convention requires specific intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnic, racial, or religious group. Mass killing or persecution can be a crime against humanity without meeting this narrower intent requirement.

Design conclusion:

- Do not call every Event 22 network genocide.
- A genocide classification requires a valid protected-group registry and a campaign policy establishing destructive intent.
- Political, class, resistance, or broad domestic purges can still be extermination, persecution, mass atrocity, or crimes against humanity.
- The game must not invent ethnicity percentages from cores, ownership, ideology, or state population.

## Lawful civilian internment and prisoner-of-war detention

The International Committee of the Red Cross explains that civilian internment in international armed conflict is an exceptional security measure, not a punishment, and must meet legal safeguards and humane-treatment obligations.

Design conclusion:

- Lawful and humane internment is outside Event 22.
- An emergency or quarantine label does not prevent Event 22 classification when detention becomes arbitrary, abusive, exploitative, indefinite, or lethal.
- Player-facing conditions need to show why the site belongs to the atrocity system.

## Death marches and forced evacuation

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum documents forced evacuations of camp prisoners as Allied forces approached. Prisoners died from shooting, starvation, exposure, exhaustion, and abuse during marches and transport.

Design conclusion:

- Front distance should trigger emergency state missions.
- Supplied transfer, closure in place, abandonment, forced march, liquidation, and evidence destruction need different consequences.
- A forced march can move the crisis across several states and create witnesses, escapees, and evidence.
- The system should remain strategic and avoid procedural depictions.

## Medical experiments

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum documents non-consensual and often lethal experiments in Nazi camps. Modern scientific and ethical standards reject the methods and many claimed results.

Design conclusion:

- Experiment sites can connect to country-specific packages and Event 16 evidence.
- Event 22 must not award scientific progress merely because prisoners were used.
- Any technology or project result requires an explicit, accepted Event 16 bridge.
- Discovery creates separate experiment evidence and accountability.

## Chemical killing and the nerve-agent correction

United States Holocaust Memorial Museum material on gas chambers and killing centers identifies carbon monoxide and hydrogen-cyanide pesticide formulations such as Zyklon B among the principal historical killing agents used by Nazi Germany.

Sarin and soman were not the standard historical camp-killing method represented by those sources. Event 22's nerve-agent integration is alternate-history Chaos Redux content linked to the mod's existing chemical technologies.

Design conclusion:

- Player-facing historical text must not claim that sarin or soman was the normal historical method in extermination camps.
- The restricted chemical route needs an explicit alternate-history context.
- It must consume real stockpile and use contamination, accident, Deaths, evidence, and discovery systems.
- The design should not contain technical operating instructions.

## Evidence and the Nuremberg trials

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum notes that the Nuremberg prosecution used large bodies of German documents, photographs, films, and eyewitness testimony.

Design conclusion:

- Evidence should have several categories.
- Destroying one archive cannot erase all proof.
- Network verification should be stronger when central orders, transport records, site records, testimony, and physical evidence agree.
- Trials and accountability need a sufficient evidence basis, not one generic discovery flag.

## Displaced persons after liberation

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum documents the postwar displaced-person crisis, including survivors who could not or would not return home and who needed shelter, medical care, tracing, legal status, and resettlement.

Design conclusion:

- Liberation does not end the crisis when the building changes controller.
- Survivor relief, family tracing, safe return, resettlement, and receiving-country capacity are required aftermath systems.
- The planned migration mechanic should own actual population movement when available.
- Forced return to an unsafe successor should be a visible negative choice.

## Gulag system

The United States Library of Congress describes the Soviet Gulag as a forced-labour system that contributed to major industrial and construction projects while imposing severe deprivation and high mortality in many periods and locations.

Design conclusion:

- Gulag networks remain a Soviet-specific building and adapter.
- Event 22 can activate, expand, expose, or connect to them.
- Evolution I must not automatically rename half of all gulags as extermination camps.
- Soviet policy, famine, purge, and direct-killing conditions decide which sites become extermination or lethal-deprivation sites.

## Boer War concentration camps

The United Kingdom National Archives documents British concentration camps during the South African War, including severe deaths among women and children from disease and malnutrition.

Design conclusion:

- The term concentration camp is not limited to Nazi Germany.
- A camp can cause mass death through deprivation, disease, poor administration, and coercive population control without being an extermination camp.
- Generic Event 22 variants should preserve that distinction.

## Historical gain and strategic cost

Historical forced-labour systems could contribute materially to war production, construction, extraction, and infrastructure. Their output existed alongside coercion, high mortality, corruption, low productivity, security burdens, sabotage, transport costs, and long-term human and institutional damage.

Design conclusion:

- Strong short-term state bonuses are historically and mechanically defensible for forced-labour assignments.
- Extermination itself should not be treated as an economic engine.
- The network should consume transport, guards, supplies, and administration.
- Output must fall as the detained workforce is killed, disabled, released, transferred, or liberated.
- Broad domestic purges should damage officers, administration, factories, and political stability.

## Target-group abstraction

No reviewed source supports inventing a general ethnicity map from HOI4 state ownership or core data. The mod needs explicit campaign registries.

Accepted target sources:

- named country-specific protected group with research and scripted marker
- occupied or non-core civilians
- existing refugee, minority, displaced-person, or migration marker
- resistance-linked detainees and hostages
- political or ideological enemies defined by a regime
- broad domestic purge after other target sources are exhausted

Design conclusion:

- Final localisation names the source category that exists.
- It does not claim how many people belong to an ethnicity unless the campaign system actually tracks a bounded population marker.
- The genocide label remains conditional on protected-group and intent proof.

## Evidence concealment limits

Historical records, survivor testimony, physical sites, demographic loss, transport data, and foreign intelligence can survive even when a perpetrator destroys local documents.

Design conclusion:

- Cover-up can reduce immediate Exposure.
- Cover-up creates separate evidence and a larger later reaction.
- Building demolition does not clear responsibility.
- Destroyed sites remain discoverable.
- A staged inspection can fail when independent evidence exists.

## Research implications for localisation

Use:

- accurate site terms
- campaign-specific target source
- concrete observations
- clear operator responsibility
- uncertainty when evidence is incomplete
- restrained descriptions of relief and discovery

Avoid:

- invented real quotations
- procedural killing descriptions
- graphic detail
- treating victims as an economic resource in narration
- calling every atrocity genocide
- generic racial census claims
- claiming alternate-history nerve-agent use was historical
- jokes, memes, or horror spectacle

## Archival asset research direction

Preferred visual subjects:

- exterior gate or fence
- barracks or empty site
- railway or transport infrastructure
- forced-labour work scene with known provenance
- records and investigation
- liberation and medical relief
- displaced-person accommodation
- tribunal or evidence presentation

Avoid:

- unidentified images
- images falsely attributed to a different country or camp
- graphic bodies
- generated documentary photography
- perpetrator propaganda used without context
- watermarked commercial images without a valid licence

## Research limits

- This research does not quantify one universal camp mortality rate.
- It does not provide a complete country-by-country camp registry.
- It does not justify a generic ethnicity model.
- It does not validate a specific HOI4 balance value.
- It does not replace local vanilla and repository source inspection.
- It does not authorize any archival image until licence and provenance are checked by the asset researcher.

Exact game numbers therefore remain tuning anchors subject to implementation and probability testing.
