# Event 22 Concentration Camps

## Part 4: Discovery, liberation, and aftermath

## Discovery purpose

Camp operation creates damage before the world can prove who is responsible. Discovery turns hidden evidence into public knowledge, links individual sites into one network, activates foreign reactions, and creates postwar accountability.

Discovery must preserve the existing shared rule:

- hidden deaths and evidence accumulate while the responsible regime controls the network
- public Condemnation does not rise from a whole-world passive scan
- a verified leak, inspection, occupation, liberation, regime change, or physical discovery exposes evidence
- public consequences are attributed to the actor that built, operated, converted, or concealed the site

The discovery system should reward investigation and evidence preservation without treating the suffering as a collectible resource.

## Evidence model

### State evidence

Each registered state can hold evidence from several sources:

- camp construction and operation
- detainee intake and transfer records
- forced-labour assignments
- local deaths and disappearances
- extermination operation
- restricted chemical operation
- medical or biological experiments
- confiscated property
- guard, administrator, railway, police, military, or company records
- survivor and witness testimony
- physical remains and destroyed-site traces
- cover-up attempts
- forced evacuation or death-march records

The state evidence ledger should record source types and responsible actors. It should not store only one generic number when the source affects later reactions.

### Country evidence

The responsible country's hidden evidence combines:

- attributed state evidence
- `genocide_deaths`
- `genocide_escalation`
- `genocide_visibility`
- `genocide_coverup_effort`
- network reach
- extermination-site count
- discovered-site count
- experiment and restricted chemical sources
- documentary centralization
- responsibility transfers

The shared Condemnation system should receive a bounded public source when evidence becomes verified. It should not receive the same evidence every pulse.

### Evidence confidence

Evidence should have a confidence state:

- fragmentary report
- corroborated testimony
- physical or documentary proof
- verified site
- verified network

A destroyed record can lower one documentary component, but it cannot erase survivor testimony, physical evidence, population loss, transport records held elsewhere, or foreign intelligence.

## Discovery triggers

Discovery is event-driven. It can begin through one or more of these triggers.

### Liberation or hostile occupation

A country that gains control of a registered camp state checks for:

- an active camp building
- an abandoned or destroyed-site marker
- undiscovered state evidence
- surviving detainees
- recent forced evacuation
- contamination or epidemic risk
- the recorded responsible actor

This is the strongest normal discovery route. The new controller does not become responsible merely by entering the state. It becomes responsible only if it continues or reuses the operation.

### Escapee or survivor testimony

A successful escape, mass release, front collapse, or refugee arrival can reveal:

- the existence of one site
- a transfer route
- names of officials or guards
- links to other sites
- a killing, labour, experiment, or chemical policy

The first testimony can remain fragmentary. Multiple independent witnesses, documents, photographs, or physical access raise confidence.

### Foreign intelligence

Foreign intelligence can discover transport, construction, missing-person, industrial, aerial, radio, or diplomatic evidence.

The investigation should require a valid target, intelligence capacity, access, or a specific campaign incident. It must not be a free global reveal decision.

### Inspection

A government under restrictive review can invite or accept inspectors. A foreign country, faction, humanitarian organization, or neutral commission can request access after credible reports.

Inspection outcomes depend on:

- actual access
- cover-up effort
- site condition
- intimidation of witnesses
- preserved records
- intelligence support
- whether the network is still operating

A staged inspection can delay exposure, but later discovery adds cover-up evidence.

### Resistance capture

A resistance group can seize records, free detainees, or briefly occupy a site. This creates a discovery attempt and can trigger a relief crisis if the group lacks food, medicine, transport, or secure territory.

### Natural disaster or infrastructure failure

Earthquakes, floods, fires, landslides, storms, industrial accidents, or other Event 13 disasters can:

- damage fences and buildings
- expose remains or hidden structures
- scatter records
- create escape opportunities
- interrupt food, water, and medicine
- reveal the site to neighboring states or foreign observers

The disaster system owns physical damage. Event 22 owns the camp evidence and survivor consequences.

### Bombing or front movement

Strategic bombing, nuclear damage, artillery, or a moving front can expose or destroy a site. The responsible country receives an emergency decision before retreat when the campaign gives enough notice.

Bombing deaths must be separated from camp-operation deaths when the cause can be distinguished.

### Regime change and internal disclosure

A successor government, civil-war rival, military coup, or released subject can open archives, arrest administrators, or expose its predecessor. The evidence remains attributed to the actor that operated the network.

A successor that continues operation creates a new responsibility generation.

## Discovery sequence

### First credible report

The affected foreign or domestic observer receives a report whose text direction uses partial information, frightened witnesses, missing people, transport patterns, or physical traces. It should not announce that the player is seeing a warning. It should show the evidence that makes the report serious.

Possible responses:

- investigate discreetly
- support witnesses or escapees
- demand access
- dismiss or suppress the report
- pass the information to an ally, faction, or press network

### Site verification

When one site is physically or documentarily verified:

- mark the state discovered
- expose its state-specific evidence source
- identify the responsible actor when the evidence supports it
- add the bounded public Condemnation source
- update the Event 22 history
- reveal foreign reaction decisions appropriate to confidence and severity
- increase the chance that linked sites are found

### Network verification

A network becomes verified when evidence links several sites, central orders, shared transport, a common administrator, or a national policy.

Network verification:

- exposes a larger bounded public source
- updates the responsible country's crisis idea
- can trigger arms restrictions, sanctions, intelligence cooperation, or diplomatic isolation through the shared Condemnation system
- unlocks tribunal and documentation pressure
- changes AI strategy for allies, enemies, subjects, and neutral powers
- causes state-specific discoveries to remain meaningful without repeating the full national spike

### Full documentation

Full documentation requires broad site access, preserved records, testimony, and responsibility evidence. It supports the strongest postwar accountability and survivor-tracing outcomes.

It should be difficult to achieve when the network was destroyed, the front moved rapidly, or many survivors were killed. It should remain possible through multiple evidence sources. One document is insufficient.

## Public reaction

Foreign reaction should depend on evidence confidence, network severity, ideology, alliance, war state, distance, and Condemnation. A government should not automatically declare war because one rumour exists.

### Investigation support

Eligible countries can:

- share intelligence
- protect witnesses
- fund documentation teams
- provide reconnaissance or translation support
- coordinate with a liberating country

The action costs intelligence capacity, political attention, relations, or transport. It raises confidence and can expose linked sites.

### Humanitarian reception

Eligible neighboring or allied countries can:

- admit escapees and survivors
- provide food, medicine, transport, or shelter
- create temporary displaced-person capacity
- assist family tracing

These actions reduce deaths and relief pressure. They can also create domestic political, supply, and housing costs.

### Diplomatic protest and access demand

A country can demand inspection or public explanation after credible evidence. The responsible regime may accept, restrict, stage, or reject access.

Rejection raises suspicion and foreign pressure. It does not directly prove every allegation.

### Sanctions and military restrictions

Verified severe evidence can contribute to the existing Condemnation thresholds for:

- diplomatic concern
- formal censure
- arms embargo
- strategic restrictions
- total isolation
- pariah status

Event 22 should add its source to the shared system and let that system own the final restrictions. It must not create duplicate sanction mechanics.

### Rescue and protection operations

A country at war with the responsible regime can prioritize capturing or protecting a known site. Possible actions include:

- assign a liberation objective
- drop limited supplies when air access exists
- coordinate with resistance
- prepare medical units
- secure evidence teams

These actions should have real military, air, intelligence, or supply costs. They should not create a separate combat minigame.

## Liberation state transition

When a non-continuing controller takes a camp state, organized camp operation stops immediately. The state enters a liberation transition.

### Immediate checks

The liberation effect determines:

- surviving detained population
- current deaths and medical emergency
- food and water condition
- disease or contamination
- active guards or armed administrators
- escape and missing-person status
- surviving records
- physical evidence
- recent evacuation
- unexploded or damaged infrastructure
- responsibility actor and network generation

### Immediate state status

The active camp assignment is removed. The state receives one of these statuses:

- liberated site with survivors
- abandoned site with survivors
- emptied site after forced evacuation
- destroyed site with evidence
- contaminated liberated site
- mass-grave or physical-evidence site
- site with uncertain records

The physical camp building can remain temporarily as a location marker while relief and evidence work proceeds. It must not continue granting the former operator's bonuses.

## Liberation decisions and missions

### Secure the site

Purpose: stop guard violence, looting, revenge killing, record destruction, and uncontrolled movement through a dangerous area.

Requirements:

- control of a liberated site
- available divisions, police, or local authority

Costs and commitments:

- temporary unit or garrison commitment
- support equipment
- command or administrative capacity

Success:

- protects survivors and records
- lowers immediate disorder
- prevents the new controller from accidentally reusing the site

Failure:

- records disappear
- collaborators flee
- survivors scatter without support
- disease and revenge violence increase

### Emergency food and medical relief

Purpose: reduce post-liberation deaths.

Dynamic costs:

- food or civilian supply abstraction
- trucks or trains
- support equipment
- temporary civilian-factory burden

Effects:

- lowers mortality and disease
- stabilizes survivor condition
- reduces Resistance Pressure in the state
- raises relief capacity

The action should be urgent and meaningful. A delayed liberator can still face deaths after organized killing has stopped.

### Quarantine and decontaminate

Available only when disease, biological contamination, chemical contamination, or severe sanitation collapse exists.

The action must distinguish humane medical isolation from arbitrary detention. It should use medical and contamination systems and include a release condition.

### Register survivors and missing persons

Purpose: create a reliable survivor and missing-person ledger.

Effects:

- improves family tracing and resettlement
- preserves testimony
- raises evidence confidence
- reduces later identity and displacement confusion
- makes transparent closure possible

Destroyed records increase the cost and duration.

### Preserve the site and records

Purpose: protect documentary and physical evidence.

Effects:

- raises evidence confidence
- blocks local demolition until the investigation ends
- supports tribunal outcomes
- can impose a temporary construction or land-use restriction

The action should avoid turning the site into a decorative reward. Preservation is a documentation and accountability choice.

### Transfer survivors to safe accommodation

Purpose: move people from the camp into medical, temporary housing, family, or resettlement systems.

Requirements:

- safe destination capacity
- transport
- no active epidemic or an approved medical transport plan

Effects:

- reduces local overcrowding
- creates displaced-person or return missions
- can move survivors across borders with receiving-country approval

### Dismantle the site after documentation

Purpose: remove the operational infrastructure once relief and evidence work are complete.

Requirements:

- survivors transferred or safely accommodated
- evidence preserved or lawfully transferred
- no contamination blocker
- no continuing operation

Effects:

- removes the camp building
- ends the site's active Event 22 status
- can convert the land to a memorial, ordinary civilian use, or another locally appropriate state status through future content

## Displaced persons and migration

Liberation and closure can create a large displaced population. Event 22 should provide a bridge to the planned migration system instead of instantly returning everyone to a previous state.

### Population destinations

Survivors can:

- return to a safe home state
- reunite with family in another state or country
- remain temporarily near the liberation site
- move to an allied or neutral receiving country
- become long-term displaced persons when borders, destroyed homes, ideology, or security make return unsafe

### Destination selection

Destination priority should consider:

- surviving core or home marker when available
- current controller and security
- family or refugee links
- border access
- transport capacity
- receiving-country policy
- housing, food, and medical capacity
- war and occupation state

The game must not invent individual identities or precise ethnic origins where no registry exists.

### Migration effects

Migration should transfer or represent population through the migration system without duplicating the original camp death. Possible consequences include:

- temporary population gain in receiving states
- housing and supply pressure
- skilled-worker or community recovery after stabilization
- political testimony and public discovery
- return missions after peace
- statelessness or border disputes
- resistance recruitment when return is blocked

### Refugee and family events

Possible event directions:

- families search transport lists
- a receiving state cannot house new arrivals
- survivors refuse return to a hostile successor government
- two countries dispute citizenship or return rights
- children or separated families require tracing
- witnesses choose whether to testify publicly

These events should focus on choices and consequences. They should not use sensational descriptions.

## Retreat and forced evacuation

A responsible regime approaching loss of a camp state receives an emergency mission when enough warning exists.

### Close and leave survivors in place

- stops operation
- preserves the chance of liberation relief
- leaves strong evidence
- can trigger hardliner resistance
- reduces retreat deaths

### Order an organized transfer

- requires trains, convoys, guards, food, and a valid receiving site
- can preserve detainee lives if genuinely supplied and de-escalatory
- increases exposure and escape risk
- can become a lethal evacuation when transport or supplies fail

### Force an evacuation on foot

This route represents death-march conditions.

- used when transport is absent or deliberately withheld
- creates high deaths, escape, witness, and evidence pressure
- can spread the crisis across several states
- may be interrupted by enemy advance, resistance, weather, famine, or disaster
- stores the route and responsible actor for later discovery

The mechanic remains strategic. It should not depict procedural killing methods.

### Kill remaining detainees

Available only to an active extermination or extreme cover-up route.

- creates an immediate exact population loss
- adds the strongest local atrocity and cover-up evidence
- can delay survivor testimony but cannot erase physical or documentary evidence
- raises military objection, administrator flight, and internal collapse risk
- makes later discovery much more damaging

### Destroy infrastructure and records

- can remove or damage the building
- creates destroyed-site evidence
- can kill people still present
- may contaminate the area if chemical or biological systems are involved
- reduces some records and raises cover-up evidence

### Abandon the site

- leaves survivors, guards, records, and supplies unresolved
- ends organized operation
- creates a high-risk liberation state
- can cause deaths from neglect before relief arrives

## Civil war and regime collapse

### Split responsibility

When Event 21 creates a civil war:

- every site keeps its original pre-split responsibility record
- each side becomes responsible for operation after it gains control and chooses to continue
- network values are partitioned by controlled sites
- hidden evidence remains linked to the original regime and later operators
- extermination shocks are not reapplied because a state changed side

### Rebel discovery

A rebel government can expose the former regime's sites to gain legitimacy, foreign support, or intelligence cooperation. It must still provide relief and avoid continuing operation.

A rebel faction that uses the sites gains its own responsibility generation and loses the clean-liberator status.

### Coup or peaceful transition

A successor government chooses among:

- immediate transparent closure
- restrictive review
- temporary secure administration pending release
- concealment of predecessor crimes
- continued forced labour
- continued extermination

The choice affects liability, diplomacy, internal security, and refire protection.

### Annexation and puppet transfer

Annexation does not erase evidence. The annexer receives management and relief responsibility for sites it controls. It becomes an atrocity operator only if it continues or reactivates the network.

A puppet master can be recorded as a contributing responsible actor when it ordered, supplied, staffed, or forced continued operation through an explicit campaign effect.

## Tribunals and accountability

### Tribunal eligibility

Tribunal content becomes available when:

- at least one site or network is publicly verified
- a responsible actor has lost control, changed government, surrendered, or accepted investigation
- sufficient evidence identifies institutions or officials
- a valid investigating or victorious country exists

### Evidence categories

Accountability can use:

- central orders
- transport and construction records
- labour contracts
- camp administration records
- military or police reports
- survivor testimony
- physical evidence
- chemical or experiment evidence
- cover-up orders
- responsibility-transfer records

The strongest outcome should require several evidence categories. One captured note should not automatically prove the whole network.

### Responsible institutions

The system can identify broad institutions without creating hundreds of characters:

- head of government or ruling leadership
- security service
- camp administration
- military command
- occupation administration
- railway or transport authority
- collaborating local authority
- concession company or industrial partner
- scientific or medical program

Named historical figures require sourced character work and should remain in country-specific packages.

### Postwar choices

The investigating or successor government can:

- conduct public trials
- cooperate with an international or allied tribunal
- prosecute only direct operators
- preserve records but grant limited amnesty
- obstruct investigation
- destroy or hide remaining evidence
- trade officials or documents for diplomatic concessions

Consequences should affect legitimacy, Condemnation, relations, intelligence, internal factions, and survivor confidence. The system should not turn trials into a large political-power reward.

### Liability outcome

Possible outcomes:

- comprehensive accountability
- partial trials
- domestic purge without public process
- negotiated amnesty
- unresolved missing-person crisis
- cover-up and later rediscovery
- perpetrators flee abroad
- collaborator conflict
- victor obstruction

A country can reduce future diplomatic pressure through genuine accountability and relief. It cannot reverse deaths or erase the Event 22 history.

## Postwar reconstruction

### Survivor relief and resettlement

A country can run a long reconstruction program covering:

- medical recovery
- housing
- family tracing
- return or resettlement
- documentation and citizenship
- restoration of confiscated property where possible
- support for witnesses
- local economic reintegration

The program should use a staged idea lifecycle. It begins as a burden and can become a recovery institution after successful missions.

### Camp-state recovery

Former camp states can retain:

- population loss
- damaged infrastructure
- disease or contamination
- labour-market disruption
- resistance and local distrust
- destroyed housing
- evidence-preservation status
- memorial or documentation role

Recovery should require local construction, relief, and time. Removing the building should not instantly remove every state consequence.

### Reparations and restitution

Where the campaign supports it, a responsible successor can provide:

- financial or equipment restitution
- return of confiscated assets
- rebuilding support
- transport for return or resettlement
- official records and citizenship restoration

These actions cost real resources and improve legitimacy or relations. They should not be mandatory in every alternate-history legal order, but obstruction should remain visible.

| Restrictive review | suspended | supplied and reviewed | partly opened | unresolved | can move toward closure or renewed repression |
| Secret demolition | removed or destroyed | uncertain | cover-up evidence | concealed | later rediscovery and missing-person pressure |

## Event and report families

Use working labels only. Final localisation should be written during implementation from the stated direction.

### Domestic report families

- network activation
- camp assignment selected
- labour quota collapse
- epidemic or famine in a site
- escape and sabotage
- military objection
- administrator corruption
- extermination conversion
- target source exhausted
- purge broadens inward
- restricted chemical accident
- forced evacuation
- transparent closure
- underground continuation

### Foreign report families

- first witness account
- transport or aerial evidence
- inspection request
- staged inspection
- verified site
- verified network
- liberation report
- survivor reception
- missing-person lists
- tribunal opening
- evidence obstruction

### News-event families

Use news only for public thresholds that matter beyond one country:

- verified large network
- liberation of a major network
- widespread tribunal or accountability process
- public exposure of a restricted chemical or experiment network

A small local site discovery should remain a report or country event.

## Player-facing text direction

### Perpetrator-side text

Viewpoint:

- cabinet, security administration, military logistics, local officials, or political leadership

Tone:

- severe, bureaucratic, self-incriminating, evasive, or ideological depending on route

Show:

- people being moved, output demands, missing supplies, resistance, records, deaths, and political conflict

Avoid:

- comic language
- spectacle
- victim-blaming narration
- euphemisms that conceal the mechanic from the player
- procedural descriptions of killing
- generic remarks that the situation is a warning

### Victim, witness, or liberator-side text

Viewpoint:

- survivors, families, medics, soldiers, local officials, investigators, or receiving communities

Tone:

- factual, grave, uncertain where evidence is incomplete

Show:

- condition of survivors, missing people, transport lists, physical site state, relief needs, and conflicting evidence

Avoid:

- graphic detail
- invented testimony presented as a real quotation
- voyeuristic suffering
- triumphal liberation that ignores continuing deaths and displacement

### Option direction

Important options should communicate the actor's stance:

- closure and responsibility
- restrictive review
- coercive exploitation
- ideological escalation
- concealment
- relief
- investigation
- obstruction

Atrocity options can use cold official euphemism or grim self-damning irony. They should not use cheap jokes.

## Cleanup and persistence

### State cleanup

A state leaves the active operation loop only after:

- no active concentration, extermination, or participating gulag operation remains
- no forced-labour assignment remains
- no active intake remains
- residual population-loss pulse is stopped
- responsibility and evidence have been archived to persistent history
- relief or abandonment status has been set

Do not clear evidence merely because the building is removed.

### Country cleanup

A country leaves the active Event 22 country loop only after:

- no attributed active sites remain under its operation
- no covert local continuation remains
- no evacuation mission remains
- no restricted chemical operation remains
- country pulse and decision category are stopped
- outstanding relief, tribunal, or discovery hooks are handed to their owning systems

### Tag and controller changes

Every controller, owner, overlord, civil-war, cosmetic-tag, annexation, and government transition must preserve:

- state evidence
- original responsible actor
- later operator generations
- evolution shock markers
- survivor and relief status
- discovery status
- Deaths ledger history

### Save and reload

Save and reload must not:

- recreate removed sites
- reapply percentage shocks
- lose responsible actors
- duplicate Condemnation sources
- restart completed missions
- clear discovery
- restore exhausted labour pools
- remove liberated-site relief status

## Edge-case rules

- A country with one eligible state receives one baseline concentration camp. Evolution I conversion is policy-gated unless an extreme route makes it automatic.
- A state at the protected population floor receives no further percentage shock, but ongoing systems still record its crisis state and can require closure.
- A state controlled by an ally of the responsible country remains the ally's management problem only if the ally continues operation. Supply or orders from the original country can preserve contributing responsibility.
- A site destroyed by nuclear attack can still leave evidence and contamination. Nuclear deaths remain attributed to the nuclear cause, while pre-strike camp deaths remain Event 22 deaths.
- A nonhuman or special Chaos country should normally be excluded by `is_special_chaos_country`. A future exception needs a dedicated design because the civilian, legal, and demographic assumptions differ.
- If no valid investigator, liberator, or successor exists, evidence remains on the state until a later controller can discover it.
- A peace treaty that returns a camp state to its former operator can reactivate the site only through an explicit continuation effect. It should not resume automatically.
