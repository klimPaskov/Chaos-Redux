# AI, Country Programs, and Designers

## AI architecture

AI behavior is divided into five decisions:

1. whether to build defensive protection
2. whether to adopt Chaos Warfare
3. which delivery and protection roles to research and produce
4. whether to authorize and execute an operation
5. how to respond to contamination, evidence, and sanctions

The AI should not receive a hidden free arsenal. It must produce protection and payload, assign headquarters, and maintain viable templates.

## National CBRN posture types

### Civil Defence State

Priorities:

- masks
- shelters and distribution
- decontamination
- medical response
- retaliation only after confirmed use

Typical countries or routes:

- Britain
- defensive France
- democratic United States
- smaller exposed democracies

### Retaliatory Arsenal

Priorities:

- strong protection
- maintained chemical stockpile
- prepared air or artillery response
- low first-use willingness

Typical countries or routes:

- Britain
- France
- United States
- selected Soviet or German non-radical routes

### Battlefield Chemical Army

Priorities:

- artillery and projector delivery
- protected assault formations
- theater headquarters
- limited battlefield authority

Typical countries or routes:

- radical Germany
- Soviet military route
- Italy
- Japan in China
- high-chaos majors

### Strategic CBRN Power

Priorities:

- advanced agents
- chemical aircraft
- biological raids
- strong biosecurity and evidence control
- high sanction tolerance

Typical routes:

- extreme or late-war majors
- high-chaos routes
- special-project focused paths

### Desperate Release State

Priorities:

- use remaining arsenal near capitulation
- low concern for long-term contamination
- high self-harm tolerance

Available only through explicit route, ideology, or doomsday conditions.

## AI research priorities

AI scores technologies from:

- current posture
- enemy known capability
- own doctrine
- current stockpile
- military factory count
- special-project progress
- expected theater
- current protection gap
- Condemnation and sanction vulnerability

### Defensive research order

1. basic gas masks
2. improved masks
3. surveillance and detection
4. decontamination
5. medical countermeasures
6. advanced masks and sealed vehicles

### Offensive research order

1. one early agent class
2. one preferred delivery method
3. protective equipment
4. headquarters integration
5. shell or air payload logistics
6. advanced agent only after stockpile and protection are stable

AI should not research every agent sequentially merely because each node is visible.

## AI production ratios

Suggested first-pass national production targets after doctrine adoption:

| Item | Defensive posture | Battlefield posture | Strategic posture |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Gas-mask equipment | 4 to 8% of military IC | 5 to 9% | 5 to 10% |
| Decontamination equipment | 1 to 3% | 2 to 4% | 2 to 5% |
| CBRN instruments | 0.5 to 1.5% | 1 to 2% | 1 to 3% |
| Chemical payload | 0 to 1% | 2 to 5% | 3 to 7% |
| Chemical shell or air lots | 0 to 1% | 1 to 4% | 2 to 5% |
| Biological payload | 0% | 0 to 1% | 1 to 3% after project |

These are shares of available military production after minimum infantry, artillery, truck, and aircraft needs. A country in equipment deficit pauses offensive CBRN expansion before masks and ordinary army supply.

## AI template behavior

### Protected line infantry

Adds Gas Mask and Decontamination Detachment when enemy capability or confirmed use exists.

### Hazard assault

Adds Gas Mask, Hazard Pioneer, and possibly Chemical Reconnaissance. Uses Chaos Assault Battalion after mastery 3.

### Chemical artillery

Adds Chemical Ammunition Train and Projector Battery only when shell or payload reserve is sufficient.

### Armored breakthrough

Adds Armored Chemical Delivery and protective support. Avoids the unit when fuel or tank deficit is severe.

### Garrison

Uses Nerve Suppression only with policy permission and severe resistance. Defensive garrisons in contaminated states prefer masks and biosecurity.

### Headquarters

AI creates one CBRN-capable Army HQ before attempting major operations. It chooses support companies from theater role rather than adding all six.

## Operation target scoring

### Chemical battlefield operation

Score increases with:

- fortified or urban enemy position
- high enemy unit density
- low enemy mask coverage
- important supply hub
- stalled offensive
- favorable forecast
- protected friendly order

Score falls with:

- friendly state population
- allied units in target
- rapid front movement
- low payload
- high sanction vulnerability
- target already near capitulation
- advanced enemy protection

### Strategic chemical raid

Score increases with:

- capital or high-value supply state
- large aircraft force and air superiority
- retaliation status
- severe or high-chaos posture

Score falls sharply for neutral, allied, subject, or occupied friendly targets.

### Biological operation

Score increases with:

- strategic target value
- low target surveillance
- low spread risk back to attacker
- high Biosecurity
- covert network strength

Score falls with:

- shared border and uncontrolled movement
- weak domestic containment
- likely attribution
- high import dependence and sanction risk

## Country starting programs

Starting values should use formulas and profiles. The following are target identities.

### Britain

Starting profile:

- basic and improved protective technology where historically and technically appropriate
- highest civilian mask reserve
- strong meteorology and CBRN headquarters potential
- chemical retaliatory stockpile
- high defensive AI priority

Unique gameplay strength:

- efficient civilian distribution
- forecast confidence
- protected logistics

Tradeoff:

- low first-use willingness
- strong domestic and alliance reaction to unrestricted use

### France

Starting profile:

- strong First World War chemical experience
- military mask reserve
- artillery delivery readiness
- priority-city civil defence

Strength:

- fortified-front chemical defence and counterbattery

Tradeoff:

- doctrine investment competes with conventional army reform

### Germany

Starting profile:

- advanced chemical industry
- strong mask and military reserve
- nerve-agent research potential
- high secrecy and special-project routes

Strength:

- advanced agent and sealed-crew development

Tradeoff:

- advanced program creates accident, evidence, atrocity, and sanction risk

### Soviet Union

Starting profile:

- large military reserve
- broad but uneven civilian protection
- artillery and decontamination scale
- high industrial burden

Strength:

- mass theater protection and cleanup

Tradeoff:

- poor distribution efficiency and high wastage unless reformed

### United States

Starting profile:

- moderate reserve, limited public distribution
- strong industrial expansion capacity
- lewisite and medical research identity
- high retaliatory posture

Strength:

- rapid mass production after mobilisation
- advanced medical and air delivery

Tradeoff:

- low peacetime readiness and high political resistance to first use

### Italy

Starting profile:

- smaller masks and payload reserve
- operational use experience
- regional air and artillery delivery

Strength:

- cheap limited operations

Tradeoff:

- low sustained protection, payload, and cleanup capacity

### Japan

Starting profile:

- offensive biological specialisation
- limited civilian mask coverage
- chemical battlefield capacity in China
- program-specific scientists and facilities

Strength:

- biological operations and occupation-linked delivery

Tradeoff:

- catastrophic evidence and retaliation risk
- vulnerable home-island civil defence

### Poland, Czechoslovakia, Belgium, and the Netherlands

Starting profile:

- meaningful First World War or exposed-front protection
- limited offensive stockpile
- strong defensive AI

Strength:

- early mask issue and civil-defence decisions

Tradeoff:

- limited industry for advanced delivery

## Minor-country access

Smaller countries can participate through:

- licensed mask production
- protective aid
- defensive CBRN headquarters
- chemical reconnaissance
- decontamination units
- retaliation stockpiles

They should not need a major-power industry to protect themselves. Advanced offensive programs remain expensive.

## Military Industrial Organizations and designers

Final implementation should use current MIO or designer patterns and existing country firms when available. Working families follow.

### Protective Equipment Consortium

Trait themes:

- mass respirator production
- reliable filters
- protective clothing
- tropical comfort
- civilian fitting program
- sealed crew sets

Tradeoffs:

- mass production can lower reliability
- sealed sets increase cost

### Chemical Munitions Combine

Trait themes:

- shell filling
- payload stability
- projector ammunition
- persistent-agent formulation
- reduced storage accident risk

Tradeoffs:

- higher production cost
- stronger evidence after captured standardized munitions

### Mobile Decontamination Works

Trait themes:

- truck-mounted wash systems
- route clearance
- vehicle decontamination
- low fuel use
- rapid field repair

### Aerosol and Air Delivery Bureau

Trait themes:

- lighter module installation
- reduced aircraft agility loss
- payload reliability
- raid accuracy
- improved dispersal control

Tradeoffs:

- lower payload volume or higher cost

### Biological Security Directorate

Trait themes:

- containment safety
- surveillance
- secure transport
- field sampling
- accident prevention

Offensive weaponization traits should sit on a separate branch with higher accident and evidence risk.

### Medical Countermeasure Directorate

Trait themes:

- antidote production
- burn treatment
- respiratory care
- vaccination scale
- mobile hospital support

## Country-specific designer rule

- use existing in-game firms or documented historical institutions where possible
- source historical names before localisation
- do not invent historical corporations
- generic designer names are acceptable for countries without a researched unique institution
- each major program should receive at least one protective and one offensive or medical identity if its content supports them

## AI designer choice

AI selects designers according to posture:

- defensive countries prefer protection, medical, and decontamination
- artillery countries prefer munitions and route cleanup
- air powers prefer air delivery after protection is adequate
- biological powers prefer security before weaponization
- desperate routes can choose high-output unsafe traits

## Sanction response AI

At high Condemnation, AI can:

- accept inspections
- destroy or mothball stockpiles
- shift to defensive production
- pursue autarky
- seek faction shielding
- use black-market procurement
- continue unrestricted escalation

The decision depends on ideology, war state, economic dependence, program investment, and chance of victory.

## AI validation scenarios

- Britain faces confirmed German chemical use and expands masks, headquarters protection, and retaliation reserve.
- Germany with nerve project but no masks does not launch a mass offensive.
- Japan in China uses theater-gated biological operations only under the intended route.
- Soviet AI builds decontamination and artillery capacity rather than every air module.
- United States mobilises mass mask production after first confirmed use.
- a minor under chemical attack requests aid and protects its capital rather than attempting an unaffordable offensive program.

## Acceptance criteria

- each major country feels different
- AI protects before it attacks
- AI does not research every agent without a plan
- production ratios respond to shortages
- headquarters and templates are fielded before operations
- designers have distinct trait trees and tradeoffs
- historical names are sourced rather than invented
