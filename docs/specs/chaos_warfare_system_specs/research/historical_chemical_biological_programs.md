# Historical Chemical and Biological Program Profiles

## Purpose

This note guides country differentiation without turning the design into a claim that every planned gameplay capability existed exactly as represented.

## General historical anchors

- First World War belligerents retained doctrine, protective equipment, laboratories, and industrial knowledge after mass chemical warfare.
- Interwar policy often combined defensive preparation, retaliation planning, treaty commitments, and secret offensive research.
- Britain, France, Germany, the Soviet Union, Italy, Japan, and the United States had distinct levels of chemical or biological capability, readiness, and political willingness.
- Japanese biological warfare programs and human experimentation require careful separation between historical program content and fictional gameplay escalation.
- German nerve-agent research creates a plausible route to advanced agents, but widespread battlefield deployment did not occur historically.
- The United States and Britain developed large defensive and retaliatory capacities while publicly framing use around retaliation.
- Soviet scale supports large reserves and dispersed programs, but documentation quality and exact readiness estimates vary.

## Gameplay profiles

### Britain

Profile: mass civil defence, mature meteorology, protected logistics, retaliatory chemical reserve, strong medical research.

Preferred AI: protection first, retaliation after confirmed enemy use, strategic air delivery only under severe escalation.

### France

Profile: First World War experience, strong military protection, prepared artillery doctrine, defensive use around fortified fronts.

Preferred AI: heavy protection, prepared fire support, restrained first use unless homeland collapse is near.

### Germany

Profile: advanced chemistry, nerve-agent potential, protected mobile operations, high secrecy and evidence control.

Preferred AI: doctrine adoption under radical or desperate leadership, advanced agents after special projects, high sanction tolerance on extreme routes.

### Soviet Union

Profile: large reserve, mass issue, dispersed production, theater decontamination, willingness to accept severe military burden on radical routes.

Preferred AI: large-scale protection and artillery use, high logistical investment, lower precision, strong cleanup capacity if industrially prepared.

### Italy

Profile: smaller stockpile, colonial precedent, limited protection and logistics, regional air and artillery delivery.

Preferred AI: opportunistic use against weaker or poorly protected enemies, limited endurance, greater supply sensitivity.

### Japan

Profile: offensive biological program, occupation-linked experimentation, limited civilian protection, operational use in China under radical policy.

Preferred AI: biological and chemical escalation in the China theater, strict theater and political gates, severe international evidence risk.

### United States

Profile: strong industry, lewisite and defensive research, late mobilisation, high potential for mass protective production and advanced medical countermeasures.

Preferred AI: low first-use willingness, rapid response after enemy use, high capacity for air delivery and civilian protection once mobilised.

## Designer naming rule

The spec uses generic designer families such as Protective Equipment Consortium and Biological Security Directorate. Country-specific final names should use existing in-game firms, documented agencies, or researched historical institutions. Do not invent a real-sounding company and present it as historical.

## Sensitive-content boundary

Military CBRN doctrine, occupation suppression, genocide infrastructure, and biological experimentation must remain distinct systems. Historical atrocity programs can connect through evidence and Condemnation, but they should not be treated as ordinary doctrine rewards.
