## Historical research and design inferences

The design separates attested context from alternate-history mechanics. The research below informs patron conflict, political plurality, institutional imagery and the treatment of regional claims. It does not establish that historical IMRO created a worldwide Front or adopted the fictional death doctrine.

### H01. Interwar patronage

Petra Hamerli's archival study describes Italian and Hungarian dealings with IMRO as part of their Balkan policies and discusses the movement's own interests and factional divisions. This supports a game relationship in which useful foreign aid can come with competing goals. The contract, repayment and refusal mechanics are design inferences, not historical simulations of particular deals. The study was used selectively for its interwar diplomatic material. [Hamerli, *Forum Historiae* 15, no. 1, 2021, DOI 10.31577/forhist.2021.15.1.3](https://www.forumhistoriae.sk/sites/default/files/forhist.2021.15.1.3.pdf).

### H02. Anarchist context without conflation

Alp Yenen and Ramazan Hakkı Öztan distinguish the Gemidzhii as an anarchist group with loose connections to IMRO and the Supremacists. That supports acknowledging a regional anarchist context while avoiding the claim that the entire organisation shared one anarchist programme. The game's durable commune constitution, elected command and production compacts are explicit alternate-history design. Relevant discussion appears in their introductory chapter, printed pages 17 to 18. [*Age of Rogues*, Edinburgh University Press, 2021](https://edinburghuniversitypress.com/pub/media/ebooks/9781474462648.pdf).

### H03. Local banners

Marija Georgieva's study states that the Ilinden detachments did not share one universal flag and describes local banner traditions. The design therefore keeps identified local banners attached to their actual contexts and treats a later national adoption as a fictional political decision. The complete six-page article was retrieved and its relevant text read. [Georgieva, “The Flags and Seals from the Ilinden Uprising in 1903,” *Istorija* 20, no. 2, 1984](https://www.zirm.mk/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/IST-1984.2.32-Georgieva-M.-Znaminjata-i-grbovite-od-vremeto-na-Ilindenskoto-vostanie.pdf).

### H04. Heraldic context and distinct civic proposals

Jovan Jonovski's monograph surveys regional revolutionary symbols and later civic-state proposals. The asset specialist read the relevant historical sections and distinguished the St. Petersburg colony's proposal from IMRO material. The parent opened the source and verified publication and contextual material. Its illustrations are modern reproductions with their own rights. It is a reference for further exact design selection, not permission to copy the book's images. [Jonovski, *The Sun and the Lion as Symbols of the Republic of Macedonia*, Flag Heritage Foundation, 2020](https://heraldika.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/jonovski-ms-full.pdf).

### H05. A specific museum banner

The Bulgarian National Museum of Military History identifies a silk painted banner dated 1902 with Ivan Tsonchev's detachment, accession 012 VA359. This is evidence for that object, not a universal IMRO flag. Its modern museum photograph has no open reuse licence established by this pass. [Museum object record](https://militarymuseum.bg/item/zname-ot-ilindensko-preobrazhenskoto-vastanie-1903-g/).

### H06. Different organisations and interpretations

Vančo Ǵorǵiev's article distinguishes the Sofia-based Supreme Macedonian Committee from the internal organisation based in Thessalonica. This supports keeping related organisations and their symbols separate in the asset and political register. The article's national and political interpretations remain attributed to its author. [Ǵorǵiev, “Bulgaria, for or against the Ilinden uprising,” *Glasnik* 59, 2015](https://repository.ukim.mk/bitstream/20.500.12188/22586/1/Gjorgjiev2015.pdf).

### H07. IMRO United institutional material

The Commons catalog identifies a 1925 IMRO United statute scan sourced to a 1991 document collection. It is useful as a distinct institutional document reference. The page's public-domain labels and missing US-specific tag note must be reviewed together before reuse. The scan alone is not a complete account of the organisation's later politics. [Statute catalog and scan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:IMRO-united-statute.jpg).

### H08. Autonomy document material

A Commons scan credited to the State Archive of North Macedonia is cataloged as a 1920 executive-committee letter about autonomous Macedonia. Its page describes the reproduction as CC BY-SA 4.0. This supports a sourced paper-and-institution visual reference, with precise authorship and translation still needing verification before any quotation. [Archive-provided letter scan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%D0%9F%D0%B8%D1%81%D0%BC%D0%BE_%D0%BE%D0%B4_%D0%98%D0%B7%D0%B2%D1%80%D1%88%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BE%D1%82_%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%82_%D0%B4%D0%BE_%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BA._%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B0_1920_-_1.JPG).

Historical research was targeted rather than a full reading of every cited book or archive collection. In particular, the 426-page *Age of Rogues* volume was used for pertinent sections, not claimed as fully read. Several direct Sofia University article routes, a guessed Britannica route and an attempted Hoover document route were inaccessible. They are not cited as if their full contents were read. The accessible university-press source supplies the narrow anarchist-context point used here.

The familiar red-and-black interwar flag remains a provisional historical design candidate because the readily available drawings lead through later secondary and modern political references. Exact period attribution, final flag geometry, full character biographies, final quotations, cultural remarks and recording licences remain implementation research gates. No unverified candidate has been promoted to a final historical asset.
