# Underground organisation and the Macedonian uprising

## A movement with a location

The opening selects a verified Macedonian territorial state or connected group of states under another country's control. National ownership, military occupation and an existing subject relationship are recorded separately. A foreign occupier cannot negotiate away land owned by a third country without that owner's settlement participation. If the controller changes while the movement is preparing, the cells remain in their territory and the new controller inherits the appropriate warning and response obligations.

The territorial registry distinguishes the Vardar homeland, the Pirin claim area and the Aegean claim area. These are political claims and regional categories, not a statement that every resident shares an identity or wants IMRO rule. Exact installed-map state IDs and the treatment of mixed states are an implementation evidence gate. The country part defines the default homeland and expansion rule. A script must never guess modern borders from a state name.

The origin needs at least one inhabited, eligible land state with a viable capital province and access to a support base. An active independent Macedonia with meaningful existing institutions uses the integration opening instead. It receives an internal IMRO political movement and external territorial contacts, not a second Macedonian country on top of its territory. If that country is already IMRO-led, the event attaches its new systems to the existing government once. If no eligible origin exists, the automatic candidate stays ineligible and consumes neither the fire-once record nor its cost.

## Five opening situations

| Situation | What the movement receives | What the controller sees | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet foreign administration | Small local cells, limited arms, uncertain public support | An intelligence warning with incomplete strength information | Longest preparation window and the strongest peaceful containment opportunity |
| Active occupation resistance | Existing contacts and locally attributable cadres, a resistance-derived readiness benefit | A linked warning that distinguishes the wider resistance from IMRO | Better mobilisation, with no automatic transfer of all resistance support |
| Controller at war | Shorter windows for movement and procurement, a higher chance of useful military material | A choice between diverting security capacity and making concessions | Faster preparation if there is a real front and supply disruption |
| Recent repression or broken autonomy | Survivors of the affected communities and an enforceable grievance record | Evidence that earlier policy strengthened the independence case | More Mandate potential and recruitment, alongside depleted population and damaged infrastructure |
| Independent Macedonia already exists | Domestic organisation, a constitutional challenge and contacts in remaining claim areas | Existing Macedonian player chooses lawful participation, coalition or opposition response | Existing national setup survives. No tag replacement, free civil war or cloned leader |

A foreign sponsor may add a delivery or a promised route to any eligible opening. The promise alone grants no rifles, divisions or Organisation. Delivery success, interception and debt are resolved explicitly.

## Opening strength

Use the common tuning constants to calculate a preparation band. The inputs are current Chaos, local resistance, actual war exposure, recorded repression, retained movement infrastructure, paid and delivered foreign support, and eligible prior event receipts. A single incident cannot contribute twice through resistance, repression and its originating event record.

| Band | Typical conditions | Organisation at discovery | Mandate at discovery | Initial military potential |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fragile | Calm controller, little resistance, no delivered support | 15 to 25 | 25 to 40 | One to two understrength irregular formations if the rising is attempted immediately |
| Established | Useful contacts and one substantial support condition | 30 to 45 | 40 to 55 | Two to four formations after real recruitment and arms allocation |
| Dangerous | Several independent support conditions or high Chaos with local opportunity | 50 to 65 | 45 to 65 | Four to six formations with limited trained cadres |
| Exceptional | Extreme Chaos plus strong territorial resistance and delivered support | 65 to 80 | 50 to 70 | Six to ten formations only where manpower, equipment and territorial supply support them |

Mandate is not a monotonic Chaos reward. The radicalised or patron-dependent opening may have high Organisation and poor Mandate. Ten formations are an upper opening target, not a guarantee. The force budget also obeys the actual population, recruitable manpower, equipment and supply limits defined in the country part. A high band with insufficient resources produces a better organised smaller force and stronger procurement opportunities. It cannot create ten fully equipped divisions from an empty province.

## The controller's first decision

The controller receives a meaningful report before a scheduled revolt whenever it has functioning administration in the origin. Its directions convey what is known, the affected territory, civilian grievances and uncertainty. It receives three core approaches and may use supporting actions during the response mission.

**Targeted containment** commits security capacity to intelligence, arrests of identified organisers and disruption of arms routes. It reduces Organisation when supported by sufficient administration and an actual security commitment. It avoids automatic civilian loss. Overextension and poor intelligence can produce a failed operation, after which IMRO gains evidence of the controller's weakness rather than a fabricated massacre.

**Autonomy negotiations** open a compact with concrete terms: local administration, a defined language and civic-rights policy, a protected assembly, demobilisation conditions and a review date. The owner of the territory must participate when it differs from the military controller. IMRO may accept, ask for stronger guarantees or continue preparing. Acceptance lowers immediate risk, but a later material breach reactivates the existing event chain. An honest compact can permanently settle the uprising without making Macedonia independent yet.

**Military confrontation** prepares garrisons and supply for an expected revolt. It does not launch the uprising itself or automatically grant IMRO legitimacy. The controller may occupy administrative centres and protect railways. Collective punishment is a distinct escalation, visible as such, and calls the shared civilian-loss owner if civilians actually die. War preparation competes with ongoing military needs.

Choosing nothing lets the response mission run its full time. The AI never interprets silence as an accepted treaty. A player receives a reminder before expiry, with no forced pause or repeated popup loop.

## The clandestine play cycle

### Who acts before a country exists

Before an IMRO government controls a real country, the movement is an event-owned nonplayer territorial organisation. Its data belongs to the origin and episode registry. It does not have an empty country tag, a national treasury or a human country-selection slot. The player controlling the affected territory uses the controller's response actions. A real foreign sponsor can fund movement operations through its own country and explicitly quoted contracts. A player may switch into IMRO only after the uprising has created or legitimately changed the real Macedonian country, as the final step of that transaction.

| Political state | Actual operator | Payer and resources | Player access |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No independent IMRO country | Event-owned NPC movement, with an actual origin and participating cells | Autonomous organisation uses time, local consent and finite retained cadres. Arms require a traced delivery, transfer, capture or completed local production. No PP or CP is invented or charged to the controller | Controller response view, or a real sponsor's selected-movement support view |
| Foreign-backed underground | Same NPC movement plus the named real sponsoring government | Sponsor pays its own quoted PP, CP where appropriate and actual stores after consent | Sponsor may choose and inspect its funded operations, without owning the movement's political decisions |
| Existing non-IMRO Macedonia | NPC political opposition within the living country | No spending of incumbent PP, equipment or manpower without an explicit lawful coalition, reform or assistance decision | Existing player keeps the government and receives lawful participation, negotiation and security choices |
| Legitimate IMRO government | Actual Macedonian country and its government | Country pays its quoted resources and uses its real army, industry and administration | Full country movement, reconstruction and later network interfaces |

Autonomous pre-country organisation uses a bounded 120 to 180-day operation selected from current support and security conditions. It can establish a cell, protect an existing contact or attempt a real procurement route. It gains no equipment from the passage of time alone. A local production operation must consume the material and productive capacity of a consenting real actor or a verified existing project. A successful capture or theft transfers the documented quantity from the actual losing stockpile, with a discovery and retaliation consequence, rather than creating matériel from nothing.

Country-funded U and B action prices in the action map apply to a real sponsor or an actual IMRO government. The NPC versions do not pretend to pay those national resources. Their opportunity is limited by the event's real local support, active cadres, exposure and the same mission-slot discipline. They report their observed results to the controller and any involved sponsor at meaningful boundaries, without exposing hidden cell locations to an uninformed country.

The movement normally runs one organisation mission and one political or procurement mission. A third slot is reserved for a genuine crisis such as a discovered cell or a threatened supply delivery. It has no more than six primary actions visible at one time.

| Action family | Useful decision | Real price or commitment | Benefit | Risk and counterplay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local cells | Expand into an eligible neighbouring state | Political organisation cost and a trained-cadre commitment from the existing force budget | Reach and retention improve, Organisation rises after establishment | Discovery can expose that cell and connected logistics. It does not delete every cell in the country |
| Arms | Procure or receive a bounded shipment | Actual equipment, money-equivalent political cost if the shared system supports it, or a sponsor contract | Usable infantry arms and limited support equipment | Interception, delay or sponsor conditions. Exact delivery receipts prevent free duplicate weapons |
| Civic support | Protect local services and resolve an attributable grievance | Political power and time, sometimes construction or equipment support | Mandate and a more viable postwar administration | Broken promises later undo the political gain |
| Underground discipline | Separate compromised cells and protect records | Organisation setback and time | Stops a chain of discoveries, preserves leaders and surviving cadres | Delays the uprising and may abandon a local opportunity |
| External contact | Seek a patron or diaspora contribution | Diplomatic commitments and a bounded political cost | A possible real delivery, recognition or refuge | Rival patrons compete, and domestic supporters can reject dependency |
| Timing | Begin the rising, postpone it, or negotiate | A declared choice after live readiness checks | Converts preparation into a territorial military outcome | Premature rising, loss of a fleeting opportunity, or durable peaceful settlement |

Each action has a target and a reason to use it. Repeated recruitment in a state has diminishing opportunity because the local pool and existing receipt are checked. The design does not create an endless political-power shop for Organisation.

## Discovery and rare openings

Discovery is a weighted outcome at an operation boundary, never an unconditional monthly coin flip over every country. The probability inputs are the controller's relevant intelligence capacity, cell exposure, recent security commitment, terrain, movement discipline and supply-route compromise. Strong resistance helps concealment in some places, while coercion against civilians can make it harder in others. The AI and probability handoff must compare explicit scenarios using actual source and candidate pools.

| Variant | Trigger and visible warning | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compromised courier | A failed route or recent targeted investigation | Cancel the delivery, reroute with a new cost, or risk losing the shipment and one cell. No random death of an unrelated historical leader |
| Split sponsorship | Two patrons offer incompatible obligations | Choose one, reject both, or accept limited nonexclusive material terms. Double payment does not buy contradictory exclusive loyalty |
| Garrison transfer | The controller moves forces because of a real war | IMRO may accelerate its rising with a shorter preparation phase and lower readiness. The controller receives a clear warning |
| Local assembly refuses IMRO command | Good independence sentiment but low Mandate for the organisation | Negotiate a coalition and share political control, accept civic limits, or impose command at a lasting legitimacy cost |
| Existing Macedonian claimant army | A connected event already mobilised forces from the same territory | Adopt or coordinate those actual formations. Do not spawn a second army from the same mobilisation |
| Owner and occupier disagree | Foreign military control over another country's Macedonian territory | A tripartite settlement route or a revolt against the actual occupier. No invented ability to grant another owner's sovereignty |
| Player changes country | A player takes IMRO control after its formation or changes tags later | Transfer player-facing tasks to the actual owner last. Resource payments, treaties and existing unit receipts remain with their correct countries |
| Empty or devastated origin | Earlier catastrophes left no viable population or capital | Select a different verified eligible origin if available. Otherwise postpone candidacy and explain the eligibility failure through event details |

## The rising transaction

The opening freezes the origin, current controller, legal owner, participating cells, paid stores, recruitable pool and existing character owners. It revalidates them immediately before mutation. A changed controller causes a fresh response assignment, not war against a nonexistent enemy.

Create or activate the legitimate Macedonian country identity, grant its initial controlled territory, choose its viable capital and only then run capital-dependent country setup. Reconcile an existing country before changing government. Transfer or generate forces through the approved force budget. Establish the appropriate independence war against the actual controlling master or occupying enemy, with the legal owner included only where the war and settlement rules require it. Set the uprising receipt after the real country, territory and hostility exist. Player country switching, if requested through the event option, occurs last.

The first military objective is a defensible homeland and an administrative centre. Starting territory cannot be an arbitrary distant coastal foothold merely to make the tag exist. A disconnected cell outside the homeland remains an underground asset until a valid local uprising or settlement takes place.

## The war and its settlements

The controller has advantages in regular troops, industry and international standing. IMRO has local terrain knowledge, prepared positions and a compact objective. IMRO can seek recognition, protect its supply base, negotiate a ceasefire, support a local defection or pursue a decisive homeland offensive. It cannot instantly annex every claimed Macedonian region upon taking its first city.

| Result | Required evidence | Continuing content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Military independence | The independence peace or scripted settlement actually establishes independent Macedonia with a viable homeland | Country tree, reconstruction and territorial policy |
| Negotiated independence | All necessary sovereign parties accept the defined territorial settlement and leave the relevant war | Recognition benefits, treaty obligations and restrained opening diplomacy |
| Autonomy after fighting | The movement accepts a specific autonomy arrangement and the settlement genuinely ends the conflict | Domestic institutions and a compact-monitoring mission, with no false independent-country receipt |
| Foreign-backed independence | Independence is real, but the accepted contract creates an alliance, access or economic dependency | Patron route is available. Becoming a subject is a separate explicit bargain and blocks the independent-country evolution conditions |
| Military defeat | IMRO loses a viable independent homeland and its field command is neutralised | Surviving civilian institutions may persist under an honoured settlement. Underground remnants do not refire the original event |
| Evacuated leadership | A real refuge and a surviving route exist before defeat | Exile support can aid later local politics, but cannot pretend an independent IMRO Macedonia still exists for global or terminal eligibility |

A 180-day survival requirement begins only after verified independence and a viable capital. Time as a subject, a tag with no territory, or a defeated government in exile does not satisfy it. War during the survival period is allowed if the state and administration actually endure.

## Autonomous settlement play

Autonomy is a playable branch for the controller and the movement. The compact defines which administration governs the territory, which security units may remain, which civic institutions are protected, and what demobilisation actually returns to the country's manpower and stockpiles. The first review mission lasts 180 days, shortened only by a specific emergency breach. A successful review confirms the compact and reduces Organisation's military role while preserving legal civic participation.

The controller may strengthen the compact, negotiate independent status, or revoke its guarantees with a clear consequence preview. IMRO can respect the compact, pursue lawful constitutional reform, or breach it by remobilising. Blame follows the recorded first material breach, not whichever side opens the event first. New repression changes resistance and Mandate through shared owners, with actual civilian losses logged once if present.

The peaceful resolution retires the current crisis, releases committed resources according to receipts and leaves a historical outcome record. A later genuine breach reopens the same lifecycle at its appropriate state. It cannot replay the manifestation Chaos award or initial free equipment.
