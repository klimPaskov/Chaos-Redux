# Macedonia as a playable country

## A homeland that already has people and institutions

An independent Macedonia inherits the population, damage, towns, transport and political disagreements of the territory it actually receives. Its government needs to turn clandestine authority into public administration while keeping a small army supplied. This makes its first year distinct from the later choice to lead foreign liberation movements.

The default identity is the existing vanilla Macedonian country identity, **MAC**, subject to the installed-game and all-enabled-mod collision audit. The pinned Chaos Redux repository already references MAC in the Independence Wave's Macedonian package. This is evidence for reuse, not proof that every installed dependency or save is compatible. The implementation must inspect the current tag, history, characters, ideas, country-specific equipment and focus ownership before touching them.

An existing independent MAC does not receive a replacement tag, erased tree, reset ideology or duplicated army. An IMRO government can arise through a valid domestic political process and receive additive branches and decisions. An event-created MAC whose setup is exclusively owned by this event may use the full IMRO tree. A shared country integration must record which content came from Event 006 and which came from Event 110 so that removal of the terminal regime restores the actual surviving constitutional content.

## Political identity

Macedonia's citizens are not treated as a uniform faction. Political choices address language, local government, property, veterans, minority protection, labour, rural institutions and relations with neighbouring states. Civic protection applies to all residents. The design does not identify ethnicity with allegiance, recruit every resident into IMRO, or use contested borders as evidence of unanimous consent.

| Route identity | Government and institutions | Leadership presentation | Foreign posture | Visible identity changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Provisional revolutionary administration | Temporary cabinet and constituent process, with military command under defined emergency authority | Authentic organisation or a verified living person with a lawful character transfer | Recognition and survival | Provisional government identity and a sourced institutional emblem |
| Civic republic | Elected assembly, civilian defence oversight, local-rights guarantees | A verified suitable living civilian or authentic institutional leadership | Negotiated borders, recognition, defensive cooperation | Civic flag proposal clearly described as an alternate-history adoption, constitutional country name and party structure |
| Anarchist confederation | Recallable local delegates, communal economic agreements and federated defence | People-free institutional council image built from authentic organisational material, with individual advisers only when sourced | Voluntary association and assistance without imposed sovereignty | Commune identity, distinct flag, militia and logistics presentation |
| Socialist federation | Worker and peasant institutions with a real fork between autonomous federation and external party dependency | Verified participants of relevant left or federalist institutions where available | Federal compacts, industrial coordination and conditional external support | Federal constitution, appropriate organisation and route-specific flag |
| Sovereign revolutionary state | Central command, national reconstruction, disciplined administration, internal struggle over emergency rule | Verified movement figure or authentic executive institution | Territorial revision, diaspora mobilisation and independence from patrons | Centralised state identity, disciplined forces and interwar design references |
| Patron-aligned government | A domestic cabinet with explicit military or economic patron obligations | A verified government actor and named foreign relationship, never a cloned foreign leader | Aid, access and alignment, with an optional explicit subject bargain | Contract-dependent cosmetic identity and clear independence or subject status |
| Radical terminal faction | Fictional doctrine, coercive command and a destructive administrative apparatus | A people-free fictional faction symbol or institution, not an invented historical person's extremist portrait | Conquest, rupture with rejecting Front members and extermination | Fictional transformed emblem, country presentation, threatened human status and altered institutional idea states |

Ideology categories use the installed political system. A route's actual institutions and mechanics define its identity. Do not force a historically inappropriate leader into an ideology merely because a portrait is available. The anarchist route is an alternate-history constitutional choice informed by regional anarchist traditions. It is not a claim that all historical IMRO factions were anarchists. See the [anarchist-context source and its limits](110_imro_research_reading_record.md#h02-anarchist-context-without-conflation). The death doctrine must never be attributed to a real person as historical fact.

## Characters and source discipline

Every historical person must have an individually verified lifespan, availability at the actual campaign date, relevant role, source portrait and current character owner. Dead organisers can appear only in attributed historical memory, objects or documented accounts. They cannot become living 1936 ministers because their names are famous. A character already serving another country requires an explicit transfer or invitation result, with the original owner's state reconciled. Two factions cannot simultaneously own the same individual.

Ivan Mihailov, Dimitar Vlahov and Pavel Shatev are research candidates for relevant interwar political contexts, not approved appointments in this plan. A portrait researcher must verify their exact date, politics, availability and compatible existing game character before assigning a role. A verified institutional representation is the preferred complete solution where personal identification would be misleading. Institutional leadership is a deliberate government form, not permission to invent a biography or reuse a generic silhouette as a finished portrait.

The implementation character register must contain identity, original-language spelling, transliteration, birth and death dates, role evidence, source URL, image rights, current owner, route eligibility, invitation or transfer rule, removal rule and portrait status. Static named characters are recruited through history or the project's dated dormant pool before guarded transfer. Leader creation uses the recipient country's actual scope. There is no temporary fictional replacement for an unresolved grounded leader.

Portrait production follows the supplied portrait-owner workflow. Preserve the untouched source and a documented crop. A pending authentic placeholder must be labelled in the handoff. The user alone supplies the final styled grounded portrait through the specified workflow. No generated imitation of a real face is an acceptable final.

## Initial territory and claims

The homeland should be the installed-map representation of Vardar Macedonia that provides a viable capital and recognisable local basis. The pinned Event 006 package explicitly uses state 106 as its Macedonian capital anchor and state 970, Western Macedonia, as an optional extension. These are source-backed starting bindings to verify against the current installed map, not permission to guess the remaining Pirin and Aegean pieces. The proposed capital priority is Skopje, then a verified appropriate controlled provincial centre such as Bitola if the primary site is unavailable. A living Event 006 MAC retains its state-106 capital requirement while that package owns its lifecycle, unless the package owner explicitly supports a lawful migration. See the [integration handoff](110_imro_integration_handoff.md).

Pirin and Aegean Macedonian claim areas are separate expansion tracks. A focus may open a diplomatic claim or local political contact without granting a core. Claims do not transfer ownership. Cores require the relevant territorial settlement and civic integration conditions. An entire mixed state cannot become a free core merely because a small part is described as Macedonian.

| Territorial outcome | Requirements | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Independent homeland | Verified founding territory, viable capital, an actual independence settlement | Core or rightful homeland treatment consistent with existing MAC setup, repaired rather than duplicated |
| Negotiated border settlement | Required governments accept, eligible territory is transferred, local institutions are guaranteed | Specific claims resolved, local administration established, later integration possible |
| Wartime occupation | Actual military control | Occupation rules apply. No immediate national core or assumed Mandate |
| Civic integration | Settled ownership, functioning administration, adequate Mandate, protected residents and no recent breach | A specific eligible state may gain a core through the common qualification receipt |
| Federal partnership | An independent state accepts a constitutional compact | Shared institutions and aid without annexation or removal of sovereignty unless a separate explicit unification vote is chosen |
| Macedonian reunification | The exact required pieces are settled, governed and qualified through the territorial map | Country cosmetic and national milestone, with no automatic claim over unrelated Balkan peoples |

The formable state puzzle uses exact current state geometry, grey for unmet and green for met conditions plus non-colour symbols and explanatory text. The same qualification helper powers map pieces, tooltip and final action. Small shapes need a selectable enlarged inset and a text list. A rendered map is mandatory if the implementation offers the multi-area reunification action. A simple static map is acceptable only for an explicitly reduced single obvious territory action with a recorded reason and the same eligibility checks, not as a substitute for the multi-area design.

The three to five research-slot progression is proposed balance built on the documented native identity. Verify the actual installed baseline before implementation and never rewrite existing MAC research downward. A mature existing country may already exceed that baseline, in which case an equivalent relevant research bonus replaces a duplicate slot reward.

Starting technology selection is deterministic and provenance-based. Use the verified MAC history for the actual date, preserve every technology already held by a living country, and inspect the prerequisites of each proposed additional grant. A weapon capture does not transfer unrelated industrial, naval, air or doctrinal knowledge. Add a required prerequisite only where the same verified controller transfer or completed training contract supplies it. Record the exact installed technology IDs, source, date and consumer in the setup receipt. Do not copy the controller's whole technology tree or reset research backwards. A missing required technology leaves the associated production or template unavailable until the documented grant or research completes.

## Three focus-owned institutional spirits

The full country tree may own **at most three simultaneous national spirits**, including its starting penalties. A timed modifier representing another persistent institution still counts. Do not evade the cap by splitting the same government across hidden modifiers.

| Slot | Starting institution | How play changes it | Mature route versions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Political order | Provisional revolutionary authority, with contested civilian legitimacy | Constituent process, autonomy history, civic guarantees, patron concessions or centralisation | Constitutional assembly, federated communes, socialist federal administration, central revolutionary executive, patron-bound cabinet or radical command |
| Productive life | Disrupted local economy, with damaged transport and weak industrial coordination | Repair, food distribution, land and labour settlement, workshops, trade and shared research | Cooperative reconstruction, regulated civic economy, planned federal economy, central mobilisation or destructive terminal allocation |
| Defence organisation | Fragmented irregular command, with good local knowledge and weak regular coordination | Cadre training, depot building, elected or professional command, equipment standardisation | Confederated defence, professional territorial army, central expeditionary command or coercive terminal forces |

The starting versions have meaningful disadvantages that can be mitigated through play. Typical opening effects are slower administrative mobilisation, limited coordinated offensive performance and strained production efficiency. Route rewards upgrade these institutions, grant concrete buildings, technology bonuses, templates, diplomatic actions and laws, and unlock decisions. They do not add a fourth institution after every focus.

Anarchist decentralisation is not a permanent universal production penalty. Successful supply compacts give resilient dispersed production and local defence. Its disadvantage is that distant expeditionary mobilisation and unified command require explicit delegate agreements. Centralisation offers faster concentration while raising the consequences of command loss and political exclusion. These tradeoffs should remain visible even when both routes become powerful.

## The armed force

IMRO needs one distinctive light irregular subunit family because its local sustainment and mountain operations materially differ from ordinary line infantry. The working family is a komitadji detachment. It is human, vulnerable to sustained fire, armour and air power, and suitable for early hills and mountains. It is not a universal elite battalion or a special-forces cap bypass.

| Property | Intended behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Role | Light local defence, reconnaissance and limited mountain manoeuvre |
| Equipment | Ordinary compatible infantry weapons plus a small support-equipment requirement where the template uses support capability |
| Strengths | Lower supply footprint, useful movement and entrenchment in suitable terrain, retained experience when reorganised |
| Weaknesses | Weak breakthrough, piercing and hard attack, lower sustained offensive value, limited heavy support |
| Manpower | Full real human requirement, drawn from an eligible recruitable pool |
| Upgrade | Retrain into territorial or professional infantry through a paid mission. Equipment and manpower transfer rather than duplicate |
| International use | Volunteer and training detachments require actual force availability, recipient acceptance and a viable route |
| Terminal use | Reorganisation may improve coercive command and equipment access, but does not create immortal or nonhuman soldiers |

Opening forces are allocated from a single mobilisation receipt. It includes source manpower, weapons, support stores, template, strength, experience, origin and assigned country. The minimum viable rising may receive one or two understrength formations with borrowed local rifles where the controller's stores are actually transferred or the source is a paid shipment. Every additional formation requires available manpower and equipment. Captured stockpiles are credited only once after a real capture or agreed transfer.

The exact battalion and equipment numbers should be tuned against current vanilla and project templates after inspection. The intended opening bands are in the underground part. The implementation cannot reduce all openings to identical generic infantry and still claim this custom-unit design is complete.

## Force growth and reconstruction

| Path | What the player commits | Result and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local recruitment | Available manpower, infantry equipment, training time and a functioning home area | Reinforces existing units before raising new ones unless the player deliberately chooses expansion |
| Veteran cadre school | Experienced cadres and a training institution | Improves training and unlocks reorganised templates without minting veteran divisions |
| Depot programme | Construction capacity and supply equipment in a chosen controlled state | Better operational sustainment and an actual map asset |
| Foreign mission | A documented patron contract, access and accepted advisers | Equipment or training with debt and political consequences |
| Communal defence compact | Delegate consent, local stores and a defined defence area | Strong dispersed defence, with a later agreement required for distant expeditions |
| Federal arsenal | A willing partner, industry commitment and a shared production plan | Complementary manufacture and limited shared research, without copying every partner's factories |
| Professionalisation | Training time and sufficient regular equipment | Regular formation capability, better combined operations and a smaller irregular niche |
| Demobilisation | A settled peace and political choice | Proper return of surviving eligible manpower and equipment through engine-supported mechanisms, reducing military burdens |

Country forces require their complete visible package: original counters, appropriate 3D model family, equipment images where needed, sourced unit sound and actual runtime wiring. These are acceptance requirements for implementation, not assets produced by this planning task.

## Losing and recovering the country

Capitulation, annexation, loss of government, becoming a subject and elimination of the radical regime are distinct events. A capitulated Macedonia with a viable allied refuge may continue an ordinary war. It cannot satisfy the independent homeland or terminal authority conditions while the required homeland administration is absent. An annexed IMRO government cannot keep killing people through a stale country flag.

After the radical regime is defeated, surviving Macedonian institutions choose a provisional recovery process consistent with actual territory, population and previous route history. A prior commune route can restore delegate institutions if they still have surviving participants. A civic route can restore its constitutional framework. A defeated nationalist dictatorship can yield a new constituent process. The package never resurrects killed characters or restores the pre-war population by changing a cosmetic tag.

Foreign countries liberated by Freedom or Death remain independent unless their own separate, actual wars or later explicit settlements change that status. The fall of IMRO is not a global rollback of independence.
