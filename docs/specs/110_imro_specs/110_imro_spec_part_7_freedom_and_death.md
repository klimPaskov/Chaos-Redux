# Freedom AND Death

## The doctrine and its fictional boundary

At extreme Chaos, a faction within IMRO argues that life itself is captivity. It rejects the earlier meaning of national liberation and claims that everyone must die to become free. This includes Macedonians, its own fighters and leaders, former subjects it helped liberate, its allies, its enemies and every other living human.

This is an explicitly fictional corruption within the mod. It is not a historical claim about IMRO, Macedonia, anarchism, socialism or any real political community. The presentation should make the betrayal of the earlier movement intelligible and frightening without reproducing real extremist propaganda or using graphic spectacle. The game's objective concerns the survival or extinction of humanity, with real population consequences and a playable opportunity to stop the regime.

The route has three distinct political and mechanical moments: the 800 Chaos evolution, the start of actual conquest through its focuses, and the separate world-end commitment strictly above 1000. The first does not automatically produce the third.

## Evolution at 800

The evolution becomes available when current Chaos reaches at least 800, an independent IMRO-led Macedonia still has a viable homeland and the worldwide Freedom or Death transaction has completed. It reveals a radical doctrine contest and the relevant focus branch. An active event receives the reveal at the next bounded event-owned eligibility check. A pre-fire evolved opening may contain radical organisers, but it must still pass through local independence, survival and worldwide liberation before this contest can become the government's route.

The reveal does not set a world-end flag, begin extermination, award Chaos or erase the constitution. It gives the player a clear explanation of the threat to the current institutions and of the possible route commitment. A world-end scenario toggle controls the later terminal lock. It does not silently remove the separate 800 political evolution or the conventional conquest play it unlocks, unless the broader event is itself disabled.

## The political contest

The contest is a 120-day crisis in normal conditions. A country already in severe military collapse can receive a 90-day emergency version, justified by loss of command and immediate institutional danger. There is no unavoidable seven-day surprise coup. It uses the existing Organisation and Mandate values and concrete institutional objectives rather than a fourth radicalism meter.

Three objectives matter: a functioning legitimate civil or delegate institution, loyal effective field command, and control of the communications and logistics linking them. Each is visible as a condition with its specific action and failure reason. The government can protect representation, secure military allegiance, expose the doctrine, honour suspended rights or negotiate a nonterminal leadership change. A state that used permanent coercion earlier has fewer trusted institutions and must spend more effort rebuilding them.

| Player or AI course | Commitment | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defend the existing political order | Complete at least two of the three institutional objectives, including the civil or delegate institution, while keeping Mandate at least 45 | Defeat the takeover, preserve the constitution, remove this crisis and gain a guarded restoration-of-consent result |
| Negotiate a renewed constituent process | Retain civilian institutions and an actual secure homeland, accept loss of some emergency authority | Pause coercive expansion and resolve through a new constitutional settlement with a real political cost |
| Embrace the radical faction | Select and complete the explicit route commitment focus after seeing its meaning | Transfer government to the fictional faction, transform the three institutional spirits and force a Front doctrine response |
| Fail the defence | Lose required institutions and fail the clearly shown crisis objectives | Radical takeover can occur. The game reports which conditions failed and preserves actual survivors and political history |

For a human player, a successful defence is deterministic from the stated objectives. The design does not secretly override it with a random coup because Chaos is high. A player can deliberately enter the radical route. An AI can choose it based on its actual political trajectory, extreme Chaos, command centralisation, low trust and expansion strategy. AI weights still require measured review and are not a substitute for eligibility.

A defeated attempt cannot repeat every month. A later attempt requires a new material institutional collapse, Chaos still at least 800, 365 days since the resolved attempt and a distinct crisis receipt. Only one radical government lineage and terminal launch can belong to this event. This permits a real later reversal without an endlessly rerolled coup.

## The Front refuses or follows

The radical government immediately reveals its doctrine to the Front. Every current member must make a fresh choice. Prior liberation, ordinary membership, a defensive mandate or a past shipment is not consent to this doctrine.

| Response | Result | Authority consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reject the doctrine | Revoke applicable IMRO command mandates, separate from its operational faction through a safe transaction and gain resistance-coordination access | Remove that country's command contribution before terminal eligibility is recalculated |
| Remain neutral while withdrawing | End active command and future support, retain sovereignty and prepare defence | No command weight for IMRO, with continuing diplomatic uncertainty |
| Accept radical central command | Explicitly adopt the fictional doctrine through an actual political decision | Count only if the government, contribution and logistics truly implement the commitment |
| Resist a domestic radical faction | Open a local bounded political crisis without automatically changing the player's government | Authority remains withheld until a verified result |

Human members retain their countries and decide their response. Ordinary AI governments strongly prefer rejection when the doctrine threatens their own populations. A narrow extremist trajectory can accept, but there is no default universal compliance. This makes the requirement for enormous retained IMRO power meaningful.

A rejecting member can remain part of the original liberation tradition and cooperate with other former Front members. The interface distinguishes the founding liberation organisation, IMRO's claimed central command and the surviving opposition. The regime cannot retain Authority by keeping defectors' names in an old roster.

## Conquest before the terminal lock

After takeover, the unlocked focus groups build the radical command, repudiate national territorial limits, establish actual expeditionary capability and commit to universal conquest. Completing the conquest commitment opens war planning against independent targets beyond the Macedonian territorial question. It can occur between Chaos 800 and 1000. The regime is already a dangerous aggressor at that stage, but it has not yet qualified for the world-end scenario.

Conquest uses real military forces, supply, equipment and geography. The regime can issue aggressive demands and war goals, fight several fronts and reorganise captured administration. It cannot kill civilians in an inaccessible neutral state by selecting it on a list. Pre-lock wars produce normal shared civilian and military losses where the existing systems actually apply. The dedicated terminal extermination scheduler remains inactive until the separate lock.

The conquest branch is allowed to be powerful. Mature radical command can provide a major mobilisation and offensive advantage, with initial tuning targeting roughly 20 to 35 percent better relevant attack or planning capability in its prepared theatres and substantially faster military allocation. These are bounded institution states, not a pile of new spirits. The disadvantages are severe domestic consent loss, diplomatic isolation, defections, logistical overreach and the erosion of the very population that maintains the army once extermination begins. Exact modifier selection must be checked against current stacking and existing modifiers.

## The separate world-end lock

The terminal commitment focus becomes selectable only when every condition below is true. Completion rechecks them all in one fresh transaction, after doctrine responses and defections. A disabled or conflicting world-end scenario prevents commitment without deleting the already existing conventional radical government.

| Requirement | Proposed exact design rule | Why it is needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chaos | Current value strictly greater than 1000 | Preserves the user's threshold. Exactly 1000 fails |
| Independent IMRO Macedonia | Correct current government, not a subject, not eliminated, viable controlled homeland capital and functioning command | A tag, exile flag or conquered capital is insufficient |
| Earlier worldwide liberation | Completed observed Freedom or Death cohort with at least one actual subject freed | A zero-subject announcement cannot satisfy the history |
| Radical conquest commitment | Takeover completed and the unlocked conquest commitment focus completed | Thresholds alone cannot start the terminal route |
| Front Authority | At least 80, sustained for 90 days after the doctrine split and freshly recomputed at lock | Temporary nominal leadership is insufficient |
| Real participating states | At least six independent surviving states including Macedonia under valid central or theatre command, with at least five others | One annexing country cannot call itself the entire world Front |
| Meaningful members | Each of those five other states contributes at least 1 percent of the Front's baseline weighted capacity at the worldwide launch | Empty token members cannot fill the state count |
| Geographic reach | Functioning command and supply in at least three verified world macroregions, including two outside the Balkans and at least one outside Europe | A Balkan bloc cannot qualify as world command |
| Human and industrial reach | Commanded governments and actual territory account for at least 20 percent of the launch-world living-human baseline and 20 percent of its usable military-industry baseline | A shrinking denominator or the death of outsiders cannot manufacture dominance |
| Military reach | At least 15 percent of launch-world effective land-force capacity under actual verified command, with sustainable access to required theatres | Population and factories alone do not prove operational power |
| Shared scenario availability | Global enablement, the IMRO scenario enabled, no incompatible active world-end owner and valid identifier registration | Prevents competing terminal engines from corrupting each other |
| One commitment | No previous terminal launch or completed extinction for this event lineage | Prevents replay and duplicate world-end rewards |

The numerical power thresholds other than 800 and above 1000 are proposed tuning choices. They deliberately require a world-scale command structure. Their implementation belongs in one constants owner and must be tested against credible large-Front scenarios. Macroregions are a verified project map grouping, proposed as Europe, Africa, Asia, the Americas and Oceania with precise country/state bindings at implementation. They must not be guessed from capital coordinates or country names.

The baseline denominators are frozen at the worldwide liberation launch and exclude no living human because of ideology or IMRO alignment. An initially zero industrial or military component blocks the corresponding proof until an explicit comparable baseline policy is established. It is not divided by one or treated as automatically met. New growth can count in the numerator through observed capability, while killing or annexing outsiders cannot reduce the frozen denominator. The baseline is internal evidence, not another public meter.

These conditions are a launch test. After a valid commitment, losing some foreign members does not make the campaign's history disappear or switch its doctrine off automatically. It does weaken military reach and administrative capacity. Defeat, loss of the actual executing apparatus or a successful internal overthrow stops the campaign through the rules below.

## What commitment does

The lock records a durable world-end commitment, an IMRO-owned active campaign, a one-use super-event receipt and an IMRO-specific world-threat source. It activates bounded administrative and military campaign jobs. The existing shared world-end owner decides which unrelated event families are suppressed while preserving survival, migration, diplomacy and defeat counterplay required here.

The worldwide super-event presents the betrayal of the liberation promise, the doctrine's inclusion of IMRO's own people, and the danger that now exists. Its visual and audio package is source-reviewed separately. The world remains populated after it fires. No percentage of humanity disappears merely because the presentation played.

The game displays active threats by affected territory, real administrative reach and the existing Deaths interface. It never uses a fabricated global countdown that guarantees extinction even if IMRO has already been defeated.

## A progressive campaign

The campaign operates only where the regime or an explicitly participating government has actual territorial or custody authority and a functioning administrative connection. Military conquest expands that reach. Neutral and liberated territories outside its reach remain alive and can organise resistance. A nominal ally's refusal prevents IMRO from using that ally's population as if it were controlled territory.

Three types of campaign consequence matter at the game's strategic level: losses caused by actual war, civilian losses from the regime's exterminating administration, and losses or survival during displacement. Their physical ownership remains with the existing military, population, custody and migration systems. The IMRO layer supplies authorised cause and pressure, then consumes measured receipts. It does not implement a second population model that can disagree with the world.

The administrative campaign has a 90-day initial mobilisation period, shortened to 60 days only in territory with a mature radical apparatus created before lock. Its normal operating cycle is 30 days over a bounded registry of active affected states. Regional administrative projects take 120 to 180 days to establish or expand, and at most three major regional projects may be active for IMRO at once. A country under attack sees a warning as soon as a real project begins affecting its people.

Initial simulation tuning should request a state loss of 0.5 to 2 percent of currently reachable living civilian population per 30-day cycle, modified by functioning administration, local resistance, supply, disruption and evacuation. The actual debit is clamped to remaining verified people and current capacity. This is an abstract game pacing parameter, not an operational depiction of real methods. Repeated cycles, conquest and administrative expansion make the campaign progressively destructive. A single cycle must not erase an ordinary inhabited state.

Mature uncontested administration may reach 3 to 6 percent per cycle after its associated projects complete. It pays through military and administrative allocation and accelerates the loss of its productive and recruitable base. Deep resistance, damaged links and interrupted control reduce or halt its effect. No minimum invented loss is applied when actual capacity or population is zero.

The final phase cannot rely on multiplying a population by a fraction forever. A state becomes eligible for a residual operation only when its reachable living civilian residual is at most 5,000 people and no larger than that state's verified preceding 30-day operating capacity. The operation lasts 90 days, requires continuous actual reach and uncontested administration, and then requests the exact remaining represented eligible population through the same measured owner. Any loss of reach cancels the uncompleted operation. This is a finite game-resolution rule, not a new real-world method. It cannot include refugees elsewhere, deployed people whose ownership is unresolved, or a person already removed from that state's population. There is no floating-point tolerance that declares a living residual dead. If the engine cannot represent or debit that exact residual truthfully, extinction remains blocked until the population owner supports the required precision.

## The regime kills its own society

IMRO's homeland and loyal supporters enter the same campaign. The regime cannot indefinitely exempt Macedonians, administrators or soldiers while claiming ideological victory. It may sequence temporary labour and military requirements during conquest, but every exempted living cohort remains counted, visible through the appropriate native system and disqualifying for extinction.

As the homeland is consumed, productive output and recruitment decline, command becomes harder to maintain and internal rejection becomes more likely. These consequences use the actual remaining people and institutions rather than a separate cosmetic penalty. The regime can attempt a final self-destruction phase only when external human survivors have actually been accounted for and eliminated and the remaining human carrier register is complete.

The final self-destruction operation must include its own civilians, reserves, deployed land, air and naval personnel, custody cohorts, leaders represented as living people, and any other exclusive off-map survivors. The last terminal transaction records the final measured losses and cessation of all living actors together. It does not need a surviving administrator after the last death to click another decision. Before that final verified transaction, the regime's destruction by an opponent is a defeat, not an extinction victory.

## Counterplay for surviving countries

| Response | Prerequisite and commitment | Practical effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordinate survival | Sovereign government rejects doctrine and has a valid communication partner | Shared strategic coordination without forcing every country to abandon unrelated alliances |
| Defend threatened territory | Actual troops, supply and local control | Prevents or interrupts regime administration and protects its population from this campaign |
| Evacuate through the migration owner | Real route, destination capacity and actual people to move | Moves surviving people and records arrivals. Emigration is not death |
| Support local resistance | Valid reachable partner, matériel and intelligence | Disrupts command or administrative capacity through the normal shared resistance owner |
| Encourage defections | Real disaffected command or government and a credible safety offer | Transfers surviving allegiance where supported, lowers IMRO capability and preserves those people |
| Sever command and logistics | Actual military action against relevant controlled nodes | Stops affected projects rather than magically suppressing all losses worldwide |
| Overthrow the radical government | Successful internal constitutional or military resistance with surviving institutions | Stops the IMRO campaign and opens recovery |
| Defeat the regime militarily | Actual elimination or surrender of the executing regime and its remaining authorised apparatus | Cancels future campaign jobs and begins postwar settlement |

Survival cooperation may create a compatible operational faction or use treaties and shared actions across existing factions. It must be meaningful for countries already fighting unrelated wars. The NLF's rejecting members are natural organisers, but independent countries that were never subjects can also participate. Ideology is not an automatic exclusion from defending humanity.

Evacuation is not invulnerability. Route failure can cause real losses through the migration system, and capacity limits matter. Successful migrants remain alive in their destination or a verified transit carrier. IMRO cannot reach them merely by retaining ownership of their origin state.

## Defeat and campaign shutdown

Defeat is observed when the radical government is overthrown, the regime is eliminated through an actual settlement, or all executing command and authorised local apparatus have ceased to exist with no viable continuation. Mere temporary loss of a single capital is not automatically global defeat if the regime still has a real command elsewhere. Conversely, a dead tag with a stale flag cannot keep executing jobs.

The shutdown transaction cancels pending IMRO mortality and administration jobs, invalidates outstanding authority, stops new offensive campaign actions, clears the IMRO active scenario and threat contribution and refreshes shared threat state. It preserves real deaths, displacement, completed independence, wars not settled by the defeat and the historical world-end commitment. Other active threats remain active.

A scheduled loss rechecks active authority immediately before any debit. If defeat already occurred, it closes without loss. This prevents an old timer from killing people after liberation. A partially completed operation preserves its actual prior losses and refunds only resources reserved and not spent. No refund resurrects people.

## The complete-human accounting contract

Ultimate victory needs a fresh, reconciled proof of zero living humans. The proof covers state residents and all exclusive off-map carriers. It also reconciles any military, custody or administrative subset still included in the resident total. An unverified category blocks completion.

| Carrier | Required accounting rule |
| --- | --- |
| Resident state population | Measured current population from the shared owner, classified by actual humanity rather than political alignment |
| Free manpower and recruited reserves | Establish whether the engine represents these people inside resident population or in an exclusive pool. Count each human once |
| Deployed land, air and naval personnel | Preserve living personnel until actual loss, surrender with surviving custody, demobilisation or transfer is reconciled. A disbanded unit is not proof of death |
| Refugees and migrants at destination | Count in the destination's measured credited population or an exclusive reception carrier, never both |
| People in transit | Count in the migration owner's exclusive custody until arrival, return or verified death |
| Camps, detention and trapped cohorts | Establish whether a cohort is a subset of the host state's population or an exclusive carrier, then reconcile any loss with that owner |
| Exile and off-map survivors | Government identity alone has no invented population. Any actual represented living cohort remains counted |
| Scripted surviving persons | Verify whether represented leaders and other explicit survivors are included in an existing cohort. Do not retain a living personal survivor after global extinction |
| Newly created or changed carriers | Birth, recruitment, demobilisation, transfer, rescue and return invalidate an earlier census until reconciled |

The accounting equation is resident humans plus exclusively off-map humans, with every included subset reconciled. It is not resident population plus all manpower numbers plus every migrant row. That sum can count the same person several times. The complete-human interface is a required shared integration deliverable because the targeted current repository review did not find a complete proven one.

Physical civilian loss occurs exactly once. Measure before and after the real debit, then register that observed loss in the existing Deaths system with population application disabled and state-ledger recording enabled. The current helper discrepancy and Deaths-setting issue are explicit integration gates. A disabled optional display must not turn the campaign into unrecorded fictional deaths. The shared owner needs a documented accounting-versus-display separation while preserving the user's general settings and the existing death-linked Chaos policy. IMRO may not masquerade as Fallout to bypass a setting.

## Terminal outcomes

| Outcome | Exact condition | Presentation and continuing state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Radicals defeated before takeover | Successful institutional defence | Ordinary Macedonia and Front play continue |
| Radical conquest defeated before lock | Actual regime defeat without terminal commitment | Major war aftermath, no false world-end history |
| Terminal campaign defeated | Active campaign existed and then its execution authority is verifiably destroyed | Worldwide relief and mourning presentation where the campaign was globally consequential, truthful Deaths and recovery |
| World conquered but humans remain | Military victory with a positive or unresolved living-human total | No extinction victory. The remaining campaign or internal resistance continues |
| Human extinction | Fresh complete census is exactly zero, including IMRO's own humans, with all transactions reconciled and no unresolved survivor carriers | One final end-state receipt and presentation. Human gameplay ceases because no living humans remain |

No ending restores the old subject order merely because the founder of worldwide liberation has fallen. The surviving world decides its own subsequent relations through actual diplomacy and peace settlements.
