# Balkan Revolutionary Network

## A network of different movements

A surviving IMRO can build a regional association of movements and independent governments. Each partner has its own people, territory, political programme and relationship with its current controller. The network succeeds when those partners can sustain their own goals and cooperate. It can also divide over overlapping claims, patron pressure, political coercion and the difference between independence and incorporation into another state.

The competing-patron design draws on documented interwar relations while the contract mechanics and sovereign network are alternate-history choices. The [patronage source record](110_imro_research_reading_record.md#h01-interwar-patronage) states that distinction and the research scope.

The proposed evolution threshold is Chaos 200. An active independent Macedonia normally needs 180 days of survival, Organisation at least 45 and a valid external contact. A surviving underground movement may instead establish a limited exile and communication network after 240 days of continuous organisation, a safe foreign base and a real delivery route. The underground version cannot command foreign armies, create a global Front or satisfy the independent-Macedonia conditions for later evolutions.

At an evolved opening, the original movement may already have a contact or sponsor-linked route. It still needs local support, a viable territory and an actual uprising or autonomy process. Activation adds no Chaos by itself.

## Partner identity and regional design

The following are geographic and political partner families. They are not a roster of countries to be created regardless of current conditions. Use existing compatible countries and the current liberation registry first. A state without a verified releasable identity can host a supported civic or resistance organisation, but it does not receive an invented national tag to fill the table.

| Partner family | Distinct concern | Useful IMRO interaction | Conflict to resolve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Remaining Macedonian claim areas | Local rights, external rule and fear of annexation or coercion | Civic guarantees, dialogue, protection and bounded material support | Independence, union with Macedonia and local autonomy are separate choices |
| Albanian and Kosovo contexts | Sovereignty, border security and competing regional claims | Transit agreement, local protection and mediation with a current legitimate partner | A partner's territorial ambition may conflict with Macedonia or another member |
| Montenegrin and Bosnian contexts | State restoration, local institutions and security among neighbouring powers | Recognition, cadre training and federal discussion | Different constitutional and identity preferences cannot be collapsed into one ideology |
| Greek or Bulgarian regional movements | Particular autonomy or independence demands under a specific controller | Negotiated rights or support where a real current movement exists | Opposition to a government is not evidence that all residents want secession |
| Thracian and borderland contexts | Mixed communities, access, displaced people and contested administration | Border guarantees, return agreements and supply corridors | A broad nationalist claim can undermine a member's consent |
| Danube partners | Transport, industry and defensive cooperation among independent states | Technical exchange, complementary manufacture and trade | Regional economic association must not silently annex member countries |

The current Event 006 registry already contains several relevant Balkan packages and formable systems. The implementation must reuse their identities and membership evidence where compatible. An IMRO compact does not replay an Event 006 release, inherit its authority merely by naming the same tag, or bypass a partner's uncompleted national crisis.

## Relationship states

A partner relationship moves through contact, assisted organisation, recognised partner, active compact and settled or broken relationship. A contact is a diplomatic or clandestine link, not a new numerical gauge. The player sees the relationship's current condition and outstanding obligations.

**Contact** requires a real actor and route. It opens information and negotiation. **Assisted organisation** requires an actual delivery or administrative commitment. It can help a movement survive, without granting IMRO control of the recipient. **Recognised partner** requires an explicit agreement about sovereignty and conduct. **Active compact** adds defined common logistics, defence or political institutions. A relationship can end through withdrawal, defeat, settlement, incompatible demands or the destruction of its actual actor.

Organisation affects how many reliable operations the network can maintain, not how many nations have a right to exist. A low-Organisation Macedonia may lose the ability to sustain several aid projects, while its independent partners remain sovereign.

## Main network actions

| Action family | Target and prerequisite | Resolution | Consequence and guard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Establish contact | Existing movement or government with a valid regional connection | Contact becomes actionable after time and diplomatic or clandestine effort | One relationship record per real actor and generation |
| Deliver material aid | A willing recipient, actual equipment and a route | Transfer measured matériel and record delivery or interception | No benefit on promise alone, no duplicate payment, and no repeated reward for the same shipment |
| Train local cadres | Actual instructors and recipient support | Improve the recipient's military capability or retain surviving local experience | Training is finite and cannot spawn a second army on both ends |
| Offer civic guarantees | A specific political dispute or proposed compact | Improve trust when the agreed policy is enacted | Breaking the guarantee damages Mandate and creates a real dispute |
| Coordinate an uprising | At least two ready movements with distinct territories and explicit participation | A linked timing mission launches their actual local processes | No duplicate declarations for movements already at war or already free |
| Mediate a border dispute | Two willing partners and a concrete territorial disagreement | Negotiated border, local autonomy, arbitration or refusal | Refusal is a valid outcome. IMRO cannot manufacture consent |
| Build a supply compact | Participating governments, access and a viable land or sea route | Shared logistics improves actual aid reliability and military sustainment | Route loss degrades the compact until repaired |
| Convene a federal compact | At least three willing independent governments including Macedonia | Create shared institutions through consent | Sovereignty continues unless each affected country explicitly authorises unification |
| Expose patron interference | A real coercive demand or contradictory contract | Reveal terms and offer renegotiation, collective refusal or alignment | The patron can withdraw support or retaliate. It is not forced into automatic war |
| Withdraw assistance | Existing commitment with a defined end or breach clause | End future aid and settle unspent reservations | Delivered matériel is not magically returned and the partner remains independent |

A single controller can face several local movements without receiving dozens of identical popups. Group reports by episode and region, with each actual territorial decision accessible in the shared category. Each local movement still retains its own outcome and resource record.

## Patron conflict

Foreign assistance is strong enough to matter. A patron can supply a decisive early shipment, secure a training base, help with recognition or fund an industrial project. Its terms can include military access, strategic coordination, exclusive procurement, ideological alignment or a territorial position. The recipient sees those terms before accepting.

Three recurring disputes make sponsorship dynamic. A patron may demand an operation that helps its wider war while endangering a member's independence. Two patrons may offer incompatible exclusive terms. A growing network may use its own institutions to refuse both and act independently. The response depends on actual delivered support, treaty wording, current military necessity and the movement's Mandate.

No patron permanently controls the network through a hidden influence score. Control exists only in documented contracts, access, command mandates, dependencies and political decisions. Patron disappointment produces concrete consequences such as suspended deliveries, diplomatic pressure or a separately chosen war. It does not automatically delete all foreign-sourced equipment or switch the country's government.

## Coordinated uprisings

A linked regional rising requires a living movement in each target area, a viable political demand, a current controller and actual readiness. The coordination mission lasts 180 to 240 days by default. Strong communications and a common controller under severe war pressure can shorten it to 120 days. A shorter emergency opening needs an explicit battlefield opportunity and provides less preparation.

Participants may proceed together, postpone together or withdraw before commitment. A withdrawal cancels only the withdrawing actor's unspent commitments. If an eligible partner becomes independent before the launch, it changes into a supporter and is not released again. If it already declared its local war, coordination becomes assistance to that war rather than a second declaration.

The outcome is regional variation. One partner may gain independence, another may accept autonomy, and a third may lose. The network does not need an all-or-nothing victory flag to remain playable. Surviving partners can exchange refugees, supplies and diplomatic support through the shared owners.

## Regional institutions and formables

The sovereign association is the default mature network: an agreement among independent countries with common support and dispute procedures. It offers complementary production, shared training and more reliable aid. Members can leave according to their agreements. The association's identity persists through a successor coordinator if Macedonia becomes temporarily unable to lead.

A deeper federal compact is an optional route. Its founding conditions are at least three eligible independent governments, a settled dispute process, a viable communication route and explicit consent from every founding member. The partners may select economic federation, defensive federation or full political unification. The last option requires separate country-level approval and exact transfer rules. Human players are never absorbed by another country's focus completion alone.

If the implementation uses an existing Event 006 Balkan or Danubian formable, it must satisfy that formable's own verified registry, territory, consent and completion requirements. Membership in IMRO's network is not a substitute. A new duplicate formable family is unnecessary where the existing one can carry the accepted design through a documented adapter.

The regional map shows the actual partner countries and selected territorial obligations. Territorial qualification uses current state shapes. Diplomatic links are represented in the member list and selected-target panel if the engine lacks route arrows. Do not invent map capabilities or display a decorative map as if its borders were authoritative.

## Failure, recovery and international growth

The network can fracture over interference, breached rights, conflicting borders or failed protection. A member can withdraw, demand reform, suspend its contribution or oppose IMRO. A defensive coalition may survive with another coordinator. This is a meaningful result, not an automatic event reset.

A successful regional network improves the credibility of worldwide liberation. It can help Macedonia meet the outward-policy and Mandate conditions for Freedom or Death. It is useful but not an artificial mandatory checklist if another credible international liberation path exists. Worldwide inspiration still needs an independent surviving IMRO-led Macedonia and at least one actual subject to liberate.

Network achievements reward difficult consent, survival, delivered aid and dispute resolution. They do not unlock simply for clicking the evolution button or accumulating a list of nominal contacts.
