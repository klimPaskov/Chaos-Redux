# Focus tree architecture

## A large tree built around different ways to govern

The tree starts with survival and then expands into constitutional politics, industry, military organisation, territorial settlement and international liberation. The player should be able to pursue useful side branches while a political anchor is being prepared. Major route decisions carry lasting consequences and late rewards. The implementation chooses the final node count and coordinates while preserving every group, fork, prerequisite, decision unlock, tradeoff and payoff described here.

A group normally becomes several connected focuses where each step creates a distinct capability. Groups must not be collapsed into one generic bonus focus. Conversely, splitting a single effect among four near-identical focuses does not add depth. Regular work should have varied 35, 49 and 70-day pacing, corresponding to native five, seven and ten-week focus units. A short 21-day, three-week response is reserved for a genuine time-sensitive crisis. Derive the final duration from these native units and the capability's scale rather than assigning arbitrary day values. Long programme groups can contain several focuses and associated 120 to 240-day missions.

All labels below are working labels. Final names, descriptions and effect tooltips require a separate localisation pass. Focus icons are original assets for their actual concepts. A related institutional upgrade can reuse its own evolving visual language, while unrelated groups cannot share a generic political symbol merely to fill the tree.

## Architecture map

| Lane | Entry | Major forks | Convergence | Late payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Survival and constituent process | Verified independent country or additive existing-country integration | Emergency command versus civilian coalition, then constitutional route | Functioning home administration and constitutional commitment | Stable sovereign government with a defined military relationship |
| Civic republic | Constituent representation and minimum Mandate | Strong parliament versus local constitutional autonomy, both with enforceable rights | Civil oversight and recognised citizenship | Regional diplomacy, refuge and a voluntary liberation charter |
| Anarchist confederation | Local assemblies, civilian consent and dismantled permanent command privilege | Rural communes versus urban syndical coordination, with a later negotiated combination | Federated defence and recallable delegates | A durable stateless or confederal political order that can coordinate internationally without annexation |
| Socialist federation | Labour and peasant organisation plus political settlement | Independent federal socialism versus externally aligned party discipline | Planned industrial cooperation under the chosen sovereignty model | Balkan economic and political federation or a powerful aligned state |
| Sovereign revolutionary state | Consolidated executive and a credible security claim | Temporary developmental emergency versus permanent command state | Independent military and productive capacity | Territorial settlement or forceful revision, with an internal reform exit |
| Patron alignment | Accepted sponsor contract | Limited strategic treaty versus binding dependency | Delivered military and economic programme | An aligned regional power, with an earned route to renegotiate dependence |
| Productive life | Surviving administration | Land and food, workshops and transport, skilled institutions | Reliable home supply and reconstruction | Advanced national industry or a distributed federal production system |
| Defence | Actual forces and training stores | Territorial defence versus expeditionary concentration | Standardisation and logistics | A capable small army with meaningful doctrine choices |
| Territorial policy | Stable homeland | Negotiated claims, civic integration or military revision | Resolved specific Macedonian territorial question | Reunification milestone or a respected limited-border settlement |
| Balkan network | Evolution and real partner contacts | Sovereign association versus deeper federal compact, patron independence versus dependency | Joint material support and dispute procedures | A network that can survive the founder's weakness |
| Global Front | Freedom or Death completed | Delegated coordination versus central command, mutual aid versus coerced alignment | Regional victories and settlements | Durable liberation order or major IMRO command power |
| Radical evolution | Chaos at least 800 and prior global liberation | Defeat the takeover or commit to the fictional doctrine | A changed government and a real Front split | Conquest focuses, then a separate qualified world-end commitment |
| Recovery | Defeated radical apparatus or costly liberation peace | Restore viable prior institutions versus new constituent process | Reconstruction and protected sovereignty | A playable aftermath with truthful population losses |

Political routes are mutually exclusive after their constitutional commitment anchor. Preparatory civic, industrial and defence work may be shared. The patron relationship is a crosscutting foreign-policy commitment with a dedicated lane, not an extra ideology that must erase all domestic history. A binding subject bargain blocks independent-country evolutions until genuine independence is restored. It does not erase completed industrial work.

## Shared survival and constitution

| Anchor group | Order and prerequisite | Gameplay change | Cost, failure and interaction | AI and visible payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Establish public administration | First independent administration, after capital setup | Opens repair, supply and civil-service actions, transforms the provisional authority idea | Uses actual controlled territory. Loss of the capital pauses location-dependent projects | Every route prioritises this, with offices and reconstruction visible through decisions |
| Account for wartime stores | Parallel to administration | Reconciles arms, veteran cadres and captured matériel, opens reinforcement priorities | No free stockpile reward for equipment already owned | AI fills damaged units before making unnecessary new ones |
| Convene representation | Administration functioning and no immediate collapse | Opens the constitutional contest and minority protection commitments | Low Mandate requires outreach or coalition concessions before commitment | AI uses credible local support to choose a feasible constitution |
| Define emergency powers | Can precede the full assembly during war | Selects temporary constrained command or stronger executive discretion | Temporary powers expire or need renewal through an actual crisis. Permanent command hurts civic access | Changes political institution and later route entry |
| Settle civilian guarantees | Before any mature political payoff | Creates route-specific legal or delegate protections and a grievance channel | Breach creates a real domestic crisis and weakens international credibility | Enables consent-based integration and better Front retention |
| Commit the constitution | After relevant preparatory groups and public choice | Locks the chosen route, sets government and legitimate institution | No bypass may award competing constitutions. Existing-country integration respects completed exclusive routes | Distinct flag, governing institution, party presentation and branch filters |

The tree must show why a route is locked without exposing a page of hidden variables. A player can inspect the relevant Mandate threshold, treaty conflict or institutional prerequisite. A focus that loses a temporary condition pauses or becomes unavailable. A bypass is permitted only when the intended outcome already exists and cannot duplicate a reward.

## Civic republic

This route makes independence durable through citizenship, representation and recognised sovereignty. It has strong diplomacy and recovery, reliable defensive mobilisation and a broad voluntary coalition. Its limits are procedural time, constraints on coercive territorial expansion and the need to honour commitments even when inconvenient.

| Group | Internal progression and decision unlock | Reward and tradeoff | End-state and AI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Representative constitution | Constituency arrangements, local-rights settlement, civilian executive accountability | Upgrades political institution, opens coalition cabinet and civic reconciliation. It cannot instantly cure all grievances | AI favours it with high Mandate, a negotiated independence and useful democratic partners |
| Local government fork | Choose strong parliamentary administration or constitutionally protected local autonomy | Central option coordinates projects faster. Local option improves regional trust and makes abrupt coercive mobilisation harder | Both converge on accountable administration rather than one being a dead branch |
| Citizenship and return | Equal civic status, lawful return and property adjudication | Opens return and reconstruction missions through actual migrant ledgers. No population resurrection | A tangible refugee reception and civic integration role |
| Public investment | Independent procurement, skilled civil service, anti-corruption process | Construction and research capability, better contract quality, slower emergency requisition | Connects to industry without adding a fourth spirit |
| Constitutional defence | Civil oversight, reserve training, defensive planning | Reliable territorial units, defensive cooperation and mobilisation readiness | Reduced access to unprovoked expansion incentives |
| Recognition diplomacy | Border assurances, trade access, international mediation | Recognition and settlement options with stronger acceptance where treaties are credible | A route to secure sovereignty without conquering every claim |
| Liberation charter | Aid standards, sovereignty guarantee, accountable Front delegation | Front members keep more freedom and retain trust, while IMRO central authority grows slowly | Durable global cooperation and a hard achievement for keeping the charter under pressure |

The late payoff is a respected independent republic with a viable economy, civilian defence and the ability to shelter and support other independent nations. Civic success is not measured only by map size.

## Anarchist confederation

This route is designed around local consent, communal production and federated defence. It needs more coordination work to mount distant campaigns, but it can become resilient under invasion and highly effective at helping other communities govern themselves. It does not receive arbitrary permanent incompetence as a substitute for mechanics.

| Group | Internal progression and decision unlock | Reward and tradeoff | End-state and AI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer authority to assemblies | Local assemblies, revocable mandates, abolition of permanent revolutionary privilege | Replaces the political institution, opens delegate agreements and a peaceful leadership-transfer process | Needs Mandate at least 60 and no unresolved coercive patron contract |
| Communal production fork | Rural cooperative provisioning or urban syndical manufacture, then a negotiated coordination group | Rural first improves dispersed supply and recovery. Urban first accelerates workshops and technical training | Both can combine later through a real compact, with no free duplicate factory grants |
| Federate logistics | Inventory agreements, depot sharing, voluntary transport obligations | Opens supply compacts and makes local defence efficient | Distant operations require a funded route and consenting assemblies |
| Elected military command | Recall rules, trained staff, agreed emergency powers | Upgrades the defence institution and offers competent coordination without a permanent dictatorship | Repeated emergency extensions reduce Mandate and create a recall crisis |
| Education and technical exchange | Common schools, open technical institutes, partner exchange | Research capacity and bonuses tied to actual institutions | Does not invent a unique technology folder merely to display anarchist content |
| Voluntary internationalism | Aid to autonomous partners, equal delegate representation, freedom to leave | Balkan and NLF support with high retention and low coercive Authority growth | Stronger cooperative logistics and lower demand for central command |
| Defend the compact | Resolve local secession peacefully or negotiate war-time duties | Preserves a meaningful choice between federation and central coercion | Forcing permanent command exits the mature anarchist identity through an explicit constitutional crisis |

The late payoff is a functioning confederation whose strength comes from resilient production, consent and trained coordination. A player can lead worldwide liberation while keeping Front Authority below coercive thresholds. The 800 Chaos contest is particularly dangerous if emergency powers have repeatedly overridden recall. Strong, honoured institutions give a concrete route to defeat the radicals before they take control.

## Socialist and federalist route

This route links social transformation to negotiated regional institutions. It distinguishes independent federal cooperation from a patron-directed party state. Both can be militarily and economically useful, with different costs and future choices.

| Group | Internal progression and decision unlock | Reward and tradeoff | End-state and AI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Worker and peasant settlement | Land settlement, labour representation, a political congress | Political and productive institutions change, opening cooperative planning | Requires actual property and administration choices, not a flat stability grant |
| Sovereignty fork | Independent federal mandate or external party alignment | Independent path strengthens partner trust. Aligned path provides faster verified aid and doctrine access with obligations | AI assesses patron reliability and existing diplomatic danger |
| Plan complementary production | Home industry inventory, partner specialisation and arsenal agreements | Actual factories, production efficiency and partner shipments | No cloned industry, research slots or shared stockpiles |
| Federal constitution | Equal-state compact, representation and withdrawal procedure | Opens negotiated federal institutions and optional explicit unification votes | Partners can refuse. A country is not annexed because IMRO completes a focus |
| Social mobilisation | Technical education, medical and civil services, trained reserves | Better reconstruction and sustainable mobilisation | Service provision competes with immediate military output |
| External discipline dispute | Patron requests political alignment, bases or command | Accept, renegotiate or break the contract with real diplomatic and supply consequences | An aligned state can earn independence of policy through fulfilled obligations and diversified supply |
| Balkan federal leadership | Common standards, dispute resolution and regional defence | A powerful voluntary compact or a clearly defined aligned bloc | No claim that the game's global Front is a historical organisation |

The route's late payoff is material integration among willing countries. A federal compact can survive IMRO's defeat because the partners own its institutions collectively. Forced centralisation damages that compact and can feed the radical command route only through visible repudiation of the earlier constitution.

## Sovereign revolutionary state

This route preserves strong central revolutionary leadership. It can become a developmental state, a territorial revisionist power or a permanent emergency dictatorship. It has rapid mobilisation and concentrated projects, with more severe consequences from political exclusion, failed expeditions and the loss of command centres.

| Group | Internal progression and decision unlock | Reward and tradeoff | End-state and AI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consolidate the executive | Defined command hierarchy, security accountability choice, administrative appointments | Faster mobilisation and decisive project execution | Repression is not automatically required. Abusive choices reduce Mandate and partner trust |
| Diaspora and national reconstruction | Verified diaspora contacts, investment guarantees and home workshops | Trade, equipment procurement and industrial projects | Patron and diaspora interests can conflict, requiring a real choice |
| Emergency duration fork | Developmental emergency with a sunset or permanent command state | Temporary path opens later reform. Permanent path gains concentration with stronger legitimacy risk | AI chooses permanent command only when threat and political support make it credible |
| Independent officer corps | Cadre school, regular command, logistics and combined-arms preparation | Professional templates and military capabilities | Requires actual trained people and equipment |
| Territorial doctrine | Settle defensible borders or pursue broader Macedonian claims | Diplomatic settlement or bounded war-goal access | Revision brings regional opposition and cannot auto-core occupied mixed territories |
| Refuse patron ownership | Diversify supplies, expose coercive contracts, repay or repudiate obligations | Sovereignty and stronger independent diplomacy | Repudiation can lose deliveries or trigger a hostile patron response |
| Reform the revolution | Civil institutionalisation after survival and reconstruction | A late peaceful route to a constrained executive or civic transition | Must honour earlier harms and obligations, with no instant Mandate reset |

The permanent command branch has a more vulnerable radical-takeover profile, but ordinary nationalism does not automatically become the death doctrine. The player or AI must still pass the explicit 800 evolution contest and commitment sequence.

## Patron relationship lane

The lane can accompany a compatible constitution until an exclusive treaty or subject bargain conflicts with it. Its purpose is to make aid powerful and politically consequential.

| Group | Capability | Required consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accept a strategic mission | Training, advisers and a real equipment delivery | Named terms, duration, route and recipient acceptance |
| Build a patron-backed industry | A chosen factory or infrastructure project | Material delivery and a construction commitment, with a commercial or strategic concession |
| Permit military access | Reliable supply and emergency assistance | Actual access and the associated diplomatic risk |
| Dependency fork | Limited treaty or explicit subject status | The player sees the sovereignty consequence before commitment. Subject status blocks independent IMRO evolution gates |
| Renegotiate the contract | Diversified economy, repaid obligations and credible defence | A route to remove burdens without pretending the original aid was free |
| Break with the patron | Reject a coercive demand or leave dependency through a real conflict or settlement | Possible war, sanctions, lost support and domestic coalition changes |

Foreign sponsors choose among containment of rivals, strategic access, ideological affinity and commercial opportunity. They cannot own the network merely by paying once. A strong movement may pursue goals its patron opposes, and the sponsor has to respond to the changed facts.

## Productive life and research

The industry lane has independent early branches for food and land settlement, town repair, workshops, transport and technical institutions. A player can solve immediate supply while preparing a constitutional route. The mid-game convergence is reliable home production, followed by a choice between concentrated national arsenals and distributed partner production.

| Group | Concrete reward | Decision integration and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repair inhabited towns | Targeted repair and infrastructure projects | Costs and completion depend on actual damage, capacity and control |
| Settle rural production | Food and local supply resilience through the productive institution | Route-specific ownership and governance choice |
| Reopen workshops | A bounded arms-production project and a real factory where eligible | Requires free building slots and one completed-project receipt |
| Connect the homeland | Rail, roads, supply nodes and depots appropriate to current geography | Location-sensitive construction and defence missions |
| Train technicians | Fourth research slot or equivalent missing-capacity reward, plus relevant bonuses | Completion needs the education programme and functioning administration |
| National arsenal or distributed production | Concentrated output versus resilience and partner complementarity | Industry and supply advantages must differ materially |
| Advanced cooperation | Fifth research slot where not already present and specialised research bonuses | Needs mature industry or a genuine federal research compact |

Technologies and bonuses must match the country's equipment and era. Any changed technology, doctrine prerequisite, grant or research-bonus surface requires the technical graph inspection and compare workflow. The plan does not request a new technology folder solely for visual volume.

## Defence and strategic capability

The military lane begins with common training and stores, then branches into territorial defence and expeditionary concentration. Later it adds air defence, combined operations and a legitimate foreign training mission. Neither branch makes a small country instantly self-sufficient in every arm.

| Group | Capability and reward | Tradeoff and branch interaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve cadres | Retain experience, create a training mission, reinforce surviving units | Cadres committed to training are unavailable for immediate expansion |
| Standardise equipment | Compatible production and supply, regular template access | Paid refits retain resources correctly |
| Territorial defence | Prepared positions, terrain training and local supply | Strong home defence with limited distant concentration |
| Expeditionary command | Staff training, logistics and overseas support actions | Requires transport, route access and actual equipment |
| Support arms | Artillery, engineers and air defence when technology permits | No free units with unresearched equipment |
| Professional or confederated command | Upgrades the defence institution according to constitution | Professional command concentrates quickly, confederated command needs explicit common mandates |
| Liberator training mission | Actual instructors and volunteer support to a partner | Recipient consent and finite manpower, with cooldown and return receipts |
| Strategic endurance | War industry coordination, dispersed depots and reconstruction | Prepares the country to survive high Chaos without forcing the terminal route |

## Territorial settlement and reunification

This lane is separate from politics and industry. It first defines the claim and diplomatic position, then offers negotiation, local consent and military revision as distinct tools. Its actions operate on the verified state puzzle. A player can renounce claims through a recognised settlement and still achieve a strong national payoff.

The principal groups are a homeland border settlement, a Pirin diplomatic or local-organisation branch, an Aegean access and minority-guarantee branch, a cross-border transport agreement, wartime revision where authorised, civic integration after settled ownership, and the final reunification or limited-border compact. These groups have different counterparties and acceptance factors. Greece, Bulgaria, a Yugoslav successor, another occupier and an existing Macedonian state are evaluated from the current world, not assumed to own specific states forever.

The late reunification action requires every selected required state piece to be settled and integrated. It produces a meaningful country identity and diplomatic result. It does not add a Balkan empire route by changing the definition of Macedonia. Wider regional ambitions belong to consensual network and federal institutions, or to explicitly coercive conquest branches with the corresponding opposition.

## Balkan and worldwide lanes

The Balkan lane follows contact, support, political guarantees, shared logistics, dispute resolution and a sovereign-association or federal-compact fork. The global lane follows the completed worldwide declaration, regional emergency aid, a sovereignty charter, command delegation, common supply and a postwar-order fork. The separate network and Freedom or Death parts define the full actions, partner responses and payoff.

Delegated coordination gives high member retention and strong mutual aid without requiring high Front Authority. Central command can make IMRO much more powerful, but members demand tangible protection and may reject its interference. A focus opens negotiation and a potential benefit. It cannot assign another human player's command delegation without that player's action.

## The 800 Chaos branch and conquest

At Chaos 800 or higher, prior global liberation and independent survival reveal a radical political contest. Before that reveal, the tree does not advertise a guaranteed future extinction ending as if it were a normal national goal. An event-details warning may identify that extreme Chaos has additional consequences without revealing completed fiction as fact.

| Group | Condition and commitment | Mechanical result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confront the doctrine | Evolution revealed, movement institutions still exist | Opens the contest and defensive constitutional actions |
| Defeat the radical faction | Sufficient institutional support, protected command and successful crisis actions | Closes this takeover attempt, rewards restored consent and preserves the ordinary route |
| Transfer command to radicals | Explicit political commitment or successful bounded AI contest outcome | Changes government and institutions, creates the Front doctrine vote and visible defections |
| Repudiate national limits | Radical government and an actual surviving command | Opens conquest planning beyond Macedonian claims, with severe diplomatic rupture |
| Build an expeditionary apparatus | Real industry, command and logistics | Strong war capability and administrative reach, with costs and source-appropriate equipment |
| Begin universal conquest | Prior radical groups and an explicit focus commitment | Starts actual world-conquest policy and war-goal or declaration actions before the >1000 terminal lock if necessary |
| Claim command of the world Front | Major retained Front power and real member obligations | Opens the distinct terminal commitment test. It does not itself grant world-end status |
| Commit Freedom AND Death | Current Chaos strictly above 1000 and every terminal condition currently true | Locks the world-end scenario, fires its super-event and activates the progressive extermination campaign |

The radical conquest state is playable and dangerous even if it never qualifies for the terminal lock. Completing its conquest focuses matters. Simply crossing 800 changes what is possible, and crossing strictly above 1000 changes eligibility. Neither threshold performs the player's or regime's commitment automatically. The terminal focus, decision preview and completion callback all call the same read-only proposed predicate `imro_terminal_launch_is_valid`, whose full inputs are defined in [Freedom AND Death](110_imro_spec_part_7_freedom_and_death.md). No local abbreviated gate may substitute for it.

## Recovery and closure

A costly liberation peace opens reconstruction, treaty verification, demobilisation and the return of actual displaced survivors. Defeating the radical regime opens protection of survivors, accountable removal of its apparatus, restoration of a viable constitution and the confirmation of existing foreign independence. These are real branches with buildings, laws, diplomacy and population-system actions, not a single victory popup.

AI focus policy must understand current needs. A country losing its homeland prefers supply and defence over an optional claim. An anarchist government prepares delegate mandates before expeditionary war. A republic protects its charter when coercion would break it. A sponsor-dependent state secures alternative supply before repudiation. A radical government cannot choose unavailable terminal focuses with zero chance of meeting the required conditions.

## Layout, filters and acceptance

The final layout should have a compact common opening, distinct political wings and shared functional lanes with readable convergence. Reserve crisis branch space and avoid long connectors crossing unrelated routes. A small focus inlay may show the current constitutional commitment and links to the country decision category, using existing Organisation and Mandate. It must not become a second large management panel or add another meter. The inlay is optional if the native tree communicates the state clearly, and any implemented inlay needs its own reference and render evidence.

Provide filters for constitution, reconstruction, defence, territorial settlement, regional cooperation, global liberation, crisis and recovery. Hidden branches reveal only when their conditions or warnings are relevant. Missing content must be reported by route and group, even if the tree looks visually large. Acceptance requires every major branch to have an early capability, a middle decision change, a consequential fork or constraint and a late payoff, with route-specific AI and proper assets.

Provide Focus Navigation for branch families that the final layout places in spatially separate regions. Stable navigation anchors lead to the constitution, industry, defence, territorial, Balkan, global, crisis and recovery group roots where those regions actually exist. Hidden crisis entries remain hidden until their reveal conditions are met. Preserve the existing framework owner's navigation for a living-country overlay, add only the relevant IMRO destinations, and verify each destination in the native rendered tree. Navigation is a practical aid for the large graph, not a second list of every focus or a substitute for readable prerequisites.
