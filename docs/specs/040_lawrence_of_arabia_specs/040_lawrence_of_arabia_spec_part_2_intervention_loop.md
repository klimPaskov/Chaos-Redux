# Event 40: Intervention Loop

## Decision category role

The active target receives one Event 40 decision category. Britain receives a compact sponsor category or a target-selected subsection inside an existing Event 40 category. The two surfaces read the same campaign generation and active target.

The ordinary decision interface is sufficient. Use a strong static category picture and concise dynamic text. Do not build a dedicated scripted GUI for the baseline system.

The target category should answer four questions at a glance:

- How strong is Lawrence's Influence?
- Is it rising, stable, or falling?
- What is the next important threshold?
- Which current action or mission gives the government a practical response?

The British surface should answer:

- Which country is the active target?
- What access does Britain currently have?
- Which commitment is active?
- Which route, exposure, or resource problem is blocking further support?

## Visibility and clutter budget

A normal intervention phase shows three to five primary target actions. Six is the absolute maximum. Only one or two Event 40 missions should normally be active.

The category replaces obsolete actions as the campaign changes.

- Opening actions disappear after the access settlement.
- Basic counterintelligence actions are replaced by stronger exposure or detention actions when enough evidence exists.
- British supply actions disappear when the route is closed.
- Federation actions appear only when Evolution III and regional readiness make them relevant.
- Captivity actions replace ordinary British mission actions while Lawrence is detained.
- A settled target loses the active category and keeps only its durable outcome tools.

The player should never see every possible intervention action at once.

## Target action families

All names below are working labels.

### Terms of access

#### Set the Mission's Terms

The target defines Lawrence's legal status, travel rights, access to military offices, contact with local leaders, and use of government communications.

Possible policy forms include:

- broad access under government invitation
- military liaison access only
- travel under escort
- access limited to named regions
- no private contact with officers
- refusal of the mission

This opening choice changes starting Influence, British action availability, exposure, and future settlement routes. It is not a permanent one-click ideology choice. Later events can tighten or loosen the terms.

#### Accept Arms Under National Custody

The target accepts British weapons while requiring delivery to government depots and distribution through its own command.

Possible costs and requirements:

- command power
- army experience
- support equipment needed to inspect and distribute the shipment
- enough state capacity to hold the delivery

A successful action gives equipment and limited training while restricting Influence growth. Failure, corruption, or officer complicity can divert part of the shipment into British-backed local networks.

#### Open an Arab Liaison Office

The target creates a formal office that handles Lawrence, British officers, local leaders, and aid routes through one accountable institution.

The office can reduce uncontrolled access and create later independent federation links. It also gives Britain a clear point of contact and can raise Influence if the office is captured by pro-British officials.

#### Place Lawrence Under Escort

The target assigns an escort and limits private travel.

This ties down a small security commitment and may cost command power or support equipment. It lowers the chance of uncontrolled contact, but it can offend local allies, slow useful missions, and increase personal tension with Lawrence.

### Counterintelligence and exposure

#### Audit the Gold Ledger

The government traces British funds, payments to intermediaries, transport expenses, and officer stipends.

This action should use intelligence capacity, civilian administration, and time. A strong result reveals contacts and lowers Influence. A partial result finds irregularities without proving control. A failed audit can expose investigators and allow the network to clean its accounts.

#### Build a Counterintelligence File

The target begins a sustained investigation into agents, couriers, routes, and political contacts.

The action unlocks evidence-based decisions and improves future detection. It should not instantly remove the network. Countries with La Résistance can use agency strength, operative presence, decryption, and counterintelligence upgrades. Countries without the DLC use a fallback model based on stability, government capacity, encryption, local control, military presence, and relevant national spirits.

#### Turn a Contact

The government attempts to recruit one British-backed intermediary as a controlled source.

The action requires a mature file, a valid contact, and enough intelligence strength. Success gives a temporary view into British plans and can support a double-game route. Failure raises exposure and can increase Influence by showing the network that the government is vulnerable.

#### Feed Controlled Intelligence

The target passes selected information through a turned contact.

This can misdirect a British mission, protect a real route, or create a false impression of cooperation. Repeated use builds double-game readiness, but a detected deception can trigger British retaliation and internal scandal.

#### Expose the Network

The government publishes or privately circulates evidence of British operations.

Public exposure can sharply reduce promise credibility and help neighboring states prepare. It can also damage foreign relations, frighten cooperative officers, and provoke Britain into a rescue, cover-up, or government-replacement attempt.

The action should require enough evidence. It cannot be available from the first day.

### Restriction and removal

#### Restrict Tribal and Officer Travel

The government limits unsupervised movement and meetings in the active intervention regions.

This is a blunt security tool. It can slow Lawrence's contacts and reduce sabotage risk, but it strains local support, military cooperation, and ordinary administration. It should never be a free Influence reduction.

#### Detain the Mission

The target arrests Lawrence and selected staff.

The action requires a low enough British protection state, sufficient evidence or a severe security incident, and enough local force to hold the detainees. A reckless detention can trigger officer revolt or a British recovery operation.

#### Expel the Mission

The target orders Lawrence and British staff to leave.

Expulsion is safer than detention but leaves more of the local network intact. It succeeds cleanly when Influence is low and the target controls routes. At higher Influence it can create refusals, local demonstrations, mutiny, or an Evolution I uprising.

#### Dismantle the Contact Network

The government conducts a broader cleanup after exposure or expulsion.

The action targets money channels, arms caches, communications, compromised offices, and local cells. It should run as a mission with a deadline and partial outcomes. A complete result creates regional resistance memory and can reduce British regional reach.

### Bargaining and independent use

#### Offer an Independent Charter

The target offers Lawrence a public political program based on sovereignty, accountable aid, and regional cooperation outside direct British control.

This action begins the rare personal-defection route. It is visible only when the target has built trust, retained political independence, and shown that it can protect the mission without serving British policy. It does not cause immediate defection.

#### Play Britain Against a Rival

The target requests British aid by pointing to a nearby enemy or sponsor.

The action can secure equipment, guarantees, intelligence, or military access while limiting broader concessions. A repeated strategy increases dependence and gives Britain leverage to demand a formal settlement.

#### Balance Foreign Sponsors

The government accepts limited aid or recognition from another valid power to reduce dependence on Britain.

The action must identify a real sponsor with access and political reason to help. It cannot conjure a generic foreign benefactor. Sponsor competition can preserve independence, but it can also increase instability and turn the target into a contested arena.

#### Preserve National Custody

The target consolidates British aid, trained officers, and liaison institutions under its own laws after keeping Influence below the dominant bands.

This is one route toward a sovereign armed partner or independent ally settlement.

## British action families

### Resource and route commitments

#### Authorize Gold and Arms

Britain sends a mixed package of political funds, infantry equipment, support equipment, and advisers.

The package scales with target size, British industry, route distance, and current Influence. Britain must pay the real stockpile and political costs. An intercepted or mismanaged package can be partly lost.

#### Send Officer Cadres

Britain commits training officers, liaison staff, and specialist advisers.

This can improve target military performance and raise Influence among the officer corps. The action should create a temporary British personnel commitment and an exposure risk.

#### Secure the Red Sea Route

Britain commits convoys, escorts, fuel, and port access to an intervention route.

This action should matter most for the Peninsula and Red Sea corridor. It is unavailable when Britain lacks a plausible route. A successful mission reduces delivery time and supports later regional nodes.

#### Open the Desert Air Route

Britain uses air transport, airbases, and fuel to maintain contact with a remote target.

This is a stronger but more expensive route that becomes more important under Evolution II. It should require actual air access and sufficient transport capability or an event-owned approximation grounded in airbase control and fuel.

#### Repair the Rail and Telegraph Link

Britain helps restore a route that serves both military supply and political communication.

The action can create real railway, infrastructure, supply, or communication effects in named states. It should not grant abstract logistics bonuses without a geographic consumer.

### Political penetration

#### Back the Officer Corps

Britain directs training, money, and promises toward sympathetic officers.

This raises Influence quickly in a weak government and carries a high exposure risk. Under Evolution I it can prepare defections or a government replacement.

#### Expand the Radio and Press Network

Britain funds newspapers, radio messages, public meetings, and local intermediaries.

The action changes political support and promise credibility. It should be less effective in countries with strong censorship, secure communications, or exposed British contradictions.

#### Promise Constitutional Guarantees

Britain offers a public or private guarantee that the target will retain internal autonomy.

The promise can lower resistance and support an independent ally route. Repeated broken promises reduce credibility across the whole region.

#### Tie Aid to Bases and Access

Britain offers stronger aid in exchange for military access, docking rights, airfields, intelligence offices, or supply privileges.

This creates a clear tradeoff. The target receives a meaningful package and accepts a visible loss of freedom.

#### Invite a Favored Partner Conference

Britain gathers existing clients and the active target to demonstrate the benefits of regional cooperation.

This appears under Evolution II. Earlier clients can contribute diplomatic pressure, officers, equipment, and route access. The active target can attend, refuse, or use the meeting to coordinate autonomy demands.

### Crisis and recovery

#### Attempt a Recovery Operation

Britain tries to extract a detained Lawrence.

The action needs intelligence, local contacts, and a plausible route. It risks violence, exposure, and diplomatic rupture. A failed operation can kill Lawrence, strengthen the target, or accelerate an anti-British coalition.

#### Negotiate an Exchange

Britain offers material, diplomatic, or prisoner concessions for Lawrence's release.

The target can accept, demand more, expose the agreement, or use the talks to seek recognition.

#### Disavow an Overexposed Mission

Britain publicly distances itself from Lawrence and selected agents.

This lowers immediate diplomatic damage and abandons the current network. It reduces British regional credibility and can increase the chance that Lawrence defects if a credible independent host exists.

#### Reinforce a Client Government

Britain supports a settled client facing revolt, invasion, or autonomy pressure.

The action can provide equipment, advisers, access, or limited intervention. It cannot endlessly farm free units or permanently protect a failed client without cost.

## Mission families

### Trace the Gold Ledger

- Normal duration: 100 to 150 days
- Owner: target
- Purpose: identify funding routes and compromised offices
- Active requirement: a live British financial network
- Success: evidence, lower Influence, turned-contact opportunity
- Partial success: evidence without a clean Influence reduction
- Failure: network cleans records, investigators exposed, temporary Influence increase

The mission should progress faster with a strong intelligence service, secure government records, and controlled ports or borders.

### Guard the Rail, Port, and Telegraph Nodes

- Normal duration: 120 to 180 days
- Owner: target
- Purpose: secure the real infrastructure used by the intervention
- Objective: hold or garrison named states, ports, rail hubs, or communications routes
- Success: route control, lower British reach, reduced sabotage chance
- Partial success: one route secured while another remains exposed
- Failure: smuggling, sabotage, or a British delivery breaks through

Targets must be selected from actual relevant states. Do not show generic instructions such as guard the required states.

### Secure the Officer Corps

- Normal duration: 120 to 180 days
- Owner: target or Britain, with opposing objectives
- Purpose: determine whether senior and regional officers follow the government or the British-backed network
- Target actions: rotations, oaths, pay, training, arrests, independent command reform
- British actions: training, patronage, equipment, personal guarantees
- Success: a durable shift in Influence and future coup risk
- Failure: defections, resignations, or a divided command

### Keep the Arms Route Open

- Normal duration: 90 to 140 days
- Owner: Britain
- Objective: retain a valid route and complete a delivery
- Requirements: convoys, fuel, port or air access, and sufficient stockpile
- Success: delivered aid and stronger regional reach
- Partial success: reduced delivery, higher exposure
- Failure: loss, interception, or diversion of the package

### Expose the Network

- Normal duration: 90 to 150 days
- Owner: target
- Objective: collect enough evidence and survive British countermeasures
- Success: public or diplomatic exposure, neighboring preparedness, major Influence loss
- Partial success: private leverage for bargaining or double-game use
- Failure: witnesses disappear, files are discredited, government reputation suffers

### Convene an Arab Congress

- Normal duration: 150 to 240 days
- Owner: a sovereign target or regional coalition
- Evolution relevance: II and III
- Objective: gather enough valid governments, maintain security, and agree on a public charter
- Success: counter-bloc, autonomy compact, or federation-readiness gain
- Partial success: bilateral agreements and shared intelligence
- Failure: Britain divides the participants, a rival sponsor captures the meeting, or members walk out

### Break the Revolt Cells

- Normal duration: 120 to 210 days
- Owner: target
- Evolution relevance: I
- Objective: secure named transport and political centers, disrupt arms caches, and retain officer loyalty
- Success: cell collapse and strong Influence reduction
- Partial success: one network family survives
- Failure: sabotage, officer defection, local uprising, or coup attempt

## Cost design

No decision or gameplay-changing action may spend more than four distinct cost types.

Costs should match the action and scale with the country.

### Target cost families

- political power for laws, negotiations, public charters, and government appointments
- command power for escorts, garrisons, officer rotations, and detention
- army experience for reorganizing command and integrating foreign training
- support equipment for security, communications, depots, and inspections
- infantry equipment for loyal guards or local forces
- fuel, trains, or convoys for route control and transport
- stability or war support for exposure, repression, and public confrontation
- civilian factory burden for liaison offices, infrastructure, and secure depots
- temporary division commitments for route missions
- intelligence exposure as a consequence of failed deception

### British cost families

- infantry and support equipment
- fuel, convoys, trains, or aircraft commitments
- political power for promises, guarantees, and patronage
- command power for officer or recovery operations
- civilian factory burden for infrastructure and aid
- intelligence exposure
- military access obligations
- client autonomy concessions

Routine actions must not all cost political power. Large military or logistical actions must consume real material or capacity.

## Intelligence integration

Intelligence strength matters at every stage, but the event must remain playable without La Résistance.

### With La Résistance

Relevant factors can include:

- agency existence and upgrade quality
- counterintelligence
- decryption advantage
- operative presence
- infiltration or network strength
- captured agents and resistance contacts

### Without La Résistance

The fallback model should combine:

- stability
- government and administrative capacity
- encryption and decryption technology
- control of capital, ports, and relevant routes
- military or police presence in named states
- national spirits tied to intelligence or security
- prior Event 40 preparedness
- recent civil conflict or government disruption

The fallback should preserve the same decisions and outcomes. It should not make DLC ownership a requirement for resisting Lawrence.

## AI strategy profiles

Each active target receives one primary AI strategy profile. The profile can change after a coup, ideology change, war, or major Event 40 incident.

### Cooperative client

This government values British protection and accepts high Influence.

It prefers arms, officer training, guarantees, bases, and a client settlement. It resists only when Britain cannot deliver or when another patron is clearly stronger.

### Sovereign pragmatist

This government wants British help under national custody.

It prefers liaison offices, controlled arms, independent ally settlements, and balanced sponsors. It acts against Influence when dominance becomes likely.

### Counterintelligence state

This government treats the mission as a security threat.

It prioritizes audits, escorts, route control, turned contacts, detention, exposure, and dismantlement. It avoids reckless arrest when Influence is too high for a safe confrontation.

### Anti-imperial challenger

This government seeks to defeat the British network and build regional resistance.

It favors exposure, an Arab congress, support for resistant neighbors, and a sovereign federation route. It can court Lawrence personally only when a credible political program exists.

### Opportunistic double gamer

This government accepts aid while building the capacity to deny Britain its settlement.

It prioritizes turned contacts, controlled intelligence, national custody, sponsor balance, and a late betrayal. It should use this route only with enough intelligence and political capacity.

### Fractured government

This government cannot maintain a consistent line.

Its actions depend on officer loyalty, stability, war, and local crisis. It is vulnerable to Evolution I coups and uprisings. It can still recover through successful missions and outside support.

## British AI plan

Britain evaluates the value and cost of each intervention.

Britain should commit heavily when:

- the target controls a strategic route, port, oil region, air corridor, or border
- existing clients can support the mission
- Britain has surplus equipment and secure access
- a rival power is gaining regional influence
- the target is unstable enough for a quick settlement
- Evolution II makes the target important to the regional system

Britain should limit, pause, or abandon the mission when:

- its own war and supply position is severe
- the route is unsafe
- equipment or convoy losses are unsustainable
- the target has overwhelming counterintelligence and low strategic value
- repeated exposure has destroyed promise credibility
- Lawrence is captured and a rescue is unlikely to succeed
- a client would require permanent subsidies that exceed its regional value

Britain must not spend itself into a broken loop merely because an action is available.

## Outcome confirmation and anti-spike rules

Influence thresholds use confirmation windows.

Suggested design anchors:

- a dominant settlement normally requires at least 30 days at `95+`
- an embedded client settlement normally requires at least 60 days at `80+` with a valid institution or government route
- a clean dismantlement normally requires low Influence plus completed exposure or cleanup work
- an independent ally settlement normally requires sustained middle-band control and a negotiated agreement
- a personal-defection outcome requires hidden conditions across several stages

A single incident cannot move the value from safe control to final settlement without an extraordinary validated crisis. Large changes should be bounded and explained.

## Exploit controls

Implementation must prevent:

- repeated British aid without stockpile payment
- repeated target equipment rewards after the same delivery
- cycling access policies to farm bonuses
- arresting and releasing Lawrence to repeat concessions
- reselecting a settled country without a genuine government replacement
- forming several incompatible regional systems at once
- duplicating Lawrence across adviser, commander, operative, and leader roles
- duplicating units or stockpiles during federation formation
- using a client settlement to escape all autonomy costs
- using a failed intervention to generate unlimited negative or positive Chaos
- repeated mission success farming after the relevant network has been removed

Use generation IDs, one-shot receipt flags, outcome memory, cooldowns, target validity, and explicit cleanup.

## Settlement memory

Every resolved target stores one durable settlement identity:

- British client
- independent British ally
- sovereign armed partner
- restricted or neutral settlement
- expelled mission
- dismantled network
- anti-British coalition member
- Lawrence-aligned independent partner
- federation member

A later government change can modify this identity, but it must use a bounded transition. It cannot silently clear history and allow the entire opening package to repeat.
