# Event 40: Evolutions

## Evolution structure

Event 40 has three true evolutions. Ordinary interventions, target settlements, arrests, aid deliveries, revolts, and federation negotiations remain baseline stages or consequences. They do not become evolution log entries.

Evolution activation gives zero Chaos. Each evolution unlocks new behavior. Chaos changes only when the unlocked behavior produces a concrete destabilizing or stabilizing result.

Every evolution supports two entry paths.

- Active-event entry changes a campaign that is already running.
- Pre-fire evolved opening changes the first intervention when Event 40 has not yet fired.

An evolution that is disabled through Event Details must not set its recorded flag, unlock its content, or block baseline completion.

## Evolution I: The Arab Revolt Network

- Minimum Chaos: `200`
- Evolution log role: the intervention develops a direct underground and revolt capacity
- Working evolution type: regional intervention escalation
- Working stage: `1`

### Design promise

British-backed contacts can move from persuasion and patronage into armed internal pressure. Smugglers, officers, local leaders, railway workers, couriers, political clubs, and irregular fighters can support sabotage, mutiny, revolt, or a government replacement.

The network remains tied to real routes and local political conditions. It is not a random disaster generator.

### Active-event entry

When Evolution I activates during an intervention:

- the active target receives a first cell incident
- existing British access determines which cell family appears
- Britain gains revolt-network actions
- the target gains cell-detection and suppression missions
- high Influence becomes more dangerous because expulsion can trigger armed resistance
- low Influence gives the target a better chance to break cells before they mature
- earlier exposed contacts reduce the initial cell footprint
- Lawrence's character state changes the network's coordination quality

A captured, expelled, defected, or dead Lawrence reduces coordination. Local agents can still act when the network was already established.

### Pre-fire evolved opening

When Evolution I is active before Event 40 first fires:

- the first target begins with a larger contact network
- Britain commits a stronger opening package
- one relevant route or officer group already contains British-backed contacts
- the target receives an early sign of arms movement, payments, or officer disloyalty
- starting Influence is higher, but the target also receives an immediate counterintelligence response

The opening must remain contestable. Evolution I does not start with an automatic coup or civil war.

### Cell families

The event selects cell families according to geography and current access.

#### Arms and smuggling cells

These cells use ports, desert crossings, frontier roads, or allied borders to move equipment.

They create delivery missions, cache discoveries, convoy losses, and local armed groups. They are vulnerable to route control and turned couriers.

#### Railway and telegraph cells

These cells target railways, supply hubs, bridges, and communications.

They can damage infrastructure, delay reinforcement, cut supply, or create a military opening. Targets must be named, controlled, and relevant to the current intervention.

#### Officer cells

These cells use training relationships, patronage, equipment access, and political promises to recruit officers.

They can produce resignations, delayed orders, depot seizure, unit defection, or a coup attempt. They require real command weakness and cannot instantly take over a consolidated state.

#### Tribal and local-leader cells

These cells organize regional resistance, escorts, scouts, safe routes, and irregular forces.

Their strength depends on local support, government treatment, terrain, prior promises, and British access. The event must avoid treating every rural community as one interchangeable tribe.

#### Political and press cells

These cells spread a British-backed settlement, publicize government weakness, and coordinate demonstrations or elite defections.

They are strongest where Britain has a credible protection offer and weakest after public proof of broken promises.

### Evolution I incidents

Possible incidents include:

- an intercepted arms column
- a railway demolition attempt
- missing payroll funds
- a commander who refuses a transfer
- an officer clique demanding a new government
- local leaders offering access in return for recognition
- a British radio network operating outside government control
- an assassination attempt against a security official
- a seized depot
- a limited provincial revolt
- a coup attempt

Each incident needs a cause, target, response, success result, failure result, and cleanup path.

### Government replacement

Britain can replace an uncooperative government only when all of these are true:

- Evolution I is enabled
- Influence is in a high band
- the government is unstable or the officer corps is divided
- a British-backed coalition exists
- Britain has enough access and resources to support the attempt
- the replacement government has a valid leader or institution
- the country remains playable and its armed forces do not disappear

The result can be a coup, palace settlement, officer takeover, or provisional client administration. It does not require a full civil war when a political transfer is credible.

A civil war requires a real territorial and military split. It must create viable sides, valid capitals, forces, equipment, supply, AI, and rollback logic.

### Containment and failure

Strong counterintelligence can break the network before it reaches armed action. A target can also split the network by offering local reforms, buying out intermediaries, securing routes, rotating officers, or exposing contradictory British promises.

Heavy repression can lower cell strength while damaging stability and local support. The result must state the tradeoff.

## Evolution II: The British Arabian System

- Minimum Chaos: `400`
- Evolution log role: separate interventions become a coordinated regional order
- Working evolution type: regional system formation
- Working stage: `2`

### Design promise

Earlier British clients and allies begin helping later interventions. Britain gains a network of ports, railways, air routes, intelligence offices, officers, arms standards, commercial agreements, and diplomatic pressure.

Clients remain political actors. They can cooperate, compete for favored status, demand autonomy, refuse a new obligation, or help dismantle the system from inside.

### Active-event entry

When Evolution II activates during an existing campaign:

- every settled client or ally is assessed as a possible regional node
- Britain can establish shared institutions from real participants
- the current target sees which neighboring nodes support the intervention
- clients receive limited decisions to contribute, refuse, bargain, or demand compensation
- independent states can begin a counter-bloc
- Lawrence can act as a conference organizer or regional adviser

No institution appears without a valid participant and consumer.

### Pre-fire evolved opening

When Evolution II is active before the first firing, the opening may begin with a partially established network only when the current world already contains valid British clients, subjects, bases, guarantees, or regional access.

The event can recognize existing British relationships and convert them into Event 40 nodes. It cannot create clients or rewrite sovereignty merely because the Chaos threshold is high.

The first target begins under visible regional pressure. It may receive invitations, warnings, arms offers, or counter-bloc requests before Lawrence arrives.

### Regional institutions

All names are working labels.

#### Arabian Liaison Conference

A periodic meeting of Britain, clients, allies, and invited targets.

The conference can coordinate arms, routes, defense, and political settlements. Clients can use it to form a joint autonomy position.

#### Arms Standardization Office

A shared system for equipment, training, repair, and officer exchanges.

It improves practical military cooperation and creates dependence on British supply. It must use real equipment and training effects, not a vague military bonus alone.

#### Regional Intelligence Office

A shared intelligence and counter-subversion network.

It helps Britain find resistant contacts and helps clients monitor one another. A compromised office can expose the entire system.

#### Desert Air Route

A chain of airbases, fuel commitments, transport links, and landing rights.

The route improves intervention speed and regional supply where geography supports it. It can be severed by loss of bases, fuel, or air access.

#### Oil and Transport Agreements

Britain and regional partners exchange protection, investment, supply access, oil, rail, port, and road privileges.

The agreements create real economic and strategic effects. They also create autonomy disputes and foreign competition.

#### Joint Defense Charter

Participants agree to consultation, access, and limited mutual support.

The charter can use an existing British faction where appropriate. It should not create a second incompatible faction only to display the event.

### Favored partner rivalry

Clients can compete to become Britain's preferred regional partner.

Benefits can include:

- priority equipment
- infrastructure projects
- regional conference leadership
- intelligence access
- officer training
- greater say in later interventions

Costs can include:

- deeper dependence
- obligations to support Britain
- rivalry with other clients
- resentment inside the target country
- reduced federation flexibility

The rivalry remains qualitative or event-driven. It does not need a second public global meter.

### Client autonomy routes

A client can remain loyal, seek gradual autonomy, coordinate a collective autonomy demand, defect to a counter-bloc, or join an independent federation project.

Britain can offer concessions, punish defiance, replace a government under valid Evolution I conditions, or accept a looser alliance.

### Independent counter-bloc

A counter-bloc becomes available when at least two credible independent governments oppose British encirclement. Three participants should normally be required for a durable regional institution.

The counter-bloc needs:

- a founding charter
- membership and refusal rules
- a shared intelligence or diplomatic purpose
- a route for military cooperation
- an exit or expulsion rule
- a failure state
- AI behavior

It should not form because one country clicked a decision while every invited state refused.

Working outcomes include:

- shared counterintelligence
- mutual consultation
- route warnings
- arms cooperation
- coordinated exposure of British promises
- preparation for an Arab congress

Failure can lead to member exits, British penetration, sponsor rivalry, or one member accepting a private settlement.

### System failure

The British Arabian System can weaken or collapse when:

- Britain loses its regional access
- several clients defect
- the regional intelligence office is exposed
- Britain cannot supply agreed arms or projects
- client rivalry becomes open conflict
- a counter-bloc wins recognition
- Lawrence defects or is killed during a system crisis
- Evolution III supersedes the network with a federation

## Evolution III: Lawrence's Arabia

- Minimum Chaos: `600`
- Evolution log role: the accumulated regional network becomes a unification project
- Working evolution type: Arabian federation transformation
- Working stage: `3`

### Design promise

The intervention network, client system, counter-bloc, and independent Arab institutions can be reorganized into one larger political project. The federation is earned through participants, routes, leadership, political consent, and military security.

The strongest existing client or sovereign member normally becomes the core. Lawrence can become an architect, adviser, commander, or rare central ruler. He does not automatically replace local governments.

### Active-event entry

When Evolution III activates during an existing campaign:

- federation readiness is calculated from current settlements
- valid core candidates are identified
- eligible governments receive congress or unification actions
- Britain receives a choice between direct control, looser alignment, or opposition
- Lawrence's role is assessed from loyalty, trust, captivity, health, and political standing
- the active intervention can continue while the federation negotiations begin

### Pre-fire evolved opening

When Evolution III is active before Event 40 first fires, the first intervention presents regional unification as a declared long-term objective.

The event still begins with one active target and a real intervention contest. It does not create a federation from no prior support. Starting readiness can recognize existing British clients, Arab alliances, independent regional organizations, and valid political claims.

### Federation readiness

Readiness is hidden. The player sees a concise qualitative statement and the unmet public requirements.

Readiness considers:

- number of durable participating governments
- geographic connection or valid transport links
- functioning ports, railways, air routes, or allied borders
- shared military or intelligence institutions
- public sovereignty agreements
- client autonomy settlements
- British support or inability to prevent formation
- strength of a counter-bloc or Arab congress
- Lawrence's role and personal trust
- existence of a legitimate core government
- absence of unresolved war between required members

A federation should normally require at least three durable participating governments or an equivalent political and territorial base. Two governments can begin negotiations but should not usually form the full regional state.

### Formation process

Formation uses a staged process.

1. A sponsor or sovereign member calls a congress.
2. Participants choose representation, autonomy, defense, foreign policy, and economic arrangements.
3. A core government is selected through valid strength and legitimacy rules.
4. Members ratify or refuse the charter.
5. Territory, armed forces, stockpiles, leaders, subjects, and wars transfer through one validated transaction.
6. The new state begins with integration problems and staged core decisions.

A failed congress can create a looser league, return governments to separate settlements, split the region into rival blocs, or trigger a limited conflict when the failure has a real military cause.

### Outcome A: British Arabia

British Arabia is a large British-aligned federation, dominion, or protectorate.

Requirements emphasize:

- dominant British regional influence
- several clients or dependent partners
- functioning British routes and institutions
- a core government willing or unable to resist the arrangement
- British capacity to sustain the federation

Britain gains bases, supply access, commercial and oil advantages, intelligence cooperation, and a powerful regional partner. The federation receives military, industrial, and administrative support.

The federation retains internal politics. It can seek autonomy through its focus tree and decisions.

### Outcome B: Independent Arab Federation

The Independent Arab Federation forms when sovereign governments, an Arab congress, a counter-bloc, or a defected Lawrence convert the regional network into an independent state.

Requirements emphasize:

- a credible sovereign charter
- enough independent or autonomy-seeking participants
- retained national custody of forces and aid
- a legitimate core government
- the ability to defend the settlement
- British acceptance, defeat, overreach, or inability to prevent formation

This route can preserve friendly relations with Britain, adopt nonalignment, or oppose British regional policy according to earlier choices.

### Outcome C: Lawrence's Kingdom

Lawrence's Kingdom is a rare high-Chaos personal settlement. It can be a monarchy, military federation, or personal union according to the countries and institutions that support it.

It requires all of these conditions:

- Evolution III is enabled
- Lawrence is alive and active
- he is not simultaneously a British operative, target adviser, or prisoner
- personal trust is exceptionally high
- Britain has overreached, disavowed him, lost control, or accepted the break
- a credible regional base exists
- at least three governments or an equivalent territorial coalition support formation
- no accepted local sovereign claimant has a stronger uncontested right, unless that claimant consents, joins the settlement, or has politically collapsed
- Lawrence has achieved major military, diplomatic, or constitutional success
- the player or AI has completed a chain of earlier choices that built personal authority

The route is never a simple event option. AI willingness is extremely low and should depend on a specific rare strategy profile.

The resulting state must avoid the claim that Lawrence alone created Arabia. Local institutions, councils, commanders, and rulers remain part of the country package and focus tree.

### Federation super-events

Each federation outcome has a separate regional-order super-event package.

- British Arabia uses imperial ceremony, coordinated bases and routes, and an uneasy federal settlement.
- Independent Arab Federation uses a congress, public charter, local leadership, and visible sovereignty.
- Lawrence's Kingdom uses the strange elevation of a British liaison into a regional ruler, with local supporters and rivals visible in the scene.

These are not world-end scenarios. They do not set the shared `world_end` state.

## Evolution Chaos consequences

The following values are design anchors. Centralize them in Event 40 tuning and audit them during implementation.

| Concrete outcome | Event-owned Chaos change | Guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First successful covert government replacement | `+5` | once per campaign |
| First cross-border revolt network | `+5` | once per campaign |
| Lawrence defects from Britain | `+5` | once |
| British Arabian System is formally established | `+10` | once |
| British Arabia forms | `+15` | once |
| Independent Arab Federation forms through a negotiated settlement | `-5` | once |
| Lawrence's Kingdom forms | `+20` | once |
| Networks are fully dismantled in at least three countries | `-10` | once |
| A counter-bloc ends the campaign through a negotiated regional settlement | `-5` | once |

Wars, puppeting, annexation, ideology change, deaths, and other shared sources remain owned by the Chaos Meter system. Event 40 must not register them again.
