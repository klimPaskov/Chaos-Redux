# Event 131: Immigrations

## Part 2: Responses and outcomes

### 1. Response structure

The initial report explains the situation and directs the affected player to the existing Migration panel. Operational choices remain with shared migration, national policy, and humanitarian action owners. A report acknowledgement does not purchase services, change a law, admit people, or complete the crisis.

Response families below are working labels, not final localisation. They group the brief's intended choices. Existing action identifiers and current action names are implementation references, not proposed new buttons.

The useful choice changes with the player's role. The origin can improve conditions, prepare safe departure, negotiate access, or support a later voluntary return. A prospective host can prepare a real reception site, consent to an exact request, propose onward arrangements, or refuse. A current host can distribute, integrate, resettle, or arrange return for the people actually present. A donor can support a valid delivery of material aid.

### 2. Response families

| Family | When it is useful | Required action and cost meaning | Result and tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Controlled admission | A real incoming request names this host and an appropriate reception state | Use current law, host consent, route, food, transport, and full headroom checks | Admit surviving arrivals within capacity. Control does not remove the continuing support obligation |
| Expand emergency reception | A genuine local threat, displaced cohort, or exact incoming request justifies a site | Pay the existing shelter, medical, supply, and transport costs for a real service improvement | More usable reception capacity when the owner proves the improvement. Paying alone does not move people |
| Distribute arrivals | One hosting state is crowded while other reachable domestic states can receive people | Choose valid receiving states and provide the shared transport and reception requirements | Relieve local pressure through actual internal transfers. Preserve the episode and cohort lineage |
| Onward transit | The current host can support passage but lacks a durable settlement arrangement | Obtain the next destination's consent and a supported route, then meet transport and temporary reception needs | Move the same living people onward. The current host remains responsible until the next transfer succeeds |
| International aid | A real shortage has a valid recipient, delivery route, and willing donor | Use a shared humanitarian donor or delivery contract, with actual payer and delivered-resource receipts | Improve actual food, transport, shelter, or medical provision. A promise of aid supplies no capacity |
| Restrict or refuse entry | The government chooses restriction through its actual law and available operation | Apply native law and exact request handling. Preserve the real whereabouts of affected people | Stop this admission or constrain movement. People wait or seek another route, with consequences from the resulting facts |
| Local integration | People have arrived and their current host can meet the shared settlement conditions | Complete the existing integration action with its time, safety, and support requirements | End reception obligations once while residents remain in host population |
| Safe resettlement or return | A current cohort has a willing safe destination, or its original home is genuinely viable again | Use the appropriate shared resettlement or voluntary-return contract, including route and destination proof | Resolve the cohort through measured movement and its existing terminal outcome |

For international aid, the implementation pass must find the actual shared consumer. The inspected list of migration panel operations does not by itself prove that a donor-facing aid button exists. Use the current humanitarian donor and delivery path where it supports the required response. If a small missing connection prevents this family, add it under the shared owner with equivalent human and AI access. Do not represent an unimplemented donation as available gameplay.

### 3. Origin actions

Improving conditions should reduce the reason people need to leave when the relevant owner confirms that the conditions actually improved. Food relief can address a food crisis. Safe housing, restored access, and the end of a specific danger can support a durable resolution. The event should not offer a generic payment that deletes displacement regardless of its cause.

Before departure, safe resolution in place can cancel the exact accepted intention. This is a distinct outcome from voluntary return after crossing a border. If people are already elsewhere, origin recovery makes return worth considering. It does not erase the cohort, restore absent population automatically, or compel their return.

Existing evacuation and corridor actions can organize movement when the origin has the necessary departure point, transport, local authority, and valid target. A country cannot prepare an unrelated quiet island simply because some other state in the country has a crisis. Class-specific evacuation follows the existing owner inputs and costs. Event 131 does not invent a census of workers, administrators, or vulnerable people.

Calm voluntary movement uses the same physical safeguards. The event does not create a separate employment simulation or a free industrial reward. Perceived opportunity belongs in the report's explanation unless an existing owner supplies a stronger measured fact.

### 4. Prospective host actions

The prospective host needs to see that a request exists, which state would receive people, what it can support now, and what preparation is missing. Intention and requested destination remain explicitly provisional until actual passage.

Opening emergency reception should prepare the exact requested location through the current service and payment owners. Acceptance follows fresh cohort and headroom checks. Physical passage then revalidates the current facts. A valid acceptance can become impossible if control changes, the route is lost, or capacity is consumed before the transfer. That failure must remain understandable to the player.

A rejected or expired request clears only its exact bilateral request mirrors and pending action state. The migration owner retains the people, the original request lineage, and any unresolved pressure. Its existing retry rules can reuse the retained row. Repeated refusals do not create another cohort each time.

Where full headroom is required for a retained prospective contract, insufficient capacity prevents that contract from passing. An implementation may use a supported shared split operation only if it preserves the exact parent and child identities, total obligation, and fresh consent. Quietly reducing the row to fit the host is unacceptable.

### 5. Current host and transit actions

The host should have usable ways to reduce pressure after admission. Distribution, transit, integration, safe resettlement, and voluntary return must remain available under their actual requirements even when the state is overloaded. The same overload should constrain further admission where the shared owner requires more headroom.

Internal distribution after an international arrival is a legitimate downstream action. It does not retroactively change the original crossing or count as a second immigration episode. Resettlement and return likewise operate on the cohort's current host population. Reusing its original state as the debit source would create a second loss for people who already left.

After a portion actually arrives abroad, its initial foreign-only routing intention is fulfilled. Domestic distribution can then use ordinary shared routing. Any still-unmoved remainder retains its own foreign intention and remaining request budget. The original episode identity persists for both portions.

Temporary transit remains a material responsibility. It requires food, shelter, and a feasible next leg. An unaccepted proposal to a third country is not a completed departure. A transit state cannot clear its load while people remain there.

### 6. Policies and physical actions

Reception and departure laws use their current native national policy purchase surface. Operational migration actions use the current material-resource payment owners. Preserve that distinction in availability, tooltips, AI, payment, cancellation, and refunds.

Apply that native purchase surface to countries eligible for it. Preserve documented exceptions, including the Holy Realm's Event 003 refuge-policy adapter outside the shared civilian-law panel. Humanitarian eligibility does not automatically grant access to every ordinary law or achievement surface. Use each participant's actual existing policy authority.

Affordability should be inclusive at the exact quoted cost. The amount shown, the amount tested, and the actual debit must agree. Delayed paid work retains its payer, target, generation, due date, and paid receipt. A stale selection must not spend resources on a newly selected state.

An unchanged service cannot repeatedly charge as a successful capacity expansion. Rejected paid setup follows its owner's matching refund rule. No blanket refund is due after a real population transfer merely because a later step fails.

Restrictions can produce waiting, rerouting, or pressure at the current location. The refusal itself causes no automatic deaths. Existing coercive operations, including unsafe forced return where the shared system permits it, keep their exact safeguards, shared consequences, and disqualifiers. The report must distinguish coercion from consent and actual harm from an unexecuted policy intention.

Disease exposure requires a real outbreak-related fact. Refugee status, nationality, origin ideology, or a border crossing alone does not establish contagion. Controlled medical reception remains useful when exposure is actually present.

### 7. Development and resolution

| Outcome family | Evidence required | What remains afterward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Safe reception progressing | Surviving people have arrived and support remains viable | Real host population and remaining integration or onward obligations |
| Durable integration | The shared owner completes settlement for the exact living cohort | Host residents remain. Reception load is removed exactly once |
| Safe onward resettlement | The next host receives the surviving people and the shared owner resolves the appropriate obligation | The new host's population and any continuing shared obligations |
| Voluntary return | The original cause has sufficiently ended and the actual route, home, and consent conditions are valid | Surviving returnees at the real destination and retained history |
| Foreign intention averted | The still-unmoved request is safely resolved by its owner, or an internal safety outcome replaces that intention | People remain where they truly are. No fictitious arrival, return, or integration credit |
| Unresolved pressure | Any living portion remains stranded, in temporary reception, or without a durable next step | Shared cohorts, pressure, support needs, and current location remain active |
| Severe loss | Shared owners record actual deaths or other lasting harm and reconcile surviving obligations | Recorded losses persist. Administrative closure is reported as failure or loss, not successful relief |

The starter's report can finish immediately while its shared episode continues. Event cleanup may remove obsolete presentation references or a finished dispatch token. It cannot terminate living migration obligations.

For a successful overall resolution, the accepted request's still-living population must have a valid settled, safely returned, safely resettled, or safely resolved-in-place outcome. No stranded remainder or temporary reception obligation may be discarded. A country that has passed people onward may complete its local task while the broader episode remains unresolved elsewhere.

Use the shared owner's durable resolution criteria first. If the event needs an additional quiet-period observation for its own outcome report or Chaos recovery, a proposed baseline is 30 days after verified relief. That interval is a confirmation window, not a deadline that erases people. A fresh linked pressure condition interrupts the observation. Existing sparse scheduled work should perform it.

### 8. Repetition and changing authority

A repeat must introduce a genuinely new regional episode or additional uncommitted people. The same rejected request, rerouted cohort, expired mission, border-law cycle, or save/reload is still the previous episode.

Use the shared repeatable event pacing and diminishing caps. A proposed additional origin-state interval is 180 days after acceptance of an Event 131 request. That interval restrains repeated depletion of one place. It does not grant the right to fire again when the unique-population check fails, and it does not delay ordinary management of existing people.

Loss of state control, annexation, civil war, and country disappearance trigger the appropriate shared authority reconciliation. New valid authorities inherit only obligations that the owner explicitly rebinds. Historical origin and episode identity remain immutable. Missing or ambiguous authority suspends or rejects the operation without deleting people or inventing a successor.

Existing cooldowns and accepted-request identities survive save/reload. A newer event generation cannot consume a receipt from an older one. A failed payment or authority-loss cleanup cannot grant a success outcome or a normal completion reward.

### 9. AI intent

AI countries use the same legal actions, exact request targets, costs, route checks, and capacity constraints as human players. There is no automatic capacity, free transport, or immunity to overextension for the AI.

Evaluate validity before preference. Among valid actions, favor the response that addresses the actual local bottleneck. A prepared host can admit. A host with an affordable real service improvement can prepare first. An overloaded host should prioritize viable relief, distribution, onward passage, or settlement. An origin can prefer improving conditions when that genuinely resolves the need to move.

Relations, ideology, perceived security, and policy can influence preferences within the valid candidate pool. They cannot turn an unsafe route into a safe one or substitute for consent. Real humanitarian hazard facts may outweigh ideological affinity. Do not force every authoritarian state to refuse or every democratic state to accept irrespective of capacity and policy.

The shared owner should allow AI-origin and AI-host episodes to progress without a player click. Conversely, an unavailable human action must not remain secretly executable by an AI wrapper. Calibrate relative weights only after the complete candidate pools and named scenarios are known. A raw weight is not a probability.
