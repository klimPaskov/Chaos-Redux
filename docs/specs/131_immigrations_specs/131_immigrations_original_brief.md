# Immigrations

## Catalog entry

- Event ID: `131`
- Original catalog ID: `149`
- Type: Minor Repeatable *(proposed)*
- Status: Rough idea, awaiting full specification
- Chaos level: 1, available from 0+ Chaos *(proposed)*
- Cluster: Domestic Unrest, Medium member *(proposed)*

The event is restored as a crisis starter connected directly to the existing migration mechanics. Classification and cluster placement remain proposals.

## Core idea

A sudden movement of people creates a migration crisis. Families leave an inhabited region and seek entry into another country, putting pressure on border crossings, transport, food supplies, and accommodation.

The event starts the episode. The existing migration system handles the people, routes, border policies, reception, settlement, and consequences.

## Starting the crisis

Choose a real origin population and a plausible receiving country. Majors, minors, subjects, and player countries can be involved. Existing war, famine, occupation, or another crisis can explain the departures. In calmer conditions, the event can create a sudden movement toward perceived safety, employment, or better living conditions.

People begin leaving through the shared migration system. Actual routes and border policies determine whether they can arrive. A closed border can leave people stranded or seeking another route. It does not erase them or place them inside a country that refused entry.

The movement should be substantial relative to the affected countries without exceeding the population available at its origin. Existing migrants cannot be counted as a new wave twice.

## Responses and consequences

The receiving government uses the existing choices: controlled admission, expanded reception capacity, distribution among suitable states, onward transit, international assistance, or restrictions on entry. These require actual shelter, supplies, transport, and capacity.

Overcrowding can worsen shortages and local instability. Prepared countries can receive and settle people without automatically suffering unrest. Origin and transit countries can improve conditions at home, arrange safe movement, or negotiate return when it becomes possible.

Population is removed from the origin and surviving arrivals are added to the destination. Unsafe movement can cause losses through the shared Deaths system. Integration, resettlement, return, and famine consequences remain with their existing systems.

## Resolution and repetition

The crisis eases when stranded people and reception pressures are resolved through settlement, onward movement, safe return, or improved conditions. Population changes and real losses remain. Ending the event cannot delete unsettled people.

Later firings can start another regional episode or a genuinely additional flow. They do not duplicate an earlier cohort.

## Progression

Larger flows, overcrowding, blocked borders, and onward movement are ordinary developments of the migration system. No separate evolution is proposed for this crisis starter. Its variety comes from the countries, causes, routes, and responses involved.
