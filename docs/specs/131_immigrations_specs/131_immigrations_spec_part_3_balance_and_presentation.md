# Event 131: Immigrations

## Part 3: Balance and presentation

### 1. Balance objectives

This minor event should make regional capacity, geography, and policy matter. A prepared country should be able to handle a meaningful arrival. An unprepared country should face a practical problem that its existing tools can improve. The origin must lose only people who actually depart or die through a validated shared consequence.

The proposed 0.5%, 1%, and 2% request ceilings, 180-day origin interval, and optional 30-day resolution observation are a small initial tuning set. Validate them against small states, large states, minors, majors, subjects, occupied origins, closed borders, and depleted transport. They are not promises of observed campaign frequency or time to resolution.

Higher global Chaos can make real crises and strained neighbors more common through existing systems. It should not automatically manufacture more people, waive eligibility, or multiply the event's wave by an unexplained global factor. The brief's proposed level 1 entry allows a calm voluntary episode when its actual requirements are met.

Domestic Unrest membership and Medium severity describe a proposed scheduling relationship. They do not impose an unrest penalty on people solely because they migrate. Cluster scheduling cannot count an existing wave as a fresh one or bypass the event's population claim.

### 2. Chaos impact

The following bands describe the event's direct contribution, after shared-owner overlap is removed. They are proposed tuning. Shared deaths, war, famine, coercion, and other material consequences retain their own Chaos attribution.

| Manifestation or outcome | Direct event Chaos direction and band | Why and dynamic factors | Repeat guard and overlap | Recovery treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted intention with prepared orderly handling | 0 | Intent or movement alone is not destabilization | One request identity. No charge for the report, picker, or registration | None |
| A new material local disruption attributable to this episode | Up to +2 | Verified new unresolved pressure disrupts a locality. Scale with actual affected share and practical shortfall | At most once for that episode and severity milestone. Use 0 if migration or another owner already counts the same disruption | Recover only the event's own outstanding contribution after durable relief |
| The same episode creates a distinct regional disruption across affected endpoints | Raise the event-owned total to +3 through +5 | Real linked stranded or reception pressure spreads and remains materially unresolved | Cumulative event-owned cap of +5 for the episode. Charge only the incremental severity increase. Existing shared consequences remain separate | Recovery cannot exceed the unrecovered event-owned amount |
| Deaths or other harm already attributed by shared owners | 0 additional | Actual deaths and their causes are recorded through the established owners | No duplicate event charge for the same victims or operation | Deaths remain recorded and cannot be repaid by event cleanup |
| Safe, durable relief of event-owned disruption | Downward, up to the remaining attributable direct contribution | The linked pressure is truly relieved, with any required quiet observation completed | Once against the exact prior contribution. Law cycling and report dismissal are insufficient | The contribution can return to zero, never become a farmable net reward |

Do not automatically grant +2 at firing. The migration-owned accepted request may be orderly, and shared consequence owners may already cover every disruptive fact. In either case, the direct event delta stays zero. The design's cap does not limit genuine consequences owned elsewhere.

Use existing receipt and severity mechanisms for attribution where available. A rerouted cohort or a repeated notification cannot create another direct award. A fresh episode requires a fresh population claim and its own causal proof.

### 3. The first report

The ordinary report should answer five questions in a compact scene and description: who is seeking to move, where they are now, why this episode began, which foreign destination is being considered, and what currently prevents or enables safe passage.

| Audience | Emphasis | Truth condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origin player | Actual place, cause, intended movement, and available origin response | Requested people are still at origin until the shared transfer says otherwise |
| Prospective host player | Exact request, intended receiving state, current headroom, and relevant policy | A request is not an arrival, and a proposed state is not an accepted destination |
| Current host or transit player | Actual surviving arrivals and their immediate support or onward needs | Use current-host data from the shared owner |
| Uninvolved observer | Regional context through the ordinary event history and appropriate shared notifications | Use qualitative information when exact population data is not visible to that viewer |

If neither endpoint is player controlled, routine history and existing migration notifications can carry the episode. Avoid forcing an uninvolved player through operational choices that belong to another country.

The main action direction is to inspect and manage the affected situation through the shared panel. The wording must not imply that acknowledging the report itself admits people, pays for reception, or resolves the pressure.

### 4. Text direction by stage

| Stage | Writing direction |
| --- | --- |
| New intention | Describe a concrete local scene and the perceived or evidenced reason to leave. Make the foreign destination an intention |
| Prepared reception | Explain the practical arrangements already available, including any remaining consent or transport step |
| Closed or refused route | Describe waiting and the real location. Attribute refusal to a particular government only when the owner has exact attribution |
| Successful passage | Describe measured surviving arrivals and the next reception responsibility |
| Overloaded reception | Describe actual shelter, food, transport, or medical shortage without blaming a nationality or treating newcomers as intrinsically dangerous |
| Onward transit | Explain the accepted next destination and what remains before departure |
| Durable integration | Describe settlement and reduced temporary pressure while preserving the permanent population change |
| Voluntary return | Explain the changed conditions at home and the actual safe return |
| Averted departure | Explain why the foreign intention ended before movement. Do not describe a return journey |
| Severe loss | State the verified outcome soberly and distinguish survivors from recorded deaths |

The tone is grounded, humane, and appropriate to the 1936–1945 setting. Any irony should concern official conduct or administrative contradiction. Civilian suffering is not a source of slapstick. Do not invent quotations, cultural proverbs, or historical claims. Historical examples in the research note guide plausibility and representation rather than supply copy-ready text.

Final titles, option text, descriptions, tooltips, Event Details, and spreadsheet-facing wording are written and checked during implementation. These tables provide direction only.

### 5. Existing interface

The current Migration panel has four canonical primary values:

| Existing value | Event 131 relationship |
| --- | --- |
| Displacement | Reflect the existing owner's live obligations and roles |
| Reception Capacity | Reflect real registered services and their current capacity |
| Reception Law | Display the authoritative current native law |
| Departure Law | Display the authoritative current native law |

Preserve the existing Overview and States tabs and quiet access. Opening a panel during calm conditions must not create a migration incident, cohort, runtime registration, or event firing.

Preserve the panel's actual eligibility and policy exceptions. The Holy Realm uses its documented Event 003 refuge-policy adapter outside the shared civilian-law panel. Event 131 does not replace that authority or grant panel and achievement access merely because the country passes humanitarian movement validation.

Requested amount, current location, route status, source episode, action cost, due date, and exact selected-state context are contextual details. Present them through existing report, state, and work surfaces. They do not become a fifth persistent headline value.

The implementation parent owns changes to shared Migration UI, Event Details, event history, settings, and any shared report header. The event UI specialist is not an owner of these shared surfaces.

### 6. Event history and map information

History should identify the original affected country and its role before the initial firing is recorded. Include a secondary actor only when the actual record can distinguish its role truthfully. A prospective host is not yet a receiving country. Missing or invalid actors should not be replaced with whichever country happens to be the current script scope.

The event's baseline developments can produce contextual shared reports without creating new event-pacing pulses or evolution records. Actual movement, pressure-only intention, and terminal outcomes need distinct descriptions.

Use the existing migration map mode and its current endpoint projections. The source, current host, intended destination, and actual movement facts come from owner-published data. A requested destination does not display as an arrived population. Refresh projections after validated transactions and valid lifecycle changes, with cleanup that respects other cohorts still using a state.

Exact local amounts remain visible to the owner or controller according to current rules. Other viewers receive qualitative information. National displacement and selected-state reception load must remain clearly distinguished. Native map layers do not imply a route-arrow overlay.

### 7. Report art

There is one ordinary Event 131 report-image consumer. Inspect the seven migration report images that `docs/systems/migration_system.md` declares, which are stored under `gfx/event_pictures/famine_and_migration_system/`. Four of them are wired for the compact migration header by `interface/migration_report_pictures.gfx`, and the other three serve famine-owned scenes, so inspect their actual formats first. Reuse a suitable image when its subject and consumer contract match. A new scene is justified only when those assets cannot communicate the initial pending movement without implying successful admission or a different event.

If production is needed, the scene direction is a period frontier reception environment with luggage, a baggage trolley, an open shelter, and a distant railway carriage. It should suggest people preparing to move without identifying a real historical group or asserting an outcome. Keep the composition readable at the report's native size and avoid readable generated lettering.

The ordinary report output is 210 by 176 RGBA, with the canonical report processor providing the tilted paper treatment and transparent corners. Shared compact migration reports keep their existing consumer contracts. The separate [asset prompt](131_immigrations_asset_prompt.md) owns the production handoff.

### 8. Existing achievement integration

Event 131 can produce legitimate opportunities for the five existing migration achievements. It should preserve their current identities, thresholds, difficulty, artwork, and exact completion predicates.

The integration task is to keep provenance and actual outcome receipts intact. A real completed protected passage can feed the relevant corridor achievement. A durable integration can contribute to the existing settlement record. A real return, medically controlled reception of an actually exposed cohort, or national recovery can contribute only when all of that achievement's existing conditions are met.

Starting the event, consenting to a request, dismissing a report, or closing an episode does not award an achievement. Naturally scheduled Event 131 can participate under the current natural-campaign rules. Debug, forced, or scenario shortcuts keep the current disqualification behavior. The [achievement prompt](131_immigrations_achievement_prompt.md) defines the scoped integration review.
