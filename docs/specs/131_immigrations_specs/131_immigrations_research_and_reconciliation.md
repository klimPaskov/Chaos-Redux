# Event 131 Immigrations: research and reconciliation

## Authority and intended use

The preserved brief, `131_immigrations_original_brief.md`, is the primary design authority. It explicitly restores Immigrations as Event 131, identifies its original catalog number as 149, and assigns the event a limited role: start a genuinely additional movement toward another country, then hand the people and consequences to the existing migration system. Real origin population, valid routes, border permissions, reception, surviving arrivals, and persistent unresolved people are requirements. The brief proposes Minor Repeatable classification, availability from 0 Chaos, and Domestic Unrest membership at Medium severity. Those classifications remain proposals rather than findings about installed registration.

The brief also rejects an independent evolution ladder. Larger movements, blocked routes, overcrowding, onward travel, settlement, and return remain ordinary migration developments. This package elaborates that requested starter. Repository evidence establishes the current implementation boundary and identifies changes needed to support the brief. It does not silently replace the user's direction.

## Current migration contracts that supersede older assumptions

The current panel exposes exactly four canonical values: **Displacement, Reception Capacity, Reception Law, and Departure Law**. Older summaries using three values and a single Border Policy label do not describe the current surface. Reuse the existing panel and native law categories. A new event meter, independent policy purchase surface, or parallel migration ledger is not required.

Keep the existing eligibility boundaries. The Holy Realm's Event 003 refuge-policy adapter remains outside the shared civilian-law panel while its humanitarian movement validation permits ordinary refuge, relief, and reception. That exception does not grant eligibility for the shared achievements, whose predicates separately exclude special Chaos countries.

The current `migration_request_cross_border_flight` wrapper delegates to spontaneous pressure submission. Submission registers flight pressure rather than creating a cohort or making a physical departure. Later processing still tries internal safety first and derives internal or foreign movement from actual origin and destination ownership. The wrapper's name therefore does not prove retention of Event 131's foreign intent or episode identity. A bounded migration-owned request extension must supply that missing behavior while preserving ordinary dispatch for unrelated movement. A refused or expired request must preserve its people and matching obligations.

The [core migration effects](https://github.com/klimPaskov/Chaos-Redux/blob/41bc4e5045a7b5c131be69dda2f08079d478634c/common/scripted_effects/migration_core_effects.txt) and spontaneous owner were inspected for those distinctions. Existing war, capture, and famine requests also make duplicate cause capture a material risk. A narrative cause does not prove an additional uncommitted population amount.

The exact transfer contract is equally specific. Finalized movement must satisfy `actual origin debit = route deaths + actual survivor credit`. If destination credit falls short, the exact residual returns to the origin. A reduced, conserved transfer is not automatically a complete rollback. Conversely, prospective reception still requires its own full-headroom checks and cannot quietly resize a retained row. Route deaths are logged without a second population deduction. The inspected safe spontaneous path requests zero route deaths, so it supplies no basis for adding random casualties to a merely blocked border.

## Historical background, kept separate from design

### St. Louis, 1939

**Supported facts.** [USHMM's account](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/voyage-of-the-st-louis) describes refusal of admission followed by negotiated refuge in Britain, France, Belgium, and the Netherlands. Its linked [archival photograph record](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/photo/refugees-aboard-the-st-louis) identifies passengers waiting off Havana. The account is museum-authored history, while the photograph caption describes an archival item.

**Design inference.** Arrival, admission, onward routing, and lasting safety can be distinct outcomes. Preserve the context of Nazi persecution when referencing this case. Do not imply that all passengers were sent directly back to Germany or use its headcounts as universal tuning.

### Basque child evacuation, 1937

**Supported facts.** [Warwick Modern Records Centre](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/library/mrc/collections/digital/scw/ninos/) documents arrival aboard the Habana, initial shelter at North Stoneham, later placement in smaller homes, and continuing voluntary support. Its catalogue identifies period records about transport, healthcare, accommodation, finance, and repatriation. The collection page was read, but inaccessible linked scans were not claimed as inspected originals.

**Design inference.** Transport should connect to prepared reception and continuing care. This organized child evacuation had particular demographics and relief institutions. It does not establish a generic demographic model or an automatic instability penalty for refugees. These sources inform the existing-system handoff and supply no final localisation or runtime assets.
