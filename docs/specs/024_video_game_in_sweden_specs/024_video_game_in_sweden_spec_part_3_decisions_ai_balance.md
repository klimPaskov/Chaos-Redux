# Event 24 Decisions, AI, and Balance

## Decision-system purpose

The decision category represents the active Swedish program. It should let the player choose how much authority the simulation receives, how thoroughly its assumptions are tested, and whether public enthusiasm is useful or disruptive.

The category should never become a store for small bonuses. Every action must do at least one of these:

- Move Simulation Reliance by a visible amount.
- Start or resolve a real validation mission.
- Change the current staged idea.
- Change the risk or severity of a model-mismatch incident.
- Expand or restrict the user base.
- Create a bounded foreign response.
- Move the event toward conclusion or an evolution.

## Presentation choice

Use the normal decision system with:

- One event-owned category.
- One static category picture.
- A concise dynamic header.
- One visible Simulation Reliance value.
- One current phase label.
- One current review or mission status.

This normal decision category is the final presentation layer. Its one principal value and compact phase-based action set fit cleanly in that surface.

The current staged idea icon supplies the main changing visual. The category picture provides identity and atmosphere. Tooltips explain exact thresholds and consequences.

## Visible action budget

| Phase | Normal visible primary actions | Active missions | Hard limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | 3 to 4 | 0 or 1 | 5 primary actions |
| Evolution I | 4 | 0 or 1 | 5 primary actions |
| Evolution II | 4 to 5 | 0 or 1 | 5 primary actions |
| Evolution III choice | 4 | 0 | 4 primary actions |
| Recovery route | 2 to 3 | 0 or 1 | 4 primary actions |

Obsolete actions must disappear when the phase changes. A decision that only repeats an earlier effect at a larger number should replace the earlier decision instead of appearing beside it.

## Cost philosophy

Costs should match the action. No action may use more than four spendable cost types.

Preferred cost families:

- Army experience for doctrine, adjudication, and staff time.
- Command power for senior command attention.
- Support equipment for field exercise control, communications, and observation.
- Fuel for realistic movement and operational testing.
- Trains and trucks for logistics validation.
- Civilian factory burden for publishing, public facilities, and administrative work.
- Political power for public regulation, educational coordination, and diplomatic licensing.
- Stability or war support only when the policy creates real public backlash.
- Convoys for overseas licensing and delivery.

Control of Stockholm, living Swedish training areas, available divisions, supply status, war state, and autonomy are requirements. They are not extra spendable costs.

## Baseline action family

### Controlled staff exercise

Availability:

- Visible throughout the baseline program.
- Replaced by the stronger field-validation action after Evolution I.

Action:

Sweden runs a supervised scenario with a human red side, a formal debrief, and comparison against current staff doctrine.

Cost direction:

- Army experience.
- A small amount of support equipment.
- Optional fuel when Sweden chooses a movement-heavy exercise.

Outcome:

- Small temporary planning or training benefit.
- Reliance falls when the debrief exposes a bad assumption.
- Reliance rises slightly when the exercise appears predictive and Sweden does not challenge it.
- Repeated use without new scenario conditions should have diminishing value.

AI:

Preferred by cautious, democratic, neutral, or low-reliance Sweden. Also preferred after a mismatch incident.

### Commission a logistics module

Availability:

- Baseline and Evolution I.
- Replaced by an advanced logistics review after Evolution II.

Action:

Engineers add rail capacity, vehicle availability, ports, fuel, weather, and maintenance to the model.

Cost direction:

- Trains.
- Trucks or support equipment.
- Civilian factory burden.

Outcome:

- Reduces supply-mismatch chance and severity.
- Grants a finite logistics or planning benefit.
- Can lower Reliance because the new module exposes earlier simplifications.
- May raise Reliance when the player markets the module as proof of greater realism.

AI:

Strongly preferred when Sweden has supply deficits, few trains, fuel pressure, or a long active front.

### Expand access

Availability:

- Route-sensitive baseline action.
- Hidden after access has reached the intended route state.

Action:

Sweden expands the program from controlled military use to commercial clubs, schools, public halls, or a formal officer league.

Cost direction:

- Civilian factory burden or political power.
- A smaller route-specific resource such as army experience for the officer league.

Outcome:

- Raises Reliance.
- Changes the event route and future incident pool.
- Increases public or officer benefits.
- Makes the matching evolution more likely when Chaos permits it.

AI:

Chosen according to the route matrix. It should not be spammed or reversed repeatedly.

### Conclude the trial

Availability:

- After the minimum review period.
- Requires Reliance below Doctrine.
- Hidden during an active validation mission or pending evolution event.

Action:

Sweden closes the temporary program and records its useful lessons.

Cost direction:

- No spendable cost.

Outcome:

- Removes the category and staged idea.
- Grants a route-sensitive finite aftermath.
- Permanently closes Event 24 progression.

AI:

Preferred by peaceful Sweden with adequate army experience, low Reliance, and no immediate threat.

## Evolution I action family

### Publish a rules revision

Action:

The official designers change assumptions, scoring, production, or combat rules after staff feedback.

Cost direction:

- Army experience.
- Command power.

Outcome:

- Improves one aspect of the current staged idea.
- Raises Reliance unless paired with evidence from field validation.
- Changes the next mismatch incident pool.
- Uses a cooldown and escalating staff cost.

AI:

Preferred by officer-league and militarized routes with adequate supply. Avoided after repeated failures.

### Field-validation exercise

Action:

Sweden takes one important game assumption into the field and tests it with actual units, transport, weather, and command friction.

Cost direction:

- Fuel.
- Support equipment.
- Trains.
- Army experience.

Requirement direction:

- A meaningful number of divisions available in Sweden or on a stable active front.
- The selected training regions remain controlled and supplied.

Outcome:

- Starts one 90 to 120 day objective.
- Success lowers Reliance, reduces mismatch risk, and can correct the mixed commander trait.
- Partial success lowers Reliance less and creates a targeted follow-up.
- Failure creates a visible timed penalty and still records the exposed weakness.

The mission should auto-complete when its objective is met. The player must not pay a second click.

AI:

Strong after supply problems, failed offensives, low stability, lost cores, or one prior mismatch. Less attractive during an emergency defense when the required equipment would be dangerous to commit.

### Invite an independent red team

Action:

A separate group receives control of the opposing side and authority to challenge favored assumptions.

Cost direction:

- Political power.
- Army experience.
- Optional temporary civilian factory burden for a broad civilian expert panel.

Outcome:

- Lowers Reliance.
- Reduces the chance that successful game predictions create overconfidence.
- Improves enemy-behavior variety.
- Slightly reduces the immediate planning bonus because sessions become less predictable.

AI:

Preferred by democratic and cautious governments, by Sweden facing an adaptive or stronger enemy, and after diplomatic or terrain mismatch incidents.

### Formalize the officer league

Action:

Tournament performance becomes an official part of staff culture.

Cost direction:

- Command power.
- Army experience.

Outcome:

- Strong army-experience and planning benefit.
- Significant Reliance increase.
- Unlocks the rare commander-trait incident.
- Raises Evolution II pressure if public reporting is also active.

AI:

Preferred by authoritarian, fascist, war-focused, or army-experience-poor Sweden when supply and stability are adequate.

## Evolution II action family

### Hold a national league season

Action:

State-backed leagues organize public and institutional competitions across Sweden.

Cost direction:

- Civilian factory burden.
- Political power.

Outcome:

- Raises military interest and war support.
- Raises Reliance and public reach.
- Adds temporary productivity cost.
- Can trigger a national-final report and a foreign reaction.

AI:

Preferred under high world tension, high stability, and adequate industry. Avoided during acute factory, supply, or stability crises.

### Protect essential shifts

Action:

Sweden restricts workplace games and public competitions during important production hours.

Cost direction:

- Political power.
- A small temporary administrative or civilian factory burden.

Outcome:

- Reduces or prevents productivity penalties.
- Lowers public-reach growth and Reliance.
- Weakens the national-league benefit.

AI:

Strongly preferred when factory output is poor, war production is urgent, or the economy is smaller than the enemy's.

### Direct clubs toward preparedness

Action:

Public groups concentrate on map reading, civil logistics, emergency organization, and recruitment preparation.

Cost direction:

- Political power.
- Support equipment or trucks for practical demonstrations.

Outcome:

- Converts part of the cultural benefit into practical preparedness.
- Keeps moderate recruitment or war-support value.
- Raises Reliance less than a national league season.
- Reduces the chance of party appropriation incidents.

AI:

Default public route for a democratic Sweden at high tension.

### License foreign editions

Action:

Sweden authorizes limited exports and chooses one or more foreign partners.

Cost direction:

- Convoys.
- Civilian factory burden.
- Political power when diplomatic negotiation is required.

Requirement direction:

- Valid foreign partners and route access.
- Subject Sweden may need sufficient autonomy or overlord consent.

Outcome:

- Selects bounded foreign reactions.
- Grants a modest temporary domestic commercial or diplomatic benefit.
- Raises public reach and Reliance.
- Does not export the full Swedish mechanic.

AI:

Preferred when Sweden has spare convoys, friendly partners, and strong industry. Avoided when blockaded, isolated, or under an exploitative overlord.

### Separate play from policy

Action:

Public clubs remain legal, while official ministries and senior commands are forbidden to cite game results as evidence without independent review.

Cost direction:

- Political power.
- A small stability or war-support cost if public expectations are already high.

Outcome:

- Lowers Reliance.
- Retains a reduced cultural benefit.
- Delays Evolution III.
- Opens a clean Evolution II conclusion route.

AI:

Preferred by stable democratic governments and after political-appropriation incidents.

## Evolution III response family

The initial Evolution III phase should show only four major decisions. Taking one hides the others and opens that route's limited follow-up actions.

### Begin the Reality Audit

Cost direction:

- Army experience.
- Support equipment.
- Fuel.
- Trains.

Outcome:

- Starts the principal 120 to 180 day Reality Audit mission.
- Suspends the strongest positive and negative Simulation Orthodoxy effects.
- Uses real field and supply requirements.
- Resolves into success, partial success, or failure.

### Establish a Dual-Track Staff System

Cost direction:

- Army experience.
- Command power.
- Civilian factory burden.

Outcome:

- Immediately reduces Reliance to Doctrine.
- Opens one verification mission.
- Keeps moderate planning benefits.
- Ends severe mismatch risk after verification.

### Restrict Official and Public Use

Cost direction:

- Stability.
- War support.
- Political power when enforcement is difficult.

Outcome:

- Rapid Reliance reduction.
- Removes Event 24 military and public benefits.
- Creates a finite backlash period.
- Closes the category after enforcement.

### Trust the Model

Cost direction:

- Command power.
- Political power.
- War support or stability as public commitment.

Outcome:

- Starts a maximum-reliance surge with a fixed duration.
- Provides the strongest temporary military benefits.
- Raises supply, adaptability, recovery, and political risks.
- Starts a mandatory reassessment timer.

AI:

Reserved for a confident, militarized, high-Chaos Sweden with strong supply, high stability, and a plausible war objective. It is invalid when Sweden is near capitulation, lacks trains or fuel, has recently lost core territory, or has already suffered a severe mismatch.

## Reality Audit mission

The Reality Audit is the event's main objective and should be a real action, not a passive timer.

### Setup

The mission uses three Swedish training regions representing southern, central, and northern conditions. Implementation should use existing named state groups or clearly localised state lists. The player must place and maintain a meaningful number of supplied divisions across the required regions or active fronts.

### Success conditions

- Required divisions remain available and supplied for the mission duration.
- Stockholm remains controlled.
- Sweden does not lose a new core state during the audit.
- The required trains, fuel, and equipment remain committed.
- At least one exercise result is reviewed through the red-team or logistics action.

### Partial success

Partial success applies when Sweden completes most field requirements but suffers a supply interruption, loses one training region temporarily, or skips independent review.

### Failure

Failure applies when Sweden abandons the commitment, loses the capital, loses several core states, or cannot sustain the required field package.

Failure must not lock the event. It unlocks a cheaper restriction route and a second, narrower audit after recovery.

## AI decision principles

1. AI must understand the next Reliance threshold.
2. AI should not raise Reliance when doing so would unlock a crisis it is materially unable to manage.
3. AI may accept high Reliance when it has a time-sensitive war objective, strong supply, and an intended route to reassessment.
4. AI should prioritize logistics validation when trains, trucks, fuel, or supply are weak.
5. AI should prioritize red-team review against a stronger or unpredictable enemy.
6. AI should close a low-value baseline program instead of paying maintenance forever.
7. AI must not license copies to invalid, dead, hostile, unreachable, or duplicate foreign partners.
8. AI must not start the Reality Audit without the divisions and resources to make success plausible.
9. AI must not select Trust the Model near capitulation or after a severe mismatch unless a rare high-Chaos rule explicitly permits a desperate mistake.
10. AI behavior for each evolution should become inactive when that evolution is disabled.

## Foreign AI response

Foreign AI evaluates one of four responses based on relationship, ideology, war state, intelligence interest, and military need.

| Factor | Copy | Study | Ban | Ridicule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Friendly relation with Sweden | Strong | Medium | Very low | Low |
| Ally or faction member | Strong | Medium | Very low | Very low |
| Rival or enemy | Low | Strong | Medium | Medium |
| Democratic and peaceful | Medium | High | Low | Medium |
| Authoritarian with dissent concern | Low | Medium | High | Low |
| Low army experience | High | High | Low | Low |
| High intelligence capability | Medium | High | Low | Low |
| Low world tension | Low | Medium | Low | High |
| Sweden recently won a war | High | High | Low | Very low |
| Sweden recently suffered a visible mismatch | Low | Medium | Medium | High |

The response pool must always include a valid no-action result. A foreign country should not be forced to spend resources on a minor Swedish product.

## Balance targets

Exact values require later in-engine and probability review. The following bands define the intended strength.

### Baseline

- Immediate army experience roughly equivalent to one useful early staff action, not a doctrine unlock by itself.
- Planning-speed gain in the low single digits.
- Maximum-planning gain in the low single digits.
- Duration roughly six to twelve months depending on route and activity.

### Evolution I

- Planning-speed and maximum-planning gains in the medium single digits.
- Small doctrine or army-experience efficiency benefit.
- Mismatch penalties strong enough to matter for one operation, normally lasting one to three months.
- A red-team route trades part of the immediate bonus for lower incident risk.

### Evolution II

- Public enthusiasm should create a noticeable war-support or recruitment effect.
- Productivity or political-attention costs should be large enough to justify Protect Essential Shifts.
- No permanent recruitable-population windfall.
- Foreign copies receive smaller and shorter benefits than Sweden.

### Evolution III

- Simulation Orthodoxy should offer strong planning benefits, around the level of a serious temporary military policy.
- Its supply, adaptability, organization-recovery, stability, or political costs must make the route dangerous.
- Trust the Model can exceed normal minor-event strength for a short period because it has a fixed reassessment and serious failure pressure.
- Recovery outcomes should grant moderate finite benefits without permanent superiority.

## Incident severity

Incident severity should scale through:

- Simulation Reliance state.
- Chaos tier.
- Current war state.
- Whether the relevant assumption was validated.
- Supply, terrain, weather, enemy strength, and recent losses.
- Prior mismatch count.

A peacetime Habit-stage incident should be embarrassing and temporary. A wartime Worldview-stage incident can affect an operation and force a policy response. The event should not directly destroy armies, remove states, start wars, or cause a civil war.

## Exploit controls

- Rules revisions use cooldowns and escalating staff costs.
- Exercises cannot generate unlimited army experience. Repeated scenarios have diminishing returns until the campaign state changes.
- Foreign licensing uses a bounded partner list and cannot target the same country repeatedly.
- National league seasons have cooldowns and cumulative productivity pressure.
- Field-validation costs are paid once, remain committed during the mission, and are not refunded on ordinary failure.
- The mixed commander trait can affect only one commander.
- Reliance cannot be farmed below zero or above 100.
- Closing the program removes all active Event 24 decisions, missions, temporary targets, and staged ideas.
- Annexation, capitulation, civil-war resolution, and invalid actor cleanup cannot create duplicate rewards.
- Trust the Model cannot be restarted after its mandatory reassessment.
- Achievement tracking must reject force-trigger and debug paths according to the repository's normal achievement rules.

## Save and reload

The following must persist:

- Fired state.
- Principal Swedish host identity.
- Release route.
- Current evolution and staged idea.
- Simulation Reliance.
- Active mission progress.
- Prior mismatch families and count.
- Foreign response recipients.
- Commander trait target and conversion state.
- Mandatory reassessment deadline.
- Achievement tracking.

Rebuilding the decision category after load must not reset Reliance, duplicate a foreign response, restore an obsolete action, or replay an evolution.

## Cleanup states

Clean closure is required when:

- Sweden concludes the baseline trial.
- An evolution resolves through validation or restriction.
- The principal Swedish host is annexed or ceases to exist.
- A civil war changes the principal host.
- Event 24 is disabled while active.

Disabling the parent event should stop new progression and hide its decisions. Existing temporary effects may either end safely or remain until their stated expiry, but no hidden recurring logic may continue indefinitely.
