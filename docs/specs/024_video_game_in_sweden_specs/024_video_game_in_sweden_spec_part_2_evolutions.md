# Event 24 Evolutions

## Evolution structure

Event 24 has three evolutions. Each evolution changes the way the program plays and replaces the current staged idea. They should use delayed pacing, current campaign state, Simulation Reliance, and the player's prior choices.

| Evolution | Minimum Chaos tier | Normal earliest role | Core pressure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evolution I: Gamified Officer Corps | Gathering Storm, 200 Chaos | After the baseline program has developed an officer culture | Military professionals optimize for the rules |
| Evolution II: A National Obsession | Rising Chaos, 400 Chaos | After public distribution or sustained high Reliance | Civilian life and politics absorb the game |
| Evolution III: Losing the Sense of Reality | Chaos Tier, 600 Chaos | After Reliance reaches the Worldview state | Government and command decisions treat the model as authority |

A normal baseline campaign should not be pushed toward evolution. A player who uses controlled exercises, keeps Reliance low, and concludes the trial can finish Event 24 without seeing any evolution.

## Evolution pacing

The evolution system should follow these principles:

- Evolution I normally takes about four months after it becomes eligible.
- Evolution II normally takes about five months after it becomes eligible.
- Evolution III normally takes about four months after it becomes eligible.
- War, high world tension, prior reliance-raising choices, and successful game predictions shorten the wait.
- Field validation, red-team work, failed predictions, low public reach, and active restrictions lengthen the wait.
- A recent Swedish defeat can increase the pressure to rely on the game or provoke skepticism. AI and event context should decide which response is stronger.
- No evolution should fire while the program is concluding, under a completed restriction route, or after the actor no longer exists.

Exact MTTH and weighted factors belong in centralized tuning and later probability review. The scenario matrix in this package defines the required ordering.

## High-Chaos opening variants

Event 24 may first fire after one or more evolution tiers are already open. The entry event should become more intense without removing the player's first meaningful choice.

### Gathering Storm opening

The officer corps is already interested. The Competitive Officer League route begins near Evolution I eligibility. Controlled Staff Trial can still prevent immediate escalation by starting with a formal debrief and low Reliance.

### Rising Chaos opening

Unofficial civilian copies and public demonstrations already exist. Civilian Commercial Release begins with broad reach. Controlled release can contain the craze, while the officer route starts with simultaneous military and public pressure.

Evolution I may be recorded immediately when the chosen opening already satisfies its full premise. Evolution II may be recorded immediately only when the opening clearly includes broad civilian spread together with Doctrine-level Reliance. The player must still receive a response choice. The route cannot be selected invisibly.

### Chaos Tier or higher opening

The world is already receptive to simple strategic explanations. Event 24 may begin with Evolution I or II active, depending on the opening choice. Evolution III should not fire in the same opening chain. It requires a separate review window so the player can test, restrict, or exploit the game first.

At Totalen Chaos or World Collapse, Evolution III can become much faster after that window. Its consequences may be stronger, but it remains the same evolution and retains all recovery routes.

## Evolution I: Gamified Officer Corps

### Premise

Officers begin to treat game results as professional evidence. Staff schools run formal seasons, commanders compare rankings, and tactical arguments are settled by replaying scenarios. Part of this improves planning, while another part teaches officers to defeat the game's rules instead of adapting to an enemy.

### Eligibility

Evolution I becomes eligible when all of the following are true:

- Event 24 is active and has not concluded.
- Gathering Storm or a higher Chaos tier is active.
- Simulation Reliance has reached at least the Habit state, or the Competitive Officer League route remains active long enough to create the same institutional culture.
- Evolution I is enabled.

The following factors should increase its pace:

- Sweden is at war.
- Sweden has low army experience.
- The officer league route was chosen.
- Sweden repeatedly published rule revisions or rewarded tournament performance.
- A recent scenario appeared to predict a real military event.

The following factors should reduce its pace:

- A successful field-validation mission.
- An active red team.
- The controlled trial route.
- A recent mismatch incident that Sweden publicly accepted as a model failure.

### Evolution event choice

The player should choose how the officer culture is handled.

#### Formal auxiliary doctrine

Sweden accepts the game as an official secondary training method. Sessions require debriefing and comparison with field evidence. This produces reliable moderate benefits and keeps Reliance near the lower half of Habit or Doctrine.

#### Competitive command culture

Rankings, tournament victories, and scenario records influence prestige. This produces the strongest military benefit and increases Reliance. It also unlocks the mixed commander-trait incident and raises mismatch risk.

#### Independent red-team supervision

A separate team receives authority to change assumptions, control the enemy side, and challenge the favored plan. Immediate benefits are smaller, but later mismatch risks and Evolution II pressure fall.

### Mechanical identity

Evolution I should strengthen:

- Planning speed and maximum planning.
- Army experience gain from exercises.
- Doctrine discussion or research efficiency within a restrained range.
- Training coordination.

It should introduce risks through timed incidents instead of a constant blanket penalty. The main incident families are:

- Terrain and weather mismatch.
- Supply and transport mismatch.
- Enemy behavior outside the preferred scenario.
- Over-standardized divisions.
- Commanders optimizing for score instead of real objectives.

A country that keeps a red team and completes field validation should experience fewer incidents. A country that raises Reliance without validation should experience more severe incidents.

### Commander trait route

One eligible Swedish commander may gain a mixed Event 24 trait. The working role is a rulebook commander who prepares detailed plans and gains more from accumulated planning, but adapts poorly when conditions change.

Rules:

- Only one commander receives the trait.
- Prefer an active field marshal or general with meaningful command experience.
- Do not create a new character.
- Skip safely when no eligible commander exists.
- A successful Reality Audit or field validation removes the temporary Rulebook Commander trait; the user retired the former Field-Validated Planner conversion in the 2026-09-20 implementation request.
- The trait should remain a rare route result, not a guaranteed reward.

### Evolution I decisions

The phase should normally show four actions:

- Publish a rules revision.
- Run a field-validation exercise.
- Invite or strengthen the red team.
- Standardize or limit the officer league.

Each action must move Reliance, change incident risk, or alter the staged idea. None should exist only to grant a small generic modifier.

### Evolution I conclusion

Evolution I can end without Evolution II when Sweden:

- keeps public reach limited,
- lowers Reliance below Doctrine,
- completes at least one meaningful validation action,
- and reaches the next scheduled review.

The program then converts to a finite validated-training outcome and closes.

## Evolution II: A National Obsession

### Premise

The game becomes a national pastime. Study circles, school clubs, military associations, newspapers, youth groups, workplaces, and political organizations all organize campaigns. Citizens debate production and fronts through game terminology. Recruitment interest rises. Workplaces lose time. Political attention shifts toward tournament results and simplified military arguments.

The public spread should feel Swedish through organized associations and collective learning. It should not look like a modern online fandom.

### Eligibility

Evolution II becomes eligible when all of the following are true:

- Event 24 is active.
- Rising Chaos or a higher tier is active.
- Evolution I has occurred, or the high-Chaos opening already created an equivalent officer culture.
- Simulation Reliance has reached the Doctrine state.
- The game has broad public reach through commercial release, school clubs, national leagues, press coverage, or repeated public demonstrations.
- Evolution II is enabled.

The following factors increase its pace:

- Civilian Commercial Release.
- Repeated national league seasons.
- High war support and high world tension.
- A publicized prediction that seemed correct.
- Foreign demand for copies.

The following factors reduce its pace:

- Essential-shift protections.
- Private-club restrictions.
- Low stability or an obvious military failure blamed on the game.
- A successful controlled review that lowers Reliance.

### Evolution event choice

#### Channel the clubs into preparedness

Sweden organizes the public movement around civil preparedness, recruitment, map reading, and logistics education. Benefits remain useful, but civilian factory burden and public attention still suffer when the network grows too large.

#### Let the national league flourish

The state endorses tournaments, school competitions, newspapers, and public halls. Recruitment interest and war support improve, Reliance rises quickly, and foreign reactions become more visible.

#### Keep the craze outside government

The public may play, but ministries and senior commands cannot cite results as evidence. This preserves some cultural value while slowing Evolution III and reducing political penalties.

### Civilian tradeoffs

The National Obsession stage should create a clear package instead of scattered tiny modifiers.

Potential benefits:

- Increased military interest and volunteer enthusiasm.
- Stronger war support during external danger.
- Modest recruitment or training participation.
- A small cultural or educational effect when clubs remain voluntary and practical.

Potential costs:

- Reduced factory output or production efficiency from lost shifts and workplace distraction.
- Reduced political-power gain from public and ministerial attention.
- Stability pressure when tournament arguments become political disputes.
- Higher Simulation Reliance.

The benefits and penalties should scale by route and Reliance. A controlled preparedness network should not suffer the same productivity loss as an unrestricted national league.

### Public event families

#### School and study-circle campaigns

Local groups use simplified scenarios to discuss geography, logistics, and politics. The player can keep the program educational, turn it into recruitment, or limit state involvement.

#### Workplace leagues

Factories form teams and compete over production plans. This can reveal useful bottlenecks, but repeated seasons reduce actual output unless essential shifts are protected.

#### Political appropriation

Parties and organizations claim that game outcomes prove their preferred foreign or economic policy. The government can ignore them, sponsor one narrative, or require official neutrality.

#### A national final

A major public championship becomes a flavor milestone. Its outcome may raise or lower Reliance depending on whether an unexpected strategy defeats the official favorite.

### Foreign reaction system

Evolution II makes the Swedish game visible abroad. Foreign response must remain bounded.

At most three foreign countries should receive a meaningful response in one campaign. The pool should favor:

- Sweden's living Nordic neighbors.
- Major powers with an interest in doctrine or intelligence.
- Sweden's allies, enemies, overlord, or principal trading partners.
- Countries whose ideology and war state support a distinct response.

Foreign responses:

| Response | Typical actor | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copy | Friendly neighbor, ally, or military reformer | Temporary small training or planning benefit and a report to Sweden |
| Study | Major power, intelligence-focused rival, or cautious neighbor | Limited army experience or intelligence insight without public adoption |
| Ban | Regime worried about dissent, distraction, or foreign influence | No benefit, a small domestic effect, and a diplomatic reaction |
| Ridicule | Rival, complacent peacetime government, or hostile press environment | Minor opinion effect and flavor, with a small chance of later embarrassment if Sweden wins a war |

AI countries may resolve their response silently. Sweden should receive no more than two visible foreign follow-up reports unless a human country made a direct choice.

Foreign copies never gain Simulation Reliance or the Swedish decision category. Their effects expire.

### Evolution II conclusion

Sweden can contain the obsession by reducing Reliance, protecting essential work, and separating public clubs from official policy. The category then closes with a finite cultural or training aftermath.

Evolution II should not require Evolution III to resolve.

## Evolution III: Losing the Sense of Reality

### Premise

Senior officers, officials, newspapers, and sections of the public begin to treat the simulation's rules as a reliable description of the world. Fronts are discussed as clean lines. Political alignments are reduced to categories. Production can be rearranged instantly on paper. Casualties are treated as replaceable pools. Supply becomes a single number in official thinking, detached from ports, railways, vehicles, people, and weather.

The crisis should be shown through concrete decisions and failures. It should not announce that Sweden has lost touch with reality in abstract dramatic language.

### Eligibility

Evolution III becomes eligible when all of the following are true:

- Event 24 is active.
- Chaos Tier or a higher tier is active.
- Evolution II has occurred or the high-Chaos opening created an equivalent national obsession.
- Simulation Reliance has reached the Worldview state.
- Evolution III is enabled.

The following factors increase its pace:

- Competitive officer culture remains dominant.
- Ministries use game results in policy.
- The national league is state-backed.
- Sweden has not completed a field validation for a long period.
- Several game predictions appeared correct.
- Totalen Chaos or World Collapse is active.

The following factors reduce its pace:

- A standing red team.
- A recent public mismatch.
- Protected essential shifts and private-club limits.
- A successful war plan that Sweden explicitly treats as one case without accepting it as proof of the model.

### Opening crisis incidents

Evolution III should select one opening incident based on the campaign.

#### Diplomatic category error

Officials assume a country will cooperate because its ideology, faction position, or opinion appears to fit the game's simplified rules. The real government refuses or reacts unpredictably.

#### Production without transport

A cabinet plan reallocates production cleanly but neglects trains, trucks, ports, maintenance, or regional bottlenecks. The resulting penalty focuses on supply and production efficiency.

#### The perfect offensive

A plan depends on ideal terrain, fixed enemy behavior, and uninterrupted planning. When war conditions differ, Sweden receives a timed military penalty and an urgent validation choice.

#### Casualties as counters

Officials delay reform or negotiation because the model makes replacement appear immediate. The effect should target manpower, war support, organization recovery, or political legitimacy without creating mass-death content.

### Response paths

Evolution III opens four response paths. Each path is visible and understandable. None is a hidden trap.

#### Reality Audit

Sweden suspends official scoring, commits divisions and equipment to field tests, invites independent adjudicators, and compares the game with real supply, terrain, weather, and command friction.

Gameplay:

- Starts a substantial timed mission.
- Temporarily reduces or suspends the strongest planning benefits.
- Requires army experience, support equipment, fuel, and trains within the four-cost limit.
- Requires real divisions in Swedish training regions or active fronts.
- Success lowers Reliance to Habit, corrects the mixed commander trait, removes Simulation Orthodoxy, and grants a finite validated-methods aftermath.
- Partial success lowers Reliance to Doctrine and requires one follow-up review.
- Failure still exposes weaknesses, but Sweden pays a longer military or political recovery cost.

This is the strongest long-term safety route and should be the common AI choice after supply failure, lost cores, or military defeat.

#### Dual-Track Staff System

Sweden keeps the game but requires every simulated plan to have an independent staff plan built from field reports and conventional methods.

Gameplay:

- Immediate moderate cost in army experience, command capacity, and administrative or civilian-factory burden.
- Caps Reliance in the Doctrine state.
- Retains moderate planning and training benefits.
- Removes the most severe mismatch incidents after one successful validation cycle.
- Ends the crisis with a finite dual-track idea and no permanent mechanic.

This is the balanced institutional route for a stable Sweden that still values the program.

#### Restrict Official and Public Use

Sweden removes the game from official planning, closes state leagues, and restricts public facilities. Private clubs may persist, but they no longer drive national policy.

Gameplay:

- Rapid Reliance reduction.
- Immediate loss of military and public benefits.
- A short stability and war-support cost from backlash and embarrassment.
- The category closes after a brief enforcement period.
- A rare underground-club report may occur, but it cannot reopen the full event.

This is the fastest and safest route when Sweden is collapsing or lacks resources for an audit.

#### Trust the Model

Sweden treats criticism as proof that reality has been measured badly. Staffs receive more authority, rules are revised around recent failures, and the state commits to a short maximum-reliance planning surge.

Gameplay:

- Strong temporary planning, army-experience, and war-support benefits.
- Maximum Reliance.
- Serious supply, adaptability, political-attention, and recovery risks.
- Higher chance of a major mismatch incident during war.
- A fixed mandatory reassessment no later than one year after the choice.
- A Swedish victory can soften the reassessment but cannot remove it.
- A defeat, lost core, severe supply crisis, or capital threat forces early reassessment.

This route is risky and achievement-relevant. It must remain reversible and finite.

## Mandatory reassessment

The event cannot remain indefinitely in Simulation Orthodoxy.

The mandatory reassessment occurs when the first of these conditions is met:

- The maximum duration of the Trust the Model surge ends.
- Sweden wins or exits a meaningful war.
- Sweden loses a core state, suffers severe supply failure, or approaches capitulation.
- A major mismatch incident completes.
- The player voluntarily begins an audit or restriction route.

The reassessment offers Reality Audit, Dual-Track Staff System, and Restrict Official and Public Use. A victorious Sweden may receive a better starting position, but it still must choose one.

## Disabled evolution handling

Each evolution must have a clean alternate route.

- If Evolution I is disabled, baseline decisions and ordinary conclusion remain complete. Evolution II and III cannot require it.
- If Evolution II is disabled, Evolution I can conclude through validation, limitation, or retirement. Evolution III remains unavailable.
- If Evolution III is disabled, Evolution II can be contained and concluded. No Worldview-stage lock may remain.
- Disabling an evolution must prevent its recorded flag, staged idea, decisions, incidents, and achievement gates from activating.
- Re-enabling an evolution later may allow future eligibility, but it should not retroactively create skipped history entries without a real evolution event.

## Resolution states

Event 24 ends in one of five broad outcomes:

1. Baseline trial concluded.
2. Officer culture validated and retired.
3. National craze contained as a cultural institution.
4. Reality Audit or Dual-Track reform completed.
5. Official and public use restricted after Evolution III.

The Trust the Model route is an interim surge, not a final permanent state.

## Replay variation

Replay value should come from route combinations and campaign state:

- A controlled program can still evolve during a major war if Reliance rises.
- A commercial release can remain harmless when Sweden protects work and keeps official policy separate.
- An officer league can produce real benefits before field conditions expose its limits.
- A high-Chaos opening can begin at Evolution I or II without forcing Evolution III.
- Foreign countries react differently based on ideology, war state, relations, and proximity.
- The opening Evolution III incident reflects diplomacy, production, supply, or combat conditions.
- The mixed commander trait may appear on different eligible commanders or not appear at all.

Variation should not depend on invisible arbitrary rolls that ignore the player's route.
