# Event 24: Video Game in Sweden

## Catalog identity

- Event ID: `24`
- Event slug: `video_game_in_sweden`
- Event name: Video Game in Sweden
- Type: Minor Fire-Once
- Chaos level: 1, Calm World
- Cluster: Unclustered
- Primary actor: The principal Swedish state
- Primary country: Sweden

## Event promise

Swedish engineers, military planners, publishers, and hobbyists produce a detailed strategy simulation built around contemporary geography, industry, logistics, politics, and military organization. Its machinery is far ahead of the period. The event should accept this as a deliberate Chaos Redux anachronism and present it through a 1930s material culture of relay cabinets, punched cards, projected maps, telephone exchanges, wooden counters, printed rule books, and public halls.

The first version is useful. It lets officers test plans cheaply, debate doctrine, compare supply arrangements, and expose weak assumptions without moving an army. The problem begins when its convenience becomes authority. Officers optimize for the rules. Civilian clubs turn military policy into a national pastime. Officials learn to speak about diplomacy, production, and casualties as if the world must obey the model.

The player should experience a compact arc with real choices:

1. Sweden chooses how the game is released and who may use it.
2. A temporary training program creates modest benefits and opens a few practical actions.
3. The player can validate the model, expand it, commercialize it, or end the trial.
4. Higher Chaos can unlock three distinct evolutions.
5. The program either concludes, becomes a controlled institution, or passes through a reversible crisis.

The event should be funny through institutional behavior and misplaced confidence. It should not depend on direct references to the player, modern storefronts, online communities, achievements, patches, or the real Hearts of Iron series.

## Valid Swedish host

The event belongs to one principal Swedish state. The valid host should be selected using this priority:

1. A living country with original tag `SWE` that owns and controls Stockholm.
2. A living country with original tag `SWE` that owns Stockholm but temporarily lacks control.
3. A living country with original tag `SWE` that controls the largest share of Swedish core territory.

Only one host may own the program. A civil-war splinter, occupation authority, puppet, or later released Sweden cannot create a second copy while the first program or its fired history exists.

A Swedish subject remains eligible. Its foreign licensing and diplomatic choices should respect its autonomy and overlord relationship. A Sweden that has lost most of its territory can still receive the event, but the opening should emphasize emergency staff work and should reduce civilian commercialization preferences.

If no valid Swedish host exists, Event 24 is unavailable and the Events list shows `N/A` instead of zero weight. If Sweden is released before Event 24 has fired, it may become eligible. If the event already fired before annexation or civil war, its fire-once state remains permanent.

## Period presentation

The machine is an alternate-history invention, not a claim that Sweden possessed a normal electronic video game in 1936. The visual and written presentation should combine four layers:

- A wall-sized projected map and mechanical plotting table serve as the public face.
- Relay cabinets, punch-card readers, teleprinters, and switchboards perform calculations behind the scenes.
- Officers use wooden or metal counters, printed orders, and referees because much of the system still requires human adjudication.
- The civilian edition simplifies the system into boxed map sets, club rules, public demonstration machines, and radio or newspaper match reports.

The machine may calculate industry and movement faster than human staff. It should still need human interpretation, imperfect data, and a debrief. Those limits drive the later crisis.

## Baseline opening

The entry event should present three release philosophies. The labels below are working route labels and are not final localisation.

### Controlled Staff Trial

The armed forces keep the simulation inside a small training program. Experienced officers supervise the sessions, debrief the participants, and compare results with field exercises.

Player experience:

- Lowest starting Simulation Reliance.
- Small immediate army experience and a modest temporary planning benefit.
- Best access to validation actions.
- Slowest evolution pacing.
- Ordinary conclusion becomes available early.

This is the safest and most common AI route for a stable democratic or neutral Sweden at peace.

### Civilian Commercial Release

Publishers distribute a simplified edition through clubs, schools, newspapers, public halls, and popular education associations. The military gains a broader pool of ideas and recruits, while the product becomes a recognizable national pastime.

Player experience:

- Moderate starting Simulation Reliance.
- A modest cultural and public-interest benefit.
- Faster public-spread events and foreign reactions.
- Earlier productivity and political-attention risks.
- Evolution II becomes substantially more likely when Chaos permits it.

This route should feel plausible through Swedish study-circle culture and organized public associations. It must not become a modern mass-market launch scene.

### Competitive Officer League

The army organizes formal competitions between staffs, commands, and schools. Results influence prestige, doctrine debate, and promotion culture.

Player experience:

- Highest starting Simulation Reliance.
- Strongest early army experience and planning benefits.
- Fastest path to Evolution I.
- Greater chance that one commander gains a mixed game-shaped trait.
- More frequent model-mismatch incidents if the player skips field validation.

This route is attractive to a Sweden at war, a militarized government, or an AI with weak army experience and adequate supply.

## Simulation Reliance

Simulation Reliance is the event's only player-managed value. It measures how much Swedish institutions trust the game's rules when making real decisions.

The value uses a 0 to 100 scale and four visible states. The category header should show the current number, current state, and the next threshold. The current staged idea supplies the main visual identity.

| Range | Working state label | Meaning | Gameplay role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 24 | Instrument | The game is one training tool among several | Useful low-risk planning and army experience |
| 25 to 49 | Habit | Staffs return to the game by default | Stronger benefits and first overreliance risks |
| 50 to 74 | Doctrine | Rules and results shape policy and military culture | Powerful planning gains with recurring mismatch costs |
| 75 to 100 | Worldview | Officials expect reality to follow the model | Evolution III crisis, political penalties, and forced reassessment pressure |

Reliance should rise when Sweden expands access, publishes rules revisions, rewards game performance, repeats unvalidated simulations, or treats successful predictions as proof. It should fall through field exercises, red-team play, public restrictions, independent staff review, failed predictions, and deliberate program closure.

The player should understand every visible change. Decision tooltips should state the Reliance movement and the public consequence. Internal chance factors may remain hidden, but the visible reason for a stage change must be clear.

## Baseline benefits

The baseline should be worth noticing without creating a permanent national advantage. Target strength:

- A small immediate army experience grant.
- A temporary planning-speed and maximum-planning benefit.
- A small training or doctrine-discussion benefit.
- A route-specific secondary effect, such as modest public enthusiasm for the civilian release or better field-validation access for the controlled trial.

The baseline should not grant factories, permanent research slots, large doctrine progress, free divisions, permanent organization, or a permanent national spirit.

The strongest baseline route should remain weaker than a major national focus reward. Its value comes from the choice between early military utility and later reliance risk.

## Staged idea lifecycle

Only one Event 24 idea may be active at a time. Each stage replaces the previous one.

| Working idea label | Entry condition | Role | Exit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Experimental War Game | Baseline program begins | Modest planning, training, and army-experience support | Replaced by Evolution I, removed at ordinary conclusion, or replaced by a recovery form |
| Gamified Staff Culture | Evolution I | Stronger planning and doctrine discussion with model-mismatch risk | Replaced by Evolution II, recovery, or closure |
| National Strategy Craze | Evolution II | Recruitment interest and public enthusiasm with productivity and attention costs | Replaced by Evolution III, containment, or closure |
| Simulation Orthodoxy | Evolution III | Maximum reliance benefits and serious military or political distortion | Replaced by one recovery outcome or removed by restriction |
| Validated Wargaming Methods | Successful Reality Audit or Dual-Track outcome | Bounded temporary institutional benefit without crisis behavior | Expires after a long but finite period |

The final recovery idea should be temporary. The event must not leave Sweden with a permanent stack of related bonuses.

## Baseline program loop

The baseline program should normally last about one year. It can remain open longer when the player keeps raising Reliance or when an evolution becomes eligible.

The baseline phase contains three or four visible actions:

- Run a controlled staff exercise.
- Commission a logistics module.
- Expand or limit the user base according to the chosen release route.
- Conclude the trial once the minimum review period has passed.

The ordinary conclusion should become available after enough time for the player to use the program. It should require Reliance below the Doctrine state and no active validation mission. The conclusion removes the category and current Event 24 idea, grants a final small lesson or temporary modifier, and leaves Event 24 permanently fired.

An automatic review should close an ignored low-reliance program after a reasonable upper duration. An ignored high-reliance program should not vanish. It should first offer containment or make an eligible evolution more likely.

## Baseline conclusion outcomes

The conclusion depends on how Sweden used the game.

### Validated training instrument

Sweden ran exercises, debriefed results, and kept Reliance low. The program ends with a finite planning and army-experience benefit that represents retained teaching methods.

### Popular cultural success

The civilian edition remained popular without taking over national policy. The program ends with a short public-morale or recruitment-interest effect and a minor foreign report.

### Officer competition retired

The army obtained useful doctrine debate but recognized that rankings were distorting priorities. The league closes, the mixed commander trait can be corrected, and Sweden retains only a finite training benefit.

None of these outcomes should create a permanent global mechanic.

## Campaign-state reactions

The baseline should react to the real campaign:

- War raises the appeal of the officer league and makes field validation more urgent.
- Low army experience makes military adoption more attractive.
- Poor supply, train shortages, fuel shortages, or occupied Swedish cores make aggressive reliance less attractive and strengthen the value of logistics review.
- High stability and peace support the commercial route.
- Low stability, lost cores, or recent defeats make public obsession harder to sustain.
- High world tension increases civilian interest and foreign attention.
- A Swedish subject is more cautious about foreign licensing when its overlord can block or appropriate the product.

The event should observe these conditions through bounded checks tied to Sweden and active Event 24 actions. It should not add a recurring whole-world scan.

## Rare baseline incidents

Rare incidents add replay value without creating new branches.

### The unexpected counterplan

A junior officer or civilian player defeats a respected staff by exploiting an assumption the official scenario treated as fixed. Sweden may reward the insight, dismiss it as gamesmanship, or revise the rules.

### The logistics table is wrong

The game produces a clean offensive timetable that collapses when real train capacity, weather, or regional supply is inserted. This can motivate a logistics module, reduce Reliance, or embarrass the officer league.

### The perfect template craze

Clubs and staffs converge on one game-efficient division design. Standardization briefly helps training and planning, but reliance on one structure reduces adaptability until field validation occurs.

### A foreign bootleg

A neighboring or major country obtains an unofficial copy before Sweden authorizes exports. Sweden can tolerate it, protest, or use the leak to observe foreign play.

Rare incidents should use current route, Reliance, war state, and prior actions. They should not repeat endlessly.

## Cross-system connections

Event 24 should connect to shared campaign state more than to a long list of named events.

- Rising world tension increases adoption, public interest, and foreign study.
- Active war provides real validation opportunities and raises the cost of model failure.
- Supply, terrain, weather, and enemy adaptation influence mismatch incidents.
- A recent military defeat makes audits and restrictions more attractive.
- Major technological or strategic shocks can appear as new scenarios inside the game, but they should remain flavor unless another accepted event specification defines a direct integration.

This bounded approach keeps Event 24 connected without making it dependent on unfinished events or absorbing their content.

## Event identity in logs

The Event Log actor is the principal Swedish host. Event Details should describe the development of a Swedish war simulation and the risk of institutional overconfidence. It should avoid listing raw bonuses, thresholds, hidden incidents, disabled evolution logic, or achievement conditions.

Baseline stages are ordinary program progression. Only the three named evolutions belong in the Evolutions log.
