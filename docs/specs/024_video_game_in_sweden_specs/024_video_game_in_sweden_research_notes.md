# Event 24 Research Notes

Research date: 2026-08-18

These sources provide design anchors. They do not turn the fictional Event 24 premise into a historical claim.

## Research conclusion

The strongest version of Event 24 treats the game as a useful training instrument whose value depends on coaching, debriefing, and comparison with real experience. The crisis grows when Sweden removes those safeguards and treats game outcomes as evidence.

The civilian route is most convincing when it spreads through Swedish associations, study circles, schools, clubs, and public halls. The visual direction should use alternate-history relay and projection machinery because a 1936 grand-strategy video game predates the known chronology of electronic games by many years.

## Source 1: Swedish Defence University, Wargaming and education

Source:

https://www.fhs.se/en/swedish-defence-university/education/role-playing-and-wargaming/wargaming-and-education.html

Relevant points:

- The Swedish Defence University uses war games to teach planning and operations across land, air, and sea.
- Wargaming supports tactics, leadership, capability development, and military decision-making.
- Analog and digital map games can create controlled, lower-cost practice.
- Games are most useful when accompanied by experienced teachers, coaching, debriefing, military theory, and proven experience.

Design use:

- Baseline benefits should come from planning, army experience, training, and logistics discussion.
- Controlled Staff Trial should be a strong safe route.
- Red teams, experienced adjudication, and debriefing should reduce Reliance and incident risk.
- Field Validation and Reality Audit should compare game results with real operations.

Source confidence: High. Official Swedish Defence University material.

## Source 2: Swedish Defence University, doctoral thesis article on wargaming

Source:

https://www.fhs.se/en/swedish-defence-university/stories/2024-09-09-new-doctoral-thesis-analyses-how-wargaming-functions.html

Relevant points:

- The article describes research into how wargaming supports learning and battlefield understanding.
- It explicitly notes that the field can contain strong overconfidence and strong skepticism.
- It states that understanding of whether and how wargames work as learning tools remains limited.
- The research frames good use as a process that adjusts beliefs and assumptions through reflection.

Design use:

- Evolution I should create credible reasons for use together with credible grounds for skepticism.
- Simulation Reliance should measure institutional confidence, not game popularity alone.
- The safe route should repeatedly revise assumptions and refuse to treat one success as proof.
- Evolution III should arise from rigid confidence, not from the mere existence of a game.

Source confidence: High. Official Swedish Defence University article about a named thesis.

## Source 3: U.S. Army University Press, Adapting the Art of Design

Source:

https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Journals/Journal-of-Military-Learning/Journal-of-Military-Learning-Archives/October-2020/Hillison-PME-Game-Design/

Relevant points:

- Well-designed games can support military education and practical warfighting skills.
- Wargames can suffer from oversimplification and difficult adjudication.
- Poor design can mislead through incorrect information, overstated or understated risk, and failure to account for chance and friction.
- Games become ineffective when they drift away from the learning objective.

Design use:

- Incident families should focus on supply, terrain, enemy behavior, chance, and friction.
- Rules Revision should sometimes make the game better and sometimes increase confidence without solving the underlying problem.
- Trust the Model should offer strong short-term utility with serious mismatch risks.
- The event should avoid portraying all simulation as bad.

Source confidence: High. Official U.S. Army professional military education publication.

## Source 4: U.S. Army Mad Scientist Laboratory, Wargaming as a learning experience and assumptions check

Source:

https://madsciblog.t2com.army.mil/281-would-you-like-to-play-a-game-wargaming-as-a-learning-experience-and-key-assumptions-check/

Relevant points:

- The author describes a game as an assumptions check, not a literal simulation of reality.
- The article warns about paying too much attention to the outcome instead of using play to identify what was missed, overestimated, or undervalued.

Design use:

- The core distinction in Event 24 is between using the game to test assumptions and using its result as a prediction.
- Reality Audit should reward Sweden for identifying missing variables without claiming that the game is correct.
- Apparent prediction success should raise Reliance unless Sweden keeps an independent review process.

Source confidence: Medium to high. Official U.S. Army-hosted professional discussion. It is commentary, so it supports design reasoning and does not establish a universal claim.

## Source 5: Folkbildningsrådet, What is folkbildning?

Source:

https://folkbildningsradet.se/om-oss/translations/english/what-is-folkbildning/

Relevant points:

- Swedish popular education developed through collective discovery, informal exchange, associations, and study circles.
- Study circles were organized by labor, sobriety, religious, and other popular movements.
- The method spread through institutions and local groups and became part of Swedish society.

Design use:

- The civilian edition should spread through study circles, folk high schools, clubs, associations, newspapers, and public halls.
- Evolution II can involve political movements, workplaces, schools, and voluntary organizations without using modern internet culture.
- Public clubs should be capable of useful collective learning as well as distraction and political appropriation.

Source confidence: High for the organization's description of Swedish folkbildning and its history.

## Source 6: Computer History Museum, Graphics and Games timeline

Source:

https://www.computerhistory.org/timeline/graphics-games/

Relevant points:

- A dedicated electromechanical NIM machine was publicly demonstrated in 1951.
- OXO, one of the earliest computer games with a cathode-ray display, appeared in 1952.
- Tennis for Two appeared in 1958.
- Spacewar appeared in 1962.

Design use:

- A 1936 Swedish grand-strategy video game is a deliberate alternate-history leap.
- The event should not present a modern personal computer as ordinary period technology.
- The visual solution should use relay cabinets, projection, punched media, human adjudicators, and map tables.
- The event's improbability is part of Chaos Redux, but the room, clothing, photography, and operating practices should remain period-authentic.

Source confidence: High. Computer History Museum chronology.

Source confidence: High. Official Swedish National Museum of Science and Technology page.

## Source-derived design rules

1. Wargaming benefits require debriefing, experienced oversight, and comparison with real practice.
2. Overconfidence is a separate problem from game quality.
3. Model failure should emerge through missing information, chance, friction, transport, terrain, and adaptive enemies.
4. Civilian spread should use Swedish collective institutions and voluntary associations.
5. The alternate technology needs period-authentic visual language.
6. The event should preserve the useful side of simulation so that player choices have a real tradeoff.

## Inferences and uncertainty

The specific Swedish relay war-game machine, national league, military adoption path, foreign reactions, and crisis events are fictional design inventions.

The research supports the plausibility of military wargaming as education, organized Swedish study-circle spread, and risks from overconfidence or poor model design. It does not support a claim that Sweden built a video game of this kind before or during the Second World War.

The project deliberately uses anachronism. Final text should avoid presenting fabricated hardware dates, named inventors, or historical institutions as real unless implementation performs additional research and chooses sourced references.
