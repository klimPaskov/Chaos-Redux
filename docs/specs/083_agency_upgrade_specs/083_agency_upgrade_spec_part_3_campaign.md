# Agency Upgrade: campaign interactions and replay value

## Different agencies, different opportunities

A new agency has the sharpest change in capability. Its first operative can create an external presence or work at home, while the random upgrade determines which part of the institution has advanced first. The country remains responsible for the industrial and material cost of expanding further.

An established agency has a broader allocation problem. More personnel allow more simultaneous commitments, but foreign countries receive the same expansion. The player's best use of the reward depends on current wars, the strength of opposing services, existing networks, and the resources required by proposed operations.

A fully developed agency receives conversions rather than empty results. Its replay value comes from accumulating capacity and exploiting distinct temporary windows. A decryption window rewards a different set of immediate assignments from a network window. The guaranteed high-tier suite supports several lines of work at once without removing their ordinary opportunity costs.

## Allocation patterns

These are player strategies and AI priorities, not selectable event branches or additional reward packages.

| Campaign position | Meaningful use of the reward | Cost or exposure that remains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small independent country with a new agency | Establish its first external presence while developing enough domestic protection to survive retaliation | Limited industry and operation resources |
| Country fighting one main opponent | Reinforce the existing enemy network or prepare an operation tied to that war | Operatives committed abroad cannot simultaneously protect home territory |
| Major country fighting in several theaters | Divide personnel between distinct targets and retain specialists for useful operations | Wider commitments, replacement needs, and competing material demands |
| Country facing active foreign penetration | Keep additional personnel at home and use the defensive part of the package | Reduced immediate offensive reach |
| Country with strong networks and available operation resources | Use preparation-speed windows for a sequence of achievable operations | Native network and equipment requirements remain binding |
| Mature agency with no valid upgrades left | Turn every roll into capacity or a timed specialty | Temporary effects expire and do not make later operation costs disappear |
| Subject or faction member | Use its own enlarged agency within its actual diplomatic permissions | Native subject restrictions and faction rules still matter |

## AI conduct

AI countries receive exactly the same package and candidate entitlements as human countries. The event must not give a hidden AI replacement bonus, remove the recruits from minor AI countries, or treat human countries as the only meaningful agency users.

The AI should promptly claim Event 83 recruits when the additional capacity is available. This action does not require a new political decision or expenditure. Afterward, use the existing native assignment system unless inspected evidence proves it cannot use the expansion.

The priority order is functional rather than ideological. Preserve ongoing viable assignments. Address serious domestic penetration when present. Support a current major war target before opening unrelated foreign commitments. Prepare an operation only when its ordinary requirements are attainable. Exploit an active temporary specialty where an appropriate target or activity exists. Retain the flexibility to protect home territory rather than assigning every spare person abroad.

Do not hard-code a single foreign target, make every minor an aggressive saboteur, or override an existing faction spy master. Event 83 adds country-owned capacity and recruits. Native faction and spy-master effects can continue to operate normally, but may not divert all of a recipient's promised recruits to somebody else.

## Intel Leaked

Agency Upgrade makes the existing leaked-information crisis more consequential because more countries have usable agencies, larger operative pools, and stronger capabilities with which to exploit the information. This consequence should emerge through actual espionage behavior and through explicit hooks owned by Intel Leaked where that event supports them.

Agency Upgrade does not itself reveal a cipher, grant a network in a selected victim, hand out infiltration tokens, or trigger an immediate leak. The player's agency remains subject to the same compromise rules as other agencies.

A recipient with a strengthened agency may be better positioned to exploit a leak. A country whose own information is compromised may have more invested in its exposed organization. Stronger counter-intelligence can help detect hostile activity, but it cannot retroactively make already leaked information secret.

Cross-event presentation can acknowledge that a current leak is attracting more active foreign services. Mechanical consumption must read actual current agency capability rather than a simple historical flag saying that Event 83 once fired. Temporary bonuses that have expired must not continue to strengthen the interaction.

## Parliament of Fear

The political crisis owns suspicion, conspiracies, investigation outcomes, purges, and political legitimacy. Agency Upgrade supplies genuine intelligence capacity that the crisis may take into account when resolving appropriate investigations.

A capable service can improve the prospects of investigating a real conspiracy. It can also produce more reports for a paranoid political leadership to misinterpret. The event must preserve the difference between evidence and suspicion. High counter-intelligence is not automatic proof that accused ministers are guilty, and an agency expansion is not a guaranteed reduction in political paranoia.

A purge or intelligence failure originating in Parliament of Fear can affect real operatives only through that event's accepted rules. Event 83 does not automatically refill later losses or protect its recruits from political decisions. It also does not grant a second package when a conspiracy is discovered.

The supplied brief establishes this connection by name. No unverified event number, variable name, or outcome chance is assigned to it here. Implementation must use the accepted Parliament of Fear package and its owner-defined interfaces rather than binding to an unrelated catalog entry.

## Other espionage systems

Other systems can use the expanded agencies and recruited operatives through normal native mechanics. Agency ownership, capacity, current bonuses, and actual operative assignments are the relevant facts. They should not need a parallel Event 83-only agency screen or a second type of operative.

Another event granting an operative or an upgrade remains independent. Event 83 does not count that reward as one of its own future grants. Conversely, a native milestone triggered by an Event 83 upgrade retains its normal reward without being subtracted from the stated package.

## Country change and campaign continuity

An agency remains attached to its country identity through a normal cosmetic tag change, government change, faction change, or independence transition. Such changes do not reroll upgrades, erase entitlements, or create an extra firing.

Territorial conquest does not transfer Event 83's permanent slots or unused recruits to the annexing country. Existing native outcomes for the annexed country's agency and operatives remain authoritative. Event-owned bookkeeping must distinguish a continuing government from a newly created country using a previously dormant tag, so old claims cannot be redeemed twice.

An active government in exile that the native agency system supports remains a recipient. A country that ceases to exist during execution must not redirect its award to an overlord or the next country in a loop. Its outcome is recorded accurately, and the implementation must prove how the supported engine treats that case.

## Multiplayer and receipt timing

There is one worldwide award per firing, regardless of which player or country initiated the containing cluster incident. All rolls are committed through the host-authoritative game state. A client opening a report, changing an assignment, or acknowledging a popup does not execute another reward roll.

The package tier and recipient results remain fixed in saved history. Different countries may see different upgrades and specialties because their rolls are independent. A report for one country may not reveal another country's recruited personnel or hidden intelligence activity.
