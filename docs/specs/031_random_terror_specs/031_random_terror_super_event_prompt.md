## Hard research rule

Do not treat the working branch names as automatically final super-event titles.

Do not invent:

- a quote
- a quote attribution
- a slogan
- a lyric fragment
- a cultural reference
- final button text
- a final audio source
- source rights

Every final text reference and audio choice needs a recorded source and confidence assessment.

A weak or uncertain source is a blocker.

## Representation rules

The movement is a wholly fictional jihadist movement introduced after Evolution IV.

The entity is ambiguous and impossible.

The game never confirms that it is Allah.

Muslim governments, communities, clerics, soldiers, and fictional religious councils can be central opponents.

Do not use:

- a depiction of Allah
- Quran recitation
- the call to prayer
- Islamic sacred chant
- Quranic calligraphy as hostile branding
- real extremist symbols
- real extremist propaganda
- a real extremist organization name
- ethnic or religious caricature
- a sacred Islamic phrase as a hostile button joke
- cheap comedy about religion or mass death

The reveal should communicate that extremists proclaim the entity divine while the game and wider world remain uncertain.

The defeat should communicate liberation, loss, reconstruction, surviving cells, and uncertainty about what appeared.

## Super-event 1: reveal

### Role

First reveal and terminal world-end announcement.

The super-event fires after:

- `1000+` Chaos
- Evolution V
- substantial territorial success
- widespread active crises
- high International Unity
- a major strategic victory
- hidden readiness
- an enabled public branch
- a successful delayed transition

It announces that the entity has appeared, a dominant command accepts or contests it, uprisings begin, and the final campaign starts.

### Title research direction

Find several short title candidates centered on one or more of these ideas:

- false revelation
- disputed divinity
- false prophet
- coercive miracle
- manufactured apocalypse
- final claim
- idolatrous command
- deceptive presence

The title should be specific to this event.

Avoid generic titles such as The End Begins, The Final Crisis, Darkness Rises, or Judgment Day.

The final title should remain concise and should not state that the entity truly is divine.

### Description direction

Write final description text only after the implementation state is clear.

The description should communicate:

- the command location
- the entity's appearance through observable effects
- acceptance by some extremist leaders
- rejection or fear among others
- synchronized uprisings
- open opposition by religious, civic, and governmental actors
- uncertainty about the entity
- the start of a terminal global campaign

Avoid an administrative report, map summary, staged warning formula, or explanation of hidden mechanics.

Do not reveal Apocalyptic Readiness or exact conditions.

### Button and cultural remark research

Research several brief reaction directions.

Suitable modes include:

- grim rejection of false authority
- frightened understatement
- public-domain literary allusion
- public-domain religious or philosophical allusion
- concise historical remark about deception or false prophecy

The final button text should be short enough for the UI.

It should not be a generic command such as We Must Act.

It should not be a sacred Islamic phrase used as an enemy reaction.

For every candidate, record:

- exact wording
- source work or context
- author or speaker
- date when known
- source URL
- direct quote, title reference, short fragment, or paraphrase status
- copyright status or risk
- reason it fits
- confidence

### Main quote research

Research several verified quote candidates about:

- false prophets
- deceptive authority
- idolatry
- coercive belief
- mass delusion
- resistance to false command
- judgment against fraudulent sanctity

Preferred source families:

- public-domain literature
- historical political or philosophical writing
- religious texts with careful context
- public-domain poetry
- legal or historical documents
- mythology

Avoid:

- invented internet quotes
- uncertain attribution
- long modern copyrighted dialogue or lyrics
- a Quranic verse used to brand the enemy
- a quote that states the entity is truly supernatural

A Biblical, classical, literary, or philosophical source can fit if it does not turn one living religion into the event's villain marker.

For every candidate, record exact source, wording, attribution confidence, public-domain or copyright status, why it fits, and any translation issue.

Select one main quote only after comparison.

### Image direction

Generated source mode.

Create a period-authentic documentary or surreal report image showing a captured command hall, public building, railway headquarters, or government interior during the entity's appearance.

Possible visual elements:

- impossible light from no visible source
- altered or doubled shadow
- followers reacting in fear or submission
- commanders divided between acceptance and rejection
- damaged period architecture
- radio and telegraph activity
- smoke, dust, or atmospheric distortion
- human observers unable to focus on the entity

Avoid:

- a direct deity portrait
- a generic horned demon
- a modern science-fiction alien
- sacred calligraphy
- real extremist emblems
- modern weapons and armor
- fantasy game concept-art composition
- readable generated text

The image must match the final title and description role.

### Audio research direction

Find a unique licensed or public-domain musical recording with an ominous, processional, coercive, or apocalyptic structure.

The preferred final in-game duration is one to two minutes.

The cue should work immediately when the terminal reveal opens.

It should have enough musical structure to feel like a super-event, not a sound bed.

Forbidden audio:

- generated audio
- test tones
- oscillators
- primitive waveforms
- noise beds
- drones used as the whole cue
- simple sound effects
- Quran recitation
- the call to prayer
- Islamic sacred chant used as enemy music
- unlicensed commercial film, game, album, or trailer music
- a public-domain composition with an unusable copyrighted recording

For each candidate, verify separately:

- composition title
- composer
- performer or recording source
- source URL
- recording date when known
- license
- composition rights
- recording rights
- usage terms
- attribution requirements
- duration
- editing allowed or not
- why it fits
- uncertainty

Reject unclear licensing.

Preserve the original download and checksum.

Prepare the selected final WAV through mechanical trimming, fading, normalization, channel conversion, resampling, and codec conversion only when the license permits it.

## Super-event 2: defeat aftermath

### Role

Reflective defeat and reconstruction announcement after the global final state is broken.

The event should mark:

- liberation of major territories
- defeat or disappearance of the entity
- collapse or fragmentation of the final command
- surviving victims and displaced civilians
- ruins, prisons, and damaged infrastructure
- remaining splinter cells
- the beginning of reconstruction and vigilance
- uncertainty about what the entity was

The tone should acknowledge cost.

It should not present a clean triumphant reset.

### Title research direction

Find several concise title candidates centered on:

- the false claim broken
- an empty throne or command
- dawn after coercive rule
- the end of the presence
- liberation from a fraudulent revelation
- survival and uncertain absence

Avoid generic victory titles such as Humanity Prevails or The Nightmare Is Over.

The title should not confirm the entity's nature or permanent destruction.

### Description direction

The final description should show:

- the fall of command centers
- liberation and relief
- recovered prisoners and survivors
- rebuilding transport and public life
- surviving ideological remnants
- arguments over the entity's nature
- the coalition's postwar obligations

Do not reduce the aftermath to a military map change.

### Button and cultural remark research

Research a brief reaction about survival, vigilance, memory, rebuilding, or the cost of victory.

A restrained public-domain literary or historical allusion is preferred.

Avoid triumphal mockery, cheap comedy, or a line that claims complete certainty.

Record the same source and copyright fields required for the reveal.

### Main quote research

Research verified candidates about:

- memory after tyranny
- rebuilding after fanatic rule
- vigilance after deception
- survival with loss
- truth after coercion
- liberation and responsibility

Prefer public-domain literary, historical, philosophical, legal, or religious sources.

Avoid generic victory quotations and uncertain internet attributions.

### Image direction

Generated source mode.

Create a period-authentic documentary image showing liberated ruins, civilians, relief workers, soldiers outside a restored public or religious building, damaged command emblems, and uncertain evidence that the presence is gone.

The image should show recovery and cost.

Avoid a parade, celebratory fireworks, enemy corpses, graphic gore, or a direct visual answer about the entity.

### Audio research direction

Find a second unique licensed or public-domain musical recording.

It should be reflective, mournful, restrained, and capable of turning toward recovery.

It must not reuse the reveal track.

The same rights, source, preservation, conversion, duration, and documentation rules apply.

## Blockers

Stop and report a blocker when:

- no quote has reliable wording and attribution
- no suitable button reference can be verified
- no legally usable musical recording can be found
- composition or recording rights remain unclear
- generated art cannot avoid sacred hostile branding, real symbols, modern details, or identity drift
- the live super-event slot and sound pattern cannot be inspected
- the final entity role changes in implementation and invalidates the text or image

Do not substitute a generic quote, placeholder audio, default track, unrelated image, or invented cultural line.
