# Event 31 research notes

## Research purpose

The research supports four design questions.

First, the event needs a response model where security action, public legitimacy, civil rights, victim support, and local cooperation affect the outcome.

Second, the event needs credible transnational behavior through financing, smuggling, safe havens, foreign fighters, and cross-border coordination.

Third, the jihadist evolution needs clear separation between a fictional extremist movement and Islam or Muslim populations.

Fourth, the world-end branch needs a false divine claim without depicting or confirming Allah.

The research guides structure and representation.

The specification does not reproduce real operational tradecraft, real organization structures, real attack planning, or real extremist propaganda.

## United Nations Global Counter-Terrorism Strategy

Source: United Nations Office of Counter-Terrorism, United Nations Global Counter-Terrorism Strategy.

URL: https://www.un.org/counterterrorism/en/un-global-counter-terrorism-strategy

Design use:

The strategy treats terrorism as unacceptable in all forms and places prevention, capacity, human rights, and international cooperation inside one framework.

Event 31 therefore gives countries several response families.

The player can protect civilians, build intelligence, secure infrastructure, cooperate across borders, and use force.

No single action is always optimal.

The strategy also supports the rule that terrorism cannot be assigned to a religion, nationality, civilization, or ethnic group.

The event applies this rule to target selection, movement generation, visuals, names, and player-facing text.

## United Nations Plan of Action to Prevent Violent Extremism

Source: United Nations Office of Counter-Terrorism, Plan of Action to Prevent Violent Extremism.

URL: https://www.un.org/counterterrorism/en/plan-of-action-to-prevent-violent-extremism

Design use:

The plan uses a broad prevention model that includes governance, participation, education, local resilience, religious leaders, civil society, and human rights.

Event 31 translates that structure into Response Legitimacy.

Victim support, restoration of services, local protection, credible legal action, and defection channels improve legitimacy and intelligence quality.

Indiscriminate repression, collective punishment, civilian harm, and arbitrary restrictions reduce legitimacy and make recruitment easier.

The event does not claim that social policy instantly ends an armed network.

These actions change the quality and cost of later security operations.

## UNODC rule of law and criminal justice guidance

Sources:

- UNODC Counter-Terrorism Module 3, Defining Rule of Law.
- UNODC Counter-Terrorism Module 4, Criminal Justice Responses.
- UNODC Promoting and Protecting Human Rights.

URLs:

- https://www.unodc.org/cld/pt/education/tertiary/terrorism/module-3/key-issues/defining-rule-of-law.html
- https://www.unodc.org/cld/en/education/tertiary/terrorism/module-4/key-issues/criminal-justice-responses.html
- https://www.unodc.org/unodc/en/terrorism/expertise/promoting-and-protecting-human-rights.html

Design use:

UNODC notes that counterterror responses that abandon rule of law can lose legality and effectiveness and can support extremist narratives.

Event 31 uses this as a gameplay relationship.

A force-heavy response can reduce immediate Terror Pressure, but poor intelligence and low legitimacy increase civilian harm, hidden survivors, and later recruitment.

A legal and intelligence-led response takes longer and needs more state capacity, but it produces stronger long-term cleanup.

The design avoids a moralized binary.

Every route has material limits, time costs, and possible failure.

## Victims of terrorism

Source: United Nations Office of Counter-Terrorism, Victims of Terrorism.

URL: https://www.un.org/counterterrorism/en/cct/victims-of-terrorism

Design use:

Victims are active subjects of the crisis, not a number used only to increase Chaos.

The response category includes victim support, emergency services, restoration of transport, and public accounting.

These actions can restore Response Legitimacy, reduce panic, and prevent copycat escalation.

The Deaths system still records actual population loss.

The event also needs aftermath decisions after a major territorial or world-end crisis.

## Security Council resolution 2178 and foreign fighters

Source: United Nations Security Council Resolution 2178 of 2014.

URL: https://www.undocs.org/S/RES/2178%20%282014%29

Design use:

The resolution supports modeling foreign fighters, travel networks, cross-border cooperation, financing, and prevention as transnational pressures.

Event 31 introduces those pressures through Evolution II and later stages.

Foreign-fighter recruitment changes manpower and network reach.

It does not create a new custom battalion.

Countries disrupt the network through border cooperation, transport controls, intelligence exchange, and actions against financial or logistical support.

The design remains abstract and does not describe real travel routes or evasion methods.

## FATF terrorist financing research

Sources:

- FATF, Comprehensive Update on Terrorist Financing Risks, 2025.
- FATF, Terrorist Financing Risk Assessment Guidance.

URLs:

- https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Methodsandtrends/comprehensive-update-terrorist-financing-risks-2025.html
- https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Methodsandtrends/Terrorist-financing-risk-assessment-guidance.html

Design use:

FATF describes changing financing methods and stresses risk-based responses suited to local context.

Event 31 uses a flexible support ledger instead of one universal money source.

A network can depend on a foreign sponsor, criminal revenue, captured administration, smuggling, or dispersed donations.

The implementation uses broad categories and does not simulate real financial channels in operational detail.

A country that disrupts the source suited to the current network weakens External Supply.

A country that applies the wrong response wastes capacity and may reduce Response Legitimacy.

## INTERPOL and the crime-terror nexus

Source: INTERPOL, Priorities for INTERPOL and United Nations collaboration.

URL: https://www.interpol.int/Who-we-are/Our-partners/International-organization-partners/INTERPOL-and-the-United-Nations/Priorities-for-INTERPOL-United-Nations-collaboration

Design use:

INTERPOL identifies foreign fighters, emerging technologies, organized crime, and cross-border cooperation as linked concerns.

Event 31 therefore connects organized cells with smuggling and criminal patrons at Evolution I and II.

The connection does not make every criminal group a terrorist group.

It creates specific incidents, sponsorship routes, and rivalries.

The Cannibalism interaction uses this same rivalry principle because the two movements compete for territory, recruits, supplies, and fear.

## The Amman Message

Source: The Amman Message official site.

URL: https://ammanmessage.com/

Design use:

The Amman Message was endorsed by a broad group of Muslim scholars and addressed religious authority, excommunication, unity, and the misuse of religious claims.

Event 31 uses this research to make Muslim clerics and institutions active opponents of the fictional jihadist movement.

The jihadist movement may use unlawful declarations against Muslim governments and communities.

Muslim actors can answer through public condemnation, local defense, protection of worship sites, support for victims, and resistance to forced rule.

The design does not copy the Amman Message into event text.

Final localisation should research period-suitable equivalents and avoid presenting one modern declaration as a 1936 world institution.

## Al-Azhar Observatory for Combating Extremism

Source: Al-Azhar Observatory for Combating Extremism.

URL: https://azhar.eg/observer-en/Al-Azhar-Observatory-for-Combating-Extremism

Design use:

The existence of a major Muslim religious institution dedicated to countering extremist ideas supports the event's representation standard.

Religious opposition is not a cosmetic disclaimer.

It becomes a playable response, a source of legitimacy, a network-splitting pressure, and a foreign reaction.

The event avoids real current organizations and present-day institutional names in ordinary gameplay.

The final text researcher can use the source to understand the role of religious rebuttal without copying modern wording into a World War II setting.

## United Nations guidance on religious leaders and prevention

Sources:

- United Nations Plan of Action for Religious Leaders and Actors to Prevent Incitement to Violence.
- United Nations Peacebuilding coverage of local religious leaders preventing violent extremism.

URLs:

- https://www.un.org/en/preventgenocide/rwanda/assets/pdf/Guidance-Plan%20of%20Action_Religious-rev4.pdf
- https://www.un.org/peacebuilding/zh/content/mauritania-mourchidates-take-lead-preventing-violent-extremism-local-communities

Design use:

The research supports local religious and civic leadership as part of prevention and public resilience.

Event 31 does not turn clerics into a generic resource bonus.

Their involvement affects public trust, defections, recruitment pressure, protection of civilians, and the credibility of the jihadist movement's claims.

## Research conclusions for the final design

1. Terrorism is an action and organization category, not a population identity.
2. The event selects targets through state weakness, war, infrastructure, existing pressure, and network opportunity.
3. Religion, ethnicity, nationality, and civilization never increase baseline target or recruitment weight.
4. The jihadist branch is one later fictional evolution with its own internal logic.
5. Muslim governments and communities receive specific opposition content and can become priority targets of the jihadist movement.
6. State response quality needs a legitimacy component because civilian harm and unlawful repression can undermine later effectiveness.
7. Victim support, service restoration, and public accountability need visible gameplay effects.
8. Foreign fighters, financing, sponsors, safe havens, and cross-border intelligence belong in the transnational evolutions.
9. The design should remain tactically abstract and should not model real attack preparation, evasion, or recruitment methods.
10. The False Revelation must remain ambiguous and must avoid depicting Allah or using Islamic sacred material as enemy presentation.
