# Part 12: Chaos generation, balance and difficulty

## The event should reward judgment

Mistresses needs uncertainty without arbitrary punishment. A player who builds reliable institutions, protects people and acts on corroborated evidence should usually enter the open war in a better position than a player who ignores the crisis or purges indiscriminately. Success must still require tradeoffs. Investigation occupies resources, family support competes with military expenditure, and protecting the home country does not stop another country from falling.

All numerical values in this package are proposed launch tuning unless they reproduce a fixed user requirement or an inspected shared-system contract. No campaign simulation or native game test has established these as final balanced values.

## Chaos reflects actual abnormal consequences

Eligibility, evolution activation flags, logging, opening a window and displaying a cinematic do not create Chaos by themselves. A contribution requires a real manifestation, loss of institutional control, unusual international coordination, major alien expansion or a meaningful reversal.

Ordinary deaths, wars, annexations, contamination and faction changes already have shared owners where those systems account for them. Mistresses does not repeat their generic contributions. Its own additions describe a distinct abnormal fact, such as the synchronized revelation of an international hidden species or the emergence of a supreme biological command.

### Proposed event-owned contribution ledger

| Concrete outcome | Proposed change | Frequency and guard | What makes it distinct |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Actual initial worldwide relationship disturbance | +5 | Once for event manifestation | An abnormal global social change has occurred |
| First verified functioning cross-border protection network in a region | +5 | Once per immutable region, maximum six | Coordinated hidden international influence, beyond ordinary movement |
| First successful major institutional takeover in a region during Replacement Wave | +10 | Once per region, maximum six | A real national command or government function has been captured |
| A wrongful-purge response produces an actual institutional breakdown | +5 | Once per region, maximum six | Collapse of the response's legitimacy beyond the individual death already accounted for |
| Coordinated public revelation with actual surviving cells or revolts | +20 | Once for the global Unveiling | A synchronized alien presence becomes a visible global crisis |
| First strategically significant cross-host brood consolidation in a region | +5 | Once per region, maximum six | New common alien operational capacity beyond the generic annexation |
| Broods first maintain a significant foothold in two world regions | +15 | Once globally after a 30-day sustained result | The invasion has become a durable international war |
| Mother actually emerges and establishes supreme command | +50 | Once, after its separate eligibility succeeds | A new global survival threat has become real |
| A region's viable covert network is eliminated and remains unable to rebuild for 90 days | −5 | Once per region, maximum six | Durable removal of the abnormal hidden threat |
| A captured major institution is restored through verified recovery | −5 | Once per region, maximum six, after a corresponding takeover | A substantive reversal of alien capture |
| The Mother is genuinely defeated | −50 | Once for the consumed Mother instance | Supreme alien command has been broken |
| All event-owned viable cells and open broods are resolved | −20 | Once globally after confirmation | The event's remaining abnormal threat has ended |

The two-region foothold uses at least 10% of frozen regional population weight in each of two regions, using Part 8's authoritative durable-holding receipts. Its own 30-day milestone interval begins after those holdings qualify. It is a lower milestone than Mother qualification. It does not bypass her higher thresholds or recent-success requirement.

A strategically significant consolidation must connect at least two original host outbreaks and produce a supplied operational corridor or a verified new concentration of forces. Annexing an empty shell or merely renaming a country earns no extra contribution. The ordinary annexation owner still handles its own accounting.

The theoretical positive event-owned ceiling from this table is 240 Chaos, including the Mother's 50-point consequence. Before the Mother it is 190. Many campaigns will realize only part of that ceiling. The normal hidden crisis alone cannot push a world at Chaos 200 directly to 1,000. Shared world events and real war consequences continue to matter.

The negative ceiling is 130 across all listed durable reversals. The same region cannot be used repeatedly by releasing a tag, allowing recapture and restoring it again. If a consequence was not actually created, its corresponding reversal cannot be manufactured solely for a reduction.

## Attribution and anti-farming

Keep immutable milestone receipts using original event instance, original host identity, world region and outcome type. Country tags, faction leaders and current owners are mutable and cannot be the only keys. Mergers combine history without re-awarding it. Liberation preserves the history of the state. Save/load resumes pending accounting exactly once.

A single action can have a measured human loss and a separate abnormal institutional consequence, but the same human death is never counted twice. A failed purge does not receive a national-breakdown contribution unless a defined institution or response capability genuinely breaks. The implementer must supply the cause and outcome in the shared Chaos call.

No Chaos is awarded for looking at a suspicious photograph, selecting an investigation, meeting the numeric threshold for an evolution, copying a registry flag or replaying a super-event. No automatic reduction is awarded for every correct case. Those cases help the player through actual network disruption and later durable milestones.

## Screening difficulty and useful uncertainty

The initial conditional priors in Part 3 are selected-case tuning targets. A broad voluntary channel can contain fewer aliens than a linked-cell referral channel. The actual finite candidate set takes priority. A cleared country cannot spawn extra aliens merely to maintain a desired 20% suspect rate.

The appearance cue has a likelihood-ratio ceiling of 1.25. At a 20% prior, even a cue at that ceiling raises the posterior only to about 23.81%:

`(0.20 × 1.25) / (0.20 × 1.25 + 0.80) = 0.238095...`

At a 10% prior it becomes about 12.20%, and at a 35% prior about 40.23%. These are arithmetic examples of the design cap, not measured player accuracy. They explain why visual perfection alone should remain a poor basis for a lethal decision.

Evidence from two reports that copied the same source is not independent. It cannot be multiplied into artificial certainty. Repeated investigation is bounded and buys a new justified channel, not a free reroll. A replacement inquiry requires an observable audit concern and a new independent route under Part 3. The same bounded remedy is available whether the concern reflects real compromise or a false concern. Its availability cannot reveal the source's hidden integrity.

A player's best policy depends on the consequences of each error. Sparing an alien can preserve a useful node. Purging a human kills an innocent person and damages the response. Investigating a low-value case can leave a dangerous office unprotected. The difficulty comes from prioritizing under uncertainty rather than guessing the artist's preferred face.

## Purge-all must fail as a universal strategy

Every purge has a previewed Confidence consequence even before the case is proved right or wrong. At Confidence zero, the action remains possible under the exact affordability rule in Part 4. It still consumes real response capacity and can cause unrest, stability damage, institutional harm and future protection burdens. Zero Confidence is not an immunity state that makes further wrongdoing free.

Automatic bounded post-action review prevents the player from avoiding accountability by refusing to open the audit screen. Innocent purges have serious consequences. Genuine alien removals disrupt actual nodes, with a bounded benefit. Deleting a single suspect never removes an entire state's population-sized cohort.

Review four strategies against the same saved case sets and resource budgets: purge everyone, spare everyone, investigate everyone affordable, and prioritize independently corroborated cases while protecting institutions. Compare saved human lives, final network readiness, resource use, civil disorder and eventual revolt strength. There must not be a single dominant strategy across every situation.

Do not tune success solely as the number of aliens killed. Saving an innocent person, protecting a partner and keeping a region from revolting are meaningful successes. A response that kills many aliens and destroys its own institutions can still be a strategic failure.

The following examples use the proposed Part 5 formula. They isolate the effect of preparation. Candidate prerequisites still apply.

The last row prevents a public meter from granting blanket immunity regardless of the actual surviving network. The response wins by disrupting real cells and institutions, not simply by filling a bar. Conversely, a low Reach value does not force a revolt where no aliens remain.

## Country scale and geography

Large countries receive larger finite cohorts and more strategic targets. They also have more industry and investigative resources, with higher capacity-scaled commitments. Their challenge is coverage across multiple states and overseas territory. One national click must not provide free perfect protection everywhere.

Small countries receive smaller cohorts and can concentrate their response. A one-state country can suffer a dangerous local insurrection without receiving an impossible second owned state. Severe full takeover is reserved for the defined high-readiness case and a supported continuation route. Small responders can make meaningful contributions to the Mother war through evidence, logistics, refuge and relay operations.

Island broods need actual ports and shipping. Landlocked broods need actual corridors. A connected continental brood is more dangerous because its real supply and armies can work together. The contact-merger rule does not turn water into land or instantly supply an overseas pocket.

## Army scale and recovery

Initial adult-alien strength comes from living infiltrators and measured absorbed adults. Deployment commits 30%, 45% or 60% according to actual preparedness. A tiny brood can begin with a verified funded understrength Cadre only if at least 50 actual combatants and their equipment can be committed. Otherwise Part 5 resolves a nonterritorial pocket with real host counterplay. A large fully prepared brood can field several formations. No country receives a fixed minimum army unrelated to its people and resources.

Cadre, Line and Siege have different roles, equipment bills and combat weaknesses. Their merger weights of 1.00, 1.50 and 3.00 are comparison values, not a substitute for native combat balance. The heavy organism must pay for its role in industrial cost, supply, movement or terrain limitations. A very small high-weight formation must not dominate actual battles simply because it wins a merger ranking.

Long-war adult-alien maturation uses paid 90-day batches, living seed staff, valid supplied sites and the country package's finite output caps. It never consumes human newborns or passively converts every human survivor. A merger preserves existing production commitments and their origin receipts. It does not restart the same batch or multiply its output through new tags.

Evaluate whether industrial maturation compounds faster than human military recovery. If it does, adjust throughput, input cost, site exposure or interruption risk before adding an arbitrary global human combat bonus. Humanity should beat the aliens through better strategy and production, with a feasible path to recovery.

## AI competence and restraint

The human AI sees the same admitted evidence and pays the same costs. It can use statistical expectations rather than human intuition, but it cannot read the hidden guilt field to choose a purge. The alien AI knows its own cells, resources and relays. It does not know unshared human investigation results.

AI decision weights are priorities under conditions, not automatically normalized percentages. Evaluate choices only among eligible actions with sufficient resources. An unavailable action should not absorb probability mass or drain resources. A poor country should prefer an affordable protection or cooperation route to repeatedly attempting an impossible national program.

Use representative configurations: peaceful small state, wartime major, fragmented occupied empire, low-Confidence security state, highly cooperative low-Reach state, isolated island brood, connected continental brood, and Mother host facing an interrupted supply line. A single generic AI profile cannot express all of these well.

## Difficulty options

Preserve the same evidence rules and portrait uncertainty across difficulty settings. Difficulty can adjust network opportunity frequency, AI resource reserves, warning generosity and alien logistical resilience within documented bounds. It should not make all attractive women aliens or turn a claimed 60% clue into a hidden 100% test.

The default experience retains the 90-day minimum late-opening response window and the explicit playable Mother victory route. A harsher optional preset can shorten protection opportunities or increase growth within approved tuning limits, but its consequences must be described. Existing shared game settings still determine whether ordinary events, evolutions or world-end scenarios may activate.

## Evaluation order

First validate accounting and state transitions. Then test evidence quality and policy incentives. Then test territorial outbreak scale and long-war production. Finally assess Mother frequency and the time required to defeat her. This order matters because a duplicated manpower grant or a hidden evidence leak can make a superficially convincing balance result meaningless.

The aim is a varied campaign. Some countries should contain the threat early. Some should make painful mistakes and recover. Some should fall from inside. A successful global invasion should create the Mother only after it earns the necessary position, and a determined human response should still have a plausible military route to victory.
