# Event 19 Infantry Spawn
## Part 3: Evolution I and Evolution II

## Evolution structure

Infantry Spawn has four true evolution stages. Each evolution changes the event’s identity and unlocks new systems. The evolution entry must support two paths.

- **Active-event evolution:** the country already has unresolved formations when the evolution becomes active. Existing lots are re-evaluated and gain the new opportunities or risks without waiting for another global firing.
- **Pre-fire evolved opening:** the event has not fired for a country since the evolution became active. Its next generation begins directly under the evolved rules.

An evolution records one global evolution milestone for Event 19. Country-level consequences remain ordinary history and detail events.

Evolution pacing should use the project evolution pipeline with mean time to happen. The normal design anchor is around 90 days once its world-state conditions are eligible, modified by chaos, event history, total event divisions, war pressure, and prior management outcomes. It should not unlock instantly unless a triggerable scenario explicitly forces an evolved setup.

## Evolution I: Organized Muster

### Identity change

The new formations begin arriving with recognizable tables of organization, coherent officer groups, better training, and larger equipment shares. The event stops feeling like a scattered local irregularity and begins resembling a parallel mobilization system that the state did not authorize.

The country gains better military value, but the formations become harder to dismiss as temporary curiosities. They expect places in the army, equipment replacement, promotion, and long-term command authority.

### Entry conditions

Evolution I becomes possible through a combination of:

- global chaos entering an appropriate early evolution range
- Event 19 having fired enough times globally or in enough countries to establish pattern memory
- a substantial world count of integrated event divisions
- wars involving a meaningful share of countries
- repeated successful or failed baseline management

No single fixed threshold should be the only gate. High global warfare can accelerate the evolution even when previous generations were managed well. Widespread baseline neglect can also accelerate it.

### Active-event evolution

When Evolution I activates for a country with unresolved baseline lots:

- coherent lots gain an accelerated standardization option
- selected weak lots can be reorganized into stronger reserve templates
- equipment debt is recalculated rather than erased
- officer and support-company opportunities appear
- current audit missions receive a revised success package
- stacked muster remains a liability and does not convert automatically into free quality

The country should receive a concise report direction explaining that the formations are reorganizing themselves or arriving with new staff structures. It should not imply that the government designed the change.

### Pre-fire evolved opening

A country whose next generation begins under Evolution I receives:

- fewer skeletal divisions
- higher average training
- larger coherent lot size
- more support companies
- more artillery and specialist weight
- more equipment on arrival
- more officer burden
- stronger political expectations from the new formations

### Evolution I template families

Evolution I retains the baseline families and adds stronger versions or new families. Working labels are not final localisation.

#### Line Infantry Regiment

- coherent infantry core
- moderate battalion count
- one or two support companies
- serviceable training more common

#### Gun Line Group

- infantry with line artillery or stronger artillery support
- high ammunition and supply demand
- useful for countries already producing artillery

#### Mobile Reserve

- cavalry, motorized, bicycle, camel, or mixed mobility based on terrain and available definitions
- stronger reconnaissance and deployment value
- variable fuel or animal sustainment burden

#### Engineer Reserve

- infantry core with engineer and logistics-oriented support
- favored in industrial, river, fortification, and front-adjacent states
- high support-equipment demand

#### Anti-Armor Detachment

- infantry with anti-tank support or battalions where valid
- more common when the country faces armored enemies
- equipment compatibility risk if the country lacks the relevant production

#### Air Defense Reserve

- infantry with anti-air support or battalions
- more common under enemy air superiority or heavy bombing

#### Composite Territorial Division

- larger local defense formation
- mixed infantry and mounted elements
- limited offensive capability
- efficient for garrison or regional defense after integration

### Training and equipment

Evolution I shifts the quality distribution but does not guarantee elite armies.

Typical result direction:

- improvised training becomes uncommon
- minimally drilled and partially trained remain common
- serviceable formations become credible outcomes
- fully trained formations remain rare and should be tied to favorable national conditions

Equipment arrival increases, but so does the burden on replacement stockpiles. A formation that arrives at 70 percent strength with artillery and support equipment can be more expensive than a baseline rifle cadre at 40 percent strength.

### War-state modifier

At-war countries gain a stronger Evolution I package when the state can support it.

Possible advantages:

- higher training
- higher initial organization
- more combat support
- front-aware state placement
- one emergency integrated lot

Possible burdens:

- shorter audit timer
- larger congestion increase
- higher equipment debt
- more local requisition
- higher chance that an officer group demands immediate command

A country losing a war may receive particularly strong but politically unstable formations.

### Evolution I decisions

#### Establish Muster Districts

Working role:

- groups selected states into manageable command districts
- reduces per-lot clutter
- improves audit and reinforcement
- creates a future structure that claimants can attempt to capture at Evolution III

Costs can include:

- army experience
- command power within the project cap
- support equipment
- trains for very large countries
- temporary staff burden

#### Appoint Integration Staff

Working role:

- improves standardization success
- lowers short-term field readiness while officers and units are reassigned
- creates an officer capacity record used by later generations

#### Issue Common Tables

Working role:

- converts a compatible group of event templates toward a national standard
- consumes equipment and training time
- prevents template proliferation

#### Preserve Specialist Companies

Working role:

- keeps valuable support elements while dissolving or consolidating weak combat cores
- yields a better long-term force but costs support equipment and time

#### Recognize the Emergency Reserve

Working role:

- accepts the event formations as a permanent reserve institution
- improves future readiness
- increases future political expectations and makes mass demobilization harder

### Evolution I flavor incidents

#### The Duplicate General Staff

Two officer staffs claim authority over the same formation district. The country can merge them, favor the regular army, or recognize the event staff as a separate reserve command.

The result affects Muster Control, officer capacity, and future claimant susceptibility.

#### A Division With Its Own Depot

A formation arrives with a functioning depot and unusual equipment records. The country can absorb the depot, preserve it as a separate system, or strip it for compatible equipment.

The choice affects equipment debt and the chance of future advanced lots.

#### The Regiment Demands Colors

A coherent formation seeks recognition as a permanent regiment. Accepting improves organization and local loyalty while increasing resistance to demobilization. Refusal can keep central control but lower readiness.

### Evolution I AI

AI should value organized formations more than baseline lots, but it must still account for burden.

- countries in difficult wars favor emergency integration
- stable industrial countries favor common tables and integration staff
- defensive countries favor muster districts and territorial reserves
- low-equipment countries favor specialist preservation and selective demobilization
- authoritarian or military governments are more willing to recognize a permanent reserve
- fragile civilian governments are more cautious about separate command institutions

## Evolution II: Arsenal Lottery

### Identity change

The event begins creating serious armies rather than a broad collection of weak infantry. Multiple units can appear in a selected state. Advanced armored, mechanized, and other high-value formations can appear even when the country has not unlocked the technology. Strange and narrow formations also become possible, including armored-car-heavy, helicopter-only where valid, or otherwise impractical lots.

The country gains an explicit way to request another random formation. The event becomes a deliberate gamble.

### Entry conditions

Evolution II becomes possible when several of these conditions are present:

- global chaos reaches a middle evolution range
- Event 19 has produced a high global formation count
- multiple major wars are active
- a meaningful share of countries have permanent event reserve institutions
- prior Evolution I lots have won significant battles or created serious supply crises
- repeated global firing establishes that the event can reproduce complex equipment

### Active-event evolution

Countries with active generations gain:

- a chance for unresolved coherent lots to receive additional equipment or support
- a decision to expand selected formation lots
- access to the random formation request system
- new supply and command missions
- the first advanced-equipment handling actions

Existing weak lots do not all transform into tanks. The evolution changes available growth and future generation weights.

### Pre-fire evolved opening

A new Evolution II generation can produce:

- more than one division in high-capacity selected states
- larger formation lots
- motorized, mechanized, armored, and armored-car families
- narrow specialist lots
- advanced equipment that the country cannot normally replace
- heavier initial supply strain
- stronger wartime formations

### Units per selected state

Evolution II introduces dynamic multiplicity.

Typical direction:

- remote or weak states usually produce one division
- ordinary states can produce one or two divisions
- capital, industrial, high-population, and front-adjacent states can produce two or more
- very large countries still use diminishing state coverage, so multiplicity does not return to one division on every state

The units per state value reacts to:

- population
- industry
- rail and supply capacity
- war state
- local front pressure
- chaos
- current congestion
- prior event generation history

High existing congestion should reduce the number of useful units but can increase the chance of disorganized extra shells, depending on management quality.

### Evolution II serious formation families

Working labels are not final localisation.

#### Armored Reserve Group

- tanks with motorized or mechanized support where valid
- high fuel and equipment burden
- limited reinforcement if technology is absent

#### Mechanized Column

- mechanized core or mixed motorized and mechanized composition
- high industrial demand
- valuable breakthrough or mobile defense potential

#### Motor Rifle Formation

- motorized infantry with support
- more replaceable than full mechanized lots
- fuel and truck burden

#### Assault Gun Group

- infantry with self-propelled artillery or armored support where valid
- strong but difficult to replenish without technology

#### Armored Car Screen

- armored-car-heavy formation
- useful for reconnaissance, security, or exploitation
- potentially poor against serious opposition

#### Air-Mobile Detachment

- helicopter-only or helicopter-heavy lot only when a valid Chaos Redux or game unit definition exists
- high fuel, equipment, and supply incompatibility risk
- no implicit technology unlock

#### Amphibious Reserve

- amphibious tanks, marines, or amphibious mechanized elements where valid
- favored in ports and coastal states
- expensive and narrow

#### Heavy Support Formation

- small combat core with many support companies
- can be extremely efficient or impossible to maintain

#### Single-Arm Curiosity

- a narrow formation dominated by one battalion family
- examples include only armored cars, only cavalry, only helicopters, or another valid narrow type
- creates the first deliberately weird outcomes without full Evolution III randomness

### Technology-locked equipment rule

Evolution II can create advanced equipment before the receiving country unlocks it. This is a finite military anomaly, not a research shortcut.

Rules:

- the division receives an initial bounded equipment package
- the country does not gain the technology automatically
- ordinary reinforcement is unavailable unless compatible equipment enters stockpile
- template editing cannot clone or expand the locked equipment family freely
- reverse engineering can provide research progress or a limited production route only through a costly decision or special project where supported
- capture, lend-lease, future event draws, and actual research can sustain the formation
- disbanding returns only controlled salvage and cannot become an equipment farm

A country can therefore win a battle with borrowed future equipment, but it cannot turn one strange division into an unlimited advanced army.

### Random formation request system

Evolution II unlocks a decision category action that requests one additional event formation lot.

The result is generated after costs are paid. It can be excellent, ordinary, narrow, weak, or logistically absurd.

#### Request cost model

The cost should scale with:

- controlled state count
- current event division count
- Army Congestion
- number of prior requests in the generation
- war state
- current equipment and manpower position
- desired urgency

Possible costs include:

- army experience
- support equipment
- infantry equipment
- trains
- trucks
- fuel
- manpower or labor burden
- temporary factory commitment
- stability or war support for coercive recruitment
- a cooldown

Political power or command power can be supporting costs, but they cannot be the whole price.

#### Request modes

The player can choose a broad purpose without choosing the result.

Working modes:

- field reinforcement
- mobile reserve
- territorial defense
- specialist request
- unrestricted draw

Each mode changes weights and costs. Unrestricted draw has the widest quality and absurdity range.

The player cannot request a tank specifically and guarantee a tank. The mechanic remains a lottery.

#### Escalating request risk

Each request within a generation:

- raises congestion
- increases cost
- slightly widens the weird-outcome tail
- creates a longer cooldown
- increases the chance of claimant attachment once Evolution III exists
- increases Anomalous Saturation once Evolution IV exists

The system should reward one or two deliberate gambles and make repeated spamming dangerous.

### Organization and supply category

Evolution II expands the decision category into a meaningful management system.

#### Survey the Formation Lots

Reveals or refreshes the lot list and current burden.

#### Open a Training Cycle

Starts a timed mission for selected lots. It consumes equipment and ties down training capacity. Success raises training and control. Failure wastes resources and can worsen organization.

#### Reserve the Rail Corridors

Temporarily dedicates trains and rail capacity to moving event formations. It lowers congestion but can weaken civilian or front supply elsewhere.

#### Build a Command District

Assigns a state group and lots to a coherent command structure. It is efficient but creates a future asset a claimant can seize.

#### Cannibalize an Advanced Lot

Breaks down an unsustainable advanced formation. It returns partial compatible and special salvage, lowers congestion, and may grant limited research insight. It destroys the formation’s immediate combat value.

#### Preserve the Prototype Formation

Keeps the advanced lot intact. It gains a special reinforcement restriction and a possible experience-based improvement path.

#### Request Another Formation

Runs the random formation request system.

### Real-effect flavor incidents

#### The Tanks Have No Manuals

An advanced armored lot arrives with crews who can operate the vehicles but cannot explain maintenance. The country can assign its best engineers, preserve the vehicles for one offensive, or dismantle part of the lot.

#### Rotorcraft Over the Parade Ground

A helicopter-heavy formation appears only when the unit exists in the installed game and mod context. The country must decide whether to commit scarce fuel and maintenance capacity or reduce it to a ceremonial curiosity.

#### Twelve Radios, Three Frequencies

A support-rich lot has incompatible communications. The player can spend signal equipment and time, accept command penalties, or split the lot.

#### The Cavalry Rejects the Trucks

A mixed mobility lot refuses standardization. The choice affects organization, local loyalty, and future random weights.

#### A Regiment of Armored Cars

The formation looks impressive but has narrow battlefield value. The country can use it for reconnaissance and occupation, retrain it, or gamble on preserving its unusual identity.

### Evolution II wartime strength

Countries at war can receive stronger units than countries at peace, with safeguards.

The wartime quality modifier considers:

- enemy strength relative to the country
- capital threat
- current division deficit
- front supply
- equipment stockpile
- casualties
- war duration

A desperate country can receive a powerful emergency formation even with poor stockpiles, but the resulting debt, congestion, and claimant susceptibility are high.

A winning major power with abundant divisions should not receive the same desperation bonus.

### Evolution II AI

AI uses request modes and management decisions through strategic profiles.

- a losing wartime AI can request one or more emergency draws
- an industrial AI preserves advanced prototypes more often
- a low-fuel AI avoids mobile reserve requests
- a low-supply AI favors territorial or supply-light outcomes
- an AI with severe congestion stops requesting formations unless the capital is threatened
- an AI cannot reroll bad results
- an AI should cannibalize an advanced lot it cannot sustain when the combat value is not strategically decisive

### Evolution II acceptance criteria

Evolution II is satisfied when:

- selected states can create multiple units through dynamic factors
- serious armor, mechanized, motorized, armored-car, amphibious, and valid helicopter families can appear
- technology-locked units remain finite and non-clonable
- wartime countries can receive stronger formations with greater burden
- the player and AI can request random formations on demand
- request costs are dynamic and materially varied
- repeated requests increase risk and congestion
- supply, training, command, and prototype handling use real missions or decisions
- flavor incidents alter gameplay state
- the system remains manageable at lot level rather than requiring one action per division
