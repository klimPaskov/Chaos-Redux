# Historical Design Research

This note records design anchors. The event is supernatural and global, so historical precedents are used for mechanics, social burden, and command politics rather than as claims that the event reproduces one real mobilization.

## Levée en masse

Ambrogio A. Caiani’s overview describes the 1793 levée en masse as an effort to place the population at the disposal of the war effort and create a logistics framework for an unprecedented army. It also emphasizes the tension between ideological promise, military organization, coercion, regional resistance, and the long legacy of mass mobilization.

Design uses:

- a new army consumes manpower, logistics, and labor
- national scale and local resistance can coexist
- mass formations create political identity and command expectations
- a large army can remain institutionally fragile

Source:

- [Ambrogio A. Caiani, “Levée en Masse,” European History Online](https://d-nb.info/1020542446/34)

The article’s license is CC BY-NC-ND. This package paraphrases concepts and does not reproduce extended text.

## British Home Guard

Imperial War Museums collection material records the early Home Guard’s shortage of firearms and use of improvised weapons. Other IWM material describes local defense of towns and villages.

Design uses:

- local formation identity
- uneven equipment
- territorial-defense roles
- improvised weapons and training
- weak units that remain useful in the right mission

Sources:

- [Home Guard truncheon and improvised weapon context](https://www.iwm.org.uk/collections/item/object/30003198)
- [Growing Up in the Second World War](https://www.iwm.org.uk/history/second-world-war/home-front/growing-up-in-the-second-world-war)

## Volkssturm and coercive desperation

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum notes that boys in their early teens were drawn into civil defense and the Volkssturm during the last desperate months of Nazi Germany. USHMM material also describes the Volkssturm as a national conscript militia including older men and youths.

Design uses:

- desperation can increase formation strength on paper while worsening quality and moral cost
- late-war recruitment can draw people outside normal military standards
- coercive mobilization should be presented critically, without celebratory or comic treatment

Sources:

- [USHMM Holocaust Encyclopedia, Hitler Youth](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/hitler-youth-2)
- [USHMM Holocaust Encyclopedia, Mauthausen](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/mauthausen)

## Petrograd Order No. 1 and dual military authority

The text of Order No. 1 called for elected soldier and sailor committees, placed weapons under committee control, and made military commission orders conditional on compatibility with Soviet decisions. Michigan State University’s contextual essay describes the resulting struggle over discipline, command, desertion, officer authority, and political meaning.

Design uses:

- event formations can develop their own representative or claimant structures
- control of weapons and depots is political power
- dual authority can produce negotiation, co-option, collapse, or revolt
- claimant politics should not be reduced to a simplistic claim that soldier participation alone destroys an army

Sources:

- [Yale Avalon Project, Order No. I of the Petrograd Soviet](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/soviet_001.asp)
- [Seventeen Moments in Soviet History, Revolution in the Army](https://soviethistory.msu.edu/1917/revolution-in-the-army/)

## Design cautions

- The event should not equate all militias with one ideology or regime.
- The historical sources include coercion, political violence, child mobilization, and mass social disruption. Player-facing humor must not trivialize those subjects.
- Claimant generals are fictional supernatural figures. Historical command crises provide structural inspiration, not direct character models.
- Regional name pools and uniforms need separate research during asset and localisation production.
