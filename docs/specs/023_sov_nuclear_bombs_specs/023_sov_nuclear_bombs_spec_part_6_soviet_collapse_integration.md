# Event 023 specification, Part 6: Soviet Collapse integration

## Design purpose

Event 23 should become especially important during Event 5, Soviet Union Collapse. The arsenal is physically distributed across a large country, while political authority, transport, technical control, and delivery forces may split.

The collapse bridge must create a custody crisis without treating every breakaway as an instant nuclear power. A country can control a storage state and still lack codes, technical personnel, usable devices, delivery forces, or a coherent authorization chain.

Event 5 remains the owner of the collapse, republic release, coalition, wars, and terminal collapse logic. Event 23 owns the nuclear custody ledger, Soviet recovery or coercion actions, breakaway nuclear bargaining, and the transition from physical possession to operational capability.

## Activation gate

The Event 23 collapse bridge activates only when all relevant conditions hold:

- Event 5 begins or enters a state-transfer phase.
- Event 23 has fired, or another verified source has created registered Soviet nuclear devices in the Event 23 custody ledger.
- At least one storage, production, command, test, or delivery site is affected by the collapse.
- No terminal world-end state has frozen the relevant systems.

If Event 23 has not fired and no registered Soviet arsenal exists, Event 5 proceeds without the nuclear custody layer. Event 23 should not create a retroactive stockpile merely because the Soviet Union collapses.

## Pre-collapse custody snapshot

Before Event 5 transfers states, releases countries, or changes controllers, the bridge records a frozen custody snapshot.

The snapshot includes:

- Total Soviet operational bombs.
- Bombs reserved for tests or strikes.
- Bombs assigned to each registered storage state.
- Devices in transit.
- Devices under dismantlement.
- Missing or already disputed devices.
- Production and assembly sites.
- Test sites with active missions.
- Central command and backup command states.
- Technical staff pools or institutional access markers.
- Delivery forces and their current owners.
- Current custody doctrine.
- Current Readiness, Integrity, posture, and public knowledge.

The snapshot must occur before state ownership changes. Call it from a narrow Event 5 integration point. A periodic world scan is forbidden.

## Post-transfer reconciliation

After Event 5 completes one state-transfer tranche, the bridge compares the new owners and controllers with the frozen snapshot.

Each affected device or facility receives one custody state.

| Custody state | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Central Soviet custody | The Soviet Union controls the site, staff, and command route |
| Soviet custody under siege | The Soviet Union still owns the devices, but local control or transport is contested |
| Breakaway physical custody | A breakaway controls the site or convoy, but lacks verified operational control |
| Joint custody | Soviet and breakaway authorities, or a foreign observer arrangement, share access under a settlement |
| Foreign custody | A non-breakaway foreign country has seized, occupied, or received the devices |
| Technical denial | The site changed hands, but devices were disabled, disassembled, or stripped of essential components |
| Missing | The ledger cannot prove where the device is |
| Destroyed or dismantled | The device no longer exists as an operational bomb |

A custody transaction must conserve the total arsenal. The bridge cannot create a new bomb when a site changes hands or lose one without a recorded reason.

## Physical custody is not operational control

A breakaway can become operational only through a staged process.

### Stage 1: Physical possession

Requirements:

- The breakaway controls a registered storage state, convoy, or device.
- At least one device survives technical denial and occupation damage.

Effects:

- The country can bargain, conceal, return, or attempt to secure the device.
- It cannot launch a weapon.
- Foreign intelligence and Soviet recovery pressure begin.

### Stage 2: Technical access

Requirements can include:

- Captured or recruited nuclear technicians.
- Access to assembly or maintenance records.
- A functioning laboratory or facility.
- Time without successful Soviet recovery.
- No complete Soviet disablement package.

Effects:

- The country can inspect and maintain a small number of devices.
- Accident and exposure risk rises.
- The country still lacks a verified launch route.

### Stage 3: Command formation

Requirements can include:

- A recognized political authority.
- A command chain with adequate Integrity.
- Secure custody and accounting.
- A deliberate doctrine choice.
- No foreign joint-custody restriction.

Effects:

- The country can issue threats based on custody.
- The threat remains less credible than an established tested arsenal.
- A new internal dispute can block progress.

### Stage 4: Delivery integration

Requirements can include:

- A current vanilla nuclear delivery platform.
- Range, fuel, access, and crew preparation.
- A valid exact target route.
- At least one certified operational device.

Effects:

- The country becomes an operational nuclear actor for shared systems.
- It does not inherit Soviet Event 23 evolutions, reactor grants, production capacity, or global event ownership.
- Its arsenal remains limited to the devices and capabilities it actually secured.

The progression should take months, not days. Foreign aid can accelerate it, while sabotage, inspection, blockade, or staff departure can reverse it.

## Breakaway classifications

The bridge should distinguish several breakaway situations.

### Ordinary breakaway with no nuclear site

- Receives no nuclear decisions.
- May react to Soviet threats through Event 23 target responses.
- Can seek a guarantee or publicize the threat.

### Breakaway with a storage site but no technical staff

- Receives a custody crisis category with return, concealment, joint custody, and foreign aid options.
- Cannot claim operational capability.
- Faces high accident and theft risk if it tries to move devices.

### Breakaway with staff or facility access

- Can attempt technical access.
- Can negotiate from a stronger position.
- Foreign majors may offer removal, security, recognition, or technical containment.

### Breakaway with delivery forces

- Can pursue operationalization if all other stages are completed.
- Delivery forces alone do not create usable bombs.

### Breakaway already recognized and externally backed

- Is less likely to accept immediate Soviet reintegration.
- Is more likely to accept monitored disarmament, joint custody, or a transfer-for-assurances settlement.
- A supporting major can deter a Soviet strike without automatically granting nuclear control.

## Soviet collapse decision family

The Soviet player should see no more than three to five primary collapse actions at one time. Actions are filtered by the selected custody crisis.

### Recall the arsenal

A pre-transfer or early-collapse decision orders devices back toward secure central storage.

- Requires a valid rail or transport route.
- Consumes trains, fuel, guards, and time.
- Raises the chance that the central arsenal survives.
- Can expose convoys and lower Readiness during transit.
- Fails or pauses if the route becomes contested.

### Secure the rail corridor

A timed objective asks the Soviet Union to hold named states, hubs, or rail links between a depot and central custody.

- Requires real units and supply.
- Should auto-complete when the route is secure.
- Failure can transfer physical custody to a breakaway or create a missing-device incident.

### Reinforce the depot

- Commits equipment, manpower, and a local garrison.
- Improves Integrity and delays seizure.
- Can provoke local resistance or an early clash.
- Cannot be repeated indefinitely on the same site.

### Negotiate return

- Offers recognition, aid, autonomy, security assurances, fuel, civilian support, or a limited political settlement.
- The breakaway can return all devices, accept joint custody, or refuse.
- A negotiated return improves Integrity more than a forced raid.

### Demand immediate transfer

- Uses the Event 23 coercion system.
- Available after Evolution II or during a direct custody emergency.
- The demand identifies the depot, devices, and response deadline.
- Refusal can lead to a raid, conventional war, limited nuclear warning, or broader settlement.

### Conduct a recovery raid

- Targets one exact depot or convoy.
- Requires intelligence, special forces or current equivalent, transport, and a verified route.
- Risks destroying devices, scattering material, killing staff, or pushing the breakaway toward operationalization.
- A successful raid returns the accounted devices to Soviet custody.

### Disable the devices

- Uses technical access, sabotage, or a preinstalled denial package.
- Converts operational or intact devices into Technical Denial status.
- Reduces future risk at the cost of permanently losing some arsenal value.
- Can fail if local staff have already removed the relevant components.

### Destroy the site

- Last-resort action when recovery is impossible and operationalization is imminent.
- Uses a shared accident, demolition, or strike consequence profile based on what actually happens.
- Can create deaths, contamination, and condemnation.
- Must not apply the full nuclear-strike profile unless a nuclear detonation truly occurs.

### Restore the ledger

- Becomes available after territory is recovered or a settlement is signed.
- Audits devices, staff, transport, and control.
- Resolves Missing, Joint Custody, and disputed entries.
- Restores Command Integrity when the totals reconcile.

## Breakaway decision family

### Declare custody

- Makes physical possession public or formally claims administrative control.
- Increases bargaining power and foreign attention.
- Does not grant operational use.

### Conceal the devices

- Moves or hides devices inside controlled territory.
- Raises Soviet recovery difficulty.
- Lowers local Integrity and increases accident risk.
- Can produce Missing status if the breakaway's own accounting fails.

### Return for recognition

- Offers devices in exchange for Soviet or international recognition, security assurances, aid, or withdrawal.
- Can create a durable nonnuclear settlement.

### Accept joint custody

- Places access under a Soviet, breakaway, and possible foreign observer arrangement.
- Blocks unilateral launch.
- Creates a timed settlement that can mature into transfer, dismantlement, or renewed dispute.

### Request foreign removal assistance

- A valid foreign major can provide transport, technicians, guards, or dismantlement support.
- The foreign actor should not automatically receive operational ownership.
- The process can fail if routes are blocked or the Soviet Union intervenes.

### Attempt technical access

- Begins a long, risky mission toward Stage 2.
- Requires actual staff or facility access.
- Can trigger accidents, defections, sabotage, or foreign pressure.

### Build an independent command

- Available only after technical access.
- Requires political stability, secure custody, and time.
- Creates internal faction choices over use, bargaining, or disarmament.

### Integrate a delivery route

- Final operationalization step.
- Uses current vanilla requirements.
- Does not create free bombers, missiles, fuel, or air access.

### Dismantle under observation

- Removes devices through a verified process.
- Can return fissile material or economic compensation if a negotiated settlement provides it.
- Ends operationalization progress for the dismantled devices.

## Foreign intervention

Foreign majors can react through bounded diplomacy and aid.

Possible actions include:

- Offer security assurances for return or dismantlement.
- Fund safe transport and technical removal.
- Send observers.
- Guarantee the breakaway against a Soviet recovery war.
- Pressure the breakaway not to operationalize.
- Pressure the Soviet Union not to strike the depot.
- Offer a neutral storage arrangement.
- Conduct a covert removal or sabotage operation only when an accepted intelligence route supports it.

Foreign intervention should create obligations and risk. A guarantee can deter Soviet coercion while making the crisis international. Technical aid can increase safety while also improving the breakaway's scientific capacity if mishandled.

No foreign decision should create a free operational arsenal from devices it does not control.

## Soviet nuclear threats against breakaways

Breakaways are a core Evolution II target family.

Valid demands include:

- Return the devices.
- Accept joint custody.
- Allow Soviet evacuation of the depot.
- Withdraw forces from a nuclear site.
- Accept a ceasefire while removal occurs.
- Accept Soviet protection or a negotiated reintegration arrangement.

Threatening a storage state with a nuclear strike creates a special contradiction. A strike may destroy the devices, spread material, kill technical staff, and make recovery impossible. The target-profile logic should treat a depot strike as a dangerous denial action, not an ordinary way to reclaim the arsenal.

Soviet AI should prefer negotiation, rail security, conventional recovery, and technical disablement before nuclear use against a breakaway depot.

## Collapse and command integrity

Event 5 pressure should affect Event 23 dynamically.

Integrity falls when:

- Central authority loses storage states.
- Several breakaways receive physical custody.
- Technical staff split.
- The capital or backup command is lost.
- Devices enter transit through contested territory.
- Conflicting Soviet authorities issue orders.
- Missing-device entries accumulate.

Readiness can move in either direction.

- Dispersal and local delegation can keep devices usable during collapse.
- Lost transport, maintenance, fuel, or delivery forces can make the arsenal unusable.
- A broken central chain should not create free rapid launches.

If Integrity reaches Broken Chain, normal central authorization pauses. The Soviet Union must restore accounting, establish a verified emergency command, or use a separately validated local retaliation route.

## Collapse outcomes

### Central recovery

- All devices return to Soviet custody or are accounted as dismantled.
- Integrity receives a major recovery.
- The Soviet Union can keep the existing posture or enter a moratorium.
- Breakaways retain political consequences from coercion and intervention.

### Negotiated distribution

- Some devices enter joint custody, transfer, or monitored dismantlement.
- The Soviet Union loses part of the arsenal.
- Integrity can recover if the ledger reconciles.
- Foreign assurances and recognition shape the post-collapse order.

### Nuclear successor emergence

- One breakaway completes operationalization.
- It becomes a shared nuclear actor with a limited inherited stockpile.
- It receives its own bounded command and custody state, not the Event 23 Soviet evolution ladder.
- The world receives a news event when capability becomes public.

### Arsenal fragmentation

- Several actors hold devices, staff, or delivery assets without stable control.
- Missing-device and accident risk rises.
- Event 23 enters Broken Chain.
- Recovery, inspection, or dismantlement becomes the main play loop.

### Foreign seizure

- A foreign actor gains physical or joint custody.
- The Soviet Union receives recovery, diplomatic, or war options based on the actual relationship.
- Operational use still requires the foreign actor's real capability and the shared nuclear route.

### Soviet extinction

If `SOV` no longer exists:

- Event 23 no longer has a primary host.
- Soviet-only production, reactor grants, and evolution decisions stop.
- Existing custody actors continue their local custody or operationalization tracks.
- Shared nuclear consequences and Fallout remain active.
- No arbitrary successor inherits the entire Soviet arsenal or Event 23 identity.
- A restored `SOV` does not automatically regain devices that have been transferred, dismantled, destroyed, or operationalized elsewhere.

## Cleanup and idempotence

The collapse bridge must be safe when Event 5 releases countries in several waves.

Required behavior:

- Snapshot each affected tranche once.
- Reconcile only states and devices changed by that tranche.
- Prevent duplicate device transfer.
- Clear stale selected targets and missions when a breakaway is annexed or changes side.
- Return recovered devices once.
- Preserve devices under active joint custody through save and reload.
- Remove obsolete decision categories after settlement.
- Keep shared nuclear actor registration aligned with current operational capability.

## Historical inspiration boundary

The design draws on the post-Soviet distinction between weapons being located in successor states and launch command remaining centralized. It also draws on negotiated transfer, security assurances, technical assistance, and monitored dismantlement.

The event remains alternate history. Its 1930s or 1940s breakaways, state locations, delivery systems, and political settlements must come from the current campaign and Event 5 implementation. Do not copy the 1991 dissolution directly.

## Writing direction

Collapse text should focus on guarded trains, sealed depots, missing officers, local commanders, technicians deciding whom to obey, foreign aircraft arriving with inspectors, and civilians living near contested sites.

Avoid calling every breakaway a nuclear state. Text must distinguish possession, access, command, and operational use.
