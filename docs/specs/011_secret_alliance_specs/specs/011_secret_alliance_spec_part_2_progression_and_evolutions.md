# Event 011 Secret Alliance

## Part 2: Progression, operations, recruitment, and evolutions

## Operational rhythm

The pact acts through an event-owned operation cycle. Operations should be infrequent in the calm-world baseline and should accelerate through chaos, evolution, successful recruitment, target aggression, and a major sponsor.

The system should avoid a fixed calendar. A base pacing near the project evolution norm of roughly ninety days can be shortened or lengthened by:

- current chaos value and tier
- pact cohesion
- pact readiness
- the number of active members
- major sponsor participation
- target world-tension contribution
- target wars and annexations
- player counterintelligence and preparedness
- recent pact failures
- the risk that repeated incidents would become noisy or repetitive

Only one substantial operation should normally be active against the player at a time. Minor flavor incidents may overlap when they do not create stacked penalties.

## Operation families

### Diplomatic isolation

These operations try to make the target look dangerous or unreliable.

Possible incidents include synchronized diplomatic complaints, coordinated withdrawal of attachés, pressure on neutral trade partners, mirrored guarantees around the target, refusal of transit rights, and a campaign to move regional conferences away from target influence.

Effects should act through opinion, access, temporary diplomatic friction, recruitment willingness, and pact cohesion. They should not create free war goals.

### Intelligence penetration

These operations seek plans, technology, deployment information, and political vulnerabilities.

Possible incidents include a compromised military liaison, stolen mobilization papers, a recruited clerk, missing cipher material, a suspicious technical delegation, and an attempted theft from an air or naval office.

Success should raise pact readiness and make later sabotage or border planning stronger. Failure should raise player evidence and may create a suspect.

### Industrial and transport sabotage

Baseline sabotage begins with disruption that can resemble negligence, labor confusion, theft, or administrative failure. Later stages can use deliberate destruction.

Possible incidents include damaged machine tools, a railway routing failure, contaminated lubricants, missing spare parts, warehouse fires, cut signal lines, and the disappearance of a specialized engineer.

The target should feel the effect through production disruption, repair burdens, lost equipment, delayed supply, and local protection decisions. The event should avoid repeated permanent factory destruction. Permanent building damage belongs to the more aggressive Evolution II operation pool and should be uncommon.

### Political and social pressure

These operations spread rumors, fund hostile networks, exploit opposition groups, and create uncertainty among officials and officers.

Possible incidents include copied propaganda themes appearing in several regions, unexplained money moving through front organizations, a compromised newspaper syndicate, threats to a minister, and an attempted political killing.

The event should not become a generic ideology conversion system. Political pressure serves coalition preparation and target confusion.

### Military preparation

These operations prepare routes, depots, border plans, air access, naval basing, and common staff assumptions.

Possible incidents include survey teams near a border, ammunition stockpiles in a neutral port, military observers traveling under civilian cover, coordinated exercises, and the quiet standardization of call-to-arms plans.

These operations mainly raise pact readiness. They become more visible in Evolution II and dominate Evolution III.

### Recruitment operations

Recruitment operations target one valid candidate. The candidate may accept, refuse, leak, or attempt to manipulate the pact.

Acceptance depends on motive compatibility, fear of the target, sponsor pressure, relations, distance, faction status, expected gains, and the pact's recent success.

Refusal can lower cohesion. A private refusal has little player impact. A leaked refusal raises evidence. A public refusal can force early exposure.

## Recruitment limits and quality

The pact should prefer a smaller set of plausible countries over a very large random coalition.

Suggested membership bands are:

| State | Typical active membership | Major participation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | 3 founders | none |
| Evolution I | 4 to 6 members | none |
| Evolution II | 6 to 9 members | up to 1 major |
| Evolution III | 8 to 12 members | up to 2 majors when earned |

These are tuning bands. The actual cap should react to the valid candidate pool and target strength. A small isolated target should not face twelve countries by default. A globally dominant target may attract a wider coalition.

A recruited country that later becomes invalid receives a loyalty test. A country that joins the target's faction, becomes the target's subject, enters an incompatible faction as its leader, collapses into civil war, or loses independence cannot remain an active pact member.

Invalid countries are removed from the active member count before reveal. Their prior actions remain part of the history, but the reveal contract applies to the current valid members. This prevents the system from promising that every member will enter war when the engine state makes that impossible.

## Member disputes

Mixed motives create internal incidents. Disputes are important because they give the player ways to weaken the pact without identifying every country.

Useful disputes include:

- a territorial promise made to two members
- ideological hostility between recruits
- a minor country resenting major sponsor control
- a member refusing political assassination
- an opportunist demanding an early war
- a defensive member objecting to offensive partition plans
- a sponsor withholding promised arms
- a member fearing exposure after a failed operation

A dispute can lower cohesion, expose one participant, delay an operation, change doctrine, or create a defection opportunity.

## Baseline behavior

The calm-world baseline is slow and subtle. It should remain noticeable across time through recurring method, not through large penalties.

Baseline operations should favor diplomatic isolation, intelligence tests, low-level sabotage, and recruitment. The player may receive sparse report events, but no decision category and no direct statement that governments are conspiring.

The baseline can end through one of four routes:

- Evolution I becomes available and later occurs
- exposure rises unexpectedly through a leak
- a member enters a hostile war with the target, causing immediate reveal
- the pact fractures or loses too many members to continue

## Evolution I

### Design role

Evolution I turns a three-country conspiracy into a growing network. Additional minor countries join, operations become more coordinated, and the player can recognize that several incidents share a method.

The pact remains secret. There is no direct war option and no formal player response category.

### Chaos placement

The default unlock is the Gathering Storm tier. Chaos changes likelihood and intensity, but the evolution should still use dynamic pacing rather than occur immediately at the threshold.

### Active-event entry

When the event is already active, Evolution I raises the minor-member cap, opens stronger recruitment, broadens the operation pool, and allows coordinated incidents involving more than one country.

Existing members immediately receive the evolved recruitment and operation behavior. The event does not need to fire again.

### Pre-fire evolved opening

When Event 011 first fires after Evolution I is available, the opening may begin with four minor members instead of three, or with three founders and an accelerated first recruitment.

The player-facing opening remains subtle. The evolved opening changes internal strength, pacing, and the frequency of recurring signatures.

### New content

- more minor-country invitations
- paired diplomatic pressure
- coordinated intelligence collection
- wider rumor and propaganda patterns
- multi-country logistics preparation
- member disputes over recruitment promises

### Containment and failure

A major leak can stop recruitment for a period. Repeated member refusals can return the pact to three members. If cohesion collapses before Evolution II, the pact can dissolve without public reveal.

## Evolution II

### Design role

Evolution II changes the event from a suspicious pattern into an active national-security crisis. One major country may join. Operations become more damaging. The player receives a decision category for investigation, protection, diplomacy, and limited counteraction.

The player knows that foreign governments are coordinating. The player does not receive a complete member list.

### Chaos placement

The default unlock is the Rising Chaos tier. The Chaos tier above it increases aggression and readiness without creating a separate evolution stage.

### Active-event entry

When the event is already active, an eligible major country can be invited after dynamic pacing. The major may become sponsor, prospective faction leader, intelligence coordinator, arms supplier, or political patron.

A major does not join automatically. It evaluates target threat, ideology, strategic rivalry, distance, faction commitments, and the pact's cohesion. A refusal can expose the pact or split the minors.

The player response category appears after the first unmistakably coordinated incident. Existing incidents and suspects are carried into the new system.

### Pre-fire evolved opening

When Event 011 first fires after Evolution II is available, one eligible major can found the pact and recruit two to four minors. The first player-facing incidents arrive sooner and are more damaging. The response category opens after a short discovery sequence rather than requiring the full baseline.

This is a major-led opening, not a baseline opening with a major added as decoration.

### New operation pool

- deliberate industrial destruction
- assassinations or attempted killings tied to a clear strategic purpose
- arms caches and covert military aid
- border provocations
- coordinated threats and diplomatic ultimatums
- attacks on rail, port, air, or communications infrastructure
- false evidence designed to implicate an innocent country
- recruitment of a target-country official
- support for a hostile proxy or opposition network

Killings should be uncommon and consequential. They should not become flavor spam. When deaths are large enough to matter, the implementation should use the shared Deaths system rather than invent a separate tally.

### Player-facing changes

- Evidence and Preparedness become visible
- a small suspect list appears
- confidence bands replace raw hidden membership
- protection, investigation, negotiation, and limited border actions become available
- wrong-country actions can help the pact
- successful counterplay can mark members as compromised before reveal

### Containment and failure

The player can prevent a major from joining, turn a minor member, force a sponsor withdrawal, expose recruitment, or harden the target enough that the pact delays war.

The player cannot directly declare a full coalition war through this category yet. Limited border conflict can occur against a credible neighboring suspect. A border conflict does not count as full war for automatic reveal, but escalation from that conflict into a normal war does.

## Evolution III

### Design role

Evolution III ends strategic ambiguity. The pact becomes public and forms a visible faction. War is likely and begins soon unless the player has fractured the coalition, forced negotiations, or created strong deterrence.

A second major may join when earlier player choices left recruitment open and the first major retained control. A successful player investigation can prevent this.

### Chaos placement

The default unlock is Totalen Chaos. It should use dynamic pacing after the threshold and should not occur instantly for an already active pact.

### Active-event entry

The pact compares readiness, exposure, cohesion, and player preparedness.

- High readiness and low exposure produces a coalition reveal on the pact's terms, with strong opening coordination.
- High exposure produces a forced public reveal, weaker cohesion, and more player knowledge.
- Low cohesion can produce a partial reveal, member withdrawal, or sponsor dispute.
- High player preparedness can delay the offensive and create a negotiation window.

The faction appears on the map when the reveal completes. A dynamic pre-war mission begins when war has not already started.

### Pre-fire evolved opening

When Event 011 first fires while Evolution III is available, it begins with the Evolution II major-led package. It does not jump directly to public war.

The opening uses a compressed discovery period. Evolution III then occurs after a shorter dynamic delay. The player receives a brief but real opportunity to investigate, protect the country, and affect which members answer the final call.

### New content

- public faction formation
- member list and leader reveal
- estimated war countdown
- direct player war and preemption options
- final recruitment of a second major when permitted
- member mobilization incidents
- coalition ultimatum or public justification
- emergency countermeasures
- faction fracture and negotiated withdrawal paths

## Rare variants

### The unlikely front

Ideologically incompatible countries join because fear of the target outweighs their hostility to one another. Cohesion begins lower, but the player has difficulty predicting membership through ideology.

### The double agent

A recruit accepts the invitation while secretly cooperating with the target. This variant becomes possible through strong diplomacy, high evidence, or a member dispute. The double agent can feed false readiness data, delay the call to arms, expose an operation, or reveal a founder.

### The false architect

One founder manufactures attacks or leaks to make the target appear responsible. Recruitment becomes easier, but discovery of the fabrication can collapse cohesion.

### Two majors, two plans

Evolution III gains a second major sponsor. Each major expects leadership and different war aims. The coalition is stronger in material terms and more vulnerable to a leadership split.

### The frightened compact

The target's expansion, annexations, or threat generation gives the pact a broadly defensive motive. Diplomatic reassurance and restraint become more effective, while reckless preemption strongly reinforces the coalition.

### The manufactured enemy

A peaceful target is selected by a sponsor that wants a convenient enemy. Evidence can reveal the sponsor's role and make minors abandon the pact.

### The neighbor's knife

A neighboring member becomes the operational leader. Border incidents, reconnaissance, and arms caches become common. The player gains stronger border-war tools and faces a higher accidental-reveal risk.

### Empty promises

The pact recruits through territorial and economic promises it cannot satisfy. Exposure of conflicting promises creates a powerful fracture opportunity.

## Evolution log direction

Evolution entries must describe the changing public pattern without spoiling future stages.

Evolution I should communicate that separate incidents have begun to share timing and methods.

Evolution II should communicate that foreign interference has become organized enough to require a national response.

Evolution III can use the public pact identity because the faction is revealed.

The evolution record should include the target country as actor context where the log supports it. It should not name hidden member countries before they are confirmed.
