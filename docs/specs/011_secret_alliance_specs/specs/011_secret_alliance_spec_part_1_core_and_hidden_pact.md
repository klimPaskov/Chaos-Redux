# Event 011 Secret Alliance

## Part 1: Core experience and the hidden pact

## The playable promise

Secret Alliance creates a campaign-long diplomatic threat that begins below the level of open hostility. Three countries decide that the target country has become too dangerous, too disruptive, too vulnerable, or too valuable to leave alone. They establish a concealed pact whose public members continue ordinary diplomacy while private representatives coordinate influence, espionage, sabotage, recruitment, and eventual war.

The target initially sees disconnected consequences. A factory loses a specialized machine after a visit by foreign technicians. A military courier disappears near a neutral border. Identical rumors spread in ports that do not normally share newspapers. A foreign attaché leaves one capital and appears in another under a different title. None of these incidents should identify a coalition, list its members, or tell the player that a war is coming.

The event becomes legible through repetition. Similar methods appear in several places. Suspects overlap. Timings begin to align. The player eventually understands that foreign governments are coordinating, but exact membership remains uncertain until investigation or public reveal.

The system should support four broad player experiences:

1. **The patient investigation.** The player builds evidence, protects vulnerable systems, turns one or more members, and breaks the coalition before it can reveal itself.
2. **The armed deterrent.** The player discovers enough to prepare the army and border, survives the reveal, and exploits the pact's weak coordination during the opening war.
3. **The reckless accusation.** The player acts on weak evidence, harms innocent countries, strengthens recruitment, and gives the pact a stronger public justification.
4. **The surprise encirclement.** The player dismisses the incidents, reaches Evolution III with low preparedness, and faces a public coalition with completed plans and little warning time.

## The target country

The target is the country controlled by the player when Event 011 begins. The target remains fixed for the life of the event. Tag switching does not retarget the conspiracy to the new player country.

In multiplayer, a normal firing belongs to one recipient country. Other human countries may become allies, suspects, or pact members only if the multiplayer safety rules allow it. The event must never silently convert another human country into an involuntary enemy. A human-controlled candidate requires an explicit event choice to join, refuse, leak the invitation, or expose the approach.

If the target country ceases to exist before reveal, the pact records that its objective has been overtaken by events and closes. It does not redirect itself to the target's conqueror. If the target becomes a subject, the pact evaluates whether the original government still exists and whether hostile operations remain meaningful. A surviving player-controlled target continues to be the object of the pact, while a fully absorbed or non-playable target ends the chain.

## Founder selection

The ordinary opening chooses three minor countries.

A valid founder should be:

- alive and independent
- not capitulated
- not in a civil war state that makes foreign commitments unreliable
- not at war with the target
- not in the target's faction
- not the target's subject or overlord
- not a nonhuman or special chaos country that ordinary diplomacy should exclude
- able to enter a faction later if the pact is revealed

Countries outside a faction receive a strong preference. A country in a faction can enter only when it is not the faction leader, its faction is not aligned with the target, and its later withdrawal can be handled safely. This is a preference path, not the default.

The selection should be weighted by motive. The three founders should not feel like names drawn from a hat when the world offers stronger candidates.

Useful positive factors include:

- a land or naval approach to the target
- claims, cores, border disputes, or recent territorial losses involving the target
- strong ideological hostility
- fear created by the target's army, industry, wars, annexations, or world-tension contribution
- diplomatic isolation from the target
- existing rivalry with a target ally
- pressure from an eligible major sponsor at an evolved opening

Useful negative factors include:

- high opinion of the target
- a guarantee from the target
- military access or deep trade dependence
- membership in the same wider diplomatic system
- distance that gives the country no plausible strategic interest
- severe domestic collapse

No single motive should be required. A coalition can form from fear, revenge, ideology, opportunism, or sponsorship. Mixed motives are a central source of later weakness.

If fewer than three valid minor founders exist, the event is unavailable and should show `N/A` in the event catalogue weight surface. The event must not use an invalid country, a subject, or an impossible faction member as a substitute.

## Member motives

Every founder and recruit receives one hidden primary motive. The motive shapes operations, willingness to accept risk, defection chance, and behavior after reveal.

| Motive | Why the country joins | Preferred methods | Main weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fear | The target appears likely to dominate its region | containment, guarantees, military planning | can be reassured by credible restraint |
| Grievance | The country has lost land, prestige, influence, or security | sabotage, border pressure, punitive war | may accept a specific settlement |
| Ideology | The target's government is treated as an existential political threat | propaganda, infiltration, regime pressure | can clash with ideologically incompatible members |
| Patronage | A stronger sponsor provides money, intelligence, or protection | recruitment, logistics, coordinated operations | may leave when the sponsor withdraws |
| Opportunism | The country expects territory, concessions, or status | late joining, spoils demands, military pressure | low loyalty when victory becomes costly |
| Regime survival | The government believes the target supports domestic enemies | counter-subversion, assassinations, censorship | vulnerable to guarantees and noninterference terms |

The motive should not be shown to the player before the member is identified. After reveal, member-specific events and tooltips can explain why the government entered the pact.

## The hidden pact as a living system

The pact is not a Hearts of Iron IV faction before reveal. It is an event-owned network with a target, founders, recruits, a prospective leader, operations, and internal values.

Four values organize the system:

### Pact cohesion

Cohesion measures whether the members agree on purpose, timing, and acceptable costs. It rises through successful operations, sponsor leadership, shared ideology, target aggression, and member confidence. It falls through exposure, conflicting motives, failed operations, player diplomacy, member casualties, and disputes over war aims.

Cohesion is hidden before reveal. Evolution II can provide a broad intelligence estimate such as fragmented, functioning, disciplined, or brittle. Exact values remain hidden.

### Pact readiness

Readiness measures military plans, stockpiles, mobilization coordination, access routes, intelligence on the target, and willingness to enter war. It rises slowly in the baseline and faster at later evolutions. Sabotage success, border access, a major sponsor, and low player preparedness accelerate it.

Readiness is hidden before Evolution III. At Evolution III the player sees a broad war-readiness estimate and an estimated time window, not a precise declaration date.

### Evidence

Evidence is the player's visible investigation progress once the Evolution II response category appears. It is built through successful missions, captured operatives, decoded traffic, cooperative foreign services, turned members, and repeated operation signatures.

Evidence unlocks better suspect confidence, safer diplomatic approaches, public disclosure, and preemptive action. Weak evidence can be spent on risky accusations, but the cost of error is high.

### Preparedness

Preparedness is the player's visible defensive progress. It represents protected communications, guarded industry, hardened transport, counterintelligence, contingency mobilization, and plans for a coalition war.

Preparedness reduces sabotage impact, slows pact readiness, delays some members at reveal, improves the opening defense, and carries into the coalition war. It cannot reveal members by itself.

## Visibility contract

The event must preserve mystery without becoming unreadable.

Before Evolution II, the player can see incidents and temporary consequences. The player cannot see a pact meter, member list, suspect list, recruitment count, or faction name.

At Evolution II, the player gains a response category built around **foreign interference**, not a title that reveals the Secret Alliance name. The player sees Evidence and Preparedness, a small suspect list, confidence bands, and a broad estimate of coordination. The player still cannot see every member.

At Evolution III, the pact becomes public and an actual faction appears. Known members become visible. Any active sympathizers that cannot legally join the faction are shown as exposed supporters rather than counted as faction members.

The Event Logs catalogue must use scripted localisation so Event 011 does not spoil itself. The development and spreadsheet name remains Secret Alliance. The player-facing event name and detail title use a concealed direction before reveal, a coordinated-interference direction at Evolution II, and the real pact identity after reveal.

Evolution previews must follow the same rule. Locked or unreached evolution entries use spoiler-safe descriptions. The event log must not announce that a major country can join, that a second major exists, or that the final result is an anti-target faction before the player reaches those states.

## Ordinary baseline stages

These stages describe the normal life of the event. They are not evolutions and should not be recorded as evolution entries.

### Founding

Three founders are selected, motives are assigned, a prospective leader is chosen, and the first operational doctrine is established. The player sees at most one low-information incident.

### Quiet coordination

The founders exchange representatives, test communications, identify target vulnerabilities, and look for possible recruits. Player-facing consequences remain small and widely spaced.

### Interference

The pact begins low-risk operations. Similar methods appear across several countries. The player can notice a pattern, but no formal response category appears in the ordinary baseline.

### Strategic preparation

Recruitment, arms arrangements, staff contacts, and military access planning become more important. A calm-world baseline may remain in this stage for a long time without producing open conflict.

### Reveal pressure

Exposure, readiness, war state, and evolution rules begin to compete. The pact may choose a controlled public reveal, suffer an accidental reveal, fracture, or enter war through one member's actions.

## Strategic doctrines

The pact chooses one hidden doctrine at founding. The doctrine is a behavior plan, not a permanent outcome.

| Doctrine | Core intention | Typical operations | Reveal tendency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Containment front | stop further target expansion | guarantees, intelligence sharing, border defense, diplomatic isolation | later reveal, defensive public case |
| Punitive coalition | weaken or defeat the target | sabotage, military planning, arms caches, provocations | readiness-driven reveal |
| Regime pressure | force political change inside the target | propaganda, contacts, assassinations, elite disruption | exposure-driven reveal |
| Spoils compact | divide expected gains among members | recruitment promises, border incidents, opportunistic pressure | unstable reveal, high fracture risk |

The doctrine can change after a major sponsor joins, after a failed operation, or after a member dispute. A doctrine change should be rare and should create a visible shift in incident type without naming the pact.
