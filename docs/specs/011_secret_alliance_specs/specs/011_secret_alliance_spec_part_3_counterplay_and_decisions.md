# Event 011 Secret Alliance

## Part 3: Player counterplay, decisions, missions, and mechanic UI

## The response system

The response category appears in Evolution II after a coordinated incident gives the target a credible reason to act. The public category direction should concern foreign interference, unexplained coordination, or national protection. It must not use the Secret Alliance name before reveal.

The response system has two visible values:

- **Evidence**, which improves identification and makes disclosure safer
- **Preparedness**, which protects the country and weakens the pact's opening war

A broad coordination estimate and up to three suspect cards supplement the values. The system should avoid showing a complete country list or every possible decision.

The category closes or transforms after the pact ends. Obsolete missions, suspect flags, target selections, and temporary state are cleaned up.

## Suspect confidence

Suspects are generated by real clues, failed pact operations, diplomatic patterns, and disinformation. Each suspect has a confidence band.

| Confidence | Player meaning | Allowed actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trace | one weak indicator | observe, cross-check, no public action |
| Plausible | several related indicators | targeted investigation, quiet diplomatic contact |
| Credible | strong operational connection | border preparation, controlled leak, private accusation |
| Confirmed | direct evidence or confession | public disclosure, demand withdrawal, preemptive action at Evolution III |

The pact can plant evidence against an innocent country. A suspect card is therefore an assessment, not a guarantee.

A maximum of three suspect cards should be visible at once. The player can close a weak lead to make room for another. AI pact logic can continue evaluating all countries without using the human-facing selector.

## Investigation decisions

### Trace courier routes

The player commits security personnel, transport access, and time to follow recurring couriers or liaison travel.

Costs can include a civilian factory burden, trains or trucks, and temporary intelligence exposure. Success adds Evidence and may create a suspect. Failure alerts the pact and shortens its next operation delay.

### Audit front organizations

The player investigates unusual money, associations, trade intermediaries, or charities linked to foreign influence.

Costs can include political power, civilian factory capacity, and a temporary reduction in trade efficiency. Success can expose a recruitment route or reduce pact cohesion. A heavy-handed failure can reduce stability and make innocent foreign partners hostile.

### Cross-check attaché movements

The player compares military, naval, and air liaison travel.

Costs should use the relevant service experience when appropriate, plus administrative capacity. Success improves confidence against a military-planning suspect and can reduce pact readiness.

### Decode repeated traffic

The player directs communications and cryptographic resources toward a recurring signature.

The decision should use intelligence capability when available and a non-agency state-security path when it is not. These are supported variants, not a hidden weaker substitute.

Success adds significant Evidence. Failure can feed the player prepared false traffic.

### Seize a compromised courier

This mission requires a credible suspect or a recent operation. It creates a timed objective with partial success.

- full success captures material and identifies a member
- partial success disrupts the route but yields incomplete evidence
- failure causes the courier to disappear and raises pact alertness

### Compare sabotage signatures

The player commits industrial inspectors and military engineers to identify common methods across damaged sites.

Costs can include support equipment, army experience, and temporary factory repair burden. Success protects future sites and adds Evidence.

## Protection decisions

### Compartmentalize staff plans

The player changes access to mobilization and deployment information.

It costs command attention and slows planning for a period. It reduces intelligence-penetration success and pact readiness gain.

### Guard critical industry

The player assigns equipment, manpower, and local units to vulnerable factories.

The action should target an industrial region or the currently threatened site family. It costs infantry equipment, support equipment, manpower, and a temporary production burden. It reduces sabotage damage and can capture operatives.

### Secure rail and port nodes

The player protects named supply routes, ports, depots, or rail junctions.

The mission should require actual control and supply access. It can ask the player to station supplied divisions in named regions for at least a normal mission duration. Success increases Preparedness and protects logistics at reveal.

### Harden communications

The player replaces compromised procedures and distributes secure equipment.

Costs can include support equipment, trucks, civilian factories, and temporary command disruption. Success reduces false orders, stolen plans, and coordinated propaganda effects.

### Protect officials and specialists

The player protects ministers, commanders, engineers, and technical experts after credible threats.

The action ties down manpower and security resources. It prevents or reduces assassination outcomes and can create a captured-agent incident.

### Emergency dispersal plan

The player prepares alternative stockpiles, airfields, headquarters, and transport routes.

This is expensive and becomes strongest at Evolution III. It raises Preparedness, reduces the first-war opening penalties, and limits the effect of one successful infrastructure operation.

## Diplomatic decisions

### Quietly approach a suspect

The player sends an envoy or intermediary to a plausible or credible suspect.

The outcome depends on confidence, the suspect's motive, relations, pact cohesion, and whether the pact planted the clue.

Possible results include denial, a warning, a demand for concessions, a private withdrawal, a leak to the pact, or a double-agent opportunity.

### Offer withdrawal terms

A credible or confirmed member can be offered a narrow settlement connected to its motive.

A grievance member may seek a border commission. A fear member may seek a guarantee or noninterference pledge. A patronage member may need protection from the sponsor. An opportunist may demand concessions that the player should hesitate to grant.

The decision should never make every member buyable. Strong ideological members and committed sponsors may refuse all terms.

### Request friendly intelligence

The player asks an ally, friendly major, or neighboring service to share relevant information.

Costs include diplomatic favors, equipment aid, access, or future obligations rather than political power alone. A friendly country can provide a clue, protect a route, introduce false information, or become a new target for pact pressure.

### Turn a member

This is a high-evidence action against a member with a suitable motive or dispute.

A turned member can expose an operation, delay mobilization, refuse the call to arms, reveal one founder, or feed false plans. The player must decide whether to use the asset immediately or preserve it for reveal.

### Present a private dossier

The player confronts one government with compiled evidence without going public.

A correct dossier can force withdrawal or reduce cohesion. A weak or incorrect dossier damages credibility and improves pact recruitment.

### Convene a defensive consultation

The player invites friendly states to discuss the pattern without naming unconfirmed members.

This can increase Preparedness, provide limited intelligence, and discourage recruitment. It can also convince fearful pact members that the target is building a counter-bloc, which raises cohesion if handled aggressively.

## Deception and disruption decisions

### Feed controlled plans

The player gives a suspected channel false military or industrial information.

Success reduces pact readiness, misdirects an operation, or creates a delayed member at reveal. Failure gives the pact real information hidden inside the false package.

### Stage a controlled leak

The player releases selected evidence to provoke member reactions and identify communication links.

This raises public attention. It can expose a member, accelerate reveal, or harm an innocent suspect. It should be strongest when Evidence is already high.

### Interdict an arms corridor

A credible neighboring or maritime suspect can be targeted through a limited interdiction mission.

Costs include fuel, convoys, naval or air experience, and diplomatic risk. Success removes pact readiness and may reveal a supplier. Failure can cause casualties, condemnation, or an early war.

### Raid a covert depot

A confirmed or near-confirmed neighboring member can be targeted through a limited border action.

This is a border conflict or covert raid, not a full declaration of war. It consumes equipment, manpower, command power, and local unit commitment. Success weakens the member's reveal package. Escalation into normal war triggers immediate pact reveal.

### Broadcast a false fracture

The player creates the impression that one member has betrayed the others.

This is a late Evolution II action. Success lowers cohesion and may trigger a dispute. Failure unifies the members and raises readiness.

## Public and military decisions at Evolution III

### Publish the evidence

The player publicly identifies confirmed members and describes the coordination.

High Evidence can force a reveal on weak pact terms, cause withdrawals, and improve neutral opinion. Weak evidence turns the publication into a diplomatic disaster and strengthens the coalition's justification.

### Demand public guarantees

The player offers suspected states a final chance to deny hostile intent through verifiable commitments.

Fearful or doubtful members may withdraw. Committed members use the demand to coordinate their final response.

### Preempt the coalition

The direct war option becomes available at Evolution III against confirmed members or the revealed faction.

Preemption should carry a legitimacy and diplomatic cost that scales with Evidence. A well-supported preemption can catch the pact before full readiness. A weakly supported attack can cause neutral countries to join or support the coalition.

### General counter-mobilization

The player commits manpower, equipment, trains, fuel, and command attention to the coalition front.

The decision converts Preparedness into a stronger opening posture. It should not create free divisions. It may improve mobilization, supply, planning, entrenchment, and call allied support through temporary or staged effects.

### Open a fracture conference

The player invites doubtful members to separate talks under a short deadline.

The conference can remove members, delay war, split the faction, or fail publicly. It is most effective when cohesion is low and member motives differ.

## Mission design

Important missions should require action rather than passive thresholds.

Examples include:

- hold and supply named rail and port regions for a protection period
- place a required number of supplied divisions along a confirmed suspect border
- keep a courier route under surveillance while maintaining civilian transport capacity
- protect an industrial region through a sabotage window
- maintain a friendly intelligence arrangement without losing the partner to pact pressure
- identify two matching operation signatures before a deadline
- keep a turned member undiscovered until reveal
- prevent the pact from completing a major sponsor recruitment window

Normal missions should generally use ninety to one hundred eighty day bands. Emergency Evolution III missions can be shorter when the player has a clear reason and enough agency to act.

Every mission should have distinct success, partial success, and failure where the objective supports it.

## Cost palette

The response system should use varied resources:

- infantry and support equipment for guards and raids
- trucks and trains for transport security and dispersal
- fuel and convoys for maritime or air interdiction
- manpower and temporary unit commitments for border and infrastructure protection
- army, navy, or air experience for service-specific analysis and action
- civilian factory burden for audits, repairs, secure communications, and dispersal
- stability and diplomatic credibility for public accusations
- command power at conservative levels for emergency military coordination
- intelligence capability and operative risk when available
- time and opportunity cost through missions and cooldowns

No major decision family should become a political-power store.

## Wrong-country consequences

False clues and player overreach are essential to the event.

A wrong accusation can:

- damage relations and trade
- reduce the target's diplomatic credibility
- raise pact recruitment willingness
- create a new fearful member
- lower Preparedness through wasted resources
- make later true evidence harder to sell
- trigger a neutral country's defensive guarantee against the target

The system must avoid trapping the player through pure randomness. Confidence rises from evidence quality, and high-risk actions clearly show their uncertainty.

## Idea and modifier lifecycle

The target should use a small number of deep staged ideas or dynamic modifiers.

| Stage | Target state | Role | Exit or transformation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | no visible pact idea | preserve uncertainty | Evolution II discovery |
| Evolution II | foreign interference pressure | represents active disruption and security burden | improved by protection, removed at reveal |
| Counterplay | prepared countermeasures | stores meaningful protection and mission progress | converts into wartime preparedness or post-crisis security |
| Evolution III | coalition under arms | represents public military pressure | removed by war resolution or fracture |
| After successful prevention | exposed foreign network | short aftermath benefit and diplomatic leverage | expires after cleanup |

Pact members can use hidden or member-only ideas with the same lifecycle:

- secret commitments
- compromised network
- coalition readiness
- fractured commitments
- coalition resolve after reveal

These should be staged forms of a few institutions, not a separate spirit for every incident.

## Mechanic UI

The response category should use an attached compact scripted GUI rather than a large separate window.

The GUI should show:

- Evidence bar
- Preparedness bar
- broad coordination estimate
- up to three suspect cards
- confidence on each card
- selected suspect details
- a small list of active missions
- an Evolution III war-readiness estimate when public escalation begins

The GUI should not display hidden pact size, hidden motives, exact cohesion, exact readiness, or unconfirmed member names.

### Animation decision

Most of the interface should remain static because constant motion would make uncertainty harder to read.

One state-driven animation is justified. At high Evolution III danger, the category header uses an animated inward-closing network seal or warning border. The animation communicates that the coalition is entering its final public phase.

The asset requires real source frames, a horizontal frame sheet, a static fallback, and a review GIF. A transform-only pulse is not acceptable. The animation should stop when the pact fractures, war begins, or the category closes.
