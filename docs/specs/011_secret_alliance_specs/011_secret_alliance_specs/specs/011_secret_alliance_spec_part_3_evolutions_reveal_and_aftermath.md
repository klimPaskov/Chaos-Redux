# 011 Secret Alliance, part 3, evolutions, reveal, and aftermath

## Evolution design rule

The baseline hidden pact is not an evolution. Baseline covers the ordinary lifecycle from first hidden meetings through subtle incidents. Evolutions change the scale, actor type, and public danger of the same event.

Each evolution has two entry paths where relevant:

- active-event evolution: the event has already fired, and existing hidden pact actors receive new behavior
- pre-fire evolved opening: the event has not fired yet, and the first firing starts in a stronger form because the world is already chaotic enough

The event has three planned evolutions, matching the user prompt. The unused higher evolution slots should not be filled with thin content just to use them.

## Baseline stage

Baseline creates three hidden minor signatories. It begins slowly and should feel deniable.

Baseline member package:

- three full signatory minors
- one Convener role among them
- at least one operational role, usually Saboteur, Agitator, Border Hand, or Banker
- hidden pact cohesion and readiness values
- no public faction
- no player decision category at first unless evidence rises unusually fast
- scattered incidents with low direct damage
- slow invitation checks for liaisons and sympathizers

Baseline incident pacing:

- first signs are mostly reports and low damage
- sabotage should be rare and small
- evidence gains are modest but persistent
- countries with no path to the player can still run propaganda, money, and diplomatic incidents
- neighbors are more likely to cause border unease

Baseline exit paths:

- ignore and drift toward Evolution I or II later
- investigate enough to open the dossier early
- accidentally trigger partial reveal through border clash or war
- expose a member early if evidence is high
- fail to control pressure, which helps the pact grow

## Evolution I, expanded minor network

Evolution I adds more minor countries and makes the hidden network more noticeable. It should still avoid direct war and avoid public faction formation.

Active-event evolution:

- invite two to five additional minor countries as liaisons, armed associates, or full signatories based on cohesion and readiness
- widen propaganda and sabotage incident pools
- add more meeting reports in neutral or small capitals
- raise invitation chance for countries with poor relations toward the player
- make evidence easier to gather because more people know the secret
- give the pact a higher chance to accidentally expose members

Pre-fire evolved opening:

- the event starts with three full minor signatories and two to four outer-ring minors
- early incidents can mention that the pattern already crosses several capitals
- the first sabotage incident can happen sooner, but still not as a full reveal
- the player can reach the dossier phase faster than baseline

New player-facing content:

- more recurring report events
- first suspect naming if Evidence is high enough
- small decision category unlock if the player has unusually high evidence or the pact makes a major mistake
- more diplomatic pressure from third countries that echo the pact line

Containment and counterplay:

- investigations can peel off liaisons
- careful diplomacy can keep outer-ring countries from full membership
- hard sweeps can reveal one member but risk player isolation

AI behavior:

- AI pact members should prefer low-risk sabotage and propaganda
- minor members with low stability should leak more evidence
- nearby minors should be more aggressive than distant minors
- minors in factions should be reluctant unless their faction leader is also aligned or the event later allows it

## Evolution II, major patron phase

Evolution II is the main playable escalation. One major can join the pact. If the event first fires at Evolution II conditions, the pact may be founded by a major patron that recruits minors.

Active-event evolution:

- select one major or strong regional power as Patron if a valid candidate exists
- if no valid major exists, select a strong regional minor or coalition financier and mark the evolution as major-patron equivalent for mechanics, not for localisation
- major patron increases membership ceiling
- major patron improves sabotage quality, funding, and propaganda reach
- player decision category appears if it did not already appear
- active sabotage becomes more serious and easier to connect to foreign support
- threats, murders, industrial damage, and border provocations become possible

Pre-fire evolved opening:

- the founding member can be a major patron
- two or three minors join as full signatories or armed associates
- the pact starts more organized
- the player receives earlier signs of major-backed pressure
- the dossier category appears after the first serious incident rather than after a long baseline

Member rules:

- major patron should not be at war with the player at selection time
- major patron should not be in a faction that makes hidden anti-player work nonsensical unless its faction politics support the move
- major patron should have ideological rivalry, strategic rivalry, geographic interest, or fear of player expansion
- major patron should not be scripted into another special crisis that would make this event incoherent

New incident types:

- coordinated industrial sabotage
- officer assassination attempt
- armed provocations in border states
- public threats through proxies
- false intelligence leaks
- pressure on the player's trade partners
- funding of exiled opposition or hostile parties
- military mission sightings in minor pact countries
- deniable attacks on rail hubs or ports

Player decisions unlocked:

- full dossier category
- investigations with named suspects
- counter-sabotage missions
- neutral inquiry
- secret bargaining with Convener
- quiet demarche to major patron if identified
- border watch and controlled border incident when geography allows
- public accusation with dossier

Preparedness outcome:

Player decisions in Evolution II should shape the revealed war. High Preparedness and Evidence should make the pact weaker at reveal. Low Preparedness should let the pact start with stronger cohesion, better war readiness, and more outer-ring converts.

Possible player achievements are tied to this phase because it rewards careful play rather than waiting for the public faction.

## Evolution III, public pact and war pressure

Evolution III makes the pact visible enough that the player can see it as a map and faction problem. The faction appears directly on the map. War is very likely, but the stage should still give a short final window before full war unless a direct reveal trigger has already occurred.

Active-event evolution:

- hidden full signatories become public pact members
- the faction name becomes Anti-[player country] Pact
- outer-ring members decide whether to join, withdraw, or stay sympathetic based on cohesion, Evidence, isolation, and player actions
- a second major can join if conditions allow and prior player decisions failed to weaken the network
- player receives a war option
- War Clock replaces hidden readiness as the visible pressure value
- aggressive actions continue but now look like public mobilization, ultimatums, border incidents, and war councils

Pre-fire evolved opening:

- if the event first fires under Evolution III conditions, it should begin from the Evolution II package with a major patron, early serious incidents, and the decision category
- a short timer, pressure sequence, or report chain moves it into Evolution III after the player has had a chance to read the system
- it should not skip directly from no knowledge to immediate faction war unless another event or direct war trigger forces reveal

Public faction behavior:

- the faction appears openly
- pact members can coordinate guarantees, war goals, volunteers, and preparation decisions
- all full signatories should be expected to join war if the pact war begins
- armed associates may join based on confidence
- liaisons usually withdraw unless cohesion is high or player isolation is severe
- a major patron can become faction leader if present
- if two majors are present, leadership can be contested, which weakens pact cohesion but increases danger

Player options in Evolution III:

- expose full dossier to isolate pact members
- offer last exits to minor members
- demand public withdrawal from suspects
- prepare first-strike war planning
- launch preemptive war if rules allow
- fortify border and supply lines
- ask neutral observers to certify evidence
- attempt to split major patron from minor members

War likelihood:

War Clock should grow faster when:

- pact cohesion is high
- pact has one or more majors
- player preparedness is low
- player is already at war elsewhere
- border incidents succeeded
- the player used premature threats with low evidence
- chaos tier is high

War Clock should slow when:

- Evidence is strong and public
- pact cohesion is low
- minor members defect
- player preparedness is high
- major patron has other wars
- neutral inquiry succeeded
- player made credible concessions or withdrawal offers

## Reveal triggers

The pact should reveal when secrecy can no longer hold.

Hard reveal triggers:

- any full signatory goes to war with the player
- any player war declaration on a confirmed full signatory after the pact is sufficiently exposed
- Evolution III public pact transition
- major patron publicly guarantees a member against the player after high Evidence
- a border incident escalates into formal war
- a pact member calls other signatories into an anti-player war

Soft reveal triggers:

- player exposes a full dossier with high Evidence
- neutral inquiry certifies the pact
- a member defects publicly
- a captured courier names the whole pact
- a major patron fails to deny military mission documents

Soft reveal can create a partial public state before formal faction war. The pact may become visible, but war can still wait for War Clock or player action.

## Super-event role

The reveal super-event marks the transformation from invisible conspiracy to public faction. It should not use final text, quote, or audio until the super-event research workflow is complete.

Super-event role:

- public reveal of the Anti-[player country] Pact
- all earlier incidents become legible in hindsight
- the world now sees a bloc formed around opposition to the player
- the player should feel surrounded rather than merely attacked
- the tone should be ominous, diplomatic, and military at once

Image direction:

- generated or sourced depending on final art decision
- strong central composition around sealed conference, shadowed delegates, maps as secondary props, and national emblems without readable generated text
- should avoid a generic map-table image as the main subject
- should convey a coordinated foreign circle closing around the player

Quote direction:

- research required
- themes: secret treaties, hidden enemies, false peace, alliances, betrayal, encirclement, diplomacy becoming war
- prefer public domain, historical, philosophical, or diplomatic sources with verified wording

Cultural remark direction:

- research required
- short, grim, possibly ironic line about friends, knives, pacts, encirclement, or the circle becoming visible
- avoid comedy that makes the threat trivial

Audio mood:

- research required
- tense orchestral, chamber, martial, or diplomatic dread
- must be actual licensed or public domain music, unique to this super-event unless reuse is explicitly approved

The super-event prompt file carries the full research handoff.

## War reveal package

When the pact reveals through war:

- form the public faction immediately
- full signatories join the war against the player
- major patron joins if it is a full signatory or leader
- armed associates evaluate joining based on confidence and player isolation
- liaisons mostly withdraw or become diplomatic supporters
- player receives immediate effects based on Preparedness
- pact receives immediate effects based on Cohesion and Readiness
- hidden sabotage incidents stop or convert into public war actions
- decision category switches from hidden dossier to public confrontation and war support

Player preparedness conversion examples:

- high Preparedness: reduced surprise penalty, temporary border defense, better supply protection, lower sabotage continuation
- medium Preparedness: smaller shock, normal defense tools
- low Preparedness: surprise mobilization penalty, temporary supply vulnerability, panic or war-support hit

Pact readiness conversion examples:

- high Readiness: strong first months, more coordinated attacks, better logistics support among members
- low Readiness: faction forms but members suffer coordination penalties and slower war entry

## Revealed war decisions

After reveal, the player should still use the system. It should not vanish the moment war starts.

Possible revealed-war decision families:

- exploit exposed split between members
- appeal to outer-ring neutrals
- publish captured pact plans
- strike coordination offices
- defend suspected sabotage corridors
- negotiate separate exit with weak minor member
- pressure major patron's rivals for support

If the player defeats a pact member, the system should check whether the remaining pact cohesion collapses. A defeated Convener or Patron should create bigger cohesion loss than defeating a marginal member.

## Pact collapse and peace outcomes

Pact collapse should be possible through war, diplomacy, or exposure.

Collapse conditions:

- faction leader capitulates or exits
- Convener and Patron are defeated or exposed
- Pact Cohion falls below a critical threshold after public proof
- enough minor members sign separate exits
- player wins the main anti-pact war
- pact members are no longer able to maintain a common front

After collapse:

- remove hidden pact mechanics
- clear active hidden incident timers
- keep historical memory for achievements and event logs
- allow short aftermath reports
- apply postwar modifiers based on player conduct and evidence strength
- avoid leaving invisible old members with hidden war logic

Peace outcomes:

- decisive player victory with high Evidence should isolate surviving pact governments
- player victory with low Evidence can look like conquest against fearful states, raising diplomatic risk
- negotiated settlement can dissolve the faction but leave some members hostile
- pact victory or player defeat can create a forced concession, demilitarized border, puppet pressure, or hostile diplomatic order depending on implementation scope

## Interaction with other Chaos Redux systems

Secret Alliance can read the wider world without hijacking it.

Good connections:

- chaos tier affects invitation pace, sabotage severity, and evolution eligibility
- world tension affects faction justification and neutral willingness
- condemnation or atrocity systems can make the player easier to isolate
- event log should show evolutions and details cleanly
- existing faction systems should be respected when selecting members
- if world threat framework exists, a revealed anti-player pact should not automatically become a world-threat source unless the player or pact becomes existential at a separate threshold

Cross-event restraint:

- do not recruit special nonhuman countries into this ordinary diplomatic pact
- do not make the pact override major crisis actors that need their own route
- do not force neutral countries into war if their ring status and evidence outcome say they should withdraw

## Hidden to public memory

The event should remember hidden-phase results. The revealed faction is stronger or weaker based on what happened earlier.

The reveal should calculate:

- number of full signatories
- number of outer-ring countries that convert to full members
- Evidence strength
- player Preparedness
- player isolation
- pact cohesion
- pact readiness
- major patron presence
- border member presence
- player prior decisions, especially reckless accusations and successful negotiated exits

This memory is what makes the hidden phase worth playing.
