# Research notes for Secret Alliance

These notes support the event's design direction. They are not player-facing localisation and should not be pasted into event text.

## Anti-Comintern Pact as a design analogue

A useful historical analogue is the Anti-Comintern Pact. It began as a public anti-communist arrangement between Germany and Japan in 1936 and carried a secret part in which each country promised not to aid the Soviet Union if the other was attacked by Stalin. This is useful because Secret Alliance needs the feeling of visible ideological theatre hiding a more dangerous operational commitment.

Design use:

- The public layer can appear as ordinary conferences, defensive speeches, press delegations, and shared rhetoric.
- The hidden layer stores the real anti-player commitments.
- A revealed pact can be framed as the moment that the private commitment becomes an open war bloc.

## Covert action and sabotage analogue

The British Special Operations Executive is a useful covert-action analogue. It was created in 1940 to conduct secret war through sabotage and subversion behind enemy lines, including attacks on trains, bridges, factories, and support to resistance movements.

Design use:

- Early sabotage should hit railways, dockyards, factories, intelligence networks, officers, and convoys rather than only stability.
- The player should be able to counter the pact by protecting industrial nodes, securing courier routes, and raising counterintelligence readiness.
- The pact's evidence trail can be built from forged papers, radios, explosives, courier routes, and suspicious safe houses.

## Tacit triangles and non-treaty alignment

A 1937 U.S. diplomatic dispatch described an Italo-German-Japanese anti-communist triangle and a tacit German-Japanese understanding even without a formal German-Italian agreement. This supports a Secret Alliance design where not every member must be bound by a formal treaty from the start. Some members can be aligned by tacit understandings, shared fear, military missions, or patronage.

Design use:

- Pact members can enter as `sympathizers`, `liaisons`, `outer ring`, `armed associates`, or `full signatories`.
- Minors can join quietly before the player can identify a treaty.
- Major involvement in Evolution II can feel like a patron network rather than a normal faction invite.

## Small-state defensive pacts as contrast

Small powers have historically used mutual diplomatic coordination to defend territorial integrity. This helps keep the event from becoming a generic evil conspiracy. Some pact members should believe they are joining a defensive arrangement against an overmighty player.

Design use:

- Democratic and neutral members may describe the pact internally as self-preservation.
- Authoritarian members may use the pact to justify repression and covert attack.
- A player's aggressive history, world tension contribution, faction leadership, conquest, or nearby troop massing should raise eligibility and pact willingness.

## Research boundaries for implementation

The super-event prompt must research final quote, cultural remark, and audio through the super-event workflow. This research note does not provide final super-event wording, quote text, button text, or audio choice.

The implementation should use these historical references as inspiration, not as a claim that the fictional pact is a direct reenactment of any one treaty.
