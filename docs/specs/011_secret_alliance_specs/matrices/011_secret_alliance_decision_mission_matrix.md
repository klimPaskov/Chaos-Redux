# Event 011 Secret Alliance decision and mission matrix

All names in this matrix are working labels, not final localisation. Costs are design bands. Implementation should calculate them from target scale, current pressure, prior use, and available resources.

## Category lifecycle

| Phase | Player-facing identity | Visible systems | Decision volume | Closure condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | No dedicated category | Ordinary event responses only | None | Evolution II or rare forced exposure |
| Evolution II opening | Foreign interference response | Evidence, Preparedness, suspects, recent incidents | 5 to 8 relevant actions | Reveal, collapse, or temporary quiet period |
| Evolution II sustained | Counter-network campaign | Investigation, protection, diplomacy, deception, border actions | 7 to 11, with active mission cap | Evolution III, reveal, or collapse |
| Evolution III | Coalition crisis | War pressure, known members, Preparedness, emergency actions | 6 to 10 | War, settlement, or pact collapse |
| Revealed war | Anti-coalition campaign | Coalition Resolve, known members, exposed plans | 5 to 9 | Target war ends |
| Aftermath | Settlement and cleanup | Confirmed responsibility and settlement choices | 2 to 5 | Final settlement recorded |

## Border conflict family

Border actions exist only against a neighboring pact member or high-confidence suspect. They are not ordinary war until escalation.

| Step | Player action | Pact response | Success result | Failure result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Patrol reinforcement | Place supplied divisions in named border states | Increase local surveillance or move routes | Preparedness and local defense rise | Other fronts lose readiness |
| Intercept survey team | Launch a limited operation against mapped routes | Deny, retaliate, or expose patrol | Evidence and route denial | Casualties and border tension |
| Secure contested crossing | Hold a named crossing for a deadline | Probe with irregulars or local troops | Depot and access plan disrupted | Pact gains a staging route |
| Limited border conflict | Accept controlled military risk | Member commits local forces | Member readiness falls and target gains confidence | Conflict escalates, recruitment rises |
| Escalation decision | Continue, negotiate, or withdraw | Pact chooses solidarity or distance | Can trigger fractured reveal or separate settlement | Can become normal hostile war and force immediate reveal |

## False accusation safeguards

- Suspects must use confidence bands rather than binary membership until confirmed.
- A decision that targets a suspect must show the confidence band and visible basis.
- Innocent countries can react through relations, counter-propaganda, faction choices, or even later pact recruitment.
- One mistaken low-confidence probe should be recoverable.
- Repeated accusations without new Evidence should damage credibility and increase recruitment attraction.
- A public dossier needs a stronger threshold than a quiet probe.
- An accused human country receives a choice and cannot be silently scripted into guilt.

## Active mission cap and clutter control

The category should normally show no more than:

- two active investigation missions
- two active protection projects
- one diplomatic probe against the selected suspect
- one offensive counter-network action
- one border mission when geography allows it
- two emergency actions during Evolution III

Completed, obsolete, invalid, or route-incompatible decisions close cleanly. Suspect-targeted actions use a selected-target flow so the category does not list every country at once.

## Cost principles

- Military protection consumes equipment, units, XP, fuel, trains, or command capacity.
- Intelligence operations consume agency capacity, time, diplomatic access, and exposure risk.
- Public actions consume credibility, stability, relations, and political attention.
- Diplomatic settlements require concrete guarantees, access, claims arrangements, or aid.
- Repeated emergency preparation creates economic and military opportunity cost.
- No major family should be a tray of political-power purchases.

## Success, partial success, and failure memory

Every important mission records an outcome band. Later operations can read that memory.

- Full success can identify a member, turn a channel, remove a network, or reveal a plan.
- Partial success can protect the target while losing the source, or gain Evidence without identifying a member.
- Failure can raise pact alertness, strengthen cohesion, create a false suspect, kill a source, or accelerate the next operation.
- Repeated success shifts the pact toward false traffic, recruitment, and indirect methods.
- Repeated failure shifts the player toward more expensive emergency protection and a harder reveal.
