## Baseline stage map

Baseline stages are ordinary progression. They must not be logged as evolution stages.

### Stage A: Founding conference

**Hidden setup**

- Fix the target country.
- Select three valid minor founders.
- Assign each founder a primary motive.
- Choose one hidden pact doctrine from Containment Front, Punitive Coalition, Regime Pressure, or Spoils Compact.
- Establish low starting cohesion and readiness bands based on motive compatibility.
- Seed a first liaison route and a first operational vulnerability.

**Target experience**

The target receives no coalition notice. The first visible incident should be plausible as a local failure or isolated foreign operation.

**Exit**

The pact completes one successful operation or survives one failed operation without collapsing.

### Stage B: Quiet coordination

**Hidden activity**

- Members exchange reports.
- One operation family receives priority.
- A recruitment approach can begin.
- Conflicting motives can create a private dispute.

**Target experience**

The target sees incidents with weak links, such as repeated methods, overlapping travel, or common intermediaries.

**Exit**

Readiness, operation count, or time moves the pact into sustained interference.

### Stage C: Sustained interference

**Hidden activity**

- Operation frequency rises.
- Recruitment becomes more likely.
- The pact begins preparing transport, access, and wartime assumptions.

**Target experience**

A pattern is noticeable, but the event still avoids a public category that names a coalition.

**Exit**

Evolution I, Evolution II, a premature exposure event, pact collapse, or hostile war reveal.

### Stage D: Strategic preparation

This is a late baseline state for campaigns where evolutions remain disabled or delayed.

**Hidden activity**

- The pact stores plans and access.
- Members discuss coordinated guarantees and possible war entry.
- Recruitment can continue at a conservative rate.

**Target experience**

Incidents become harder to dismiss. The player can receive broad defensive choices through ordinary events, but the full investigation category remains reserved for Evolution II.

**Exit**

Hostile war reveal, player-forced exposure through rare evidence, pact collapse, or an enabled evolution.

## Recruitment chain

### Candidate screening

A candidate enters the pool only when it remains valid for eventual reveal. The score considers motive, reach, risk, faction status, target relationship, existing wars, ideology, claims, and sponsor pressure.

### Invitation outcomes

| Outcome | Conditions | Hidden pact result | Target result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accept | Candidate sees security or gain | Member added, cohesion shifts by motive fit | No immediate disclosure |
| Conditional accept | Candidate demands territory, aid, guarantee, or leadership | Member added with obligation and future dispute | Possible clue through concession trail |
| Refuse quietly | Candidate sees little benefit | No member, small cohesion loss | Usually no notice |
| Leak to target | Good target relations or distrust of sponsor | Evidence gain, candidate becomes cooperative lead | Suspect and response opportunity |
| Feed a false answer | Candidate cooperates with target intelligence | Pact receives manipulated information | Turned channel becomes available |
| Expose publicly | High risk, public legalist behavior, or human choice | Cohesion loss and early exposure pressure | Possible forced reveal route |
| Counter-recruit | Candidate seeks to bring another country or sponsor | Network can expand unpredictably | More complex clue chain |

## Evolution entry map

### Evolution I: Wider private front

**Active-event entry**

- Apply to the existing pact after dynamic MTTH.
- Add a recruitment burst weighted toward nearby and factionless minors.
- Increase operation diversity and allow two coordinated incidents in one cycle when they affect different surfaces.
- Preserve secrecy.

**Pre-fire evolved opening**

- Select four to five minor founders rather than three.
- Start with one prior liaison success and a modest readiness band.
- Give the target a slightly earlier pattern incident without naming the pact.

**Failure and containment**

A pact with repeated recruitment failures can remain small. Evolution I changes potential and pacing, not a guaranteed member count.

### Evolution II: Organized hostile network

**Active-event entry**

- Evaluate one eligible major sponsor.
- Unlock lethal and permanent sabotage incidents at controlled frequency.
- Open the foreign-interference response category.
- Reveal Evidence and Preparedness values to the player.
- Create suspect cards and confidence bands.
- Allow counter-network missions, diplomatic probes, and border conflict preparations.

**Pre-fire evolved opening**

- A major country can become the founder and sponsor.
- Select two to four minor partners based on reach and motive.
- Begin with a sponsor-selected doctrine and one developed network.
- Open the response category shortly after the first serious incident.

**Failure and containment**

A sponsor can withdraw after exposure, strategic defeat elsewhere, or diplomatic concession. This can split the pact or return it to a minor-led form.

### Evolution III: Public coalition threshold

**Active-event entry**

- Evaluate another major only if the coalition remains strategically credible.
- Raise visible war pressure and expose the faction on the map.
- Begin a dynamic offensive countdown.
- Unlock player preemption, ultimatum, fracture, and emergency preparation actions.
- Permit pact-controlled reveal before war.

**Pre-fire evolved opening**

The event begins with the Evolution II major-led package. A shortened dynamic delay then advances it toward Evolution III. This preserves a playable investigation and preparation window rather than creating an instant unexplained faction.

**Failure and containment**

Low cohesion, a turned founder, major-sponsor withdrawal, or a successful public dossier can produce a fractured reveal or collapse before the offensive countdown completes.

## Reveal convergence map

All reveal routes should call one shared conceptual contract.

1. Refresh active-member validity.
2. Remove invalid, dead, absorbed, target-aligned, or impossible members.
3. Select the faction leader.
4. Create or reuse the event-owned faction identity.
5. Add every valid active member.
6. Convert hidden values into wartime state.
7. Apply turned-member and exposed-plan weaknesses.
8. Set public reveal state.
9. Fire the reveal super-event once.
10. If a hostile target war already exists, join every valid member immediately.
11. If reveal precedes war, start the offensive countdown and public coalition decisions.
12. Close hidden-only incidents and migrate the category to coalition-war presentation.

## Pact collapse before reveal

The hidden pact can end without a public coalition war when all of these pressures converge:

- fewer than two committed valid members remain
- cohesion falls below a sustained collapse threshold
- no sponsor can or will preserve the network
- the target has exposed or turned the remaining coordination channels
- no active member is already in a hostile target war

Collapse should produce a subdued target event, a history entry, cleanup of targets and variables, removal of temporary penalties, and diplomatic consequences for identified participants. It should not reveal members that the player never proved unless the collapse itself produces documentary evidence.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coalition victory | Target defeated or coerced, doctrine goals met | May survive as regional bloc | Spoils, security guarantees, or sponsor dominance |

| Negotiated settlement | High war cost, mixed fronts, defensive doctrine | Can become security forum or dissolve | Claims, access, guarantees, or neutrality terms |
| Sponsor collapse | Leader capitulates or leaves | Leadership contest or rapid fracture | Strong founder can inherit, others may exit |

## Event log and detail surfaces

- Event Details describe recurring foreign interference, concealed coordination, and the possibility of an organized hostile network without exposing member rules or hidden values.
- Baseline stages appear through history incidents, not the evolution log.
- Evolution I, II, and III each receive one evolution log identity with active and pre-fire entry paths sharing the same evolution stage.
- The reveal records a normal history event and the super-event presentation.
- A scenario launch records its scenario identity separately from the normal automatic firing history.
