# Event 011 Secret Alliance research notes

This event is fictional. The sources below are inspiration anchors for mechanics and tone, not direct claims that a specific historical pact appears in the event.

## Historical anchors

### Public treaty with hidden consequences

The German-Soviet Pact is useful as a research anchor because it had a public non-aggression face and a secret protocol that divided spheres of influence in eastern Europe. The design uses this split between visible diplomatic language and hidden operational meaning to shape the pact's reveal pattern.

Source: https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/german-soviet-pact

### Ideological anti-pact framing

The Anti-Comintern Pact is useful because it shows how a pact can publicly define itself through opposition to a target idea or enemy. The Secret Alliance adapts the structural idea of an anti-target label, but targets the player's country dynamically rather than a fixed ideology.

Source: https://avalon.law.yale.edu/wwii/tri1.asp
Source: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1937v01/d620

### Small-state defensive alliance pressure

The Little Entente is useful as a small-state security inspiration. It shows how several smaller countries can coordinate around fear of revisionism and restoration. The event adapts that logic into a hidden pact of minor countries that fear or resent the target.

Source: https://www.encyclopedia.com/reference/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/little-entente

### Covert sabotage and subversion

The Special Operations Executive gives a useful period anchor for sabotage, subversion, and assistance to resistance groups. The event uses this as inspiration for covert action families such as courier routes, industrial surveys, sabotage, and signal work.

Source: https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C153
Source: https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/special-operations-executive

## Design translation

| Research anchor | Translation into event design |
| --- | --- |
| Secret protocol behind a public pact | Hidden compact becomes public only after evidence, leak, ultimatum, or war |
| Anti-pact identity | Dynamic public faction name becomes `Anti-[target country] Pact` |
| Small-state defensive alliance | Baseline founding members are minors that fear the target |
| Covert sabotage organizations | Evolution II covert action families include sabotage, courier routes, and signal work |
| Patron-backed security systems | Evolution II can add a major patron that turns a minor conspiracy into a public crisis |

## Research caution

The event should not copy real treaties, real leaders, or real flags. Visuals should be fictional or symbolic unless an implementation pass deliberately chooses an archival source for a general historical mood image. Generated documentary-style assets are appropriate because the pact itself is fictional and dynamic.
