# Event 41 research bibliography

## Centers for Disease Control and Prevention

**About Bartonella quintana.** National Center for Emerging and Zoonotic Infectious Diseases. Updated 23 March 2026.

Used for body-louse transmission, crowding, hygiene, shared clothing and bedding, and the historical trench-fever association.

## U.S. Army Center of Military History

**Into the Fight, April to June 1918.** World War I commemorative campaign series.

Used for the scale of American Expeditionary Forces nonbattle deaths and the role of influenza in wartime military mortality.

## U.S. Army Center of Military History

**Ryukyus.** World War II campaign series.

Used for the reported Army nonbattle casualty total during the Okinawa campaign and the relationship between long operations, rain, logistics, and military burden.

## WW2 US Medical Research Centre

**Brief Overview of the Medical Department.**

Used for the four main medical service functions of evacuation, hospitalization, sanitation, and medical supply, plus the organization of medical detachments and mobile treatment.

This is a specialist historical research site and not an official government archive. Its organizational summary should be checked against installed or official references during implementation when exact historical wording matters.

## WW2 US Medical Research Centre

**1st Medical Battalion.** Unit history, Sicily section.

Used for the documented combination of flies, fleas, lice, mosquitoes, dysentery, malaria, preventive measures, and the claim that malaria cases exceeded battle casualties during part of the campaign.

This source is used as a design anchor. Exact quantitative claims should not become fixed game values.

## Zemke, Juliana N., Jose L. Sanchez, Junxiong Pang, and Gregory C. Gray

**The Double-Edged Sword of Military Response to Societal Disruptions: A Systematic Review of the Evidence for Military Personnel as Pathogen Transmitters.** Journal of Infectious Diseases, volume 220, issue 12, 2019, pages 1873 to 1884. DOI: 10.1093/infdis/jiz400.

Used for military movement as a route for transporting pathogens into and out of deployment areas, and for the importance of preventive medicine and surveillance.

## Barsoumian, Alice E., and colleagues

**Status Update on Infection Prevention and Control at Deployed Medical Treatment Facilities.** Military Medicine, volume 185, issues 3 to 4, 2020, pages 451 to 460. DOI: 10.1093/milmed/usz359.

Used for deployed infection-control practice, training, hand hygiene, monitoring, cohorting, accountability, and reassessment.

## Sanchez, Jose L., and colleagues

**Respiratory Infections in the U.S. Military: Recent Experience and Control.** Clinical Microbiology Reviews, volume 28, issue 3, 2015, pages 743 to 800. DOI: 10.1128/CMR.00039-14.

Used for the operational-readiness impact of respiratory infection in dense military settings and the role of surveillance and nonpharmacologic interventions.

## Research handling note

The specification uses these sources for design logic. Final event text should not state medical facts more narrowly than the sources support. The event remains a fictional adaptable field epidemic and should avoid claiming that every episode is one named disease.
