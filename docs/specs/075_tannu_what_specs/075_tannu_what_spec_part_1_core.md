## The country that everyone helped

Tannu Tuva asks the world to help it survive.
Its embassies describe exposed borders, unreliable supplies, underfunded workshops, and an army that cannot protect the country.
Each request is small enough for its recipient to regard it as a manageable favor.
The same government is making comparable requests in many capitals at once.

The player builds a military economy from those separate favors.
Rifles need recruits, recruits need officers, factories need transport, and tanks need fuel and maintenance.
A large delivery can solve one shortage while leaving another untouched.
The military strength that eventually appears must follow from this preparation.

The reveal ends the protective arrangements that made the operation possible.
Tannu Tuva fields its prepared forces, attacks every country across its current land borders, and receives the first **Tannu What?** super-event.
Its later expansion continually opens wars against new non-allied neighbors.
Conquering a continent at 1000 or more global Chaos can unlock a second super-event and a final campaign across the oceans.

## What the player does

Before the reveal, the main choices concern sponsors, the balance of aid, domestic investment, and the moment to commit the army.
The player can spread requests across many governments, rely more heavily on a few protectors, or spend longer building a domestic production base.
Every route can approach the wider world.
Their differences concern the terms, timing, and use of that assistance.

After the reveal, diplomacy has consequences on the map.
A former patron can become an immediate enemy.
A gifted truck fleet may carry troops toward its donor's capital.
A useful new border can create another war before the first front has been settled.
The player chooses operational priorities and the treatment of conquered countries while accepting the event's outward-war rule.

The world-end phase adds a further problem.
A continental army cannot cross an ocean through enthusiasm alone.
Tannu Tuva must turn continental resources into functioning shipyards, fleets, transport, air cover, ports, and overseas supply.

## Availability and ownership

The event belongs to the existing TAN country.
It requires TAN to exist, control a populated state in which a government can operate, and have independent authority to conduct the event's diplomacy.
An annexed, capitulated, or foreign-controlled puppet TAN does not receive an invented replacement government or automatic independence.
The event can become eligible after genuine restoration of independent authority.
This independence requirement is an added design boundary for the diplomatic and war systems.

Faction membership does not prevent the event.
An existing defensive war also does not prevent it when TAN still has a functioning government and usable territory.
A defensive emergency changes what aid is useful and how quickly it must be employed.
The event supplies no automatic peace, invulnerability, or territorial rescue.

Only one Tuvan host can own the episode.
Later cosmetic names, leader changes, faction changes, and capital moves preserve that ownership.
A civil-war offshoot cannot copy the episode or inherit every reserve merely by using a similar country name.
The episode never creates another ordinary minor-event occurrence when it sends an appeal, delivers aid, completes a focus, or opens a conquest front.

## The baseline campaign

| Phase | Main activity | Exit |
|---|---|---|
| Protection | Seek a credible protector and open foreign contacts | The aid network is established, with or without faction acceptance |
| Accumulation | Receive material aid, train reserves, expand industry, and prepare supply | The player commits to a reveal, or the AI reaches its readiness objective |
| Reveal | End protective ties, mobilize paid reserves, and open all current border wars | The opening transaction is complete |
| Expansion | Fight outward, repair captured industry, and settle conquered territory | Continental dominance is earned, TAN is defeated, or ordinary play continues |
| Global campaign | Build overseas capability and pursue every independent opponent | Tuvan world empire or defeat of the Tuvan campaign |

These phases are available without evolutions.
Evolutions strengthen the aid network and the scale of preparation.
They do not supply the baseline transition from diplomacy to conquest.

## The reveal is earned

A promise is not delivered equipment.
A foreign volunteer division is not Tuvan property.
An unfinished industrial project is not a working factory.
A research agreement is not ownership of every technology its sponsor knows.
The buildup must preserve these distinctions.

A poorly supported TAN can remain a weak state for a long time.
The player may reveal early with a smaller prepared force and accept the danger.
The AI has no deadline that silently awards the missing army.
Its normal policy is to wait for a strong, supplied force backed by useful industry and reserves.

The first reveal should be abrupt in the public event presentation.
There is no compulsory warning chain of territorial demands.
Normal intelligence, visible construction, military observation, and player suspicion remain possible.
The event does not promise engine-level secrecy that the rest of the game cannot provide.

## Campaign boundaries

There is no automatic ideology conversion, religious conversion, replacement leader, new tag, or removal of TAN's existing history.
God's Plan is a preferred protective faction when it actually exists under The Holy Realm.
Joining it does not transfer the Realm's religious powers to TAN.

The first reveal deliberately breaks protective alignment before attacking every bordering country.
Later genuine allies and Tuvan subjects are exempt from the recurring neighbor-war rule until a later diplomatic rupture or the final global campaign changes that relationship.
This resolves the difference between the brief's universal opening attack and its later non-allied-neighbor rule.

Continental conquest, global conquest, and the defeat aftermath are separate outcomes.
The first super-event is not a world-end trigger.
The second super-event does not declare victory.
A country that has only joined TAN's faction has not been conquered or subordinated.
