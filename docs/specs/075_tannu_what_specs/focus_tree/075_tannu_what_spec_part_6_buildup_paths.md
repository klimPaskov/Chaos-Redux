# 075 · Buildup and political paths

All stage labels below are working design labels unless they reproduce an anchor supplied in the original idea.
They describe route content and are not finished localisation.

## Please Help Us

The opening establishes the aid office, the donor list, and the first defensive request campaign.
The player chooses a priority suited to the present shortage.
It also opens the protector appeal when TAN does not already have a useful arrangement.
The first results can arrive while later institutional focuses are running.

The government then chooses one of three administrative approaches.
Each changes how the same worldwide network works.
It does not remove the possibility of asking other countries.

| Approach | First institutional step | Developed capability | Converging payoff | Lasting tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Broad sponsorship | Establish separate small appeals and a distributed contact office | Better coverage of minor and medium donors, more useful substitutions, fewer idle contacts | Coordinate mixed deliveries into balanced training and construction programs | More incompatible equipment and less concentrated technical depth |
| Concentrated patronage | Give a small group of protectors regular access to the aid program | Stronger faction packages, reliable adviser schedules, and focused industrial contracts | Build coherent equipment and training relationships with major sponsors | Future supply depends more heavily on governments that may become opening enemies |
| Industrial independence | Prioritize civilian projects, machine tools, transport, and domestic instructors | Reinvest completed aid into working production and reduce dependence on future shipments | Maintain useful replacement output after sponsors withdraw | A slower initial military buildup and fewer immediately deployable cohorts |

The approaches converge through coordinated aid administration.
That endpoint improves matching between delivered resources and actual shortages.
It does not erase the earlier dependency, equipment-mix, or timing tradeoff.
The AI selects an approach using available donors, industrial space, access, and the urgency of its borders.

## We Are Surrounded

This family gives the player better diplomatic explanations and practical access.
It should make the aid requests feel like a campaign by a government, not a random equipment vending machine.

| Stage | Requirement | Change in play | Risk or cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Border assessment | A functioning government and at least one real external relationship | Appeals use current regional conditions and identify useful defensive resources | A claim aimed at a donor's ally is less persuasive |
| Embassies and couriers | The initial aid office | Reach more distant donors and arrange verified transit discussions | Diplomatic effort competes with a direct high-value appeal |
| Protective arrangements | A willing and valid foreign partner | Better access or faction protection, with no forced ideology | The arrangement can expose TAN to the protector's wars |
| Repeated emergency dossiers | A developed contact network | More varied and persistent appeals within the active evolution's limits | Reusing the same story with the same donor increases local fatigue |
| Sovereignty guarantees | A completed credible protection agreement | More reliable aid channels and an alternative to depending on one patron | Guarantees do not make TAN immune to attack or annexation |

The branch can continue without God's Plan.
A successful Holy Realm relationship adds specific flavor and access to that faction's ordinary support.
A refused or absent Holy Realm route cannot leave a dead prerequisite in the tree.

## A Few Factories Would Help

The industrial family first solves basic siting and supply.
The player then chooses which kind of investment to emphasize.
Civilian, armaments, and logistics work can eventually coexist, but the first large project consumes time and resources that could have supported the others.

| Path | Early program | Middle program | Payoff and retained value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Civilian foundation | Survey usable sites and finance a first real construction project | Expand construction support and domestic machine-tool capacity | Completed factories help fund later industrial and transport projects |
| Armaments workshops | Select an equipment family with real demand and available production knowledge | Complete a funded arms project and establish replacement stocks | A working domestic source for the army's main equipment |
| Transport and dispersal | Improve the actual corridor between stores, industry, and deployment areas | Prepare redundant depots and repair capacity | More of the accumulated army can operate at once and losses are easier to recover |
| Dense industrial districts | Reach the supported building-space limit or a genuine efficiency opportunity | Fund additional staff, equipment, and productive capacity at existing complexes | A bounded output contribution that follows the host district and does not multiply every future conquest |
| Dual-purpose development | Invest in civilian-facing capacity and a paid conversion plan | Prepare eligible facilities for military use at the reveal | A one-time conversion with the original civilian capacity removed, never both building types at once |

Dual-purpose development is the main industrial-cover choice.
It can make the pre-reveal economy appear developmental while preparing a real conversion.
It still appears in ordinary game statistics and requires a funded plan.
Choosing visible military construction is faster for immediate arms production but makes a harmless image less convincing to well-informed donors.

## No One Is Watching

The security family is about compartmentalized administration and preparation.
It does not introduce an omnipotent intelligence service or a new suspicion currency.

| Stage | What it enables | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| Separate donor files | Keep each foreign report focused on that donor's own commitment | Hide normal public game information |
| Reserve administration | Maintain paid cohorts and prevent mixed deliveries from being wasted | Create recruits or equipment from an unsigned agreement |
| Domestic officer preparation | Retain useful training after advisers leave | Copy a foreign historical commander or every donor doctrine |
| Mobilization timetable | Prepare distributed deployment and support requirements | Deploy an unlimited army into an unsupplied province |
| Final staff review | Open the normal reveal commitment when the program is ready | Waive the all-neighbor opening or promise victory |

The route's mature payoff is a coherent army that can be mobilized together.
The industry and military paths provide the material basis.
The staff family coordinates it.
The final commitment should not require every optional branch or force the player to research irrelevant aircraft technology.

## Crisis and recovery branches

A lost protector, a blocked corridor, or an actual attack can change the route without replacing the tree.
A small recovery connection lets TAN shift to independent contacts, reopen a usable route, or deploy paid reserves defensively.
It does not refund every prior investment or award an equivalent free sponsor.

When a donor disappears, unfinished patron-specific tasks can be replaced by a compatible new sponsorship task or bypassed without unpaid rewards.
Completed factories and learned training remain where they genuinely belong.
A blocked foreign contract remains blocked until its terms are fulfilled or it closes.

## Political consequences

Broad sponsorship leaves the civilian aid office with an important coordinating role.
Concentrated patronage strengthens the offices and officers managing the major foreign relationship.
Industrial independence gives domestic production and training institutions more control over preparation.
These differences alter later mobilization and administration choices.

They do not invent a historical coup, replace the real leader's portrait, or assert that a named politician secretly planned the fictional conquest.
The political story belongs to the alternate campaign.
New personal characters require a separate sourced character decision and portrait workflow.
