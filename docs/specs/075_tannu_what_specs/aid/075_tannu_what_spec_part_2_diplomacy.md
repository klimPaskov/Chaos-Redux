# 075 · Protection and the worldwide appeals

## Choosing protection

TAN first approaches the faction led by The Holy Realm when that faction is the actual God's Plan formation and can admit it.
The preference is strong, but the faction leader retains the right to refuse.
An existing unrelated Holy Realm alignment does not become God's Plan through a name check or a forced rename.

When that route is absent or refused, TAN considers the strongest valid protective factions.
Their usefulness depends on military and industrial strength, survival, relations, access to the region, and whether they would immediately involve TAN in an unwanted war.
A defeated faction with a familiar historical name is not automatically preferred over a functioning alternative.
There is no fixed British fallback.

An already protected TAN need not leave a useful faction merely to replay the opening.
The player can retain that protection or seek a change when diplomacy permits it.
The AI does not abandon a safe arrangement for a slightly higher ranking.
A protector's refusal closes that particular appeal for a substantial interval and moves the search onward.
Aid remains available while the faction search is unsuccessful.

Protection asks for mutual defense and recognition of sovereignty.
It does not require a change of ruling party.
A faction may insist on conditions that TAN cannot meet, in which case it is an invalid or unattractive protector.
An offer from a country already at war with TAN is invalid.

## Reaching the world

The network includes every existing country that can provide at least one useful, transferable form of assistance.
Size, ideology, distance, and faction are not universal exclusion rules.
They influence which package is requested, how it can reach TAN, and how willing the donor is to agree.

Each country has one active request at a time.
The network can nevertheless have many different countries considering offers simultaneously.
A campaign priority influences the mix of requests without forcing an aircraft-poor donor to supply aircraft or a manpower-poor donor to supply recruits.
A rejected request does not immediately return with a different label.

The normal dispatch cycle is roughly monthly.
Individual donors are approached less often, with a baseline interval around three months.
Evolutions shorten these intervals and allow additional campaign priorities, as specified in Part 11.
A donor that has an unfinished project or an outstanding delivery is not charged again for the same commitment.

The player selects broad priorities and may make a limited direct appeal to a chosen country.
Routine dispatch handles the wider network.
This keeps worldwide solicitation practical without requiring a hundred individual clicks.
The player can pause new appeals without deleting paid aid or abandoning active projects.

## Stories that fit the recipient

The request describes a defensive need related to the current campaign.
When a threatening neighbor actually exists, the claim can refer to it.
When the claim is fabricated, the text presents it as TAN's allegation rather than an objective statement by the game.
A Soviet, Mongolian, Chinese, or other named threat is used only when that country exists and has a plausible regional connection.

| Situation | Appeal direction | Useful assistance |
|---|---|---|
| Weak border defense | Border posts and local units cannot cover the approaches | Rifles, support equipment, artillery, training |
| Actual defensive war | The government needs immediate help to maintain independence | Available equipment, fuel, permanent recruits, valid native military support |
| Refugee or mobilization pressure | Reception and mobilization have exceeded the state's resources | Transport, construction, supplies, medical or recruitment support where supported |
| Industrial weakness | A sovereign country cannot rely on a single vulnerable workshop | Factory projects, machine tools, production contracts |
| Officer shortage | Rapid recruitment has outgrown the command schools | Advisers, training programs, relevant research assistance |
| A donor's regional rivalry | TAN offers a modest obstacle to a rival's ambitions | Equipment or sponsorship that serves that donor's interests |
| Few credible named threats | An unspecified external threat is alleged | A small, general defensive package without invented territorial facts |

Refugee language never produces free civilian population or recruits.
Actual migration and humanitarian resources belong to the existing systems.
An appeal may exaggerate need, but its mechanical payment and outcome remain explicit to the donor.

## Why the offer can succeed

The donor considers its own affordable contribution and its own information.
It does not receive a hidden worldwide total or knowledge of TAN's future conquest focus.
A minor contribution to a much weaker friendly government should often be acceptable, particularly under shared protection or rivalry with TAN's neighbor.

Generosity has boundaries.
An army missing equipment for its own field divisions should not give away those requirements.
A government without free manpower should not accept a manpower transfer.
A country with no route for delivery should not promise immediate physical arrival.
A donor at war with TAN must refuse new assistance.

Repeated local requests can create modest fatigue for that donor.
This is a memory of its own prior dealings, not a global suspicion meter.
Friendly relations, a new genuine emergency, completed projects, or a useful regional purpose may offset fatigue.
The high evolutions improve persuasiveness without removing affordability checks or player refusal.

## The donor's choice

A donor normally receives up to four alternatives: provide the full quoted package, provide a smaller package, offer a useful substitute, or refuse.
The substitute is computed from resources that donor can actually supply.
An aircraft request might become trucks or industrial support, but it cannot become a generic reward box.
The donor sees the selected amount, what will be consumed or committed, and any delivery condition before accepting.

A human donor can establish a standing policy for later requests.
That policy can favor modest assistance, require a fresh decision, or refuse further appeals.
It cannot authorize a package larger than its chosen affordability limit.
A policy change affects future approvals and does not confiscate deliveries already completed.

A protector can prefer aid that builds dependence.
The concentrated-patronage path accepts more of those practical obligations, such as reliance on a particular equipment family or scheduled adviser access.
No patron receives automatic annexation rights, secret control of TAN, or an involuntary future war merely because it supplied rifles.

## Local information and deception

Donors can review their own deliveries and the progress of their own projects.
The selected donor record shows a concise history, its present offer, and the status of its outstanding commitment.
TAN can inspect the complete internal history.
Other countries cannot open TAN's aggregate aid record through this event's interface.

Foreign focus-tree browsing, espionage, visible divisions, factory statistics, and normal multiplayer communication are not disabled.
An attentive player may work out the deception.
The event's secrecy comes from fragmented transactions and the absence of an automatic worldwide warning.

A donor that becomes an enemy stops new aid immediately.
Paid goods already delivered remain Tuvan property.
Unpaid promises close.
Paid goods still in transit follow their declared custody and cancellation terms, with no duplicate refund or delivery.
