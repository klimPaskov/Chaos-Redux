# 075 · Turning aid into lasting strength

## The small-state industrial problem

A large aid network can overwhelm TAN's original building space and transport.
The industrial route must address both limitations.
It first develops real factory sites, repairs existing buildings, improves transport, and adds supported building capacity.
Those projects make the early industrial base visible through ordinary game systems.

A second layer develops dense industrial districts around the working factories.
A completed, donor-backed district project adds a bounded amount of productive capacity tied to its host state.
This is a deliberate gameplay abstraction for workshops, machine tools, skilled staff, and multiple production shifts.
It is not a claim that an unlimited number of physical factories fits into one state.

The added output is an absolute funded contribution spread over TAN's working industry.
It must not become a permanent percentage that multiplies every factory TAN later conquers.
As the physical industrial base grows, the relative bonus falls unless TAN funds further eligible improvements.
Losing or disabling the contributing district removes its contribution until it is recovered and repaired.

This industrial-depth system is part of the proposed design.
Its exact output consumer and limits require installed-game verification.
An unsupported local modifier must not be replaced without disclosure by an unlimited national factory bonus.

## Three uses of an industrial gift

| Use | Immediate consequence | Later value | Cost of choosing it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Civilian foundation | Fewer immediate weapons | More construction and a stronger domestic investment cycle | The reveal is delayed while resources go into development |
| Armaments capacity | Earlier military production | Better replacement and reserve maintenance | Less capacity for civilian expansion and transport |
| Logistics and dispersal | More usable supply, storage, access, and repair resilience | A larger army can operate without consuming its entire stockpile at once | Less headline factory growth |

None of these routes is automatically the correct choice.
A country receiving ample rifles but few recruits may need training and manpower support first.
A country with a large paid army and poor supply needs depots and transport.
A country planning a long buildup can profit from an early civilian foundation.

Foreign projects do not improve themselves for free.
Follow-on expansion requires a new material or industrial commitment and another completion period.
Domestic reinvestment consumes construction opportunity that could have produced other buildings.
This is the intended compounding loop.

## Paid training cohorts

The future army is assembled from a series of training cohorts with documented manpower, equipment, instruction, and time.
A cohort receives actual resources once, undergoes training, and becomes eligible for mobilization.
Its resources cannot also be used by ordinary production, another cohort, or a donor's army.

The implementation must preserve the actual custody of manpower and equipment throughout that process.
A reserve record with no backing is not a trained division.
The exact native training, reserve, equipment-variant, and deployment mechanism must be verified before implementation is accepted.
The plan does not assume that an untested unit-spawn command consumes stock automatically.

Completed cohorts can be mobilized at the reveal or earlier for genuine defense.
Mobilizing a cohort consumes its reserve entitlement once.
Early deployment reduces the concealed reserve available at the reveal and exposes the normal unit on the map.
It does not duplicate the manpower, refund the equipment, or create an extra copy when the main reveal fires.

Training quality depends on actual advisers, domestic instruction, completed focuses, and the time invested.
A newly trained force can be competent without every unit becoming a veteran.
Specialist formations need their own compatible equipment and instruction.
A hundred rifle cohorts cannot be reclassified as armored divisions because TAN later receives a tank donation.

## The readiness value

TAN has one persistent custom value: **Readiness**.
It summarizes how much of the currently chosen reveal program can be fielded and sustained.
The player sees a percentage, the current qualitative ambition, and the most important unresolved requirement.
Detailed native stockpiles and the selected project remain accessible through their ordinary surfaces.
There is no second spendable aid currency.

Readiness is limited by the weakest essential part of the program.
Surplus rifles cannot hide a manpower shortage.
A trained army without working supply or replacement capacity is not fully ready.
Unsigned promises, foreign-owned divisions, unfinished factories, and unreachable deliveries do not count.

The program compares paid fieldable strength with an ambition appropriate to the campaign and active evolution.
Its targets are refreshed at deliberate planning points, not every day in response to the world's largest army.
TAN must not chase a continually moving goal forever.
A newly activated evolution can raise the ambition for future preparation without confiscating existing reserves or secretly forcing a player to abandon an early reveal.

The player may inspect the readiness explanation and choose a smaller early commitment.
The normal AI target is a well-supported army that would be extraordinary for the country, with higher evolutions aiming at established-major or leading-major strength.
Part 20 defines the balance fixtures used to check those claims.

## Supply before deployment

Supply preparations include usable home territory, operating hubs or local supply, repaired approaches, trains, trucks where needed, fuel, and sensible unit placement.
The reserve program plans several deployment groups instead of placing every unit in one province.
A reserve that exceeds safe local supply remains in reserve until it can be deployed usefully.
Its later deployment still consumes its original paid entitlement.

The first reveal must field enough troops for an immediate land war.
It cannot display the super-event and then leave TAN with an empty deployment queue.
It also cannot solve supply by granting total attrition immunity or unlimited supply everywhere.
Any large high-evolution supply benefit remains geographically and mechanically tied to completed preparation.

## Mobilization choices

A broad infantry army uses the most common foreign donations and can open several fronts.
A smaller mixed force uses more transport, artillery, trained support, and maintenance.
An armored spearhead demands a narrower set of equipment and more fuel but can exploit a favorable route.
The player chooses the mix within the actual reserve and supply budget.

Aviation supports this choice.
Donated fighters need airfields and operating capacity.
Close support needs suitable range and a sustainable fuel budget.
Aircraft that cannot operate remain in reserve rather than becoming a decorative air-force number.

The force is assembled from existing compatible unit families.
Foreign training is expressed through organization, templates, training, and appropriate experience.
It does not require a new subunit, a new biological identity, or a custom model family.

## Wartime interruption

A real attack during the aid phase can force TAN to use reserves early.
Defensive emergency deployment preserves the aid network where donors still have a valid reason to help.
It does not automatically declare war on every other neighbor.
Only the deliberate Tannu What? reveal invokes the universal opening attack.

The AI can defend itself with the force it has and continue requesting useful assistance.
It may commit an emergency reveal only when the actual position supports that decision.
An emergency does not waive manpower, equipment, supply, or ownership requirements.
If TAN loses independent government before the reveal, the episode closes without manufacturing a successor army.
