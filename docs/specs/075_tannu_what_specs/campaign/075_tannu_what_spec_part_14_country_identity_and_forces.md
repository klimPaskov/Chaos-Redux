# 075 · Country identity, institutions, and forces

## Existing country, new campaign

TAN keeps its actual country identity, ruler, ideology, characters, owned land, and ordinary diplomacy when the episode begins.
A protective faction preference does not justify an automatic ideology replacement.
The event does not manufacture a leader portrait or rename a historical ruler to fit a joke.

The dedicated tree gives the state a new political program.
The early government speaks through embassies, aid commissions, border-defense offices, and industrial delegations.
The revealed government uses the same institutions for mobilization and conquest.
This continuity makes the earlier assistance recognizable in the later war machine.

The local institutional language can draw cautiously on the documented existence of military organization, foreign advisers, and officer training in historical Tuva.
The deception, worldwide sponsorship, immense army, and conquests are fictional developments of this mod.
Research context and asset rights are documented separately.

## Staff and command

Existing Tuvan commanders remain usable.
Officer training and foreign advisers can improve the preparation of the army or support additional generic command capacity when the scale of the actual army requires it.
Do not copy a famous foreign general into TAN because that general's country sent an adviser mission.
A borrowed mission does not transfer the advisor country's entire command roster.

New generic commanders, if implementation requires them, use the game's generic character and portrait system with grounded names and no claim to be specific historical people.
Their experience follows training and service.
They do not all appear as maximum-level specialists at the reveal.
Portrait-specific work becomes a separate verified production task only if the final character design actually creates a named historical character.

## Military families

The army uses existing infantry, cavalry where appropriate to the current country, artillery, support units, motorized formations, armor, and compatible air roles.
The world campaign adds existing naval and marine families.
No special Tuvan infantry type, fictional vehicle technology, custom weapon, or new combat-stat system is required.

The starting emphasis is practical defense and reserve organization.
It can develop into several useful force mixes.
A rifle-rich network produces many infantry formations.
A network with trucks, fuel, and workshops can motorize selected formations.
A heavy-equipment sponsor can support armor once crews, maintenance, and replacements exist.
Air support needs pilots, fuel, appropriate aircraft, and fields.

Equipment compatibility matters.
A donated tank design does not make an unsupported battalion type available by itself.
An aircraft gift cannot be turned into strategic bombers through a generic “aircraft aid” total.
Research and template access are separate from physical custody.

## Foreign support

Volunteers, expeditionary forces, advisers, and permanently transferred manpower have different ownership and withdrawal rules.
Their distinct identities remain visible in the aid system.
Only permanently accepted and legally transferred manpower contributes to TAN's sovereign recruitment pool.
Only TAN-owned equipment or already paid sovereign reserve backing can form the reveal's new Tuvan units.

Foreign formations can help TAN survive a real defensive war.
They cannot be silently retagged into Tuvan divisions at the reveal, retained after a lawful recall, or duplicated when the original sponsor later becomes an enemy.
If the installed game cannot safely supply a requested support mechanism under the current conditions, the offer uses another valid support type or is unavailable.

## Names and appearance

The first reveal keeps the recognizable Tuvan identity.
A later earned great-power title can change the cosmetic country name without changing the host tag or pretending the regime became a different historical state.
A global-victory cosmetic identity is a fictional empire outcome.
Its flag, if added, needs its own approved design and all native size variants.
Existing TAN flags remain the default until that production work is complete.

The army should look compatible with its era, equipment, and existing country graphical culture.
Use existing legal models, counters, sounds, and native subunits.
Do not require custom 3D production to represent ordinary donated weapons.
A future request for a genuinely new unit family would reopen the complete 3D and unit-integration workflow rather than inherit an unsupported placeholder from this plan.

## Occupation and integration

Military occupation remains distinct from political integration.
Captured territory contributes only what the actual control, damage, occupation, resistance, and subject rules allow.
The direct-administration route invests in stable control and repairs.
The subordinate-state route builds real dependencies with their own territory and loyalty requirements.

The tree does not instantly core every conquered state.
It does not transfer every subject soldier and factory to TAN as if the subject ceased to exist.
Integration should have meaningful regional requirements and costs tied to established control.
A rebellion can break a supply line, remove qualifying continental control, or create a new hostile neighbor.

## Other Chaos Redux systems

TAN is an ordinary human country for the shared Chaos, casualty, pollution, diplomatic, and warfare systems.
It does not gain undead, divine, alien, or other special-state exemptions from requesting help from those powers.
Religious faction membership alone does not confer the Holy Realm's identity or mechanics.
Ordinary diplomatic deception does not automatically become a chemical or biological atrocity.
Qualifying actions still use their real shared-system consequences.
