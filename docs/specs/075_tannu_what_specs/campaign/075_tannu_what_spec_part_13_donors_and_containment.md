# 075 · Donor reactions, resistance, and defeat

## A donor remembers its own contribution

Each donor's reaction is grounded in completed assistance it actually provided.
A rifles-and-trucks donor can discover its equipment in Tuvan formations.
A manpower sponsor can face the political consequences of having recruited for the new enemy.
An industrial sponsor can recognize a funded complex now producing weapons.
An adviser provider can recognize training methods it helped establish.
A country that refused every request does not receive an apology for having built the army.

A failed promise is not counted as a delivered gift.
A small donor is not described as the principal financier merely because it answered first.
A recipient-specific message uses that country's record.
A major-donor message uses a comparative contribution assessment based on actual aid categories and their significance to TAN's preparation.
That assessment is for flavour and bounded campaign recognition, not an invented spendable aid currency.

## Reaction families

| Donor history | Reaction subject | Practical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Delivered equipment | Identifiable foreign arms in the opening army | Ends future gifts and may permit a targeted review of that aid program |
| Sent manpower or training support | Recruitment and instruction used for the invasion force | Ends repeat recruitment channels and recalls still-owned personnel |
| Completed industrial projects | The funded facilities are now war factories | Ends unfinished foreign-dependent support, while completed TAN-owned plant remains |
| Provided research or licenses | Modernization helped a new opponent | Ends future cooperation through the actual agreement rules |
| Protected TAN in a faction | The supposed dependent minor has broken alignment | Political reaction and ordinary war/faction behavior |
| Refused or only promised | The threat developed without that government's material gift | Observation or vindication, without a fabricated contribution |

One donor gets one principal reaction with its most relevant details.
A mixed donor can have a short secondary reference.
It should not receive a separate blocking popup for each truck shipment.
A player controlling several affected governments through multiplayer still gets the information appropriate to each controlled country.

## A pre-reveal audit

Repeated requests and an individual donor's own increasing commitments can justify an audit decision.
This is an optional response to information the donor actually has.
It does not use TAN's hidden worldwide aid total as an AI trigger.
Intelligence and visible military or industrial growth can support suspicion when those observations are available through real game systems.

An audit suspends that donor's new aid while it reviews its own program.
It can confirm irregular paperwork or inconsistent explanations and let the donor refuse further assistance.
It does not instantly reveal every other sponsor, transfer private equipment inventories, or trigger the Tuvan reveal on the donor's behalf.
The user concept requires a sudden military reveal, not a mandatory escalating global accusation chain.

A donor may later resume aid with smaller terms or continue refusing.
The episode never overrides a human refusal with a secret random acceptance.
A high evolution can make persuasion more effective for AI governments, but cannot remove donor agency.

## Containment after the reveal

Countries threatened by TAN can stop support, recall their still-owned missions, prepare their borders, repair supply, and coordinate with real potential partners.
A defensive-coordination action invites eligible independent countries into a practical diplomatic response through the existing faction and relationship rules.
It does not merge hostile factions by event decree.
A government can refuse cooperation even while fearing TAN.

The most useful immediate counterplay is geographic.
Cutting a real supply corridor, recovering a funded industrial state, destroying or capturing an actual port, and exhausting the prepared equipment reserve all weaken the Tuvan army through its material base.
There is no separate “break the spell” button that deletes the military.

Former donors may receive a limited opportunity to identify their own equipment standards or training assumptions in a specific opposing theater.
Any advantage is bounded and expires with that campaign condition.
It is not a global permanent combat bonus against every future Tuvan division.
Likewise, TAN's former-donor campaign bonus cannot stack once per historical shipment.

## Failed deception

A network with many refusals may never reach the AI's preferred reveal strength.
TAN can change aid priorities, invest its actual domestic resources, seek a different lawful protector, and wait for funded work to complete.
If no viable support remains, it can stay a small state with an exposed or exhausted diplomacy program.
That is a legitimate outcome of the premise that its eventual army depends on what it extracted.

The player retains the early-reveal route when its real military and political conditions are met.
The event does not award a consolation army for having spent several years in the focus tree.
Human sponsors are allowed to prevent the strongest version of the event by refusing or withdrawing uncompleted support.

## Failed first invasion

A failed offensive can leave TAN with a trained but depleted army, inadequate replacements, and multiple active wars.
The post-reveal tree still provides reconstruction, production organization, and defensive operational choices.
Its outward-war policy makes new borders dangerous even when it is not winning.
It cannot return to the original innocence and request the same emergency gifts again.

A country that recovers Tuvan-held territory changes the actual continental calculation.
A country that wins an ordinary peace receives only the actual settlement.
A relief or containment milestone should require a durable strategic result, not one empty province traded back and forth.

## Defeat of the Tuvan episode

When the dominant TAN loses the authority required to continue, pending offensive target acquisition, aid requests, mobilisation rewards, and world-end initiation stop.
Existing units and equipment follow real ownership and peace rules.
Unactivated paid reserves are resolved without copying them into both the victor's inventory and a restored TAN.
Foreign-owned support follows its owner-safe return rules where those still apply.

The aftermath can describe the scale of the deception and the governments that materially enabled it.
It should distinguish a small failed scheme from a defeated continental empire.
It must not accuse a particular historical country of donating in a campaign where that country did not participate.

A later ordinary release of TAN does not reset Event 075's Fire-Once status or create another full aid campaign.
Any accepted recovery route must belong to the surviving episode and preserve its losses, donor record, completed rewards, and defeated-state conditions.
This package does not add a repeatable resurrection route.
