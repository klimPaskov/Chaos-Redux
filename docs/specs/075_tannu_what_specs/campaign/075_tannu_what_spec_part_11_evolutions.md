# 075 · Evolutions

## What evolves

The aid operation has three increasingly capable forms above the baseline.
Each changes the reach, credibility, scale, or organization of aid and the ambition of military preparation.
The operation's ordinary phases remain protection, accumulation, reveal, expansion, and an optional global campaign.
Reaching an evolution threshold does not automatically declare war or replace one of those phases.

Chaos thresholds make an evolution eligible.
The project-wide evolution mode decides whether eligibility is resolved before the ordinary event fires or through the active evolution process.
Under the active process, use the shared evolution timing, whose planning baseline is roughly 90 days once eligible.
A threshold crossing alone is not a guaranteed same-day activation.

Only the highest active tier's version of a shared benefit applies.
The tiers do not stack three copies of the same aid discount or frequency bonus.
Paid equipment, completed factories, trained cohorts, and settled donor transactions remain real when the tier changes.
Falling global Chaos does not reverse those deliveries or convert trained soldiers back into foreign aid.

## Baseline · A vulnerable minor

The opening network sends modest requests to any useful eligible donor.
It can coordinate many separate national contacts, while one main aid campaign gives them a common priority.
Equipment, limited manpower, basic industry, valid foreign support, fuel, and available research assistance are useful even before evolutions.
Baseline is a complete playable event and can eventually reach the reveal and conquest routes.

Its weakness is the time required to combine many individually small offers.
An isolated TAN with few sponsors struggles to fill all of its military needs.
A well-connected TAN can produce a large force, but supply and training still limit the opening.

## Evolution I · Please, Just a Little More

**Chaos requirement: 200 or more.**

The aid administration learns which justifications and package sizes its donors accept.
Repeat requests can be somewhat larger and can arrive sooner.
Support routes become more effective at securing heavier weapons, aircraft, advisers, manpower, and larger volunteer commitments when the donor really has them available.
These types are not mysteriously absent from the baseline when an existing valid route already supports them.
The evolution improves their practical availability and scale.

Industrial appeals can fund more complete projects with fewer separate installments.
Research relationships can support a longer modernization program instead of one narrow intervention.
The story remains a collection of plausible defensive needs.

The AI normally selects a larger prepared-force objective.
It is willing to spend longer building the combined manpower, equipment, industry, and replacements needed for that objective.
A player can still reveal early using the force already paid for.
An evolution cannot revoke that choice merely by moving a progress target upward.

## Evolution II · The World's Most Defenseless Country

**Chaos requirement: 400 or more.**

TAN can operate two coordinated aid campaigns with different priorities at once.
For example, one may emphasize industrial and railway work while another recruits manpower and acquires field equipment.
Each donor still sees at most one unresolved request from this event at a time.
Two campaign labels cannot extract two payments for the same delivery.

Threat stories become more elaborate and adapt to the recipient's concerns.
A rival of a neighboring power can hear about that power's pressure.
An industrial sponsor can hear about transport and refugee demands.
A friendly military government can be asked to help prepare officers and a defensive reserve.
Specific claims should remain consistent with the visible campaign where they assert an actual country or war.
An invented pretext is clearly TAN's claim, not the narrator's established fact.

The aid system can fund substantial industrial complexes, larger stockpiles, trained formations, and advanced equipment that the sponsor can genuinely supply.
The prepared army can become comparable to a major power when the deliveries and home production support it.
That comparison is an earned outcome, not a guaranteed rank granted by the tier.

## Evolution III · Everyone Helped

**Chaos requirement: 600 or more.**

The operation can coordinate three aid campaigns.
Its diplomatic framing works across ideological and faction divisions that previously made generous sponsorship less likely.
The scale of each request can be substantial while remaining bounded by that donor's actual ability to spare it.
A struggling minor is not asked to provide the same manpower or armor package as an intact major power.

The preferred package mix now supports large reserves, armor, artillery, aviation, military training, and a substantial production base together.
The strongest outcomes come from broad participation and time for funded projects to finish.
TAN can emerge as one of the strongest countries in the world immediately after the reveal, but the event does not promise that result when sponsors have refused or collapsed.

The title describes the scale of the network.
Localisation must not assert literally universal participation in a campaign where only some countries provided aid.
A donor refusal remains a refusal at every tier.

## Evolution after the reveal

A later active evolution cannot reopen the original deception.
The world has already seen the opening wars.
Instead, its organizational improvement applies to the legitimate remaining material base: reserve activation throughput, handling of mixed equipment, army support, and conversion of completed aid-funded industry into sustained production.

No post-reveal evolution creates retrospective donations, takes money from a former donor without consent, or grants a second opening army.
A saved paid reserve can be activated more effectively when the improved organization permits it.
An empty reserve stays empty.
This gives late evolution activation a useful effect without changing the event's history.

## Tuning seeds

These values are proposed starting points for playtesting, not measured balance results or verified AI probabilities.
They can be revised together after the required tool and playtest evidence.

| Aid-network property | Baseline | Evolution I | Evolution II | Evolution III |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Coordinated campaigns | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Network dispatch interval in days | 30 | 20 | 15 | 10 |
| Normal same-donor cooldown in days | 90 | 75 | 60 | 45 |
| Maximum share of eligible spare material in an ordinary full offer | 5% | 5% | 10% | 10% |
| Example rifle-package ceiling | 2,000 | 5,000 | 10,000 | 25,000 |
| Example manpower-package ceiling | 5,000 | 10,000 | 25,000 | 50,000 |

A ceiling is never a minimum payment.
A donor with 400 safely exportable rifles at baseline cannot donate 2,000 merely because that is the listed ceiling.
The example share would yield at most 20 rifles, and a minimum-useful-package test could choose another aid family instead.
Stockpile and mobilization safety rules take priority over generosity.
Stronger focused emergency packages require their own affordability assessment and do not silently bypass these protections.

## World-end is separate

The 1000-Chaos global-conquest route is an earned world-end scenario.
It is not a fourth ordinary evolution and is not unlocked by completing the three evolutions alone.
Continental control, the surviving dominant Tuvan state, the scenario settings, and the shared world-end ownership rules all remain necessary.
