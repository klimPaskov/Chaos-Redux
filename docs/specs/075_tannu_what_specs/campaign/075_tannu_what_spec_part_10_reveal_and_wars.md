# 075 · The reveal and outward wars

## Choosing the moment

TAN's player can commit through the short **Tannu What?** focus or the early-reveal decision.
Both enter the same irreversible transition.
The focus represents the normal prepared opening.
The decision permits a smaller, riskier opening once TAN has a usable paid force and the means to supply it.
Neither requires the other to be completed afterward.

The commitment screen names the countries that presently share a valid land border with TAN, marks its current protector, and explains which treaties and borrowed support will end.
It describes the actual force ready for deployment and the material that must remain in reserve.
It does not predict a guaranteed victory or promise that every item in a warehouse will become a combat unit.

The AI waits for its selected preparation objective and a workable opening deployment.
A defensive emergency can justify using reserves to survive, but it does not by itself authorize an unprepared war against every neighbor.
There is no scripted date on which the AI receives the missing resources and reveals anyway.

## The opening border set

The opening considers countries controlling provinces that directly touch TAN-controlled provinces across traversable land borders.
A country with ten adjacent provinces is one political target.
A distant owner of land currently occupied by another government is not an additional target merely because its ownership color differs.
Ordinary impassable boundaries and sea connections do not manufacture a direct land neighbor.
New territory acquired before commitment changes the opening set.

Countries already at war with TAN remain part of the opening campaign without receiving duplicate declarations.
Where a bordering subject's overlord must receive the legal declaration, the war relationship must still place the actual bordering subject in the opposing side.
A declaration against a distant overlord that leaves the bordering subject neutral does not satisfy the opening rule.

TAN leaves its protective faction before it opens these wars.
A former faction member on the border is included.
A former protector elsewhere can enter through ordinary faction and guarantee behavior, but the opening does not directly declare war on every donor in the world.

The rare case of TAN already having a bordering subject needs an explicit rule.
For the opening only, that subject is released from TAN's authority before it becomes an opening opponent.
This strict interpretation of “all countries it currently borders” is an added design choice.
The player must see the release in the commitment warning.
No free annexation, transferred army, or fabricated independent country accompanies it.
Subjects created during subsequent conquest are protected by the later expansion rule.

## One commitment, one mobilization

Preparation for the transition checks political legality, army backing, deployment space, support ownership, and valid targets before it changes the public campaign state.
A resolvable engine restriction must be handled explicitly.
An unresolved restriction blocks commitment with a useful reason instead of producing a reveal that announces wars it never opens.

After commitment, the transition closes new defensive appeals, invalidates unanswered requests, and reconciles funded projects and paid reserves.
Completed gifts remain TAN's property.
Foreign-owned volunteers and expeditionary forces are returned or withdrawn through a verified ownership-safe route before hostilities can put their owner on the opposing side.
Future deliveries under foreign production agreements cease when their terms are broken.
No donor receives a refund for weapons that were genuinely delivered and consumed.

The prepared military is activated once.
Manpower, equipment, and training already held in a paid cohort cannot also remain freely spendable in the native pool.
Free manpower used in a newly formed unit is debited once.
Units that were activated earlier for actual defense are already part of the army and are not created again.

Opening declarations follow this commitment as one political action.
The presentation is delivered after the transition has a real military and diplomatic result to show.
Reloading between messages cannot repeat grants, faction departures, subject release, or the first super-event.

## Deployment that can fight

The initial army is distributed between supplied home positions, valid approaches to the opening fronts, and usable rear areas.
A large reserve does not justify placing hundreds of divisions in the capital province.
The opening should immediately provide a serious active force when the preparation earned one.
Additional paid formations that would exceed workable local support remain organized reserves until there is room to use them.

Rifle abundance primarily supports infantry.
Motorization needs trucks and fuel.
Armor needs appropriate vehicles, trained crews, maintenance, and replacement capacity.
Aircraft require an airfield and usable aircraft of the intended role.
Unsupported heavy equipment remains a stockpile or a development opportunity.
It does not turn into an unrelated free unit family.

Deployment neither clears enemy units from a province nor grants foreign border territory.
A TAN already under attack uses the valid land it actually holds.
The event cannot deploy a complete army into enemy-controlled former home territory.

## The first super-event

The title is **TANNU WHAT?**.
The message is that the diplomatic minor has committed its foreign-supported army to a regional war.
The presentation should contrast the scale of the opening with the individual modest requests donors remember.
It must not claim that every country donated, that every neighbor has already fallen, or that TAN now rules a continent.

All eligible human observers receive the same one-time world development.
Local donor reactions are separate, smaller messages.
A player who refuses a cosmetic super-event display still receives the underlying political and military outcome.

## New borders, new wars

After the opening, every newly eligible non-allied neighboring country enters the target set.
TAN's genuine faction allies and its loyal subjects are exempt at this stage.
Countries already at war with TAN do not generate new declarations or repeated rewards.
A new hostile border can arise from conquest, a peace settlement, independence, civil war, a subject change, or a loss of allied status.

The recurring check is periodic and also responds to meaningful political changes.
The planning target is a declaration within about five days of a newly valid border, subject to verified legal restrictions.
A player can prioritize the movement of armies without cancelling the event's political expansion rule.
The AI can redistribute strength and defend some fronts while attacking another.
It cannot make an inconvenient valid neighbor permanently immune through its operational priorities.

Ordinary military access is not an alliance.
A guarantee is not an alliance.
A temporary truce is a temporary legal limitation, not a completed conquest.
TAN respects actual war-system limitations and reevaluates when they end.
The event does not end and restart a war every few days to farm rewards or shared Chaos.

## Peace and loss

A genuine peace leaves only the territory and relationships the settlement actually grants.
The event does not restore lost conquered land afterward.
An ordinary white-peace effect can interrupt TAN's wars and creates a visible operational pause.
The outward policy remains active unless the Tuvan campaign itself has been defeated or disabled by an explicit applicable system.

The player can suffer a failed first offensive and remain in the expansion phase with a damaged army.
The diplomacy deception cannot be restarted under another name.
Defeat of the dominant Tuvan state ends its automated conquest behavior and pending campaign rewards.
Its historical donor record remains available for the aftermath.
