# 075 · Continental victory and the world-end campaign

## What counts as a continent

The campaign evaluates the installed map's inhabited, playable continental land.
It recognizes the continents the map actually supplies, including a first conquest outside Asia.
It does not hardcode “control the Soviet Union” or “own China's capital” as a substitute for continental conquest.
Oceans, Antarctica-like uninhabited map areas, and non-playable decorative land are not easier victory targets.

An eligible continent must have a meaningful organized-country presence in the campaign's opening map.
The eligible land-state set and its victory-point weights are fixed when this episode begins.
Destroying population, deleting victory points, moving a capital, or depopulating a difficult state must not shrink the denominator and make a conquest easier to certify.
A supported map-changing overhaul needs an explicit reviewed continental definition, not an empty-set victory.

## Continental dominance

The proposed victory threshold is control of at least 90% of the eligible land states and at least 90% of the fixed victory-point weight on the same continent.
Both conditions must hold for 30 consecutive days.
These thresholds interpret the brief's “overwhelming majority” and are added tuning choices.
They are not a claim about a native HOI4 continent-victory mechanic.

Qualifying control belongs to TAN itself or a loyal actual subject under TAN's authority.
An equal faction ally does not count.
Military access, a guarantee, a peace-conference claim that has not taken effect, and a state still occupied by a hostile force do not count.
A subject in open revolt does not contribute its contested control to TAN's victory.

The condition may be met through wartime occupation, since the brief permits conquest or control.
The 30-day holding period prevents a single-day map sweep from immediately becoming continental victory.
An enemy can interrupt certification by recovering enough qualifying territory.

The player sees the current leading continent, the two completion requirements, and whether the holding period is intact.
This is an objective description based on the map, not another currency or a separate public “continental power” score.
The first certified continent receives a milestone record even when global Chaos is below 1000.

## Starting the world-end scenario

Starting requires all of the following at the same time: a revealed and surviving dominant TAN, currently valid certified continental control, global Chaos of at least 1000, the applicable scenario setting enabled, and permission from the shared world-end manager.
A stale achievement flag from a lost continent is insufficient.
A renamed or independent civil-war splinter is not automatically the dominant Tuvan host.

TAN's player receives the option to begin the final campaign once these requirements are valid.
The AI commits when continental control and the practical opening of its global preparation are secure.
The **Tannu Tuva, Great Power** route gives a normal narrative connection to this point, but completion of an unrelated optional focus cannot replace or defeat the core world-end conditions.

The second super-event announces the decision to pursue the rest of the world.
It does not announce that the oceans have already been crossed or that every surviving government has surrendered.
The global branch becomes available in the same committed transition.

If another scenario already owns a mutually exclusive world-end state, TAN retains its ordinary conquest campaign and a clear blocked condition.
This event cannot take over another scenario by setting a separate private “world end” flag.
Once the Tuvan global campaign has validly begun, a later Chaos drop below 1000 does not undo it or refund its preparations.

## Continental resources become global capability

TAN receives access to a one-time continental mobilization program with an explicit world-end origin.
This program can provide additional manpower, reserve equipment, construction assistance, convoys, aircraft, and training capacity based on the surviving continental resource base.
These are scenario rewards for an earned continent, recorded separately from earlier foreign donations.
They do not retroactively make a weak sponsor ledger look strong.

The program must still distinguish finished equipment from promised production and usable naval facilities from empty coastal land.
The largest naval assets come through functioning shipyards and timed construction support.
Ships do not appear in an inland capital or in an enemy-controlled port.
Additional manpower is bounded by the governed population and the campaign's existing manpower rules.
A continent-wide mobilization is not permission to invent unlimited recruits after losing the continent.

Follow-up reinforcement opportunities require retained resources and actual operational progress.
The first global mobilization grant never repeats on a new continent, a recertification, a reload, or a cosmetic country change.
An additional continent expands the real economy and strategic position through ordinary play.

## Reaching the sea

A functioning first coastline must have controlled ports and a usable route to the military economy, either on land or through a verified alternative supply connection.
If TAN's first certified continent lacks an accessible port under its own or loyal-subject control, the global tree begins with securing one through real conquest.
A naval focus cannot make leased foreign access equivalent to permanent sovereign shipyards.

**A Navy, Apparently** commits the government to ocean warfare.
**Tuvan Shipyards** develops actual coastal facilities and an appropriate naval research and production base.
A first fleet prioritizes transport protection and the chosen theater.
Large prestige ships are optional investments after the practical sea route exists.

**Across Every Ocean** and **The Tuvan Marines** add invasion preparation, transport capacity, native marine formations, and overseas sustainment.
They still need fuel, aircraft or naval cover where appropriate, working ports, and material replacements.
An invasion order is not automatic naval supremacy.

## Global enemies

**No Border Required** removes the land-adjacency restriction on selecting campaign opponents.
**Every Country Is Our Neighbor Now** establishes a global conquest policy.
Countries are organized into practical theaters and war blocs so that TAN can pursue reachable enemies and prepare the next sea route.
An unreachable island can delay the final victory.
It does not become exempt because the AI has no current path.

The player can select a theater priority and choose between supported operational approaches.
The policy still requires the eventual conquest or actual subordination of every remaining independent government.
Former allies receive a real submission choice when the global campaign reaches them.
Acceptance changes the diplomatic relationship to actual subordination.
Refusal leaves an independent opponent to be addressed through the campaign.
Their donor history changes the reaction, not the definition of world victory.

## World victory

The final check requires the removal or subordination of all organized sovereign opposition with a functioning territorial position on the supported map.
A friendly independent state, a surviving independent microstate, or a hostile government holding a remote island prevents completion.
A government in exile that still maintains an effective independent territorial war effort is not dismissed by its cosmetic location.
An exile shell without an independent territorial position does not create an impossible hidden target.

Every counted subordinate must be in an actual dependency chain under TAN and remain loyal.
Equal allies, nominal diplomatic support, and governments in active rebellion do not qualify.
An ungoverned or hostile controlled province cannot disappear from the check through a destroyed owner tag.
The installed map's playable organized-country space defines the campaign's practical global scope.

**The Last "Tannu What?"** can complete the final settlement only after this check succeeds.
The outcome records the conquered world and connects it to the actual aid operation that helped fund the beginning.
It stops repeat submission events, target generation, and one-time scenario rewards.

## Defeat during the global campaign

Loss of TAN's independence, capitulation of the dominant Tuvan host, or destruction of its effective state can end the scenario as a Tuvan defeat.
A temporary naval setback or loss of one continent does not automatically remove every Tuvan army.
Losses instead remove the control, ports, output, and support that those positions actually provided.

The shared world-end manager receives the defeat outcome and applies the project's normal aftermath.
This package does not invent a universal restoration of old borders, resurrect every conquered tag, or decide that all other scenarios resume regardless of their own rules.
Surviving political settlements, independent resistance, and the victors' peace terms determine the map.
