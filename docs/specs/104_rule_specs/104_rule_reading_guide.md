## Key design decisions

The baseline changes one fresh rule.
Evolution I sometimes changes a second rule.
Evolution II admits previously touched rules when another valid non-default destination exists.
Evolution III scales to a requested two through five rules with current Chaos.
Evolution IV requests a larger mixed batch, reaching seven or eight when Chaos and the revisable footprint support it.
Actual batches shorten when the available compatible pool is smaller.

Every destination differs from both default and current.
A binary rule at its sole non-default option is exhausted even after revisions unlock.
The later stages therefore require genuine multi-option native rules, and the base-game acceptance targets explicitly test that breadth.

No-op candidates do not count as changes.
Clean zero-success requests do not consume a successful occurrence.
An ambiguous native write stops further Rule mutations and retains a durable incident record until verified reconciliation.
Every successful report uses immutable old/new results, preserving its meaning even if another firing occurs before the player reads it.

The original planning record reports all 132 supplied text-file copies read across its parent and three agents before substantive research and specification writing.
Repeated archive copies were read separately.
Clipped read attempts were recovered in full before being credited.
Installed engine documents, runtime validation, generated assets, and a certified implementation are not claimed.
