# Event 044 Yakub Returns research notes

## Research purpose

The research pass establishes historical and doctrinal anchors for a fictional high-chaos event. It does not turn historical organizations into a direct script for the alternate-history faction. The design borrows institutional forms, political conflicts, geographic anchors, and competing traditions. It avoids assigning fictional extremist actions to real people.

## Nation of Islam background

The Nation of Islam began in Detroit in 1930 under Wallace Fard Muhammad. The Detroit origin supports Michigan as the preferred opening state and makes an industrial urban setting stronger than a random American state.

Federal investigation and surveillance form part of the historical record. This supports a counterintelligence-centered American response, including infiltration, informants, targeted financial disruption, and the risk that a failed raid creates martyrs.

The movement developed a message of racial independence and a durable institutional model. Later Nation of Islam institutions included mosques, schools, security organizations, publications, farms, businesses, and real estate. The event adapts those forms into parallel schools, food distribution, local defense, businesses, courts, political committees, media, and welfare networks.

The Nation of Islam's own published program includes a demand for a separate state or territory. This supports negotiated territorial settlement as a plausible political outcome inside the movement's own historical logic.

Nation of Islam theology differs substantially from mainstream Sunni and Shia Islam. The event must preserve this distinction. Muslim-majority governments, Muslim scholars, Black Muslim communities, and anti-colonial organizations receive independent response paths. They may reject Yakubite theology, cooperate tactically, recognize a state for geopolitical reasons, or oppose the movement.

## Yakub doctrine

The Yakub narrative belongs to Nation of Islam mythology and theology. Academic treatments describe it as a racial creation narrative, a reversal of racist pseudo-scientific hierarchies, a theodicy explaining Black suffering, and a doctrine outside mainstream Islam.

The event treats Yakub as an anomalous character who claims the narrative is literal. His unexplained identity, apparent knowledge, disappearance, survival, or unusual physical evidence may remain mysterious. None of those signs proves any claim about the biological nature or moral character of a real racial group.

The implementation should avoid meme imagery associated with Yakub. His portrait should present a serious fictional political-religious leader in a 1930s setting. It should avoid grotesque proportions, mockery, caricature, pseudo-scientific diagrams, or visual claims about race.

## Interwar Black internationalism

Interwar Black politics contained several competing traditions. They included separatism, integration, labor organization, religious movements, Pan-Africanism, anti-colonial nationalism, Black internationalism, Ethiopianism, and socialist currents. This diversity is central to Evolution II.

The Italian invasion of Ethiopia became a major rallying point for Black activists across borders. C. L. R. James and other organizers linked Ethiopia to wider anti-colonial struggle. The event uses Ethiopia as an independent political and symbolic actor. Ethiopia can reject Yakubite theology while cooperating against colonial powers.

Négritude developed through Black students from French colonies in Paris. It supplies a French imperial and metropolitan response based on culture, anti-colonial identity, and intellectual politics. A Négritude-inspired movement should never become a simple Yakubite clone.

The Frente Negra Brasileira was founded in Sao Paulo in 1931 and placed strong emphasis on education, cooperation, legal defense, and political organization. It supplies a Brazilian civic and institutional route that can borrow movement discipline while rejecting racial theology.

Rastafari developed in 1930s Jamaica through Ethiopianism, colonial oppression, religious interpretation, and repatriation ideas. A Caribbean route can treat Yakub as a rival claimant, a tactical ally, or a false prophet. It should retain its own religious logic.

West African labor and youth politics grew through strikes, press networks, urban organizations, and anti-colonial campaigns. These conditions support local movements that use Yakubite aid while retaining their own leadership.

South African politics requires careful period treatment. Formal apartheid begins in 1948, but segregation, dispossession, labor control, and white minority rule predate it. The local route should read current laws, government ideology, repression, and colonial status rather than assuming the same legal phase in every start date.

## Historical figures

Real Nation of Islam leaders, civil rights organizers, Pan-African intellectuals, Ethiopian officials, Brazilian activists, and religious leaders should not be assigned fictional allegiance to Yakub's extremist branches without direct historical support. The event can reference real institutions, debates, writings, and movements. Major route leadership should use fictional characters or institutional councils.

## Research boundaries for implementation

The final localisation pass should verify names, dates, organizations, slogans, quotations, and religious terminology before use. Super-event text and audio require their own research handoff. No source-dependent quotation, slogan, lyric, scripture excerpt, or cultural remark in this planning package is final localisation.
