# Event 044 Yakub Returns

## Catalog identity

- Event ID: `44`
- Event name: Yakub Returns
- Replaces: Space Race
- Type: Minor Fire-Once
- Status before implementation: To Be Reworked
- Minimum Chaos level: 2
- Cluster: None

## Playable promise

A man calling himself Yakub appears in the United States and claims that the Nation of Islam's racial creation narrative is literal history. He speaks as the scientist and founder described by the doctrine, presents himself as a living authority, and begins assembling a movement around racial separation, political sovereignty, religious obedience, and the removal of existing political hierarchies.

The first campaign is an American political crisis. The movement grows through speeches, local chapters, mutual aid, religious organization, security groups, businesses, media, and the government's response. The player can contain it through intelligence, reduce the conditions that sustain it, tolerate a legal separatist movement, negotiate a territorial settlement, support state formation, or drive the movement underground through repression.

Yakub is a real game character with a persistent status. His arrest, imprisonment, death, disappearance, discredit, appointment of a successor, or choice to remain a symbolic founder changes the event. The movement never continues as if its founder's condition were irrelevant.

The event can end peacefully at baseline. It can also leave a legal movement, underground network, autonomous compact, or breakaway state that remains available for later evolutions.

## Normal eligibility and American host

The normal event first seeks the United States.

A viable American host must be an ordinary human government with a valid homeland state, a functioning capital, and authority over at least one suitable urban or industrial state. Player control never invalidates the target.

Michigan is the preferred opening state when the host controls it. Detroit gives the event a historically grounded origin and a strong industrial setting.

When Michigan is unavailable, the event uses a curated fallback among American urban and industrial centers. The fallback should favor states containing a major city, industry, rail access, a sizable civilian population, and enough political control to support an open movement. Illinois, Ohio, Pennsylvania, New York, New Jersey, Maryland, Georgia, Alabama, and Louisiana form the main backup pool.

When the United States no longer exists, the dominant ordinary American successor controlling Michigan can become the host. If that successor lacks Michigan, the event may use the strongest valid American successor controlling another approved center. The event remains unavailable when no ordinary American polity can support the crisis.

Foreign occupiers do not become the normal host merely because they occupy Detroit. The event can wait, use another American center, or remain unavailable.

An American government that has become an actual nonhuman country is invalid. A normal human American successor created by another Chaos event remains valid unless its own design makes ordinary civilian politics impossible.

## The first appearance

The opening presents a public appearance, not a secret report.

Yakub addresses a crowd in the selected city. He identifies himself, gives a compressed account of the doctrine, claims a right to organize, and declares that existing governments have lost moral authority. Journalists, local residents, religious figures, police, labor organizers, intelligence officers, Black political movements, churches, and Muslim communities begin reacting immediately.

The first event establishes five facts for the player:

1. Yakub is a persistent character.
2. His racial claims are his doctrine and propaganda.
3. He has no confirmed ordinary past.
4. A movement already exists around him.
5. Government action can change the movement's form.

The event does not decide whether Yakub is an ancient scientist, an anomalous human, a supernatural arrival, or an impossible political claimant. It also does not validate his account of any racial group.

The opening should offer strategic stances instead of resolving the crisis in one click.

The American government may:

- begin surveillance and intelligence collection
- allow monitored public organization
- open contact with moderate organizers
- announce a material reform response
- prepare an arrest
- quietly encourage the movement when the player intends to create the New Nation

The initial stance changes available decisions, AI preferences, public expectations, and the first movement incidents. It does not lock every later choice.

## Yakub as a character

Yakub receives a complete character lifecycle.

### Public founder

This is the default status.

Yakub can speak, travel, appoint organizers, negotiate, direct institutions, and lead a future state. His presence improves recruitment and gives the movement a clear ideological center.

### Monitored founder

The government allows public activity and maintains surveillance.

This status lowers the movement's freedom of action without creating a martyr. Strong intelligence services can identify financial links, local chapters, foreign contacts, and planned territorial moves.

### Detained founder

Yakub is imprisoned or held under another legal authority.

Detention removes his direct movement actions. It can reduce Influence when the arrest is credible, lawful, and supported by intelligence. It can raise Influence when the arrest looks arbitrary, violent, or politically panicked.

The movement may treat him as a prisoner-prophet. A detained Yakub can still appoint a successor through lawyers, visitors, smuggled statements, or a public trial.

### Underground founder

Yakub avoids arrest or abandons public activity.

The movement loses open speeches and legal organizing. It gains secrecy, cell discipline, and resistance to simple suppression. Intelligence decisions become more important.

### Missing founder

Yakub disappears without a confirmed death.

Competing leaders claim instructions, visions, private letters, or authority to interpret his doctrine. Personalist unity weakens. Institutional routes can become stronger.

A rare high-chaos return may occur after a meaningful absence. His unchanged appearance or unexplained knowledge can raise Influence. The event still avoids treating racial claims as proven.

### Dead founder

Yakub can die through failed arrest, assassination, war, natural causes, another event, or a later terminal conflict.

His death creates a bounded martyr surge when the movement remains active. The effect is temporary and cannot repeat.

Succession depends on prior institutions, route, designated heirs, International leadership, and military control.

### Discredited founder

Yakub loses public confidence through failed predictions, exposed corruption, internal contradiction, abandonment of followers, or a successful public challenge.

Discredit weakens literalist and personalist routes. It does not erase the economic, political, or anti-colonial grievances that helped the movement grow.

Civic, secular, internationalist, or successor-led branches can reinterpret the founding story and continue.

## Yakubite Influence

`Yakubite Influence` is the only persistent public value during the American crisis.

It ranges from 0 to 100.

The number represents the movement's ability to recruit, organize, fund institutions, protect members, spread doctrine, influence officials, mobilize supporters, and challenge government authority.

The value appears with a qualitative stage, a trend, the next important threshold, and a concise explanation of the strongest current causes.

The player should never need to memorize a component ledger.

### Influence stages

| Range | Working stage label | Public meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 to 19 | Fringe | Small circles, limited public credibility, weak institutions |
| 20 to 39 | Organized | Durable chapters, regular meetings, fundraising, media, local recruitment |
| 40 to 59 | Entrenched | Independent services, trained organizers, protected finances, strong local presence |
| 60 to 79 | Parallel Authority | The movement performs functions normally controlled by government and contests local obedience |
| 80 to 100 | Secession Crisis | Territorial control, armed defense, mass defection, or negotiated sovereignty becomes possible |

Thresholds change decisions and incidents. Influence should move gradually enough that the player can react. Large jumps require visible causes.

### Hidden inputs that raise Influence

The event can use many hidden inputs while exposing one public total.

Important positive inputs include:

- severe unemployment or an active economic crisis
- low national stability
- war losses and visible military failure
- state damage, shortages, housing pressure, and municipal collapse
- discrimination and repression represented by event history, occupation policy, camp or repression systems, and relevant national conditions
- violent police action
- a failed or unlawful arrest
- a public martyr
- successful Yakub speeches
- chapter growth
- independent schools and relief networks
- community defense success
- movement control of businesses or media
- foreign money, organizers, equipment, or recognition
- poor government intelligence
- corruption or brutality exposed inside the response
- rival political institutions losing public confidence

The design must not create a racial population percentage or treat every Black resident as a movement supporter.

### Hidden inputs that lower Influence

Important negative inputs include:

- effective infiltration
- legal and credible prosecution
- financial disruption tied to actual evidence
- material reforms that address current grievances
- competent municipal services
- employment and housing relief
- public debate that exposes doctrinal contradictions
- independent Black political, religious, labor, and civic movements offering another route
- mainstream Muslim rejection of the doctrine
- internal corruption
- failed movement promises
- leadership schism
- loss of foreign support
- defeat of armed cells
- a negotiated settlement that removes the immediate grievance without rewarding violence
- Yakub's discredit

### Influence movement rules

- Routine monthly movement should be modest.
- Decisions can create stronger temporary pressure.
- The same incident should not apply its change twice.
- A high Influence state should not collapse from one cheap decision.
- Repression can reduce open organization and increase underground strength at the same time.
- Material reform takes time and costs civilian capacity.
- Intelligence works best before the movement reaches Parallel Authority.
- Military force becomes more effective against open territorial control and less effective against dispersed political networks.
- Influence does not equal popular approval. A disciplined minority can have high Influence through institutions and strategic control.

## Local chapter states

The event tracks local presence through qualitative state statuses. These are map and decision targets, not extra public meters.

Suggested statuses:

- Watched
- Organized
- Institutional
- Parallel Authority
- Contested
- Secured by Government
- Integrated into Settlement

A state can advance through organizing, relief, business networks, local officials, armed defense, and government failure.

A state can regress through infiltration, successful reform, loss of leadership, financial collapse, military defeat, or a negotiated settlement.

The decision category highlights active states. The player sees the current status and the next local risk. Raw local numeric scores remain hidden.

## American crisis presentation

The United States receives one ordinary decision category.

The category includes:

- a static category picture
- the Influence meter
- the current qualitative stage
- Yakub's status
- the most important trend causes
- one or two active missions
- three to five primary decisions for the current phase
- state highlights for active chapters
- concise links to the current government approach

The category changes by phase. Old decisions disappear when they no longer fit.

A full separate scripted GUI is unnecessary. The core problem is a single pressure value with targeted state actions.

## Phase I decisions

Phase I covers Fringe and Organized Influence.

The government's main question is whether to learn, reform, tolerate, or escalate.

### Map the chapters

An intelligence action that identifies active states and movement contacts.

Costs and requirements:

- intelligence capacity
- time
- a risk of exposure

Success:

- reveals state statuses
- improves later infiltration
- lowers the risk of a failed arrest

Failure:

- movement security improves
- Influence can rise through a surveillance scandal

### Place local informants

A targeted infiltration action against one active state.

Costs and requirements:

- intelligence investment
- a valid active chapter
- a cooldown for the same state

Success:

- slows local organization
- can expose finances or planned violence
- improves prosecution quality

Failure:

- creates a public infiltration scandal
- raises local mistrust
- can push the chapter underground

### Open municipal talks

A civic action with local organizers and community representatives.

Costs and requirements:

- political capacity
- functioning local government
- no recent uncontrolled crackdown

Success:

- creates a reform or legal-organization path
- lowers Influence when the talks produce concrete results
- can split moderate organizers from hardliners

Failure:

- raises Influence if the government offers nothing material
- strengthens Yakub's claim that negotiation is useless

### Emergency employment and housing program

A material reform action.

Costs and requirements:

- civilian factory burden
- time
- local construction or public spending

Success:

- lowers hidden grievance pressure
- improves state recovery
- can strengthen rival civic organizations

Failure or neglect:

- wastes capacity
- produces little effect when corruption or local collapse remains high

### Permit monitored organization

A legal-tolerance action.

Costs and requirements:

- accepts short-term public organizing
- requires a government willing to tolerate the movement

Success:

- keeps chapters visible
- lowers underground growth
- opens a peaceful legal outcome

Risk:

- allows the movement to build institutions when government monitoring is weak

### Prepare an arrest

This action begins evidence collection and operational planning.

It does not arrest Yakub immediately.

Costs and requirements:

- intelligence
- local security preparation
- evidence or a high crisis threshold

The player should understand that an early rushed arrest is dangerous.

## Phase II decisions

Phase II covers Entrenched Influence.

The movement has durable institutions. The government's main question is whether to compete, regulate, negotiate, or dismantle.

### Audit movement finance

Targets businesses, donations, and foreign transfers.

Success can weaken institution growth and expose illegal support.

A broad asset seizure without evidence raises Influence and can strengthen the legal defense network.

### Support independent community institutions

The government funds or protects organizations that reject Yakub's authority.

The action lowers doctrinal recruitment when the rival institution has local credibility. It can still increase wider Black political mobilization or anti-colonial pressure.

The design should use researched period-appropriate organizations and generic local bodies. It should avoid presenting all Black politics as a contest between the government and Yakub.

### Regulate schools and security groups

The government sets legal standards for education, arms, training, and local patrols.

A credible legal framework can reduce armed growth.

Discriminatory or selective enforcement creates a grievance and increases underground support.

### Offer a legal separatist charter

The government recognizes the movement as a lawful political and religious organization under strict limits.

This action can produce a stable legal outcome when Influence is moderate and armed institutions remain weak.

The charter should preserve future political conflict. It is not a free permanent removal of the event.

### Convene a constitutional commission

The government prepares a territorial or autonomy settlement.

The commission takes time, uses political capacity, and may require state-level support.

Hardliners on both sides can sabotage it.

A successful commission opens the Negotiated Compact path.

### Decapitation operation

A high-risk effort to arrest Yakub and several senior organizers at once.

The outcome depends on intelligence, local chapter strength, evidence, security preparation, and prior repression.

Possible outcomes:

- clean detention
- escape and underground movement
- public trial
- martyrdom
- wrongful raid and scandal
- fragmented leadership
- seizure of records
- violent local resistance

The option is never a guaranteed remove button.

## Phase III decisions

Phase III covers Parallel Authority and Secession Crisis.

The movement controls institutions, territory, weapons, or local obedience.

### Secure rail hubs and arsenals

A timed state objective.

The government must hold named infrastructure and place supplied units in the affected states.

Success:

- prevents equipment capture
- reduces the strength of a breakaway
- preserves federal mobility

Failure:

- movement forces gain equipment and a stronger starting army
- nearby states can advance toward Contested status

### Federalize local security

The government replaces unreliable local command with national control.

Costs and requirements:

- command capacity
- tied-down divisions
- equipment and transport
- stability risk

Success:

- improves control of a target region

Risk:

- heavy presence raises local tension
- unreliable units can defect
- excessive force can create deaths and Condemnation through shared systems

### Protect municipal government

A state mission that keeps local authorities, courts, police, and public services functioning.

It requires supply, units, and political support.

Success can reverse Parallel Authority without indiscriminate violence.

### Negotiate the territorial compact

The government and movement define borders, citizenship, debt, trade, infrastructure, military limits, property, guarantees, and minority protections.

Possible results:

- autonomous region inside the United States
- sovereign one-state district
- multi-state compact
- demilitarized settlement
- federation with shared infrastructure
- failed talks followed by secession

The agreement must create a playable state when sovereignty is granted.

### Launch a general suppression campaign

A last-resort campaign against movement territory and institutions.

Costs and risks:

- multiple divisions
- equipment
- stability
- local damage
- high martyr and underground risk
- Condemnation when abuses become public
- possible civil war

The action is effective against open territorial control. It performs poorly against dispersed legal chapters.

### Recognize the New Nation

A support or settlement action for a player who intends to create the Yakubite state.

It requires a viable territorial package and a clear settlement.

The United States may receive guarantees, trade rights, debt arrangements, military limitations, or a later reconciliation route.

## Active missions

Only the most relevant missions should appear.

### Establish the truth about Yakub

A medium-duration intelligence and public-inquiry mission.

Objectives can include:

- intelligence network progress
- access to local records
- public testimony
- scientific and religious review
- protection of witnesses

Success can reveal an ordinary political vulnerability, a contradiction, or an unexplained absence of records.

The mission never proves racial biology.

### Keep the federal corridor open

A medium or hard mission during Parallel Authority.

The player must keep the capital connected to supply through named rail and urban states.

Success preserves authority and prevents a regional cascade.

Failure strengthens the breakaway package.

### Prevent a martyr crisis

A mission attached to detention or trial.

The player must maintain lawful process, avoid uncontrolled violence, and keep local services stable.

Success reduces Influence.

Failure creates a one-time martyr surge.

### Complete the constitutional commission

A timed political mission for the negotiated route.

The player must hold talks, prevent sabotage, maintain public order, and settle the territorial package.

Success opens the compact.

Failure raises hardline strength and can move states toward secession.

## Government approach memory

The crisis remembers the pattern of government action.

Suggested approach memories:

- Intelligence-led containment
- Material reform
- Legal accommodation
- Negotiated separation
- Hard suppression
- Covert support

The memory changes event tone, AI, available follow-ups, public trust, and the founding conditions of any new state.

The player can change course. Repeated reversals reduce credibility.

## Baseline outcomes

### Quiet discredit

Conditions:

- Influence falls into the lowest band
- Yakub loses credibility
- no durable institution remains
- no martyr crisis occurred

Result:

- the active crisis ends
- the event records a contained outcome
- a small legacy may remain only for rare high-chaos callbacks
- the strongest personalist evolutions remain unavailable unless a later resurgence occurs

### Monitored remnant

Conditions:

- Influence remains low
- some chapters survive
- the government chose intelligence or legal oversight

Result:

- the decision category closes or becomes dormant
- later evolutions can reactivate institutions
- Yakub may remain free, detained, missing, or dead

### Legal separatist movement

Conditions:

- Influence stabilizes in the middle bands
- violence remains limited
- the government accepts legal organization

Result:

- the movement operates openly
- it can contest politics, build lawful institutions, and seek a constitutional settlement
- the United States avoids immediate war
- Evolution I can turn the legal network into a state-building project

### Underground network

Conditions:

- open organization is suppressed
- leadership and cells survive
- Influence remains meaningful

Result:

- public Influence can fall while hidden institutional strength remains
- intelligence and local-state events continue
- later evolutions can produce a more disciplined and radical movement

### Negotiated compact

Conditions:

- Influence is high enough to sustain a territorial program
- a viable contiguous region exists
- the constitutional mission succeeds
- both sides accept a settlement

Result:

- an autonomous region or sovereign New Nation forms
- borders, capital, military, debt, trade, and guarantees follow the agreement
- the player can switch to the new state
- the United States receives a reconciliation, containment, or revanchist follow-up according to its government and the agreement

### Breakaway and civil war

Conditions:

- Influence reaches the highest band
- one or more states reach Contested status
- talks fail or the government chooses force
- the movement has enough institutions and equipment to form a viable country

Result:

- the New Nation forms from valid territory
- local units and a bounded share of federal forces defect
- war begins
- the new state receives the full country package
- ordinary war, death, occupation, migration, famine, and Condemnation systems apply

### Fragmented collapse

Conditions:

- Yakub is removed
- no successor controls the movement
- institutions compete
- Influence remains too weak for statehood

Result:

- local factions split
- some chapters become legal parties
- some disappear
- some join rival movements
- a small extremist remnant may survive
- the event ends without creating one artificial unified successor

## Rare baseline incidents

### The man without records

A successful investigation finds no credible life history before the appearance.

This raises public curiosity and weakens simple discredit attempts. It does not prove ancient identity.

### The failed prediction

Yakub makes a public claim about an imminent event or political outcome and fails.

Opponents gain a discredit opportunity. Hardliners may reinterpret the failure and split from moderate organizers.

### The public debate

Black religious leaders, Muslim scholars, scientists, labor organizers, and civic figures challenge the doctrine in public.

A strong debate can reduce literalist belief while leaving separatist or anti-colonial politics intact.

### The factory chapters

An industrial crisis produces rapid organizing in plants, rail yards, depots, and unemployed districts.

Relief and labor decisions become more effective than police action.

### The prison network

Detention creates a new recruitment network among prisoners and their families.

This incident becomes more likely after mass arrests and poor prison conditions.

### The intercepted treasury

Intelligence finds a large source of money, weapons, or foreign support.

The government can seize it lawfully, expose it, follow the network, or use it to feed a controlled channel.

A reckless seizure can create scandal.

### The community kitchen

A movement relief institution prevents hunger or local collapse.

Influence rises because the institution solves a real problem.

The government can compete through services, cooperate, regulate, or shut it down.

### The missing founder

Yakub disappears during a crisis.

The event opens succession, rival revelation, intelligence, and possible return incidents.

### The unexplained return

Available only after a meaningful absence and higher Chaos.

Yakub returns with no visible aging or with knowledge unavailable to ordinary investigators.

The event presents the anomaly directly and leaves doctrine unverified.

## Other countries during baseline

Other countries receive limited news and diplomatic reactions.

They may:

- dismiss the event
- condemn the doctrine
- praise racial separation
- support Black self-determination
- contact the movement covertly
- prepare intelligence monitoring
- debate colonial implications
- distinguish tactical anti-colonial cooperation from theological agreement

Foreign governments do not receive a full local crisis until Evolution II.

## Baseline end-state memory

The event records:

- Yakub's status
- highest Influence reached
- government approach
- strongest local states
- legal, underground, compact, breakaway, or discredited outcome
- martyr history
- institutional survival
- foreign support
- designated successor
- public atrocity or Condemnation history
- whether the United States and the movement reached a peaceful settlement

These facts control later evolutions and reactions.

## Baseline AI intent

The American AI should seek the cheapest credible stable outcome available under its government and current war state.

A stable democratic government with strong intelligence prefers infiltration, material reform, and legal containment.

An authoritarian government prefers surveillance and suppression. It should still avoid a premature mass crackdown when Influence is low and the expected martyr risk is high.

A government losing a major war prioritizes arsenals, railways, and unit loyalty.

An AI incapable of holding the affected region may negotiate a compact.

An AI should support state formation only under a route, ideology, or strategic situation that makes the choice coherent.

The AI must react to Yakub's actual status. Founder-only actions disappear when he is dead, missing, detained, or discredited.
