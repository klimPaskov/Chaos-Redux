# Event 044 specification part 3: The Diaspora Awakens and Yakubite International

## Evolution II

Working evolution name: The Diaspora Awakens

Chaos requirement: 600 or more

Evolution II expands Event 44 beyond the United States.

The evolution does not assume that every Black political movement accepts Yakub, Nation of Islam theology, racial separatism, or American leadership.

Foreign organizations can:

- adopt the full doctrine
- accept Yakub as a political founder and reject the racial creation story
- borrow separatist institutions
- cooperate tactically against colonial or discriminatory governments
- accept aid and preserve local leadership
- reject Yakub while growing through the wider crisis
- oppose the movement as a rival religious, civic, labor, nationalist, or revolutionary project

This range of responses is the core of the evolution.

Evolution activation gives no Chaos by itself. Concrete chapters, autonomous zones, state formation, regional spread, and violent conflict create the event-owned milestones.

## Opening conditions

When Event 44 has not fired, Evolution II changes the eventual first appearance.

The American opening begins with foreign correspondence, organizers, publications, and several overseas sympathizers or rivals already active.

When the American movement was destroyed, foreign groups can still use Yakub's story and organizational model. They do not require the founder to remain alive.

When a New Nation exists, it becomes the main source of aid and diplomacy.

When no state exists, an underground American secretariat, legal organization, imprisoned founder, successor council, or foreign movement can become the transnational center.

## Foreign target model

The spread system uses a curated regional registry.

It should never estimate racial populations through a fake universal number.

The registry identifies countries and colonial territories where the event has a plausible political context.

Target weights use:

- colonial status
- segregation or repression conditions
- instability
- unemployment and economic crisis
- anti-colonial activity
- war losses
- movement relations
- press and communication access
- ports and diaspora routes
- existing Event 44 institutions
- government intelligence
- government reform
- local rival movements
- player control
- foreign aid

Special Chaos countries and actual nonhuman countries remain invalid.

A player-controlled valid country remains eligible.

## Foreign chapter lifecycle

Each foreign host receives one local `Yakubite Influence` value when the crisis becomes active there.

The value has the same 0 to 100 scale and qualitative stages as the American crisis.

The local meaning changes by region.

A high value can represent a full Yakubite movement, a broad anti-colonial coalition containing Yakubites, or a separatist government that uses Yakub as a symbol.

The public category must identify which form currently leads.

### Local movement identities

Suggested local identity states:

- Full Yakubite Doctrine
- Symbolic Yakubism
- Separatist Adaptation
- Tactical Anti-Colonial Alliance
- Independent Black Movement
- Religious Rejection
- State Suppression
- Fragmented Network

These are qualitative identities.

They can change through local events and decisions.

## Local government tools

Each active foreign host receives a compact decision category.

The category exposes:

- local Influence
- leading movement identity
- current Yakubite or local leader
- state highlights
- one or two missions
- three to five current actions

Action families include:

- intelligence mapping
- public debate
- anti-discrimination reform
- colonial concession
- labor and housing relief
- legal recognition
- support for a rival local movement
- chapter infiltration
- regulation of foreign funding
- negotiated autonomy
- targeted suppression
- military defense against a territorial breakaway

The exact mix depends on regime and region.

## Foreign movement tools

A player controlling a Yakubite or aligned state can:

- send organizers
- fund schools
- provide printing equipment
- send food and medicine
- finance legal defense
- supply intelligence
- send arms
- arrange volunteers
- sponsor a congress
- offer recognition
- negotiate federation
- demand doctrinal conformity
- guarantee local autonomy

Costs use factories, equipment, convoys, intelligence exposure, money-like political capacity, or diplomatic risk according to the action.

The action budget remains compact.

## Regional module: Caribbean

### Political foundation

The Caribbean module draws from colonial rule, Ethiopianism, Rastafari, labor politics, repatriation ideas, port networks, religious movements, and local nationalism.

### Main conflict

Yakub enters a region with its own religious and political claims.

Local leaders may treat him as:

- a rival prophet
- an American organizer
- a useful source of money and discipline
- an anti-colonial ally
- a false claimant
- a symbol of separation
- a threat to local sovereignty

### Institutional emphasis

- religious communities
- mutual aid
- port workers
- newspapers
- rural settlements
- repatriation societies
- food and transport networks

### Government response

Colonial authorities can surveil, deport organizers, regulate meetings, offer reforms, support rival religious leaders, or use force.

Independent governments can judge the movement through local politics and diplomacy.

### Outcomes

- legal religious movement
- independent anti-colonial party
- Yakubite port enclave
- rural autonomous community
- revolutionary island government
- tactical alliance with Ethiopia
- rejection of Yakub and continued Pan-African mobilization

### State formation

A state can form only from actual local control, a colonial collapse, or negotiated independence.

The result uses a local national identity and regional module.

It does not become a generic copy of the American New Nation.

## Regional module: other valid countries

Additional candidates can use shared modules only when their political and geographic context supports the event.

The player-controlled country may receive a special path when it belongs to an approved region or controls a relevant colonial territory.

A foreign breakaway requires:

- local Influence at a high stage
- one or more institutional states
- a viable capital
- contiguous territory or a valid island state
- military or negotiated control
- a local leader
- dynamic starting forces
- a local relationship with the International
- a valid tag or carrier
- local flags and portraits
- a shared core tree plus regional module

The breakaway can choose full doctrine, adaptation, tactical alliance, or rejection.

## Shared regional state tree

Foreign Event 44 states do not need a full unique tree of the American size.

They receive:

- an opening survival branch
- a local political convention
- one regional institution branch
- a defense branch
- an independence and recognition branch
- an International relationship branch
- a route-specific late game

Country-specific modules change names, leaders, decisions, AI, and local effects.

A long-lived major regional state can receive an expanded tree where its role justifies one.

## Evolution III

Working evolution name: Yakubite International

Chaos requirement: 800 or more

Evolution III turns the separate movements and states into a coordinated international system.

The International requires real members, institutions, leadership, and a geopolitical center.

It does not appear because Yakub exists.

## Formation requirements

Normal formation requires:

- one viable Yakubite or aligned state
- at least three durable organizations or states
- representation in at least two world regions
- an active leadership center
- no completed permanent discredit outcome that erased every movement
- enough diplomatic or organizational reach to hold a congress
- a successful formation event or decision chain

A stronger automatic formation may require three regions.

A player can attempt an earlier congress with lower odds of success.

## Founding congress

The congress defines:

- the anchor state
- leadership
- member rights
- doctrinal requirements
- military coordination
- economic aid
- intelligence sharing
- accession
- exit
- sanctions
- succession
- relations with independent movements
- treatment of members that reject literal doctrine

The congress can succeed, fail, split, or create a weak provisional International.

## Anchor selection

The strongest surviving Yakubite state is the default anchor.

Another state can become anchor when it has:

- more members
- higher recognition
- stronger institutions
- better access
- a successful congress
- a route built around International leadership

Yakub remains ideological founder when alive.

He does not automatically control the anchor government.

## International Cohesion

Once the International forms, the anchor state's main public international value becomes `International Cohesion`.

Cohesion ranges from 0 to 100.

It replaces the need to show domestic Yakubite Influence for the anchor state.

The value represents:

- member confidence
- shared command
- financial cooperation
- ideological agreement
- willingness to follow congress decisions
- confidence in leadership
- effectiveness of aid
- acceptance of succession

### Cohesion stages

| Range | Working stage label | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 to 19 | Fragmenting | Members ignore decisions, leave, or prepare rival blocs |
| 20 to 39 | Provisional | Limited cooperation and weak institutions |
| 40 to 59 | Functional | Regular aid, congresses, and political coordination |
| 60 to 79 | Integrated | Strong shared programs and military coordination |
| 80 to 100 | Unified | Federation, terminal readiness, or strong central command becomes possible |

Cohesion is the only persistent public International meter.

Member status appears qualitatively.

## Cohesion gains

- successful congresses
- fulfilled aid promises
- defensive victory
- shared relief
- fair member representation
- respected autonomy
- recognized succession
- successful mediation
- common enemies
- economic investment
- credible collective defense

## Cohesion losses

- forced doctrine
- failed aid
- abandoned members
- unilateral war
- supremacy
- leadership assassination
- unequal burdens
- member annexation
- exposure of atrocities
- economic exploitation
- failed succession
- anchor defeat
- incompatible foreign alliances

## International offices

### Secretariat

Maintains member lists, congresses, diplomacy, and succession.

### Relief bureau

Coordinates food, medicine, refugees, reconstruction, and famine response.

### Education and press bureau

Coordinates schools, publications, translations, and organizers.

### Intelligence bureau

Shares counterintelligence, foreign contacts, and covert support.

### Volunteer command

Coordinates military volunteers and training.

### Economic council

Coordinates investment, trade, industrial aid, and reconstruction.

### War council

Activates only during major conflict or terminal preparation.

The player should not manage six separate values. Offices exist as ideas, decisions, missions, and qualitative upgrade states.

## International decisions

### Call a congress

Addresses succession, doctrine, membership, war, or federation.

The action costs time, political capacity, and member confidence.

### Fund a member institution

Sends civilian capacity, equipment, or aid.

Success increases Cohesion and local resilience.

### Send organizers

Raises local chapter capability and creates intelligence exposure.

### Coordinate legal defense

Supports members under repression.

### Open an aid corridor

Uses convoys, trains, trucks, or access.

### Dispatch volunteers

Requires war, transport, equipment, and member approval.

### Share intelligence

Improves defense against infiltration and assassination.

### Mediate a member dispute

Prevents exit or civil war.

### Discipline an abusive member

Uses sanctions, suspension, or expulsion.

### Demand doctrinal conformity

Available to strict routes.

It can centralize the bloc and trigger schism.

### Propose federation

Requires high Cohesion and member approval.

It starts a formation process rather than annexing members instantly.

- Full member
- Autonomous member

## Member accession

Accession considers:

- local movement identity
- route
- sovereignty
- religion
- colonial status
- foreign relations
- current war
- prior aid
- Yakub's reputation
- the International's doctrine
- Condemnation
- member autonomy rules

Joining does not automatically change the country's ideology or leader.

## Member exit

Members can leave through:

- congress vote
- route conflict
- supremacy
- abandoned defense
- leadership dispute
- foreign pressure
- peace settlement
- state defeat
- doctrinal rejection

Exit can be peaceful, hostile, or produce a rival coalition.

## International forms

### Doctrinal International

A personalist anchor requires broad acceptance of Yakub's authority.

It is cohesive and smaller.

### Federal International

Member states keep domestic autonomy and share defense, diplomacy, and infrastructure.

It can become a Diaspora Federation.

### Revolutionary International

Members coordinate anti-colonial revolution, volunteers, and political training.

It accepts secular and doctrinally independent movements.

### Congress International

A broad coalition with weak central command and wide membership.

### Supremacist International

A hardline anchor demands racial hierarchy and political obedience.

It is powerful, unstable, and prone to member rebellion.

## International schism

A major dispute can create two or more blocs.

Schism causes:

- Cohesion collapse
- competing congresses
- member choice events
- leader and flag changes
- rival aid networks
- border conflict
- possible war

A schism does not delete every member or force all states into war.

## Evolution III success states

- stable International
- Diaspora Federation
- doctrinal bloc
- revolutionary alliance
- broad congress
- supremacist bloc
- divided rival Internationals
- failed congress and independent regional movements

The world-end route reads the actual result.
