# Event 044 specification part 2: The New Nation

## Evolution I

Working evolution name: The New Nation

Chaos requirement: 400 or more

Evolution I changes the event from a movement crisis into a state-building struggle.

When Event 44 has not fired, the evolution strengthens the eventual opening. Yakub begins with established organizers, financing, legal defense, trained security, and several local chapters. The United States still receives time to react. The opening Influence value starts higher and the first institution incidents arrive sooner.

When Event 44 is active, the movement begins creating parallel institutions in states where it already has an organized presence.

When the baseline crisis became dormant, surviving legal or underground chapters can reactivate.

When Yakub was killed, detained, missing, or discredited, the evolution uses the movement's actual successor structure. It never restores him by default.

Evolution activation gives no Chaos by itself. Chaos changes come from institutions reaching dual power, state formation, violence, negotiated settlement, or later international spread.

## The institutional program

The movement attempts to perform functions that government, markets, churches, unions, charities, and municipalities normally perform.

Institutional growth changes local chapter status and the strength of a future state. The player sees institutions through state descriptions, decisions, incidents, and map highlights. The event does not add a second national meter.

### Schools and political education

Schools provide doctrine, literacy, organization, leadership training, and a durable youth network.

Possible forms include:

- religious schools
- political education circles
- vocational schools
- adult literacy programs
- officer and organizer schools
- local history and anti-colonial study groups

Government responses include accreditation, inspection, public-school investment, legal recognition, closure, infiltration, or partnership.

A school network strengthens long-term organization more than immediate armed power.

Hardline routes can turn schools into ideological control institutions. Civic routes can convert them into public schools. Congress routes can allow regional curricula.

### Food and relief networks

Food distribution, clinics, housing aid, funeral support, childcare, and emergency transport give the movement practical authority.

These institutions become stronger during depression, famine, war damage, migration pressure, or municipal collapse.

Relief actions interact with the shared Famine and Migration systems when those systems are active.

A government that competes through effective services can reduce Influence without repression.

A government that blocks relief during a real crisis creates a strong recruitment cause.

### Businesses and finance

The movement can create shops, farms, workshops, transport firms, newspapers, credit associations, and property networks.

These assets fund organizers and reduce dependence on hostile institutions.

Government actions can regulate, tax, audit, prosecute, cooperate with, or seize movement businesses.

Lawful action against actual crime lowers Influence. Arbitrary seizure raises it.

A future state inherits only assets and factories that exist in its controlled territory. Movement finance does not create free factories outside the map.

### Local courts and arbitration

Movement committees begin resolving disputes, debts, discipline, family conflicts, and business disagreements.

The first forms are voluntary arbitration.

Higher stages create compulsory local courts and direct conflict with state law.

Civic routes can integrate arbitration into a constitutional settlement.

Personalist and supremacist routes can centralize it into ideological courts.

### Community defense

Local defense groups guard meetings, businesses, schools, leaders, and neighborhoods.

They begin as security organizations.

They can become armed militias when violence, repression, war, or territorial control increases.

The government can license, regulate, disarm, infiltrate, tolerate, or fight them.

Community defense is the main source of local military cadres. It does not create a custom race-based unit.

### Press and communication

Newspapers, radio, public meetings, printing networks, couriers, and foreign correspondence spread the doctrine and coordinate chapters.

Press networks improve recruitment and international reach.

Censorship can slow public growth and strengthen underground organization.

A strong independent press can expose movement corruption or government abuses.

### Political committees

Local committees coordinate elections, petitions, strikes, negotiations, security, and territorial claims.

At high local status, a committee can become a parallel municipal authority.

The committee's composition shapes later route strength.

## Internal movement factions

Evolution I introduces a hidden faction contest. The player sees leading tendencies through events, leaders, institution choices, and the founding convention. Raw faction numbers stay hidden.

### Founder loyalists

They want Yakub to retain supreme doctrinal authority.

They favor personal rule, religious institutions, centralized appointments, and strict loyalty.

They lose strength when Yakub is discredited, absent, or unwilling to rule directly.

### Constitutional separatists

They want a recognized Black-led state with a constitution, negotiated borders, local autonomy, and durable relations with neighboring governments.

They gain strength through legal organizing, municipal talks, peaceful settlement, and strong civic institutions.

### Secular revolutionaries

They treat Yakub as a catalyst, symbol, or disputed founder and place political revolution above literal theology.

They gain strength through labor conflict, war, repression, military defection, and anti-colonial cooperation.

### Originalist supremacists

They accept the racial creation narrative as literal state doctrine and demand an inverted racial hierarchy.

They gain strength through martyrdom, severe repression, doctrinal schools, security organizations, and failed compromise.

They are an extremist faction with serious internal and external consequences.

### International congress faction

They want to connect the movement with independent Black-led governments, anti-colonial movements, diaspora organizations, labor groups, and religious currents.

They gain strength through foreign chapters, broad coalitions, relief work, and tactical cooperation with movements that reject the doctrine.

## Faction contest outcomes

The founding convention does not simply select the largest hidden faction.

The player can compromise, elevate a minority, accept a coalition, or allow a breakdown.

Possible results:

- one faction takes full control
- two factions form a coalition government
- Yakub remains founder while a council governs
- the military imposes an emergency directorate
- the movement fragments before statehood
- regional branches form separate governments
- a negotiated compact requires a federal structure
- hardliners reject a moderate settlement and continue underground

The selected political route should reflect the movement's history. A player may choose an unlikely route, but the transition carries costs, opposition, and leader changes.

## The territorial program

A territorial state requires political control, institutions, and a viable map.

The event never creates borders from a racial demographic estimate.

### Valid territorial proof

A state can join the founding package through one or more of these proofs:

- Parallel Authority or Contested status
- negotiated cession
- movement control of local government
- military defection and actual control
- successful local accession vote or congress
- federal collapse
- connected wartime occupation followed by settlement
- a valid autonomy agreement

A state with no movement institution, no local authority, and no actual control cannot be transferred merely because it belongs to a broad historical region.

### Contiguity and enclaves

The normal founding state should be contiguous.

Disconnected states can become autonomous members, claims, or later accession targets.

A negotiated district may begin as a one-state enclave only when it has secure supply, trade access, and guarantees.

The design should prefer a playable small country over a larger broken map.

### Formation paths

#### Negotiated sovereignty

The United States and movement agree on territory.

The new state begins with:

- more stability
- clearer borders
- trade and transit rights
- fewer starting units
- external guarantees or demilitarization
- a strong federal or congress faction
- a weaker martyr and hardline faction

#### Unilateral secession

The movement declares sovereignty from states under Parallel Authority.

The new state begins with:

- more militia
- uncertain recognition
- stronger personalist, revolutionary, or supremacist factions
- immediate military risk
- disputed borders
- a stronger need to secure railways and arsenals

#### Federal collapse

The American government loses control during civil war, invasion, or severe fragmentation.

The movement consolidates a region.

The new state begins with:

- mixed equipment
- defecting units
- weak administration
- several competing local leaders
- a larger territorial opportunity
- higher risk of internal fragmentation

#### Autonomous compact

The movement accepts internal autonomy without full independence.

The compact receives local institutions and decisions.

Later events can produce peaceful independence, reintegration, federation, or renewed conflict.

## Territorial packages

The country package matrix defines the full formation requirements.

The preferred package is a Great Lakes industrial compact centered on Detroit.

An Atlantic urban compact, Southern congress, or negotiated district becomes available when its region holds the strongest institution network.

Each package changes:

- capital selection
- starting economy
- supply problem
- military composition
- diplomatic access
- focus emphasis
- local event set
- United States response
- likely political route

## Formation event

State formation deserves a major news event.

The news direction should show civilian institutions, local flags, guarded streets, rail stations, public meetings, and the uncertain status of federal authority.

The event should mention the actual capital, settlement or secession path, Yakub's status, and whether war has begun.

The player controlling the United States receives a switch option when a sovereign state forms.

The player controlling another country receives a diplomatic reaction suitable to ideology, colonial status, religion, and existing relations.

## Human country classification

The formed state remains human.

It uses normal:

- civilian population
- Famine
- Migration
- occupation
- resistance
- compliance
- laws
- diplomacy
- war
- Deaths
- Condemnation
- economy
- research
- recruitment

It may receive a shared special Chaos-country marker where routing requires one. It must remain outside actual-nonhuman classification.

## Starting state package

The New Nation starts with a small number of deep problems.

### Disputed Revelation

The state exists because a movement built around Yakub reached political power.

Supporters disagree about whether he is a prophet, scientist, founder, symbol, fraud, or useful myth.

The idea gives recruitment and political energy.

It reduces recognition and increases route conflict.

The founding convention replaces it with one route-specific form.

### Parallel Institutions

Schools, businesses, relief groups, courts, committees, and defense organizations keep the state functioning.

They overlap, compete, and lack a single revenue or command system.

The idea supports local supply and organization.

It weakens central administration and tax collection.

The economy branch transforms it into a route-specific public institution.

### Improvised National Defense

Militias, defectors, local guards, and seized depots form the first army.

The state can defend cities and local ground.

It lacks unified command, equipment standards, training, and supply.

The defense branch transforms it into a route-specific military institution.

## Starting government

The provisional government depends on origin.

A negotiated compact begins with a constitutional commission or federal council.

A secession led by Yakub begins with the founder and an emergency council.

A secular military revolt begins with a revolutionary committee.

A fragmented collapse can begin with a temporary defense council.

The first political focuses hold a convention and select the durable route.

## Starting economy

The state inherits buildings, infrastructure, resources, and damage in its territory.

It also inherits movement businesses and local service institutions represented by decisions and ideas.

The founding package can create temporary burdens:

- interrupted trade
- divided tax authority
- refugee and migration pressure
- broken federal contracts
- rail bottlenecks
- equipment shortages
- recognition barriers
- emergency consumer needs

The player must solve these through the tree and decisions.

No route begins with a generic factory windfall.

## Starting technology

The state receives compatible researched technology from the American host or controlling predecessor.

The grant preserves the recipient's own research and respects mutually exclusive industry branches.

The state does not inherit every special event technology, secret project, or foreign technology without a valid connection.

Research slots and technology breadth should scale with formation date, territory, and the host's development.

## Starting land forces

The starting army scales from actual conditions.

Sources include:

- community defense groups
- trained local guards
- federal or state units stationed in participating territory
- movement-controlled depots
- veterans
- police or security defectors
- local military schools
- foreign volunteers already present

A one-state peaceful compact starts with a small defense force.

A regional wartime breakaway begins with more militia and a bounded share of defectors.

The whole American army never transfers.

The formation should remain capable of surviving the first months without becoming stronger than the United States by default.

## Land templates

The state begins with a small template set.

### Community defense brigade

A light infantry formation for urban and local defense.

It has low equipment cost and limited offensive power.

### National guard brigade

A trained infantry formation available when the state inherited officers and equipment.

It becomes the basis of the regular army.

### Mobile reserve

Available only when trucks and supply exist.

It provides local response and rail defense.

### Artillery group

Available only when the state secured arsenals or inherited federal formations.

It should not appear in every small compact.

Route branches can rename and upgrade these templates through training, doctrine, support companies, and equipment.

## Reinforcement decisions

The state can gain forces through action.

### Organize community defense

Consumes infantry equipment, manpower, training time, and local supply.

Raises a limited number of units from institutional states.

### Integrate defectors

Requires screening, officer capacity, equipment compatibility, and political trust.

Success converts defecting formations.

Failure can create disloyal units or a coup risk.

### Secure the depots

A state objective around actual arsenals, rail hubs, and supply nodes.

Success unlocks equipment and trained formations.

### Invite volunteer cadres

Requires an eligible foreign chapter or International member, route access, and transport.

Volunteer units remain bounded by the sender and current war.

### Build the national arsenal

Uses military factories, resources, and time.

It strengthens domestic production instead of granting free stockpiles.

### Demobilize the emergency militias

Available after peace.

It reduces supply burden and instability while returning manpower to the economy.

## Navy and air force

A state without a coastline receives no navy.

A coastal state can inherit a small patrol or transport force only when valid assets exist.

A state without aircraft receives no token air force.

An inherited airbase, pilots, and aircraft can produce a small reconnaissance, transport, or fighter wing.

The focus tree opens naval and air content only when these forces matter.

## State-building decisions

The new country receives a progressive decision category.

Early phase actions:

- secure the capital
- restore rail supply
- ration equipment
- integrate local committees
- negotiate emergency trade

Middle phase actions:

- standardize schools
- create a revenue service
- convert businesses into the chosen economic model
- regularize the army
- seek recognition
- integrate additional states

Late phase actions:

- support foreign chapters
- admit federal members
- create International offices
- coordinate volunteers
- prepare the terminal route

The category shows three to five primary actions per phase.

## The founding convention

The convention is the main route-selection event.

It reads:

- Yakub's status
- origin path
- strongest faction
- institutions built
- repression history
- martyr history
- foreign contacts
- war state
- negotiated guarantees
- military loyalty

The convention can choose among five political routes.

The options use distinct political voices and consequences.

The final text should not use generic ideology labels as the entire choice.

## Personal Revelation route

This route places Yakub or a designated messenger at the center of state authority.

### Political identity

The state treats the founder's revelation as the source of law and national purpose.

A council can administer daily government.

Yakub retains final ideological authority when alive and free.

### Main mechanics

- fast centralization
- Temple districts
- founder appointments
- religious courts
- disciplined public ceremonies
- a trained guard
- succession planning
- doctrinal standards for International members

### Strengths

- rapid authority recovery
- strong mobilization
- coherent leadership
- resistance to faction drift

### Costs

- poor recognition
- dependence on one leader
- conflict with civic and secular organizers
- limits on broad coalition membership
- succession crisis risk

### Route variants

Yakub can rule directly.

He can appoint a Designated Messenger.

He can remain symbolic while a Council of Temples governs.

A detained Yakub can lead through prison statements until release, death, or succession.

### End state

A religious commonwealth can lead a doctrinal International.

It can enter The Yakubite World through a route that demands ideological obedience without adopting the explicit supremacist constitution.

## Federal New Nation route

This route turns the movement's local institutions into a constitutional federation.

### Political identity

Local states, municipalities, and councils retain meaningful autonomy.

The federal government controls defense, foreign affairs, common infrastructure, and negotiated national law.

### Main mechanics

- constitutional congress
- member-state accession
- federal confidence
- shared revenue and rail authority
- negotiated integration
- recognition treaties
- autonomous regional modules

Federal confidence can remain a hidden derived state or a qualitative institution status. It should not become another permanent public meter unless implementation proves it changes a distinct decision.

### Strengths

- stability
- recognition
- gradual core integration
- peaceful expansion
- strong settlements
- member-state resilience

### Costs

- slow mobilization
- local vetoes
- fiscal disputes
- weak central coercion

### End state

The federation can become the anchor of a Diaspora Federation and a federal terminal variant.

## Black Republic route

This route creates a secular revolutionary republic.

### Political identity

The state recognizes Yakub as founder, catalyst, or disputed historical symbol.

Civil government, labor councils, veterans, and political committees control the state.

### Main mechanics

- republican constitution
- labor and municipal councils
- political education
- land or workplace reform
- people's defense force
- anti-colonial volunteer missions
- secular alliance building

### Strengths

- strong mobilization
- broad anti-colonial cooperation
- less dependence on Yakub
- easier relations with movements that reject the doctrine

### Costs

- conflict with religious loyalists
- economic disruption
- military faction risk
- ideological purges if the route radicalizes

### End state

The republic can lead a revolutionary Black International or join a broader anti-colonial coalition.

## Original Supremacy route

This route is covered in full in part 4.

Evolution I gives it a clear entry through hardline control of the convention.

The route embraces Black supremacy, literal racial doctrine, exclusionary citizenship, and authoritarian state power.

Its mechanical strength comes from coercive mobilization and central command.

Its costs include resistance, Condemnation, diplomatic isolation, schism, economic damage, and possible atrocities.

No modifier treats any race as biologically superior.

## Diaspora Congress route

This route gives the broad anti-colonial and internationalist faction control.

### Political identity

The state hosts a congress of movements, states, unions, religious groups, and political organizations.

Members may reject literal Yakubite doctrine.

Yakub can remain founder, disputed symbol, ceremonial leader, or retired figure.

### Main mechanics

- congress representation
- permanent secretariat
- regional autonomy
- cross-border aid
- cultural and educational exchange
- volunteer and intelligence coordination
- guarantees against forced doctrinal conformity

### Strengths

- broad coalition
- foreign recognition
- regional flexibility
- strong Evolution II and III access

### Costs

- weak central command
- repeated congress disputes
- doctrinal dilution
- risk of member exit

### End state

The Congress can lead the broadest version of the Yakubite International and a coalition terminal variant.

## Community economy routes

The economy branch offers three main models.

### Cooperative commonwealth

Movement businesses become cooperatives and municipal associations.

The route supports local stability, consumer supply, and gradual development.

It struggles to mobilize industry rapidly.

### State-directed industrialization

The government centralizes factories, transport, and strategic production.

The route supports military production and infrastructure.

It creates administrative burden, consumer pressure, and faction resistance.

### Regulated movement enterprise

The state recognizes movement businesses and private firms under national regulation.

The route supports revenue, trade, and foreign investment.

It risks patronage networks and unequal local power.

Each model transforms Parallel Institutions into a distinct final idea and decision family.

## Defense route identity

The military branch should solve real founding problems.

It must:

- unify command
- standardize equipment
- secure supply
- train officers
- protect the capital
- integrate defectors
- define civilian control
- create a reinforcement path
- prepare foreign volunteers
- demobilize after war

Political routes change the final army identity without creating unrelated custom units.

## Recognition and diplomacy

The new state starts with disputed recognition.

Neighboring states consider:

- settlement legality
- war status
- doctrine
- route
- treatment of minorities and opponents
- relations with the United States
- foreign sponsor influence
- anti-colonial policy
- Condemnation
- Yakub's status

Recognition provides diplomatic and economic benefits.

It should arrive through missions, treaties, aligned governments, and International support.

It should never become a simple political-power purchase.

## North American integration

The state can expand inside North America through:

- local chapter support
- accession
- negotiated border settlement
- civil war control
- postwar integration
- federal membership
- autonomous regional compacts

Every new state requires political proof.

A conquered state without local institutions begins as occupied or claimed territory.

Integration takes time and may create resistance.

## The North American New Nation

Working formation label: North American New Nation

This is a non-terminal regional federation.

Requirements include:

- a stable Event 44 state
- control or accession of a substantial contiguous region
- institutions in the included states
- a constitutional or route-specific integration mechanism
- no free core grant across all American territory

The formation can change the country name, flag, focus access, federal decisions, and diplomatic status.

It remains below the world-order tier.

## Diaspora Federation

Working formation label: Diaspora Federation

This route unites several Yakubite or aligned states under a federal arrangement before the terminal world order.

Requirements include:

- Evolution III
- several independent member states
- at least two world regions
- high International Cohesion
- member approval or successful federation conflict
- a valid anchor state
- no active succession crisis

Member territory does not become instant cores of the anchor.

The federation uses member autonomy, integration missions, shared defense, and congress rules.

## United States aftermath

A peaceful settlement gives the United States:

- treaty obligations
- trade and transit decisions
- reconciliation or containment routes
- border-security missions
- domestic political consequences
- a future reunification, coexistence, or rivalry policy

A civil war gives the United States:

- war goals and settlement choices
- local resistance and migration pressure
- military loyalty events
- postwar amnesty, occupation, recognition, or renewed separatism

The United States should retain meaningful play after the state forms.

## Evolution I completion states

Evolution I can end in several durable states:

- movement contained before state formation
- legal movement with mature institutions
- autonomous compact
- independent New Nation
- fragmented regional movements
- defeated breakaway
- federal settlement
- underground hardline remnant

Evolution II reads the actual state instead of assuming independence.
