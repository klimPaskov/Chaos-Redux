# 102 Serial Killer: investigation casebook

The casebook gives each archetype three different forms of reasoning.
The Gourmet manipulates how an ordinary social setting is understood.
The Cipher Killer manipulates the origin, meaning and circulation of messages.
The Hunter repeats an observable relationship between movement and civilian routines.
The Ritualist imposes an invented order on objects and acts.
Each family contains four decisive deductions and a permanent stopping outcome.
The later evidence that exposes a failed lead also explains the mistake to the player.

Every family name, role name, location label and evidence token below is a working design label.
Evidence descriptions specify what the player must be shown.
Choice descriptions specify intent.
They are not finished event text, option text, names or localisation.
The cases are original fictional constructions.
Their solutions require the supplied observations, not real criminal cases, specialist forensic knowledge, knowledge of a religion or external historical research.

## How the cases preserve a fair investigation

The player chooses the next supported interpretation or line of inquiry at each decisive step.
A question about which lead to pursue must not be phrased as a demand to convict someone when the available evidence establishes only access.
The final step supplies identity and current whereabouts strongly enough to support an arrest.
An early wrong answer commits investigators to a misleading inquiry.
It does not automatically execute an innocent suspect or permanently treat an innocent association as guilt.

The four deductions are numbered D1 to D4 for design review.
Each new event recalls the earlier facts needed for its choice.
A player who returns after an interruption must not need to remember an exact number or infer an omitted photograph.
The available intent set includes the competing explanations described in that step.
The supported answer always follows from the displayed case facts.
The delayed disproof confirms why the wrong inquiry failed.
It must not introduce the first reason why the correct answer was preferable.

All observations described as verified are displayed as verified.
Uncertain witness impressions remain uncertain.
A distinctive object can link a person to a scene or message without proving that person committed murder.
Final attribution therefore combines that link with independently observed conduct and opportunity.
The case never treats a confession, profession, collection, symbol, diet, nationality or unusual appearance as sufficient proof by itself.

A failure begins a later case against the same still-active person.
The retry uses a new incident and new evidence.
It never rewrites a fact from the failed case.
Known identity information, past crimes, travel history and the actor's permanent status survive.
The new case can change a communication rule, the direction of a transfer, a civilian routine or an observed symbolic transformation.
It cannot silently exchange the original culprit for a different suspect.

Every crime scene used in a comparison belongs to an actual dated Event 102 incident.
The routes that compare three scenes require three such records.
An opening can reveal a small already occurring series with a bounded aggregate loss, or the comparison can become available through later ordinary incident history.
The core pacing design chooses that timing.
The casebook does not add bonus murders merely to complete a table.
If a comparison uses a protected person, recovered preparation or interrupted incident instead of a completed murder, the evidence identifies that distinction.
A fresh retry does not imply that an entire new three-victim series is created immediately.
It can combine valid retained history with a new incident, provided the new facts do not contradict that history.
The reversal examples describe separately dated evidence from a later attempt.
An older incident keeps the rule and observations it actually had.
The new comparison must visibly establish the changed practice.
It must not mix a before-image from one version with an after-image from the other.

Each Event 102 death is a real loss from the correct state's available civilian population and is accounted once under the shared owner contract.
A recap, joined foreign file, newly authenticated message or reopened investigation does not apply the same loss again.
Civilian deaths attributed to another event remain with that event.
The examples' victim counts describe evidence relationships, not a separate casualty schedule.

The actor and the case are separate design objects.
The actor is one continuing individual with one continuing archetype.
The case is one attempt to establish that individual's connection to particular incidents and stop them.
Route-local descriptions such as borrower, visitor or parcel collector describe the conduct of that continuing person.
They do not create new identities.
A later family must respect everything already established about that person.
If its proposed role conflicts with known biography, use another compatible family.
Once a person has been reliably identified, a later case does not pretend investigators have forgotten their name or face.
Its identity step instead tests whether the new observed conduct belongs to that known person or to an innocent person being imitated or implicated.
The evidence and choice framing must acknowledge the retained identification.
New whereabouts and a current opportunity can still require a complete fresh chain.
An earlier dated sighting does not remain a live interception opportunity forever.

Local scene locations are fictional case details placed inside valid usable territory.
The route must fit the available setting and known actor history.
A local sketch or printed timetable cannot create a country border, a railway or an international travel connection.
Evolution I follows the original actor through real neighbouring countries when a feasible connection exists.
Evolution II may carry actual actors beyond the original continent and creates separately identified independent threats.
Evolution III adds only eligible links to events active in the campaign.
These remain the three evolutions at 200, 400 and 600 Chaos respectively.

The repeated late-stage physical links below are fictional observations, such as two torn parts visibly fitting together.
They do not imply that every fibre, mark or mass-produced object can identify a person.
The evidence event must explain both the specific match and the independent conduct that makes it relevant.
No solution depends on an undisclosed search result.

On a successful D4, the conclusion confirms the identified culprit in secure custody and permanently ends that person's murder activity.
There is no extra unannounced deduction, automatic escape or hidden chance to invalidate a complete correct chain.
Civilian protection occurs before the concluding operation.
No civilian is used as bait.
An independent killer or cult from Evolution II keeps its separate identity and separate unresolved history.

## Gourmet

The viewpoint stays with civilians whose familiar dinners, displays and social visits have become difficult to trust.
The disturbing detail is the deliberate arrangement and the offender's control over interpretation.
Descriptions should convey interrupted lives and fearful witnesses without graphic anatomy or instructions for harming people.
Investigative options are restrained and precise.
Any grim irony belongs to the offender's vanity or to misplaced confidence in an attractive theory.
It never targets the victims.

### G1. Borrowed table

This family begins with carefully arranged crockery at three civilian crime scenes.
The culprit has borrowed ordinary objects and used their respectable associations to direct suspicion toward a restaurant.
The deductions move from the full combination of objects to their custody, then from custody to a particular person.

**D1, identify the shared source.**
The first scene contains an oval lid from set A, the second a square plate from set B and the third a narrow cup from set C.
The same removable monogram appears on all three.
The restaurant prominently associated with that monogram owns set A only.
The player is also shown a complete photograph of a travelling still-life display containing the exact A, B and C pieces, including their individually visible repairs.
The choice intents are to trace the display's borrowed objects, investigate the restaurant's food service or infer a common medical profession from the neat arrangements.
Tracing the display is supported by all three objects.
The monogram alone supports only the restaurant association and explains why that lead looks attractive.

**D2, identify the relevant custody interval.**
The lending staff saw all three pieces inside the closed preparation room.
An opening inspection then found each replaced by a plain substitute.
The missing objects therefore left before the public display opened.
The visitor entrance remained closed throughout that interval.
The player chooses between the preparation handover, ordinary visitors and the later return collection.
The preparation handover is the only displayed interval in which the real pieces could have left.
This determines whose observed conduct to examine.
It does not yet identify a murderer.

**D3, distinguish the person carrying the evidence from innocent access.**
The current case has three relevant people, a borrower, a porter and a restaurant representative.
A lending attendant watched the borrower take the unopened case containing all three pieces.
A second civilian saw that same person carry it into a room later connected to the second scene.
The porter carried a different open crate whose contents the attendant checked.
The restaurant representative left before the case was opened.
A torn fastening recovered at the second scene visibly joins the remaining fastening recovered from the borrower's case.
The intent is to pursue the borrower's individual movements, rather than question every worker or treat the restaurant as a collective suspect.
Two witnessed transfers and the joined fastening establish the relevant chain.
The borrower's stable identity is mapped to the original culprit.

**D4, locate the next act without guessing from taste.**
The display has a fourth missing piece.
A caretaker has just seen the borrower arrange that piece with the same linen in an unused reception room.
The room has a scheduled private collection attended by the borrower.
The competing fashionable dinner still has its complete ordinary service and no missing display piece.
The player chooses to protect the reception-room staff and contain the identified borrower at that existing collection, rather than place civilians at the dinner under suspicion or wait for another scene.
The distinctive fourth piece, the witnessed preparation and the known person's commitment establish the intervention point.

**Permanent stopping resolution.**
The reception-room workers are removed from the situation.
The identified borrower is taken into secure custody at the collection.
The fourth piece and the remaining matched case are recovered.
The conclusion attributes the three investigated incidents to that actor and ends their personal waves permanently.

**False lead and later disproof.**
If investigators follow the restaurant at D1, its intact A service is subsequently examined beside the recovered scene piece.
The restaurant's piece has a different documented repair.
Its stock contains neither B nor C.
A lending attendant identifies the monograms as detachable exhibition labels attached to the borrowed display.
These facts disprove the restaurant-supply theory and make the ignored three-object match explicit.
A later separately recorded crime opens the retry.

**Retry with a genuine reversal.**
In the new case, photographs and two closing witnesses establish that all three distinctive objects remained on public display until closing.
The return inspection finds substitutions only after collection.
D1 still identifies the shared display.
D2 must now follow the return collection, because preparation cannot explain a loss occurring after public closing.
The same culprit is the established borrower returning the objects, not a replacement criminal.
D3 shows that person's observed return collection, the separate innocent porter's checked crate and a new matching fastening.
D4 shows the same person arranging the newly missing final piece for a return appointment.
The four supported intents become shared display, return custody, identified borrower and protected return appointment.
Reusing the first attempt's preparation answer fails against the new observations.

The apparent art connection is useful only if investigators identify how the offender could see the arrangement.
The case uses supplied sightline comparisons.
It never requires the player to know perspective drawing or infer a room's geometry from an unseen image.

Each of three scenes contains a tall vase, a shallow bowl and a small display fruit.

In this version, only the direct view from the service alcove reproduces all three overlaps.

The wall mirror was covered for the full relevant interval, as shown by an intact cover and two witnesses.
This step asks the player to match the supplied viewpoints and choose the next inquiry.
It does not establish the sketch's provenance or identify a culprit.
The choice intents are to trace access to the alcove, question every public visitor or attribute the sketches to the owner of a superficially similar painting.
Access to the alcove explains the observed view at all scenes.
The other views fail one or more explicitly displayed comparisons.
The painting association alone does not justify attribution to its owner.
Whether the sketch copies old art or reflects current observation remains the question for D2, which supplies the necessary object comparison.

Civilian staff leave the demonstration room.
The observer is identified and secured on return for the existing appointment.
The recovered sketchbook connects the current preparation with the earlier messages.
The offender's personal case ends permanently.
The viewpoint of the conclusion remains with the relieved sitters and staff.

**False lead and later disproof.**
The old painting makes its owner an attractive suspect at D1.
The later side-by-side comparison shows the square bowl and fruit absent from every publicly available version of that painting.
Verified civilian testimony also places the painter elsewhere during two preparations.
The failure report explains that resemblance in subject matter was weaker than the supplied exact view.
It clears the painter from that lead before a fresh case begins.

**Retry with a genuine reversal.**
The next case includes an uncovered mirror throughout every relevant preparation.
Each scene also has a small tag with the same asymmetric printed mark facing normally on both sides.
The new sketches retain the object orders in the table but reverse that mark.
The displayed doorway reflection has those object orders and the reversed mark.
The direct alcove view has those object orders but the normal mark.
The direct doorway and far-window views still differ in object order.
D1 therefore follows the public reflected view rather than restricted alcove access.
D2 still distinguishes current observation from old art through newly added objects.
D3 now shows the same persistent observer watching the reflected view from the doorway, alongside the newly matched sketchbook evidence.
D4 identifies their preparation of another reflected composition and their witnessed return appointment.
The reversal concerns how the image was obtained.
It does not relabel the same room or exchange the culprit for the painter.

### G3. Unserved course

The apparent menu is an index of social appointments.
The offender wants investigators to infer a common food or diet.
The real pattern lies in which part of a civilian's ordinary day the scene marks.
The case gives every symbol's meaning explicitly through a small programme that the civilians used.
The programme is evidence, not an assumed cultural code.

**D1, decide what the marked course represents.**
The programme associates four distinct object shapes with a study visit, a gallery visit, a meal and a recital.
The first civilian completed the gallery, meal and recital but missed the study visit.
The second completed the study, meal and recital but missed the gallery.
The third completed the study, gallery and recital but missed the meal.
The first scene marks the study shape, the second the gallery shape and the third the meal shape.
The player chooses whether to trace missed appointments, completed meals or a shared profession.
Only missed appointments explain all three scene marks.
The programme uses meal-like imagery, which makes a food interpretation initially plausible.

**D2, identify who caused the shared change.**
The three venues confirm that the original appointments were available.
Three independently interviewed civilians describe the same social host arranging a cancellation for each victim and collecting the corresponding invitation case afterward.
The victims did not independently refuse the appointments.
The choice is to investigate control of the cancellations, question venue staff as a group or search for a dietary link.
The host's intervention explains why those three otherwise unrelated omissions occurred.
The venue staff cannot explain changes they did not make.

**D3, separate the host from innocent messengers.**
A messenger delivered one replacement invitation in an envelope supplied by the host.
A patron arranged the original programme and never handled the changed cases.
The host personally collected all three marked invitation cases.
Two witnesses identify the host placing the second case in the room linked to that scene.
A small broken catch from that scene visibly fits the host's remaining case.
The supported intent is to pursue the host's personal conduct.
The messenger's single delivery and the patron's broad association cannot account for all three collections and the scene placement.

**D4, protect the next person exposed to the actual pattern.**
The next programme has the recital cancelled for a civilian who is still safe.
That cancellation came from the identified host.
The invitation case is due to be returned at a witnessed existing appointment with that host.
The competing meal remains scheduled and its invitation has not been handled by the host.
The player chooses to protect the civilian whose recital was cancelled and secure the host's return appointment.
The correct response follows the observed omission and known actor.
It does not assume that a cuisine itself identifies future victims.

**Permanent stopping resolution.**
The civilian receives protection without attending the collection.
The identified host is secured at the existing appointment, with the matching fourth case.
The conclusion resolves the original actor permanently and records that the expected next civilian harm was prevented.

**False lead and later disproof.**
Following the apparent menu leads to a meal service.
Its three supposed common customers turn out not to share a meal.
The third civilian never attended the meal at all.
That fact was already visible in the programme comparison.
The service's verified attendance accounts later confirm it and expose the mistaken food theory.
The host remains free until a new chain is solved.

**Retry with a genuine reversal.**
In the new case, the first civilian completed only the study visit, the second only the gallery visit and the third only the meal.
The same three scene shapes are shown.
Now they mark completed appointments, while each missed appointment has a different shape.
D1 must follow attendance.
The venues show that the other activities were cancelled by genuine venue closures.
D2 must therefore follow the shared escort on the completed visits, rather than the source of those innocent cancellations.
The same persistent culprit acted as that escort.
D3 gives two witnessed scene placements and a new matching invitation catch from the escort's collection.
D4 shows the culprit arranging to accompany a civilian to the remaining recital.
Protection and a separate appointment secure the actor before that visit.
The complete retry answer chain is completed visits, escort access, identified host acting as escort and protection of the next accompanied civilian.

## Cipher Killer

The viewpoint belongs to civilians receiving unwanted correspondence and investigators trying to distinguish a real connection from an attractive puzzle.
Every decoding convention appears in the evidence.
Ciphers use a small amount of readable data.
They do not require an external language, a famous cryptogram, manual arithmetic beyond the displayed task or recognition of a historical quotation.
The offender's confidence can be unnerving without making the text boast about its danger.
The investigative voice stays calm and specific.

### C1. Coordinates with a witness

The family separates three questions that a superficial investigation can confuse.
Is this message connected to the case, how is it read and what does the decoded place mean?
A decoded place is not automatically a place where civilians will be killed.

Two letters claim responsibility.
One repeats a publicly printed motif.
The other carries a torn strip that visibly fits a uniquely torn card recovered at the first scene.
The fit includes an irregular drawn border that continues across the join.
The public letter's paper and motif have no matching feature.
The choice is to examine the physically connected letter, trust the more elaborate claim or treat both writers as established murderers.
The fitted strip supports a connection to the evidence.
It does not yet prove who wrote the letter or that every claim inside it is true.

**D2, derive the coordinate convention.**
The letter supplies a small grid and two check pairs whose meanings are independently verified by the places at which earlier connected cards were collected.
The first number denotes a row in this version.
The evidence must define rows as horizontal and columns as vertical.
The working grid is below.
The verified check pairs are 1,2 for Well and 2,1 for Mill.
The new pair is 2,3.
The choice intents are to read the first number as a row, read it as a column or treat the pair as a calendar date.
Only row-first reading reproduces both known checks.
It yields Depot for the new pair.

At both earlier checked locations, a civilian caretaker saw a reply envelope collected.
The associated civilian crimes happened elsewhere.
The current authenticated request explicitly describes another reply exchange, and the new place has a matching empty envelope holder.
The player chooses between following the reply collector, predicting that the next victim must live at the decoded location or decoding the same numbers as casualty totals.
The repeated exchange function and present holder support the collection inquiry.

**D4, identify the person behind the exchange.**

This is a rule reversal established by the examples, not a reshuffled answer list.

### C2. Two editions

A message uses a changed illustration from a local newspaper.
The important question is whether the detail was private when the sender used it.
The route makes publication order explicit and does not ask the player to guess how quickly information might have circulated.

**D1, test the claim of special access.**
At 07:00 a proof's square illustration was replaced by a circle.
A witness saw the new proof made at that time.
The connected message, containing the circle version, was handed to its recipient at 09:00.
It also accurately depicts a specifically marked scene card in the position in which two first-arriving civilians independently saw it, before that position was made public.
That detail gives the message an evidential connection to the case without making its author's other assertions true.
The first public copies containing the circle were sold at 17:00.
The evidence confirms that no earlier public copy contained it.
The player chooses to trace access to the unpublished proof, question ordinary evening readers or infer prediction of the future.
Access during the 07:00 to 09:00 interval explains the known information.
The public-reader explanation conflicts with the displayed chronology.
No supernatural explanation is needed.

**D2, identify which version the sender actually saw.**
The player sees the previous public illustration, the early square proof and the corrected circle proof.
The message reproduces a small uneven notch that appears on the corrected proof only.
It does not reproduce an unrelated mark added during the later public printing.
The intents are to follow the corrected proof's physical handling, pursue the author of the previous illustration or question everyone who bought a newspaper.
The combination of the circle and the uneven notch identifies the particular proof stage.
The sender did not simply know that a revision was planned.

**D3, distinguish access from authorship.**
An editor, a delivery worker and a visiting subscriber handled material that morning.
The editor was continuously observed in a public meeting from before the connected message left the building until after its hand delivery.
The delivery worker carried the early square version and is independently identified delivering that checked bundle elsewhere.
The visiting subscriber inspected the corrected proof and was seen personally handing over the connected message.
Two witnesses also watched the subscriber write and seal that same message page during the visit.
A page fragment left with the message visibly joins a page in a folder that the subscriber retained during that visit.
A civilian witness also identifies the subscriber placing an earlier connected scene card.
The supported intent is to follow the visiting subscriber's individual role.
Employment at the newspaper is not proof.

**D4, use a verified appointment to stop the sender.**
The subscriber has arranged to collect another corrected proof in person.
A civilian attendant has just seen the same person leave a folder containing the case's distinctive bordered cards at the collection room.
The planned collection is separate from the public newspaper queue.
The player chooses to protect the attendant and secure that known appointment, instead of closing every newsstand or blaming the whole editorial staff.
The identified person, recovered preparation and existing commitment support the intervention.

**Permanent stopping resolution.**
The attendant is safe before the appointment.
The subscriber is identified and placed in secure custody.
The folder links the current preparation to the earlier physical evidence.
The actor's crimes end permanently.
The newspaper and unrelated correspondents are not treated as a criminal organisation.

**False lead and later disproof.**
The editor attracts suspicion because the message contains editorial material.
If that role is treated as sufficient, the later verified hand-delivery account identifies the subscriber instead.
The editor's continuously witnessed meeting overlaps the delivery, and the subscriber's retained page fits the message fragment.
The failure report makes the original distinction visible.
Someone with access was demonstrated, while the editor's authorship was merely assumed.
The witnessed composition by the subscriber and the linked original page establish whose message it actually was.

**Retry with a genuine reversal.**
In the new case, the circle edition was publicly sold at 08:00 and the connected message arrived at 09:00.
The issue reached the recipient's neighbourhood before the message.
D1 must reject the claim of exclusive proof access and follow public circulation.
The message also contains an added hand-drawn double ring that is absent from every printer's version.
The vendor is shown marking a small purchaser bundle with that double ring at the purchaser's request.

A fragment from the message fits the wrapping retained in the subscriber's witnessed folder, and the independent scene witness supplies the continuing actor link.
D4 uses the subscriber's existing return collection at the vendor, with the civilian vendor protected.
The four supported intents are public circulation, purchaser addition, identified subscriber and protected return appointment.
The changed publication chronology makes the first attempt's private-access inference false.
