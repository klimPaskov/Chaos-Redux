# 16 · Presentation and assets

## The two accounts of the same test

Victim presentation follows a specific place and its people. Preparation describes the arrival of observers, survey teams, range restrictions and confused evacuation notices. Impact reports describe damaged streets, lost buildings, interrupted transport and overwhelmed local services. Later reports can refer to an earlier test in the same country and the reconstruction that was still unfinished.

American presentation follows authorization, instrument readings, analysis and development decisions. Its tone is dry and administrative. It can describe useful data or improved development while the paired victim account records what was destroyed. The facts remain consistent. American technical language must not make the casualty value disappear from the underlying result.

Reserve irony for institutional language and option direction. Do not use jokes about individual victims, invent historical quotations, glorify mass deaths, or turn every response into the same sarcastic line. The brief's contrast is strongest when both accounts are specific and credible within the game.

All wording here is direction-only. The supplied event and evolution names are source labels. The implementation localisation writer must produce the final titles, descriptions, buttons, tooltips, idea names, achievement names and report text after inspecting the exact runtime fields.

## Report sequence

| Moment | Victim direction | American direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First demand | State and weapon family, five-day response deadline, full consequences and actual war threat | Selected research objective, assigned territory, real preparation requirement |
| Repeated demand | Mention prior losses and unfinished repairs without exaggerating beyond receipts | Treat the known range as administratively convenient, while retaining its real history |
| Preparation | Observers, transport, boundaries and notices appropriate to that family | Test article preparation, authorization, scheduled commitment |
| Refusal | Explain the real USA declaration and actual allies, with no promised victory | Record rejection and the USA-led war, no completed-test reward |
| Conventional impact | Local damage and actual loss figures, surviving facilities and new objectives | Test conducted, results pending analysis |
| Nuclear impact | Regional destruction, broken communications and recorded casualties without graphic imagery | Prototype or mature capability identified accurately, development analysis pending |
| Unconventional aftermath | Medical uncertainty, owner-confirmed illness or contamination and practical containment | Owner-approved findings, uncertainty preserved, no invented diagnosis |
| Successful analysis | Optional notice that the foreign report called the experiment successful | Specific useful development result, actual reward and destination |
| Recovery | State which routes or productive levels are restored and what remains lost | Relief or diplomatic response only if the USA actually participates |

For repeated victims, use at least a first incident, previous-test and heavily repeated-test text direction. These variants use real counters. Do not create an endless series of bespoke report events for each occurrence number.

## Information hierarchy

The demand must make the country, state, family, deadline and refusal-war consequence easy to identify. A tooltip distinguishes repairable damage, possible permanent destruction, population loss, deployed-unit risk and owner-controlled aftermath. It must not show a false precise casualty prediction before the provider has determined actual results.

After impact, present exact recorded losses where available. Distinguish this impact from the test's later cumulative losses. Unknown military or tail attribution cannot be printed as zero merely because the adapter is incomplete.

The native decision category shows active experiments as compact records. Each has its own target, stage and date. Completed results can move to the established event history or an appropriate read-only summary. The UI must not depend on one global selected state that changes when another country's report opens.

Use ordinary event windows, news, decisions and timed missions. Asset dimensions and layout must follow actual installed consumers. Any proposed category illustration is conditional on a simple compatible category layout and the supplied decision skill's restrictions.

## Icons and ideas

The American institution and the victim backlash each receive a clear 64 × 64 idea icon. They evolve through modifiers and descriptions, with icon variants only if they materially improve recognition. Do not create a national spirit for every individual test, family, casualty band or research payout.

Prepare up to six 32 × 32 decision-family icons for commissioning or reviewing tests, ending a pre-commitment test, rebuilding access, restoring production, organizing relief, and seeking protection. Related actions can share the correct family icon. Existing owner containment actions retain their own icon ownership.

A category illustration is optional and must pass the decision-surface rule before production. The reference examples include a 114 × 101 category asset, but the actual installed consumer and its layout must be checked. This planning figure is not proof that the current target category uses that canvas.

Generate icon families from their own readable silhouette and material treatment. Do not shrink a crowded report scene into an icon, resize a focus icon as a decision icon, or import a copied vanilla silhouette as an original final asset. Transparent icon masters need genuine alpha from generation, clean boundaries and review at actual display size.
