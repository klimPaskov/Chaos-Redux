# 10 · Repeated victims and recovery

## Domestic Hostility

Domestic Hostility is the one new public mechanic value. It runs from 0 to 100 and represents pressure against the government's handling of American testing. It is not a currency, a measure of civilian deaths or a substitute for the shared Condemnation system.

A first completed conventional test adds 15 Hostility. Heavy conventional testing adds 20. A nuclear or unconventional test adds 35. Each previous completed test in that country adds a further 5 to the incident increase, capped at an additional 15. Refusal itself adds no Hostility, and an invalidated proposal cannot repeatedly raise it without a real incident. Coercive demands and cancellations remain in narrative history even when they produce no completed-test increase.

The physical state's history also matters after political change. A new controller inherits a state with its damage and people already lost, but it does not automatically inherit personal responsibility for the previous government's acceptance. Apply the shared identity policy and the successor's actual involvement when transferring country-level backlash.

| Hostility band | Domestic meaning | Initial institutional effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 24 | Anger remains local or recent repairs have restored confidence | No national penalty |
| 25 to 49 | Survivor groups and local officials press the government | 5 percent Stability penalty |
| 50 to 74 | Opposition treats testing as a national issue | 10 percent Stability penalty |
| 75 to 99 | The next acceptance threatens a political crisis | 15 percent Stability penalty |
| 100 | Sustained refusal pressure and public distrust | 15 percent Stability penalty and 10 percent political-power-gain penalty |

Use one evolving backlash spirit. Do not create a separate penalty for every test, state or victim association. Its political effect complements actual destroyed capacity. A country's existing unrest owner can consume severe backlash as a pressure input through a verified adapter, but Event 076 does not silently start a new civil-war subsystem.

## Repair does not erase memory

Successful reconstruction reduces Hostility by 10 when its actual objective is met. Effective externally supplied relief can reduce it by another 5 once for that incident. After 180 days without a completed test and without an unresolved event recovery crisis, Hostility may decline by 5 per 90 days. A new test interrupts this cooling period.

The same ruin cannot earn repeated reconstruction rewards by opening and canceling projects. Record the damaged asset set and the incident objective. Later rebuilding does not restore dead civilians, reset the country's lifetime tests or remove the original Deaths receipts.

## Restore useful access

The first recovery task concerns access to a damaged population or industrial center. Select an actual damaged connection, port, infrastructure level or supply facility that the country's forces or relief route need. The objective requires a real restored route or installation and continued friendly control of the affected area.

The normal time window is 180 days, rising to 240 days for nuclear-scale or severely isolated damage. Completion occurs automatically when the verified map conditions are met. Waiting for the timer to expire does not complete the mission.

The state receives temporary reconstruction support only after the country commits a valid relief or repair allocation. The player still needs to use the actual construction queue, regain lost control or reopen access. A Political Power payment by itself cannot make a destroyed railway reappear.

## Rebuild the damaged district

After access is usable, identify the experiment's meaningful industrial loss. A second objective requires restoration of at least half of the recorded lost productive capacity, rounded up to an actual building level where necessary, and repair of the remaining designated installations below the serious-damage threshold supported by the engine.

Use 240 days for conventional destruction and 365 days for nuclear-scale reconstruction. The objective is based on the frozen incident damage record and current real assets. Building a factory in an unrelated safe state does not restore the destroyed district.

If a later test hits the same area, keep the first mission's record and deadline. The country can consolidate multiple recovery records into one visible district objective with explicit component progress. Do not restart the deadline or grant the first success twice. If the state is annexed, its physical recovery opportunity follows the controller while the original country's achievement proof remains tied to its own campaign.

## Aid that arrives somewhere

A foreign government can send a bounded relief package to an affected country with a usable route. The package uses real supported equipment transfers or a verified owner relief allocation. Support equipment, trucks or trains can be relevant, but a transport item is not also deducted a second time as a fictional humanitarian currency.

Aid must record the sender, recipient, delivered resources, route and intended recovery instance. The recipient can spend those assets through its real systems. A blocked route delays or invalidates delivery according to the owning transfer rules. Do not grant invisible reconstruction progress because a donor clicked a button.

Foreign help can make later refusal more credible by rebuilding the minor's capacity and supporting a real guarantee. The choice to join a war remains a real diplomatic commitment. Relief is not an automatic military alliance.

## Containment

Nuclear fallout, chemical contamination and biological outbreaks expose their existing owner response actions. Event 076 can bring those actions into the relevant decision view or point the player toward them, but the owner controls costs, protection and termination conditions.

An affected government may need to secure access, restore supply and maintain the owner's protective measures simultaneously. Use at most three visible active objectives. Their clocks and outcomes remain separate when their owners have different requirements.

A containment success requires the owner's verified safe or controlled state, not a new Event 076 variable set by paying resources. Failure leaves actual contamination or disease active. It does not add a second arbitrary casualty estimate.

## Choosing the next response

The next ultimatum acknowledges the previous tests and current damaged states. A country with high Hostility is more likely to refuse, especially when a major ally offers credible help. It still has exactly the original two responses.

A refusal war has no guaranteed victory, automatic ceasefire or scripted American withdrawal after a short timer. Survival depends on actual forces, geography, allies and the war outcome. A country that defeats the threat can restore political confidence, but it cannot reclaim the population already lost in accepted trials.
