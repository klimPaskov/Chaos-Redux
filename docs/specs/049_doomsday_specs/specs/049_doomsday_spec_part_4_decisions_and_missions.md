# Event 049 Doomsday Specification

## Part 4: Decisions and Missions

## Decision-system purpose

The decision system asks the country to preserve or redirect real institutions during the countdown.

It should not become a store of political power purchases. Important actions require equipment, fuel, trains, convoys, manpower, industrial capacity, stability, War Support, military experience, local control, unit presence, or a sustained mission.

Each action can use at most four spendable cost types.

The player should normally see three to five primary decisions and one to three active missions. Six primary actions is the hard maximum for one phase.

## Presentation

The event uses one main decision category.

The category header shows:

- Doomsday Conviction and recent trend.
- The exact predicted date.
- Time Until the End.
- The country’s posture as a short qualitative label.
- One short institutional status line.

The category picture changes at broad stages:

1. Early convergence and public gatherings.
2. Organized societies and abandoned long projects.
3. Last Calendar mass mobilization.
4. Final-month vigil or parallel government.
5. Failed-date dawn and reopening.

These are static state variants. Animation is optional only if an asset review finds a clear presentation gain. The system does not require a full scripted window.

## Phase structure

### Phase 1: First response

The player chooses a posture and takes one first public action.

Visible decisions should include only the relevant posture choices and one immediate emergency action. Obsolete posture choices disappear after commitment.

### Phase 2: Keep the country functioning

The category rotates continuity missions around education, transport, food, military rolls, public evidence, and local administration.

### Phase 3: War and peace pressure

Countries at war receive negotiation, service, deployment, and defensive continuity actions. Peace countries receive reserve, shelter, labor, and public-order variants.

### Phase 4: Last Calendar policies

Evolution I unlocks stronger national policies around prisoners, borders, debts, reserves, projects, observance, and representation.

### Phase 5: Parallel government

Evolution II replaces ordinary response actions with authority transfer, local council, military refusal, government survival, or revolutionary emergency choices.

### Phase 6: Final month and failed-date recovery

Final-month actions preserve people, records, and services. After the date, the category converts into reconstruction phases.

## Posture commitment decisions

Each posture choice should apply a significant package, not a tiny modifier.

### Commit to Civic Continuity

Requirements:

- The country retains a functioning central government.
- Public information and institutional missions remain possible.

Costs and commitments:

- Political authority and a temporary civilian administrative burden.
- A public promise to keep named institutions open.

Immediate effect:

- Unlocks evidence, education, archive, and continuity missions.
- Reduces open endorsement of the prediction.
- Makes failure of public promises more damaging.

AI preference:

- Stable states with functioning education, moderate or low local conviction, and no severe military collapse.

### Negotiate a Concordat of Vigil

Requirements:

- At least one trusted religious, civic, communal, labor, or municipal network can participate.

Costs and commitments:

- Political authority, state supplies, and a share of public legitimacy.

Immediate effect:

- Unlocks joint relief, observance, shelter, and local-order actions.
- Raises movement organization while reducing violent conflict.

AI preference:

- Countries with strong trusted institutions, high conviction, low coercive capacity, or severe relief needs.

### Declare Emergency Order

Requirements:

- The government retains police, military, or administrative capacity.

Costs and commitments:

- Command Power, equipment, stability, and unit commitment where appropriate.

Immediate effect:

- Reduces open organization and protects selected strategic institutions.
- Begins suppression backlash tracking and legal or violent incident risks.

AI preference:

- Authoritarian governments, high military pressure, strong coercive institutions, and fear of immediate capture.

### Begin National Preparation

Requirements:

- The country controls enough territory and industry to build or distribute preparations.

Costs and commitments:

- Civilian factory burden, support equipment, trains or convoys, and fuel according to geography.

Immediate effect:

- Unlocks shelters, reserves, records, seed, medicine, and continuity-site actions.
- Raises public acceptance that the threat may be real.

AI preference:

- Governments facing disasters, bombing, famine, contamination, or strong survivalist currents.

### Seek Peace Before the End

Requirements:

- The country is at war or under severe mobilization strain.

Costs and commitments:

- War Support, military readiness, and diplomatic or alliance credibility.

Immediate effect:

- Unlocks truce, prisoner, limited-service, and arms-conversion actions.
- Reduces offensive capacity before peace is guaranteed.

AI preference:

- Losing wars, high casualty burden, low War Support, strong pacifist organization, and a plausible negotiating partner.

### Proclaim Nothing Left to Lose

Requirements:

- High local conviction or a strong apocalyptic state-loyalist current.
- A government capable of enforcing or inspiring continued war.

Costs and commitments:

- Stability, long-term institutional continuity, manpower, and equipment depletion.

Immediate effect:

- Opens a temporary offensive and mobilization window.
- Increases failure, mutiny, and post-date collapse risk.

AI preference:

- Militarized regimes, leaders facing existential defeat, strong state-loyalist currents, and low willingness to negotiate.

## Continuity mission pool

Only the most relevant missions appear. The normal active cap is three.

### Keep the Schools Open

Purpose:

Preserve primary schools, universities, technical institutes, and teacher networks through a period of withdrawal.

Typical duration:

120 to 180 days.

Requirements:

- Maintain civilian capacity in named states or the capital region.
- Avoid mass closure or violent seizure of the institutions.
- Keep a minimum level of transport and public order.

Costs:

- Civilian factory burden, manpower, and political authority.

Success:

- Raises institutional continuity.
- Reduces local conviction growth among students and skilled workers.
- Preserves research recovery after the date.

Failure:

- Causes a visible education exodus incident.
- Worsens research and skilled-labor penalties.
- Raises movement organization among youth.

### Maintain the Technical Institutes

Purpose:

Keep engineers, researchers, medical schools, and industrial laboratories working on projects that extend beyond the predicted date.

Typical duration:

150 to 210 days.

Requirements:

- Maintain power, transport, and a minimum industrial base.
- Protect one or more named institutions or industrial states.

Costs:

- Civilian factory burden, fuel, and support equipment.

Success:

- Preserves research capacity and selected long projects.
- Improves failed-date recovery.

Failure:

- Cancels or delays a long project.
- Creates a report about staff departure or equipment abandonment.

### Hold the Rail and Food Network Together

Purpose:

Keep food, medicine, coal, and public transport moving while workers leave and societies redirect supplies.

Typical duration:

120 to 180 days.

Requirements:

- Control named rail hubs, ports, or supply routes.
- Keep trains, convoys, and fuel available.

Costs:

- Trains or convoys, fuel, manpower, and temporary civilian capacity.

Success:

- Protects institutional continuity.
- Sends a bounded positive input to famine and migration systems when relevant.
- Reduces local panic and hoarding.

Failure:

- Raises food insecurity and trapped-population pressure through the owning systems.
- Strengthens communal and survivalist organization.

### Preserve the Muster Rolls

Purpose:

Keep military personnel, unit records, medical classifications, depots, and command links intact.

Typical duration:

90 to 150 days.

Requirements:

- Maintain supplied divisions in named home regions or military districts.
- Keep depots and transport connected.

Costs:

- Command Power, army experience, support equipment, and fuel.

Success:

- Reduces desertion and improves later reconstitution.
- Allows defensive-only service arrangements without losing the whole force.

Failure:

- Units lose reliability or fragment.
- The country receives a refusal or missing-regiment incident.

### Protect the Archives and Registers

Purpose:

Preserve tax records, property records, court files, medical records, military records, scientific archives, and identity documents.

Typical duration:

90 to 135 days.

Requirements:

- Retain control of the capital or designated archive state.
- Prevent seizure or destruction by state or movement actors.

Costs:

- Manpower, support equipment, and civilian capacity.

Success:

- Raises institutional continuity strongly.
- Reduces post-date debt, ownership, conscription, and administrative disputes.

Failure:

- Creates a later reconstruction penalty.
- Revolutionary or survivalist currents may gain control of sensitive records.

### Keep the Hospitals Staffed

Purpose:

Maintain public health, emergency medicine, burial services, and disaster care.

Typical duration:

120 to 180 days.

Requirements:

- Preserve medical manpower and supply routes.

Costs:

- Support equipment, convoys or trains, fuel, and civilian capacity.

Success:

- Reduces real deaths from event-owned riots or abandonment.
- Improves famine, outbreak, disaster, and migration resilience through bounded adapters.

Failure:

- Raises local conviction through visible institutional collapse.
- Worsens mortality only through the relevant owning death or crisis system.

## Public evidence and reassurance actions

### Open the Archives to Public Review

The government allows independent civic, religious, scientific, and press observers to inspect the evidence behind public statements.

This action is strongest when institutions are credible. It is dangerous when the government has concealed reports or fabricated certainty.

Possible outcomes:

- Credible review lowers local conviction.
- Ambiguous evidence produces little change.
- Exposed concealment raises Conviction and organization.

### Sponsor Independent Calculations

The state funds several independent institutions and publishes their disagreements without imposing one official answer.

The decision should consume research capacity and political authority. It cannot guarantee disproof.

### National Broadcast of Continuity

The government publicly commits to named projects, schools, railways, or harvests that will continue beyond the date.

The action creates a mission. Completing the promise matters more than the speech.

### Expose a Fraudulent Society

This action requires proven fraud, embezzlement, or false protection sales.

It should reduce the relevant current’s organization without implying that every movement is fraudulent.

## Cooperation actions

### Form a Civic and Religious Relief Council

Creates a joint institution for food, shelter, burial, medicine, and public gatherings.

The council gains practical capacity and political legitimacy. It can later become part of a voluntary Doomsday administration.

### License Communal Kitchens and Shelters

The government provides controlled access to buildings, tools, and supplies.

Success improves relief and order. Failure can create hoarding, exclusion, or local authority transfer.

### Protect Final Observances

The state provides transport and public-order support for mass vigils and religious observance.

The action can reduce violence and increase conviction at the same time.

### Recognize Society Delegates

This Evolution I action gives formal consultation rights to movement representatives.

It reduces immediate unrest and increases organization. It also opens peaceful government-transfer routes under Evolution II.

## Preparation actions

### Build Regional Shelters

Costs should scale with population, industry, and state coverage.

Shelters consume civilian capacity, support equipment, steel-related industrial effort where represented, fuel, and transport. The final action should use no more than four spendable cost types.

Completed shelters provide genuine protection against bombing, disaster, plague isolation, famine distribution failure, or severe weather where the owning systems support an adapter.

### Create National Reserves

The state stores food proxies, medicine, seed, tools, fuel, and transport equipment.

The reserve should not create a second visible stockpile meter. It can use staged national spirits or a hidden capacity state with clear decision results.

### Duplicate the National Archives

Creates regional copies of essential civil, military, scientific, and financial records.

### Establish Continuity Sites

Moves selected government, research, medical, and communications staff to protected sites.

This preserves continuity while reducing current output and public trust if the sites appear elite-only.

### Regulate Private Stockpiles

The government licenses civic preparations and targets predatory hoarding.

A fair policy improves order. Elite seizure or discriminatory allocation strengthens revolutionary currents.

## War and peace actions

### Request Exploratory Armistice Talks

The country opens indirect negotiations without immediate demobilization.

Availability depends on war type, enemy existence, diplomatic contact, and peace pressure.

Failure can weaken government authority if the enemy publicizes the approach.

### Offer a Time-Limited Truce

This action proposes a temporary ceasefire around the Last Day, prisoner exchange, civilian evacuation, harvest, or burial.

A truce should have a clear duration and breach consequences.

### Recognize Conscientious Service

The country allows medical, transport, civil-defense, or relief service instead of combat duty.

The action reduces open refusal and offensive manpower while preserving some state capacity.

### Release Conscientious Objectors

Evolution I action that reduces prison pressure and pacifist hostility.

Militarized allies and state-loyalist currents may react negatively.

### Convert Arms Lines to Civil Protection

The country commits military factories or output to trucks, trains, support equipment, shelters, and medical logistics.

This is a real production tradeoff, not a small generic modifier.

### Prepare the Final Offensive

Nothing Left to Lose action that creates a temporary military window with explicit aftermath.

The mission should name the strategic objective, duration, and expected cost. Failure creates severe mutiny, conviction, and continuity penalties.

## Last Calendar policies

Evolution I unlocks a rotating set of stronger policies.

### Wind Down Long Military Programs

Cancels or pauses selected long military projects and redirects resources to immediate defense, relief, or reserves.

### Cancel Prestige Construction

Ends projects with completion dates beyond the Last Day. The player chooses which projects to preserve when possible.

### Open the Borders for Final Pilgrimage or Family Reunion

This action uses the Migration system for real movement and reception. It can create relief and security pressure.

### Proclaim a Debt Settlement

The government freezes, forgives, restructures, or suspends selected debts.

This can calm households while damaging banks, state finance, and post-date recovery.

### Distribute Emergency Reserves

Provides immediate relief at the cost of final-month capacity.

### Declare National Days of Observance

Reduces disorder and output while strengthening devotional or prophetic organization.

### Grant Public Representation to the Societies

Creates an advisory or legislative body. This is a major step toward peaceful transfer.

## Evolution II authority decisions

### Form a Custodial Administration

The existing government voluntarily narrows its purpose and shares power with society delegates.

### Convene the Last National Assembly

A legislature, municipal congress, civic council, or constitutional body decides the country’s final political structure.

### Transfer Local Services to Communal Councils

The state delegates food, shelter, clinics, and local order. Repeated use can produce a municipal cascade.

### Recognize Defensive-Only Military Command

The armed forces refuse offensive deployment and retain only civil protection and territorial defense roles.

### Defend the Existing Government

The country launches emergency continuity missions to prevent capture, and coercion alone cannot produce success without functioning institutions.

### Break the Parallel Government

A high-risk crackdown targets society administration. It can restore authority or trigger revolutionary collapse.

### Petition the Final Assembly

A Doomsday administration applies for membership after meeting its route requirements.

## Final-month actions

Final-month actions should replace most ordinary policy decisions.

Recommended actions include:

- Protect the harvest and water system.
- Keep hospitals and burial services open.
- Secure archives and seed reserves.
- Maintain one national communications chain.
- Organize public vigils without blocking relief routes.
- Issue final military standing orders.
- Release selected prisoners.
- Prevent elite flight with public resources.
- Keep ports and railways open for family reunion and evacuation.
- Prepare an orderly reopening plan in case the date passes.

The player should not be able to complete every action. The final month forces prioritization.

## Failed-date reconstruction decisions

### Reopen Schools and Universities

Restores education and research through a staged mission. Countries with preserved institutions recover faster.

### Recall Engineers and Researchers

Uses domestic recall, foreign return, and institutional repair. Migration owns any real movement.

### Reconstitute the Armed Forces

Rebuilds rolls, command, depots, and service law. It should not instantly recreate destroyed or dispersed units.

### Settle the Last Days Debts

Resolves suspended contracts, spent savings, forgiven debts, and emergency claims.

### Convert the Shelters

Turns useful shelters into civil defense, hospitals, storage, or housing. Poorly designed or corrupt shelter programs become financial burdens.

### Reintegrate the Settlements

Negotiates return, autonomy, recognition, taxation, or continued communal status.

### Investigate the Suppression

Exposes unlawful arrests, disappearances, deaths, destroyed records, and elite profiteering. Condemnation and Deaths remain owned by their shared systems.

### Retire the Last Calendar

Closes the acute mechanic after the country has restored enough institutional continuity and resolved its movement outcome.

## Mission failure and partial success

Every mission should have distinct success, partial success where useful, and failure results.

Examples:

- A rail mission can keep food moving while losing passenger service.
- A school mission can preserve primary education while universities collapse.
- A muster mission can retain defensive formations while offensive units disperse.
- A shelter mission can protect cities while excluding rural areas.
- An armistice can stop fighting around the Last Day while leaving the war unresolved.

Partial success should create a meaningful next decision, not a small consolation modifier.

## AI action rules

The AI must evaluate:

- Whether the action supports its chosen posture.
- Whether it can pay the real costs without destroying another critical system.
- Whether the named states, routes, institutions, enemies, or partners exist.
- Whether the action remains useful at the current countdown stage.
- Whether a stronger later action has replaced it.
- Whether repeated use would create an exploit or duplicate reward.

The AI should not attempt a full list of all actions. It should receive the same phased visibility and active-mission cap as the player, with hidden AI access only where target selection requires it.

## Cleanup rules

- Posture decisions disappear after commitment.
- Early actions are replaced by final-year and final-month variants.
- War actions disappear after peace, annexation, or invalidation of the target.
- Cooperation actions close if the partner network collapses or government capture replaces them.
- Suppression actions close after a posture change or failed-date settlement.
- Terminal authority actions close after Final Vigil commitment.
- Countdown actions convert into aftermath actions after the date.
- No action can retain a stale country, state, war, or organization target.
