# Event 049 Doomsday Specification

## Part 8: World-End Scenario, The Final Vigil

## Terminal definition

The Final Vigil is a political world end.

The planet survives and humanity remains, while the campaign reaches an end state because conventional geopolitics loses its social foundation across enough of the world.

States stop planning for victory, expansion, development, ideology, or future prosperity. Armies demobilize or fragment. Military production collapses. Long construction stops. Doomsday administrations and the Final Assembly organize food, shelter, peace, records, and public order until the predicted date.

## Public branch identity

The Final Vigil is a public world-end route owned by Event 049.

It requires:

- Global Chaos at 1000 or more.
- Event 049 active.
- Evolution II enabled and active.
- The Final Vigil branch enabled in Event Details.
- No other world end already committed.
- A valid event-owned terminal readiness path.

The branch must have its own Event Details row, detail text, availability state, persistent toggle, scenario identity, super-event linkage, and final state flagging.

Disabling The Final Vigil must not disable Event 049 or its evolutions. It only removes the terminal route from automatic readiness.

## Terminal readiness philosophy

Global Conviction alone should not trigger the world end instantly.

The Final Vigil requires proof that belief has replaced political purpose. Several routes can provide that proof.

A route normally needs a sustained confirmation window. A one-day threshold crossing should not end the campaign.

## Path A: Voluntary administrations

### Premise

A large, geographically broad set of countries voluntarily installs Doomsday administrations and transfers public authority to custodial, communal, devotional, or pacifist institutions.

### Readiness proof

The path should consider:

- The bounded weighted share of eligible countries under Doomsday administrations.
- Geographic spread across several world regions.
- At least one meaningful major-power or large regional-power participation, unless the overall country share is overwhelming.
- Government status sustained beyond temporary local incidents.
- Active international coordination through the preparatory congress.

### Terminal character

This path tends toward a negotiated Final Assembly with Peace Delegates, Communal Stewards, Keepers of Revelation, and Reserve Custodians.

It produces the least violent consolidation.

## Path B: Overwhelming global Conviction

### Premise

Doomsday Conviction reaches an overwhelming level and remains there long enough that ordinary governments cannot recover public belief in a future.

### Readiness proof

The path should require:

- Conviction in the highest stage.
- A sustained period.
- High local conviction in several independent regions.
- Material institutional withdrawal or government capture alongside the global numeric state.
- No strong global reversal trend.

### Terminal character

The Assembly’s dominant current follows the world movement composition. Prophetic and devotional influence is usually stronger here.

## Path C: Major-power demobilization

### Premise

Enough major powers renounce offensive war, demobilize, and join the Final Assembly that military geopolitics can no longer function as before.

### Readiness proof

The path should consider:

- A bounded number or weighted share of existing major powers.
- Real demobilization or defensive-only conversion.
- Full Final Assembly membership.
- Withdrawal from offensive faction command.
- At least one relief or transport compact that proves practical cooperation.

### Terminal character

Peace Delegates and Reserve Custodians gain influence. Holdout powers become isolated and face severe internal pressure.

## Path D: Pacifist transfer of military authority

### Premise

An international pacifist movement coordinates soldiers, families, transport workers, officers, prisoners, and governments until military authority transfers to defensive civil administration.

### Readiness proof

The path should require:

- Strong pacifist organization across several countries.
- Coordinated refusal or defensive-only service.
- Successful armistice or prisoner actions.
- Military or government recognition in several states.
- A common demobilization compact.

### Terminal character

The Final Assembly begins with a strong Peace Delegates mandate and broad disarmament rules.

## Path E: Suppression-driven collapse

### Premise

Repeated violent suppression radicalizes the societies. Governments fall through strikes, military refusal, prison uprisings, municipal revolt, and revolutionary takeover.

### Readiness proof

The path should require:

- Severe suppression backlash across several countries.
- Real government collapse or revolutionary administration.
- Documented deaths, disappearances, destroyed relief, or exposed atrocity where the owning systems support them.
- High revolutionary and avenging organization.
- Failure of ordinary international mediation.

### Terminal character

Avenging Witnesses and Communal Stewards enter the Final Assembly with strong influence. Consolidation is more violent and post-date restoration is less likely.

## Mixed-path readiness

Partial progress across several paths can combine.

Examples:

- Moderate voluntary government transfer plus major-power demobilization.
- High Conviction plus a strong pacifist compact.
- Suppression-driven collapse in several states plus municipal transfer elsewhere.

The combined route should still require sustained broad proof. The implementation should avoid a hidden point score that can be farmed through repeated minor incidents.

## Country weighting

Terminal readiness uses bounded weights.

A major power or populous country matters more than a microstate, but one country cannot satisfy a global path alone.

The model should reward:

- Geographic spread.
- Major-power participation.
- Number of valid administrations.
- Depth of political transfer.
- Military demobilization.
- International coordination.

It should not reward repeated tag splitting or artificial release of tiny countries as a terminal exploit.

## Final Assembly

The Final Assembly is an event-owned international institution that operates outside ordinary military-faction presentation.

Its purpose is to coordinate:

- Food and reserve distribution.
- Shelter access.
- Medical support.
- Prisoner release and family reunion.
- Transport corridors.
- Demobilization and defensive service.
- Public observance.
- Records and continuity.
- Relations with holdout governments.

The Assembly can use existing diplomatic and scripted systems internally, but its visible identity should remain a nonmilitary authority.

## Assembly currents

### Peace Delegates

They seek global disarmament, armistice, conscientious service, prisoner release, and protection of civilians.

A Peace Delegates majority creates the most complete military demobilization and the strongest pressure on holdouts.

### Keepers of Revelation

They organize humanity around observance, judgment, confession, ritual, and preparation for the Last Day.

A Keepers majority gives religious and prophetic institutions greater public authority. It must preserve plural regional expression unless one route has legitimately created a narrower order.

### Communal Stewards

They favor local councils, shared property use, kitchens, autonomous settlements, and weak central borders.

A Communal majority decentralizes states and gives local communities broad control.

### Reserve Custodians

They prioritize records, shelters, food, medicine, transport, technical staff, and disciplined continuity.

A Custodian majority preserves more state capacity and creates the strongest possible reopening plan.

### Avenging Witnesses

They seek public judgment for suppression, war, hoarding, disappearance, and elite escape.

An Avenging majority creates tribunals, seizures, prison openings, and higher revolutionary risk. It should grow only from real campaign history.

## Current influence

Each member contributes influence according to:

- Its administration route.
- Its dominant and secondary movement currents.
- Focus choices.
- Relief and demobilization commitments.
- Suppression history.
- Major-power status within a cap.

The Assembly’s dominant current should be visible as a qualitative political identity after the terminal route begins. It does not need another player-managed meter.

## Membership

A country can become a full member when:

- It has a valid Doomsday administration.
- It accepts the Assembly’s basic custodial purpose.
- It follows the required military policy for its route.
- It contributes a practical obligation, such as relief, transport, shelters, prisoners, records, or demobilization.
- It has no incompatible world-end commitment.

Observer status is available earlier and does not count fully toward terminal readiness.

## Holdouts

Countries that reject the Assembly remain holdouts.

Holdouts can include:

- Governments preserving ordinary institutions.
- Militarist states using Nothing Left to Lose.
- Aggressive powers exploiting demobilized neighbors.
- Nonhuman or special Chaos actors outside normal civilian politics.
- Revolutionary states that reject the Assembly’s authority.
- Countries whose populations are divided and whose governments remain functional.

Holdouts should not be annexed or deleted automatically.

They face:

- Internal Conviction pressure.
- Desertion and transport refusal.
- Trade and relief isolation.
- Diplomatic pressure.
- Local councils seeking observer or member status.
- The strategic problem of fighting countries that no longer value normal victory.

A player can remain a holdout. The campaign is still in a world-end state, and normal event firing has stopped, but the player retains a final resistance or continuity experience according to the terminal design.

## Terminal commitment sequence

### Step 1: Readiness recognized

The event confirms that one terminal path or valid mixed path has matured.

A short global event announces that enough governments and movements are preparing to transfer authority.

### Step 2: World-end commitment

The shared world-end state is set. Automatic normal event firing stops.

The Final Vigil super-event fires with its own image, text, quote, button, and unique audio package.

### Step 3: Founding session

Member governments and movement delegates establish the Final Assembly and determine its dominant current.

### Step 4: National decisions

Countries confirm membership, observer status, or holdout status. AI choices use government state, local conviction, war, invasion risk, and movement composition.

### Step 5: Consolidation

A 30 to 90 day event-owned sequence applies demobilization, relief, war settlement, transport, records, and political transformation.

### Step 6: Terminal order

The Assembly’s current, membership, holdouts, remaining wars, and institutional state define the final campaign condition.

## Military effects

### Member armies

Member armies move toward one of these states:

- Full demobilization.
- Defensive civil protection.
- Relief and transport service.
- Local communal defense.
- Custodial protection of food, shelters, records, and hospitals.

The exact result depends on focus choices and invasion status.

### Shared military production

Members sharply reduce offensive military production. Lines can convert toward transport, support equipment, repair, shelter, and medical logistics.

The terminal route can impose severe output collapse because normal geopolitical competition has ended. It should still preserve essential production needed for survival.

### Holdout military pressure

Holdout forces retain more normal military capacity, but they face internal desertion and supply problems according to local Conviction.

### Weapons and depots

Weapons do not vanish. Orderly members register and store them. Communal members distribute local defense arms. Revolutionary members may seize them. Poor consolidation leaves scattered armed groups.

## War settlement under The Final Vigil

### Member against member

Member governments face overwhelming pressure to end the war through status quo, withdrawal, prisoner release, and negotiated security.

A member-to-member war should normally end during consolidation unless one side rejects Assembly authority or a civil-war exception remains unresolved.

### Member against holdout

The war can continue. The member side suffers limits on offensive war and may defend territory, civilians, and relief routes.

The Assembly can demand ceasefire, offer corridors, coordinate sanctions, or recognize local administrations.

### Holdout against holdout

The war can continue under the terminal world state, though both sides face the general social crisis.

### Civil wars

A civil war can end through a shared administration, local federation, partition, constitutional congress, or continued holdout conflict.

The Assembly should not impose one generic result on every civil war.

### Occupations and colonies

Assembly principles encourage release, local administration, negotiated withdrawal, or custodial transition. Territory should not transfer without political handling.

## Borders and sovereignty

Borders become less important within the Assembly, but they do not disappear as map objects immediately.

Member agreements can create:

- Open relief corridors.
- Family reunion routes.
- Pilgrimage access.
- Shared shelter access.
- Local communal autonomy.
- Reduced customs and military control.
- Joint management of railways, ports, and rivers.

A Communal Stewards majority moves further toward local autonomy and weak central borders. A Reserve Custodians majority preserves more national administration.

## Economy under the terminal state

Long-term investment and prestige construction largely stop.

The Assembly prioritizes:

- Food.
- Water.
- Medicine.
- Shelter.
- Heat and power.
- Transport.
- Records.
- Burial and public health.
- Communications.
- Limited defensive maintenance.

The terminal economy should feel radically different from ordinary wartime production. It should not reduce every output to zero, because the Assembly’s purpose is to keep humanity alive and organized.

## Diplomacy under the terminal state

Ordinary alliance rivalry loses importance among members.

Member diplomacy focuses on:

- Admission and observer status.
- Relief obligations.
- Demobilization verification.
- Prisoner release.
- Corridor access.
- Disputes over reserves and shelters.
- Treatment of holdouts.
- Current leadership inside the Assembly.

Guarantees, war goals, faction leadership, and claims should be blocked or reinterpreted where incompatible with member status.

## Member government forms

The Final Assembly does not erase national differences.

Members can remain:

- Custodial states.
- Communal federations.
- Pacifist administrations.
- Religious and civic concordats.
- Defensive civil-protection governments.
- Revolutionary vigil administrations.

Their focus choices and dominant currents shape the Assembly.

## Date arrival during the terminal state

The Last Day can arrive during or after consolidation.

Because the world end is already committed, the date does not restore normal event firing automatically.

The terminal design can resolve through a final epilogue based on the Assembly’s dominant current and institutional continuity:

- Peace Delegates confront the survival of humanity after global demobilization.
- Keepers of Revelation reinterpret the non-event through mercy, symbolic fulfillment, or schism.
- Communal Stewards preserve decentralized communities and resist restored states.
- Reserve Custodians begin a planned reopening.
- Avenging Witnesses face the consequences of judgment carried out under a failed prophecy.

The world-end state remains a campaign resolution. The game does not revert to the ordinary event pool because the date failed.

## Existing world-end conflicts

The Final Vigil cannot commit after another world end has already set the shared terminal state.

If another terminal threat is active but not yet committed, Event 049 can treat it as a confirmation shock. The separate owner’s readiness and branch rules remain authoritative.

The implementation must prevent two world-end super-events and terminal states from firing together.

## Nonhuman and special actors

Actual nonhuman countries do not join the ordinary Final Assembly as civilian administrations.

The Assembly can classify them as:

- A threat requiring defense.
- A population requiring unusual contact.
- An external force outside ordinary politics.
- A reason for continued holdout military capacity.

Special human Chaos countries can participate only if they use normal civilian systems and their owner design permits it.

## Multiplayer

Every player country receives its own national membership decision.

One player cannot force another player’s country into membership through a hidden global effect unless the country has already undergone a valid AI or event-owned government collapse outside player control under established multiplayer rules.

The super-event and world-end state are global. National follow-up choices remain country-specific.

## Super-event direction

The Final Vigil deserves a dedicated world-end super-event.

The image should show a mass final gathering, extinguished military activity, surrendered weapons, crowded public space, shelters, or a broad human assembly. It should not show Earth exploding or a literal supernatural event.

The text should make clear that humanity has surrendered ordinary political ambition while preserving uncertainty about the predicted end.

The quote, cultural remark, and audio must be researched through the super-event workflow. No final quote or track is specified in this planning file.

## Event Details direction

The public world-end row should explain:

- The premise that Doomsday administrations replace ordinary governments across enough of the world.
- The Final Assembly’s purpose around food, shelter, peace, and order.
- The collapse of conventional geopolitics.
- The fact that the route is political and does not mean literal planetary destruction.

It should not list raw thresholds, hidden readiness points, or secret path calculations.

## Terminal acceptance rules

- Chaos is at least 1000.
- Evolution II is enabled and active.
- The branch toggle is enabled.
- No other world end is active.
- A valid readiness path has matured over time.
- The shared world-end state is set once.
- Normal event firing stops.
- The correct Final Vigil super-event fires once.
- The Final Assembly forms with a valid dominant current.
- Member, observer, and holdout countries receive valid states.
- Wars are handled by relationship and context.
- No country is annexed solely because the Assembly forms.
- Actual nonhuman countries do not receive ordinary civilian membership.
- The Last Day does not undo the committed terminal state.
