# Event 049 Doomsday Specification

## Part 5: War, Economy, and Institutional Withdrawal

## War as a crisis of future meaning

Doomsday changes why people accept war.

A government can no longer rely on victory years later, postwar prosperity, future security, national development, or a promised historical destiny when large parts of society believe that only months or a few years remain.

The war system must preserve strategic differences. A population under invasion can still fight because surrender creates immediate suffering. A government pursuing distant conquest faces a much harder argument. A civil war over the identity of the state can either lose meaning or become a final judgment struggle.

## Per-war peace pressure

Peace pressure is hidden and connected to each relevant war and belligerent.

It rises from:

- High global Conviction.
- High local conviction.
- Short remaining time.
- Prolonged war without clear progress.
- Recent military and civilian casualties.
- Low War Support.
- Conscription strain and desertion.
- Lost capitals, encirclement, occupation, bombing, famine, and supply collapse.
- Strong pacifist society organization.
- A Peace Before the End posture.
- An enemy that has already offered a truce or demobilized.
- Allies leaving the war or joining the Final Assembly.

It falls from:

- Immediate invasion or existential defense.
- Recent decisive victory.
- A credible prospect of ending the war quickly.
- Strong apocalyptic state-loyalist organization.
- A Nothing Left to Lose posture.
- Enemy atrocities or occupation policy that make surrender intolerable.
- Reliable institutions and functioning military command.
- A defensive-only settlement that the public sees as necessary for survival.

Peace pressure must have country-specific results. It should not act as one global chance to end every war.

## War-type interpretation matrix

| War context | Normal Doomsday effect | Strong peace route | Strong continued-war route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Defensive war against invasion | Peace pressure rises slowly while territory and civilians remain threatened. | Local truce, evacuation corridor, limited armistice, or settlement that removes immediate danger. | Territorial defense framed as protection of families and final communities. |
| Offensive war of conquest | Peace pressure rises quickly as the date approaches. | Withdrawal, status-quo settlement, or renunciation of distant claims. | Nothing Left to Lose route that promises final victory at severe cost. |
| Ideological total war | Movements split between pacifism and final judgment. | Prisoner exchange, ceasefire congress, or government replacement. | State-loyalist and revolutionary currents intensify the conflict. |
| Civil war | Each side develops separate conviction, organization, and posture. | Local truces, shared vigils, prisoner exchange, constitutional congress, or exhaustion settlement. | Each side claims that the other must be defeated before the date. |
| Colonial war | Subject populations may interpret the date as the end of imperial rule. | Imperial withdrawal, local autonomy, ceasefire, or transfer to local councils. | Colonial repression or settler defense creates revolutionary escalation. |
| War of liberation | Peace pressure depends on whether occupation remains active. | Negotiated withdrawal or accelerated independence. | Resistance continues because liberation has immediate value before the date. |
| Faction-wide global war | Major-power choices affect every member. | Coordinated armistice conference and phased demobilization. | Holdout majors can keep the war alive while allies fragment. |
| Border war or limited conflict | The conflict becomes difficult to justify early. | Quick ceasefire, arbitration, or border freeze. | A state-loyalist government escalates before public opposition organizes. |

## Peace process stages

### Informal contact

Societies, religious networks, neutral states, prisoners’ families, or military intermediaries open contact.

This stage does not reduce military readiness automatically. Exposure can embarrass the government or create enemy propaganda.

### Limited humanitarian agreement

The sides can agree on prisoner exchange, burial, harvest access, evacuation, or a temporary local ceasefire.

This stage is available even when a full peace is impossible.

### Last Day truce

The sides suspend fighting around the predicted date for a defined period.

The truce must include breach rules. A government that violates it suffers severe Conviction, trust, and international effects.

### Armistice framework

The sides accept a military standstill, demobilization schedule, monitoring, and a political conference.

### Political settlement

A full settlement can use status quo, withdrawal, limited border revision, subject release, recognition, or a negotiated postwar arrangement.

The event should use existing peace and war systems where possible. It should not create a separate territorial settlement engine.

## Conditions for genuine peace

A war should end through Doomsday pressure only when one of these conditions exists:

- Both principal sides accept a settlement.
- One side’s government has collapsed and its successor accepts peace.
- Enough faction leaders accept a coordinated armistice.
- Military refusal makes continued operations impossible.
- A limited war reaches an agreed status quo.
- The Final Vigil terminal sequence imposes a member-to-member settlement under its world-end rules.

One country’s pacifism cannot force an enemy to surrender territory or end all wars by itself.

## Refusal consequences

When a government rejects a strong peace movement, pressure shifts into concrete consequences.

Possible outcomes include:

- Draft resistance.
- Desertion and missing formations.
- Units refusing offensive orders.
- Railway workers delaying military transport.
- Dockworkers refusing arms shipments.
- Military families joining public marches.
- Officers requesting a defensive-only mandate.
- Local truces made without central authorization.
- Cabinet collapse or legislative revolt.
- Revolutionary takeover after violent suppression.

The consequences should match organization and country institutions. Low organization produces individual desertion and absenteeism. High organization produces coordinated refusal and political capture.

## Defensive-only military service

Several routes can preserve a reduced military without accepting offensive war.

Defensive-only service allows units to:

- Guard cities, shelters, railways, hospitals, ports, and food depots.
- Defend controlled territory from invasion.
- Escort relief and evacuation.
- Maintain order under civilian authority.
- Refuse new offensive deployments outside defined territory.

This route reduces offensive capacity and alliance reliability. It preserves more manpower, command records, and post-date military continuity than chaotic demobilization.

## Nothing Left to Lose military window

The militarist route creates a limited period of restored War Support, recruitment, command obedience, and offensive tempo.

The window needs a named military objective. It cannot be a free general buff.

Examples include:

- Break one encirclement.
- Retake a capital region.
- Force a border settlement.
- Destroy an immediate invasion force.
- Reach a defensible line before the final month.
- Complete one final offensive against a rival government.

The country pays through manpower, equipment, stability, institutional continuity, and later demobilization pressure.

Failure creates:

- A sharp rise in peace pressure.
- Mutiny and refusal incidents.
- Increased revolutionary organization.
- Loss of confidence in state-loyalist societies.
- A severe failed-date military reconstruction burden.

Success can reduce immediate military danger and strengthen the regime. It should not erase the event’s social crisis.

## Military institution withdrawal

The armed forces suffer in several layers.

### Recruitment

Potential recruits question long service commitments. Conscription systems face legal refusal, evasion, family resistance, and absent registrants.

### Training

Cadets, instructors, and reservists leave programs whose completion date approaches the Last Day.

### Deployment

Units may agree to defend home regions while refusing distant fronts.

### Command

Officers divide between state loyalty, pacifism, personal survival, religious duty, and political ambition.

### Maintenance

Depots, mechanics, railway troops, medical units, and clerical staff become harder to retain. A country can keep nominal divisions while losing practical readiness.

### Demobilization

Orderly demobilization preserves records, weapons, depots, and trained personnel. Chaotic demobilization scatters equipment and creates local armed groups.

The game effects should distinguish these layers where possible. A single recruitable-population penalty is insufficient for the whole military story.

## Alliance and faction behavior

Doomsday pressure tests alliances.

Possible alliance outcomes include:

- Members demanding a common truce policy.
- A major power threatening members that demobilize.
- Pacifist members refusing offensive operations while retaining defensive guarantees.
- Final Assembly members withdrawing from military command structures.
- Holdout members seizing shared stockpiles or bases.
- Smaller allies seeking separate peace.
- An alliance becoming a humanitarian transport and observation framework after military collapse.

Existing factions should not disappear through one global effect. Member decisions and faction leadership matter.

## Economy of a disappearing future

The economic crisis begins when households, firms, workers, governments, and investors stop valuing outcomes beyond the predicted date.

The event should affect time horizons, not every sector equally.

Short production runs, repairs, food, transport, medicine, shelter, and immediate consumption can remain active or expand. Long construction, education, capital investment, shipbuilding, research, and deferred savings suffer first.

## Household behavior

Households may:

- Spend savings on family reunion, food, travel, celebration, pilgrimage, shelter, or fraudulent protection.
- Stop paying long-term debts.
- Sell property or durable assets at unstable prices.
- Leave urban employment for communal settlements.
- Keep cash or goods outside banks.
- Donate wealth to religious, pacifist, or relief organizations.
- Hoard food, fuel, medicine, and tools.
- Destroy wills, records, or property claims.

The country’s posture changes the dominant outcome.

Civic Continuity encourages ordinary contracts and public guarantees. National Preparation redirects savings into shelters and reserves. Concordat routes channel spending into relief. Emergency Order tries to prevent flight through controls. Nothing Left to Lose redirects resources into final mobilization.

## Finance and debt

The financial system faces:

- Bank runs and withdrawal pressure.
- Collapse of long-maturity lending.
- Refusal to service debts that mature after the date.
- Insurance disputes over doomsday clauses and abandoned property.
- State pressure to freeze accounts or force investment.
- Rapid inflation in shelter, food, medicine, land, and transport.
- Worthless prestige assets and speculative protection products.
- Local currencies or communal account systems.

The player should see major policy choices, not a separate finance minigame.

Useful actions include deposit guarantees, debt freezes, debt settlement, rationing, controlled withdrawals, public reserve bonds, and prosecution of fraudulent protection markets.

A debt jubilee can stabilize households and weaken banks, tax systems, and post-date recovery. A strict contract policy preserves finance and raises public anger. A targeted settlement can preserve essential institutions while accepting losses.

## Construction and infrastructure

Construction effects depend on completion time and public meaning.

Projects most vulnerable to abandonment include:

- Battleships and long naval programs.
- Monumental public works.
- Distant infrastructure with no immediate survival role.
- Long industrial expansion.
- Universities, laboratories, and planned cities.
- Civilian housing intended for future growth.
- Strategic fortification without an immediate threat.

Projects that can retain support include:

- Rail repair.
- Ports used for food and family movement.
- Hospitals.
- Shelters.
- Water systems.
- Food storage.
- Local power generation.
- Repair workshops.
- Defensive works protecting current population centers.

The player should choose which projects survive. The event should not cancel every queued construction automatically.

## Industrial labor

Industrial effects should distinguish worker absence, skilled-worker loss, and deliberate conversion.

Possible incidents include:

- A shipyard loses workers because its vessels will finish after the date.
- A tank plant retains workers under a final offensive pledge.
- A rail workshop becomes a communal repair center.
- A chemical plant is abandoned because workers fear an industrial accident.
- A civilian factory shifts to shelters and storage.
- Skilled engineers leave for protected continuity sites.
- Factory councils take control of food and wage distribution.

The country can preserve priority sites through missions, concessions, coercion, or conversion.

## Military production

Military production does not decline uniformly.

A pacifist or custodial route converts lines toward transport, support equipment, civil defense, and relief.

A state-loyalist route accelerates arms production during a short window and suffers later exhaustion.

A fragmented country loses output through absenteeism, seizure, local control, and broken supply.

A survivalist current can divert weapons and vehicles into private stockpiles. A revolutionary current can seize depots.

## Research and education

Research suffers through loss of people, institutions, and time horizon.

### Student withdrawal

Students leave long programs that will not finish before the date.

### Researcher departure

Researchers join communities, religious groups, shelters, foreign institutions, or continuity sites.

### Project cancellation

Governments and firms cancel work whose payoff lies beyond the date.

### Knowledge preservation

Archives, libraries, laboratories, blueprints, data, seed collections, and trained teams can be protected.

### Short-horizon research

Medicine, food preservation, communications, shelters, civil defense, weather observation, and emergency logistics may gain priority.

The event should reduce general research capacity while allowing selected emergency work. It should not delete technologies already researched.

## Education response routes

Civic Continuity preserves ordinary schools and long courses.

Concordat routes combine public education with religious, civic, or communal instruction.

Emergency Order can compel attendance and staff retention, which may preserve capacity and raise backlash.

National Preparation creates emergency curricula around medicine, agriculture, engineering, records, and civil defense.

Doomsday administrations may shorten education into practical final-years training. This helps immediate survival and weakens deep research.

## Agriculture and food

The event affects food through labor movement, hoarding, transport, settlement growth, and loss of future planting incentives.

Farmers may refuse to plant crops harvested after the predicted date. Others plant more because feeding communities becomes a moral duty. Survivalists hoard seed. Communal groups organize shared harvests. Governments requisition food or distribute reserves.

The famine system retains ownership of Food Security, Food Reserves, Relief Access, mortality, and famine decisions.

Event 049 can:

- Add bounded labor, hoarding, transport, or governance pressure to famine inputs.
- Improve relief access through successful rail, port, shelter, and council actions.
- Provide reserves through completed preparation projects.
- Trigger movement or evacuation requests through the migration adapter.
- Receive famine severity as a confirmation shock.

It cannot duplicate famine’s visible values, mortality transaction, or decision ledger.

## Migration and settlement

Doomsday settlement movement can be internal or cross-border.

Motives include:

- Joining a religious or communal settlement.
- Leaving a city believed to be unsafe.
- Reuniting families before the date.
- Reaching a pilgrimage site.
- Escaping conscription or suppression.
- Moving toward food, shelter, or a Final Assembly administration.

The migration system owns real population transfer, route harm, reception, border policy, and settlement.

Event 049 can request movement and use returned outcomes. It cannot subtract or add population separately for the same movement.

Closed borders should create trapped people and political pressure. Violent pushback uses the migration and Deaths contracts.

## Ports, railways, and transport

Transport becomes a central institutional test.

Railways and ports carry:

- Food and medicine.
- Family reunion traffic.
- Pilgrims and society delegates.
- Troops and military supplies.
- Refugees and settlers.
- Archives and continuity staff.
- Shelter materials and fuel.

The player should face allocation choices. A train used for final military concentration cannot simultaneously carry civilians and food. A convoy route opened for pilgrimage can strain imports or security.

Transport missions should name actual states, rail hubs, ports, or regions where possible.

## Public order and crime

Disorder comes from several sources:

- Fraudulent protection markets.
- Hoarding and theft.
- Elite flight.
- Abandoned police posts.
- Crowds blocking routes.
- Hedonist celebrations.
- Revolutionary seizure.
- Repressive raids.
- Armed survival settlements.
- Unpaid workers and soldiers.

The government should have targeted responses. A general crackdown should be a posture choice with broad consequences, not the answer to every incident.

## Elite behavior

Political, military, financial, and industrial elites can:

- Flee to protected sites.
- Move wealth abroad.
- Sponsor private shelters.
- Embrace the Last Day publicly.
- Fund continuity institutions.
- Join state-loyalist final-war movements.
- Negotiate with society leaders.
- Destroy records of debt, property, or abuse.

Elite flight should damage legitimacy and continuity. A fair preparation program should reduce revolutionary growth. Exclusive shelters should strengthen avenging currents.

## International trade

Trade contracts become unstable when delivery, repayment, or industrial benefit falls beyond the predicted date.

Possible effects include:

- Shorter contracts and immediate payment demands.
- Export restrictions on food, fuel, medicine, and transport equipment.
- Final-year barter and reserve agreements.
- Countries refusing to supply a state that is expected to demobilize.
- Final Assembly members pooling relief and transport.
- Holdouts using trade access to pressure pacifist states.

The event should use existing trade and economic systems. It does not need a separate global commodity market.

## Useful preparation after failure

Preparation should not be a pure mistake if the date passes.

Shelters can become:

- Civil-defense centers.
- Hospitals.
- Housing.
- Food and seed storage.
- Archives.
- Emergency command sites.

Reserves can reduce later famine or disaster harm. Duplicated archives improve reconstruction. Trained relief councils can become permanent civil-defense institutions. Rail and port repair remains useful.

The failed-date cost comes from debt, canceled projects, distorted investment, unequal access, and institutions that were abandoned to build the preparations.

## Economic recovery after the date

Recovery should occur in stages.

### Stabilize contracts and currency

Resolve bank runs, debt freezes, wages, emergency currencies, and property claims.

### Recall labor and skilled personnel

Reopen workplaces and negotiate the return of communal or survival settlements.

### Restart transport and essential production

Prioritize rail, food, power, medicine, and repair.

### Resume long projects

Choose which canceled programs remain relevant and which are abandoned permanently.

### Restore research and education

Rebuild institutions according to preserved records and staff.

### Settle military status

Reconstitute forces, confirm defensive service, or retain a demilitarized political identity.

A country with strong institutional continuity can move through these stages quickly. A country that destroyed records, dispersed staff, and emptied reserves faces a multi-year aftermath.

## Balance rules

- No opening penalty should make a normal country unplayable by itself.
- High Conviction and short remaining time can create severe combined pressure, but the player must retain a response.
- Emergency preparation produces real protection and real opportunity cost.
- Repression provides short-term control and serious long-term risk.
- Pacifist peace can save a losing country and expose it to an aggressive enemy.
- Nothing Left to Lose provides a meaningful military window and a severe aftermath.
- Research and construction penalties need floors before terminal commitment.
- Conversion of arms production must change production choices, not grant free civilian equipment.
- Event 049 cannot duplicate famine, migration, Deaths, Condemnation, Air Cleanliness, or generic war and peace Chaos effects.
