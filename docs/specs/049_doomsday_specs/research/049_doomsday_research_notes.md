# Event 049 Research Notes

## Research purpose

The research supports social structure, regional variation, suppression outcomes, peace pressure, and failed-prophecy aftermath. It does not claim that the fictional global convergence has a real historical counterpart.

The event should use historical material as design grounding. It should avoid copying a real religion, pacifist movement, or persecuted community into a generic apocalyptic villain.

## Interwar pacifism as an organized political force

The interwar period supports a model in which antiwar conviction becomes an active international network with political and humanitarian functions.

War Resisters’ International traces its origin to a 1921 meeting of conscientious objectors in Bilthoven. The Peace Pledge Union emerged in Britain during the interwar crisis and developed transnational links. The 1932 World Disarmament Conference also drew broad civil-society interest after the First World War, even though the diplomatic process failed and collective security weakened.

Design use:

- Pacifist Doomsday Societies should organize prisoner support, legal defense, shelter networks, relief work, public pledges, and cross-border congresses.
- Refusal of military service should create individual and institutional consequences beyond a flat War Support penalty.
- Peace organizations can keep food distribution, medical support, and refugee relief functioning while opposing mobilization.
- A pacifist current can become politically powerful without becoming a country tag or armed rebel faction.

## Religious cooperation and institutional networks

The World Council of Churches history shows that large transnational church cooperation was already developing in the late 1930s. Its 1937 Oxford conference addressed the relation between church, community, state, and the growth of totalitarian power.

Design use:

- Governments can work with broad religious and civic councils without endorsing one prophecy as official truth.
- A cooperative response can preserve order, keep schools and hospitals open, and channel mass gatherings into relief work.
- Religious networks can distribute information and supplies across borders when state institutions weaken.
- Cooperation should be a negotiated posture with gains and risks. It can strengthen trusted local institutions while shifting legitimacy away from the state.

## Conscientious refusal under coercive states

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum documents the Nazi persecution of Jehovah’s Witnesses for refusing military service, political oaths, salutes, and participation in state organizations.

Design use:

- Suppression can reduce open organization while increasing clandestine conviction and international outrage.
- Prisoner networks and family support can preserve a movement after arrests.
- Military refusal should be connected to oath, deployment, and mobilization systems, not portrayed only as cowardice.
- The event should never turn a persecuted real community into the secret source of the crisis.

## Prophetic and redemptive movements

Research on Oomoto, Yiguandao, and the John Frum movement shows that prophetic or redemptive movements can become social institutions, anti-colonial vehicles, communal systems, or targets of state repression.

Oomoto’s own history presents the 1935 suppression as mass arrest and destruction of religious sites. Because this is a movement-owned source, it is useful for understanding the movement’s memory of persecution, not as the only neutral account.

Academic work on Yiguandao provides a basis for a large redemptive movement that spreads through organization, ritual, moral reform, and networks. Smithsonian’s account of John Frum links prophecy with anti-colonial expectation and resistance to missionary and colonial authority.

Design use:

- Colonial and occupied territories can read the Last Day as the end of an unjust political order.
- A movement can be devotional, communal, anti-colonial, or millenarian without being uniformly violent.
- State destruction of meeting places can decentralize a movement and strengthen its martyr narrative.
- Local movements should have distinct social demands even when they share the global date.

## Astronomical fear and consumer behavior

Science Museum material on Halley’s Comet records long traditions of treating comets as omens. It also notes anxiety around the 1910 return, including attempts to seal homes and the sale or use of anti-comet remedies.

Design use:

- The event should create practical mass behavior such as spending savings, buying false remedies, sealing homes, stockpiling, leaving cities, and joining vigils.
- Commercial opportunism should appear through fraud, speculative collapse, inflated shelter markets, and miracle products.
- Scientific reassurance can help when institutions are trusted. Failed or contradictory reassurance can strengthen the belief that authorities are concealing evidence.

## Failed prophecy and adaptive survival

The research literature around failed prophecy rejects a simple assumption that every group disappears after disconfirmation. Communities can reinterpret the date, deny that the prediction failed, claim that collective action prevented disaster, shift the event into symbolic meaning, or split into rival explanations.

Design use:

- The failed Last Day should cause a large Conviction collapse, but not an automatic global reset to zero.
- Movement response depends on organization, social costs, suppression history, and whether leaders made falsifiable claims.
- A revised-date hardliner branch is allowed once as a bounded aftermath problem. It cannot restart the full event loop indefinitely.
- Governments that preserved schools, records, logistics, and public trust recover faster than governments that relied only on force.

## Disarmament failure and the limits of moral pressure

The failure of interwar disarmament supports a hard rule for the peace system. Moral pressure alone should not guarantee peace when strategic fear, territorial claims, ideological stakes, and weak enforcement remain.

Design use:

- Peace pressure is calculated by war context, casualties, duration, war support, territorial momentum, and government posture.
- A bilateral truce requires action by each side or political breakdown severe enough to force a ceasefire.
- A state can resist pacifism by presenting the war as immediate survival.
- Aggressive regimes can use the Last Day to justify accelerated conquest.

## Regional design cautions

- Do not treat Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Buddhism, Hindu traditions, Chinese redemptive movements, Pacific movements, indigenous traditions, or secular politics as internally uniform.
- Do not assign irrationality to a culture or religion as a permanent national trait.
- Do not use a real persecuted group as the event’s hidden mastermind.
- Do not make repression mechanically correct in every authoritarian country.
- Do not make liberal or democratic governments automatically competent.
- Let war, occupation, social trust, disasters, famine, inequality, and existing institutions shape movement strength.

## Strong design conclusions

1. The societies need practical institutions beyond belief labels.
2. Pacifism needs missions, prisoner support, demobilization pressure, and relief work.
3. Suppression should disorganize open activity and increase hidden backlash.
4. Religious cooperation can preserve state capacity while shifting political legitimacy.
5. Failed prophecy should create splits, reinterpretation, and reconstruction.
6. The terminal route needs broad surrender of political purpose, not a single high Conviction threshold.
7. Regional variation should come from campaign state and institutions, not stereotypes.
