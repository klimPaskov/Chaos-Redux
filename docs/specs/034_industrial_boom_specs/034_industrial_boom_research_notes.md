# Event 34 Industrial Boom research notes

## Research purpose

The research was used to identify concrete forces that can drive an extraordinary industrial expansion and concrete pressures that can make the same expansion unstable. Event 34 remains fictional and exaggerated. Historical institutions and episodes supply useful mechanisms, visual details, and consequences. They do not define one national route or imply that every target country follows the United States wartime model.

## Industrial mobilization and material allocation

The United States War Production Board and its predecessor bodies coordinated procurement, production, critical materials, priorities, conservation, construction, and industrial reconversion. The National Archives record includes the Supply Priorities Allocation Board, Controlled Materials Plan regulations, requirements committees, conservation committees, resources protection work, and a dedicated history of industrial mobilization and reconversion. Combined United States and United Kingdom bodies coordinated raw materials and production while trying to reduce shipping demands and preserve essential civilian needs.

Design use:

- The boom can be real only when raw materials, transport, and factory schedules remain coordinated.
- `Stabilize Supply Chains` should commit actual logistics and civilian capacity.
- Protected Industrial Regions should reduce the effect of damaged bridges, railways, ports, and plants on Overheating.
- Material allocation and construction restraint are credible cooling tools.
- Reconversion belongs in the controlled-landing phase. The bonuses should not vanish instantly.

Primary source guide:

- National Archives, Records of the War Production Board, Record Group 179.
- National Archives, records of the Combined Raw Materials Board and Combined Production and Resources Board within Record Group 179.

## Cheap finance, inflation pressure, rationing, and credit control

Federal Reserve History describes wartime policy that kept financing cheap while incomes, employment, and the money supply rose and consumer goods became scarce. Price and wage controls, rationing, consumer credit rules, and short loan maturities were used to contain inflationary pressure. The same source describes guaranteed industrial loans that expedited expansion.

Design use:

- Baseline Industrial Boom can combine abundant finance with a real shortage risk.
- Speculative Mania can amplify the part of the boom driven by cheap credit and confidence.
- `Cool the Expansion` can represent credit restraint, contract cancellation, and slower project approval.
- `Build Industrial Reserves` can represent spare capacity and inventories that are deliberately withheld from immediate output.
- A boom can appear healthy in output terms while Overheating rises through shortages and credit expansion.

Primary source:

- Federal Reserve History, The Federal Reserve's Role During WWII.

## Price administration and rationing

The National Archives records for the Office of Price Administration cover rationing, enforcement, fuel accounting, consumer goods, transport, utilities, price controls, and regulated industries. The agency attempted to stabilize prices and rents and ration scarce essential commodities. Most controls were removed after the war, which forms a useful contrast between emergency expansion and difficult normalization.

Design use:

- Cooling and stabilization decisions should impose visible short-term sacrifices.
- Regulation can lower Overheating while creating stability, war support, or output costs depending on government and war conditions.
- Speculative Mania should make restraint more politically difficult.
- Black-market or evasion incidents can raise Overheating without creating a second corruption meter.

Primary source guides:

- National Archives, Records of the Office of Price Administration, Record Group 188.
- National Archives, America on the Homefront.

## Labor shortage, inexperienced workers, and safety

The Bureau of Labor Statistics describes the wartime shift from labor surplus to labor shortage. The National Park Service describes rapid defense-industry expansion, inexperienced workers, crowded machinery, multiple shifts, reduced maintenance time, older converted machines, and weaker safety enforcement. These conditions increased accidents and injuries.

Design use:

- Labor pressure remains a hidden contributor to Overheating.
- High Overheating can create accidents, temporary factory disruption, stability pressure, and project setbacks.
- Industrial safety incidents should be state-linked and scaled by the concentration of boom projects.
- The player can protect regions and build reserves, but cannot turn the event into a separate labor-management simulator.
- Runaway Industrialization may create severe maintenance debt and recurring industrial accidents without using mass civilian death as cheap spectacle.

Primary sources:

- Bureau of Labor Statistics, The Current Population Survey, tracking unemployment in the United States for 75 years.
- National Park Service, Hazards on the Home Front, Workplace Accidents and Injuries during World War II.

## Prefabrication, regional networks, and extraordinary output

The National Park Service account of San Francisco Bay shipbuilding describes a dispersed network of shipyards, machine shops, metal fabricators, and wood fabricators. It records a rise from 23 ships launched in the decade before 1940 to 4,600 ships in the five years after 1940. Bay Area yards built 1,400 vessels during the war. The account attributes this expansion to national commitment, cooperation, existing skills, facilities, labor access, and logistics.

Design use:

- The initial event bonus should be genuinely extreme.
- The Industrial Miracle can develop networks of linked states, railways, factories, and suppliers.
- Miracle Regions should reward an existing industrial base, infrastructure, and adjacent expansion potential.
- Runaway Industrialization can spread from existing centers into neighboring states.
- Permanent legacy should come from completed networks and production practices, not from unexplained free factory levels.

Primary source:

- National Park Service, World War II Shipbuilding in the San Francisco Bay Area.

## Speculation and diverted capital

Federal Reserve History describes 1920s margin accounts that allowed stock purchases with borrowed money and records Federal Reserve concern that speculative credit diverted resources from commerce and industry. The market collapse provides a historical basis for a boom that looks self-sustaining until credit confidence breaks.

Design use:

- Evolution I should create sudden speculative opportunities and sudden Overheating spikes.
- Some high-yield projects should be fragile and easier to cancel before they become embedded.
- `Liquidate Speculative Projects` can be an emergency action that lowers Overheating but damages stability and temporary output.
- The event should not model stock prices as a second public value.

Primary source:

- Federal Reserve History, Stock Market Crash of 1929.

## Overworked resources and the output gap

The International Monetary Fund describes an output gap as an economy running at an inefficient rate through overworking or underworking resources. Event 34 exaggerates the overworking side into a playable crisis.

Design use:

- Overheating can serve as one aggregate public value for labor, capital, logistics, materials, infrastructure, and price pressure.
- A high output bonus and a rising crisis value can coexist without contradiction.
- Cooling should reduce immediate performance because it returns part of the economy toward sustainable capacity.

Primary source:

- International Monetary Fund, Back to Basics, What Is the Output Gap?

## Logistics reliability

The World Bank describes supply-chain reliability as central to logistics performance and links it to transport infrastructure, border controls, services, and reliable import access.

Design use:

- Infrastructure, trade access, fuel, trains, convoys, ports, and control of industrial regions should affect Overheating drift.
- A landlocked country and a maritime trading country should pay different stabilization costs.
- A blockade, destroyed port, lost rail hub, or convoy crisis should create a larger shock when the boom depends heavily on imported materials.

Primary source:

- World Bank, Boosting trade and economic development through better logistics.

## Postwar inflation and the landing problem

Federal Reserve History records high United States inflation after World War II, including 17.6 percent CPI inflation between June 1946 and June 1947. The policy conflict between cheap financing and inflation control shows that ending an extraordinary production regime can remain difficult after the immediate emergency ends.

Design use:

- A controlled landing should be a real phase with a public objective.
- Removing emergency controls too quickly can produce a late Overheating rebound.
- A country may preserve capacity while losing part of the extraordinary bonus.
- A poor landing can enter Event 35 even when the country avoided the first terminal threshold.

Primary source:

- Federal Reserve History, The Treasury-Fed Accord.

## Research boundaries

The specification does not claim that one historical institution maps directly onto every country. The following elements are fictional extrapolations:

- An economy-wide Overheating value on a 0 to 100 scale.
- Instant event-driven output comparable to doubling effective productivity.
- Industrial development that reaches physically impossible speed under Evolutions II and III.
- A direct mechanical inheritance from Industrial Boom into Great Depression 2.0.
- Chaos gains from impossible industrial manifestations.

The historical material supports the causal structure, resource choices, incidents, and presentation. The evolved scale belongs to Chaos Redux.
