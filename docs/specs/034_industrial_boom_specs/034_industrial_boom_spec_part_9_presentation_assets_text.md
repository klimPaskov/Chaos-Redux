# Industrial Boom specification, part 9: Presentation, assets, and text direction

## Presentation concept

Industrial Boom should look productive, crowded, and physically strained. The visual center is industrial work in motion: furnaces, rail sidings, cranes, workers, machine halls, stacked materials, construction, smoke, electric light, and overloaded transport. The event should not default to a map, stock chart, boardroom, or generic ministry desk.

The presentation changes from abundance to strain through state, category, and report variants. It should remain readable and period-appropriate.

## Decision category presentation

### Category layout direction

The ordinary decision category should provide:

- A strong static category picture.
- One compact Overheating display.
- A trend arrow or equivalent non-text cue.
- A visible next-threshold marker.
- Reserve status.
- A short landing forecast once relevant.
- A compact selected-region area when state projects are active.

The category picture may change by broad phase:

1. Productive surge.
2. Stretched industrial system.
3. Dangerous overheating.
4. Landing and reconversion.

Phase variants are useful because the category remains active for months. They should be separate finished images or coordinated generated outputs, not one picture recolored locally.

### Meter direction

The Overheating meter should use:

- A clear 0 to 100 range.
- Threshold ticks.
- A stable low-pressure visual at the left.
- Increasing heat, congestion, and danger toward the right.
- A readable current marker.
- Text and icon cues in addition to color.

The meter should avoid a literal modern dashboard or financial trading interface. It belongs inside HOI4's period interface language.

A static meter with changing fill frames is adequate. Animation is not required for clarity. A later implementation may add a restrained warning pulse at terminal pressure only if the frame-animation workflow creates real source frames and the static fallback remains clear.

### State selection direction

State-targeted actions should use the ordinary decision targeting pattern or a compact selector. The player should see only current Industrial Regions, active project states, and vulnerable spread states. The category should not list every controlled state.

State entries should show:

- State name.
- Project role.
- Broad stage.
- Protected, tested, compromised, integrated, fragile, or threatened status.
- The current action that can be taken.

## Event surface map

### Opening event

Viewpoint:

- The affected country's government and industrial authorities.

Visible information:

- Production and construction accelerating.
- Orders, labor demand, rail traffic, and new shifts.
- The first Industrial Regions.
- The existence of Overheating as a management problem.

Uncertainty:

- No single policy or market change explains the full scale.
- The event should not confirm a supernatural cause at baseline.

Tone:

- Confident and practical.
- The benefit is real.
- The final paragraphs should show crowded systems and mounting commitments without declaring that collapse is inevitable.

Option direction:

- One reaction that embraces the opportunity with restrained confidence.
- Avoid generic approval text.
- Avoid a joke that undermines the core economic choice.

Dynamic references:

- Target country.
- One or two leading Industrial Regions.
- Current war state when it materially changes the opening.

### First threshold report

Viewpoint:

- Factory managers, transport officials, local authorities, and workers.

Visible information:

- Delays, missing inputs, crowded rail, maintenance, housing, and overtime.

Tone:

- Concerned but manageable.
- The report should show causes through concrete details.

Option direction:

- Practical acknowledgement of the need to choose priorities.

### Dangerous imbalance report

Viewpoint:

- National leadership receiving evidence that the expansion is damaging its own foundation.

Visible information:

- Production interruptions, unsafe conditions, failed projects, and material queues.

Tone:

- Serious and specific.
- No cheap comedy around industrial injury or mass unemployment.

Option direction:

- A cold commitment to choose between output and control.

### Controlled landing report

Viewpoint:

- The country after emergency pace has been reduced.

Visible information:

- Which projects survived.
- Which regions retained infrastructure or practices.
- The disappearance of extraordinary output.
- A more sustainable industrial base.

Tone:

- Measured and concrete.
- Avoid triumphal claims when the country accepted large sacrifices.

Dynamic references:

- Landing quality.
- Named converted regions.
- Number or type of secured projects.

### Rough or forced landing report

Viewpoint:

- The government managing cancellations, idle shifts, maintenance, and unfinished works.

Visible information:

- The boom ended without a full depression.
- Temporary exhaustion and limited legacy remain.

Tone:

- Sober.
- Avoid implying that the country escaped without cost.

### Crash transition report

Viewpoint:

- The same country as orders, finance, and production fail.

Visible information:

- The abrupt removal of boom conditions.
- Idle or disrupted Industrial Regions.
- The immediate Event 35 crisis.

Tone:

- Severe and direct.
- No raw formulas, inheritance stage numbers, or implementation language.

The final Event 35 opening text belongs to Event 35, but it should use the source marker and inherited state for tailored wording.

## Evolution text direction

### Speculative Mania

Text should show:

- Endless project announcements.
- Firms and local authorities treating future capacity as certain.
- Pressure against restraint.
- Contracts and credit growing faster than verified work.

Humor mode:

- Mild official irony is acceptable in small incidents.
- Serious failures use euphemism, denial, or self-damning confidence. Avoid jokes.

Avoid:

- Modern stock-market slang unless it fits the period and country.
- Generic lines about bubbles bursting.
- Exact historical claims that do not fit the target country.

### The Industrial Miracle

Text should show:

- Completed work ahead of any credible schedule.
- Missing material records.
- Engineers unable to reconcile output and installed capacity.
- Foreign interest when the result becomes observable.

Tone:

- Technical wonder and uncertainty.
- Do not tell the player that the event is a warning or that the miracle is evil.

Avoid:

- Final religious quotations or slogans.
- A supernatural explanation before the mechanic establishes one.

### Runaway Industrialization

Text should show:

- Work beginning beyond approved boundaries.
- Railways, mines, factories, and settlements spreading into neighboring states.
- National records failing to keep pace.
- Productive networks that are hard to stop.

Tone:

- Administrative control under real pressure.
- Concrete and region-specific.

Avoid:

- Describing the industry as sentient unless a later event establishes sentience.
- Generic map-change language.
- Repetitive claims that growth cannot be stopped.

## Decision text direction

Every decision should communicate:

- The public action.
- The visible cost.
- The immediate expected direction of Overheating.
- The output, construction, state, project, or landing tradeoff.
- The duration or cooldown.
- Important dynamic factors.

Decision descriptions should not reveal hidden incident chances, exact AI weights, secret evolution conditions, or future Event 35 branches.

### Run the Economy Hot

Speaker and stance:

- Industrial leadership choosing maximum use of capacity.

Tone:

- Confident and deliberate.
- At high pressure, the same family should sound more coercive and less celebratory.

Dynamic references:

- Current Overheating.
- Duration.
- Production benefit.
- Additional pressure.

### Stabilize Supply Chains

Speaker and stance:

- Transport, procurement, and production authorities coordinating scarce movement capacity.

Tone:

- Practical and logistical.

Dynamic references:

- Trains, trucks, convoys, fuel, civilian commitment.
- Strongest current bottleneck.
- Expected relief band.

### Build Industrial Reserves

Speaker and stance:

- Authorities choosing spare capacity and repair stores over immediate output.

Tone:

- Restrained and strategic.

Dynamic references:

- Current reserve status.
- Output sacrifice.
- Shock and landing benefit.

### Cool the Expansion

Speaker and stance:

- Government imposing limits on contracts, finance, construction, and shifts.

Tone:

- Serious.
- Under Speculative Mania, text may show resistance and political cost.

Dynamic references:

- Overheating relief.
- Temporary output loss.
- Duration.

### Protect Key Industrial Regions

Speaker and stance:

- National and local authorities hardening one state.

Tone:

- Concrete and state-specific.

Dynamic references:

- State name.
- Project role.
- Current vulnerability.
- Equipment and civilian commitment.

## Tooltip direction

### Overheating tooltip

Should explain:

- What the value represents.
- Current band.
- Current trend.
- Next threshold.
- Up to three important causes.
- Which available action addresses each cause.

Should not show:

- Every hidden component.
- Long arithmetic.
- Raw internal variable names.

### Reserve tooltip

Should explain:

- Current qualitative state.
- Which shock it can absorb.
- Whether it is depleted.
- How it affects landing and Event 35 severity.

### Region tooltip

Should explain:

- Project purpose.
- Current stage.
- Protection and fragility status.
- Main progress factor.
- What may convert at landing.

### Landing forecast tooltip

Should explain:

- Broad outcome forecast.
- Main positive and negative factors.
- Required public objective.
- The fact that external shocks can still change the result.

It should not give a false exact percentage when the final result is dynamic.

## Report and category artwork

### Main opening report image

Source mode:

- Generated fictional period documentary scene.

Subject:

- A large factory hall or rail-linked industrial complex operating at extraordinary pace.

Composition:

- Workers and machinery in the foreground.
- Finished equipment, crates, rail wagons, cranes, furnaces, or assembly lines showing volume.
- Signs of strain such as crowding, temporary scaffolding, stacked materials, and several shifts.

Period direction:

- 1936 to 1945 industrial technology, clothing, safety equipment, lighting, transport, and architecture.

Avoid:

- Modern robotics.
- Digital displays.
- Contemporary high-visibility clothing.
- Readable generated text.
- A generic war room.
- A map as the subject.
- A modern financial chart.

### Category picture, productive phase

Subject:

- Coordinated industrial flow from factory to rail or port.

Mood:

- Energetic, useful, busy.

Gameplay purpose:

- Establishes that the category manages real production and logistics.

### Category picture, strain phase

Subject:

- The same economic identity under congestion, maintenance, and material shortage.

Mood:

- Overloaded, crowded, still productive.

Gameplay purpose:

- Shows that pressure has changed the state without pretending the category is a separate disaster screen.

### Category picture, landing phase

Subject:

- Reduced shift intensity, inspection, repair, conversion, and retained infrastructure.

Mood:

- Controlled transition.

Gameplay purpose:

- Communicates that the event is ending through management.

The phase pictures should be coordinated but separately generated for their states. Local recoloring or smoke overlays do not count as separate final art.

## Icon inventory

Every icon family needs its own reference inspection and source art.

### Decision category icon

Motif:

- A factory complex with a heat gauge, overworked flywheel, or compressed industrial symbol.

Readability goal:

- One strong silhouette that remains clear at category size.

### Core decision icons

1. Run the Economy Hot
   - Overdriven flywheel, furnace, or production line.
2. Stabilize Supply Chains
   - Rail, truck, port crane, or linked material flow.
3. Build Industrial Reserves
   - Spare machine tools, stored components, or reserve capacity.
4. Cool the Expansion
   - Throttled machinery, closed valve, or reduced furnace intensity.
5. Protect Key Industrial Regions
   - Factory with repair shield, redundant rail, or civil-defense protection.

Each decision icon should be designed for its actual decision-icon consumer. Do not resize the category icon to satisfy the decision family.

### Project icons

- Production Practices.
- Rail and Supply Integration.
- Factory Conversion Capacity.
- Industrial Repair Network.
- Resource Efficiency.
- Permanent Industrial Capacity.
- Synthetic and Recycling Complex.
- Controlled Industrial Corridor.

Project icons may form a coordinated family. Each still needs distinct source art and a clear final-size silhouette.

### Country effect icons

Needed states:

- Industrial Boom baseline.
- Industrial Boom under Speculative Mania.
- Industrial Miracle.
- Runaway Industrialization.
- Cooling period.
- Industrial exhaustion.
- Post-boom vulnerability.
- Retained industrial legacy.

The implementation should prefer staged replacement or dynamic modifiers over a large simultaneous stack. Icon planning should follow the lifecycle and avoid many unrelated spirits.

### State modifier icons

Needed states:

- Industrial Region.
- Protected Industrial Region.
- Tested or compromised protection.
- Active project.
- Secured legacy project.
- Fragile Miracle Region.
- Evolution III spread state.
- Abandoned or unfinished works.
- State exhaustion.

Where several states are visually related, use a coordinated family with clear symbol or frame differences. Color alone is insufficient.

### Achievement icons

Six achievement icon directions are defined in part 10 and in the achievement prompt. The final achievement system may require completed, grey, and not-eligible variants according to the project pattern.

## Asset production rules

The asset worker should:

- Inspect the exact canonical reference family and contact sheet before production.
- Create the contact sheet when the required family lacks one.
- Use native transparency for alpha-backed icons.
- Preserve source PNGs, processed PNGs, final DDS files, prompts, source mode, and manifests.
- Review icons at actual in-game size and on contrasting backgrounds.
- Keep each asset type separate.
- Place final assets in the event-scoped runtime folders required by the consumer.
- Use the temporary event asset workspace only during active production and review.
- Promote durable provenance and coverage facts before the temporary workspace is removed.

## Accessibility and clarity

- Overheating status must use text, icons, thresholds, and shape in addition to color.
- Trend should use arrow or motion direction plus text.
- Protected and fragile state markers must have distinct symbols.
- The current selected state must be obvious.
- Cost strings must use matching text icons.
- Tooltips should remain concise enough to scan during a war.
- The category picture must not contain fake buttons or numbers.

## Asset acceptance tests

The presentation passes when:

- The player can understand the current phase from the category.
- The opening art centers productive industry and avoids a generic crisis room.
- The strain art shows physical causes without modern imagery.
- Every decision, project, effect, state modifier, and achievement has the correct asset-type-specific art.
- Transparent icons have no opaque square, white halo, checkerboard, or chroma spill.
- Icons remain distinct at final size.
- Phase variants are real source images, not local filter variants.
- No final player-facing localisation appears in the planning files.
- Final implementation text follows the actor, information, uncertainty, tone, and dynamic-reference directions above.
