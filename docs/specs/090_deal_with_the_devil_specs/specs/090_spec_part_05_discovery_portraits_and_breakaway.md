# Deal with the Devil: discovery, the changing face, and resistance

## Evidence develops into a usable choice

The secret phase works when the player first notices an inconsistency, later understands that several inconsistencies connect, and finally acquires a way to act. It fails when random flavor events can indefinitely withhold the only clue that unlocks resistance.

The first acceptance schedules a rare, ambiguous incident after a proposed 60–120 days. It should relate to the actual institution: a duplicate shipment signature for a procurement bargain, an impossible patient record for a medical programme, or an appointment predating a cabinet meeting. Its presentation does not identify a demon. Dismissing it can preserve immediate calm; retaining the evidence keeps a useful witness or document.

After the second acceptance, a second independent kind of evidence becomes available. A repeated oddity alone should not be the sole route. The government can authorize a quiet ordinary inquiry into the office, audit its records, or consult a relevant specialist. These actions are framed as an investigation into an institution whose behavior has actually become suspicious.

After the third acceptance, the chain guarantees a contradiction serious enough to support meaningful precautions. The player can still deny its significance, but the game must present an actionable choice. After the fourth, identification or an equivalent unmistakable threat must arrive early enough to permit at least 60 days of counterplay before the final main offer. Random incident rolls can add variety; they cannot remove that minimum evidence path.

A government that never accepts any deal has fewer domestic clues. Repeated visits, the final clean invitation, a foreign warning, and the announced manifestation supply a different preparation path. It must not receive an elaborate purge menu for ministries that were never infiltrated.

## A small investigation surface

Before credible evidence, use occasional events and normal appointments. Do not create an always-visible “Demonic Influence” category, a disabled exorcism button, or a locked focus branch whose title gives the secret away.

After credible evidence, a compact ordinary category, proposed **The Independent Inquiry**, offers at most three relevant actions at a time. The initial choices are to protect a witness, audit the implicated institution, or seek one appropriate external assessment. A country without a scientist or useful intelligence service still has a records-and-witness route. Lack of a special character must not make liberation mathematically impossible.

An action consumes a concrete resource and has a defined purpose. Protecting a witness costs security capacity or political power and preserves testimony. An audit commits administrative capacity and exposes the office's actual reach. A medical assessment occupies a physician or treatment capacity and can separate physical symptoms from ordinary illness. An intelligence inquiry has operational risk and can identify a protected foreign contact. None simply grants “minus 20 corruption.”

A proposed ordinary inquiry takes 45 days and 75 political power. Protecting a witness can take 30 days and 50 political power. The exact provider cost replaces those figures when a specialist owns the action. Only one major inquiry and one protective measure run simultaneously in the starting design. These are tuning choices, not a requirement to add parallel progress bars.

The report names what was found, what remains uncertain, and what concrete opportunity follows. Failed investigations can lose time, a witness, or secrecy, but the mandatory late evidence path still exists. A failed roll must not convert a prepared player into an unavoidable fifth acceptance.

## Six incident patterns with distinct consequences

**The signature appears twice.** An official's signature authorizes two incompatible instructions at the same time. Preserving the original copies opens an authority challenge. Public accusation without the originals raises political conflict and may let the protected office dismiss the incident as forgery.

**The patient has no interval.** A clinical record shows a recovery and a transfer with no plausible time between them. Independent examination can identify a particular vulnerable official. Seizing every hospital immediately would sacrifice a real functioning treatment network and is a much harsher choice.

**The order was already obeyed.** A general reports executing a command that the ruler does not remember issuing. Protecting a parallel chain of command prevents a later coup from relying on that route. Ignoring the report leaves a specific headquarters vulnerable; it does not secretly lower every division's loyalty.

**The office cannot be searched.** An ordinary authority discovers the exact immunity or continuity clause granted in an earlier deal. The player sees how a concession once considered reasonable now limits state action. A legal review can establish its scope, while a forced search risks an announced breach dispute.

**The foreign ambassador refuses the meeting.** A diplomat recognizes someone in the visitor's entourage. The government can discreetly hear the warning, protect the contact, or sacrifice that relationship to preserve its current arrangement. The warning supplies knowledge, not automatic proof that every related foreign government is already possessed.

**The photograph is wrong.** A staff photograph shows an increasingly unnatural expression, while an older portrait remains normal. The incident directs attention to the living leader and can open a medical or succession inquiry. It does not require the government to possess a scientific explanation before it takes sensible precautions.

Use the patterns that arise from the chosen bargains. Repetition can reinforce an established motif, but do not replay the same mysterious clock event five times merely to fill the calendar.

## The five portrait stages

One main acceptance produces an almost imperceptible red tint around the eyes and a slight sharpening of the expression. There is no blood. Two make a faint glow, a subtly broader smile, and light shadows noticeable at close inspection. Three produce clearly red eyes, an unnatural smile, deeper shadows, and an optional restrained flicker. Four strengthen the glow and crooked expression, with a small dark or blood detail near the mouth or collar. Five show the full transformed ruler: intense eyes, a fixed unnatural smile, infernal shading, and restrained blood detail suited to the ordinary portrait scale.

Personal bargains intensify a stage's physical symptoms earlier, but cannot display the fully possessed fifth-stage package after fewer than five acceptances solely because the personal route was selected. An overt four-deal usurpation has its own revealed authority presentation, not a false fifth acceptance.

The effect should follow the actual character and current institutional relationship. The underlying portrait remains the source of identity, uniform, pose, age, and lighting. A generic demon head is not a replacement for the recognizable historical or generated ruler. The blood treatment remains restrained; the intended unease comes principally from the altered expression and eyes.

## Reusable effects and source-aware alignment

The brief requests reusable overlays rather than a library of independently repainted historical leaders. The visual plan therefore separates an immutable base portrait from a reusable effect stack and alignment metadata. Eye positions, mouth area, face angle, and relevant occlusion must be known for the selected source portrait. A fixed pair of red dots at the same coordinates for every face is not an acceptable implementation.

An expression change is more demanding than a light tint. A reusable mouth-shadow or highlight effect must not draw a second mouth or distort facial structure. Any actual deformation or animated treatment requires proof that the chosen rendering route can preserve the base identity and work on the supported portrait surfaces. This package does not assert that the game's native portrait renderer supports arbitrary facial warping.

Politics, diplomacy, important event portraits, and any other supported display of the same leader need the same stage and source ownership. Static fallbacks are required for every animated state. An external event that legitimately replaces a character or their portrait must not be overwritten by stale possession code. Unfamiliar portraits require safe alignment rather than copying coordinates from a different ruler.

The release gate is a real in-game demonstration on contrasting portraits: frontal and turned heads, glasses, facial hair, different lighting, a custom leader, and a changed successor. A baked-per-leader replacement pipeline is not silently accepted as the requested reusable solution. If the overlay contract cannot be met, record the limitation and seek an explicit presentation decision.

## Successors, former rulers, and divided vulnerability

Accepted national arrangements survive an ordinary change of leader because their institutions still exist. Personal symptoms do not automatically transfer at full strength to a successor. A protected institutional network can begin approaching the new leader, while the former ruler retains whatever personal vulnerability has been established.

A prepared succession preserves an independent lawful authority, removes the old ruler from binding decisions, and protects the relevant records. It can block a person-centered seizure and open a loophole. It cannot undo protected procurement, hospitals, and continuity boards without addressing them.

An unprepared successor can receive a newly observed sign of institutional pressure. The event explains the relationship rather than silently pasting the former ruler's fifth-stage face onto an unrelated person. During distributed possession, multiple actual hosts can exist, but they remain separately identified. A dead former ruler's portrait is not treated as the current governing body.

The possibility of split vulnerability is a narrative and political condition: the former ruler may remain personally compromised while the successor inherits a compromised administration. Do not expose two new numeric possession meters. Concrete witnesses, appointments, and protected offices are sufficient to make the split playable.

## Three ways to leave

**Negotiated separation** gives up a defined benefit, institution, or continuing service and replaces the essential work under independent authority. This is most viable after one or two main arrangements. Its competence lies in protecting the population and ordinary administration while surrendering the visitor's advantage.

**A discovered loophole** contests an actual term of a particular agreement. It needs a visible contractual basis, proof acquired through play, and a real authority capable of acting on it. It can reduce the price or dismantle a critical part of the network. It is not a magic phrase that erases all accepted history.

**Open resistance** retains or seizes disputed benefits and prepares to fight the visitor's actual institutions and forces. The player gets a larger portion of the material gains at the price of confrontation. After several acceptances, this may be the only way to preserve the country without submitting.

These methods can be combined. A country may lawfully end the archive foundation, replace the hospitals, and fight the military office that refuses to leave. Each successful action changes the concrete opening footprint. There is no single public “pay to cleanse all deals” button.

## Sacrifice should have an object

Returning surviving equipment, surrendering a trust's use of factories for a defined period, closing a protected laboratory, retiring the saved ruler, dismissing a valuable general, or accepting temporary administrative weakness are meaningful sacrifices. A flat political-power fee that leaves every extraordinary benefit and every protected office intact is not.

The object sacrificed should follow the reward. Medical separation replaces care rather than killing cured patients. Scientific separation gives up access, remaining acceleration, or dependent institutions rather than arbitrarily deleting unrelated technology. Diplomatic separation costs the visitor-dependent agreement while preserving independently valid treaties. Military separation settles delivered materiel and command access rather than disbanding an arbitrary half of the army.

When a benefit has been consumed irreversibly, the settlement names a real replacement burden. The government can repay comparable equipment from its own production, surrender an agreed industrial concession, or use proof that changes what is owed. The amount cannot increase without bound because the player took too long to click a menu item.

## Loopholes are local, earned, and fallible

A valid loophole needs four elements: the exact relevant promise, preserved evidence, a substantive fact that satisfies the loophole, and an independent capacity to enforce the result. Missing any one can produce a partial success or a contested settlement.

A contract signed by someone lacking authority is useful only if that lack of authority was real in the campaign. A new government can contest a predecessor's personal agreement without automatically repudiating valid state institutions. Two conflicting copies create an evidentiary struggle; whichever copy happens to be selected by the player is not automatically true. A destroyed named institution can end an institutional appointment while leaving the broader national mandate alive.

Mephistopheles should exploit exact language intelligently. He can dispute scope, offer a revised settlement, try to recover a document, or pressure a witness. He cannot retroactively add a clause that the visible agreement never contained merely to defeat a correctly completed player plan.

Success reduces a specific claim or prevents a particular seizure. The player sees which office has lost its authority, which person is protected, or which material obligation has been settled. They still need to deal with surviving arrangements.

## Defeating the intrusion before possession

A completed early separation removes the visitor's active authority from the surrendered arrangement, closes future unsolicited principal rewards from that relationship, and leaves the country's actual history intact. The saved people, produced equipment, and political memories remain. Scars can include a damaged institution, a retired leader, distrust of extraordinary advisers, or a modest finite reconstruction burden.

A late break can still win, but victory has to defeat or disarm the actual loyalist network as well as the visible leader. Removing a portrait overlay is the visual consequence of a successful political or personal resolution, not the mechanic that makes the government free.

After full peaceful possession, resistance moves into the shared tree's hidden liberation route. Its prerequisites and choices are developed in Part 7. The player cannot simply reopen the early investigation category and buy their country back for the first-deal price.
