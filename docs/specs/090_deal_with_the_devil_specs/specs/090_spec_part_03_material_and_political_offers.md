# Deal with the Devil: military, economic, and political offers

## How to use the offer catalogue

Every offer below is a complete design seed with a delivered reward, a visible concession, later leverage, and a possible separation cost.
The catalogue is author-facing.
Its hidden consequences and loopholes must never be copied into an acceptance tooltip.
The **Separation** and **Loophole** lines supply the content of that institution's single settlement event (Part 5); they are not separate decision families.
The **Delayed consequence** line is also what the institution contributes to retaliation (Part 2) and to a revolt footprint (Part 6).

The selected benefit should materially change the host's situation.
It should not require accepting four weak preliminary deals before anything interesting happens.
The first principal reward must already be worth considering.
Later bargains increase institutional reach and can solve larger problems without becoming seven simultaneous reward packages.

The proposed national scales are **small**, **established**, and **major-capacity**, based on controlled usable industry and the size of the problem.
Starting industry bands are below 15, from 15 up to but not including 50, and 50 or more usable factories.
A country does not jump to the largest aid package because the engine calls it a major.
A high-capacity player suffering total occupation is assessed from what it can actually use.
These are balancing bands, not a new player-facing value.

Choose the quantities that restore useful capacity, constrained by the stated package cap.
Supply a real shortage rather than useless stock.
A fallback that cannot deliver a major useful reward should select another offer.
Do not count “the player received some political power instead” as fulfillment of a promise to save an army or suppress an outbreak.

Clean reoffer improvements from Part 2 apply to the principal useful component.
They do not multiply every subreward independently or bypass safety caps.
An institution's access must be stated even when the ordinary fiscal cost is reduced.

## Military offer: The Convoys That Arrived

**Situation.** The host's ordinary frontline formations are equipment-starved, and a material shortage is more urgent than a lack of technology.
The visitor presents cargo manifests, not a miraculous promise that every battle will be won.

**Reward.** Deliver usable infantry equipment toward the measured shortage, with proposed principal caps of 5,000, 15,000, or 30,000 items by national scale.
Up to 250, 750, or 1,500 support-equipment items can accompany it when those items address a real shortage.
Do not include both support equipment and an unrelated artillery windfall merely to enlarge the tooltip.
The cargo should be immediately available after the arrangement is secured.
If the country cannot use ordinary rifles, select a compatible material offer rather than forcing an invalid equipment token.

**Visible concession.** A private procurement office supervises the cargo and its distribution.
The stated remit names procurement records and designated depots.
A first arrangement does not grant this office control of the entire army.
Later tiers can expand its remit to multiple depots or protected appointments, explicitly.

**Delayed consequence.** Clerks begin bypassing ordinary requisition procedures.
A depot commander receives instructions from an office the general staff cannot inspect.
If the relationship breaks, those particular depots and officers can support the revolt.
The grant itself does not magically make every recipient division loyal to the visitor.

**Separation.** Return an equivalent quantity of the surviving delivered cargo, surrender the protected depot, or replace the procurement structure at a material and command cost.
Equipment already lost in combat cannot be resurrected for a nominal return.
Its portion of the settlement must be paid with a real replacement burden, an agreed facility sacrifice, or an exposed contractual argument.

**Discoverable loophole.** The visible wording can appoint the office until the named consignment has been fully accounted for.
Recovering independent manifests and proving the end of that assignment can end its authority.
Burning all records destroys that evidence and may make separation harder.

## Military offer: A Front That Holds

**Situation.** The host has an army and equipment but is close to a breakthrough, exhausted, or unable to coordinate its defenses.
The visitor offers an emergency command mission.

**Reward.** A proposed 180-day defensive package supplies +25 percent division defense, +25 percent organization recovery, and a 20 percent reduction to supply consumption.
These are target effects requiring legal modifier verification, not asserted engine syntax.
The package is powerful enough to affect the crisis but does not guarantee a victory, teleport divisions, erase encirclement, or prevent a human opponent from acting.
If a qualifying temporary modifier cannot apply to the intended formations, the offer must be redesigned before being presented.

**Visible concession.** A military coordinator receives access to orders and designated staff conferences.
The later national-authority version requires protected representation within the command chain.
The player sees that change in authority.

**Delayed consequence.** Orders arrive before their recorded issuance.
Selected officers display unusual calm or repeat the same private instructions.
The future revolt gains a coherent staff and prepared positions rather than arbitrary extra tanks.

**Separation.** Dismiss the coordinator and reorganize the compromised commands.
Remaining temporary command assistance ends.
A proposed 60-day reorganization cost reduces planning and organization recovery, with sufficient warning to schedule a withdrawal or replacement.
The country does not lose an unrelated technology or every general.

**Discoverable loophole.** Authority was granted over a specifically named emergency theater.
Ending that emergency, moving the independent command seat, and proving that the original military remit no longer exists can terminate the appointment.
Renaming the theater without changing its actual function is not enough.

## Military offer: The Men Who Can Return

**Situation.** The host is short of manpower and has a documented pool of living wounded personnel, displaced recruits, inaccessible reserves, or other recoverable people.
The visitor offers to restore their ability to serve.

**Reward.** Restore a substantial portion of the eligible living pool, initially targeting 25 percent and never exceeding the actual recoverable amount.
The offer can add a 180-day casualty-recovery improvement when the owner system supports it.
It does not return people already counted as dead, create an adult population from nothing, or raise recruitment beyond the country's real population rules.
A country without such a pool receives a different military offer.

**Visible concession.** A recovery and records bureau coordinates the return.
Medical administration and military enrollment are named in its mandate.
The programme's actual saving of lives is a legitimate reason to accept.

**Delayed consequence.** Recovered soldiers report the same dream, or their officers receive identical private recommendations.
The vulnerable people remain human.
Their treatment does not automatically convert them into demons or remove normal disease and famine rules.

**Separation.** End the bureau's access, replace its services, and release compromised officers from command.
People who genuinely recovered remain alive and recovered.
Costs fall on the military administration, remaining temporary assistance, and the state's real replacement capacity.
A costly demobilization can be an explicit option, not a mandatory medical reversal.

**Discoverable loophole.** The agreement can promise the return of named personnel to ordinary military jurisdiction.
Evidence that the bureau retained unlawful custody creates a limited nonperformance case.
It does not grant a universal right to retain all other gifts without settlement.

## Economic offer: The Reconstruction Trust

**Situation.** A country has usable labor and sites but cannot restart industry after a collapse, bombing campaign, or severe construction crisis.

**Reward.** Repair or restore existing disabled industrial capacity first, with a proposed target of 5, 10, or 20 factories across viable controlled states.
New factories are offered only when the promise explicitly includes new capacity and legal building slots exist.
A proposed +25 percent construction-speed benefit for 180 days can support the restoration.
The restoration must use the existing damage or crisis owner's rules rather than deleting every economic penalty on the country.

**Visible concession.** A trust receives operating authority over the named facilities and protected access to their management.
Its territorial footprint is the actual set of benefiting states.
The concession must not secretly include factories on another continent that received no assistance.

**Delayed consequence.** Company records refer to directors who cannot be found.
Local administrators discover that ordinary inspection requires the trust's permission.
During a breach, the industrial foothold gives the rebels productive territory and a supply base.
It does not entitle them to all national industry.

**Separation.** Place the trust under independent receivership, surrender a specified portion of the restored capacity for a bounded period, or close its protected facilities while rebuilding ordinary management.
A permanently repaired building is not simply “unrepaired” by an invisible timer.
The visible cost is the loss of its use, its deliberate demolition, a replacement obligation, or the political consequences of retaining it against the settlement.

**Discoverable loophole.** A trust granted authority only for reconstruction can lose that authority once an independent completion review proves its work finished.
Securing such a review is a project with witnesses and access, not a free rename button.

## Economic offer: A Season of Impossible Output

**Situation.** The host's immediate survival depends on producing enough equipment, supplies, or civil goods from the factories it still controls.

**Reward.** Grant a proposed +35 percent factory-output effect for 365 days, with an appropriate dockyard alternative only when shipbuilding is the selected problem.
A limited production-efficiency retention benefit can replace part of the output increase if the existing crisis specifically destroys continuity.
The package is one economic intervention, not an unlimited production law permanently exceeding every other system.

**Visible concession.** A private inspectorate controls work allocation and production records inside the participating industrial sector.
At later acceptance tiers, protected labor and management appointments extend its reach.
The visitor does not require a public infernal ministry while the secret phase remains plausible.

**Delayed consequence.** Factories meet targets despite impossible shipment schedules.
Workers find that the inspectorate knows personal details never entered in their employment records.
A later regime can consolidate the inspectorate into lasting industry, while an independent host must replace the extraordinary coordination.

**Separation.** End the remaining production surge and accept a proposed 90-day normalization burden of reduced output or construction capacity.
The quantity already manufactured remains real.
A confiscation route can retain it while risking a confrontation; a negotiated route pays a defined replacement or institutional cost.

**Discoverable loophole.** The programme's authorization can be tied to a stated production target.
Meeting that target under independently verified accounting ends the inspectorate's mandate.
Falsifying the output does not work, and a target added after acceptance is not legitimate.

## Economic offer: The Supply of Last Resort

**Situation.** A resource shortage, blockade, or broken trade relationship prevents useful production.
The visitor claims access to reserves, transport, or intermediaries that ordinary officials cannot obtain.

**Reward.** Supply a bounded resource or logistics arrangement for 180 days through the existing resource, trade, or event-owner system.
The offer names what it delivers: access to a reserve, concrete transport assets, relief from a particular registered shortage, or a negotiated trading opportunity.
It cannot promise globally open sea lanes while hostile navies remain capable of blockade.
If physical convoy delivery is promised, the arrival and its risks are represented consistently.

**Visible concession.** An import board receives access to customs, manifests, or a named port.
The concession is especially attractive when it appears to solve sanctions without changing public policy.

**Delayed consequence.** The board becomes a route for foreign visitors and protected shipments.
Its infrastructure can support foreign bargains later.
A blockade lifted by one owner may remain active from another; the board does not overwrite either relationship.

**Separation.** Return surviving transport assets or replace the trade arrangement and remove the board's protected status.
A severe but finite supply shock is a meaningful cost.
Deleting unrelated convoys or every resource deposit is not.

**Discoverable loophole.** The agreement may authorize only goods of a specified kind through a named port.
Documenting unauthorized passengers or military cargo can revoke that narrow permission.
The port must be genuinely outside the board's control afterward.

## Political offer: A Government That Can Act

**Situation.** Low stability, a fractured cabinet, or emergency administrative paralysis prevents the government from executing otherwise viable policy.

**Reward.** A proposed +20 percentage points of stability and +200 political power provide immediate room to act.
For a large, severe institutional collapse, the political-power component can increase to 400 while the stability gain remains capped by the normal national limit.
Registered crisis penalties are relieved only through their owner's supported settlement.
No opposition party is silently erased.

**Visible concession.** A special secretary receives authority to coordinate cabinet papers and selected appointments.
The first-tier office is narrow.
Later versions involve a continuity board, legal exemptions, or protected officials, and state those concessions plainly.

**Delayed consequence.** Cabinet members begin agreeing before an issue has been discussed.
A dissenting minister finds that a dismissal letter predates the meeting.
Political leverage supplies administrative loyalists, police cooperation, and control over records in a later breach.

**Separation.** Dismiss the network, expose its interference, and accept a political reorganization.
Proposed costs include 150 political power and a 10-percentage-point temporary stability shock, moderated when independent institutions were prepared.
These are a starting balance envelope, not a demand to charge every listed cost together.

**Discoverable loophole.** The office exists under the authority of a specific emergency cabinet.
A lawful reconstitution that actually ends that cabinet, backed by independent records and a functioning successor, can limit the secretary's appointment.
Cycling an ordinary leader portrait alone does not.

## Political offer: The Opposition's Sudden Silence

**Situation.** A particular organized political threat is close to removing the government or breaking its wartime administration.
The offer must identify the real pressure rather than manufacture a new opposition faction.

**Reward.** Reduce the named crisis through its owning system and grant a proposed 180-day stability or authority benefit.
The reward does not mean a promise to kill an entire political population.
The visitor can obtain withdrawals, expose a real rival's scheme, secure a coalition, or bring an administrative rebellion to a halt.
Any violent outcome uses the normal consequence owners and is not hidden inside a benign stability tooltip.

**Visible concession.** A legal envoy or mediator gains protected access to the relevant courts, political leadership, or negotiations.
The government sees the appointment's ordinary breadth.

**Delayed consequence.** Judges or party officials respond to an authority the government cannot identify.
A later confrontation can fracture the state apparatus without requiring the army to have accepted a military bargain.

**Separation.** Restore the affected institution's independence, surrender some of the political advantage obtained, or reopen the dispute under an ordinary settlement.
The original opponents are not automatically resurrected from death or restored to positions they lost for unrelated reasons.

**Discoverable loophole.** The visitor's representative promised to act as a mediator rather than a permanent superior.
A surviving copy of the agreement and testimony from both sides can prove that distinction.
Obtaining the testimony may require releasing an opponent or making a concession the player would prefer to avoid.

## Political offer: Continuity of the State

**Situation.** The government expects a succession crisis, a forced abdication, or institutional collapse during war.
This is especially suitable for the fourth arrangement because its visible benefit and dangerous concession have comparable reach.

**Reward.** Protect the defined transition against a specific registered crisis, retain a bounded amount of state capacity, and supply a proposed +25 percent political-power gain for 180 days.
It does not make a leader immortal against every other event or invalidate a human opponent's victory.

**Visible concession.** The continuity authority can remain in office despite ordinary dismissals while the stated emergency lasts.
The wording is deliberately consequential.
The game must not conceal it as “appoint one adviser” when its real public terms bind the next government.

**Delayed consequence.** The network can remain effective after a leader is removed.
The player discovers why killing or replacing one possessed person is insufficient.
At the fifth arrangement this institutional continuity can become the route through which the final mandate is adopted.

**Separation.** Establish an independent lawful successor, protect the records and command chain, and dismantle the continuity authority.
Losing access to the protected government's remaining temporary guarantees is a fair cost.
Replacing every institution with a free new government is not.

**Discoverable loophole.** The terms may preserve a particular constitutional office, not whichever person Mephistopheles later places in it.
Proving that he has abolished or usurped the protected office can undermine his claim, provided a real independent authority survives to act on the evidence.

## Campaign variation without repetitive grants

A category does not repeat until at least two other categories have been offered, unless the host's crisis makes repetition necessary.
Do not offer the same principal reward twice while its first material purpose remains satisfied.
A second military offer can address manpower after rifles solved the original shortage.
A second economic offer can protect transport after industry recovered.
Reusing a category should create a new dilemma and a distinct institution or expansion of authority.

The visitor is permitted to exploit a crisis caused by another event.
He is not permitted to trigger that event secretly merely to ensure his offer remains useful unless a later, expressly designed interaction establishes that responsibility.
Let the world create most of the temptation.
