# Deal with the Devil: courtship and the five main deals

## Acceptance is a choice, not a hidden score threshold

Peaceful possession requires five deliberately accepted **main arrangements**. There is no alternate path in which side favours, personal symptoms, high Chaos, unsolicited aid, or a successor's ordinary appointment silently supplies a missing main acceptance. The hidden institutional process can become more dangerous without changing this requirement.

An arrangement counts when the government explicitly adopts its visible terms and the promised principal delivery is secured. A declined invitation, an investigated demonstration, an unread popup, a failed delivery, or an unsolicited consignment does not count. The game can store the five acceptance states internally without displaying a deal counter.

A main arrangement gives Mephistopheles a wider relationship with national authority as the chain progresses. Its material subject remains tailored to current needs. Five military crises do not force five identical shipments; the relationship can move from an emergency delivery to command appointments, protected procurement, continuity of administration, and finally the leader's binding mandate. The category of reward and the reach of the concession are separate design choices.

## The five institutional steps

The first arrangement establishes a **protected point of entry**. A representative may supervise the delivered assistance in one relevant institution. The office is small enough that a rational government can treat it as a reasonable condition. Its strange influence is still local, and removing it later is expensive but manageable.

The second arrangement establishes **trusted appointments**. The visitor recommends or seconds people who must interact with ordinary ministers, officers, physicians, or scientists. Those people can help deliver a second major benefit. They also create loyalties that survive the original visitor's absence. A government that accepted only the first deal must not already have this second level of national access merely because it waited.

The third arrangement establishes **delegated national authority**. The programme can coordinate multiple institutions or issue instructions within its stated remit. This is the point at which an ordinary disagreement with the visitor can become a national crisis. The reward should accordingly solve a larger problem than the first office solved.

The fourth arrangement establishes **continuity over objections**. The trusted network receives protection against ordinary dismissal, a continuity board, a special chain of command, or an analogous power explicitly stated in the visible terms. Other officials now have concrete reasons to object. The offer is still useful, but it is no longer innocently administrative. Before the next principal bargain becomes available, the player must have received actionable evidence and a meaningful interval in which to protect something independent.

The fifth arrangement establishes **the final mandate**. The leader or the surviving lawful authority adopts the prepared network as the lasting guarantor of government. The reward is delivered and peaceful possession follows. The state has already been prepared; there is no opening civil war, no arbitrary destruction of half the army, and no compulsory loss of the capital. The ruler remains the familiar public face, visibly transformed.

These descriptions are the design's internal structure. The popup does not label them “Step 1” through “Step 5.” Each appears as a particular solution and a particular concession in that campaign.

## Clean courtship and refusal before any acceptance

The proposal uses at most five clean invitations to secure the first accepted arrangement. A refusal at this stage declines a bid for the first point of entry; it does not consume one of the five acceptances needed for possession. This resolves the otherwise ambiguous case in which the host refuses twice and then changes its mind. That host still needs five accepted main arrangements.

The first clean refusal is entirely unpunished. The second invitation returns after a substantial delay with a better version of the same help or a response to a more urgent real problem. Subsequent clean invitations remain persuasion rather than a series of small retaliations. The visitor may become more candid or impatient in the accompanying prose, but he does not acquire ministers, factories, or soldiers for free.

Improved clean bids should increase the useful assistance or reduce an ordinary visible burden. They must not remove every concession or introduce an infinite escalator that rewards waiting forever. The proposed improvement cap is 50 percent above the relevant initial package, reached in two 25-percent improvements. Relevance can improve further if the country's actual crisis deepens. The cap applies to this bargaining bonus, not to legitimately larger national scale or a different kind of emergency.

The fourth clean refusal produces an unsettling but non-punitive sign and gives the host an opportunity to prepare against an unexplained threat. The fifth clean invitation is the final attempt at persuasion. Its refusal ends courtship and leads to the weakest open manifestation after a visible mobilization interval. The host has accepted no institutions and loses no divisions to supposed collaborators who were never established.

This finite invitation limit is new proposed tuning. It does not change the brief's requirement that rejecting every attempt produces an open revolt. The source/decision register makes the limit easy to revise without changing the five-acceptance rule.

## Pacing

The ordinary first invitation arrives with Event 090. Further clean invitations have a proposed delay of 180–270 days. After an acceptance, the next main offer has a proposed delay of 240–360 days. These are tuning ranges for the normal event scheduler, not promises that a threshold immediately fires a popup.

A country accepting the first five main arrangements therefore normally develops over several years. A country refusing several initial bids and then accepting takes longer. War emergencies can justify the short end of a range; they do not compress the complete chain into several weeks. A hard proposed floor of 120 days between committed main arrangements survives all acceleration effects.

Only one main offer can be pending. A popup waiting for the player does not let a second main offer accumulate behind it. A long pause does not produce a queue of overdue signatures when the game resumes. A material change in the country's situation can cause the pending reward to be refreshed before acceptance, but it cannot duplicate the concession or award.

A player may postpone an offer once for 30 days while a current investigation or government transition is resolved. Postponement is not acceptance and is not a clean refusal. Repeated indefinite postponement is not a route to retaining a free open offer forever. At the end of the interval the government must accept, refuse, or receive a refreshed offer because the original promise has become invalid.

## Refusal after the first arrangement

One accepted arrangement gives the visitor a real office and a real claim to the service relationship. It does not justify an immediate national coup. Refusing the second main offer is not itself punished with a secret stability loss or a surprise army. The visitor can make one revised bid after at least 180 days. The host can also begin an ordinary review of the existing arrangement if concrete discrepancies have appeared.

A second refusal at this point closes further persuasion unless the host deliberately reopens it. The government must choose what to do about the continuing concession. A negotiated separation gives up the named institution, replaces the supplied service, or returns a defined material benefit. An unresolved arrangement does not pay new rewards and does not drift toward five acceptances merely with time.

The proposed quiet closeout after one arrangement is a costly breakaway route, not a consequence-free refusal bonus. The surviving foreign or domestic officials can remain as ordinary story consequences, but their network is no longer permitted to claim the authority of a surrendered main arrangement. A cleanly completed early settlement does not later generate a large army from nonexistent accepted mandates.

The visitor can contest an attempted confiscation that retains his protected institution while declaring its obligations void. That dispute must be announced. It creates an opportunity for a limited breach confrontation, with strength derived from actual surviving leverage, rather than making every refusal after a single deal immediately dangerous.

## Refusal after two or three arrangements

The country's institutions now depend on a network that has people in more than one place. Refusal brings a specific demand: honor an existing appointment, preserve an entrusted facility, complete a delivery obligation, or surrender the supplied benefit. The demand exposes the nature of the pressure. It is not a generic hidden-counter warning.

Give a proposed 90-day settlement interval in which the government can protect its command chain, recover independent records, replace the service, and choose a settlement strategy. The ordinary refusal alone does not delete previously cured people, cancel all unrelated research, or transfer random territory. The danger comes from the compromised institutions that are actually present.

The country can make a costly negotiated withdrawal, find and execute a genuine loophole, or prepare to fight. A failed or rejected settlement leads to a partial-acceptance confrontation. Two accepted arrangements create a serious but still geographically limited foothold. Three create a broader crisis in which industrial, military, or political institutions can work together. The exact opening is determined by their location and function, not by a universal half-country split.

An ultimatum is not a sixth kind of main deal that can accidentally complete possession. Agreeing merely to the settlement terms does not count as another main acceptance. A renewed principal bargain requires its own clear reward, concession, and explicit acceptance.

## Refusal after four arrangements

The fifth main offer arrives only after the government has had actionable evidence and at least 60 days to undertake a protective response. Its presentation remains faithful to the visible reward and terms, but its surrounding events make clear that an independent decision is now at stake. The final bargain cannot be the first indication that something is wrong.

Acceptance produces peaceful possession. Refusal can produce a large internal war or an immediate attempted seizure of authority. Whether a seizure succeeds depends on concrete protections already secured: an independent command chain, a protected successor or leader, surviving records, an uncompromised seat of government, and the actual loyalty of the network's personnel. The design does not grant the player automatic safety just because a refusal button exists.

A successful overt seizure after four acceptances is **usurpation**, not five-deal peaceful possession. It has a different origin record and political settlement. It cannot earn a “five freely accepted main deals” achievement or erase the struggle that occurred. The player must retain a playable side or a supported continuation as the captured regime with liberation content; an unannounced loss of human control is not an acceptable shortcut.

If the country is protected enough to prevent the cabinet seizure, the prepared network forms its territorial revolt where viable. Prevention does not dissolve every hostile facility or convert a heavily infiltrated country into the clean-defiance origin.

## Personal offers and side bargains

A personal rescue can intensify the current leader's visual and narrative symptoms within the current acceptance tier. It may make a medical examination especially revealing or leave that individual more vulnerable during a succession crisis. It cannot count twice, skip the next main bargain, or produce full possession at four acceptances.

Side bargains concern a bounded local favor: releasing an official, securing a particular convoy, accelerating one inspection, or protecting a witness. They can create a witness, an obligation, or local leverage. They cannot create the final mandate, satisfy a missing main acceptance, grant the fourth arrangement's constitutional authority, or assemble a national coup by accumulation.

Limit side-bargain content to at most two unresolved local favors at a time. Present them as ordinary events when relevant rather than a menu of farmable transactions. No side reward repeats from a reload, a leadership change, a destroyed temporary country, or cycling the same target.

## Unsolicited and unsigned assistance

An evolution or special incident can put an unsolicited gift in the government's path. The country can inspect it, quarantine it, send it back, or explicitly put it into national service under stated terms. Its physical arrival is not a main acceptance. A successful demonstration in a hospital or an unexplained shipment waiting at a port does not authorize the visitor to bind the country.

Adopting a principal unsolicited gift can become a main arrangement only through a visible choice that states the practical benefit and concession. Merely fighting a battle while the shipment happens to be present cannot sign for the player. The visitor can lie about the source of his generosity; the game must not lie about which player action constituted agreement.

These safeguards do not make the unsigned-gift variant harmless. Inspectors can be compromised, political pressure can build to use a desperately needed delivery, and a minister can advocate accepting it. Those are playable dilemmas. They are not retroactive consent.

## Failed delivery and changes of government

A failed promise cannot advance the relationship as though the promised assistance arrived. If a reward is partly delivered, the player must be told what remains outstanding and be offered the appropriate revised terms or cancellation. A later deliberate breach can create a loophole, but ordinary engine failure is not a narrative reward for Mephistopheles.

Institutional history belongs to the country and arrangement, while personal vulnerability belongs to the actual character. Replacing the leader does not reset four established ministries. It also does not make a newly arrived successor fully possessed without a new event or an existing delegated-authority basis. Part 5 separates these effects.

Annexation, capitulation, a new civil war, and a move into exile require the active relationship to be reconciled with the real surviving authority. Do not move the visitor to whichever tag happens to reuse an old numeric slot. No successor receives a fresh copy of an already paid reward solely because the government's carrier changed.

## Route guarantees

The following guarantees govern all later parts. Five valid main acceptances produce peaceful possession and no opening civil war. Zero main acceptances followed by total clean refusal produce the weakest open manifestation. Partial acceptance retains the consequences of actual surviving institutions and offers costly resistance. Personal and side content cannot change those counts. Evidence and counterplay precede the final decision. The player keeps a playable continuation.

Every implementation and test matrix should be derived from these guarantees rather than introducing an additional hidden threshold that overrides them.
