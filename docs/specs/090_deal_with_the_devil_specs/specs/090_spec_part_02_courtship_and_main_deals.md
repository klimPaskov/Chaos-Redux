# Deal with the Devil: courtship and the five main deals

## Acceptance is a choice, not a hidden score threshold

Peaceful possession requires five deliberately accepted **main arrangements**.
There is no alternate path in which side favours, personal symptoms, high Chaos, unsolicited aid, or a successor's ordinary appointment silently supplies a missing main acceptance.
The hidden institutional process can become more dangerous without changing this requirement.

An arrangement counts when the government explicitly adopts its visible terms and the promised principal delivery is secured.
A declined invitation, an investigated demonstration, an unread popup, a failed delivery, or an unsolicited consignment does not count.
The game stores the acceptance states internally without displaying a deal counter.

A main arrangement gives Mephistopheles a wider relationship with national authority as the chain progresses.
Its material subject remains tailored to current needs.
Five military crises do not force five identical shipments; the relationship can move from an emergency delivery to command appointments, protected procurement, continuity of administration, and finally the leader's binding mandate.
The category of reward and the reach of the concession are separate design choices.

## The five institutional steps

The first arrangement establishes a **protected point of entry**.
A representative may supervise the delivered assistance in one relevant institution.
The office is small enough that a rational government can treat it as a reasonable condition.
Its strange influence is still local, and removing it later is expensive but manageable.

The second arrangement establishes **trusted appointments**.
The visitor recommends or seconds people who must interact with ordinary ministers, officers, physicians, or scientists.
Those people can help deliver a second major benefit.
They also create loyalties that survive the original visitor's absence.
Recommended officials begin defending each other's appointments, and foreign governments start to notice that the host's new policies come from unfamiliar people.

The third arrangement establishes **delegated national authority**.
The programme can coordinate multiple institutions or issue instructions within its stated remit.
Government orders begin producing outcomes slightly different from what was ordered.
This is the point at which an ordinary disagreement with the visitor can become a national crisis.
The reward should accordingly solve a larger problem than the first office solved.

The fourth arrangement establishes **continuity over objections**.
The trusted network receives protection against ordinary dismissal, a continuity board, a special chain of command, or an analogous power explicitly stated in the visible terms.
Removing one compromised minister is no longer enough, because the network can pass his authority to another official tied to the same arrangement.
Before the next principal bargain becomes available, the player must have received actionable evidence and a meaningful interval in which to secure a safeguard (Part 5).

The fifth arrangement establishes **the final mandate**.
The leader or the surviving lawful authority adopts the prepared network as the lasting guarantor of government.
The reward is delivered and peaceful possession follows.
The state has already been prepared; there is no opening civil war, no arbitrary destruction of half the army, and no compulsory loss of the capital.
The ruler remains the familiar public face, visibly transformed.

These descriptions are the design's internal structure.
The popup does not label them “Step 1” through “Step 5”.
Each appears as a particular solution and a particular concession in that campaign.

## Clean courtship: four visits before the visitor stops asking

Before any acceptance, the courtship follows the brief's four-visit pattern.
A refusal at this stage declines a bid for the first point of entry; it does not consume one of the five acceptances needed for possession.
A host that refuses twice and then accepts still needs five accepted main arrangements.

| Visit | What the visitor offers | Consequence of refusal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | The opening offer for the host's most pressing need | None. He leaves politely. |
| Second | The same help made more complete (proposed +25 percent on the principal component), or help for a more urgent problem that appeared since the first meeting | None. |
| Third | An offer tailored very closely to the host's current weakness or ambition (up to +50 percent on the principal component) | One unsettling sign that something is gathering around the host, without any material loss |
| Final | A less patient visit: still useful, but at the initial package value, with a more intrusive condition and a 30-day answer window | The visitor abandons persuasion and the announced manifestation begins (Part 6) |

The improvement cap applies to the bargaining bonus on the principal component, not to legitimately larger national scale or a different kind of emergency.
It does not remove every concession or create an escalator that rewards waiting.
Accepting at any visit starts the first arrangement with that visit's terms.
The host that refuses all four visits has accepted no institutions and loses no divisions to collaborators who were never established.

## Pacing

The first visit arrives with Event 090.
Further clean visits have a proposed delay of 180–270 days.
After an acceptance, the next main offer has a proposed delay of 240–360 days.
These are tuning ranges for the normal event scheduler, not promises that a threshold immediately fires a popup.

A country accepting five main arrangements therefore normally develops over several years.
War emergencies can justify the short end of a range; they do not compress the complete chain into several weeks.
A hard proposed floor of 120 days between committed main arrangements survives all acceleration effects.

Only one main offer can be pending.
A popup waiting for the player does not let a second main offer accumulate behind it, and a long pause does not produce a queue of overdue signatures.
A material change in the country's situation can refresh the pending reward before acceptance, but it cannot duplicate the concession or award.

A player may postpone an offer once for 30 days while an investigation or government transition is resolved.
Postponement is neither acceptance nor refusal.
At the end of the interval the government must accept, refuse, or receive a refreshed offer because the original promise has become invalid.

## Refusal after acceptance: retaliation, then a punitive offer

Once the country has accepted a deal, the visitor no longer treats refusal as a clean end to the relationship.
Every refusal after one to three acceptances follows the same three beats, scaled by how many arrangements exist; refusal after four is covered in the next section.

1. **Delayed retaliation.** A proposed 30–90 days after the refusal, one accepted institution acts against the host.
The delay keeps the connection uncertain during the early stages.
2. **A punitive offer.** The visitor returns with a worse but still valuable main offer: a smaller or shorter reward, a more intrusive visible condition, a harsher visible cost, a 30-day answer window, and, where an earlier gift has an ongoing component, the stated threat that it will be withdrawn if he is refused.
Accepting a punitive offer is a full main acceptance with its own reward and concession.
3. **Settlement.** Refusing the punitive offer moves the relationship into separation or confrontation under the sections below.

Retaliation always comes from an institution the host actually created, and it always takes something the player can feel.

| Accepted institution | Typical retaliation |
| --- | --- |
| Military | A depot withholds part of its stock, or one commander tied to the office disobeys and resigns |
| Economic | An industrial accident disables part of the restored or trust-run capacity for a period |
| Political | A protected minister disappears, taking political power and stability with him |
| Scientific | The coordinator's laboratory stalls; the dependent project or research bonus pauses |
| Medical | The network suspends continuing treatment or relief in its states; completed cures stay cured |
| Diplomatic | A diplomatic incident costs opinion with a named partner or suspends restored access |
| Personal | The leader falls ill or makes an unexplained public statement, costing stability |

Retaliation never reverses a completed cure, kills recovered people, or deletes unrelated national assets.
It cannot transfer territory or create an army.

**After one arrangement** the retaliation is limited: one effect from the table at a modest scale, or the weakening of the earlier gift's ongoing component.
If the punitive offer is also refused, the host chooses between a negotiated separation, which gives up the named institution and the earlier benefit's ongoing portion, and retaining the office in defiance.
An unresolved arrangement pays no new rewards and does not drift toward five acceptances with time.
Retaining the office while declaring its obligations void is announced and opens a limited breach confrontation, with strength derived from the actual surviving leverage.

**After two or three arrangements** the retaliation is serious: one or two effects from the table at full scale, and the punitive offer carries the explicit threat to remove an earlier gift.
Refusing the punitive offer starts a proposed 90-day settlement interval in which the government can secure safeguards, replace services, and choose a strategy: a costly negotiated withdrawal, a proven loophole, or preparing to fight.
A failed or rejected settlement leads to a partial-acceptance revolt whose footprint follows the location and function of the surviving institutions, not a universal half-country split.

## Refusal after four arrangements

The fifth main offer arrives only after the government has had actionable evidence and at least 60 days to secure a safeguard.
Its surrounding events make clear that an independent decision is now at stake.
The final bargain cannot be the first indication that something is wrong.

Acceptance produces peaceful possession.
Refusal produces no retaliation beat; the network moves directly.
Whether it attempts an immediate seizure or a large internal war depends on the safeguards already secured (Part 5).
A host without Loyal Command or a Protected Successor faces a seizure attempt, after which the visitor offers one final chance to submit on punitive terms.
Submitting at that point is the fifth acceptance and produces peaceful possession.

A successful overt seizure is **usurpation**, not five-deal peaceful possession.
It has a different origin record, cannot earn the five-deal achievement, and leaves the player either a playable loyalist side or the captured regime with liberation content.
If safeguards prevent the seizure, the prepared network forms its territorial revolt where viable.
Prevention does not dissolve every hostile facility or convert a heavily infiltrated country into the clean-defiance origin.

## Personal offers and side bargains

Personal offers to the leader are main offers and count once, like any other category.
They advance possession more directly than other categories in three ways.
The portrait advances one extra stage on acceptance, capped at stage four until the fifth acceptance.
The next main offer arrives at the short end of its delay range.
The leader becomes the network's seizure route, so a four-deal refusal without a Protected Successor favours seizure over territorial revolt.
A personal offer cannot count twice, skip the next main bargain, or produce possession at four acceptances.

Side bargains concern a bounded local favour: releasing an official, securing a convoy, accelerating one inspection, or protecting a witness.
They can create a witness, an obligation, or local leverage.
They cannot satisfy a missing main acceptance or assemble a national coup by accumulation.
At most two side favours are unresolved at a time, presented as ordinary events when relevant.
No side reward repeats from a reload, a leadership change, a destroyed temporary country, or cycling the same target.

## Unsolicited and unsigned assistance

An evolution or special incident can put an unsolicited gift in the government's path.
The country can inspect it, quarantine it, send it back, or explicitly put it into national service under stated terms.
Its physical arrival is not a main acceptance.

Adopting a principal unsolicited gift can become a main arrangement only through a visible choice that states the practical benefit and concession.
The visitor can lie about the source of his generosity; the game must not lie about which player action constituted agreement.
Inspectors can be compromised, and a minister can advocate using a desperately needed delivery.
Those are playable dilemmas, not retroactive consent.

## Rare variants

Each rare variant appears at most once per campaign and only where its condition genuinely arises.

**The unsigned gift.** The assistance arrives before the government answers, using the inspect, quarantine, return, or adopt choices above.

**The false name.** The leader signs under a disputed title or an office that does not legally exist.
The claim can later be contested as a loophole, transferred to the office instead of the person, or found to bind the wrong person.

**The resistant minister.** One official notices the pattern and cannot be compromised.
He becomes a ready-made Protected Witness if the government shields him, a resistance leader after possession, or a target whose death is itself evidence.

**The willing government.** A government that sees the stranger as a useful patron can accept the next offer immediately rather than waiting through its delay range, down to the 120-day floor.
Early resistance is weaker because fewer officials object.

**The divided cabinet.** The leader refuses, but ministers try to accept through emergency authority.
The player decides whether that authority can bind the state.
Upholding it counts as the acceptance; denying it is a refusal and leaves the ministers as compromised witnesses.

**The rival tempter.** Another supernatural actor interferes with a pending offer (Part 10), leaving the host between competing promises.

**The leader who bargains back.** With an Outside Witness or a proven loophole in hand, a leader can propose terms to Mephistopheles.
Success cancels one arrangement's institution without the usual sacrifice.
Failure creates a stronger personal claim: the portrait advances one stage and the next offer arrives early.

**The contract survives annexation.** If the host disappears before the chain resolves, the relationship follows the leader in exile, the legal successor, or the named institution, whichever actually survives.

## Failed delivery and changes of government

A failed promise cannot advance the relationship as though the promised assistance arrived.
If a reward is partly delivered, the player is told what remains outstanding and offered revised terms or cancellation.
Ordinary engine failure is not a narrative reward for Mephistopheles.

Institutional history belongs to the country and arrangement, while personal vulnerability belongs to the actual character.
Replacing the leader does not reset four established ministries, and it does not make a newly arrived successor fully possessed without a new event or an existing delegated-authority basis.
Part 5 separates these effects, and the Possession Moves evolution changes them (Part 10).

Annexation, capitulation, a new civil war, and a move into exile require the active relationship to be reconciled with the real surviving authority.
No successor receives a fresh copy of an already paid reward because the government's carrier changed.

## Route guarantees

The following guarantees govern all later parts.
Five valid main acceptances produce peaceful possession and no opening civil war.
Four clean refusals and no acceptance produce the weakest open manifestation.
The first refusal before any acceptance is never punished; every refusal after one to three acceptances brings retaliation and a punitive offer before settlement, and refusal after four brings a seizure attempt or a major revolt.
Partial acceptance retains the consequences of actual surviving institutions and offers costly resistance.
Personal and side content cannot change the acceptance count.
Evidence and counterplay precede the final decision.
The player keeps a playable continuation.

Every implementation and test matrix derives from these guarantees rather than introducing a hidden threshold that overrides them.
