# Deal with the Devil: foreign bargains, clients, and the Infernal Compact

## Assistance becomes foreign policy

An open Mephistophelean state or a fully possessed host can use the same principle abroad: solve a serious problem and acquire a protected institutional relationship.
Foreign bargaining is baseline content.
The early cross-border evolution changes when limited contacts can begin; it does not create the basic foreign system.

The foreign game should let the infernal player choose a few consequential relationships, finance useful deliveries, and decide how much to extract from resulting clients.
It should not become an enormous spreadsheet of hidden influence numbers or a global broadcast of offers every month.

Targets retain agency.
A human target receives real choices.
An AI target evaluates the visible proposal and what it knows about the benefactor.
Countries that know Mephistopheles openly can still accept assistance, but their decision concerns a known dangerous patron rather than an implausible repeated disguise.

## A bounded diplomatic desk

The proposed normal capacity is three active foreign relationships being negotiated at once, with only one live principal offer per target.
At each review, show a shortlist of at most five meaningful candidates.
The player selects a target and a deliverable offer category; the actual bargain is tailored to that target's real need.

A review of the bounded active relationships occurs through the existing event scheduler or explicit outcomes.
The implementation must not scan every country every day.
A new candidate refresh can occur at a proposed 60-day cadence, and an individual offer normally gives its recipient 30 days to respond.
A human pending popup does not create repeated identical offers.

A refused opening imposes a proposed 180-day reapproach cooldown for the same relationship.
A different genuine emergency can justify reconsideration through a specific event, but changing the label on the same cargo is not a way to bypass refusal.
The original host's main chain remains distinct and cannot be replaced with cheaper foreign steps while it is incomplete.

The infernal player's ordinary decision surface can show target name, current visible relationship, selected assistance, commitments already made by that target, and pending practical obligations.
It does not show a continent-wide grid of hidden corruption.
A target sees only its own known arrangement and evidence, not the patron's entire intelligence picture.

## Selecting relevant targets

Prioritize countries facing an actual military defeat, reconstruction need, disease or famine crisis, lost research capacity, diplomatic isolation, leadership danger, or substantial political instability.
A peaceful aspirational offer is possible where the promised industrial, scientific, or diplomatic benefit is genuinely useful.

Eligibility also depends on delivery.
A landlocked isolated patron cannot promise ordinary overseas transport it cannot provide.
A medical intervention needs access to the target population or an explicitly supported delivery route.
A scientific recovery needs the target's real loss history.
An economic offer needs useful sites and an available production or resource commitment.

Exclude actual nonhuman or terminal actors from ordinary possession bargaining unless a separately designed interaction establishes that they can meaningfully consent to this kind of institutional claim.
Complete control by another event is not silently displaced.
Ordinary subjects can receive limited assistance, but binding sovereign commitments require their actual legal and diplomatic authority.
An overlord's existing relationship is not erased by a hidden foreign-bargain counter.

A subject seeking a binding independent guarantee needs an explicit overlord response, a supported change in its status, or a visible independence conflict.
A human overlord is not forced to surrender rights without an action that represents the dispute.

## Foreign rewards consume real capacity

The patron must finance a delivery through its actual stocks, industry, medical access, research institutions, or supported supernatural production.
Foreign rewards are not unlimited copies of the original event's one-time generosity.
Holding three active negotiations therefore creates meaningful competition for resources.

An arms offer reserves and transfers a bounded useful stock.
An industrial programme commits capacity for a stated interval.
A medical offer occupies real treatment or delivery capacity and applies the disease owner's outcomes.
A scientific offer shares verified knowledge or rebuilds a valid target institution.
A diplomatic offer provides something the patron and its partners can actually grant.

If a promised delivery fails before commitment, do not debit the target's concession or advance its binding stage.
A partial delivery requires an explicit revised agreement or cancellation.
A refusal releases the patron's unused reservation without producing duplicate goods.
A war or loss of access can interrupt an ongoing service under the terms the target saw; it cannot retroactively make the initial accepted reward fictional.

## Three foreign commitments

The proposed accelerated foreign sequence has three principal commitments, distinct from the original host's five main acceptances.
This shorter structure is new design tuning and remains recorded as a proposal.

The first, **Practical Assistance**, delivers one large useful benefit and establishes a limited local office.
Its concession is narrow enough that a government can rationally accept even while distrusting the patron.
The target has not yet become a client.

The second, **A Continuing Mission**, expands assistance and permits protected staff or a more durable institutional role.
It creates stronger dependency and evidence.
The target receives an opportunity to establish independent records, replace part of the service, or refuse further expansion.
A secret system does not hide the mission's ordinary public authority.

The third, **A Guarantee of Continuity**, delivers the promised final assistance and establishes a binding client relationship under explicit visible terms.
The target must have received enough evidence to understand that accepting affects its independence.
The exact identity of a still-masked patron can remain uncertain until the appropriate reveal, but the game cannot describe the final public concession as a trivial one-time adviser appointment.

The steps are normally separated by 90–180 days, with an absolute proposed floor of 60 days.
Foreign progression is faster than the original years-long courtship because the patron has an actual state and delivery institutions.
It is not a three-click annexation in the same week.

## Client government and open possession are different conditions

A controlled client can retain a local leader and much of its domestic administration while binding policy and selected institutions to the patron.
The leader may be compromised, possessed, or constrained by loyal officials according to the actual route.
Do not use one portrait state to imply an individual transformation that has not occurred.

Open possession is a further revealed political settlement.
It can occur through an explicit client choice, a successful supported takeover after a breach, or a route developed by the patron's political branch.
It is not a free fourth acceptance that happened offscreen.
The foreign sequence does not become another complete duplicate of the original five-deal narrative.

A client retains a playable local game.
Where the chosen native subject relationship imposes restrictions, those restrictions are part of the stated commitment and must be tested.
A custom political obligation must not be described as a native autonomy mechanic unless the implementation actually uses it.

Existing completed national content is preserved where compatible.
The client receives an appropriately scoped altered political branch and obligations, rather than automatically copying every principal-state focus and world-end power.
The local government's resistance route remains available under its concrete conditions.

## Obligations follow the aid received

A military client supplies access, a defined expeditionary or auxiliary contribution, or a bounded equipment commitment.
The starting ceiling is approximately 20 percent of its effective sustainable land force for an ordinary ongoing contribution.
The final representation needs verified transfer and return behavior.
A small threatened client must not surrender its last viable defending formation merely because a fixed minimum says “one division.”

An economic client commits a bounded portion of usable industrial or resource capacity, initially targeting about 10 percent.
Use the supported native or owner allocation mechanism, not a hidden destruction of its factories followed by newly created factories for the patron.
The same capacity is counted once in all economic and terminal calculations.

A scientific client gives access to a defined programme, knowledge-sharing arrangement, or project capacity.
It does not automatically reveal every technology, duplicate a unique scientist, or transfer an exclusive project actor.
The target can preserve an independent archive as a costly source of later resistance.

A medical client provides treatment access, staff cooperation, logistics, or a supported modified-troop programme.
Its obligation is not an unexplained recurring subtraction of civilian population.
The people whom the patron saved remain living beneficiaries with political interests; their gratitude can support the client government without equating gratitude with possession.

A diplomatic client supports recognition, access, mediation, or a specific foreign position.
Its involvement follows the actual diplomatic system and cannot automatically bind every member of its former faction.
A political client can host missions or introduce the patron to neighboring authorities, but one introduction is not another country's consent.

Later accepted bargains can create a mixed obligation profile.
Present the resulting practical duties clearly and cap their combined burden.
Do not independently apply the maximum levy from all six categories until the client becomes unusable.

## Protection, extraction, and credibility

A protective patron spends resources to keep clients alive.
It can relieve an emergency, defend a client, replace an interrupted service, or accept a temporary reduction of obligations during a genuine crisis.
This strengthens its future credibility and reduces resistance without needing a public reputation meter.

An extractive patron demands the maximum supported contribution, insists on protected appointments, and treats defection as a threat to its own authority.
It gains more immediate capacity and political control, while damaging the willingness of secure foreign governments to deal with it and increasing the risk of coordinated resistance.

Failure to protect a client against a specifically guaranteed threat is meaningful evidence.
It can create a loophole or political movement.
It does not automatically cancel every treaty because the patron lost one unrelated battle.
The terms of the actual promise matter.

The patron can also make a fresh voluntary offer to a resisting client.
That delivery has a real cost and creates an explicit new settlement.
It must not reset all opposition and repay all original rewards from the beginning.

## Resistance and defection

A foreign government that accepted only the first assistance can replace the service, close the office, or settle the material obligation at a manageable but meaningful cost.
After the second commitment, separation becomes a serious institutional struggle.
After client status, it requires the local liberation route, a negotiated release, or a supported conflict.

Local resistance depends on concrete independent capacity: surviving records, officials, military commands, treatment alternatives, foreign guarantees, or a preserved lawful successor.
A fully compromised local cabinet can still face popular or military opposition, but those actors need actual organization before they can produce a new playable government.

A client can seek outside help.
A third country can offer replacement aid, protect a witness, guarantee the independent government, or intervene through a real war.
The helper pays real costs and does not simply click “liberate client” to steal a country without the local struggle.

Defection ends or contests the actual obligations.
Expeditionary units, leased capacity, and ongoing services must be returned or reconciled according to their supported ownership.
A client cannot keep all transferred foreign materiel and recover its original stockpile again through cleanup.
The patron cannot delete all ordinary national industry as a punitive shortcut.

## Conquest and imposed clients

Military victory can install an infernal client government through the supported peace and occupation systems.
This is an imposed origin, not proof that the target accepted three foreign bargains.
It has greater immediate resistance, weaker credibility as a consensual relationship, and a different route into any later ratification.

A conquered territory is not automatically a possessed signatory to the Last Signature.
It must develop an actual government, durable control, sufficient capacity, and a binding political settlement.
Releasing many tiny puppets on the eve of the finale does not manufacture a global order.

Foreign control by another event remains a conflict to resolve, not a flag to clear on conquest.
The actor that owns a terminal or nonhuman state retains its own defeat and cleanup contract.

## Forming the Infernal Compact

The proposed initial condition is the principal regime plus at least two other meaningful participants: controlled clients, openly committed allies, or governments with binding foreign commitments.
At least one participant besides the principal must have a durable binding relationship; two countries that merely received a first consignment do not form an infernal faction by accident.

Prepare the charter through the shared tree, then require the actual parties and valid diplomatic relationships before formation.
A faction does not arise from one country alone.
A member already constrained by another faction or overlord needs a supported transition and an actual choice or conflict.

The Compact coordinates access, defense, war planning, client obligations, and limited joint formations.
It can speed practical delivery because members share institutions and transport.
It does not cause every country on the same continent to begin accepting offers.

Members have different motives.
A military client wants protection; an industrial partner wants markets and reconstruction; a scientific client wants access; an openly possessed state wants ideological and supernatural consolidation.
Their disputes create useful events when the patron demands sacrifices inconsistent with those motives.

## The Compact under strain

A major defeat can expose the gap between guaranteed protection and the patron's actual reach.
A failed medical commitment can create an independent hospital network.
A distributed possessed minister can favor some clients over others.
The response can be a negotiated reduction of duties, a genuine renewed rescue, a coercive intervention, or a factional rupture.

Do not create a second faction-wide corruption meter.
The relevant relationships, promises, actual failures, and surviving institutions already provide the basis for consequences.
The player should be deciding whom to help and whom to risk losing, not managing thirty nearly identical sliders.

## Foreign activity before the principal reveal

Earlier, smaller contacts exist only under the Offers Across Borders evolution (Part 10).
When the principal state forms, surviving contacts become starting relationships with their actual history; they do not retroactively become binding clients.
