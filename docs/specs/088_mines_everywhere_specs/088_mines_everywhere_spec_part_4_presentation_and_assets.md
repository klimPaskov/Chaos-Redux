# Event 088: Mines everywhere
## Part 4. Presentation and asset direction

### Presentation intent

The first report should make an enormous geographical change understandable through a concrete maritime observation. Its viewpoint is close enough to show that mines are physically present and broad enough to establish that reports are arriving from distant seas. The reader does not need a secret explanation for who manufactured them.

The tone combines an operationally serious threat with the absurdity of universal ownership. The option can carry dry irony about the abundance being offered, but it must not trivialize casualties that the event has not actually observed. Humor belongs in the acknowledgment rather than in a fake mechanical choice.

This part provides writing direction. It does not supply finished localisation, achievement names, quotes, slogans, or ready-to-paste option lines. The canonical event name supplied by the user remains unchanged.

The report can mention that mutually hostile ownership exists without naming countries whose ownership has not been verified. Dynamic country or region names must refer to real records. A count must be labeled as an eligible owner count or valid naval region count rather than implying an unverified worldwide total of mines.

### Narrative sequence

Begin with the physical presence of a mine where the reader can picture it. Move to the operational problem of numerous independent fields across distant waters. End with the practical fact that routes can be cleared, while simply changing destination will not remove the global geographical reach of the incident.

Avoid a stock sequence of official denial, intercepted telegrams, frantic staff rooms, and a map covered with pins. Those details would substitute a familiar reporting frame for the specific phenomenon. An anonymous maritime encounter can carry the story without inventing a historical document or quoting a real person.

The mystery is limited to origin. The mechanics should be explicit. Players should never need to infer from an atmospheric paragraph whether fields belong to separate countries, whether existing mines were replaced, or whether clearance is permanent against this event's contribution.

### Report image

**Asset key:** `e088_report_global_mines`, a planning identifier pending collision checks.

The main composition depicts a period-appropriate small naval or working vessel near a visible sea mine, with additional mines receding across the water. The foreground mine must read clearly at the actual report size. The background should establish scale without making the frame an impossible aerial view of every ocean.

The subject is fictional, so generated documentary-style artwork is appropriate. The source generation should resemble a plausible wartime photograph rather than a glossy modern render. The final report treatment is black-and-white or restrained sepia according to the inspected project reference family. The report-card tilt, border treatment, and transparent outer corners come from the repository's deterministic report processor after generation.

The full source image must not prepaint a tilted card, labels, captions, a decorative UI frame, or a collage. Do not include readable flags that accidentally assign the incident to a real country, modern vessels or sensors, an exploding ship, or bodies. One local scene illustrates the incident. It is not visual proof of the full worldwide placement.

The planned final report asset is 210 × 176 pixels. Exact crop, alpha, and processing must be verified against the actual supplied skill assets before production. The current generic minelaying sprite is a legacy reference to inspect, not acceptance of reused final art.

### Shared interface presentation

Use the ordinary report and the shared event detail surfaces. The native naval map remains the appropriate place to inspect current minefields. The detail description explains what the event changed and directs the player's understanding toward ownership and clearance, without creating a second mine-management interface.

The effect tooltip needs a compact ownership explanation because a very large country-region loop is not a useful autogenerated tooltip. It must not display only the first country's conditional result as if that result applied to all countries. A single readable statement about the rules is more useful than hundreds of repeated effect lines.

If historical counts are exposed, the detail view must state their snapshot meaning. A successful application to 100 regions does not imply those regions remain fully mined months later. Do not expose a stale field count as a live navigation aid.

### Achievement icon family

Two 64 × 64 achievement subjects are specified in Part 5. They share a restrained maritime material language but must remain distinguishable at native size. The clearance achievement centers on a mine and a visibly opened channel. The offensive achievement centers on a landing craft crossing a cleared gap toward a distant coast.

Each subject is generated once as original colored artwork with a usable transparent background. The completed, grey, and not-eligible states are then derived through the verified achievement templates and overlay processor. The three states are a coherent triplet of one image, not separately generated paintings.

The grey conversion must preserve silhouette and readability. The not-eligible state uses the supplied fixed overlay without recreating or recoloring it. Evidence must compare the decoded final DDS files at 64 × 64 with the selected template family. A large source preview cannot demonstrate native-size quality.
