# Equipment Choice
## Part 5. What the player sees

## Ordinary category presentation

Use the native event report and a simple ordinary decision category.
The category has a static picture, a category icon, short context text, and the native choices.
It has three actions during the evolved format phase and at most six during category selection.
The first combined selection removes its group from the second-selection pool.
The category's ordinary controls and text carry all gameplay interaction.

The header identifies the current offer, its exact deadline, and its delivery format.
Below it, show remaining selections and the current handling condition when one exists.
A scheduled second consignment shows its date and frozen contents in the relevant receipt tooltip.
Do not fill the header with internal value allowances, raw load ratios, random seeds, helper identifiers, or queue bookkeeping.
The player should understand whether a package is immediately useful, a deliberate reserve, or temporarily blocked.

Every choice provides a readable summary of its main equipment and an expanded tooltip with all rows.
The tooltip gives concrete counts and models, complete design identity, consumer access, material-only formation equivalents where relevant, manpower and training obligations, delivery timing, the possible handling effects, and exact disclosed setback chance.
Existing stockpile and research interfaces supply further detail.
The event does not duplicate those interfaces.

Locked categories show a concise actionable reason when their purpose remains relevant.
A landlocked country with no sea-supply role does not need a permanently disabled maritime button.
A country that has just lost its airbase can see why its previously published aircraft choice is unavailable and how the entitlement is preserved.

## Writing directions

Every row below is a writing brief.
It is not pasteable localisation.
Use complete natural prose, with concrete military and material observations.
Minor absurdity can appear through impossible arrival details and the mismatch between tidy supply categories and what physically occupies the depots.
Avoid staged official-denial contrasts, modern interface jokes, borrowed quotations, and summaries of implementation changes.

Unavailable values explain their missing premise and never display a misleading zero.

## Variation that supports the event

Prepare six short arrival-observation directions for conventional reports, three for overflow, and one family-specific direction for each adopted unusual family.
Examples of direction subjects include stamped crates in an already counted warehouse, a train of military stores with no originating journey in the available records, matching packing lists in distant capitals, a depot whose aisles no longer fit its original plan, mechanical cargo following incompatible signals, and coal residue around intact golem stores.
These are fictional observation subjects for later writing.
They are not historical claims or final lines.

Choose conventional flavor uniformly from eligible observations, with an immediate-repeat exclusion.
Flavor never changes quantities, eligibility, setback chance, or AI preference.
The family-specific unusual direction follows the frozen actual family.
Its description must reflect the actual installed equipment and consumer, including current inherited presentation limitations recorded for implementation review.
