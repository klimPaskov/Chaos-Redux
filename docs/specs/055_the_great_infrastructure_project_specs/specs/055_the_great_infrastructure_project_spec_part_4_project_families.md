# Event 55 Specification, Part 4

## Project Families

## Family design rule

Each project family needs a distinct map purpose, cost profile, risk profile, and permanent reward. A railway must not be a highway with a different icon. A grand port must not be a generic coastal factory decision. A resource corridor must connect a real source state to a real destination.

Project rewards should change logistics, movement, supply, trade, extraction, repair, or strategic access in a way that the player can feel. Small national modifiers can support the result, but they should not replace real map effects.

## Continental railways

### Promise

A continental railway connects distant parts of a country or several countries through a real rail corridor.

The family covers national trunk lines, transcontinental railways, industrial freight lines, frontier railways, and extensions through newly integrated territory.

### Valid geography

A railway proposal needs:

- meaningful distant endpoints
- a connected land route
- several route states for a national or continental project
- an economic, military, integration, or relief reason
- no complete duplication of an existing high-capacity Event 55 railway

Strong endpoints include:

- capital to distant coast
- industrial belt to major port
- resource region to industrial center
- western to eastern industrial regions
- northern to southern population centers
- national core to a newly integrated frontier
- allied capital to allied port

### Map outcome

The completed project should build or upgrade actual railways along the selected route.

The route can also create or improve supply hubs at major junctions when the network would otherwise have no useful supply consumer. Supply hubs should remain rare because they are strong and expensive.

The railway should not maximize every railway level in the country. Its strength comes from one coherent trunk route.

### Costs

Railway costs emphasize:

- trains
- support equipment
- civilian construction burden
- steel access or an industrial diversion condition
- trucks for remote surveys and construction support
- political power for land and foreign agreements

A maritime or imported-material railway can also require convoys.

### Risks

Railway-specific risks include:

- bridge failure
- tunnel collapse
- landslide
- severe winter interruption
- gauge or standard conflict between partners
- shortage of locomotives
- a junction built in the wrong place
- military seizure of the line
- sabotage at a critical bridge or depot

### Permanent value

A completed railway should provide:

- real railway capacity along its route
- improved supply access to connected nodes
- faster railway repair in route states
- stronger local construction and industrial linkage near major stations
- a route-specific movement or logistics benefit where supported
- lower famine relief and migration transport difficulty through the shared adapter when the route is operational

### Strategic railway variants

Possible route roles include:

#### Coast-to-coast trunk

Connect two distant coasts across a large country.

#### Industrial spine

Connect several high-industry states and one major port.

#### Frontier railway

Connect the national core to a distant border or newly integrated territory.

#### Relief railway

Connect food, port, or secure rear areas to famine or displacement pressure.

#### Allied trunk

Connect several friendly countries through one continuous rail standard and operating agreement.

### Reduced completion

A reduced railway can end at an intermediate junction. It receives weaker benefits and preserves a later extension project.

### Damage behavior

Bombing, occupation, natural disasters, or sabotage in a critical railway state can reduce the project to Partially Disrupted or Severed. The ordinary railway building damage remains visible and must be repaired.

## Continental highways

### Promise

A continental highway creates a strategic road network connecting important regions across the country or allied territory.

Since the opening event already gives maximum ordinary infrastructure, the highway family must represent something above the standard state infrastructure building. It should model a coordinated long-distance road system, freight organization, bridges, service depots, engineering standards, and all-weather military access.

### Valid geography

A highway proposal should connect:

- opposite coasts
- capital and distant border
- major industrial regions
- isolated population centers
- port and inland market
- resource region and rail junction
- allied countries through a continuous land route

A compact country can receive a metropolitan or national ring route when a true transcontinental highway is not meaningful.

### Map outcome

The project uses route state modifiers and supporting buildings rather than claiming another level of ordinary infrastructure above the engine maximum.

A completed highway can improve:

- movement in route states
- supply grace and local supply movement
- attrition resistance in bad weather
- repair speed for infrastructure and railways
- motorized logistics efficiency
- local construction around route nodes

Exact modifiers must be verified during implementation.

### Costs

Highway costs emphasize:

- trucks
- fuel
- support equipment
- civilian construction burden
- industrial resource access
- political power for land and standards

### Risks

Highway-specific risks include:

- fuel shortage
- bridge bottleneck
- severe weather failure
- poor paving or rushed foundations
- vehicle congestion
- local land conflict
- maintenance collapse
- military overuse

### Permanent value

The highway should make motorized movement and repair visibly better along its route. It should not grant a flat national speed bonus unrelated to the selected states.

### Highway variants

#### National arterial route

Connect the capital to a distant industrial or border region.

#### Two-ocean highway

Cross a wide country between two coastal systems.

#### Industrial belt road

Connect several industrial and urban states.

#### Allied military road

Create a continuous route through friendly countries toward a threatened frontier.

#### Desert or mountain highway

Unlocked later, this route trades high cost for reliable all-weather access through difficult terrain.

### Reduced completion

A reduced highway can become a regional trunk that omits the most difficult outer segment.

### Damage behavior

The route remains physically present when damaged, but military overuse, disaster, occupation, or fuel shortage can move it to Strained or Partially Disrupted.

## International trade corridors

### Promise

An international trade corridor joins production centers, border crossings, ports, resource regions, and markets across two or more countries.

The family combines hard transport infrastructure with operating agreements. A corridor is not fully useful when rails exist but border procedures, transit rights, or partner cooperation have collapsed.

### Valid geography

A trade corridor requires:

- at least two sovereign or sufficiently autonomous participants
- a connected route or multimodal chain
- meaningful production, market, port, or resource endpoints
- plausible partner interest
- a valid host and operating agreement

The route can include railway, highway, and port segments.

### Map outcome

Each participant receives map improvements in its own route states according to the project design. The corridor also creates a shared operating framework.

### Costs

Costs are divided among participants and can include:

- civilian construction burden
- trains
- trucks
- convoys
- political power for treaty and customs alignment
- resource access commitments
- support equipment for border and communications systems

Each participant should see only the costs it is expected to pay.

### Risks

Trade corridor risks include:

- partner withdrawal
- customs conflict
- border closure
- embargo
- hostile political change
- unequal traffic demand
- funding dispute
- war between participants
- occupation of a border state
- port congestion
- incompatible standards

### Permanent value

An operational trade corridor can improve:

- participant trade access
- route reliability
- resource movement
- supply movement through partner territory
- port and railway performance
- economic cooperation and relations
- resilience against dependence on one vulnerable transit route

The corridor should not make an embargo irrelevant. An embargoed participant loses or reduces the international benefit while domestic physical segments can remain useful.

### Corridor variants

#### Industrial market corridor

Connect factories in one country to a market or port in another.

#### Resource export corridor

Connect an inland resource region to a foreign or shared port.

#### Allied supply corridor

Connect friendly countries to a threatened member or isolated theater.

#### Relief corridor

Create a protected rail, road, and port route for famine relief or organized migration.

#### Alternative transit corridor

Bypass a hostile, unstable, or embargoed transit route.

### Reduced completion

A corridor can open with fewer partners when the remaining route is continuous and valuable. The excluded partner's segment and benefits must be removed cleanly.

### Damage behavior

A corridor can be physically intact but Dormant because its agreement has failed. Renegotiation restores legal operation. Physical destruction requires repair.

## Underwater tunnels

### Promise

An underwater tunnel creates a permanent fixed link across a narrow sea or major strait.

This family is unlocked by Evolution II and is limited to predeclared crossings that the engine and map can support.

### Valid geography

A tunnel proposal requires:

- a registered crossing token
- suitable terminal states
- ownership or consent on both approaches
- an actual supported strategic connection
- a crossing length within the current evolution's limits
- a defined treatment for naval and shipping behavior
- enough National Works Capacity

Possible historical inspirations include Britain and continental Europe, Japan and Korea, Sicily and mainland Italy, and other narrow straits. The actual candidate registry must be based on the installed map and must not assume every named example is safe.

### Map outcome

The completed tunnel should enable the exact land, supply, and movement connection promised by the proposal.

If implementation cannot produce that connection reliably, the crossing must remain absent from the candidate registry. A national spirit or linked-port bonus is not an acceptable hidden substitute.

### Costs

Tunnel costs emphasize:

- long civilian construction burden
- support equipment
- fuel
- industrial resource access
- trains when the tunnel carries rail
- political power and partner agreement
- convoys for construction logistics where relevant

### Risks

Tunnel-specific risks include:

- groundwater
- pressure and ventilation failure
- fire safety
- geological fault
- approach collapse
- financing failure
- cross-border safety dispute
- military closure
- flooding after bombing or sabotage

### Permanent value

The tunnel can provide:

- a real permanent crossing
- supply and unit movement independent of ordinary shipping
- railway or highway continuity
- lower convoy dependence for the linked route
- a major strategic node that can be damaged or closed

### Safety and security

A tunnel should have a commissioning and safety stage. A rushed project can open as Strained and require an overhaul.

Wartime closure or damage should suspend the crossing rather than delete the project record.

## Great bridges

### Promise

A great bridge creates a fixed crossing over a major strait, river, or narrow waterway where a bridge is geographically and technically suitable.

The bridge family is unlocked by Evolution II for the largest crossings. Smaller major river bridges can appear earlier as part of a railway or highway project.

### Valid geography

A standalone great bridge needs:

- a registered crossing token or verified major river node
- valid approaches
- a real movement or supply consumer
- a clear reason to choose a bridge over a tunnel
- control or consent on both sides

Shipping lanes, air approaches, terrain, and military exposure can affect the design.

### Map outcome

The bridge must provide the promised crossing or route continuity. Its approaches should receive supporting road or rail improvements.

### Costs

Bridge costs emphasize:

- civilian construction burden
- support equipment
- trucks or trains according to the route
- fuel
- industrial resource access
- political power for bilateral works

### Risks

Bridge-specific risks include:

- foundation failure
- navigation conflict
- high wind
- ice
- bombing exposure
- approach bottleneck
- partner dispute
- ship collision or sabotage

### Permanent value

The bridge provides route continuity, movement, supply, and economic connection. It can also become an important radar or air-defense node at later evolutions when the location supports it.

### Hybrid bridge and tunnel

A registered crossing can offer a hybrid design when shipping, air, or geology makes one structure unsuitable for the full distance.

The hybrid should be one project object with one operational status.

## Grand ports

### Promise

A grand port transforms one strategically important coastal state into a large logistics, repair, trade, and naval support complex.

### Valid geography

A port proposal needs:

- a coastal state
- a useful sea position
- a meaningful hinterland or naval role
- enough room for real port expansion
- a valid rail or highway link to the interior

Strong candidates include:

- an existing major naval base
- the main port of an island country
- a port serving an inland resource corridor
- a port on an international trade route
- a remote military supply anchor

### Map outcome

A completed grand port can add or improve:

- naval base capacity
- dockyards where justified
- coastal supply connection
- railway link to the interior
- repair and convoy handling support
- local anti-air or radar at later evolutions

The project should not turn every port into a major naval arsenal. It selects one or a small number of critical sites.

### Costs

Port costs emphasize:

- convoys
- civilian construction burden
- support equipment
- trucks and trains for the hinterland
- fuel for dredging and heavy construction
- industrial resource access

### Risks

Port-specific risks include:

- dredging failure
- silting
- storm damage
- labor dispute
- shipyard bottleneck
- convoy shortage
- blockade
- bombing
- rail connection delay

### Permanent value

A grand port should improve:

- naval supply
- convoy handling
- repair capability
- trade access
- overseas operations
- famine relief and migration reception through adapters when the port is operational

### Port network variant

Evolution III can link several grand ports through shared railway and shipping routes. Each port remains a real node whose loss reduces the network.

## Resource corridors

### Promise

A resource corridor connects an existing remote resource region to industry, a supply hub, or an export port.

The family is especially valuable for countries with large interiors, difficult terrain, or resources trapped behind poor transport.

### Valid geography

A resource corridor needs:

- a state with meaningful existing resources or a valid Event 18 discovery
- a distant industrial, port, or logistics destination
- a connected route
- a clear access problem
- no completed Event 55 corridor already serving the same source and destination

### Map outcome

The project combines:

- railway or highway construction
- supply support
- extraction improvements
- local industry or loading facilities where justified
- an export or national distribution link

The resource improvement should be tied to the source state and route operation.

### Costs

Resource corridor costs emphasize:

- trains or trucks
- support equipment
- civilian construction burden
- fuel in remote terrain
- convoys when the destination is a port
- political power for concessions, rights, or foreign finance

### Risks

Resource corridor risks include:

- geological disappointment
- local resistance
- contractor corruption
- labor shortage
- difficult terrain
- foreign concession pressure
- criminal competition
- sabotage
- route capture

When Event 18 has created a dangerous or anomalous resource state, Event 55 should connect only through an explicit safe adapter. It should not erase Event 18's own danger or containment rules.

### Permanent value

A completed corridor can improve:

- local resource extraction
- supply to the source state
- rail or road connection
- national access to the resource
- export potential when the destination is a port
- local construction or industrial support

### Domestic lifeline variant

During a Great Embargo, a domestic resource corridor can reduce one specific imported-resource vulnerability when the country owns an underused substitute deposit. It cannot remove the wider embargo system.

## Continental networks

### Promise

Evolution III combines several projects into a continental transport network.

A network is not one oversized modifier. It is a linked set of real nodes and segments with a shared agreement and operational status.

### Valid geography

A network needs:

- at least three countries or one exceptionally large country with several completed national trunks
- at least three meaningful nodes
- a connected route or multimodal chain
- high National Works Capacity in the host
- partner support where required
- no active war among core participants

### Network forms

Possible forms include:

- a continent-spanning railway system
- a highway and rail freight grid
- a chain of linked grand ports
- a railway, bridge, and tunnel route across several straits
- a multinational resource and industrial corridor
- an allied logistics network

### Costs and duration

Networks require several staged contributions and can take multiple years. The project can open in operational segments instead of waiting for the entire network to finish.

Partial opening should not count as final completion for the hardest achievements.

### Permanent value

A complete network provides stronger route resilience and partner benefits, but it also creates shared dependence. A severed central node can disrupt several countries at once.

### Governance

The network creates a multinational authority or operating council. This is represented through agreements, project status, and decisions. It does not need a new country or a separate faction.

## Family overlap rules

Some projects combine several modes. A resource corridor can include rail and a port. An international corridor can include highways and tunnels. A continental network can include all families.

The project should have one primary family for proposal diversity, achievements, and cost logic. Secondary components can be recorded without allowing the same project to count as several completed families unless the achievement explicitly allows it.

## Project family acceptance criteria

Every family must have:

- a hard geographic validity test
- distinct candidate scoring
- a route or facility footprint
- a family-specific cost profile
- a family-specific duration range
- family-specific incidents
- a real permanent map or logistics outcome
- reduced completion behavior
- damage and repair behavior
- AI priorities
- dynamic localisation that names the real geography
- asset coverage through its own decision or mission icon family
