# Event 55 Specification, Part 5

## International Corridors and Fixed Crossings

## International design promise

The multinational side of Event 55 should create real cooperation with real points of failure. A foreign country must not become a passive route tile whose territory can be used without consent.

The system should make a shared route valuable enough that countries will accept costs and dependence. It should also make border conflict, embargo, political collapse, and unequal benefits capable of damaging the arrangement.

## Project roles

An international project distinguishes several roles.

### Host

The host proposes the project, holds the main project record, manages the primary construction mission, and carries the largest administrative burden.

### Core partner

A core partner contributes territory, resources, industry, equipment, or a major terminal node. Its continued participation is required for full operation.

### Transit partner

A transit partner grants route access and may make a smaller contribution. It receives a route benefit but does not manage the full project.

### Terminal partner

A terminal partner owns a destination port, market, industrial region, or resource area. Its node is critical even when only a short part of the route lies in its territory.

### Guarantor or financier

A friendly major power can support construction without owning a route segment. It receives influence, commercial access, or diplomatic benefit, but does not become a physical route partner.

One country can hold more than one role. The role structure should remain internal unless the distinction changes a visible contribution or right.

## Proposal preparation

Before the host can authorize an international corridor, it needs a valid preliminary route and a plausible partner set.

The proposal should identify:

- the host and intended partners
- the route states in each country
- the origin and destination nodes
- the main shared benefit
- the initial contribution expected from each participant
- the operating rules that matter
- the consequences of withdrawal

The proposal should not reveal a partner as guaranteed to accept. It should show that negotiation is required.

## Invitation sequence

The host sends one bounded invitation to each required partner.

The invitation should explain the visible project, route, expected contribution, and likely benefit. It should not expose hidden AI weights.

Partners receive context-appropriate choices:

- accept the requested share
- accept as a transit partner with lower cost and lower benefit
- request that the host pays more
- request a different route or terminal
- delay for a limited period
- refuse

A partner that has already accepted should not receive repeated invitations for the same project generation.

## Partner willingness

Partner AI should consider:

- relations with the host
- faction or alliance membership
- ideology and diplomatic alignment
- war state
- claims or active hostility
- trade and resource complementarity
- route states under threat
- expected share of benefits
- requested construction burden
- train, truck, convoy, and fuel reserves
- embargo status
- current international project commitments
- dependence risk
- prior host reliability
- relief need

A partner must refuse or receive zero willingness when the route is impossible, the invitation generation is stale, the project uses hostile territory, or the country cannot operate ordinary civilian systems.

## Contribution packages

Each partner contribution should use a coherent package with no more than four spendable cost types in one action.

Example contribution roles include:

### Industrial share

Civilian construction burden, support equipment, and resource access.

### Railway share

Trains, civilian construction burden, and route access.

### Highway share

Trucks, fuel, and civilian construction burden.

### Maritime share

Convoys, support equipment, and port construction burden.

### Security share

Command power, support equipment, and unit presence requirements. This role is suitable only when the route faces a real military threat.

A non-consumed condition such as controlling a border state is a requirement and does not count as a spendable cost.

## Benefit allocation

Benefits should reflect route role and contribution.

The host receives the strongest national coordination benefit and any host-specific project experience.

A core partner receives full benefits in its route states and a meaningful national trade or logistics benefit.

A transit partner receives local route improvements, transit income or trade access, and a smaller national benefit.

A financier receives commercial influence or diplomatic benefit, not physical route effects in states it does not own.

No partner should pay a major cost for a token reward. A route with sharply unequal benefits should increase refusal and renegotiation pressure.

## Shared operating agreement

Physical infrastructure and the operating agreement are separate parts of the project.

The agreement can cover:

- transit rights
- customs treatment
- rail standards
- maintenance shares
- route security
- wartime use
- relief priority
- embargo exceptions only where the owning embargo system allows them
- dispute settlement
- rules for withdrawal

The player should see a concise summary of the current agreement. The detailed internal ledger should remain hidden.

## Partner generation and receipts

Every invitation, acceptance, refusal, counteroffer, and withdrawal must carry the project generation identity.

A late response from an old proposal cannot alter a new project.

The host should record one result per invited partner and proceed only after all required receipts are resolved or the negotiation deadline expires.

When a partner becomes invalid during negotiation, the system should record an automatic withdrawal reason and offer rerouting or reduced scope.

## Negotiation deadline

International negotiations should normally resolve within `90` to `180` days, depending on partner count and project scale.

A delayed partner can receive one short extension. The host should not lose a project slot forever because an AI country never resolves its event.

At the deadline, unresolved partners count as refusal unless a specific active diplomatic crisis justifies suspension.

## Counteroffers

A counteroffer should change a material part of the project.

Useful counteroffers include:

- lower equipment contribution in exchange for lower benefit
- host-funded construction on the partner's segment
- a different terminal port
- stronger transit guarantees
- shared military access during war
- a relief priority clause
- a larger maintenance share from the host

Avoid counteroffers that only change a tiny opinion modifier.

The host can accept, reject, or redesign the route.

## Withdrawal before construction

A partner can withdraw before procurement with limited damage.

The host can:

- replace the partner
- reroute domestically
- reduce the project to a shorter bilateral or national route
- cancel and recover most unspent contributions

The withdrawing partner loses some trust when it had already signed a binding agreement.

## Withdrawal during construction

Withdrawal after construction begins is more serious.

Possible consequences include:

- immediate suspension of the partner's segment
- loss of the partner's future contributions
- cost overrun for the host
- a reduced route choice
- a diplomatic dispute
- an unfinished foreign segment

A partner should not be able to withdraw, keep its full permanent benefits, and recover all contributions.

## Withdrawal after completion

A completed physical route can remain in place after withdrawal, but the international operating benefit becomes Dormant or reduced.

The remaining partners can:

- negotiate a new agreement
- buy out the departing partner's operating share
- reroute around its territory
- operate only the surviving domestic segments
- use the route under occupation or imposed control when wider game rules allow it

The project object remains one record and should not duplicate during renegotiation.

## War between participants

War between core participants immediately suspends the shared operating agreement.

Physical segments remain and can be used according to ordinary ownership, control, military access, and supply rules. Event-owned international trade and cooperation benefits stop.

Critical border crossings can become Severed even if the buildings are intact.

A peace settlement can allow renegotiation. The old agreement should not automatically reactivate when territorial ownership or governments have changed.

## War with an outside country

A shared route can become more valuable during an outside war, especially as an allied supply line.

Partners can agree to military priority, which improves supply use but reduces civilian trade benefits and raises sabotage risk.

A neutral partner should not automatically allow military transit merely because it participates in an economic corridor. The operating agreement must define wartime access.

## Embargo interaction

The Great Embargo can reduce or suspend international corridor benefits for the targeted country.

Rules:

- The targeted country keeps physical domestic infrastructure.
- Foreign trade and cooperation benefits from participating countries fall or stop according to the embargo's own reach.
- A participant that joins the embargo can suspend its segment or operating agreement.
- A domestic resource corridor can remain useful as an internal substitute route.
- Event 55 cannot create a universal exemption that nullifies Event 50.
- A corridor built specifically to bypass one vulnerable route can still provide resilience when the alternative participants remain willing and legally able to trade.

The Event 55 adapter should consume embargo status and participant restrictions. It should not own the embargo ledger.

## Sanctions and condemnation

When a participating country faces sanctions or condemnation, willingness to continue can fall. The route should follow the owning sanctions system rather than inventing a parallel sanctions rule.

A partner that is legally or diplomatically blocked from cooperation can suspend its contribution. Physical assets remain.

## Border and customs operation

A corridor can be physically complete but underperform because border procedures and standards remain fragmented.

A post-construction operating mission can ask partners to complete customs, rail-standard, or freight coordination. This should be one shared commissioning stage, not a permanent stream of small decisions.

Success opens the corridor as Operational. Partial success opens it as Strained. Failure leaves it Dormant until renegotiated.

## Relief priority

An international corridor can receive a relief priority clause when famine or migration pressure exists along the route.

The clause can:

- improve relief access
- reduce convoy or transport burden for humanitarian actions
- increase migration reception capacity at connected safe destinations
- temporarily reduce ordinary commercial benefit

Famine and Migration remain owners of their ledgers and outcomes. Event 55 only publishes route capacity and operational status through adapters.

## Multinational authority

A major corridor or Evolution III network can create a joint operating authority.

This authority is a shared project institution, not a new country or faction.

It can handle:

- maintenance shares
- route standards
- dispute resolution
- emergency repair
- partner replacements
- customs coordination
- security requests

The authority should appear through event text, project status, and a small set of decisions. It does not need a dedicated scripted GUI.

## Fixed crossing registry

Bridges and tunnels require a curated registry.

Each registry entry should contain:

- stable crossing identity
- terminal state A
- terminal state B
- crossing family allowed
- normal maximum evolution requirement
- expected route mode, such as rail, highway, or mixed
- map adjacency or crossing consumer
- shipping and naval treatment
- ownership and partner rules
- approach requirements
- blocking conditions
- validation evidence reference

The registry should be built from the installed map and verified through the map tools during implementation.

## Crossing selection

A registered crossing can become a candidate when:

- both terminal states are valid
- the host owns both sides, or the other owner can negotiate
- the route creates real national or international value
- the relevant evolution is active
- the project is not already completed
- no incompatible map state blocks the crossing
- the host has enough National Works Capacity

A crossing should not appear merely because the states are adjacent through a normal sea zone.

## Bridge, tunnel, or hybrid choice

Some crossings can support more than one design.

The selection should consider:

- shipping lanes
- airfield approaches
- water depth
- terrain at each terminal
- military exposure
- route length
- railway versus highway purpose
- cost and duration
- safety risk

A bridge can be faster and cheaper but more exposed. A tunnel can protect movement and shipping but cost more and carry severe safety risk. A hybrid can solve conflicting constraints at the highest cost.

The exact options depend on the registry entry. Do not offer three choices when only one is technically supported.

## Crossing ownership

When one country owns both approaches, the project is domestic.

When two countries own the approaches, both are core partners. The link remains operational only while the operating agreement or another valid access framework exists.

When an approach changes hands:

- the physical crossing remains
- the operating agreement suspends
- the new owner can accept, renegotiate, nationalize, close, or destroy the connection according to wider game rules
- old partner benefits end
- project ownership and generation records update without duplication

## Crossing damage

A fixed crossing can be damaged by:

- strategic bombing
- sabotage
- combat over an approach
- natural disaster
- deliberate military closure
- maintenance failure

Damage should disable the strategic connection when the engine permits it. Repair must restore the same crossing rather than create a duplicate.

A severe tunnel flood or bridge collapse can require a long repair mission and a large equipment commitment.

## Crossing and naval balance

A fixed link should reduce reliance on convoys for movement across that crossing. It should not close the sea zone or make naval control irrelevant elsewhere.

Where the crossing changes naval passage or strait behavior, that effect must be explicit and validated. An unverified naval side effect blocks the registry entry.

## International route status summary

The category should show one short status for each active or completed international project:

- partners confirmed
- awaiting named partner
- agreement under renegotiation
- construction active
- operational
- strained by embargo
- severed by war
- dormant after withdrawal
- repair active

The exact partner and route names belong in tooltips and dynamic text.

## Cleanup and invalidation

International project cleanup must clear:

- invitation markers
- temporary event targets
- partner receipts
- proposed contribution records
- stale selected route states
- active negotiation mission
- temporary diplomatic modifiers
- invalid operating agreement state

Permanent completed project records remain until the physical project is destroyed beyond repair or intentionally retired.

## International acceptance criteria

The multinational system is acceptable only when:

- every foreign route needs consent
- every partner has a visible benefit and cost
- invitation responses are generation-safe
- negotiation cannot hang forever
- refusal creates reroute, reduction, or cancellation options
- withdrawal has stage-specific consequences
- war suspends cooperation without deleting physical assets
- embargo reduces corridor value without erasing domestic infrastructure
- famine and migration receive route facts through adapters
- border procedures matter at commissioning
- a shared authority exists only when project scale justifies it
- fixed crossings come from a validated registry
- every offered crossing provides the promised strategic connection
- annexation, occupation, release, and government change cannot duplicate benefits or orphan project records
