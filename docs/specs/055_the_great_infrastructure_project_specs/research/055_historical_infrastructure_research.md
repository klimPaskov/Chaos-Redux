# Historical Infrastructure Research for Event 55

## Research purpose

The research was used to sharpen project stages, route failure, multinational governance, construction risk, and visual direction.

Event 55 remains an alternate-history Chaos Redux system. It does not reproduce any one historical project and it does not present colonial or authoritarian public works as neutral models.

## Transcontinental railways

### First United States transcontinental railroad

The Golden Spike site preserves the joining point and original grade of the first transcontinental railroad. The project is useful as a reference for two distant construction efforts meeting through one national trunk.

Design use:

- coast-to-coast project variant
- route sections built from different directions
- commissioning only after the final connection
- strong national integration value
- visual focus on grade, fill, locomotives, workers, and joining rails

Source:

- U.S. National Park Service, Golden Spike National Historical Park
- https://www.nps.gov/gosp/index.htm

### Cape to Cairo railway and Victoria Falls Bridge

The Cape to Cairo plan was never completed. War interrupted work and a central missing section remained. The Victoria Falls Bridge became a major completed component inside the wider unfinished scheme. The project was also tied to extraction and imperial control, which makes it a useful warning against treating every grand corridor as a neutral public good.

Design use:

- reduced completion and unfinished continental routes
- war interruption
- one completed bridge remaining useful after the larger scheme fails
- resource extraction corridor motives
- imported materials and difficult construction logistics
- explicit political interests behind route selection

Source:

- Institution of Civil Engineers, Cape to Cairo railway and Victoria Falls Bridge
- https://www.ice.org.uk/what-is-civil-engineering/infrastructure-projects/cape-to-cairo-railway-and-the-victoria-falls-bridge

### Trans-Siberian Railway

The Trans-Siberian Railway supports the broad design of a national trunk across a very wide country, with several major regions and strategic endpoints.

Design use:

- very long domestic railway candidates
- severe climate and distance factors
- industrial and military integration
- construction in segments
- route experience improving later extensions

Source direction:

- Library of Congress search and collection material on the Trans-Siberian Railway
- https://www.loc.gov/search/?q=Trans-Siberian+Railway

## Fixed links

### Channel Tunnel

The Channel Tunnel demonstrates that a fixed link can require a very long proposal history, bilateral political support, dedicated terminals, parallel service infrastructure, detailed geology, safety systems, and large financial risk. It also shows that the shortest distance between shores does not define the full route because inland terminals matter.

Design use:

- curated crossing registry
- bilateral partner status
- survey and geology stage
- service and safety commissioning
- terminal states as critical nodes
- tunnel, bridge, and alternative design choice
- long duration and financing failure risk
- damage and closure affecting one strategic connection

Sources:

- Institution of Civil Engineers, The Channel Tunnel
- https://www.ice.org.uk/what-is-civil-engineering/infrastructure-projects/the-channel-tunnel
- Getlink, Our History
- https://www.getlinkgroup.com/en/our-group/history/

### Øresund fixed link

The Øresund link developed through long Danish and Swedish negotiation and became a combined bridge and tunnel system. Environmental and transport concerns affected the design.

Design use:

- hybrid bridge and tunnel candidate
- two core partners
- shipping and approach constraints
- partner agreement before construction
- one project object with several physical components
- environmental or local route redesign incident

Source:

- Øresundsbron, History
- https://www.oresundsbron.com/en/about-oresundsbron/about-us/history

## Public works institutions

### United States Public Works Administration

The Public Works Administration maintained project applications, status reports, priorities, legal material, construction progress, investigations, equipment and materials records, and regional administration.

Design use:

- one national works institution
- proposal, survey, authorization, status, and investigation stages
- contractor corruption and reporting incidents
- regional project administration
- a capacity score that summarizes many hidden administrative inputs
- stable project records rather than transient one-click bonuses

Source:

- U.S. National Archives, Records of the Public Works Administration
- https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/135.html

### Autobahn construction

The Autobahn program is useful as a period reference for a nationwide road campaign that mixed transport, employment, military, political, and propaganda goals.

Design use:

- strategic highway family
- accelerated schedule and prestige method tensions
- public works rhetoric
- military priority reducing civilian benefit
- visual direction based on active road construction rather than a map or office

The specification does not treat authoritarian labor policy or propaganda claims as neutral benefits.

Source:

- German History in Documents and Images, Autobahn Construction, 1936
- https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/nazi-germany-1933-1945/autobahn-construction-munich-salzburg-1936

## Staged construction and procurement

### Hoover Dam

The Hoover Dam chronology shows the value of staged contracts, site preparation, diversion work, material supply, construction milestones, and later operation.

Design use:

- survey, procurement, main construction, and commissioning stages
- visible milestone incidents
- material and equipment commitments spread across the project
- partial works that remain after cancellation
- strong facility outcome justified by long construction

Source:

- U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Hoover Dam chronology
- https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/history/articles/chrono.html

### Panama Canal

The Panama Canal history includes long commercial ambition, a failed French effort, disease and finance problems, treaty and sovereignty conflict, and a later United States project.

Design use:

- land and sovereignty negotiation
- failed predecessor works
- major health, geology, and financing risks
- strategic trade route value
- project transfer and disputed control
- long construction that cannot be reduced to one payment

Sources:

- U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian, Building the Panama Canal
- https://history.state.gov/milestones/1899-1913/panama-canal
- Panama Canal Authority, History of the Panama Canal
- https://pancanal.com/en/history-of-the-panama-canal/

## Multinational and resource corridors

### TAZARA Railway

TAZARA joined Tanzania and Zambia through a bilateral authority and external Chinese support. The railway was tied to access, economic independence, training, and a large supporting infrastructure program.

Design use:

- resource corridor to a foreign port
- two core partners and one financier
- national strategic interest in bypassing vulnerable transit
- partner contributions with different roles
- joint operating authority
- training and maintenance as part of long-term operation

Source:

- Tanzania-Zambia Railway Authority, Our History
- https://www.tazarasite.com/our-history

### Cross-border infrastructure barriers

The European Investment Bank identifies scale, difficult terrain, regulatory fragmentation, different national priorities, demand asymmetry, and funding coordination as recurring cross-border infrastructure barriers.

Design use:

- partner counteroffers
- uneven contribution and benefit
- operating agreements separate from physical construction
- funding disputes
- route dormancy after legal cooperation fails
- stronger evidence gate for multinational networks

Source:

- European Investment Bank, Cross-border infrastructure projects
- https://www.eib.org/en/publications/20230107-cross-border-infrastructure-projects

### Regional economic corridors

The OECD describes successful corridors as combinations of physical infrastructure and policy coordination. Border processes, regulation, customs, rail and maritime integration, route resilience, and alternative transit all affect value.

Design use:

- hard infrastructure plus operating agreement
- customs and standards commissioning stage
- multimodal networks
- resilience against one vulnerable route
- corridor benefits that stop when borders or agreements fail
- alternative transit proposal during embargo or geopolitical disruption

Source:

- OECD, Regional Economic Corridors
- https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/policy-issues/regional-economic-corridors.html

## Ports

### Port of Rotterdam

Rotterdam's history supports the design of a port as a growing logistics complex connected to an industrial hinterland and changing trade routes.

Design use:

- grand port family
- port and inland railway connection
- staged expansion
- congestion when an inland corridor grows faster than the terminal
- port network role in Evolution III

Source:

- Port of Rotterdam, History of the Port of Rotterdam
- https://www.portofrotterdam.com/en/about-port-authority/our-organisation/history

## Governance and cost risk

Large infrastructure projects often fail through weak selection, unstable scope, poor procurement, uncertain funding, and weak maintenance. Event 55 therefore treats overruns and operation as living project states.

Design use:

- early survey and authorization gate
- bounded cost overrun risk
- reduced scope and suspension instead of arbitrary deletion
- maintenance obligation
- investigation and contractor replacement
- stronger AI refusal when cost and benefit are badly matched

Supporting source:

- European Investment Bank, Cross-border infrastructure projects
- https://www.eib.org/en/publications/20230107-cross-border-infrastructure-projects

## Research limits

The research gives design parallels. It does not prove that every historical crossing should exist on the HOI4 map.

Map validity, state IDs, dynamic adjacency, naval passage, supply behavior, and building effects require local game inspection during implementation.

No historical route name in this file is final player localisation for a dynamic project. Country and route names should be generated from the actual campaign geography.
