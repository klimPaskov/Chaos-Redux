# Event 056: Fleet Package Catalog

## Package construction model

Every recipient receives one primary fleet identity. The identity determines the package's defining ships, secondary support, likely operational use, and the kind of burden the recipient must solve.

The package catalog is a design framework rather than a fixed order of battle. Final hull counts must adapt to the active technology system, available ship definitions, current date, evolution pool, recipient repeat scale, and global delivery budget.

The result must still read clearly as one of the identities below.

## Hull-equivalent value

Raw ship count is a poor way to compare a carrier force with a destroyer swarm. Package scaling should therefore use an internal hull-equivalent value.

The exact values require implementation inspection and balance testing. The conceptual order is:

1. carriers and the heaviest capital ships
2. battleships and battlecruisers
3. heavy cruisers and major experimental ships
4. light cruisers and advanced submarines
5. destroyers and ordinary submarines
6. convoys, aircraft, fuel, and port support as auxiliary value

This value supports three goals:

- keep packages of different identities within comparable broad power bands
- limit the total world delivery without erasing fleet variety
- preserve the defining ships when a package must be reduced

The player does not see hull-equivalent value. The personal report uses plain scale terms and a compact composition summary.

## Baseline size bands

The following bands are design targets. They are not final hardcoded counts.

| Band | Light-fleet expression | Heavy-fleet expression | Intended role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compact | Roughly 6 to 12 combat hulls | Roughly 4 to 8 combat hulls | Small countries, later repeat receipts, or globally compressed delivery |
| Standard | Roughly 13 to 24 combat hulls | Roughly 7 to 13 combat hulls | Normal first-firing package |
| Heavy | Roughly 25 to 36 combat hulls | Roughly 10 to 18 combat hulls | Strong first-firing result or higher-chaos package |
| Extreme | Roughly 37 to 48 light hulls | Roughly 14 to 20 heavy-oriented hulls | Evolution I and later, subject to strict world limits |

A heavy-oriented package includes fewer ships because each defining hull carries far more value. Carrier aircraft and escorts also consume part of the package budget.

The country should not receive a capital package containing eighteen capital ships merely because the general heavy band allows eighteen total hulls. Composition rules remain identity-specific.

## Baseline package pool

The baseline pool should produce broad variety across the world. Suggested relative starting weights are shown below for later probability inspection. They express variety, not exact final chances.

| Package identity | Relative starting weight | Availability note |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Balanced Surface Fleet | 16 | Always available when legal surface ships exist |
| Submarine Raiding Fleet | 14 | Always available when legal submarines exist |
| Destroyer Swarm | 12 | Always available when legal destroyers exist |
| Convoy-Heavy Escort Fleet | 12 | Always available when convoys and escorts are supported |
| Cruiser-Focused Fleet | 10 | Requires at least one legal cruiser role |
| Capital-Ship Fleet | 8 | Requires legal capital hulls |
| Carrier-Focused Fleet | 8 | Requires legal carriers and a credible aircraft support route |
| Coastal Defense Fleet | 9 | Always available with light defensive hulls |
| Invasion-Support Fleet | 8 | Requires convoy and bombardment support content |
| Minelaying and Sea-Denial Fleet | 3 | Available only where mine warfare and legal designs exist |

The pool must not remove an identity because it would be inefficient for the recipient. It may remove an identity when the active game content cannot create a legal version of that fleet.

## 1. Balanced Surface Fleet

### Identity

A general-purpose navy centered on screens, cruisers, and a limited heavy element. It can escort, patrol, contest ordinary surface battles, and support modest overseas operations without specializing completely in one role.

### Defining composition

- destroyers form the largest single group
- light cruisers or equivalent screening leaders provide command and anti-air support
- one limited heavy component can include heavy cruisers, battlecruisers, battleships, or a small carrier element
- a small submarine detachment may appear in larger variants

### Composition direction

A standard package should devote roughly half of its combat value to screens, a quarter to cruisers, and the remainder to a limited heavy or specialist element.

The package should not contain equal numbers of every ship type. It needs a visible center of gravity around a conventional surface fleet.

### Support

- moderate fuel reserve
- modest convoy grant
- limited naval aircraft only if the package includes carriers or a strong anti-submarine air role
- no emergency port expansion beyond what commissioning requires

### Strategic use

This is the easiest package to integrate into an existing navy. It can reinforce several missions, but it should not outperform a dedicated package at raiding, escort, invasion support, or carrier warfare.

### Awkward outcomes

A land-focused minor can receive a balanced fleet larger than its existing navy and struggle to fuel it. A submarine-focused naval power may receive a conventional force that demands a new doctrine of use. Those outcomes are valid.

### Evolution variants

Evolution I can produce an oversized traditional fleet with unusually strong screening depth. Evolution II can replace one heavy component with an approved experimental surface asset. Evolution III can add an advanced submarine or carrier group while preserving the balanced structure.

## 2. Submarine Raiding Fleet

### Identity

A force designed to attack shipping, threaten distant supply routes, and create uncertainty across broad sea zones.

### Defining composition

- submarines form roughly three quarters or more of combat hull count
- a small destroyer or light cruiser group can provide local patrol, rescue, or command support
- later variants can include specialized long-range or unusual submarines

### Composition direction

Compact packages can be almost entirely submarines. Standard and heavy packages should add a small surface support group without turning into a balanced fleet.

The package should not include capital ships at baseline.

### Support

- low to moderate convoy grant
- fuel reserve sized for an initial raiding period
- optional naval bombers in a limited quantity where they reinforce detection and port defense
- no carrier aircraft unless a later impossible hybrid explicitly includes a carrier element

### Strategic use

The fleet gives a country immediate convoy-raiding potential. It can force enemies to assign escorts, change trade routes, and protect distant supply lines.

### Awkward outcomes

A country at peace may have no immediate target. A small inland-facing coastal state may receive long-range submarines while lacking suitable forward bases. A country already rich in submarines may get further concentration in the same role.

### Evolution variants

Evolution I allows very heavy submarine packages with minimal surface support. Evolution II can include registered experimental submarines. Evolution III can create a deep-ocean package combining unusual submarines with a small advanced command or aviation element.

## 3. Destroyer Swarm

### Identity

A dense mass of fast screening ships. The force is suited to convoy escort, patrol, anti-submarine warfare, torpedo attacks, mine work where supported, and absorbing screening losses in larger fleets.

### Defining composition

- destroyers make up roughly four fifths or more of combat hull count
- one or two light cruisers can act as command and anti-air leaders in larger packages
- submarines, carriers, and capital ships are absent at baseline

### Composition direction

The fleet should feel numerically large relative to other package types. Its hull-equivalent value stays bounded through the lower value of individual ships.

The package should be divided into usable task forces instead of one enormous stack.

### Support

- useful convoy grant
- moderate fuel reserve
- limited naval bomber support when compatible
- temporary repair support because many light hulls can crowd repair queues

### Strategic use

The recipient can protect trade, screen an existing battle fleet, patrol coasts, or create several small escort groups.

### Awkward outcomes

A country with no convoy exposure may find the package excessive. A capital-heavy naval power may value the screens immediately. A low-fuel country can still struggle with a large active swarm.

### Evolution variants

Evolution I permits the largest light-hull counts in the event. Evolution II can introduce approved advanced destroyer leaders or specialist anti-submarine ships. Evolution III can mix the swarm with a small unusual surface command group while keeping destroyers dominant.

## 4. Convoy-Heavy Escort Fleet

### Identity

A transport and trade-security package built around a large convoy reserve and enough escort ships to protect it.

### Defining composition

- convoys represent a major share of package value
- destroyers form the main combat force
- light cruisers can provide command, anti-air, and patrol support
- submarines and capital ships remain absent or minimal

### Composition direction

The package should grant more convoys than any other identity. Combat hull count can be lower than a destroyer swarm because transport capacity is part of the reward.

The escort force must be large enough that the package is more than a convoy stockpile.

### Support

- the largest baseline convoy grant
- moderate fuel reserve
- optional maritime patrol aircraft
- repair support focused on keeping escorts active

### Strategic use

The fleet helps countries maintain imports, supply overseas armies, perform lend-lease, support amphibious movement, and resist submarine pressure.

### Awkward outcomes

A continental country with no overseas commitments may receive more transport capacity than it needs. A naval empire under heavy raiding may receive one of the strongest practical packages in the pool.

### Evolution variants

Evolution I can create an enormous merchant reserve with unusually deep escorts. Evolution II can add approved specialist escort or detection assets. Evolution III can produce a protected logistics armada with advanced escorts and a limited carrier or aviation shield.

## 5. Cruiser-Focused Fleet

### Identity

A fleet centered on light and heavy cruisers, with destroyers providing the necessary screen. It offers more surface power than a destroyer package while remaining faster and more flexible than a capital fleet.

### Defining composition

- cruisers form the largest share of combat value
- destroyers provide screens
- heavy cruisers can lead the force where legal
- capital ships and carriers are normally absent at baseline

### Composition direction

A standard package can use a roughly even split between cruiser hulls and destroyer screens by count, while cruisers dominate value.

Light-cruiser variants emphasize screening, anti-air, patrol, and rapid action. Heavy-cruiser variants emphasize surface combat and shore support.

### Support

- moderate fuel reserve
- modest convoy grant
- limited naval bomber support where compatible
- repair support suitable for medium hulls

### Strategic use

The package can reinforce surface fleets, hunt raiders, support invasions, and patrol broad regions without the full burden of battleships or carriers.

### Awkward outcomes

A minor country can receive several expensive medium hulls that exceed local repair capacity. A large naval power may use the force as a ready-made cruiser squadron.

### Evolution variants

Evolution I can create an unusually cruiser-dense navy. Evolution II can add approved large cruisers, missile-related naval assets, or other experimental surface ships through their owning systems. Evolution III can combine advanced cruisers with submarines or a small carrier group while cruisers remain the operational core.

## 6. Capital-Ship Fleet

### Identity

A concentrated force built around a few major surface combatants and the escorts needed to protect them.

### Defining composition

- battleships, battlecruisers, or equivalent heavy ships define the package
- destroyers and light cruisers provide screening
- a small cruiser group can support the line
- carriers are absent at baseline unless one legal hybrid branch is deliberately allowed

### Composition direction

A compact package can contain one or two defining capital ships with a credible escort group. Standard and heavy packages can increase the capital core, but the force should remain bounded.

The package must never create a large pile of unscreened battleships.

### Support

- high initial fuel reserve
- limited convoy grant
- temporary repair support
- no carrier aircraft unless the package includes a carrier in a legal later variant

### Strategic use

The fleet gives immediate surface deterrence, shore-bombardment potential, and the ability to challenge smaller navies.

### Awkward outcomes

A small port network can become congested. A fuel-poor country can own powerful ships that it cannot keep at sea. A submarine doctrine country may have no natural role for a battle line.

### Evolution variants

Evolution I can create a package centered on several major ships rather than one or two. Evolution II can include a registered experimental capital ship. Evolution III can form a rare heavy command fleet with advanced submarines, unusual escorts, or a limited carrier component.

## 7. Carrier-Focused Fleet

### Identity

A naval aviation force built around one or more carriers, sufficient screening, and an initial air group.

### Defining composition

- carriers define the package
- destroyers and light cruisers provide a full screen
- one limited heavy surface escort can appear in larger variants
- carrier aircraft are included whenever compatible content exists

### Composition direction

A compact package normally contains one carrier with escorts. A standard package can contain one or two. Heavy and evolved packages can contain several carriers, but the global budget and local port capacity remain strict.

The package must never deliver a carrier without enough escorts to form a usable task force.

### Support

- the largest aircraft support grant
- high initial fuel reserve
- moderate convoy grant
- temporary repair and organization support
- nearby airbase placement for overflow aircraft

### Strategic use

The recipient gains naval air reach, mobile strike power, and improved control of sea regions outside land-based air coverage.

### Awkward outcomes

A country without existing carrier doctrine, aircraft production, or fuel can receive a powerful but demanding fleet. A country with weak airbases may need phased commissioning. A major carrier power may receive more of an already dominant capability.

### Compatibility rule

A carrier package stays in the pool only when a legal carrier and a credible aircraft support path exist. If the active content cannot supply carrier aircraft, the package can be temporarily removed from that recipient's legal pool without replacing it with a strategically tailored result.

### Evolution variants

Evolution I allows larger carrier concentrations. Evolution II can include approved experimental carriers, aircraft, or aviation-support ships. Evolution III can produce a rare carrier-heavy armada with advanced submarines or capital escorts while retaining a complete screen.

## 8. Coastal Defense Fleet

### Identity

A compact force intended to protect nearby waters, patrol approaches, contest small incursions, and make amphibious attack more difficult.

### Defining composition

- destroyers, submarines, and light cruisers form the fleet
- short-range or inexpensive designs are acceptable where the active system supports them
- capital ships and large carriers are absent at baseline

### Composition direction

The package favors several small task forces rather than one long-range fleet. It should be cheaper to operate than capital or carrier packages.

A compact coastal package remains useful for a small country without overwhelming its port network.

### Support

- low to moderate fuel reserve
- modest convoy grant
- optional naval bombers for local defense
- limited mine support where available

### Strategic use

The force protects home waters, supports coastal supply, detects enemy movement, and gives minor states a credible local navy.

### Awkward outcomes

A global empire can receive a fleet with limited reach. A country with no nearby naval enemy may find the package defensive and narrow.

### Evolution variants

Evolution I can create a dense littoral defense network. Evolution II can add experimental coastal submarines, fast attack ships, or detection assets where registered. Evolution III can create an unusual fortified fleet with advanced small craft and one rare command ship.

## 9. Invasion-Support Fleet

### Identity

A force designed to carry troops, escort transports, provide shore bombardment, and hold a temporary sea route during amphibious operations.

### Defining composition

- convoys or transport capacity form a major support component
- destroyers provide escort
- cruisers or limited capital ships provide bombardment
- one small carrier or naval bomber element can appear when legal

### Composition direction

The package should contain a clear transport and escort relationship. It must not be a surface fleet with a token convoy grant.

The force should support an invasion opportunity without granting marine divisions, war goals, or automatic invasion orders.

### Support

- large convoy grant
- moderate to high fuel reserve
- optional carrier or naval bomber support
- temporary escort readiness

### Strategic use

The package enables overseas operations, emergency evacuation, reinforcement of islands, and amphibious attack where the country has troops and strategic reason to act.

### Awkward outcomes

A country without marines or an overseas objective may receive a capability it cannot exploit immediately. A country fighting across islands may receive an exceptionally useful force.

### Evolution variants

Evolution I can create a large expeditionary armada. Evolution II can add approved landing-support or experimental bombardment ships. Evolution III can combine heavy transport, carriers, advanced escorts, and rare support ships into a major assault package.

## 10. Minelaying and Sea-Denial Fleet

### Identity

A specialized force intended to shape local sea access through mines, patrol, ambush, and detection.

### Availability

This package exists only when the active game content supports mine warfare and legal ships can be created with the required role. It is removed cleanly when those systems are unavailable.

### Defining composition

- minelaying destroyers, submarines, or light cruisers form the core
- patrol escorts and detection ships support the force
- capital ships and carriers are absent at baseline

### Composition direction

The package can be smaller than other light fleets because its value comes from area denial. It must contain enough ordinary patrol and escort strength to remain useful when mines cannot be deployed immediately.

### Support

- moderate fuel reserve
- modest convoy grant
- optional patrol aircraft
- no separate mine stockpile unless the active naval system uses one

### Strategic use

The recipient can protect approaches, obstruct stronger navies, support coastal defense, and create risky routes for enemy shipping.

### Awkward outcomes

A country with no nearby contested waters may receive a narrow specialist force. A country defending straits or islands may receive a highly effective package.

### Evolution variants

Evolution I can create a broad denial fleet with more specialist hulls. Evolution II can include registered experimental mine or detection ships. Evolution III can combine sea denial with advanced submarines and unusual patrol assets.

## Package subvariants

Each identity can support a limited number of subvariants. Subvariants add replay value without creating a separate identity for every small composition change.

Examples include:

- anti-air emphasis
- anti-submarine emphasis
- long-range emphasis
- fast but lightly protected emphasis
- older heavy hulls with deeper escort numbers
- fewer modern hulls with stronger auxiliary support
- mixed domestic and foreign design traditions

Subvariants must not be chosen to fit the recipient. They are part of the same random roll.

## Design age and technology envelope

The event does not grant technologies. It uses ship and aircraft content that can be delivered legally under the active technology and compatibility framework.

The package pool can consider:

- current date
- recipient research, including technologies gained from Event 54
- globally registered safe grant content
- content availability from active DLC
- evolution-owned experimental permissions

The event should prefer a legal version of the rolled identity over cancelling the whole recipient. For example, if a modern cruiser variant is unavailable, the package can use an older legal cruiser that serves the same role.

This compatibility route cannot change a carrier package into submarines because the country lacks carrier doctrine. It can only find a legal carrier expression or remove that identity from the legal pool.

## Design quality

Granted ships should arrive as functional designs, not malformed empty hulls. They need legal modules, role coherence, names or numbering through a supported naming method, and task-force placement that the player can understand.

The event should reuse safe existing designs or generate from a controlled registry. It should not create arbitrary module combinations for every country.

When a country already has a suitable legal design, the package may use it. When it does not, the event can use an event-owned generic legal design for the active technology band. The generic design should still match the fleet identity.

## Package report summary

The personal report should present:

- fleet identity
- scale band
- defining ships
- approximate total combat hull count
- convoy and aircraft support
- commissioning ports
- immediate operational burden
- any delayed tranche
- any unsupported carrier or experimental element that affects the choice

It should not list every ship name or expose the hull-equivalent budget.

## Identity preservation under global compression

When the total world grant must be reduced, apply cuts in this order:

1. reduce surplus auxiliary support above the package minimum
2. reduce secondary combat hulls
3. reduce duplicate escorts while retaining safe screening
4. move part of the package into a delayed tranche where this remains useful
5. lower the local size band
6. skip the recipient only if the defining core cannot be delivered legally

The defining core must survive whenever a package is delivered.

Examples:

- a compressed carrier package still contains a carrier, a legal screen, and an air-support route
- a compressed capital package still contains a major capital ship and escorts
- a compressed convoy package still contains a meaningful convoy reserve and escorts
- a compressed submarine package still remains submarine-dominant

## Fleet identity distribution

The world result should not be forced into an even distribution. Independent rolls can create clusters of similar packages. A firing where many countries receive submarines is valid.

A soft duplicate guard can prevent one identity from consuming almost the entire world only when the random pool or content gates leave very few valid choices. This guard protects variety at the world level and must not tailor results by country.

## Rare baseline anomalies

A small chance can produce a strange but still baseline-legal package before the formal evolutions. Examples include:

- a capital fleet with an unusually deep destroyer screen
- a convoy fleet with one obsolete cruiser acting as flagship
- a submarine force with one surface command ship
- a balanced fleet assembled from several design traditions

These anomalies remain within ordinary naval technology. Experimental and impossible assets stay behind their evolutions.
