
# Recovery, Generation Change, and Ten-Year Endgame Events

Working labels in this file are not final localisation.

## Recovery is a new phase of play

Recovery should not remove the scenario's systems.

As the climate and infrastructure improve:

- new land becomes valuable
- old hazards become exposed
- new populations move
- old claims return
- emergency institutions face reform
- military leaders face civilian control
- young people challenge first-generation memory
- mutant and machine relations become formal politics
- regional compacts gain power
- technology creates new risk
- wider wars become possible

The detailed matrix is in `matrices/fallout_recovery_late_event_matrix.md`.

## Climate recovery

Required sequence:

1. soot thinning
2. unstable bright periods
3. false spring
4. thaw and flood
5. ultraviolet risk
6. first reliable seasons
7. regional agricultural divergence
8. new climate normal

The exact timing depends on cause and severity.

Visible climate recovery must synchronize with events.

## Agriculture

Recovery agriculture needs:

- cold-tolerant crops
- seed reserve
- greenhouse debt
- labor
- water
- soil and contamination
- livestock
- transport
- regional trade
- blight
- late frost

A successful food state can become a regional power. A poor distribution system can still cause famine.

## City and state recovery

Reclamation projects should change the map.

Possible results:

- state category restoration
- infrastructure
- rail
- supply
- housing
- sanitation
- factories
- ports
- power
- schools
- hospitals
- capital movement
- population return
- memorial districts
- permanent forbidden zones

Wasteland restoration remains rare.

## The second generation

The post-Fallout generation should enter play by Years 5 to 10.

Required conflicts:

- old flag versus new identity
- emergency law
- travel
- education
- military service
- mixed citizenship
- mutant relations
- machine relations
- property
- family records
- public memory
- leadership succession
- rebuilt cities
- regional order

Second-generation characters can become leaders, advisors, officers, scientists, teachers, organizers, and rebels.

## Founder succession

Every long-lived country needs a leadership succession path.

Possible succession systems:

- election
- appointed successor
- hereditary claim
- council vote
- military council
- technical board
- religious synod
- compact selection
- machine protocol
- local federation

A government that survives the founder's death should feel more mature. A failed succession can create fragmentation or civil conflict.

## Constitutional settlement

Emergency law should have an expiry or transformation point.

Constitutional events should use:

- government archetype
- first-year choices
- refugee policy
- military power
- labor institutions
- religion
- technical authority
- compact obligations
- mutant and machine citizenship
- regional autonomy
- old-world memory

The outcome changes focus and decision access.

## Interregional systems

Late world-order content requires physical and communication links.

Examples:

- passenger rail
- passenger ships
- radio
- undersea cable
- airfields
- polar relay
- shared standards
- climate data
- seed network
- medical network

A country cannot join a congress it cannot contact.

## Late world-order models

Possible models:

- loose congress
- compact federation
- regional blocs
- trade standards network
- climate and rescue organization
- health inspection order
- food reserve network
- mutant recognition league
- machine protocol network
- military directorate
- rival world orders
- no permanent order

No model should become automatic.

## Late war

Years 5 to 10 can support larger wars.

Late war causes:

- restored old borders
- regional hegemony
- food reserve
- power grid
- strategic city
- mutant recognition
- machine network
- disarmament
- restored arsenal
- compact leadership
- ideology
- second-generation nationalism

Late wars still use survival, population, buildings, climate, and settlement systems.

## Ten-year anniversary

The ten-year event should summarize:

- cause memory
- original country
- successor identity
- first leader
- current leader
- founding crisis
- greatest recovery
- greatest loss
- first treaty
- first war
- refugee policy
- altered-polity policy
- climate state
- main compact
- late ambition

It should not end the campaign.

## Open continuation

After Year 10:

- seasonal events continue
- succession continues
- compact politics continue
- late formables remain available
- wasteland recovery continues
- new wars and settlements remain possible
- climate fluctuations continue at lower intensity
- memory anniversaries continue
- country-specific late chains can continue

## Acceptance

- Recovery is visible.
- Recovery causes new crises.
- State categories can improve under hard conditions.
- Second-generation characters and politics exist.
- Founders can die or retire.
- Emergency law changes.
- Interregional order requires routes.
- Late wars have causes and settlements.
- Ten-year event recognizes player history.
- Campaign continues after Year 10.
