
# Character, Leader, and Society Arc System

Working labels in this file are not final localisation.

## Purpose

A ten-year scenario needs people who persist across events. Recurring characters let the player remember the ration crisis, the first expedition, the refugee train, the grid repair, the first contact, and the first war as connected history.

The full role matrix is in `matrices/fallout_character_arc_matrix.md`.

The Ash-week pilot candidate registry is in `matrices/fallout_orientation_pilot_memory_candidate_registry.md`. Its twenty-four fictional character directions and twelve institution directions require dedicated Fallout assets, exact install effects, final localisation, and current-generation validators before use.

## Character state

A recurring character can track:

- competence
- loyalty
- health
- injury
- radiation or toxic exposure
- influence
- ideology or institutional alignment
- public trust
- military following
- family or community tie
- foreign contact
- grievance
- office
- alive, missing, exiled, retired, imprisoned, or dead state

Only track values that affect later play.

## Character budget

A small country should normally have three to six active recurring characters.

A large country may have six to twelve.

The player should not receive a new named person in every event. New characters are introduced when they represent:

- an institution
- a major dispute
- a new generation
- a military force
- an external relationship
- a technical capacity
- a social group
- a route transformation

## Character introduction

An introduction event should establish:

- role
- competence or useful capacity
- constituency
- immediate problem
- personal or institutional risk
- connection to a mechanic
- first choice

The event should not provide a biography unrelated to play.

## Character outcomes

Characters can:

- become advisors
- become country leaders
- become commanders
- found factions
- found local governments
- become foreign envoys
- be exiled
- defect
- die
- be injured
- disappear
- become institutional offices and stop depending on one person
- be replaced by an apprentice or child
- become a late-game historical symbol

Death is not the only serious outcome.

## Institutional succession

A major institution should survive one person's death where the route supports it.

Examples:

- rail foreman becomes rail guild office
- doctor becomes public-health board
- quartermaster becomes ration ministry
- radio operator becomes broadcast service
- scavenger captain becomes expedition bureau
- weather observer becomes climate office
- water keeper becomes water court
- navigator becomes route academy

This gives the scenario continuity across a decade.

## Leadership change

Leadership events must connect to:

- actual character availability
- government archetype
- military loyalty
- constitution
- public trust
- survival performance
- focus route
- previous event memory

A country should not swap leaders from random flavor alone.

## Gender and portrait rule

For fictional one-person characters:

- use a small regional name pool
- assign an actual personal name
- match apparent gender presentation with the correct name pool
- use female metadata where supported for female-presenting leaders
- never assign opposite-gender names or metadata
- record source mode and gender presentation in the asset manifest

For councils, committees, juntas, machine systems, crowds, and collective bodies:

- use an institutional name
- use an institutional portrait
- do not assign a random personal name

Real people require sourced portraits and historical research.

## Family and generation

The event layer should remember:

- orphan care
- family separation
- adoption
- first safe birth
- shelter children
- refugee children
- mixed-settlement children
- station children
- military families
- missing relatives
- returning relatives
- marriage and inheritance law

Children introduced in Years 0 to 2 can return as young adults in Years 5 to 10.

The second generation should have different priorities:

- less attachment to old borders
- stronger attachment to survivor institutions
- frustration with permanent emergency law
- interest in travel and reopened land
- different language and identity
- different view of mutant and machine neighbors
- demand for education, representation, and property

## Society arcs

Required society subjects:

- ration citizenship
- shelter class
- refugee integration
- mixed settlement
- labor duty
- veteran status
- property
- marriage
- adoption
- education
- burial
- calendar
- language
- public memory
- sport and leisure
- crime and market law
- gender and family roles
- disability and injury
- old-age care
- technical apprenticeship
- local elections
- religious pluralism
- contact with altered or machine societies

Each society arc must change laws, ideas, decisions, characters, cohesion, population, or diplomacy.

## Character and focus integration

A focus branch may:

- empower a character
- require a character
- replace a character with an institution
- create a rivalry
- resolve an injury or scandal
- open an apprentice or successor
- change a character's office
- remove a character after failure
- trigger a country leader change

The event layer should not become optional decoration beside the focus tree.

## Character and decision integration

Decisions may:

- fund a character's project
- appoint or remove a character
- assign a mission
- protect or expose a character
- negotiate with a faction
- rescue a missing character
- train an apprentice
- manage a character's constituency
- respond to corruption or abuse

AI needs equivalent choice logic.

## Relationship characters

Some characters belong to two countries or a regional system.

Examples:

- trade envoy
- refugee organizer
- river commissioner
- medical inspector
- radio liaison
- compact secretary
- convoy captain
- border mediator
- mixed-settlement representative
- climate scientist

Their status should affect bilateral memory.

## Failure and tragedy

Character failure should have a source and consequence.

Examples:

- poor judgment causes building damage
- corruption reduces food
- unsafe reactor order raises radiation
- military refusal loses corridor
- cover-up damages legitimacy
- coercive medicine creates atrocity memory
- failed expedition loses specialist
- faction rivalry starts civil conflict

Do not kill characters randomly to create weight.

## Positive arcs

Required positive outcomes include:

- apprentice succeeds mentor
- doctor and engineer build a durable institution
- refugee organizer joins government
- former militia becomes rescue service
- altered envoy wins recognition
- teacher's students enter office
- weather observer prevents false-spring disaster
- navigator opens interregional route
- local mayor becomes federal mediator
- quartermaster creates transparent ration law

## Acceptance

- Recurring characters persist across several events.
- Characters affect mechanics.
- Every selected country has at least one recurring character or institutional body.
- Major countries have several.
- Character outcomes can change leaders, advisors, commanders, or factions.
- Names and portraits follow regional and gender rules.
- Councils use institutional identity.
- Second-generation characters appear.
- AI can resolve character arcs.
- Character memory survives archetype change where relevant.
