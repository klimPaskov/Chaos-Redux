
# Diplomacy, Trade, War, and World-Order Events

Working labels in this file are not final localisation.

## Diplomatic recovery stages

Diplomacy rebuilds in stages.

### Signal contact

Countries know that another organized group exists.

Available actions:

- identity exchange
- distress response
- weather and health data
- proof of authority
- silence
- misinformation

### Practical contact

Countries can move people or goods.

Available actions:

- aid
- trade
- inspections
- envoy meeting
- route security
- refugee coordination
- joint expedition

### Recognition

Governments decide whether the other polity is legitimate.

Recognition affects:

- diplomacy
- country legitimacy
- compact entry
- claims
- trade
- leaders
- mutant and machine relations
- old-world restoration routes

### Compact order

Three or more countries form an institution with goals, obligations, leadership, cohesion, and failure.

### Interregional order

Long-range routes allow blocs, congresses, standards, and wider wars.

## Contact range

Contact should depend on:

- adjacency
- radio network
- road or rail corridor
- river
- port and convoy route
- airfield
- polar relay
- restored cable
- allied contact
- shared compact

A country should not instantly know every successor on reveal day.

## Bilateral memory

Every important relationship stores:

- first contact
- recognition status
- aid given and received
- trade debt
- refugee treatment
- shared mission outcome
- border grievance
- inspection trust
- sabotage accusation
- war conduct
- settlement
- compact history
- altered-polity policy
- machine-contact policy

This memory changes future events and AI.

## Trade

Trade events should move actual survival resources or strategic capacity.

Possible exchange objects:

- food
- water
- medicine
- fuel
- power
- seed
- salvage
- trains
- convoys
- technical staff
- climate data
- port access
- rail access
- shelter space
- military protection

Long contracts create dependency and political constituencies.

## Compacts

A compact requires:

- at least three eligible members
- a reason
- a charter direction
- leadership method
- cohesion value
- shared decisions
- obligations
- refusal logic
- member failure response
- expulsion or suspension logic
- victory or mature-goal path
- breakup path

Compact types:

- relief
- health
- food
- water
- grid
- trade
- defense
- rescue
- climate monitoring
- mutant recognition
- machine standards
- regional federation

## Compact events

Required event families:

- founding congress
- member entry
- observer status
- leadership vote
- shared mission
- failed obligation
- secret clause
- member dispute
- external pressure
- expulsion
- reform
- breakup
- mature transformation

A compact should not be a faction name with no internal play.

## War causes

Post-Fallout wars should have material and political causes.

Required causes include:

- water diversion
- granary seizure
- port blockade
- rail sabotage
- refugee violence
- tribute revolt
- reactor security
- dead-city salvage
- old-border claim
- quarantine shooting
- mutant exclusion
- machine protocol expansion
- compact intervention
- succession conflict
- local-defection crisis

A war can still begin through focus or decision. The event layer should establish the grievance and reactions.

## War conduct events

War changes:

- population
- buildings
- survival resources
- refugee flows
- military institutions
- character loyalty
- occupation
- compact cohesion
- winter exposure
- old-world memory

Required wartime event families:

- winter offensive
- civilian corridor
- depot capture
- local guard defection
- prisoner shelter
- scorched resource
- commander refusal
- foreign volunteers
- compact intervention
- city evacuation
- contamination release
- mutiny
- ceasefire offer

## Settlements

Every significant war requires postwar handling.

Settlement subjects:

- borders
- water
- food
- ports
- rails
- power
- reactors
- refugees
- prisoners
- missing people
- local government
- resistance
- occupation
- reparations
- reconstruction
- memorial
- recognition
- compact membership

Annexation alone is not enough.

## Occupation and integration

Events should decide:

- who runs ration systems
- whether local councils remain
- how food and medicine are distributed
- whether units are integrated
- whether refugees can return
- whether old claims become cores
- how resistance changes
- how buildings are repaired
- how atrocities are investigated
- which country-memory chain survives

Integration should be staged for large or hostile regions.

## Mutant and machine diplomacy

Fictional altered and machine polities require specific diplomatic events.

Possible policies:

- recognition
- health inspection
- limited trade
- observer status
- equal compact membership
- exclusion
- containment
- forced assimilation
- war

These policies should affect domestic politics and international reputation.

## World-order events

Years 5 to 10 can introduce:

- regional congresses
- compact federations
- trade standards
- currencies
- communication standards
- climate-data networks
- shared rescue law
- mutant recognition charter
- machine interface protocol
- disarmament
- new weapons race
- continental food reserve
- city restoration consortium
- transregional passenger route

A single event cannot restore the old world.

## AI

AI diplomacy should consider:

- resource need and surplus
- route access
- recognition
- government archetype
- compact obligations
- old claims
- military balance
- winter and climate
- refugee pressure
- character influence
- previous betrayal
- cause memory
- mutant and machine policy

AI must understand when a useful trade is worth dependency and when a war threatens survival.

## Acceptance

- Contact expands through routes.
- Recognition matters.
- Trade moves actual resources.
- Compacts have internal events and cohesion.
- Wars have causes.
- War conduct affects people and institutions.
- Settlements handle resources, borders, and populations.
- Both sides store memory.
- AI uses the same systems.
- Mutant and machine diplomacy has distinct content.
- World-order events emerge gradually.
