
# Diplomacy, Trade, War, and World-Order Events

Working labels in this file are not final localisation.

## Diplomatic recovery stages

Diplomacy rebuilds in stages.

### Signal contact

Countries know that another organized group exists.

Available actions:

- identity exchange
- distress response
- weather and health data
- proof of authority
- silence
- misinformation

### Practical contact

Countries can move people or goods.

Available actions:

- aid
- trade
- inspections
- envoy meeting
- route security
- refugee coordination
- joint expedition

### Recognition

Governments decide whether the other polity is legitimate.

Recognition affects:

- diplomacy
- country legitimacy
- compact entry
- claims
- trade
- leaders
- mutant and machine relations
- old-world restoration routes

### Compact order

Three or more countries form an institution with goals, obligations, leadership, cohesion, and failure.

### Interregional order

Long-range routes allow blocs, congresses, standards, and wider wars.

## Contact range

Contact should depend on:

- adjacency
- radio network
- road or rail corridor
- river
- port and convoy route
- airfield
- polar relay
- restored cable
- allied contact
- shared compact

A country should not instantly know every successor on reveal day.

## Relief after the blackout

The pre-collapse treaty stops operating under the permanent Fallout boundary; surviving history gives governments standing to rebuild contact rather than automatically restoring the former membership network.
At the first loaded account, a banned government takes the accepted Recognition loss instead of the former-member Recognition/Cohesion grant; seed-exchange Food memory remains a separate recorded benefit.
The [inherited-choice contract](baseline/04_survival_mechanics.md#inherited-winter-choices) owns these starting changes and their apply-once generation rule.

The accepted reconstruction category is available to current survivors that were treaty members and were never banned, including bespoke USA/NZL survivors.
A proposer pays the accepted 35 political power and deposits 5 Recognition to approach one eligible current counterpart; the counterpart's choice is consent or refusal, not a restoration imposed by old membership.
After ninety days, a successful accepted compact grants the existing asymmetric Recognition benefits, Cohesion and reconstruction/opinion consequences from A19.
F5 binds each government to one exact proposal as sender or recipient, carries the actual proposer into the reply, and authenticates the current generation and counterpart before settlement.
Refusal or counterpart loss returns the surviving sender's Recognition deposit once under that contract; political effort is not described as a refundable second deposit.
Refusal blocks the same pair for the existing year, while loss/annexation cleanup closes only the lost pair and stale replies receive the owned closing notice.

This gives the player a reason to preserve trustworthy relief relations before collapse and a choice of which surviving partner to approach afterward.
No other compact, trade or country-memory event may duplicate its deposit refund, restore pre-collapse access, grant a second reconstruction reward or force another survivor's acceptance.
Where the treaty never existed or valid history/counterpart is absent, ordinary signal, practical-contact and recognition play remain available under authored gates.
AI proposer/recipient behavior stays with the existing reconstruction owner, reading affordability, current survival needs, counterpart standing and active commitments; there is no new owner-side weight from this connection.
Its required future auditor cases distinguish a solvent trusted pair, an unaffordable sender, a banned former member, a busy recipient, refusal, lost counterpart and a stale answer after a new-generation reservation.
Human and hidden AI replies must use the same exact pair, actual payment and apply-once settlement.

Acceptance basis and exact source limits remain in A10/A19 of the [October 4 ledger](../../../plans/air_cleanliness_fallout_plans/agent_decisions_2026-10-04.md) and F5/F6/F15 of the [finishing ledger](../../../plans/air_cleanliness_fallout_plans/agent_decisions_2026-10-05.md); this passage does not replace those immutable decision receipts.

## Bilateral memory

Every important relationship stores:

- first contact
- recognition status
- aid given and received
- trade debt
- refugee treatment
- shared mission outcome
- border grievance
- inspection trust
- sabotage accusation
- war conduct
- settlement
- compact history
- altered-polity policy
- machine-contact policy

This memory changes future events and AI.

## Trade

Trade events should move actual survival resources or strategic capacity.

Possible exchange objects:

- food
- water
- medicine
- fuel
- power
- seed
- salvage
- trains
- convoys
- technical staff
- climate data
- port access
- rail access
- shelter space
- military protection

Long contracts create dependency and political constituencies.

## Compacts

A compact requires:

- at least three eligible members
- a reason
- a charter direction
- leadership method
- cohesion value
- shared decisions
- obligations
- refusal logic
- member failure response
- expulsion or suspension logic
- victory or mature-goal path
- breakup path

Compact types:

- relief
- health
- food
- water
- grid
- trade
- defense
- rescue
- climate monitoring
- mutant recognition
- machine standards
- regional federation

## Compact events

Required event families:

- founding congress
- member entry
- observer status
- leadership vote
- shared mission
- failed obligation
- secret clause
- member dispute
- external pressure
- expulsion
- reform
- breakup
- mature transformation

A compact should not be a faction name with no internal play.

## War causes

Post-Fallout wars should have material and political causes.

Required causes include:

- water diversion
- granary seizure
- port blockade
- rail sabotage
- refugee violence
- tribute revolt
- reactor security
- dead-city salvage
- old-border claim
- quarantine shooting
- mutant exclusion
- machine protocol expansion
- compact intervention
- succession conflict
- local-defection crisis

A war can still begin through focus or decision. The event layer should establish the grievance and reactions.

## Settlements

Every significant war requires postwar handling.

Settlement subjects:

- borders
- water
- food
- ports
- rails
- power
- reactors
- refugees
- prisoners
- missing people
- local government
- resistance
- occupation
- reparations
- reconstruction
- memorial
- recognition

Annexation alone is not enough.

## Occupation and integration

Events should decide:

- who runs ration systems
- whether local councils remain
- how food and medicine are distributed
- whether units are integrated
- whether refugees can return
- whether old claims become cores
- how resistance changes
- how buildings are repaired
- how atrocities are investigated
- which country-memory chain survives

Integration should be staged for large or hostile regions.

## Mutant and machine diplomacy

Fictional altered and machine polities require specific diplomatic events.

Possible policies:

- recognition
- health inspection
- limited trade
- observer status
- equal compact membership
- exclusion
- containment
- forced assimilation
- war

These policies should affect domestic politics and international reputation.

## World-order events

Years 5 to 10 can introduce:

- regional congresses
- compact federations
- trade standards
- currencies
- communication standards
- climate-data networks
- shared rescue law
- mutant recognition charter
- machine interface protocol
- disarmament
- new weapons race
- continental food reserve
- city restoration consortium
- transregional passenger route

A single event cannot restore the old world.

## AI

AI diplomacy should consider:

- resource need and surplus
- route access
- recognition
- government archetype
- compact obligations
- old claims
- military balance
- winter and climate
- refugee pressure
- character influence
- previous betrayal
- cause memory
- mutant and machine policy

AI must understand when a useful trade is worth dependency and when a war threatens survival.

## Acceptance

- Contact expands through routes.
- Recognition matters.
- Trade moves actual resources.
- Compacts have internal events and cohesion.
- Wars have causes.
- War conduct affects people and institutions.
- Settlements handle resources, borders, and populations.
- Both sides store memory.
- AI uses the same systems.
- Mutant and machine diplomacy has distinct content.
- World-order events emerge gradually.
