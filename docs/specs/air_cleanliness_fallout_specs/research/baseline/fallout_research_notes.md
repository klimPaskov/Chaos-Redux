# Research notes for Air Cleanliness and Fallout planning

Canonical status: retained baseline research. The newer living-world research notes add climate, shelter, EMP, and fiction-boundary guidance.

This note lists external research used as design grounding. It is not an implementation source of final localisation.

## Nuclear winter and food systems

Lili Xia and coauthors in Nature Food modelled six soot-injection scenarios after nuclear detonations and estimated mass food shortages above 5 Tg of soot, with more than 2 billion possible deaths in an India-Pakistan scenario and more than 5 billion in a United States-Russia scenario. Design use: Fallout should attack food systems, fisheries, livestock, trade, political order, and areas far beyond blast zones.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0

## Firestorms, soot, and decadal disruption

The same Nature Food paper describes city and industrial firestorms injecting soot into the upper atmosphere, with global cooling and decadal climate disruption. Design use: winter phases should last and should be state and region based.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0

## Southern Hemisphere and island refuges

A 2023 Scientific Reports paper on frost-resistant crop production notes that Southern Hemisphere island nations may experience relatively less severe nuclear winter impacts. Related island refuge research identifies countries such as Australia, New Zealand, Iceland, Solomon Islands, and Vanuatu as more able to continue food production in sun-reducing catastrophes. Design use: southern and remote island states can be refuges, but trade collapse, refugees, piracy, and moral pressure keep them playable.

Sources: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-35354-7 and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36464495/

## Fallout shelter behaviour

CDC radiation emergency guidance says buildings block much harmful radiation, the best shelter is the basement or middle of a building, radioactive material weakens over time, and staying inside for at least 24 hours can protect families until it is safer to leave. Removing outer clothing can remove up to 90 percent of radioactive material. Design use: shelters, basements, decontamination, and first-day survival should matter.

Source: https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/safety/index.html

## EMP and infrastructure

Radiation Emergency Medical Management notes that EMP can damage unprotected electronic equipment, significantly affect communications infrastructure, and complicate communications, hospitals, gas stations, water systems, and electrical components. It also notes EMP poses no direct health threat. Design use: the blackout aftermath should damage communications and power, but should not treat EMP as a magic death pulse.

Source: https://remm.hhs.gov/EMP.htm

## Radiation genetics and mutant fiction boundary

The National Academies BEIR VII chapter states that ionizing radiation can induce genetic changes in experimental systems, but also notes no evidence for radiation-induced germ cell mutations that cause genetic disease in humans. Design use: mutant countries are explicitly Chaos Redux fiction. Their causes can use high-chaos contamination, biological or chemical interactions, secret projects, and world-end strangeness. Do not describe them as realistic radiation biology.

Source: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11340/chapter/6

## Research usage warning

These sources support climate, food, fallout safety, infrastructure, and radiation risk direction. They do not support literal super mutants, organized postwar mutant countries, or immediate species transformation. Those are fictional high-chaos gameplay content.
