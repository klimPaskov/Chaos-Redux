# 122 Gandhi, Part 5: The Ultimate Society of Peace and Nukes

Gandhi proposes a final settlement of international politics. Every state will cease independent war, accept binding world arbitration and surrender strategic arms outside the authority's approved command. The promise is universal peace. The price is an international government that no longer depends on every member's continuing consent.

The player can lead that project, reject it from within the League, organize an opposing coalition or negotiate a less coercive outcome. Gandhi must earn the ability to attempt it and can lose after the attempt begins.

## A demanding entry gate

The route is available only when Chaos is strictly above 1000. It also requires all the following current facts at the mandate vote.

| Gate | Required proof |
| --- | --- |
| Gandhi | Alive, free and the actual ruler of a consenting command host after an explicit domestic constitutional choice |
| Movement Momentum | At least 75 |
| League Cohesion | At least 70 |
| Political coalition | At least eight active independent signatories across four inhabited macroregions |
| Durable outcomes | At least ten substantive success credits, including three independent mediated peace episodes and four substantive sovereignty settlements |
| Material strength | At least twenty-five percent of current world usable civilian and military factories among the active coalition |
| Distributed arsenal | At least three consenting nuclear owner states with a total of six verified usable devices |
| Delivery credibility | At least two distinct credible national delivery commitments with reachable relevant targets |
| Existing crisis | No unresolved stock dispute, unlawful command assumption or member-aggression case that invalidates the mandate |

Each device is counted once under its real owner. One owner cannot become three through cosmetic tags, temporary custody or political pledges. A country's region follows the validated shared geography. Moving a capital does not create another regional signatory.

The thresholds are proposed balance anchors for future analysis. They deliberately demand accumulated political and material success rather than a giant Chaos bonus. Chaos cannot substitute for the coalition, and the coalition cannot substitute for real weapons.

## The constitutional choice

Before world authority can be proposed, a host country must choose whether Gandhi will hold actual national executive power. Its existing leader is not displaced merely because Gandhi lives there. The transition uses a coherent domestic political route, a real portrait and the current character identity. A human host makes the choice explicitly.

The country keeps its national identity, territory, civilian population, existing industry and legitimate research. The mandate changes leadership and institutions through accepted rules. It does not transform every member into another India or create a new blank global country.

If the current host refuses, Gandhi can seek a different consenting host through lawful travel and a new domestic mandate. The old host is free to remain a civic member or lead opposition. A teleport, forced tag conversion or invented nuclear transfer cannot repair a missing host.

## Congress before coercion

The supreme congress lasts 120 days. It presents the actual powers being requested, the proposed command arrangements, the available arsenal and the right to reject the new charter. All current members receive an informed choice.

The proposal has three possible settlements: retain a voluntary League, adopt a narrower defence and arbitration charter, or ratify the compulsory world-authority mandate. Only explicit signatories count toward the latter. Nonresponse rejects it. Nuclear owners separately ratify their command commitments and preserve their national policy requirements.

Members rejecting coercion can depart immediately with their own armies, research and weapons. They may create an opposing peace coalition. Existing paid aid and personnel receive orderly settlement. No departure vote triggers an automatic attack.

If the full entry conditions no longer pass at the final vote, the compulsory mandate fails. The congress can still produce a voluntary reform if members accepted one. Paid preparation is spent, but no war begins and no global terminal flag is set.

## A playable coercive campaign

Ratifying the mandate begins a 720-day strategic campaign. The Society emergence super-event may play after the real political transition commits. It does not end the game, disarm the world or launch a weapon.

The command coalition must create a valid native military arrangement through explicit member choices. Civic signatures do not silently extract countries from their existing factions. Countries unable to enter the common faction can supply agreed nonmilitary support, but cannot be represented as already integrated military commands.

The authority opens external demands in bounded batches. At most three live target cards are shown, but the target selector and global case registry cover every independent country. Each demand grants forty-five days to respond. A negotiated agreement may use a 180-day fulfilment window within the campaign's remaining time.

The recipient can accept defined arbitration and disarmament terms, negotiate an exemption or timetable, refuse, join an opposition coalition, or challenge the authority's credibility. A minor country has the same agency as a great power. The UI makes the actual political and military consequence clear.

After refusal, Gandhi chooses what to do. Diplomatic pressure, inspection, conventional containment, a real war or withdrawal of the demand are all possible when their conditions exist. No ultimatum expiry automatically declares war or detonates a bomb.

## Binding settlement without erasing nations

The authority's intended settlement preserves national flags, populations, local administration and cultural identity. It binds foreign policy, major security disputes and strategic arms to the supreme charter. It can require demobilization, international inspections, protected borders and compulsory arbitration.

Every binding result needs an engine-real relationship and an exact treaty receipt. Where the native system cannot represent an international obligation directly, the event can maintain a scoped treaty with concrete enforceable conditions and visible decisions. That treaty cannot pretend that an independent enemy has already become a subject or that its war has ended.

An agreed subject or faction status is acceptable only if it matches the accepted settlement. A cosmetic name change is not submission. A country defeated in war must actually surrender or accept terms through a supported settlement. The player cannot click a flag and annex an unoccupied world.

Disarmament is specific. The treaty identifies nuclear devices, prohibited production, permitted defensive forces and inspection access. It leaves a stated level of conventional domestic and border security. The authority's approved strategic arsenal remains under real national owners with explicit command obligations. It does not teleport all weapons into Gandhi's capital.

## Compliance and security

A signatory can comply, conceal a prohibited programme, obstruct an inspection or withdraw when it still has the legal and practical ability to do so. Evidence of a breach comes from actual treaty facts, inspection results or native conflict, not a random hidden accusation designed to force war.

The authority can offer a remedy period, negotiate a revised settlement or take a real enforcement action. Harsh enforcement risks member defections and Momentum loss. Honest compliance can strengthen Cohesion. The event does not need a third public control meter. Each treaty has a qualitative status and a finite observation history.

The active command coalition has lower maintenance floors than the demanding entry gate. After ratification, it needs at least five viable signatories across three regions, Cohesion of at least 35, a lawful command host and at least two consenting usable nuclear owners with two verified devices in total. It also needs at least one approved, physically viable national delivery route from a consenting usable owner, appropriate to the remaining conflict or treaty-security commitments. Readiness can persist during peace under Part 4's distinction between deterrent capability and permission for an actual launch. Losing the final viable route is a real maintenance failure. Entry requires six devices, so expending one does not instantly invalidate the whole route.

Losing a maintenance floor opens the campaign's single thirty-day emergency confidence and recovery window. It covers every currently failed floor. A newly failed floor inside that window does not reset its deadline. All floors must pass before the window closes successfully. A new bomb must be produced, a member must consent or a host must lawfully relocate where that is the real repair needed. No free replacement repairs a gate. Failure to recover by the deadline ends the mandate. Once that one window has been used and closed, any subsequent maintenance failure ends the compulsory mandate without another window.

After all external settlements are complete, the same command and arsenal conditions must remain true throughout stabilization. If the authority chooses to dismantle its final coercive arsenal instead, it can complete a voluntary universal-peace treaty, but that is a peaceful political outcome rather than the named compulsory terminal route.

## Opposition with real choices

Opponents can be former League members, unaligned states, ideological rivals or countries whose security depends on independent arsenals. Opposition does not require a particular ideology. A former beneficiary of Gandhi's independence movement can oppose his supreme government while defending the sovereignty he helped secure.

The opposition has several ways to win.

| Strategy | Concrete objective | Cost and risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constitutional challenge | Persuade the command host or a decisive group of signatories to revoke the mandate | Sustained diplomacy, credible alternative security and paid commitments |
| Coalition fracture | Secure real departures that push the authority below its maintenance floor | Member interests must be addressed. A speech alone creates no defection |
| Conventional defeat | Take the command seat and deny a lawful successor host while winning the relevant native war | Actual forces, access, supply, casualties and peace terms |
| Strategic denial | Remove credible delivery or usable arsenals through lawful inspection, negotiated dismantlement or real military success | Nuclear sites and stock remain governed by their true owners |
| Negotiated peace | Obtain a signed end to compulsory authority with sovereign exit and disarmament guarantees | Gandhi and relevant war leaders must accept and actually implement the terms |
| Endurance | Prevent universal settlement until the 720-day mandate expires | Long defence, economic burden and the risk of renewed conventional wars |

Gandhi's capture can enable negotiation or a rescue attempt. It does not automatically grant the captor his country's weapons. His death permanently ends the personal supreme route. The response can be institutional reform, fragmentation or continued ordinary conflict under surviving states. There is no scripted automatic nuclear revenge.

## Victory is a resolved world

The terminal victory check runs only after all substantive world cases have resolved. It requires:

- Every living sovereign country has ratified or is bound by a verified supported settlement, including microstates and distant survivors.
- No unresolved interstate war remains.
- No independent opposition coalition retains a sovereign ability to reject the settlement.
- No uncontrolled strategic arsenal remains outside a verified treaty or approved command arrangement.
- Required disarmament, demobilization and inspection obligations have actually completed.
- Gandhi remains the lawful supreme leader with a viable command host and the required approved deterrent.
- Ninety days of observed stable compliance have passed after the last substantive settlement.
- No other shared terminal owner has already resolved the world.

Individual dissent and nonstrategic local disorder can survive the settlement. They do not automatically invalidate victory unless they restore sovereign opposition, break the global peace or endanger strategic control. Conversely, a country that simply failed to appear in the current three-card UI is not resolved.

If the last substantive settlement completes by campaign day 720, its one ninety-day stabilization period may finish afterward. The absolute maximum is day 810 from ratification. A new material breach resets the observation only if it can still complete within that maximum. The event cannot extend itself indefinitely.

Only the final successful check requests the shared terminal world-end ownership and then plays the compulsory-peace victory presentation. Earlier congresses, ultimatums, faction formation, nuclear research and military victories leave `world_end` unset.

## Defeat and aftermath

A failed mandate ends the claim to compulsory global authority. It removes event-owned enforcement permissions, uncommitted threats and temporary command obligations. It does not erase native wars, confiscate national assets or delete populations. Existing wars need an actual negotiated settlement or continue as ordinary conflicts.

Surviving members can vote for a reformed voluntary League, preserve a conventional defence pact or dissolve. Paid civilian improvements remain. Former targets regain ordinary independent diplomacy when their actual treaties and native relationships permit it. Any transferred state requires a lawful restoration agreement, not a blanket rollback of years of history.

A major global defeat can receive the third super-event described in Part 11 when a broad, costly coercive campaign really occurred. A congress that merely failed its vote receives ordinary news. Presentation scale follows what happened.

For that presentation classification, the mandate must have been ratified and active for at least ninety days. Real opposition must have included at least three sovereign countries across two macroregions, with either an involved native major or at least ten percent of world usable industry in that opposition. Tangible cost must also be proved by a native conflict lasting at least thirty days, verified removal of at least two usable nuclear devices under a real settlement, or at least three fulfilled major ninety-day material commitments. These are factual history conditions, not a new score. They never become prerequisites for defeating Gandhi. A shared terminal ending preempts this defeat presentation.

The supreme campaign can be attempted once for this Gandhi incident. Failure closes its personal compulsory mandate rather than offering an immediate reset of timers and receipts. The reformed League retains its peaceful and defensive choices.

## Competing endings

Nuclear Fallout, Final Silence or another accepted world-end owner can resolve the world during Gandhi's campaign. Gandhi contributes only his own active world-threat source and asks the shared aggregator to refresh it. He cannot clear another source or suppress an independently reached catastrophic ending.

If another terminal owner commits first, Gandhi closes unresolved actions, preserves their historical evidence and yields presentation according to the shared terminal policy. It does not announce compulsory peace over a world already destroyed. A simultaneous terminal race has one authoritative winner, checked immediately before any final mutation or celebration.
