# Part 07. Focus paths, political choices, and strategic payoff

The focus content turns hosting, membership, liberation, and opposition into sustained national play. It preserves meaningful existing trees and adds shared route groups adapted to each country. The host receives the deepest architecture. Members and newly independent countries receive smaller groups that interact with their own economy, politics, geography, and diplomacy. A fallback civil council receives a complete proportionate administration tree.

All group and anchor labels in this part are working design identifiers. They are not final focus names or localisation. Exact node count, final identifiers, coordinates, durations, and prerequisite syntax belong to implementation after installed precedents are inspected. The required choices, unlocks, route locks, cross-branch relationships, and payoffs must survive that translation.

## 1. Tree ownership and route access

### Existing countries

Use additive shared paths attached to the actual owning country. Keep its meaningful national roots, completed history, available national strategy, leaders, and existing branch identity. The Gandhi content may legitimately change a leader, law, institution, or diplomatic policy through an accepted route. It must not erase an unrelated national tree simply because that is easier to lay out.

Hosting can unlock a substantial optional dedicated root only through a documented, explicit national choice. That choice must explain which existing route access changes and how completed progress and history remain represented. If a return or constitutional reform route is supported, define it. If the commitment is deliberately permanent, make that consequence public. The implementation must never use a silent wholesale replacement as a fallback for difficult shared-focus integration.

The host branch is active only while the country holds the relevant lawful responsibility. Moving the host pauses or closes host-only commitments through the transfer contract. It does not award the same factories or equipment to a second host. Completed national infrastructure stays with its actual owner. Institutional unlocks follow the valid office without copying consumed material rewards.

### Newly independent countries

Preserve a meaningful available national tree. Add recognition, reconstruction, charter contribution, safeguards, and opposition groups where they change that country's decisions. Adapt local targets, resources, advisors already present, eligible partners, and AI. A landlocked agricultural state and a maritime industrial country should not read or play identically.

A council with no meaningful existing tree receives the dedicated administration architecture in section 7. Its country package and fictional classification must already be accepted. Its tree cannot be a generic host template whose foreign ambitions and nuclear rewards make no sense for the locality.

## 2. Opening and pacing

Begin the host extension with one small opening group that accepts the practical responsibilities of hosting. It confirms lawful access, consultation duties, and the limits of the office. It opens a few immediately useful choices rather than a long political-power ladder.

The first meaningful choices should change at least one of the domestic build plan, a live diplomatic case, actual aid capacity, defense timetable, or institutional authority. A strained host can prioritize a narrow paid reconstruction commitment. A threatened host can seek a legitimate defense agreement. A politically divided host can consolidate a constitutional mandate. Each route must remain viable without first completing every support branch.

Use short emergency or handoff focuses for narrow immediate actions, medium commitments for early strategic forks, and substantial durations for major programs. The project's seven, 35, and 70-day bands are useful implementation precedents. A short focus earns its place by changing play. Splitting one reward into two empty clicks does not meet this requirement.

Stage visibility follows the actual public history. Civic and reconstruction content appears early. Military coordination expands when members choose real commitments. Nuclear content requires the relevant public opportunity and distinguishes a programme from an active nuclear evolution. The personal coercive branch remains hidden until its political conflict becomes public. Focus completion cannot substitute for the canonical Chaos, member, success, industrial, consent, and nuclear gates.

## 3. Host route map

| Family | Working anchor functions | Main connection | Lasting payoff |

| Opening | Accept host responsibility, establish a workable mandate, choose initial priority | Opens civic politics, reconstruction, diplomacy, and defense preparation | A host that can perform useful work immediately |
| Civic politics | Protect national consent, choose executive limits, make leadership accountable | Changes how diplomacy, defense, and nuclear commitments can be authorized | Constitutional League or a bounded executive security institution |
| Reconstruction and economy | Select real beneficiaries, build deliverable assistance, develop local productive independence | Supplies member survival, defense logistics, and the costs of later nuclear work | Productive capacity and continuing paid cooperation |
| Conventional defense | Prepare logistics, protect civilians and infrastructure, consent to a compact | Supports protection and settlement without bypassing faction legality | A useful defensive system built from national forces |
| Diplomacy | Establish neutral mediation, choose protection relationships, maintain recognition | Creates cases for settlement and tests institutional consent | Repeated meaningful peace work and credible commitments |
| Liberation and settlement | Prepare a lawful transfer, support independent survival, settle borders and obligations | Changes the map or diplomatic order and feeds reconstruction | Durable sovereignty with actual aftermath |
| Nuclear stewardship | Build a real programme, negotiate national safeguards, choose deterrence or disarmament | Depends on industry, diplomacy, national owners, and civic rules | Credible distributed deterrence, verified reductions, or peaceful disarmament |
| Constitutional crisis | Challenge emergency authority, transfer the host, authorize or reject personal rule | Connects political legitimacy to the nuclear institution and member consent | Reform, institutional succession, breakup, or a valid coercive mandate |
| Coercive campaign | Sustain real capacity, issue bounded demands, negotiate or fight, manage defection | Uses the accepted world-authority approach while preserving opposition play | Actual settled compulsory peace or a playable failure |
| Aftermath | Secure weapons, restore lawful sovereignty, reconstruct and reform | Resolves the unfinished obligations of whichever route prevailed | A durable post-crisis order and continuing national strategy |

These are distinct families with deliberate links. They must not collapse into one long political chain. Several support paths can proceed in parallel. Political choices determine how their results are used, rather than making industry, diplomacy, and defense mutually exclusive by default.

## 4. Host families in detail

### 4.1 Civic politics and constitutional government

The constitutional path begins with an institution that has practical limits. Anchor groups establish consultation, transparent contributions, meaningful selection of the host, protection of a member's national veto, and a lawful withdrawal process. Its payoff is an institution capable of surviving disagreement and a change of convenor.

Choices distinguish broad consultation from faster delegated administration. Delegation can improve response to a live crisis, but participants decide its bounds and renewal. A constitutional route should be capable of urgent action when its conditions are met. It should not be permanently handicapped until the player adopts personal dictatorship.

The security executive path provides faster coordination around real threats. It requires actual commitments, carries domestic and member costs, and has an explicit sunset, review, or reform path. Its government changes must be tangible through laws, offices, decision permissions, leader roles where lawfully chosen, and AI. A security executive can still preserve national arsenals and member consent.

The two political approaches change how cases are authorized and funded. A consent-focused host gains access and trust through reliable procedure. A delegated executive can concentrate effort during an agreed window but faces consequences when promises remain unmet. Both can deliver a legitimate nonnuclear success. Their conflict should be expressed through decisions, civic votes, national politics, and real obligations, using Momentum and Cohesion rather than a new authority meter.

Do not add a shallow branch for every ideology. Host institutions must fit the country's actual political situation. An elected government, monarchy, military government, or other host can enter the institution under its own circumstances. Appropriate domestic changes need country-specific direction and valid leaders. Faction membership and civic membership remain separate.

### 4.2 Reconstruction and productive independence

The first anchor selects a real problem and beneficiary. Examples include a damaged productive state, disrupted land supply, a necessary port connection, or a region unable to sustain its independent administration. This selection opens paid finite work rather than immediately handing out generic factories.

Offer three practical methods where the economy supports them. Local reconstruction preserves beneficiary autonomy but takes capacity and time. Trade and logistics integration improves access while creating dependence on maintained routes. A more centralized shared production commitment can supply defense or infrastructure efficiently but concentrates obligations and political exposure. The available methods follow actual geography and resources.

Intermediate groups unlock procurement, repair, training support, technical cooperation, and delivery missions. The host can stop after a coherent initial contribution. Deeper investment creates a stronger production or logistics network, with paid commitments and actual domestic opportunity cost. Rewards must name the real state, production opportunity, access arrangement, or decision family they create.

This branch supports diplomacy because reliable aid makes a settlement viable. It supports defense because infrastructure, fuel access, ports, trucks, and trains determine what a country can sustain. It supports nuclear work only through actual research cooperation and paid infrastructure. It never converts Momentum into equipment, research completion, devices, or an abstract shared arsenal.

The payoff is a continuing ability to help independent members remain viable. A reconstruction state transforms or clears the third spirit-family burden when the obligation is fulfilled, cancelled lawfully, or failed. New projects replace or aggregate the applicable obligation within that family rather than add one idea per target.

### 4.3 Conventional protection and defense

Begin with the defenses and logistics that the host can actually support. A landlocked host emphasizes infantry, engineers, supply, and air protection where available. A coastal host can invest in port access, convoys, escorts, and maritime protection. An industrial air power can support air defense or transport. Avoid token naval nodes in an inland country's unrelated political lane.

The main strategic choice is between a limited protection role and a more demanding intervention capability. Protection emphasizes evacuation, access, civilian infrastructure, reserve equipment, and defensive planning. Intervention readiness can respond to lawful requests and limited objectives, with higher costs, diplomatic exposure, and greater risk of escalation. Its payoff is a useful capability and relevant decisions, not an event-created super-unit.

The defensive compact anchor is a real consent and faction-law junction. Civic members can decline it and retain their civic membership. Current faction members need a valid native transition before any military compact membership takes effect. A focus does not secretly call their existing allies into a war or give the host command over their national army.

Army, air, and naval groups have cross-branch utility. Land logistics improves aid delivery. Air protection protects industry and reconstruction. Convoy and port work sustains trade and relief. Specialist investment remains optional when a smaller commitment can meet the country's obligations.

A limited protection conflict needs its actual objective, participants, guarantee and faction consequences, escalation conditions, peace terms, overreach, defender reversal, cleanup, and postwar obligations in the owning decision or settlement specification. Focuses prepare or unlock that process. They do not award an unexplained universal war goal.

### 4.4 Diplomacy and recognition

The diplomatic opening establishes access to the parties in a real dispute. A neutral mediator has broad potential access and limited command. A guarantee-based approach can provide stronger protection but risks war and exclusion by hostile parties. Short-term sponsorship can support a weak host, with an explicit cost in independence or future commitments.

Follow-up groups improve communication, negotiated access, verification, and the ability to maintain a settlement. The relevant actions use actual relations, consenting participants, and the selected dispute. A dead sponsor, capitulated partner, vanished war, or invalid target closes or redirects the route cleanly. Central gates must offer a way to pursue the needed relation or agreement intentionally.

The payoff is an active set of mediation cases and recognition arrangements. The player must be able to improve Cohesion through reliable behavior and recover from a failed case. A focus completion alone is not a durable peace success. The owning receipt rules observe the actual settlement and required time.

Gandhi's historical writing presents noncooperation as disciplined action that can lead back to cooperation, which informs this route's reconciliation options. The game's timers, modifiers, acceptance rules, and reward magnitudes remain authored design. [H01](https://www.mkgandhi.org/momgandhi/chap35.php)

### 4.5 Liberation and settlement

This is the distinct expansion or settlement family. It changes actual sovereignty and the diplomatic order while preserving the campaign's voluntary route. Its early anchor prepares a lawful state allocation with appropriate parties. Another supports recognition and survival after commitment. Later groups resolve remaining property, access, military withdrawal, and disputed borders.

The principal methods are negotiated immediate independence where the owner and controller can lawfully commit, a properly scoped wartime settlement, and a post-conflict settlement after an actual defensive or opposition victory. These methods have different conditions and risks. They do not all grant the same instantaneous release effect.

A stage can unlock a conference, a bounded preparation mission, or targeted reconstruction, but the final map change belongs to the shared liberation coordinator and Gandhi's distinct adapter. Core disputes, living claimants, all-state allocation, and player continuation remain authoritative. A focus cannot grant cores to bypass those questions.

The branch's continuing payoff is independent countries that can remain independent, resolved access and recognition, and a stronger ability to negotiate future cases. Repeating the same release or transferring the same beneficiary between patrons cannot generate new credit. A country that becomes sovereign is free to decline the League.

### 4.6 Nuclear stewardship

Separate three anchor groups: a possible national programme, actual shared political arrangements among national owners, and a choice of nuclear policy. The programme can make native research, cooperation, reactors, or delivery infrastructure accessible at normal elapsed time and real cost when the accepted stage permits it. The active evolution still requires the full canonical strong-League and real-capacity gates.

A research agreement does not authorize a threat or strike. A vote does not transfer a device. National owners keep custody, launch vetoes, and actual delivery assets. Focus rewards unlock appropriate inspection, reciprocal reduction, consultation, paid preparedness, or restraint decisions. The current unresolved cross-owner custody route is not a dependency of this branch.

The national-veto deterrence path preserves participating owners' consent while using credible capability to support a lawful settlement. Its tradeoff is slower agreement and the exposure of smaller states that depend on another country's decision. The verified disarmament path seeks actual reductions and safeguards, with a genuine cost in capacity foregone. It requires an alternative security arrangement rather than pretending all weapons vanish after a speech.

A coercive inspection path concentrates pressure and can expose bluff, secrecy, unequal burdens, and departures. It can create the later constitutional crisis but does not automatically win it. Nuclear use against civilians is a severe ideological rupture with the accepted resignations, losses, and split consequences. The historical source condemns atomic destruction and rejects counter-bombs as a moral solution, so the event's coercive nuclear path is an explicit fictional departure. [H03](https://www.mkgandhi.org/momgandhi/chap94.php)

### 4.7 Constitutional crisis and personal authority

The crisis follows actual contested authority and nuclear or security commitments. It should not be an automatic civil war. Use a domestic constitutional struggle, civic votes, challenged mandates, resignation, transfer of the host, institutional reform, or breakup as appropriate to the case.

The reform side can restrict executive authority, preserve national vetoes, replace the host, or transition the movement to a collective institution. Gandhi's death or capture must have a coherent institutional outcome. Members can preserve useful cooperation without pretending that the dead leader still personally issues orders.

The personal side must obtain the host's explicit domestic consent to Gandhi's rulership, meet the complete world-authority conditions, and survive the informed member response. The focus can prepare or request that mandate. It cannot create the required members, industrial share, successes, devices, or delivery reach by declaring them satisfied.

Mutual exclusion is appropriate between an active personal supreme mandate and the incompatible constitutional settlement. A bounded emergency executive remains reformable. Members and owners have their actual exit and veto rights at the transition. Defections reduce real resources and access rather than merely lowering a decorative value.

### 4.8 Coercive campaign and terminal settlement

The coercive branch develops governing institutions, production and logistics, diplomatic coercion, conventional military coordination, disarmament monitoring, and response to defections. It must be broad enough to sustain the accepted playable campaign. A single final focus that annexes the planet fails this design.

Progress requires actual demands, replies, negotiations, wars where chosen, and verified surrender or ratification. The actor sees only the permitted number of current demands and commitments while the selection process retains complete eligible-country coverage. The branch uses the canonical finite mandate, reply, settlement, and compliance windows.

Support groups help the host sustain legitimate capacity and monitor obligations. They can reduce a real operational bottleneck at a real cost. They cannot generate free strategic weapons, ignore remaining opposition, declare a microstate resolved without a settlement, or set the world-end flag because the player completed a focus.

The capstone represents actual stable compulsory peace after the shared terminal check. The unsuccessful route has real closure through defeat, failed mandate, coalition split, loss of host, Gandhi's removal or death, or a negotiated reversal. Those outcomes open aftermath or a reformed League where still viable.

### 4.9 Aftermath and durable peaceful outcomes

A successful constitutional League can complete a demanding nonnuclear institutional outcome and continue serving members. A late peaceful disengagement can close obligations, transfer or retire the host office, and let states proceed independently. Neither needs a coercive world-end branch to feel complete.

After the Society's defeat, anchor groups secure actual weapons and access, resolve unfinished wars and territory, restore lawful national sovereignty, and rebuild affected regions. A conventional opponent, a former nuclear signatory, and a liberated small state have different contributions and costs. The aftermath super-event is reserved for the qualifying global, costly threat. The focus route remains playable even when defeat was contained and receives ordinary presentation.

No terminal focus should end merely in a small modifier. The endpoint must establish a durable institution, settle real obligations, unlock continuing missions, complete a national identity, or close a meaningful optional investment. The player should understand what remains to do afterward.

## 5. Cross-branch contracts and meaningful gates

| Junction | Required relationship | Failure or alternate continuation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Politics to defense | The chosen institution determines how actual contributors authorize action | Seek a narrower consent, defend nationally, or return to consultation |
| Reconstruction to diplomacy | Aid is paid, deliverable, and received before it improves the relevant case | Renegotiate the route, refund or settle the obligation, or accept a failed promise |
| Diplomacy to settlement | The affected parties agree to the actual scoped terms | Continue noncooperation within its finite term, revise the case, or close it without a false success |
| Settlement to reconstruction | The beneficiary becomes genuinely sovereign and has a viable administration | Resolve access or reconstruction after release without quietly restoring dependency |
| Industry to nuclear policy | Research, production, delivery, and owner consent supply distinct capabilities | Remain on the nonnuclear route or continue normal paid development |
| Nuclear policy to constitutional crisis | Real coercion, secrecy, contested authority, or unmet security commitments create a political dispute | Reform, transfer the host, allow exit, or resolve obligations without personal dictatorship |
| Personal mandate to coercive campaign | Every canonical prerequisite remains true at the actual transition | Pause or cancel the mandate cleanly. Do not spend or move nonexistent weapons |
| War to settlement | Actual control and surrender produce terms with surviving parties | Continue the legitimate war, accept a negotiated limit, or respond to defender success |
| Defeat to aftermath | The real surviving countries, weapons, and institutions determine reconstruction | A smaller contained failure receives proportionate recovery rather than a fabricated global collapse |

Use a small number of deliberate prerequisite junctions. The remaining interactions can live in unlocked decisions, missions, native requirements, and public route effects. Do not turn the tree into a web of long crossed connectors.

## 6. Member and newly independent paths

| Working path | Early choice and unlock | Real payoff | Country adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition and recovery | Choose neutral recognition, League support, or a viable alternative patron | A functioning independent administration, access, and a finite reconstruction programme | Local production, supply, existing leaders, wars, and credible partners |
| Charter contribution | Offer reconstruction, defensive support, or diplomatic service that the country can actually provide | A fulfilled meaningful contribution and corresponding access to cooperation | Small states can mediate or host access without pretending to fund a great-power army |
| National safeguards | Seek consultation, clarify an obligation, appeal pressure, or begin an ordinary exit | Protected national choice and a settled relationship | Existing faction, ideology, constitution, and economic dependency shape the decision |
| Nuclear participation | Accept a specific safeguard, retain a national veto, support disarmament, or decline | A real security arrangement based on actual ownership and consent | Nuclear-free countries gain relevant diplomacy without receiving free devices |
| Opposition and defection | Preserve access, secure resources, seek protection, and resist an unlawful mandate | National survival and material participation in reform or defeat | Geography and actual forces determine whether the country shelters civilians, provides logistics, or fights |
| Post-crisis settlement | Settle weapons, property, borders, and lawful government | A durable sovereign outcome and continuing domestic route | The country keeps its own history and future rather than becoming a generic reward tag |

These groups should connect to the country's meaningful existing roots where the subject matter fits. They must not require replaying a long second opening simply to use an available event response. Joining, declining, or leaving the League is never locked behind a years-long focus queue when the canonical decision gives a timely choice.

## 7. Civil council tree

A fallback council needs a complete proportional tree with politics, economy, defense, diplomacy, and settlement. Its opening establishes a viable capital, immediate supply, and civilian administration. The first choices should address the actual area's most serious constraint rather than a generic ideological menu.

Representative civilian consolidation gives consultation, local services, and recognition. A bounded emergency executive can coordinate urgent defense or supply at a political cost, followed by review or reform. A sponsor route offers quicker support with dependency and a later opportunity to recover autonomy. These choices use existing national politics and the two event values where applicable, not a new bespoke council meter.

Economic routes can repair local production, secure commercial access, or invest in a geographic resource or transport corridor. Defense creates an affordable conventional force and protection relationships. Settlement deals with actual core disputes, recognition, and lawful neighboring arrangements. The late endpoint can be durable independence, a consented constitutional successor, or a lawful union whose people and territorial parties are represented by the accepted settlement. A neighboring claimant's core does not by itself determine that endpoint.

Councils must be adapted individually. Local resources, ports, land access, population, disputed territory, and available partners shape rewards and AI. The branch language identifies practical civic institutions. It must not present the authored council as a newly discovered ancient nation or give it an expansionist empire unrelated to its territory.

## 8. Ideas, rewards, filters, and presentation

### Three spirit families

The occupation-response family remains dedicated to the applicable refusal or fascist control treatment. The civic-institution family replaces its constructive administration, charter, defensive compact, nuclear dissent, and supreme mandate stages. The material-obligation family represents a finite reconstruction or contribution burden and transforms rather than stacking per target.

Focus rewards generally alter actual capability, laws, office access, state work, finite missions, diplomatic options, or institutional authority. Important spirit changes accompany a meaningful commitment. They do not reward every focus with a new permanent modifier. Existing unrelated spirits remain intact.

Momentum and Cohesion are the only new persistent public numerical values. Native inventory, research, factories, relations, wars, and mission deadlines remain understandable native facts. A successful route changes those realities and the two values through the owning effects. It does not create extra public authority, legitimacy, nuclear influence, or settlement-credit currencies.

### Filters and navigation

Every implemented focus receives current supported search filters matching its primary role. Political, industry, army, naval, air, diplomacy, settlement, and special content use the correct available categories. Add a custom filter only after the current supported set is shown to be inadequate. Multi-tagging does not remove the need for one primary branch home.

Large separated regions need Focus Navigation with a stable branch anchor, useful pan and zoom, a short later-localized label, and a readable accepted icon. Secret branches and their navigation remain hidden until revealed. Tiny nearby spurs do not need their own navigation button. Reuse the appropriate accepted focus-family icon where the installed navigation consumer safely supports it, with a separate row if its native asset contract requires a distinct family.

Reserve a clear late region for nuclear and constitutional crisis content. Keep army, air, and naval groups together as distinct subfamilies. Keep diplomacy and settlement adjacent but recognizable. Comparable siblings have balanced spacing. Connectors take the shortest clean practical path. Hidden and continuous focuses participate in overlap review. Every terminal group has a clear payoff or deliberate convergence.

Static art, native focus tooltips, and the existing decision category provide the required information. The two values do not require a focus inlay or an additional custom board. Final text direction states the visible public requirement, immediate baseline consequence, cost, and follow-up without exposing hidden future outcomes.

## 9. Route-specific AI

| Actor or route | Priorities after legal checks | Avoid or close |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stable constitutional host | Reliable affordable aid, broad access, completed settlements, preservation of consent, and timely host succession | Unsupported commitments, unwanted faction moves, and coercion that contradicts its chosen institution |
| Threatened security host | Actual defensive readiness, supply, valid compact partners, and bounded emergency authority | A compact that cannot form legally or a force commitment it cannot deliver |
| Poor or geographically isolated host | A few feasible cases, local reconstruction, access, and viable partners | Overextension, symbolic nuclear programmes without industry, and undeliverable overseas aid |
| Small member or recent release | Survival, recognition, reconstruction, clear safeguards, and protection proportionate to dependency | Unaffordable material promises and irreversible loss of sovereignty disguised as membership |
| Conventional great-power member | Useful cooperation while preserving real national interests and existing commitments | Treating all civic votes as military orders or offering forces already required elsewhere |
| Nuclear owner | Real capability and security assessment, owner consent, delivery feasibility, and reciprocal verification | Threats without reach, nonexistent devices, unauthorized launch, and inspections it has not accepted |
| Constitutional dissenter | Reform, consultation, a valid transfer of host, or withdrawal before an unlawful commitment | Remaining in a contradictory route only to collect incompatible rewards |
| Society command host | Viable target selection, actual diplomacy and war capacity, sustainable demands, retained signatories, and handling of defections | Impossible targets, more commitments than permitted, empty arsenals, and premature terminal claims |
| Opposition or defector | Survival, secure access and weapons, coalition diplomacy, realistic limited action, and postwar safeguards | Automatic suicidal war or imitation of the defeated mandate as the only possible outcome |

Legality and existence checks come first. A missing state, dead sponsor, invalid faction, unavailable nuclear owner, dead Gandhi, insufficient stage, or closed case makes a route unavailable or gives it zero selection weight when that route actually requires the missing condition. Gandhi's death closes his personal mandate routes while leaving lawful institutional succession, reform, reconstruction, and opposition aftermath eligible under their own conditions. Use bounded contextual priorities with ordinary, threatened, and high-Chaos profiles. Focus selection is an independent score race under the actual engine semantics. Do not describe raw weights as measured normalized probabilities without the complete candidate set and valid analysis.

## 10. Completion review

The final implementation handoff must compare every required family against actual routes, identify any renamed or merged group, and report a missing bespoke path or fallback tree as a simplification. A separate clarity table identifies each branch, opening anchor, main choice, continuation, payoff, filter family, navigation, and review result.

Review the host extension together with its real national tree. At normal zoom, the major families should be recognizable without reading every tooltip or tracing long connectors. Where rendering is unavailable within the project's bounded tool budget, review coordinates, bounds, connectors, titles, icon references, and reveal states directly and state that visual first-glance clarity was not observed. Confirmed ambiguity still needs correction.

Test the design against a nonnuclear successful League, a peaceful late disengagement, a threatened legal compact, a poor host with no viable nuclear programme, a nuclear owner refusing command, Gandhi's death, a host transfer, a council release, a valid Society approach, a failed mandate, and opposition victory. These are future acceptance scenarios. This specification has not run focus inspection, rendering, probability analysis, code changes, or live-game tests.
