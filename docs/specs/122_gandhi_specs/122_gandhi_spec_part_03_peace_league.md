# 122 Gandhi, Part 3: A League that earns its authority

The Peace League grows from settlements that have already worked. Newly independent states want protection, former occupiers want a stable settlement, neutral governments want fewer wars, and ordinary citizens want practical reconstruction. These interests can sustain cooperation without giving every member the same ideology, leader or foreign policy.

Gandhi convenes the organization. Members retain the right to refuse a proposal and to leave. The tension between service, defence and concentrated power gives the League its long campaign.

## Foundation at Chaos 400 and above

The founding congress becomes available when all of the following are true.

- Chaos is at least 400 and the Peace League evolution is enabled.
- Gandhi is alive, free, lawfully hosted and able to reach the proposed congress.
- Movement Momentum is at least 50.
- At least three independent countries across two inhabited macroregions have explicitly accepted the founding terms.
- At least three durable substantive success credits exist, including one genuine sovereignty settlement.
- No founding participant is currently attacking another prospective founding participant.

The sixty-day maturity and anti-farming rules in Part 2 apply. Peace credits require an actual negotiated end to a real conflict and the same observed durability. A constructive-work receipt can contribute to Momentum and relations, but it cannot replace the mandatory sovereignty or peace evidence.

The congress takes thirty days. Each prospective member receives its own choice. A nonresponse declines. If fewer than three qualified acceptances remain at completion, the proposal closes with a failed-congress report and a ninety-day reconsideration interval. Paid travel and preparation are spent. No League or Cohesion value is created.

Successful foundation records one evolution grant, establishes a consenting host and sets Cohesion to 60. It awards no Chaos merely for existing. Concrete subsequent cooperation can reduce disorder under Part 10.

## Civic membership and military commitments

The initial League is a diplomatic network. A member may remain in its existing faction. Its wars, guarantees, subject relationships, military access and nuclear policy remain real native relationships.

Membership supplies access to consultations, paid aid projects, mediation, common standards for sovereignty and a limited set of additive national focuses. It provides no automatic call to war, free equipment, compulsory ideology conversion or ownership of another member's army.

The defensive compact is a separate opt-in commitment. Countries that want a native common faction must first have a lawful way to leave their existing faction and must separately accept the new obligation. The event shows that cost before the choice. A civic member unable or unwilling to change factions can remain in the League and contribute nonmilitary aid.

| Role | Powers | Obligations and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Civic member | Vote on civic policy, request mediation, fund or receive agreed projects | Report honestly, respect member sovereignty, do not initiate aggression against a member |
| Project participant | Commit resources to one defined project | Pay the agreed amount and retain responsibility for its own delivery |
| Defensive signatory | Join a valid defence arrangement and coordinate declared protection | Native faction and war rules apply. No automatic offensive mandate |
| Nuclear owner participant | Share verified capability information and accept specified consultations | Retains national inventory, policy and launch veto |
| Observer | Attend selected talks and seek mediation without joining | No vote, protection entitlement or access to classified arsenal information |
| Host | Provide a lawful seat, access and administration | Cannot turn hosting into permanent command over other members |

## Admission, exit and expulsion

Admission requires independence, explicit acceptance, a genuine capacity to perform the charter's minimum duties and no ongoing aggression against a member. There is no blanket ideological ban. An authoritarian or fascist applicant must demonstrate the same truthful reporting and sovereign conduct as any other applicant. Its domestic label does not turn a promise into proof.

New admissions take twenty-one days of consultation. Existing members can raise a concrete charter objection. A two-thirds civic vote can refuse admission when a documented violation remains. Ordinary dislike alone affects AI willingness but does not fabricate a legal breach.

Ordinary departure uses thirty days of notice. New obligations stop immediately. Existing paid property, personnel, basing arrangements and projects receive an explicit settlement. Unspent escrow is refunded only through its real payment owner. Already delivered aid remains with its recipient. A country leaving cannot confiscate foreign units or weapons.

Immediate emergency departure is available when the League adopts coercive world authority, attacks a member, authorizes an unlawful nuclear use or refuses a member's basic right to leave. There is no departure fee designed to trap the player in the late route. A departing country can retain separate bilateral friendships and native defensive arrangements where those arrangements legally survive.

Expulsion requires a documented serious violation, a fourteen-day response and a two-thirds vote of eligible members, excluding the accused from the deciding count. Active aggression or confirmed unauthorized nuclear use permits emergency suspension during that process. Expulsion removes civic benefits and uncommitted obligations. It does not seize the expelled country's arsenal or automatically declare war.

## Leadership, host and person

The League distinguishes Gandhi, the elected civic leadership, the state that hosts its offices and the military leaders of any native defence faction. None of those roles automatically owns the others.

Gandhi begins as convenor. A leadership review occurs after 360 days and after a major host-loss or coercion crisis. Each independent civic member has one vote. Members select a consenting viable host and a charter direction. A host must offer actual access and a defensible working seat. A remote capital with no valid access cannot host merely because it gives an attractive geographic count.

If Gandhi is captured, the League can negotiate his release, organize a lawful rescue operation through actual forces, move its offices or continue under a temporary civic council. Rescue cannot teleport a character through hostile territory or create an army. If he dies, members choose institutional succession or dissolution. His personal world-authority route closes permanently for this incident.

Domestic leadership changes remain domestic political decisions. Hosting Gandhi or electing him League convenor never silently makes him a country's ruler. The later supreme mandate requires a separate explicit constitutional choice in the command host.

## Practical work

The League has three core activities with useful outcomes at every Chaos level where it survives.

### Constructive work

Members select a real local deficit such as damaged civilian infrastructure, unreliable supply, a neglected agricultural district or a displaced civilian population represented by an existing shared system. A project has a recipient state, a paid material contribution, an accessible delivery route and a finite completion condition.

The main anchor is sixty days for a modest repair or local administrative project and ninety days for a larger reconstruction commitment. Cost scales with the actual damaged facilities and recipient capacity. The donor may commit a temporary civilian-industrial burden, measured equipment, transport capacity and qualified personnel. The recipient contributes local effort where feasible.

Completion repairs or improves the agreed real capacity through supported effects. It does not create a factory in every new country or cure every social problem through one button. Failure returns only genuinely unspent assets and leaves a truthful record of what was delivered. Three fulfilled material contributions in the previous 180 days are part of the later strong-League gate.

### Mediation

A mediator needs access to both principal sides and a conflict they can actually settle. The parties receive the terms and can refuse. A thirty-day conference can be extended once by fifteen days only when both sides have already accepted a concrete outstanding provision.

Peace requires the appropriate war leaders and all materially relevant war relationships. It cannot leave another native war between the same parties and claim success. The Event 009 integration provides settlement execution or certified receipts only through its own accepted contract. An unrelated automatic White Peace gives no Gandhi credit.

After execution, sixty days of observed peace mature the political success. A new war caused by another unrelated aggressor does not retroactively make the original ceasefire fictional, but it can prevent the current durability credit if the protected parties are again at war. The historical record keeps both facts.

### Joint protection

Members can fund fortification, defensive supply, evacuation access and conventional deterrence for a threatened sovereign partner. Every contribution is national and measured. A guarantee or native defensive alliance carries its actual diplomatic and war risk.

Joint protection is strongest when it preserves a settlement without conquest. If members choose armed intervention, they use actual troops, equipment, access, fronts and war declarations. The League cannot end fighting through a hidden compulsory white peace and then describe the action as ordinary collective defence.

## Momentum and Cohesion changes

The following anchors are proposed balance values. They use one actual-outcome receipt and bounded incident caps. The coding handoff must expose the real cause in the tooltip and history.

| Actual outcome | Momentum | Cohesion | Guard and practical meaning |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Substantive independence commits | +2 to +4 | +1 to +3 if a member materially supported it | Beneficiary and case once. Initial incident Momentum gain capped at +12 |
| The same independence matures after sixty days | +2 | +2 | Durability receipt once, no duplicate of a rerelease |
| Genuine mediated peace matures | +4 to +6 | +4 | One peace episode, scale follows conflict and participating commitments |
| Paid constructive project completes | +1 to +3 | +2 to +4 | One recipient project, no credit for a refunded or undelivered promise |
| Fairly shared defence succeeds | +2 | +3 | Actual threatened settlement preserved, not merely a signed guarantee |

| Inspection reveals a material lie | -5 to -10 | -5 to -12 | Severity follows the deception and security consequence |
| A member departs after a fair ordinary notice | 0 to -2 | -2 | Leaving is lawful, no universal catastrophe |
| Several members leave after imposed coercion | -5 to -10 | -8 to -15 | One connected split incident, capped rather than one huge loss per microstate |
| Nuclear threat is authorized without broad consultation | -5 | -8 | Separate from national law and actual use permission |
| Confirmed unlawful or civilian-directed nuclear use | -15 to -25 | -20 to -35 | Strong political consequence, physical harm and nuclear Chaos remain with shared owners |
| Public reciprocal disarmament is actually completed | +5 to +8 | +6 | Requires verified debit and a real reciprocal security concession |

Momentum below 30 weakens persuasion and reduces future campaign endurance where a term has not yet been set. It does not revoke sovereignty. Cohesion below 40 opens a sixty-day reform congress. If Cohesion falls below 20 or viable membership falls below three, start one ninety-day viability clock. It runs while either condition remains true and resets only when both Cohesion is at least 20 and viable membership is at least three. Switching between failing conditions does not restart it. At expiry, members choose a reduced bilateral network or dissolution. Neither threshold causes automatic civil war.

At high Cohesion, members are more likely to pay, accept inspection and support mediation. They still reject legally impossible or suicidal choices. Cohesion does not override a nuclear owner's veto.

## Three long-term charter directions

The civic-service path concentrates on reconstruction, credible mediation and protected sovereignty. It can complete a peaceful campaign with no nuclear programme and no world authority.

The collective-defence path accepts conventional military protection, real alliance costs and disagreement about intervention. It can cooperate with civic members who remain outside the military compact.

The coercive path begins only through later nuclear and supreme-mandate choices. It trades voluntary legitimacy for central command and compulsory settlement. Members receive departure and opposition opportunities before obligations become coercive. It is never the automatic completion reward for investing in local sanitation or peaceful diplomacy.

Focus architecture allows shared practical work to connect these directions. Political commitments and actual national authority determine which late anchors remain available.

## Strong-League gate for Nuclear Pacifism

At Chaos 800 or above, a nuclear programme or congress requires a stronger organization than the founding League.

| Required fact | Proposed threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Independent active members | At least five |
| Inhabited macroregions represented | At least three |
| Cohesion | At least 60 |
| Momentum | At least 60 |
| Durable substantive success credits | At least five |
| Genuine mediated peace episodes among them | At least two |
| Paid material contributions | At least three completed in the preceding 180 days |
| Industrial capacity | At least fifteen percent of current world usable civilian and military factories held by active contributing members |

Regions follow one validated shared mapping. Moving a capital does not create a new regional voice. A member is active when it has a valid government, sovereign agency and an actual recent contribution or fulfilled charter obligation. A list of inert client states cannot substitute for a working coalition.

The industry denominator includes the world's current usable factories, with Gandhi denial applied once. It is sampled at the proposal and rechecked at completion. It is a capacity test, not a new public point meter. One large contributor can help a coalition meet the economic threshold but cannot count as several independent members.

The nuclear evolution itself additionally needs genuine national research, an actual usable device and credible delivery. Part 4 defines those gates. A research committee without a bomb is still a programme under the Peace League.

## Peaceful completion and aftermath

A mature civic League can recognize a durable peace settlement, complete its reconstruction obligations and step back from emergency mobilization. The finite original campaign stays closed. Members keep their countries, paid improvements and lawful bilateral relationships. The event retains a compact category for voluntary membership, leadership succession and unresolved obligations.

A peaceful legacy becomes a complete campaign outcome after the League has at least five active members across three regions, six durable substantive successes including three mediated peace episodes, and 180 days without member aggression or an unresolved serious charter breach. It grants the appropriate historical and achievement evidence, then removes routine pressure to escalate. It does not set the shared terminal `world_end` flag or end ordinary HOI4 play.

The League can later face a new external crisis through existing shared systems. This event does not add an endless random crisis generator simply to keep its buttons busy.
