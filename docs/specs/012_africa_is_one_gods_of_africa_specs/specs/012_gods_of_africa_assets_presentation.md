# Gods of Africa assets and presentation specification

## Visual purpose

The visual package should present a continental state doctrine that may be political, supernatural, or both. It should communicate authority, distance, judgment, and material power without presenting a false historical pan-African religion.

The strongest visual direction is a fictional institution created by the African unifier. The imagery can combine modernized 1930s and 1940s continental statecraft with impossible light, weather, sound, or monumental form. It should not build identity from a random collection of animals, masks, pyramids, tribal patterns, spears, and sacred objects.

## Source mode

The principal visuals are generated because the doctrine and its impossible manifestations are fictional.

Real historical material may inform:

- period dress and technology
- continental conference architecture
- diplomatic photography
- rail, port, industrial, and military logistics
- regional architecture when a specific scene has a clear location

Real sacred objects, deity images, ritual masks, or religious symbols require a specific source, context, and reason. They should never be combined as a universal emblem.

## Coherent design language

The fictional state should commission one visual system for the Gods.

Possible recurring elements:

- a ring or assembly of five abstract seats representing the voice roles
- a central continental stone or metal seal designed by the unifier
- radio towers, cables, drums, bells, or signal devices integrated into a state chamber
- shifting light or weather visible through the chamber
- a repeated geometric frame developed from one fictional state design
- industrial materials drawn from the unifier's actual route
- a restrained continental silhouette as a secondary mark, not the whole composition

The design language should stay coherent across category picture, icons, event art, and optional super-event art.

## Required asset inventory

### Activation report or news image

Asset type:

- full-canvas report or news image

Subject direction:

- the African unifier's new continental chamber during the first proclamation
- delegates, broadcasters, military officers, and religious or civic interpreters may be present
- the main subject is the act of speaking with one foreign voice
- one unexplained physical sign can suggest that the claim may be real

Period direction:

- 1936 to 1945 photographic technology and material culture
- period microphones, cameras, uniforms, clothing, transport, and architecture
- no modern LED screens, tactical gear, digital devices, or contemporary conference equipment

Mood direction:

- formal
- powerful
- unsettling
- controlled

Avoid:

- readable generated text
- a map table as the main subject
- a single caricatured priest or shaman
- a collage of unrelated sacred items
- modern AU branding copied into the fictional state
- generic fantasy throne-room art

### Decision category picture

Asset type:

- full-canvas category picture

Consumer:

- participant Gods of Africa decision category

Reference rule:

- inspect the canonical decision category picture references and the live consumer before final size

Composition:

- one institutional seal, chamber, or monumental communications apparatus
- clear calm-state readability at small size
- no fake controls, fake meters, or text

Optional variants:

- calm relationship
- hostile relationship
- defiance

Variants should be added only if the decision category consumer supports state changes cleanly.

### Gods of Africa Strength icon

Asset type:

- compact custom mechanic icon

Subject direction:

- credible continental reach
- linked military, industrial, and atmospheric force
- one clear silhouette

Avoid:

- simple muscle or sword icon
- direct copy of a real national emblem
- a map filled with lightning

### Wrath of the Gods icon

Asset type:

- compact custom mechanic icon

Subject direction:

- remembered offense and focused judgment
- one clear eye, seal, hand, scale, or fracture motif within the fictional design system

Avoid:

- gore
- skull pile
- generic demon face
- real sacred imagery used as a threat symbol

### Focus icons

Focus icons should be produced after the final branch architecture is accepted.

Coordinated icon families:

- proclamation and institution
- reciprocal doctrine
- extractive doctrine
- provision and logistics
- oaths and diplomacy
- protection
- judgment
- continental settlement

Each focus icon needs source art designed for focus size. Idea and decision icons cannot be derived by resizing these files.

### Achievement triplets

Every accepted achievement needs:

- eligible icon
- grey icon
- not-eligible icon

Suggested achievement directions:

- long obedience
- survive defiance
- continental provision
- careful gambler

Final IDs must match the achievement registry and root-only filename rule.

### Optional super-event image

The super-event image is required only if the global-credibility threshold is accepted and implemented.

Subject direction:

- foreign capitals receiving the same proclamation while a physically impossible but restrained sign appears
- Africa is the source of coordinated political power, not a distant exotic background
- the image should show a world-order shift through people, institutions, weather, or signal

Avoid:

- globe covered in flames
- giant fantasy deity above the continent
- a red map with arrows
- copied AU summit photography presented as the event
- modern cinematic color grading

## Animation decision

Broad animation is rejected for the base package because it would add cost without clarifying the default decision category.

One optional warning animation can be reconsidered if a dedicated scripted GUI is accepted.

Possible role:

- the Wrath frame changes from still to a slow, unstable seal pulse at the highest band

Requirements:

- separate generated source frames
- native transparency for every frame
- stable silhouette and anchor
- static fallback
- horizontal frame sheet
- GIF preview for review only
- verified `frameAnimatedSpriteType` consumer
- no transform-only, glow-filter-only, or opacity-only final motion

No animation should be created merely because the feature is supernatural.

## Super-event presentation direction

### Role

The super-event marks the first moment when foreign powers accept that the Gods' threats have global reach.

### Trigger direction

- system active
- Strength enters global reach or higher
- one-time proof absent
- Event 012 campaign active
- African unifier valid

### Title direction

- short
- specific to Africa speaking with one voice
- no generic final-crisis wording
- no invented direct quote as title

### Description direction

- show several governments receiving linked demands or observing linked consequences
- keep the source of the power unresolved
- mention Africa's new authority and the world response
- avoid listing mechanic effects

### Button direction

- brief reaction from an outside power
- can use a researched cultural allusion
- no generic acknowledgement
- no long copyrighted line

### Quote direction

- source from a verifiable Pan-African, anti-colonial, sovereignty, judgment, responsibility, unity, or power text
- exact wording and attribution must be researched
- public domain or historical political writing is preferred
- no invented proverb
- no line presented as African wisdom without a specific source

### Audio direction

- one unique licensed musical recording
- ceremonial, political, and unsettling
- possible choral, orchestral, percussion-led, or processional structure
- no generic tribal loop
- no generated drone, test tone, ambient bed, or placeholder
- composition and recording rights must be checked separately

## Localisation direction

### General voice

The Gods should sound like persistent personalities with memory.

Their language should react to:

- Strength
- participant Wrath
- demand family
- current African need
- war or peace
- occupied African territory
- prior compliance
- prior betrayal
- doctrine route
- current Chaos tier

### Low Wrath

Direction:

- ceremonial
- formal
- patient
- sometimes protective
- may acknowledge prior aid

The language can still be demanding. It should not read as a friendly trade offer.

### Medium Wrath

Direction:

- precise
- less patient
- references unresolved obligations
- makes the transfer deadline clear

### High Wrath

Direction:

- accusatory
- humiliating when the participant broke an oath
- specific about occupation, war, or refusal
- direct about consequences without exposing the exact punishment roll

### Weak Strength

Direction:

- grand claim with limited proven reach
- foreign response may be skeptical
- failure or small punishment should be described concretely
- avoid comedy based on African culture or religion

### Extreme Strength

Direction:

- restrained confidence
- fewer empty threats
- the player's knowledge of prior consequences should carry the fear
- avoid excessive apocalyptic adjectives

### Africa-side reports

Direction:

- practical summary of material received
- names reliable and hostile powers
- explains current need and next priority
- avoids debug-like lists

### Foreign news

Direction:

- shows reactions through public fear, diplomatic calculation, military preparation, or relief
- avoids staged official denial formulas
- avoids treating every country as culturally identical

## Fictional voice-role writing guide

### Keeper of Oaths

Uses:

- recognition
- treaties
- access
- withdrawal
- broken agreements

Tone:

- exact
- remembers wording and dates
- high Wrath becomes prosecutorial

### Provider

Uses:

- equipment
- fuel
- transport
- industry

Tone:

- concrete
- names shortages and deadlines
- high Wrath rejects waste and excuses

### Shield

Uses:

- emergency aid
- protection
- alliance
- African wars

Tone:

- reciprocal
- praises sacrifice
- condemns abandonment of a known obligation

### Witness

Uses:

- colonial territory
- bases
- occupation
- broken liberation promises

Tone:

- evidence-centered
- names states and actions
- avoids vague moralizing

### Hand of Judgment

Uses:

- refusal aftermath
- punishment
- defiance

Tone:

- concise
- describes what is being judged
- does not explain whether the force is divine

## Cultural review rules

Before final writing or art approval:

- check that no real religion is treated as a single continental faith
- check that no sacred object is used outside its context
- check that no symbol collage implies universal African identity
- check that no invented proverb is attributed to Africa
- check that no accent or dialect caricature appears
- check that no visual depends on colonial stereotypes
- check that the African unifier has internal political agency
- check that compliant foreign powers receive reciprocal treatment
- check that direct African violence carries normal diplomatic consequences when attributable

## Asset production handoff

Use:

- `chaosx_generated_event_art` for report, news, category, super-event, fictional emblem, and panel art
- `chaosx_icon_artist` for focus, idea, decision, mechanic, achievement, and optional animated icon assets
- `chaosx_super_event_text_researcher` for quote and cultural-line research
- `chaosx_super_event_audio_researcher` for licensed music research and final WAV preparation

No character portrait is required by this system alone. If implementation introduces a named fictional interpreter or leader, portrait work must be separately authorized and routed to `chaosx_portrait_creator`.

No custom 3D model is required by this system alone.

## Asset acceptance

Every accepted asset needs:

- stable filename
- stable proposed sprite name
- source mode
- final consumer
- target size based on the live consumer
- source PNG
- processed PNG
- final DDS
- manifest
- contact sheet when part of a family
- alpha validation for transparent families
- separate source art for separate icon types
- final runtime path outside `docs/assets`
- permanent provenance and handoff facts before temporary workspace cleanup

A missing final asset remains a blocker. Do not replace it with a recolored vanilla icon, primitive geometry, or an unrelated generated picture.
