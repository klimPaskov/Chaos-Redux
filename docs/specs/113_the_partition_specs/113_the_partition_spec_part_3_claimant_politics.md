# 113 The Partition: Claimant politics

A coalition is an agreement between countries that expect different rewards.
Its strength comes from compatible claims and credible commitments.
Its weakness is that every additional beneficiary gives the target another possible bargaining partner.

The partition coalition is a crisis agreement, with named signatories and limited purposes.
Existing factions remain the actual military institutions of the campaign.
Participation in this agreement does not automatically create a faction, replace a faction leader, transfer diplomatic control, or give one claimant the right to accept for another.

## Forming and leading the coalition

The opening leader is chosen from the eligible claimants using its proposed territorial interest, actual military ability, diplomatic independence, and the willingness of the other claimants to work with it.
Leadership is a coordinating role.
It determines who presents the first plan, proposes an ultimatum extension, and convenes a revised agreement.
Each beneficiary retains its own ratification and enforcement choices.

A human claimant is invited to participate and sees its proposed share before committing.
If it declines or lets the invitation lapse, the opening package is revised around the remaining accepted actors.
An announced crisis requires two actual claimants.
Preparation can reserve an invitation briefly, but an unanswered human invitation never becomes a binding share or a war commitment.

The coalition is presented publicly only after the opening membership and territory are feasible.
Where a human invitation prevents that, the wave can announce its other completed target crises first and give the pending package its full response window when it becomes ready.
If it fails to assemble within 15 days, that reserved package is dropped with a shortfall recorded, without replacing it after the wave has begun.

Leadership can pass to another claimant after a withdrawal or a refusal to enforce.
The remaining members must accept a legal revised proposal.
Leadership changes alone preserve the map version, while any change in beneficiaries, awards, promises, or enforcement obligations creates a new version.
The target is told who now speaks for the agreement.

## Coalition Cohesion

Cohesion explains the likelihood of coordinated action.
It is computed from the current agreement and remembered consequential conduct, then clamped to 0–100.
Reopening a panel, repeating a click, or resubmitting an identical proposal cannot increase it.

| Factor | Proposed contribution | Persistence and meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Feasible opening agreement | 55 | Base assessment while at least two claimants remain |
| Every active claimant ratified the exact current version | +10 | Removed when that version changes |
| Shares satisfy each claimant's stated minimum without overlap | +10 | Recomputed from the current proposal |
| All currently due coalition commitments honored | +10 | Requires actual completed commitments, with none overdue |
| Active unresolved overlap over a state | −15 | Removed after a real agreed allocation resolves it |
| A claimant unilaterally expands a previously agreed demand | −15 | Lasts through that bargaining round |
| A claimant withdraws after publicly committing | −10 each, maximum −20 | Crisis memory, not reversed by immediate rejoining |
| A due commitment is broken | −10 each, maximum −20 | Crisis memory tied to the actual commitment |
| A member attacks another member | Shared pact ends | Ordinary wars continue, with each target crisis reassessed |

Before any commitment is due, the honored-commitment bonus is zero.
A cheap unused pledge cannot activate it.
The permitted bands are fractured below 25, strained from 25 to below 50, workable from 50 to below 75, and united from 75 upward.
These are presentation and AI bands.
Legal choices, territory and actual military participation remain separate facts.

A fractured coalition can still negotiate.
It cannot launch a coordinated enforcement package until at least two claimants explicitly renew their participation in one current plan.
An expired ultimatum does not itself repair that lack of agreement.
If no pair renews within a 30-day final conference, the coordinated attempt ends.
Any separate conflict that remains is reported explicitly.

## Claimant action map

The six actions below are the maximum primary set in the claimant view.
Unavailable phase-specific actions are replaced by their current result or reason, rather than accumulated into a long menu.
All prices use the dynamic quote rules in Part 7.
Numbers here are proposed neutral-world anchors.

| C1 Ratify the proposed share | Current valid proposal, claimant receives a feasible share, exact terms visible | Accepts the listed territorial limit and treaty obligations | Adds this claimant's signature to that version | Material changes require a new signature, and peace still needs target consent |
| C2 Revise the share | Bargaining open, no award in progress, 15-day revision cooldown | 25 PP for a submitted material revision | Presents one alternative allocation and identifies who loses or gains | Other signatories can accept, counter, or withdraw, with cohesion affected by unilateral enlargement |

Ratifying an offer does not spend political resources on its own.
Its substantive sacrifice is the accepted limit on what the claimant receives and the commitments attached to the settlement.
Reducing a demand does not produce a refund for political lobbying already performed.
Unspent material escrow, if used by an implementation adapter, follows actual-paid refund rules.
Delivered supplies and consumed mobilization resources remain spent.

### Revising a share

The claimant chooses the states it wants from the current target's eligible territory.
The view shows existing cores and claims, adjacency, strategic interest, current proposed recipients, and the remnant consequences.
It can propose a swap or a larger demand in one submission.
A larger demand must identify what real change supports it, such as a new claimant, a protection failure, a shift in military balance, or an active evolution.
An unchanged campaign and an unchanged request cannot be submitted repeatedly merely to roll acceptance again.

The target sees the larger loss and the coalition sees the redistribution.
The revision can improve a claimant's expected gain while reducing the agreement's chance of surviving.
Claimants with weaker military positions are more inclined to accept a modest defensible border award.
A dominant claimant is more tempted to enlarge its share when the other members cannot obtain their claims independently.
Existing alliances, ideology, rivalry, actual economic value, and threatened foreign intervention all affect that judgment.

### Inviting a new claimant

A new neighbor cannot receive somebody else's signed share silently.
The invitation includes the offered territory and the existing members whose awards would shrink.
At baseline, invitations can replace a withdrawn member or fill the existing three-member maximum.
The evolved forms permit the larger maxima in Part 2.

Invitation acceptance gives the target another legitimate participant to negotiate with.
It can make the coalition stronger, but it can also increase overlap, produce incompatible faction obligations, or make the remaining country too small to satisfy the current remnant route.
Such a package must be revised before it proceeds.
Late invitations cannot shorten the target's remaining consent window below 15 days.
Once the single allowed extension has been used, an invitation that would require more time is unavailable.
The invitation route can remain available after an early rejection while this same review-window test passes.
After expiry, it can reopen negotiation only through the still-unused single 15-day extension and a concrete accepted mediation basis, before any enforcement begins.
If that extension is already consumed, an expired proposal cannot gain another invitation window.

### Withdrawing without receiving land

A claimant may leave because the proposed share is too small, protection has become credible, the target is harder to defeat than expected, or a rival is trying to dominate the agreement.
Withdrawal removes its unexecuted awards, pending authorization, and event-owned pressure against that target.
Full withdrawal closes claimant reentry for this crisis, including invitations that would otherwise arrive after the 540-day renunciation period.
A reduced demand remains an active claimant proposal and follows the ordinary revision and consent rules.
Other members receive a report of the concrete gap that now needs to be resolved.

The target can encourage this through a smaller consensual bilateral concession inside a revised all-party settlement, a protection commitment, or mediation that lowers the benefits of continued pressure.
It cannot purchase an invisible permanent loyalty value.
If a withdrawn claimant later attacks anyway through ordinary diplomacy, the historical withdrawal remains recorded and any applicable settlement-follow-through conditions fail.

### Authorizing enforcement

The authorization panel lists the target, all known attackers, likely faction and guarantee consequences, the current territorial demand, and the resources to be consumed.
Military estimates are advisory and show their uncertainty.
They never state that the target will surrender at a particular strength ratio.

Each human participant approves separately.
The engine's current diplomatic legality is checked at the moment of action.
An authorization that cannot produce legal participation expires after 15 days, giving the claimant a final choice to withdraw or continue ordinary diplomatic preparation outside the coordinated package.
The target receives notice of actual declarations and actual war entry.
An issued war goal or an AI desire to attack is displayed as preparation rather than an existing war.

The mobilization cost buys the political and logistical preparation for enforcement.
It does not create free divisions or instantly move the claimant's army to the frontier.
The AI prepares real fronts and discounts strength already committed elsewhere.
Resource costs paid for a successfully authorized preparation are consumed even if the claimant later changes its mind.
An invalid stale click charges nothing.

### A war between claimants

The dispute route begins with a public break over a real contested share or a refused agreed award.
The aggrieved country chooses whether to abandon the ambition, return to talks, or prepare a normal war against its rival.
A crisis casus belli must have a verified native representation and respect current diplomatic restrictions before it can be used.
The intention is to permit a conflict over the arrangement, with an explicit decision to attack, rather than to guarantee that every dispute can immediately produce a war.

The original target benefits from the collapse of coordinated pressure, but its survival is still checked against actual remaining enemies and ownership.
One claimant fighting another does not automatically free territory already transferred.
The resulting peace conference governs any new conquests.
Event 113 never constructs a punishment partition of a major rival or adds that rival's territory to the original target's award ledger.

Grand Partitions makes these breaks more likely when a claimant is excluded from a high-value corridor or discovers that a partner demanded the target's capital for itself.
The broader consequences can include rival coalitions, a protector intervening against a beneficiary, or the former target recovering lost states through ordinary war.
The event recognizes those outcomes without prescribing the new map.

## Agreements after a war starts

War does not freeze the coalition's internal politics.
Members may withdraw their future territorial request, seek a negotiated end, or dispute the division.
They cannot independently promise a state they do not own, end an ally's unrelated war, or take a state from a peace conference before that conference awards it.

Changing the coalition's desired terms produces a new diplomatic offer, not a hidden modification to the original consent.
The original war opening and actual belligerents remain recorded.
If a member ceases to exist, changes sides, enters a prohibited relationship, or becomes incapable of receiving its share, the offer is reassessed.
The target and surviving claimants receive a truthful map of what remains possible.

## Claimant aftermath

A beneficiary gains the territory that was actually awarded, including its existing buildings and population under ordinary state-transfer rules.
It receives no automatic core, instant compliance, free army, duplicated stockpile, or extra factory grant.
The cost of integrating a noncore borderland follows normal occupation and administration systems.

A claimant that accepted a modest compromise can retain better relations with the target and mediator for 180 days.
A claimant that broke a ratified commitment carries a 180-day diplomatic penalty with the affected signatories.
These relationship effects are directional and tied to the actual offended countries.
They are not a global moral reputation score.

An unsuccessful aggressor keeps its real war losses and diplomatic consequences.
The event-owned mobilization and claim pressure end when their purpose ends.
A humiliating report can acknowledge failed coercion without granting compensatory conquest rights or free resources.
