# 113 The Partition: Targets and territory

The event begins with a map that could plausibly tempt several neighbors.
An arbitrary list of weak countries is insufficient.
A target is selected only when its current territory supports at least two meaningful, compatible neighboring demands and a legal crisis can begin.

All numeric limits in this part are proposed balance anchors.
They require the scenario and campaign checks in the acceptance matrix before release.
They do not describe a measured distribution of the current map.

## Eligible threatened countries

A target must exist as an independent minor, use the normal civilian systems, and be outside an active civil war.
It must own and fully control its current capital and every state included in the proposed peaceful handover.
It must be at peace when a new crisis is opened.
It must have no active Partition crisis and no active Partition reselection protection.
It must have a free place under the documented shared three-local-crises limit.
The implementation must discover and use the actual shared capacity owner rather than creating a second incompatible counter.

Faction membership and existing guarantees do not provide absolute immunity.
They reduce opportunistic selection and make enforcement more dangerous.
The target's actual faction and guarantors appear in the opening assessment.
Subjects are outside this event's target pool because an independent territorial consent and protection route is required by this design.
An independence change can make them eligible for a future firing after all other tests pass.

Special Chaos countries follow the central classifier's exclusions.
The distinction between a special country and an actual nonhuman country remains intact.
Historical identity, ideology, ethnicity, and a cosmetic name are never substitutes for those classifiers.

### Proposed size bands

| Band | Physical owned states | Physical owned civilian and military factories | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small | At most 10 | At most 30 combined | Baseline and both evolutions |
| Medium | At most 20 | At most 60 combined | Partition Fever and Grand Partitions |
| Larger minor | Above either medium limit | No additional size ceiling | Grand Partitions when a feasible coalition and map exist |

Both limits must hold for a country to fit a band.
Factories mean buildings physically present in its owned states, including damaged buildings for the initial classification.
Factories obtained through occupation, trade, or subject arrangements do not change its territorial size band.
Dockyards, population, ports, and resources affect the attractiveness and consequences of an offer, rather than creating another hard size category.

The event requires at least three owned states for a baseline proposal and at least two for an evolved complete-partition proposal.
Every accepted recipient must receive at least one state.
A one-state country cannot support a division between multiple claimants and is therefore ineligible.
These are map granularity constraints, not judgments about historical national importance.

## Interested neighboring claimants

Every active claimant must exist as an independent country, be able to conduct diplomacy, and possess a direct usable land border with the target through owned territory at proposal creation.
Current major status is permitted for claimants.
Occupation of a foreign frontier alone does not establish a permanent neighboring interest.
Naval range, a shared sea zone, overseas ambition, and an inaccessible impassable boundary are insufficient.
Special straits count only where the verified campaign graph treats them as a usable land connection for the proposed route.

The claimant must have at least one eligible frontier state it can reasonably seek.
Its interest is grounded in one or more of the following facts.

| Interest | Meaning for a proposed share | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing core | Strong reason to seek that particular border territory | Creates no new cores on other awarded states |
| Existing claim | Strong diplomatic demand for a particular state | Does not prove a right to every adjacent state |
| Border security | A frontier state changes a salient, choke point, or defensible border | The map must show the actual change |
| Supply or access | A port, rail connection, supply hub, or route has concrete military value | Access must remain geographically usable after the proposed exchange |
| Industry or resources | The state contains meaningful factories, resources, or associated infrastructure | Valuable interior territory still needs an allowed connected corridor |

An ordinary neighboring state with none of these interests is a weak candidate and cannot be used solely to manufacture a second coalition member.
Claims and strategic interests rank feasible proposals.
They never bypass the target's major immunity, the remnant rule, or a beneficiary's lack of connection.

A claimant cannot be an ally or subject of the target, its active protector in this crisis, or an enemy of another active claimant at coalition formation.
A country already involved in several commitments receives a reduced willingness to join.
Within Event 113 it can hold at most three active claimant or protector commitments at once, across all batches.
One slot means one accepted actor-to-crisis membership, with claimant membership or an outside support membership as the mutually exclusive role.
An outside support membership can contain separately approved guarantee, arms, transport, mediation and intervention obligations.
Those child obligations do not consume additional membership slots.
An invited mediator counts as an outside support member once its role is accepted.
An invitation does not reserve a commitment until accepted.
This event-owned commitment limit is separate from the shared local-crisis capacity used to select threatened countries.

| Form | Active claimant range when available | Coalition behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | 2–3 | A small agreement that can break over one disputed frontier state |
| Partition Fever | 2–5 | Additional neighbors are considered more often and compete for interior access |
| Grand Partitions | 2–6 | Broad division plans can include several corridors and complete extinction |

Two is the minimum for a partition coalition.
The higher maxima are opportunities, not requirements to invent interested neighbors.
At least two claimants must remain willing to enforce for a coordinated partition war.

## Selecting a wave

Selection occurs once for the opening and again only when a material revision needs to validate its existing actors and states.
There is no recurring scan of every country to find extra victims during an active crisis.

The selection process first identifies eligible targets with feasible neighboring packages.
It then chooses a diverse set without replacement, preferring less recently troubled countries when other conditions are similar.
Targets may share claimants if the commitment and actual military burdens permit it.
Targets reserved for the same wave are removed from the claimant pool before the final packages are committed.
The final validation repeats after this removal, since an apparently feasible target can lose its only second claimant.

The geographic distribution follows available campaigns.
It does not promise one target per continent, one victim from a historical list, or a particular balance between human and AI countries.
The shared human-crisis-pressure rules affect the supported incident cadence after opening, without changing the hard target tests.

A failed candidate is replaced only during this bounded opening selection.
Once the wave is announced, a target that withdraws, becomes ineligible, or resolves its crisis leaves a historical outcome row.
Its slot is not refilled with a fresh victim.

## Building a territorial proposal

Each proposal contains an exact list of current states, their current owner, their proposed recipient, and the reason each claimant seeks them.
Each state has one recipient or remains with the target.
A target-owned state cannot appear twice, serve as payment in two crises, or be promised to a country that has withdrawn.
The capital, population and industrial consequences, current control, and major strategic assets are visible before consent.

The engine map's existing state boundaries are the indivisible units.
The event does not redraw provinces to make an attractive division fit.
This makes a small state's loss potentially more severe than its count suggests, which is why the remnant assessment includes population and industry.

### Baseline demands

Baseline claimants begin with frontier states that touch their own usable territory.
A recipient's share must be connected to that border.
An interior state cannot be smuggled into a baseline offer merely by assigning a thin chain through the target's heartland.
Each claimant ordinarily seeks one border state.
A second border state is possible where the target is near the top of the small band and the remnant tests remain comfortable.

The ordinary total demand budget is 35% of the target's owned states, rounded down, with a minimum of two states needed to give two claimants real shares.
The minimum is a coarse-map exception, not permission to ignore the remnant tests.
If two frontier awards would leave no viable remnant, that target is not eligible at baseline.
A baseline proposal can therefore remove two out of three states only when the remaining capital state independently passes the population, industry, and practical viability tests.

### The viable remnant

A baseline settlement preserves the current capital and the target's independence.
The capital's retained connected area must retain at least 50% of the opening population and at least 50% of the opening civilian and military factories.
Each awarded share must leave the retained capital component connected across the target's own retained usable states.
An existing detached enclave does not create a right to take a corridor through another country, and it does not need to become connected for this test.
It remains retained unless it independently qualifies as a legal proposed award.

For a target with no civilian or military factories, the industrial ratio is inapplicable.
It must still retain its capital, at least half its population, and its existing means of sustaining access within the capital component.
The proposal may not sever that component's only existing supply connection or take its only functioning port when maritime access is its sole practical route to the outside world.
This is assessed from current geography and facilities.
The event does not manufacture a replacement port, factory, capital, or supply hub to make a bad proposal pass.

These conditions create a weakened but playable country.
They are a floor rather than an economic guarantee against all later campaign pressures.
The before-and-after preview gives the target enough information to judge additional risks.

### Evolved demands

Partition Fever normally proposes 35–65% of the target's states.
Grand Partitions normally proposes 60–100%.
The exact offer moves within those bands according to existing claims, connected access, target defenses, protection, claimant agreement, and the physical value of the states.
State granularity can produce a percentage just outside a nominal band, but the interface always displays the actual result.

Evolved recipients may seek connected interior corridors.
Every state along a corridor must be part of that recipient's exact proposed share or already belong to it.
The corridor must lead to a specific claimed or strategic objective.
The algorithm may not bridge a gap through neutral territory, a sea zone, another claimant's unratified promise, or a state outside the target's ownership.

A proposal described as leaving a remnant still preserves the current capital and at least 25% of the opening population and industry in its retained capital component.
If those conditions cannot be met, the offer must be revised or explicitly presented as a complete partition.
An almost empty nominal survivor is not mislabeled as the ordinary remnant route.

A complete partition is available only in an evolved crisis with a recipient for every target-owned state and a connected admissible share for every recipient.
Unassignable overseas territory prevents a complete offer.
At least two beneficiaries must receive meaningful territory.
One country taking everything is annexation by a single power and falls outside this coordinated settlement route.

## Conflicting interests and allocation

The first allocation favors an existing core over a claim, a claim over an otherwise similar strategic demand, and a well-connected share over a disconnected one.
These priorities seed a proposal rather than award entitlement.
A stronger coalition member can press for more, but doing so has political and military costs and creates an opening for the target to divide the coalition.

Disputed states are explicitly marked.
They cannot be transferred until the dispute is resolved in one proposal version.
A claimant can exchange one wanted state for another feasible state, accept a smaller share for a narrower war commitment, or withdraw a disputed demand.
An exchange must remain within the current crisis's allowed demand budget and remnant rules.
Economic compensation from a third party requires an actual approved transfer and cannot be invented as a generic balancing credit.

Proposal value is a derived comparison of physical population, factories, resources, and strategic access.
The interface presents the components so that a composite rank cannot conceal the loss of a capital, port, or critical route.
No public territorial-value currency can be accumulated or spent.

## Changes while diplomacy is active

A change in state ownership, target identity, capital location, coalition membership, major status, or a relevant treaty can invalidate a proposal.
The interface marks affected states and disables ratification until a replacement version is prepared.
The replacement never silently selects a different state or recipient.

If the target becomes a major, every Event 113 demand, award, escalation choice, and pending participant popup concerning that target is retired.
Any ordinary war already started continues under the game's war rules.
The crisis closes as invalidated by a change of status and cannot count as successful defense, a successful settlement, or a failed defense for Tasks.

If the target enters an unrelated war before enforcement, the existing ultimatum is suspended and rechecked.
Its fixed due date continues, and the suspension does not consume or create an extra extension.
Negotiation may resume only before that due date when the proposed awards, legal participant set and required 15-day review window remain valid.
A still-unused ordinary extension can supply that review window under its existing concrete-progress rule.
If resumption is impossible by the deadline, the unexecuted diplomatic crisis closes as external invalidation with no implied consent, authored enforcement, successful defense, or successful settlement.
That closure releases the wave's diplomatic lock and leaves any unrelated native war untouched.
A Task whose target is actually extinguished or subjected by the outside war still fails under Part 9's real-outcome rule.
A still-living independent target receives neutral Task cancellation when only the external-war invalidation closes the contract.
An actor that initiated the unrelated war receives failure for its own attached objective instead of using that voluntary action to obtain neutral cancellation.
A third party taking a demanded state never entitles the original claimants to take a substitute.
The war and interruption rules in Part 5 determine whether the existing crisis can still resolve cleanly.

## Geographic acceptance cases

| Case | Required design result |
| --- | --- |
| Three-state minor with a populous industrial capital and two poor border states | Eligible at baseline only if both neighbors have real interests and every remnant test passes |
| Three-state minor whose two demanded states contain most industry | Baseline package rejected or demands reduced, even if the capital would survive |
| Two-state minor with two neighboring claimants | Baseline rejected, evolved complete proposal possible if each state has an admissible recipient |
| Inland minor with a distant overseas claimant | Distant country can protect or mediate, but cannot enter as a territorial claimant |
| Minor inside a powerful faction | May be selected, with actual defensive commitments strongly reducing escalation willingness |
| Large Poland still classified as a minor | May enter Grand Partitions if current borders and interested neighbors support a feasible package |
| Small Poland currently classified as a major | Always excluded as a target |
| Target with an overseas state no claimant can validly receive | Complete partition unavailable, limited feasible proposal still possible |
| Proposed border recipient loses its frontier before ratification | Proposal becomes stale and requires a new valid version |
