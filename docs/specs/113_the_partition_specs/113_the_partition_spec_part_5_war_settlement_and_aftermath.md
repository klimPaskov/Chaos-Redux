# 113 The Partition: War, settlement, and aftermath

The resolution must be visible on the actual campaign map.
A signature can authorize a transfer, a declaration can begin a war, and a capitulation can change control.
Each is a different event.
The Partition records success only when the world has reached the outcome it describes.

## Ratified territorial handover

All necessary parties first approve one exact complete settlement.
The agreement includes every state, recipient, retained capital condition, treaty obligation, delivery commitment, and the timing of the awards.
No award begins while a required signature is missing.
Material changes close the old ratification and require fresh consent.

The proposed handover lasts 10 days.
Ordinary frontier awards begin on day 5.
Remaining awards complete on day 10, with the target's capital last in an explicitly accepted complete partition.
The schedule groups related connected awards so that the intermediate result remains understandable.
The same exact state list is used for the preview, agreement, schedule, and result.

Before each award, the remaining agreement is rechecked against current ownership, control, target status, recipient status, capital safety, active wars, and treaty authority.
The recipient must actually receive the state before the award is recorded as complete.
An already recorded award cannot occur again after a duplicate callback or save reload.
The process cannot draw replacement states from a generic pool.

### Interrupted handover

An unrelated occupation, a newly invalid recipient, a major promotion, a civil-war identity change, or a broken treaty can stop the remaining schedule.
The target and signatories see three explicit lists: awarded states, pending states, and the reason further awards stopped.
Already completed lawful transfers remain actual history.
The event does not promise an automatic reversal of territory, troops, equipment, diplomacy, or native peace effects.

The parties may negotiate a new agreement over the remaining valid territory.
That new version must explicitly account for the current map and obtain new consent.
It cannot charge an already paid component again or describe the original plan as fully completed.
If the target is now a major, the event retires its remaining territorial authority instead of negotiating another partition against it.

An interruption before any award closes as an unexecuted settlement.
An interruption after at least one award closes or resumes as a partial handover.
These results remain distinct for Tasks, achievements, aftermath, and Chaos accounting.

### Extinction and the final award

A complete partition requires every currently owned state in the ratified version to be assigned and at least two beneficiaries to receive territory.
If the target gains another state before completion, the original complete-partition description becomes stale.
The new territory cannot be taken without a new agreement.

The last-state award is observed from a surviving participant as well as the target's record, so disappearance of the target cannot erase the outcome.
The result distinguishes loss of all owned territory, native annexation, continued government-in-exile behavior, and actual country extinction where the engine treats them differently.
Presentation must use the verified outcome.
A government continuing in exile is not falsely reported as a vanished political identity.

## A war over the target

The original ultimatum and later valid demand revisions remain a record of why the war began.
Actual declarations and actual defensive entries establish the belligerents.
Prepared war goals, commitments, and AI strategies remain preparatory facts until a war relationship exists.

The target uses its real army, allies, defenses, and supply network.
Claimants use their real forces and confront their existing commitments on other fronts.
Protectors can provide actual equipment and legally enter the defense.
No side receives a victory or capitulation from Coalition Cohesion, a timer, a probability draw, or an arbitrary division-ratio threshold.

The crisis maintains a list of verified enforcement participants and subsequent relevant entries.
This list exists to interpret the outcome and stop stale demands.
It does not establish exclusive control over the native war object.
When allies join or wars merge, the interface identifies the conflict as part of a broader war.
The ordinary game determines combat, capitulation, occupation, and peace-conference awards.

### Negotiation during war

The target and claimants can propose a ceasefire and a territorial compromise while fighting.
Every participant whose native war relationship would be affected must be accounted for.
A narrow authored peace is available only after the implementation proves that its native effect cannot terminate or alter unrelated wars or uninvolved parties.
The existing Event 009 White Peace helper is not assumed to supply that proof.
Its source applies to much narrower endpoint conditions and records application without independently confirming every resulting peace relationship.

When a safe narrow settlement is unavailable, the offer becomes a statement of desired terms for ordinary diplomacy or the native peace conference.
The event still permits the player to seek the outcome.
It does not execute a broad white peace and present it as a local settlement.
Any completed native peace is subsequently checked against actual ownership and the accepted sovereignty conditions.

### A claimant leaves the war

Withdrawal from the coalition removes future Event 113 demands and commitments.
It cannot remove a country from a native war merely because a private flag was cleared.
The claimant must actually reach peace or follow an existing legal exit route.
The target's result remains pending while a verified crisis aggressor continues hostilities.

A claimant's capitulation can make enforcement collapse, but another claimant may remain at war.
The event therefore rechecks all relevant relationships.
It does not grant a defender victory because the coalition leader alone capitulated.

### A war between claimants

If claimants attack one another, the shared division agreement ends.
Unexecuted awards lose their authority, pending signatures are retired, and each affected target crisis is reassessed.
The target may achieve full prevention if every claimant also ends aggression toward it and its opening territory remains intact.
It may instead remain at war with one claimant, suffer a completed earlier loss, or join an ordinary conflict of its own.

The rival war can erase the coalition, change the identity of a beneficiary, or let the target recover lost territory.
Its peace conference remains responsible for the resulting map.
An Event 113 major claimant remains immune from being turned into a new partition target or subjected to a scripted punishment division.

## Outcome definitions

The public report gives the most specific proven result and preserves relevant qualifiers.
The same definitions govern the mission and achievement predicates.

| Outcome | Required world facts | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full prevention by diplomacy | Target exists independently, retains every opening owned state and full control of its capital and threatened states, every claimant withdrew or accepted a no-transfer closure, no enforcement war or award remains | This attempted partition was prevented entirely |
| Full prevention by defense | The same territorial and sovereignty conditions, plus every verified crisis aggressor has actually ended hostilities and every outstanding event demand is retired | The target defeated the attempted partition through real defense |
| Protected prevention | A full-prevention result with at least one actual fulfilled protection commitment contributing before resolution | The country survives intact with a recorded protector contribution |
| Limited settlement executed | Every agreed limited award and due treaty promise is complete, target remains independent, its retained capital component meets the applicable remnant floor | A weakened country remains after a completed concession |
| Durable limited settlement | Limited settlement executed, followed by 45 days without broken agreed conditions or renewed aggression by a signatory | The compromise has held long enough for the settlement Task and containment consequence |
| Interrupted settlement | At least one award occurred, but the remaining ratified schedule cannot finish | The map changed, but the promised settlement was not completed |
| War settlement with surviving target | Native peace completed relevant aggression, target survives, actual ownership differs from full prevention or the original peaceful offer | The outcome is reported from the native settlement, including any unplanned loss |
| Complete partition | Target has lost all owned territory through a ratified complete agreement or actual peace outcome, with at least two recorded claimant beneficiaries receiving territory | The target's territory has been divided, with native extinction or exile status stated accurately |
| Single-power conquest | One power takes all of the target's territory | The defense failed, with an annexation outcome distinct from a joint partition |
| Coalition collapse with continuing war | Shared agreement ended but verified hostilities remain | Coordinated partition failed politically, while the target still needs to survive the war |
| Survival with outside territorial changes | Claimant pressure ends but the target lacks an opening state or sovereignty condition because of other campaign events | The report identifies survival and outside changes without awarding full-prevention credit |
| Neutral invalidation | Target becomes a major, transforms into an unresolvable new actor, or a verified technical authority condition removes the objective | Pending event authority is retired without a victory or failure reward |

The threatened-state set used for complete prevention is the union of the opening demands and valid later demands against that same target.
All opening owned states must still belong to the target for the full-prevention outcome.
Actual military control is checked for the capital and those threatened states.
An unrelated loss of territory prevents the strongest success claim even if the original coalition withdrew.
The more specific outside-change outcome explains what happened.

Losing territory in a peace conference is not automatically an Event 113 complete partition.
At least two recorded claimant beneficiaries must actually receive land from that target, and the target must have no owned states left.
An unrelated third party's annexation is recorded as an external conquest and is not counted toward a Grand Partitions spectacle or claimant achievement.

## Recognition and durable outcomes

Immediate reports describe the actual resolution.
Durable-settlement rewards wait for the 45-day follow-through period.
This period watches only the concluded agreement and its registered participants.
It does not restart the entire crisis or create a new target selection.

A signatory's renewed attack on the target during this period breaks the settlement even if it uses an ordinary war goal.
The event records the breach, ends pending settlement-success credit, and permits the target to seek existing protection.
It does not recreate the old territorial consent or automatically return the map.

Foreign protection remains a real diplomatic fact after the event ends where the native treaty continues.
Finite event preparation effects expire according to their own dates.
The crisis's historical record remains available for the event log and for an already attached Task's one-time outcome consumption.
It does not keep active choices or recurring effects alive indefinitely.

## Aftermath choices

Aftermath uses the same event category and a small phase-specific set.
It replaces the old ultimatum actions.
The following options are designed for distinct outcomes rather than a store of interchangeable rewards.

### Rebuild the retained country

A surviving target with damaged existing civilian or military factories, infrastructure, or relevant ports can begin a 180-day reconstruction mission.
Beginning the program has a base cost of 25 PP, 100 support equipment, and 5 trains.
The equipment and train components use Part 7's bounded material scale before the universal-cost quote, while the PP component keeps its base anchor.
It provides +10% repair speed for 180 days through a single finite rebuilding spirit.
The program does not create buildings or count undamaged new construction as repaired loss.

At activation, the mission names up to five damaged existing facilities in retained states, prioritizing the capital component and the actual damaged routes that matter to recovery.
Success requires those facilities to be repaired, the target to remain independent with its retained capital, and any agreed settlement to remain unbroken.
Only damage present in the activation snapshot counts toward the named repair goal, and restoration must occur after activation.
An already queued repair may qualify when it actually restores that damage after activation.
The mission completes automatically.
Its reward is the actual restored facilities and the completed recovery record.
If no relevant damage exists, the reconstruction mission is absent and a concise recovery report takes its place.

### Reconcile the border settlement

A surviving target and a beneficiary may each choose a diplomatic reconciliation after a durable limited settlement.
Each pays 25 PP for its own initiative.
Mutual acceptance grants +25 bilateral opinion for 180 days and records the settlement as voluntarily maintained.
It does not remove existing cores, grant new ones, waive ordinary future diplomacy, or turn noncore land into core land.

An offended target may instead retain a diplomatic grievance, affecting its willingness to cooperate with the beneficiaries and making ordinary recovery of lost claims more attractive to its AI.
That stance has no instant free war goal or army.
The strategic consequence comes from the actual lost territory and normal campaign diplomacy.

### Honor a fulfilled protector

The target can acknowledge a protector that actually completed a guarantee, credited material delivery, successful mediation, or defensive war entry.
Acknowledgment is a report and +15 target opinion of that fulfilled protector for 180 days.
It occurs once per protector and crisis.
An existing guarantee with no new action can still explain deterrence in the history, but it does not generate a new paid-action contribution receipt.

### Reassess a failed coalition

Surviving claimants receive a result-specific report after a failed attempt.
Unfulfilled pressure and mobilization plans are retired.
A member that kept its commitments can retain relations with cooperative partners, while a member that abandoned an agreed award or attacked a partner receives the relevant directional penalty.
Two claimant partners that fulfilled every due mutual commitment receive +15 bilateral opinion for 180 days after their concluded agreement.
A claimant that broke a ratified obligation receives −25 opinion from each directly harmed signatory for 180 days, once per affected pair and crisis.
These finite event-owned modifiers do not replace existing native treaty or war relationship effects.
The target's event-owned reselection protection gives it time to recover.

## Civilian consequences

Changing a state's owner does not by itself move, kill, duplicate, or erase its population.
Native occupation, damaged infrastructure, supply disruption, real fighting, and existing civilian-crisis systems determine the physical conditions.
The partition's reports can acknowledge those consequences where the campaign provides evidence.

If disrupted routes create actual famine or displacement under the shared systems, the appropriate owners handle relief, migration, population transactions, and Deaths accounting.
The Partition can link the affected player to those existing responses.
It does not submit arbitrary casualty totals, synthetic refugees, or condemnation simply because a border changed.

## Persistence and cleanup

Every target crisis, proposal, signature, award, payment, and Task attachment belongs to an immutable identity.
An open popup, selected row, country name, or reused tag cannot substitute for that identity.
Queued work rechecks the exact actor and proposal before changing the world.

Save and reload preserve due dates, completed awards, pending work, and already consumed outcomes.
The event repairs only its registered active records through a verified existing scheduling or lifecycle route.
The documented startup hook is not treated as a save-load hook.
Loss of the target must leave a surviving observer able to complete cleanup and report actual outcomes.

Closing a crisis removes its own pending requests, temporary military plans, reservations, and shared capacity claim once.
It preserves native wars, completed territory changes, preexisting claims, preexisting guarantees, and separately earned finite aftermath effects.
A restored country or a reused dynamic identity receives a fresh crisis identity if selected in the future.
