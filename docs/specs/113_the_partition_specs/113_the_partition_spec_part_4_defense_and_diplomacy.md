# 113 The Partition: Defense and diplomacy

The threatened country needs several credible ways to survive.
Negotiation can preserve its most important states, foreign commitments can change the balance, and preparation can make refusal a rational gamble.
Each route leaves consequences that continue after the opening ultimatum.

## The target's primary choices

The target sees six primary choices during the diplomatic phase.
The same persistent dossier holds the exact proposal, the map preview, and pending responses.
It does not require a sequence of popups to reconstruct what has been offered.

| Working action | Availability | Cost or sacrifice | Immediate consequence | Longer result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 Submit a counterproposal | A valid active demand, before handover or actual enforcement | 25 PP for a material submitted offer | Sends an exact smaller or redistributed allocation for consideration | Can produce a survivable settlement, split the coalition, or be refused |
| T2 Seek foreign protection | At least one eligible outside country, no unanswered protection request | 25 PP for a formal request | Presents guarantee, aid, and mediation needs to the selected country | Outcome depends on the protector's actual accepted and completed commitment |
| T3 Prepare emergency defense | Target controls its capital, preparations not already active | 25 Army XP, 250 support equipment, 5 stability points | Starts a finite defensive preparation effort | Improves entrenchment preparation while creating a domestic and material burden |
| T4 Secure supply routes | Threatened retained area and a current supply need, no duplicate active preparation | 25 CP, 5 trains, 2,500 fuel | Starts a logistics preparation effort for the existing army | Gives a modest temporary supply benefit, without spawning or teleporting units |
| T5 Ratify the exact settlement | All required terms visible, target still eligible, viable limited offer or explicit evolved complete offer | The listed states and treaty commitments, paid only through the actual handover | Adds target consent to the current version | Handover begins only when every required party has ratified |
| T6 Reject the ultimatum | An active unexecuted proposal | Accepts the risk of claimant enforcement and preserves all territorial consent rights | Ends consideration of that version and notifies the claimants | Opens the defense, renewed mediation, or withdrawal outcomes |

At baseline, the ordinary affordable path should be counterproposal or selective preparation, not purchasing every available measure.
The evolved forms can justify both defensive measures when the country has the resources.
They do not raise the number of primary actions.

### A counterproposal with a reason

The target can preserve its capital area, replace an economically vital border state with a less damaging feasible state, reduce the number of claimants, or offer an arrangement that satisfies an existing claim while refusing opportunistic additions.
Every proposed replacement still needs an interested adjoining recipient.
The target cannot offer land belonging to another country or erase a beneficiary from the agreement while retaining that beneficiary's signature.

The submission explains which physical consequence it avoids.
For example, the target may preserve its only functioning port, keep a rail connection inside its retained territory, or retain most of its military industry.
This gives the counterproposal a concrete political argument and informs AI evaluation.
It does not guarantee acceptance from an aggressive claimant that values the refused state more highly.

A target may submit another material offer after 15 days or after an outside change makes the old offer obsolete.
The single 15-day ultimatum extension requires at least one claimant to accept the revised basis or a protector to undertake a concrete mediation commitment.
Repeated rejected unchanged offers do not extend the deadline.

### Emergency defense

The preparation spends existing supplies and training capacity and accepts a visible domestic disruption.
Its proposed benefit is +5 maximum entrenchment and +10% entrenchment speed for 120 days.
The effect applies through one event-owned timed spirit with a clear source and end date.
The benefit ends 30 days after the crisis resolves if that is sooner.
It does not stack with another copy of the same Partition preparation.

The country still has to deploy and supply its real divisions.
An empty border does not become defended because the spirit exists.
A country with insufficient resources sees the exact shortfall and can pursue a cheaper diplomatic path or actual foreign aid.
The AI does not exhaust the equipment needed by its field army merely to obtain the preparation.

### Supply preparation

This action commits transport and fuel to existing military preparations.
Its proposed benefit is −5% army supply consumption for 120 days, ending 30 days after resolution if sooner.
It is a separate route because a country with a difficult border may need logistics more than extra entrenchment.
The benefit is deliberately modest and cannot stack repeatedly through multiple Partition crises.

The spent resources represent consumed preparation and allocation costs.
They do not create new trains, railways, supply nodes, or stockpiles elsewhere.
The planned modifier and its exact native scope must be verified during implementation.
If the current engine cannot express the accepted benefit, the implementation must report the gap and return to the design rather than substitute an unrelated economy bonus.

### Accepting a limited loss

The acceptance view leads with the exact lost states and the retained capital area.
It includes actual population and factories retained, strategic facilities lost, recipients, treaty promises, the handover schedule, and whether every other necessary signature has arrived.
The target can inspect the full state list before choosing.

Consent is specific to the displayed version.
A later demand for more territory cannot inherit it.
If a proposed award becomes illegal or changes owner, the target receives a new offer rather than an automatic substitute.
The territorial sacrifice is the principal price of the concession route.
An additional fixed political-power tax is unnecessary.

### Accepting an evolved complete partition

The interface explicitly shows that the target will lose every owned state and cease to exist after the final award.
It identifies every beneficiary and the disposition of the current capital.
This choice requires a separate final confirmation from a human target and every material treaty provider.
The default option cancels the confirmation and preserves the current position.

The final confirmation must explain the verified native player outcome, including country extinction and any effect on continued player control, before this route can be enabled for human targets.
If that outcome cannot be established in the current installation, voluntary complete partition remains unavailable for human targets while the limited-concession and defense routes remain playable.
AI voluntary extinction has zero weight unless a separately reviewed exceptional campaign case demonstrates why accepting is more coherent than refusal.
Complete partitions will usually arise through actual military defeat.

## Choosing a protector

The candidate list favors an existing guarantor or faction partner, a nearby capable country, a rival of the claimants, or a power with a real strategic reason to prevent the division.
The candidate must exist and be able to perform the offered action under current diplomatic rules.
A distant state may provide diplomacy or material aid, while a promise of direct intervention also needs a credible military route.

A target may hold one unanswered protection request at a time and at most two accepted outside protection commitments.
The two-commitment limit means two distinct accepted outside provider relationships in this crisis.
Each provider may fulfill multiple separately approved obligation kinds inside that relationship, including aid, mediation and intervention, without occupying another provider slot.
Adding a delivery to an existing provider relationship still needs its own legal, affordable approval and actual delivery receipt.
It chooses which commitment to seek before the request is sent.
The request expires after 15 days.
A popup timeout declines that new commitment while leaving existing treaties untouched.
Declining one request does not impose a lasting immunity against receiving a different later request after circumstances materially change.

The foreign government sees the threatened states, target status, coalition, existing promises, time remaining, likely wider war, and exact proposed cost.
It can offer less than requested.
The target then decides whether that narrower offer is useful.

## Protector and mediator choices

| Working action | What it commits | Cost and limits | Proof of completion | Strategic tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 Guarantee independence | A native guarantee when currently legal | Uses the actual native diplomatic cost and restrictions | The current guarantee relationship exists | Deterrence can also expose the protector to war |
| P2 Deliver emergency arms | A concrete equipment delivery | Anchor package 500 infantry equipment and 100 support equipment, scaled and quoted | Recipient receives the quantities actually debited from the donor | Strengthens a real defense while reducing the donor's reserve |
| P3 Deliver transport support | A concrete logistics delivery | Anchor package 5 trains and 2,500 fuel, scaled and quoted | Actual donor debit and matching recipient credit | Useful for a difficult front but cannot replace troops or a treaty |
| P4 Mediate a reduced settlement | Diplomatic work on one target-invited exact proposal | 50 PP and 25 CP for a formal mediation round | At least one material improvement is ratified and the final agreed outcome is completed | Preserves influence through a compromise the target still must accept |
| P5 Commit to defensive intervention | A pledge to enter the target's defense when the legal conditions arise | 25 CP for preparation, followed by actual war costs | Actual legal defensive war participation, not the pledge flag alone | Risks widening the war and leaves the target exposed if the pledge cannot be honored |
| P6 Withdraw or narrow the commitment | Ends an unperformed event-owned promise or requests a revised one | Loses applicable trust, with refund only for verified unspent material | Updated commitment and any actual native diplomatic change | Frees resources at the cost of credibility and possible coalition escalation |

The role view offers at most six primary entries.
Arms and transport are alternatives within the single delivery action, leaving five primary groups for guarantee, delivery, mediation, intervention, and withdrawal.
The implementation must keep both delivery packages directly comparable rather than concealing a large submenu of variants.

### Material delivery

The delivery amount is scaled from the target's physical economy within the bounded rules in Part 7.
The donor reviews and approves the final quantities.
Equipment and fuel received must equal the actual quantities paid by the donor after applicable cost adjustments.
A discount cannot create equipment from nothing, and an aborted transfer cannot credit the recipient.

Delivery is immediate only if the verified transfer adapter can debit and credit safely in the accepted conditions.
Where delivery needs a staged route, the UI distinguishes reserved, dispatched, delivered, interrupted, and refunded quantities.
The default design uses a direct diplomatic stockpile transfer, with a valid friendly route required for plausibility.
It does not pretend to model ship-by-ship losses or invent civilian deaths when a route closes.
If no safe recipient-credit adapter exists, this action is an implementation blocker with a concrete material-transfer contract to satisfy.

At most one arms package and one transport package may count as a credited contribution from the same donor to the same crisis.
Additional ordinary lend-lease remains ordinary campaign assistance.
Repeated transfers of the same supplies back and forth cannot accumulate event contribution or achievements.

### Guarantees and intervention

An existing guarantee is displayed as an existing commitment.
Requesting it again produces no extra cost, influence, protection strength, or Task credit.
A new guarantee is credited only after the native relationship actually exists.
The event does not remove a preexisting guarantee during cleanup.

An intervention pledge states the legal route expected to bring the protector into the war, such as an applicable defensive guarantee or faction relationship.
The player must be told if that route is currently uncertain.
If the route becomes illegal or impossible, the pledge becomes unavailable or unfulfilled and the target is notified.
An AI desire to join does not satisfy it.

A human protector still explicitly accepts actual war entry where the native diplomatic flow requires it.
An actual war entry can affect the coalition's willingness to continue and can contribute to a defense Task.
It does not guarantee that the target's military position improves enough to survive.

### Mediation

Mediation is a way to assemble a less destructive agreement, not a button that bypasses claimant refusal.
The target invites the mediator and remains a required signatory.
The mediator proposes a valid allocation that preserves more of the target's population, industry, or strategic access, or removes a claimant whose participation is unnecessary for a feasible agreement.
Every changed beneficiary must consent again.

The mediator can link its own guarantee or material support to the proposed settlement.
Those promises are separately visible and require actual execution.
It cannot trade another country's resources, remove another country's guarantee, or commit another country's army.
One mediation round can extend an ultimatum only through the single extension already allowed by the crisis.

An offer made after a war has widened must satisfy the war settlement conditions in Part 5.
The mediator cannot settle a bilateral relationship while claiming to have ended a larger war.
The cost of an honest failed mediation is political effort spent, not a fictional success reward.

## Ordinary missions

| Working mission | Owner | Proposed time | Success | Expiry or cancellation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 Answer the partition ultimatum | Target | 60 days baseline, 45 days evolved | Final ratification, explicit refusal, or complete withdrawal of the demand | Expiry closes that proposal and opens claimant enforcement choices, with no territorial consent |
| M2 Answer a protection request | Invited outside country | 15 days | An explicit accepted commitment is recorded | New request declined, existing diplomacy preserved |
| M3 Complete the agreed handover | Target, with one crisis record and recipient status mirrors | 10 days, with posted installments | Every agreed award and due promise actually completed | Interruption freezes pending awards and reports the actual partial state |
| M4 Rebuild the retained country | Surviving target after a damaging settlement or defense | 180 days | Capital retained, required existing damaged facilities repaired, and no broken settlement condition | No reward for unfinished work, completed real repairs remain |

The first three are short emergency or execution windows with explicit reasons.
A counterproposal, protection request, or mediation submission keeps the ultimatum mission and its original deadline active until one of M1's final responses occurs.
If the target ratifies before other required signatories, the response mission closes while the same deadline remains visible on the unresolved crisis dossier.
The reconstruction mission is a normal longer goal.
Goal missions complete automatically when their real conditions are satisfied.
They never require a second paid click to acknowledge success.
Mission expiry, cancellation, completion, and target loss must resolve once even when they occur on the same date.
