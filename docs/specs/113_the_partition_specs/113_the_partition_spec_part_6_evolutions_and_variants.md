# 113 The Partition: Evolutions and variants

Chaos changes the ambition of the coalitions and the number of countries threatened by a new firing.
It does not remove the need for neighboring interests, actual signatures, legal war participation, or a real outcome.
The two evolutions increase the scale of the same diplomatic crisis while opening distinct failure and survival routes.

## Evolution entry and persistence

Every firing chooses its target quota from the highest opening form currently eligible at Chaos 1, 200, or 600.
That quota is fixed for the life of the firing.
A new Grand Partitions firing therefore requests six to eight new eligible targets.
Earlier resolved countries are not counted toward that new quota.

An already active batch can evolve after the world crosses a threshold.
Its unresolved crises gain the new bargaining and enforcement possibilities, while its original number of target slots remains fixed.
Resolved crises and accepted handovers keep their concluded terms.
An evolution cannot insert a new beneficiary into a ratified agreement or replace a country that already escaped.

An active evolution has a proposed base MTTH of 90 days while its threshold and a meaningful unresolved crisis both exist.
This is a waiting-time distribution, not a guaranteed date.
Sustained compatible claimant ambition makes it faster, while a fractured coalition or credible outside containment makes it slower.
The proposed dynamic band is 45–180 days.
The probability audit must verify the actual adapter and timing distribution.

The two active stages proceed in order.
If Chaos rapidly rises from below 200 to above 600, the active batch first acquires Partition Fever and can then acquire Grand Partitions through its own eligible active transition.
A first firing at 600+ can open directly as Grand Partitions.
Its report identifies that evolved opening without pretending an earlier active stage occurred.

An activated form persists within that batch even if Chaos later falls.
Falling Chaos can reduce further escalation willingness and supports compromise.
Later new firings use their own current Chaos eligibility.
Historical evolution memory does not override the user's 200+ and 600+ requirements for those new openings.

Evolution eligibility, activation, and logging add zero Chaos.
Actual new coercion, territorial loss, broken settlements, wars, and successful containment use the consequence map in Part 7.
Ordinary negotiation, handover, and aftermath phases are not recorded as evolutions.

## Evolution I: Partition Fever

**Chaos requirement: 200+.**

New firings request four or five small or medium minors.
Each target can attract as many as five interested neighboring claimants where its geography supports them.
The opening ultimatum is 45 days, and the normal demand band rises to 35–65% of the target's states.
Existing claims remain important, but connected interior routes and strategic assets become stronger incentives.

### What changes in active crises

Unresolved coalitions may invite more eligible neighbors, submit larger connected demands, and propose a complete partition where every state can receive a valid award.
The target sees the revised map, the new signatories, the increased loss, and the new risk to its continued existence.
All material changes require new consent.
The original deadline is never shortened, and a late proposal needs at least 15 days of review within the crisis's one permitted extension.
If enough review time cannot be provided, that larger offer waits for an ordinary later negotiation opportunity rather than being imposed at expiry.

The existing six-action role views remain intact.
The new content appears as additional legal states, recipients, and proposal modes within those choices.
Defense and protection remain available.
The evolution introduces no automatic territorial transfer or compulsory war.

### New sources of pressure

Additional neighbors may want the same railway, resource belt, or border city.
A larger target can satisfy more claimants while still surviving, but its valuable interior is harder to divide into usable shares.
Claimants are more willing to accept a smaller immediate gain when a protector creates a credible risk of a larger war.
They are also more likely to fight over a partner's unilateral expansion of the plan.

The target can exploit the difference between a claimant seeking one core state and a claimant seeking the country's industrial heartland.
A mediator may assemble a limited agreement around the narrower claims.
Accepting such an agreement preserves a functioning country but leaves a material cost in factories, population, access, or defensible borders.

### Complete-partition possibility

A complete offer is a distinct disclosed mode.
It requires all current owned states to be legally assignable, at least two beneficiaries, and an explicit extinction warning for the target.
A partially impossible map remains a limited offer or fails validation.
The prospect of complete loss makes foreign protection and defense more attractive, especially when peaceful acceptance would end the target's play.

The AI normally resists voluntary extinction.
The primary route to a full partition remains actual military defeat and a native peace outcome that divides the territory.
The evolution increases the possibility without guaranteeing it.

### Containment and aftermath

The event recognizes full prevention, a durable limited settlement, a coalition split, and a successful defense as real containment outcomes.
Adding more claimants can make deterrence harder but gives the target more opportunities to make their interests incompatible.
Survivors can rebuild through the same material aftermath routes.
A successful settlement does not create a permanent universal peace agreement between every country in the wave.

## Evolution II: Grand Partitions

**Chaos requirement: 600+.**

New firings request six to eight minors, including larger countries that pass all current eligibility tests.
The size ceiling is removed for minors, and as many as six neighboring claimants may participate in a feasible coalition.
The ordinary demand band is 60–100% of the target's territory.
Major powers remain immune as targets.

### What changes in active crises

Unresolved coalitions may seek broader interior shares and the target's complete territorial elimination.
Large awards make the identity of the capital's recipient, the continuity of supply routes, and the allocation of industrial regions central questions.
The target's protector must weigh a real regional war rather than a symbolic diplomatic objection.

The demand map still needs a connected admissible share for every beneficiary.
No claimant obtains faraway islands or a disconnected interior region simply because the tier is high.
The target can still refuse, counter, seek protection, and defend successfully.
An evolution neither replaces its army nor changes it into an automatic loser.

### Larger wars

The wider wave can put the same major on several threatened frontiers.
It may have to choose which target to protect, which demand to support, and which coalition to abandon.
Event-owned commitment limits and AI military-burden estimates make these tradeoffs visible.
One army cannot be assumed fully available for every promised front.

Wars from different target crises may merge through real factions and shared belligerents.
The event preserves each target's origin, demands, and outcome even when the native war becomes a larger conflict.
A ceasefire request for one target cannot terminate the other targets' fighting without an actual legal and consensual settlement.

### A successful defense remains possible

A larger minor can use defensible geography, existing industry, good supply, a reliable ally, or disagreements among the attackers.
The event treats a coalition's military collapse as a possible outcome, not an error to compensate with new enemy units.
Full prevention requires actual preservation of the target's opening territory and an end to the relevant aggression.
Holding a capital for a short time while the partition war continues remains ongoing resistance.

### A rare public reckoning

A single campaign-wide outcome super-event is planned for an exceptional Grand Partitions result.
It becomes eligible only after at least two targets from the same Grand Partitions opening batch have actually undergone complete partition, at least ten of their states have been awarded in total, and at least one major participated as a claimant or actual protector.
The second qualifying partition must occur within 180 days of the first.
At least two independent claimant beneficiaries must exist in the qualifying results.

The presentation commemorates a demonstrated collapse of the regional border order.
It is not triggered by crossing 600 Chaos, opening the wave, receiving a proposal, or merely capitulating a target.
Single-power annexations and unrelated third-party conquests do not count.
The image, text, and audio direction are in Part 8 and the separate research prompt.

## Campaign variants

Variants are conditional developments inside the ordinary decision map.
They do not each require another public meter or a separate category.
Their rarity comes from the specified campaign facts and the participants' actual choices.
They are event-driven branches rather than an additional random incident lottery.
Several can describe the same crisis, but each material transition and consequence is resolved once.
Mandatory reactions to lost territory, broken protection, or merged wars cannot be skipped because an optional flavor weight failed.

### V1 The disputed junction

This variant requires two claimants to demand the same actual rail connection, supply hub, port, or resource-bearing frontier state.
The first visible change is the contested-state marker and an explanation of the asset each country wants.
The target can propose a different feasible border share, invite mediation, or hold firm while the claimants negotiate.
The claimants can trade territory within a revised map, accept a smaller share, withdraw, or pursue the dispute route after the required failed talks.

Resolution changes the real allocation and Cohesion only when the conflict is actually resolved.
The variant ends with an agreed recipient, abandonment of the claim, coalition collapse, or a claimant war.
The artwork emphasizes a map junction or a marked document, with no invented geography in the actionable map.

### V2 The narrow claimant

This variant requires one claimant whose meaningful interest is a limited existing claim or core and another whose proposal takes substantially more strategic territory.
The target sees that their reasons differ and can submit a counterproposal that satisfies the narrower claimant while reducing the larger demand.
The narrow claimant can accept the compromise, stand by the coalition, or withdraw its enforcement commitment.

If two willing claimants no longer remain, coordinated enforcement collapses.
The ambitious claimant may continue ordinary diplomacy or a bilateral conflict.
The target still needs an actual end to aggression for full-prevention credit.
The variant gives existing map claims a political role without asserting that any historical claim is morally decisive.

### V3 A claimant becomes a protector

This requires a prewar claimant to withdraw fully, renounce every award against the target, and have a legal way to provide protection.
The target must explicitly invite or accept the new relationship after the claimant's old role is closed.
The country cannot hold claimant and protector authority over the same target simultaneously.

After a 15-day separation, it can provide actual aid or a legal guarantee, or mediate a settlement with the remaining coalition.
Its old mobilization costs remain spent and its withdrawal history remains visible.
The new commitment can prevent partition, fail to deter the remaining claimants, or draw the former claimant into a defensive war.
The route gives the player a difficult reversal with actual sacrifice rather than a free opinion conversion.

### V4 The protection gap

This occurs when a protector promised a specific action but loses the legal route, fails to deliver the approved supplies, or refuses the actual defensive commitment when due.
The target receives a factual report of the missing protection.
It can approach a second eligible protector, submit a revised settlement offer, or continue defense with what it actually has.

Claimants become more willing to enforce only after the failure is real.
A mere unanswered request is not a broken treaty.
The protector can narrow its offer honestly or attempt a different legal form of assistance.
Delivered quantities and actual native relationships remain the basis of later credit and blame.

### V5 A divided front becomes one war

This requires at least two active target crises whose actual wars merge or become inseparable through joined belligerents.
The dossiers keep the targets separate and show the shared broader-war relationship.
Protectors must decide where to commit their real forces, and claimants may discover that a limited demand has created a much more expensive campaign.

The available peace route changes to ordinary native settlement whenever a narrow authored peace lacks proof of safe participant isolation.
A target that emerges intact can receive its own full-defense outcome even when another target loses territory, provided the actual relevant aggression has ended.
The variant tests the multi-target premise through genuine military interdependence without merging every outcome into one success flag.

### V6 The map changes under the signatures

This requires a material ownership, control, capital, identity, or major-status change after ratification and before the last award.
The handover displays the real completed and pending states and stops the invalid remainder.
The parties may negotiate again over the current valid map, or the crisis may close with a partial or neutral result.

A released country is an external campaign actor whose current identity and existing content are preserved.
Its territory is not silently included in the old agreement.
A restored target does not inherit stale consent from its prior existence.
The variant turns changing campaign geography into a meaningful interruption rather than a hidden transfer bug.

### V7 The victor receives less than promised

This requires actual war participation followed by a native peace outcome in which a claimant receives less than its ratified political expectation.
The public report distinguishes its expected share from its actual awards.
It can reconcile with the beneficiaries, retain a grievance, or pursue a lawful later dispute.

It receives no automatic top-up from the target or a rival.
The target may have survived because the claimants could not agree, or it may have been eliminated through a different division.
The resulting diplomatic penalties attach to an actual broken commitment where responsibility is established, rather than to every unfavorable peace result.

### V8 The settlement is broken

This requires a real signatory to restart aggression against the target or reject a due binding promise during the 45-day follow-through period.
The target sees the precise breach and can call on any valid existing protection.
Durable-settlement success is denied, while already transferred territory and delivered supplies remain actual facts.

The original signatures cannot authorize a second demand.
A new conflict uses ordinary diplomatic and war rules.
The breach can produce a rare defender recovery or protector achievement, while its concrete Chaos consequence is counted once and kept separate from the generic war source.

## Political and regional expression

Presentation adapts to the actor's current government and its actual stated interest.
A democratic government can emphasize security guarantees and an accountable settlement.
A revisionist government can emphasize a claimed border or strategic opportunity.
A communist government can frame protection or pressure through its current alliances and political interests.
Any government can behave opportunistically or cautiously when the campaign gives it a reason.

Regional flavor follows the actual frontier, terrain, industrial geography, and surviving countries.
European examples do not create a Europe-only selector.
The historical research supplies models for staged border changes, competing territorial agreements, and conditional guarantees.
The campaign supplies the countries and states.
