# Evolutions, foreign reactions, and event connections

## Evolution structure

Research Failure has three true evolutions.
They change the severity and behavior of Event 60. Ordinary reconstruction stages remain separate.

An evolution can affect the event in two ways:

- **Pre-fire evolved opening**, where the first firing after the evolution becomes available begins at the higher severity.
- **Active-incident escalation**, where a country already rebuilding suffers the evolved behavior after an MTTH-based delay when the evolution is enabled and its conditions remain valid.

Evolution activation itself changes Chaos by zero.
Only the concrete additional slot loss, technology regression, archive destruction, bonus loss, foreign exploitation, or later recovery applies Event 60-owned Chaos transactions.

The evolution state must use the shared evolution log with Event ID 60, its own stage and type, the current Chaos tier, and the affected country as actor.
A disabled evolution may not set its recorded flag, apply its losses, unlock its incidents, or become a hidden prerequisite for normal recovery.

## Evolution pacing

The normal active-incident evolution check begins only after the opening transaction has stabilized and the target has had time to respond.
A base timing around 90 to 180 days is appropriate, adjusted by the evolution, active collapse profile, war, occupation, response stance, reconstruction progress, and prior setbacks.

Escalation should become more likely when:

- Scientific Capacity remains very low.
- Archive Recovery has made little progress.
- The first stabilization mission failed.
- The country is occupied, bombed, or losing important states.
- The government continues an exposed purge or breaks promised protections.
- Sealed institutions are cut off from surviving specialists.
- A rushed archive or false-method incident remains unresolved.
- Another scientific crisis has recently affected the country.

Escalation should become less likely when:

- The target completed emergency stabilization.
- Laboratories and archives are protected.
- Specialists have returned.
- A reliable standards network exists.
- The country has already restored most institutional capacity.
- A Brilliant Scientist or Event 16 owner opportunity has explicitly contained the escalation.

The evolution is an escalation of the current incident, not a second Event 60 firing.
It keeps the same incident sequence and applies only the additional difference between the current and new severity.

## Evolution I: Lost Archives

- Chaos requirement: `200+`.

### Premise

The initial damage extends far beyond the laboratories that first reported failure.
Secondary archives cannot be reconciled with one another, retired specialists disagree about which methods were final, and entire technology branches depend on records that no longer exist in a trustworthy form.

### Pre-fire opening

An Event 60 incident that begins at Lost Archives severity receives:

- Lower starting Archive Recovery.
- Broader technology-domain coverage.
- More one-generation rollbacks and a meaningful chance of two-generation rollback in deep safe branches.
- A larger abandoned-project set.
- Higher archive and verification costs.
- A longer stabilization deadline or stronger support costs, not an unfair shorter deadline.
- More foreign interest in duplicate records and displaced specialists.

The operational research-slot floor remains two.

### Active-incident escalation

When Lost Archives activates during an unresolved baseline incident, it should not rerun the opening damage.
It performs one additional archive transaction:

- Selects safe branches that were not already regressed or that still have a valid deeper frontier.
- Removes only the additional technology depth needed to reach Evolution I severity.
- Reduces Archive Recovery.
- Converts one surviving archive package into an unresolved or disputed series.
- Extends or increases the cost of one current archive project.
- Logs one evolution milestone with the target as actor.

The player receives a follow-up event that identifies concrete new evidence, such as contradictory design series, missing test records, or an archive that was believed safe.
The event should not announce the internal evolution label as if officials know a game tier has changed.

### Reconstruction changes

Lost Archives adds:

- A verified-versus-rushed choice for major archive projects.
- More value for foreign duplicate records and retired specialists.
- Stronger false-archive incidents.
- A higher Archive Recovery threshold for the best rediscovery packages.
- A greater long-term advantage for distributed archives under the International Consortium or Independent Academy routes.

### Recovery horizon

A competent country should usually restore its institutional capacity in roughly two to three years under ordinary wartime conditions.
Recovering the full lost technology position can take about three to six years depending on research slots, foreign help, and which branches were lost.
The exact result must be tested against actual country research rates.

## Evolution II: Scientific Dark Age

- Chaos requirement: `400+`.

### Premise

The collapse reaches the institutions that sustained modern industry and war.
Advanced engineering, electronics, production methods, precision measurement, military design, and accumulated scientific advantages all become vulnerable.
The country can remain powerful because its old equipment and factories still exist, but replacement, development, and verification fall years behind.

### Pre-fire opening

An Event 60 incident that begins at Scientific Dark Age severity receives:

- A very low starting Scientific Capacity.
- Near-empty or badly compromised Archive Recovery.
- Broad regression across most safe normal domains.
- Guaranteed serious losses in industry, electronics, military technology, and advanced engineering when valid branches exist.
- Multi-generation rollback in deep branches.
- A stronger legacy-production crisis for active equipment families.
- Selective damage to unused research bonuses and stored scientific advantages through owner-safe policies.
- More scientist-diaspora and foreign-exploitation activity.
- Higher project costs and longer institute reconstruction.

The operational research-slot floor remains two.

### Active-incident escalation

When Scientific Dark Age activates during an unresolved baseline or Evolution I incident, the event applies only the additional severity:

- Additional safe branch rollback, with core industrial and electronics coverage.
- A further Scientific Capacity loss.
- A further Archive Recovery loss.
- Selective removal or degradation of owner-approved accumulated research advantages.
- One major legacy-production incident tied to a live military or industrial consumer.
- Expansion of the scientific diaspora or education deficit.
- One shared evolution-log milestone.

A country that has already restored a research slot can lose that restored slot only when the active escalation proves that the institution supporting it was damaged and the transaction ledger can reconcile it safely.
The event should prefer worsening the speed, archive, and technology state over repeatedly taking the same slot without new institutional evidence.

### Reconstruction changes

Scientific Dark Age adds:

- A national standards reconstruction project as a required institutional milestone.
- A replacement-cohort project before full Capacity can be restored.
- More expensive foreign assistance with stronger dependence.
- A military-versus-civilian priority conflict under the Central Scientific Authority.
- A stronger chance that the country emerges with a different scientific settlement from the one it had before.
- A long residual lost-knowledge phase even after slots are restored.

### Damage to research advantages

Only bonuses with a safe owner policy may be damaged.
The event can remove an unused category bonus, reduce a stored pool, or convert a strong bonus into a weaker recovered-notes state.
It may not delete permanent national focus history, remove characters, reset MIOs, or corrupt special projects without owner approval.

A damaged bonus receives a recovery route.
It cannot disappear without a receipt or become permanently unrecoverable by accident.

### Recovery horizon

Institutional reconstruction should normally take about three to five years.
Full technology recovery can take five to eight years.
A country that makes strong choices can shorten this, but direct gifts and Kruger preservation should be recorded because they affect achievement eligibility.

## Evolution III: Knowledge Collapse

- Chaos requirement: `600+`.

### Premise

The country retains only a narrow core of functioning research.
Most advanced branches depend on people, records, instruments, and production methods that can no longer be assembled into a reliable national system.
The state still owns the material products of earlier science, but the chain from teaching to experiment to factory reproduction has broken.

### Pre-fire opening

An Event 60 incident that begins at Knowledge Collapse severity receives:

- A floor of one research slot.
- Scientific Capacity at or near zero.
- Archive Recovery at or near zero.
- Rollback across most safe eligible branches.
- Two to four generations lost in deep branches where the graph permits it.
- A major education and specialist crisis.
- Multiple legacy-production families under pressure.
- The strongest foreign-poaching and archive-market activity.
- The longest reconstruction projects and the strictest overextension penalties.

A valid Kruger Directorate can preserve its current slot count only by accepting the strongest form of emergency mandate.
This should create a major Event 16 political consequence and a difficult later path back to civilian control.

### Active-incident escalation

When Knowledge Collapse activates during an existing incident, the system:

- Reconciles the operational slot count to one unless the Kruger preservation receipt applies.
- Applies only the additional technology rollback needed for Evolution III severity.
- Reduces both public values sharply without erasing progress already proven safe by completed protected projects.
- Converts the education and replacement-cohort problem into a required recovery step.
- Applies one severe but valid legacy-production crisis.
- Expands foreign efforts to recruit specialists and obtain surviving records.
- Logs the final evolution milestone.

Completed institutions should not vanish through a hidden arithmetic reset.
If an institute is lost, the event identifies the damaged institution and preserves the ledger needed to rebuild it once.

### Reconstruction changes

Knowledge Collapse requires:

- Emergency protection of the last functioning research core.
- A replacement generation of scientists, engineers, technicians, and skilled production staff.
- A national standards and verification network.
- Several sequential institute projects.
- An institutional settlement that can survive the loss of senior specialists.
- Long-term prioritization of lost domains.

The first additional research slot should be reachable within roughly one to two years for a country that commits heavily and retains territory.
Restoring the pre-failure slot ceiling should normally take about four to seven years.
Recovering the full lost technology position can take the rest of the campaign.

The event should remain severe without creating a mathematically impossible recovery.
A country with few civilian factories or one controlled state needs slower scaled projects, foreign or exile routes, and achievable minimum costs. A fixed major-power bill would make recovery impossible.

## Evolution enable and disable behavior

Every evolution can be enabled or disabled through the shared event-evolution controls.

- A disabled pre-fire evolution cannot increase the opening severity.
- A disabled active evolution cannot set its recorded flag or apply additional damage.
- Disabling a higher evolution does not disable the baseline event or lower enabled evolutions.
- Normal reconstruction never requires an evolution-specific decision when that evolution is disabled.
- If an evolution-owned project was active when the user disabled the evolution through a setting supported by the framework, the project should complete or convert cleanly without trapping the country.

The Event Details evolution preview remains a catalog surface and does not display fake history dates or sequence numbers.
Actual evolution history rows show the source event, date, tier, stage, and actor through the shared logger.

## Foreign reaction layer

Research Failure creates a bounded foreign reaction around the target.
It does not open a decision category for every country in the world.
The incident selects a small set of relevant foreign actors from actual diplomatic and scientific relationships.

The normal actor roles are:

- One trusted partner or research-sharing country that can offer records or specialists.
- One rival, enemy, or intelligence-capable country that can exploit the collapse.
- One host country for displaced specialists when a diaspora exists.
- One neutral technical power or black-market intermediary when no trusted partner is available.

One country can fill more than one role only when the resulting choices remain clear.
A foreign player receives a targeted event for a meaningful offer or exploitation choice.
AI responses use the foreign behavior matrix.

### Duplicate archive assistance

A partner supplies copies of publications, patent records, shared tests, industrial drawings, or jointly developed work.
The assistance raises Archive Recovery and can create a bounded lost-domain research bonus.
It does not transfer all of the partner's technology automatically.

The partner can request political goodwill, a license, research access, trade support, future cooperation, or no payment when the relationship is strong.
The target can refuse without losing the normal ability to recover.

### Specialist sanctuary and return

A host country can protect displaced scientists, integrate them permanently, support a joint institute, or help them return.

Permanent integration gives the host a temporary or limited field bonus and makes return harder.
A joint institute benefits both countries but increases the target's dependence.
Assisted return raises the target's Capacity and improves relations.
Coercive return by the target harms trust and can create a later purge or flight incident.

### Scientific exploitation

A rival can recruit specialists, buy stolen records, intensify intelligence work, or spread doubt about the target's equipment and standards.

The effect should be concrete and bounded:

- Temporary research benefit in one relevant field.
- Temporary intelligence advantage against the target.
- A small Archive Recovery loss or delayed project when a theft succeeds.
- Diplomatic exposure if the operation becomes public.

The rival does not receive every regressed technology.
Research Failure remains the target's crisis, not a free technology farm for other countries.

### Foreign replacement contracts

The target can license equipment, hire a foreign institute, or purchase calibrated instruments while domestic knowledge is missing.
This can bridge an urgent military or industrial gap.
It creates dependence and does not clear the lost-technology ledger.

## Connection: Gift from Scientists

Gift from Scientists becomes especially valuable during Research Failure.

When its recipient has outstanding safe lost-knowledge entries, the gift first checks those entries.
A compatible lost technology receives priority over a random unrelated discovery, unless the accepted Event 54 design provides a deliberate player choice.

The grant:

- Restores one compatible technology.
- Clears that ledger entry.
- Ends the matching family penalty when no related loss remains.
- Records that a direct gift was used.
- Leaves Scientific Capacity, Archive Recovery, and research slots unchanged.

A Gift firing inside the same Scientific Research cluster episode occurs after Event 60's rollback. It then becomes a partial recovery and is not erased immediately.

## Connection: Brilliant Scientist

A Brilliant Scientist can create rare, high-value recovery opportunities.
The opportunity should depend on the scientist's field, current allegiance, access to the target, and Event 16 state.

Possible opportunities include:

- Verify one disputed archive series.
- Restore tacit methods for one production family.
- Complete one abandoned project with a strong research bonus.
- Recover one owner-approved lost technology.
- Reduce the cost of a standards or institute project.
- Protect a threatened specialist cohort.

A Brilliant Scientist does not automatically solve the whole event.
The strongest intervention should create a price, loyalty issue, foreign reaction, or Event 16 consequence.

## Connection: Kruger Directorate

The Kruger Directorate connection has two parts.

### Slot preservation

The emergency mandate preserves the target's current research-slot count for the incident.
It records the count before the normal floor is applied and prevents Event 60 from suppressing those slots.

### Political and institutional price

Event 16 receives an owner callback that increases Directorate authority or entrenchment, weakens civilian oversight, and records the bargain for future evolution and route logic.
The exact values must match Event 16's implemented system.

Later Event 60 reconstruction decisions can strengthen, limit, or challenge the mandate, but they may not clear Event 16 consequences without that owner's approval.

## Connection: Intel Leaked

Research Failure and Intel Leaked can reinforce one another without merging into one event.

- A recent Intel Leaked incident against the same country lowers the security of surviving archives and increases foreign-exploitation weight.
- A sealed Research Failure that is exposed can make the target more eligible for an Intel Leaked follow-up.
- Intel Leaked can reveal which broad military or industrial fields are compromised.
- The two events keep separate history entries, durations, and cleanup.
- Their intelligence bonuses must not stack without a cap or duplicate the same source.

## Connection: Mysterious Man

Mysterious Man can create a rare archive incident when its final accepted design includes scientific knowledge.
The stranger may supply a genuine missing series, an impossible method, or a false but persuasive reconstruction.

The player should not know the hidden result before choosing.
The reward or danger affects one bounded branch and the archive state.
It cannot bypass owner protections or introduce an incompatible technology.

## Connection: Research Investment

A future implemented Research Investment event or decision can fund one reconstruction domain.
Investment raises Scientific Capacity, accelerates one institute, or supports one lost-domain bonus.
It does not grant a slot instantly unless the investment completes the same requirements as an Event 60 institute project.

## Connection: Doctrine Research

Normal doctrine nodes are excluded from Event 60 by default.
Doctrine Research therefore remains a separate scientific benefit.
It does not restore ordinary lost equipment or industry technologies.

If a future accepted design allows doctrine regression, Event 27 and Event 60 must share one owner-safe doctrine policy so that doctrine grants, mastery, branch choice, and rollback cannot contradict one another.

## Connection: Alien and event-owned technology

Alien technology, chemical and biological technology, unusual weapons, special projects, and other event-owned technologies follow their owner policies.

An owner can protect the technology, allow normal regression, convert it to a degraded state, or provide a special rediscovery project.
Research Failure never assumes that an unusual technology is safe because it appears in a research folder.

## Connection: Black Market

When the Black Market exists, an affected member may receive a rare offer for stolen blueprints, instruments, or the return of missing archives.
The offer can provide one branch bonus, a disputed archive package, or access to a licensed legacy design.

The trade should carry high cost, false-record risk, intelligence exposure, or criminal dependence.
It does not sell a full technology tree.
Countries outside the Black Market do not learn its membership through this connection.

## Scientific Research cluster episode

The cluster can create a concentrated period of scientific disruption and opportunity.
Event 60's role is High because it can transform a country's research plan for years.

The cluster owner should coordinate members through these rules:

- Event 60 chooses and records its target before positive grant members resolve.
- A positive technology grant checks the new lost ledger first.
- A research-speed or investment benefit can support reconstruction but does not remove the crisis.
- A doctrine member stays in its own doctrine domain.
- Two negative members avoid the same country unless their combined design is explicit.
- Event 16 owner systems keep their own lifecycle and cannot be consumed as a generic bonus.
- Cluster history records fired and skipped members with the normal reasons.

The cluster's exact ID and membership must come from the authoritative workbook and implemented registry.
The supplied CSV snapshot leaves the Scientific Research cluster ID unresolved, so implementation must not invent a conflicting ID from this planning pack.

## Chaos impact

Research Failure owns Chaos changes because the collapse of a major scientific establishment is a distinct destabilizing outcome that the generic war, death, annexation, and contamination systems do not count.

### Opening collapse

The opening applies one Chaos increase based on the actual transaction.
A suitable direction is:

| Applied severity | Intended opening Chaos |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline | About `+3`, reduced when actual rollback is small |
| Evolution I | About `+5` |
| Evolution II | About `+8` |
| Evolution III | About `+12` |

The transaction can scale from actual slots suppressed, technology nodes lost, and research advantages damaged, but it uses one bounded incident receipt.
It does not add one Chaos point for every technology.

### Active evolution consequences

When an evolution adds real damage to an existing incident, it applies only the difference appropriate to the additional loss.
The evolution flag and log entry themselves add zero.

### Secondary setbacks

A severe second laboratory loss, exposed purge, or major foreign theft can add a small one-time amount, normally `+1` or `+2`, when it causes new durable damage.
Routine decision costs, slow recovery, and ordinary project failure add no Chaos.

### Reconstruction reversal

Institutional reconstruction applies one modest Chaos reduction because the country has restored stable scientific capacity.
A suitable direction is:

| Resolved severity | Intended reconstruction reduction |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline | About `-2` |
| Evolution I | About `-3` |
| Evolution II | About `-4` |
| Evolution III | About `-5` |

Full recovery of every lost technology does not create repeated Chaos reductions.
The national scientific system was already stabilized at institutional resolution.

### Anti-farming rules

- One opening Chaos receipt per incident sequence.
- One receipt for each real active-evolution damage transaction.
- One reconstruction reversal per incident sequence.
- No reversal for annexation, tag deletion, forced cleanup, or direct debug completion.
- No duplicate Chaos for technology grants, ordinary research completion, or generic war and death effects.
- Repeated Event 60 firings create new receipts only after normal eligibility and target immunity.
