# Research reconstruction

## Recovery identity

The affected country must rebuild a scientific system, not purchase back a collection of modifiers.
Recovery requires facilities, trained people, records, standards, time, political choices, and a stable institutional settlement.

The reconstruction category uses ordinary decisions, missions, dynamic text, and a static category picture.
The decisions already carry the important actions, so a separate mechanic window would add complexity without improving control.
The category header shows Scientific Capacity, Archive Recovery, operational research slots compared with the recorded ceiling, the active project, and one concise status line.

The category changes by phase.
The player normally sees three to five primary actions and no more than two active missions.
Obsolete emergency actions disappear when their purpose ends.

## Starting values

The opening preflight calculates actual severity first.
The starting values then reflect the evolution, collapse profiles, current war and occupation state, pre-failure institutional strength, and opening response.

The following bands are design targets:

| Severity | Scientific Capacity at opening | Archive Recovery at opening | Operational slot floor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | Usually `20-30` | Usually `10-20` | `2` |
| Evolution I | Usually `12-22` | Usually `5-12` | `2` |
| Evolution II | Usually `5-15` | Usually `0-8` | `2` |
| Evolution III | Usually `0-5` | Usually `0-3` | `1` |

A physical-destruction profile lowers Capacity.
An archive-catastrophe profile lowers Archive Recovery.
A specialist-dispersal or educational-rupture profile lowers Capacity recovery until personnel actions are completed.
A verification-collapse profile increases the value of standards projects and raises false-record risk.
An open emergency raises the starting archive position and foreign exposure.
Sealed institutions lower foreign exposure and archive recovery.
The Kruger mandate changes slot suppression but does not raise either value automatically.

## Research-speed pressure

The general research-speed penalty follows Scientific Capacity through a small number of clear stages.
Exact values remain balance constants, but the difference between stages must be large enough to matter.

A suitable tuning direction is:

| Capacity band | Intended general penalty |
| --- | --- |
| `0-19` | Extreme, around three quarters slower research |
| `20-39` | Severe, around three fifths slower research |
| `40-59` | Major, around two fifths slower research |
| `60-79` | Noticeable, around one quarter slower research |
| `80-99` | Residual, around one tenth slower research |
| `100` | No crisis penalty |

The penalty should update at clear thresholds when point-by-point updates would add noise or risk.
Technology-specific normal effects remain separate.
A country that lost industrial or electronics technologies may still suffer their ordinary consequences after the general crisis penalty ends.

## Slot reconstruction

The incident records the country's pre-failure operational research-slot count before applying the floor.
That count is the initial restoration ceiling.

Scientific Capacity makes a new institute possible, but the slot is restored only through a real reconstruction project.
The country cannot click several free slot decisions at once.
Each restoration commits resources, takes time, and becomes more expensive as the network expands.

The first missing slot can normally be restored after the country reaches the Reopened Institutes band.
Further slots require higher Capacity, completion of earlier institute projects, and enough functioning personnel or facilities.
A country that began with two slots has no missing baseline slot but still needs full Capacity to resolve the crisis.

External research-slot grants received during the crisis need reconciliation.
They should increase the post-crisis ceiling or count as a completed institute only when the granting system proves that a real new institution was created.
They must not allow the country to exceed the active floor accidentally while Event 60 still claims that capacity is suppressed.
The event-owned reconciliation pulse should run only for active targets and should not use an unrestricted whole-world daily or monthly scan.

At Scientific Capacity 100, every valid slot owed by the incident must be operational.
Cleanup may not leave a permanent missing slot or duplicate a slot granted by another system.

## Reconstruction phases

### Phase I: Emergency triage

This phase begins immediately and normally lasts until Scientific Capacity reaches at least 40 and the first stabilization objective is completed.
Its purpose is to stop a second loss, identify what survived, and keep enough people and material together for later rebuilding.

The visible action set rotates according to the collapse profile and response stance.
The player should normally see four primary decisions and one stabilization mission.

#### Secure surviving laboratories

The government assigns guards, controlled access, transport protection, and emergency power or supply to the remaining research sites.
The action is strongest after physical destruction, sabotage, occupation, or sealed response.

Costs should combine a conservative amount of command power with support equipment, infantry equipment, trucks, or a temporary civilian-factory commitment according to the selected sites.
No action may exceed four spendable cost types.

The decision raises Scientific Capacity, reduces the chance of a second physical loss, and protects the next archive or instrument project.
Taking it repeatedly should require new sites, a cooldown, or an escalating cost.

#### Recover records and instruments

Teams search damaged offices, depots, private homes, libraries, military archives, factories, and abandoned laboratories for usable records and calibrated equipment.
The action uses trains or trucks, support equipment, fuel, and civilian capacity according to geography and damage.

It raises Archive Recovery and can discover one branch-specific documentary series.
An archive-catastrophe or verification-collapse profile increases both its value and its chance of returning incomplete material that needs later validation.

#### Protect and recall specialists

The government protects threatened specialists, releases them from nonessential service, recognizes foreign credentials, negotiates their return, or offers guarded positions to people in hiding.

The cost can include political power for legal action, consumer-goods or civilian-factory burden, stability when the policy reverses a purge, and transport for repatriation.
The action raises Scientific Capacity and reduces the scientific diaspora.
It can also create domestic political opposition or foreign negotiations.

A repressive country that refuses to reverse the cause of flight receives less value from the action.
The Independent Academy route later removes this limitation more effectively.

#### Establish emergency standards

The country recreates reference measurements, approved test methods, inspection procedures, common drawing conventions, and a chain of laboratories that can compare results.

This action is most valuable under verification collapse and industrial regression.
It costs civilian capacity, industrial equipment, support equipment, and time.
It raises both values modestly, reduces false-archive risk, and weakens the temporary reproduction penalty.

#### Open a foreign archive channel

This conditional action invites one trusted foreign country, faction leader, research-sharing partner, or neutral technical power to supply duplicate publications, patent copies, test data, design records, and specialist contacts.

The affected country spends political effort, convoys or trains where appropriate, and accepts a documented level of intelligence exposure or scientific dependence.
The foreign country receives a targeted event when controlled by a player.
An AI partner evaluates the request from relations, alliance, ideology, war, research sharing, and its own scientific capacity.

Success raises Archive Recovery and can create a branch-specific bonus.
It never restores every missing technology.
The open-emergency response improves access.
Sealed institutions must first relax secrecy or use a more expensive covert channel.

#### Stabilize the scientific network mission

The first mission gives the player a clear deadline to reach a modest Scientific Capacity and Archive Recovery threshold through active decisions.
The mission should last long enough for the target to gather equipment and complete at least two actions, normally between 150 and 240 days depending on severity.

Success prevents the strongest secondary-collapse incidents, opens the first institute project, and raises public confidence.
Failure applies a real setback such as another lost archive series, delayed slot restoration, specialist flight, or a temporary worsening of the crisis idea.
Failure does not make recovery impossible.
The mission is replaced by the next phase after success or a resolved failure event.

The objective should not require passive stockpile ownership alone.
It measures progress produced by the reconstruction actions and may also require control of the capital or one selected research center when the target is at war.

### Phase II: Rebuild the national network

This phase begins after emergency stabilization.
The country restores permanent institutions, reopens research slots, and prepares an institutional settlement.

Only one major institution project should run at a time unless the country pays a deliberate overextension cost.
The visible action set should include the current slot project, one people project, one archive project, one standards or facilities project, and one technology-priority action.

#### Reopen a research institute

This is the primary slot-restoration project.
The target chooses a valid institutional form based on its route and collapse profile, such as a university institute, military design bureau, industrial laboratory, national academy division, or joint technical center.
The final localisation should use a dynamic institutional type so each country rebuilds a fitting organization.

The project commits civilian factories for a long duration and may consume industrial equipment, support equipment, trains, or political capacity.
War, occupation, damaged states, and the active evolution increase duration and cost.

Completion restores one suppressed research slot and raises Scientific Capacity.
The project cannot exceed the recorded ceiling.
The same slot may not be restored twice after reload or country-control changes.

#### Rebuild the university and technical school network

This project restores teaching, apprenticeships, laboratories, examinations, and mentorship.
It is strongest under educational rupture and specialist dispersal.

The project raises Scientific Capacity over time, reduces future personnel-loss incidents, and improves the long-term resilience of the Independent Academy route.
It can also create a temporary research-speed burden because senior specialists are teaching instead of leading projects.
That tradeoff should be visible before commitment.

#### Restore the national standards network

This project creates a durable system of measurement standards, calibration laboratories, proficiency tests, technical documents, and recognized inspection practice.

It raises both values, reduces industrial reproduction penalties, and lowers the risk of false methods.
It is especially important when advanced industrial, electronics, radar, nuclear-support, or precision engineering technologies were lost.

The project should name a real national institution type in dynamic terms without inventing one universal agency for every country.

#### Reconstruct the central archive

This long project gathers recovered material, verifies provenance, indexes failures as well as successes, copies vulnerable records, and creates a usable national research archive.

It produces the largest Archive Recovery gains.
The player can choose speed or accuracy.
A rushed archive finishes earlier but carries a meaningful chance of false leads, wasted research, and a later validation incident.
A verified archive costs more and takes longer but creates stronger rediscovery support.

The category should not expose a hidden random percentage as a raw number unless the action needs it for a fair decision.
The tooltip should state the broad risk level and what reduces it.

#### Recall the scientific diaspora

The country offers return contracts, protected positions, family transport, credential recognition, laboratory access, and political guarantees.
Foreign countries hosting the scientists may cooperate, demand compensation, or try to retain them.

The action returns part of the diaspora, raises Scientific Capacity, and can create a specialist linked to one lost domain.
The open and Independent Academy routes receive better results.
A sealed or highly repressive country must first meet stronger guarantees.

The action can be repeated for distinct cohorts but cannot farm unlimited research bonuses.

#### Train replacement cohorts

This project accepts that some expertise will not return.
It funds students, technicians, toolmakers, engineers, and junior researchers under a long mentorship program.

It produces slower Capacity gains than recalling experts, but it cannot be blocked by foreign hosts and strengthens future target resistance.
The full reward arrives after a substantial delay.
Evolution III makes this route essential because archive recovery alone cannot replace the missing generation.

#### Prioritize a lost domain

The player selects one broad lost domain for archive support and AI research priority.
Only one primary domain and, at higher Archive Recovery, one secondary domain can be active.

The choice grants a bounded rediscovery advantage for technologies already present in the lost ledger.
It does not boost unrelated new technologies in the same category without limit.
Changing priority has a cooldown or administrative cost so the player cannot switch for every technology on the last day of research.

Useful domains include infantry and support weapons, artillery, armour, air, naval, electronics, industry, engineering, radar, rocketry, and owner-approved special fields.
Only domains with outstanding losses appear.

#### Commission foreign reconstruction

A country that has accepted international assistance may contract a foreign partner to rebuild one institute, standards chain, or archive series.
The project is faster and uses fewer domestic factories.
It creates foreign dependence, intelligence access, production licensing pressure, or a future diplomatic obligation.

The same partner cannot reconstruct the whole scientific system for free.
Each commission has a distinct target and escalating political cost.

### Phase III: Choose the scientific settlement

Once the country has a stable emergency network and at least half of its pre-failure capacity, it must choose how the rebuilt establishment will be governed.
The route is a durable institutional choice, not an ideology label.

The three normal settlements are mutually exclusive.
They change later costs, incidents, AI research behavior, foreign relations, and the final resilience reward.
They do not create a focus tree.

#### International Recovery Consortium

The country builds a network of domestic institutions linked to allied, neutral, or research-sharing partners.
Records are duplicated abroad, specialists move between institutions, common standards are adopted, and major projects receive foreign review.

**Strengths**

- Fastest Archive Recovery.
- Strong foreign duplicate-record opportunities.
- Better access to displaced specialists.
- Strongest research-sharing and licensing support.
- Easier recovery of fields where a partner still holds the lost technology.

**Costs and risks**

- Foreign scientific dependence becomes a visible long-term condition.
- Rivals gain more opportunities for intelligence collection.
- A partner may demand licenses, diplomatic support, trade access, or research cooperation.
- Leaving the supporting faction or research group during reconstruction can delay projects.

**Final identity**

The mature institution should provide a modest durable research-sharing or rediscovery benefit and strong protection against future archive loss.
It should not become a free permanent general research bonus larger than ordinary national development.

**AI profile**

Democratic countries, faction members, research-sharing participants, governments with trusted allies, and states with a large diaspora prefer this route.
Isolated, highly secretive, or hostile states avoid it unless the crisis is desperate.

#### Central Scientific Authority

The government places laboratories, design bureaus, universities, and industrial research under one national reconstruction authority.
Resources move quickly toward fields selected by the state, especially industry and the armed forces.

**Strengths**

- Fastest Scientific Capacity recovery.
- Lower domestic coordination cost for slot-restoration projects.
- Strong military and industrial priority actions.
- Better emergency performance during war or occupation.

**Costs and risks**

- Stability, political legitimacy, or institutional autonomy is spent to centralize the system.
- Archive verification is slower when officials suppress inconvenient failures.
- False-result and purge incidents become more dangerous if the country chooses repeated rushed projects.
- Civilian or open scientific fields recover less efficiently unless specifically protected.

**Final identity**

The mature institution should provide strong targeted research planning and wartime continuity, with a visible risk or cost attached to political interference.
It must not become a universal best route for every authoritarian country.

**AI profile**

Countries at war, authoritarian governments, states with high military production, low foreign trust, or urgent equipment deficits prefer this route.
An AI with high stability and strong allied research may still choose another route.

#### Independent Academy Compact

The government guarantees protected budgets, professional appointments, internal criticism, archive independence, and distributed institutions that can survive another purge or disaster.

**Strengths**

- Strongest long-term resistance to future Research Failure targeting and secondary incidents.
- Lowest false-archive risk.
- Best education and replacement-cohort outcomes.
- Balanced rediscovery support without foreign dependence.
- Better recovery from institutional purge and educational rupture.

**Costs and risks**

- Slowest initial slot reconstruction.
- Highest domestic civilian-factory and political commitment.
- Less direct control over military-first research priorities.
- A government that breaks the compact later receives a serious institutional setback.

**Final identity**

The mature institution should reduce future incident weight, improve the reliability of stored research bonuses, and support stable general research.
Its reward must arrive late enough to justify the difficult recovery path.

**AI profile**

Stable countries at peace, governments with strong civilian industry, states that suffered a purge, and countries with few trusted foreign partners prefer this route.
An AI facing existential war should rarely choose it until survival is secure.

## Kruger settlement

Accepting the Emergency Kruger Mandate preserves the current slot count and places the country on a Directorate-controlled settlement track.
The ordinary settlement choice is delayed or constrained.

The Directorate track provides immediate operational continuity and can accelerate selected advanced projects.
It also transfers durable authority to Event 16, weakens independent institutions, and increases the political cost of later separation.

The country may eventually:

- Confirm a permanent Directorate research order.
- Negotiate a limited Directorate charter that returns some civilian control at high cost.
- Break the mandate through a long institutional struggle after restoring Capacity, risking lost progress and Event 16 retaliation.

The exact political variables, evolution pressure, and country-state consequences belong to Event 16.
Event 60 supplies the crisis context, slot preservation receipt, settlement lock, and reconstruction effects.

A Kruger settlement must still reach Scientific Capacity 100 to resolve the institutional crisis.
Its Archive Recovery can lag behind because the Directorate may preserve working knowledge inside guarded projects without rebuilding a public national archive.
That advantage should create future dependence on the Directorate and must never grant a free complete recovery.

## Phase IV: Residual lost knowledge

After institutional resolution, the category contracts to a smaller mode.
The main crisis values remain visible only while they still affect actions.
Scientific Capacity stays at its completed state and no longer needs active management.
Archive Recovery remains visible until the national ledger is complete or every outstanding loss is resolved.

The normal visible actions are:

- Select the primary rediscovery domain.
- Use one verified archive series when available.
- Recall or appoint one specialist linked to an outstanding field.
- Review foreign duplicate records when the chosen settlement allows it.

Only relevant actions appear.
When no outstanding recoverable technology remains, the category closes and the final archive idea matures or clears according to the settlement.

## Secondary incidents

Secondary incidents should arise from player choices, unresolved profiles, war, foreign activity, and rushed projects.
They are not a random punishment disconnected from the recovery plan.

### Second laboratory loss

A poorly secured or occupied research center is damaged again.
The active project is delayed, Scientific Capacity falls, and one protected archive package may be lost.
Security actions, state control, supply, and the open profile of the affected sites reduce the risk.

### The missing index

A large body of records is recovered without a trustworthy catalog or with contradictory version histories.
The player chooses careful verification, partial use, or a rushed reconstruction.
The choice changes time, Archive Recovery, and false-result risk.

### Foreign appointments

A group of displaced specialists receives offers abroad.
The target can match the offer, negotiate a joint institute, accept a temporary loss, or use coercion when its government permits it.
Foreign player countries receive a real choice when they are the host.

### Prototype without a method

A surviving weapon, machine, or industrial process works, but the production team cannot reproduce it consistently.
The incident selects a regressed family with a live consumer and gives the player a choice between preserving the prototype, disassembling it for study, licensing foreign production, or accepting a production penalty.
It never deletes a unique event asset without owner approval.

### False archive

A rushed project produces a plausible but incorrect method.
The country may lose research time, suffer an industrial defect, or face a public inquiry.
Strong standards, high Archive Recovery, and the Independent Academy route reduce the risk.
The Central Authority route can suppress the first warning at the cost of a larger later failure.

### Concealment exposed

A country that sealed its institutions can be exposed by foreign intelligence, a displaced scientist, a failed production batch, or a public accident.
Exposure ends part of the secrecy benefit, applies a political cost, and can connect to Event 52 Intel Leaked.
The country can then continue secrecy, disclose the crisis, or seek a controlled foreign channel.

### Reconstruction fatigue

Long projects, tied civilian capacity, and repeated emergency spending create domestic resistance.
The player chooses to slow the program, reallocate resources, or accept stability and consumer pressure.
The incident prevents the optimal strategy from being permanent maximum spending without consequence.

## Wartime and occupation variants

War makes reconstruction harder and changes which actions are useful.

- Projects in occupied or contested states pause or relocate.
- The country can create dispersed laboratories and mobile archives at higher cost.
- Military priorities receive more weight, but civilian and educational recovery can fall behind.
- Convoys, trains, fuel, and secure supply routes matter for foreign aid and specialist return.
- Capitulation does not clear the crisis.
- A government in exile can maintain an exile research network with host support, but it cannot restore all domestic slots without territory and institutions.
- Liberation or return to the capital can trigger a major Capacity opportunity and a renewed archive search.

A country with no controlled state cannot begin new domestic institute projects.
It can preserve people and records in exile.
The institutional crisis resolves only after it again controls enough territory to host a functioning network, unless an owner-approved special government-in-exile system provides an equivalent settlement.

## Cost model

Costs scale with country size, pre-failure slot count, severity, war, occupied infrastructure, selected route, repeated use, and the number of institutions already restored.

Useful costs include:

- Civilian factory commitment.
- Support equipment and industrial equipment where available.
- Trucks and trains for moving archives and specialists.
- Convoys and fuel for overseas assistance.
- Political power for legal guarantees, foreign agreements, or centralization.
- Command power for military security operations.
- Stability or war support when the action creates a real political sacrifice.
- Consumer-goods or production burden.
- Research-speed diversion while senior staff teach or verify records.
- Intelligence exposure and foreign dependence as consequences.

A single decision may consume at most four distinct spendable cost types.
Non-consumed conditions such as state control, a foreign partner, a Capacity threshold, or an active route remain requirements.

Political power or command power alone is not enough for a major institute, archive, or standards project.
The resource package should describe the work.

## Failure and partial success

A failed project does not erase all earlier progress.
It can return partial Capacity or Archive Recovery based on completion, then apply a distinct setback.

Examples include:

- A laboratory reopens but cannot support a new slot until more specialists return.
- An archive is recovered but needs verification before it grants research support.
- A foreign institute sends records but demands a license or access agreement.
- A diaspora program returns senior scientists but fails to attract students.
- A central authority restores military design work while civilian science remains weak.

Partial success should change the next decision in a meaningful way. Tiny compensatory modifiers are insufficient.

## AI recovery behavior

The AI uses the same category and never receives hidden free reconstruction.
It evaluates actions from current operational slots, Scientific Capacity, Archive Recovery, lost technology domains, war, production, equipment needs, civilian industry, stability, foreign partners, and risk.

The AI should:

- Stop a secondary collapse before pursuing expensive long-term projects.
- Restore one slot as soon as it can pay the real cost without breaking essential war production.
- Prioritize industry and electronics when several domains are missing.
- Prioritize an active military family when the country produces and fields it heavily.
- Seek foreign records when a trusted partner exists and secrecy is not central to its strategy.
- Protect or recall specialists when diaspora loss is high.
- Avoid running several major projects when the civilian economy cannot sustain them.
- Slow reconstruction before equipment or convoy spending reaches a dangerous level.
- Use the residual category until the important lost branches are recovered.

The detailed AI matrix defines route and action ordering under named scenarios.
Every weighted decision surface requires the probability audit workflow before implementation is accepted.
