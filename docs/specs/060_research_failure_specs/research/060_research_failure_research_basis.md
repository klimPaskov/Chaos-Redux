# Research basis for Event 60

## Research purpose

The external research was used to sharpen the event's institutional logic.
Research Failure is fictional and country-dynamic.
It does not recreate one historical purge, war, disaster, or national science policy.
The sources show several distinct ways that scientific capability can collapse and several requirements for credible recovery.

All links were checked on 2 September 2026.

## Research finding 1: Removing people can break institutions for much longer than one budget cycle

The Max Planck Society records that the Kaiser Wilhelm Society dismissed 126 staff members after 1933, including 104 scientists, and notes that many were forced abroad or lost their careers.
This supports a specialist-dispersal profile in which recovery requires protection, return, credential recognition, and rebuilding teams. Laboratory construction alone cannot recover the lost capability.

Source: [Max Planck Society, Jewish scientists are dismissed from the Kaiser Wilhelm Institutes](https://www.mpg.de/946921/1933-jewish-scientists-dismissed-from-kaiser-wilhelm-institutes)

Design translation:

- Scientist cohorts can flee, disappear, or accept foreign appointments.
- Foreign hosts receive meaningful choices.
- A repressive government cannot obtain full value from a recall decision without changing the conditions that caused flight.
- The Independent Academy Compact gains strong value after an institutional purge.

## Research finding 2: Political control can destroy a scientific field and interrupt transmission between generations

Borinskaya, Ermolaev, and Kolchinsky describe how state intervention and Lysenkoist control defeated Soviet genetics after the 1948 VASKhNIL session and interrupted the transmission of scientific values and traditions between generations.
This supports the institutional-purge profile, the risk of false methods, and the need to rebuild criticism, teaching, and verification.

Source: [Borinskaya, Ermolaev, and Kolchinsky, Lysenkoism Against Genetics, Genetics, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6499510/)

Design translation:

- A purge can destroy more than the removed technology node.
- The Central Scientific Authority needs a real political-interference risk.
- A rushed archive can preserve a false official method.
- Educational and professional continuity belongs in Scientific Capacity.

## Research finding 3: Educational interruption creates long-lived missing cohorts

Li and Meng find that China's college admissions largely halted from 1966 to 1969 and that affected cohorts suffered lasting educational and economic effects.
Stanford's SPICE history also describes school and university closures and the later disarray of the educational system.
This supports an educational-rupture profile that cannot be solved through an immediate equipment purchase.

Sources:

- [Li and Meng, The Scarring Effects of College Education Deprivation during China's Cultural Revolution](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/713935)
- [Stanford SPICE, Introduction to the Cultural Revolution](https://spice.fsi.stanford.edu/docs/introduction_to_the_cultural_revolution)

Design translation:

- Replacement-cohort projects take years and temporarily divert senior staff into teaching.
- Knowledge Collapse requires education and mentorship before full Capacity can return.
- Training provides slow durable resilience and never an instant technology grant.

## Research finding 4: Tacit knowledge is held by people, processes, and practice

The OECD Nuclear Energy Agency warns that ageing workforces can take accumulated tacit knowledge with them and emphasizes continuity through new projects and a critical mass of national activity.
The IAEA states that nuclear organizations need to preserve and transfer explicit and tacit knowledge across people, processes, and technology.

Sources:

- [OECD Nuclear Energy Agency, Knowledge management in the context of an ageing workforce](https://www.oecd-nea.org/jcms/pl_15160/knowledge-management-in-the-context-of-an-ageing-workforce)
- [IAEA, Nuclear knowledge management](https://www.iaea.org/topics/nuclear-knowledge-management)

Design translation:

- Existing equipment can survive while reproduction fails.
- Archive Recovery alone cannot replace missing technicians, toolmakers, operators, and mentors.
- Prototype-without-a-method incidents are grounded in the difference between a physical example and a repeatable process.
- Event-owned strategic technologies need owner policies because long-life systems depend on specialized operating knowledge.

## Research finding 5: Physical destruction, equipment loss, displacement, and funding collapse compound one another

UNESCO's assessment of damage to Ukraine's public scientific infrastructure reported 1,443 damaged or destroyed buildings and laboratories, more than 750 damaged pieces of scientific equipment, displaced researchers, reduced employment, and severe funding loss.
UNESCO's later recovery plan emphasizes infrastructure, young scientists, international cooperation, talent retention, institutional resilience, and science education.

Sources:

- [UNESCO, Ukraine: Restoring scientific infrastructure will cost over 1.26 billion dollars](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ukraine-restoring-scientific-infrastructure-will-cost-over-126-billion-unesco)
- [UNESCO, Action Plan for the recovery of Ukraine's science sector](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-presents-action-plan-recovery-ukraines-science-sector)

Design translation:

- Physical destruction, specialist dispersal, and reduced funding are separate collapse profiles that can combine at higher evolution levels.
- Reconstruction projects need facilities, instruments, people, and long-term institutional support.
- Foreign assistance can help without automatically restoring domestic technology.
- Exile and occupation variants need routes for preserving people and records before domestic institutions can reopen.

## Research finding 6: Rebuilding a national science system includes displaced scientists and voluntary return

The International Science Council, UNESCO-TWAS, and InterAcademy Partnership Science in Exile declaration calls for safeguarding enquiry, data, institutions, early-career researchers, and rebuilding national scientific systems after conflict or disaster, including safe voluntary repatriation.

Sources:

- [International Science Council, International science organizations unite in support of at-risk, displaced and refugee scientists](https://council.science/news/international-science-organizations-unite-in-support-of-at-risk-displaced-and-refugee-scientists/)
- [The World Academy of Sciences, Science in Exile Initiative](https://twas.org/science-exile-initiative)

Design translation:

- Recall must be voluntary or politically costly.
- Foreign hosts can support return, joint institutes, or permanent integration.
- Early-career and replacement-cohort support is distinct from recalling famous senior scientists.
- A country can preserve part of its scientific system in exile without restoring domestic slot capacity immediately.

## Research finding 7: Measurement standards and laboratory recognition are part of reproducible capacity

NIST describes national metrology institutes as custodians of primary standards and explains how training, proficiency testing, laboratory recognition, documented procedures, and traceability support reliable measurement results.
This supports the verification-collapse profile and a standards network as a major reconstruction project.

Sources:

- [NIST, International Legal Metrology Organizational Primer](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/international-legal-metrology/ilm-organizational-primer)
- [NIST, Laboratory Metrology Program Overview](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/laboratory-metrology)

Design translation:

- Scientific Capacity includes instruments, standards, procedures, and recognized competence.
- A damaged country can possess a blueprint but still fail to reproduce tolerances.
- Standards reconstruction affects both research and active industrial production.
- False archives become less likely when methods are tested against common standards.

## Research finding 8: Open and duplicated knowledge improves resilience

UNESCO's Open Science framework emphasizes accessible and reusable publications, data, educational resources, software, hardware, open infrastructure, and international cooperation.
The source is modern and should not be copied directly into 1930s or 1940s localisation.
Its institutional principle supports duplicate archives and foreign research networks.

Source: [UNESCO, Open science](https://www.unesco.org/en/open-science)

Design translation:

- The International Recovery Consortium restores archives faster because records and expertise exist in several institutions.
- Open reconstruction increases foreign exposure and dependence, so it remains a tradeoff.
- Distributed records create future resistance to archive catastrophe.

## Historical restraint

The event should not reduce real persecution, war damage, or educational destruction to a joke or a direct one-to-one random effect.
The historical cases provide mechanisms and institutional consequences.
Player-facing text should remain fictional, country-specific, and focused on the selected campaign state.

The period presentation should use laboratories, university departments, technical schools, archives, prototype shops, testing grounds, paper records, calibration instruments, telephones, radio equipment, and industrial drawings appropriate to 1936-1945.
Modern terms such as cloud backup, digital database, cyberattack, or online repository should not appear unless a separate high-chaos technology explicitly makes them valid.

## Research conclusions used by the design

The event's strongest design conclusions are:

- Technology regression needs a people and process explanation.
- Existing equipment must remain while reproduction and improvement become difficult.
- Recovery needs facilities, records, standards, teachers, students, and institutional trust.
- Foreign aid should support but not erase domestic rebuilding.
- Political control can speed coordination while damaging verification.
- Distributed academies and archives can create durable resilience.
- A one-slot Knowledge Collapse needs a replacement-generation path so recovery remains possible.
