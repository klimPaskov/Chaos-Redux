## Findings as raised and subsequently accepted

### I105-01: Close the allied-major promotion gap in the military achievement

**Priority:** Material definition clarification before the achievement specification is finalized.

**Evidence:** S3's military challenge excludes another major on the player's side at enrolment and fails when a different major joins that side.
Its continuing requirements do not state what happens when an allied minor already in the war becomes a major.
The description direction nevertheless promises the absence of a major ally.
[S3, Achievement A table, War context, Continuing requirements, and Attempt failure]

**Boundary case:** A player enrols with a minor ally.
That ally becomes a major without joining the war again.
The current join-based failure sentence does not clearly disqualify the attempt, even though a major ally can now carry the war.
War-side changes through a war merge have the same need for an explicit interpretation.
This is a logical gap in the written conditions, not a claim about verified engine hooks.

**Recommended amendment:** Add the continuing condition that no country other than the tracked player may fight on the player's side of the tracked war while classified as a major.
Name both entry by an existing major and promotion of an existing ally to major status as disqualifying transitions.
Apply the same rule when the tracked war's side membership changes through a verified native merge or continuity operation.
The player's own promotion from its qualifying minor start remains allowed.

**Acceptance limit:** The achievement owner must show how the existing framework observes and retains the relevant disqualification.
A periodic snapshot alone must not be called proof that no forbidden interval occurred.
If authoritative coverage is unavailable, retain that exact condition as unresolved or obtain a separate accepted change to the challenge.
Do not create an extra world-wide polling system merely to support this achievement.

**Owner and destination:** Parent achievement owner, folded into S3's military condition and the acceptance handoff.

### I105-02: Include the effective evolution stage in the firing snapshot

**Priority:** Small contract clarification before the implementation handoff is frozen.

**Evidence:** S1 explicitly freezes C, the roster, and all recipients' B before grants.
S2 says each firing uses the highest enabled stage, while its settings section defines how disabled stages are handled.
The intended single stage is clear, but the documents do not explicitly prohibit rereading enabled-stage settings during later recipients' resolution.
[S1, Resolution of one firing, steps 2 and 3. S2, Evolution ladder and Entry timing and settings]

**Recommended amendment:** State that the firing chooses and saves its effective stage once from the captured C and evolution settings before the first native dispatch.
Every recipient's quantity uses that same stage and its own captured B.
A setting changed by a native callback during resolution affects a later firing, not later recipients of the current firing.
The per-country count roll and per-draw live ownership rules stay unchanged.

This adds no new tuning, setting, or management surface.
It makes the existing one-firing stage contract explicit and preserves the accepted global snapshot rationale.

**Owner and destination:** Parent event owner, folded into S1's resolution sequence, S2's settings wording, and the named callback acceptance cases.

### I105-03: Correct the partial-retention description of the Chaos formula

**Priority:** Editorial mathematical correction.

**Evidence:** S2 defines a zero award when N or A is zero, otherwise `ceil(2*A/N)` with a maximum of 20.
The baseline row says the award is reduced if only part of the roster retains an idea.
Ceiling means some partial-retention cases still award +2.
[S2, Chaos impact map, formula and baseline table row]

**Example:** If nine of ten baseline recipients each retain one direct identity, `ceil(18/10)` is 2.
There is partial retention without a smaller award.

**Recommended amendment:** Replace the row's dynamic-factor wording with the exact baseline ranges.
For a nonempty roster, the award is zero when A is zero, +1 when `0 < A <= N/2`, and +2 when `N/2 < A <= N`.
These comparison symbols express the mathematical document only, not Clausewitz syntax.
Alternatively, use the concise prose that the award can fall to +1 when no more than half the roster retains its baseline identity.
Do not alter the accepted formula.

**Owner and destination:** Parent spec owner, S2's baseline impact row.

### I105-04: Give result retention one explicit observation time

**Priority:** Small presentation and history contract clarification.

**Evidence:** S2 measures R at the firing's immediate settlement checkpoint for the Chaos award.
S3 requires retained-status information and immutable firing context for queued reports and older history.
The report wording sometimes says an identity is still held without explicitly tying that statement to the saved checkpoint.
[S2, Chaos impact map. S3, The result report and Event history and details]

**Boundary case:** A report remains open while a later firing or a native callback removes an earlier identity.
Reading live ownership for its old retained-status label would make the historical report change while its saved Chaos contribution and other outcome data stay fixed.

**Recommended amendment:** Use the same saved end-of-firing retained-identity observation for the result report, its outcome summary, and its retained-count contribution.
Make public wording such as present after this firing refer to that observation.
Later native changes remain visible through the country's normal live national-spirit display and through B at a later firing.
They do not rewrite the old report's observation.
Where the engine cannot establish the intended settlement observation, retain an unresolved diagnostic result rather than inventing callback completion.

This requires no extra status panel or second retention ledger.

**Owner and destination:** Parent event and localisation owners, folded into S2's settlement definition, S3's report contract, and callback/history acceptance cases.
