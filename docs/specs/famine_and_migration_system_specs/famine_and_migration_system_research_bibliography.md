# Famine and Migration Mechanics Research Bibliography

Accessed 2026-08-22.

This bibliography records the research used to shape the design. It is a design bibliography, not a claim that every cited case should be reproduced as a fixed scripted event.

## Food-security classification

### Integrated Food Security Phase Classification

Source: IPC Acute Food Insecurity Classification.

Link: https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipcinfo-website/ipc-overview-and-classification-system/ipc-acute-food-insecurity-classification/en/

Design use:

IPC distinguishes five severity phases from minimal through catastrophe or famine. The game design uses a shorter visible ladder because Chaos Redux needs clear state modifiers and does not need a full humanitarian classification tool. The research supports distinct thresholds and different response priorities.

Confidence: High.

## Soviet famine and movement restriction

### University of Minnesota, Center for Holocaust and Genocide Studies

Source: Holodomor resource guide.

Link: https://cla.umn.edu/chgs/holocaust-genocide-education/resource-guides/holodomor

Design use:

Supports the treatment of the 1932 to 1933 famine as a man-made crisis centered on rural communities and state policy. It informs grain extraction, rural vulnerability, repression, and historical memory.

Confidence: High for broad description.

### National Museum of the Holodomor-Genocide

Source: The History of the Holodomor.

Link: https://holodomormuseum.org.ua/en/the-history-of-the-holodomor/

Design use:

Supports movement-restriction and trapped-population mechanics. The profile uses travel restriction, sealed affected regions, confiscation, and concealment as pressure components.

Confidence: High for the institution's documented position. The legal classification and some estimates remain politically contested across states and institutions. The design avoids forcing a universal legal label into all player-facing text.

## Bengal, Vietnam, and Java

### National Library of Medicine, PubMed Central

Source: Colonial Biopolitics and the Great Bengal Famine of 1943.

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9735018/

Design use:

Supports a multi-causal Bengal profile involving war, loss of imports, crop damage, administrative choices, and unequal access. The package avoids claiming one uncontested cause.

Confidence: High for the reviewed mechanisms. Causal emphasis differs across scholarship.

Source: The Great Second World War Vietnam and Java Famines.

## Political instability and famine

### Governance and Social Development Resource Centre

Source: Food Prices, Food Security and Political Stability.

Link: https://gsdrc.org/publications/food-prices-food-security-and-political-stability/

Design use:

Supports conditional political pressure and rejects a deterministic rule that famine always creates revolution. Evidence links food insecurity and unrest, but outcomes depend on institutions, blame, repression, opposition, and context.

Confidence: Medium to high as a research synthesis.

## Research cautions

- Historical mortality estimates often vary. The game does not target a fixed total.
- Legal classification of deliberate famine can differ across institutions and states. The system can model documented policy and evidence without forcing one universal legal label.
- Famine is often multi-causal. Profiles should expose several pressure components.
- Current humanitarian cases should inform mechanisms, not be copied as period-specific statistics into a 1936 campaign.
- Refugees and migrants should not be treated as inherent disease vectors.
- Closed borders can be motivated by capacity, war, or health policy. Responsibility depends on known danger, available alternatives, force, and actual outcome.
