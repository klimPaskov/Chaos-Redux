# Historical and scientific research anchors

## Research purpose

This note records the historical anchors used to shape the Event 025 expedition race.

The event remains alternate-history fiction. The research supports logistics, communications, station-building, strategic interest, route design, and period presentation. It does not prove any alien claim.

Research checked on 18 August 2026.

## 1. Wartime Antarctic stations had strategic and scientific roles

The British Antarctic Survey history identifies Operation Tabarin as a secret British mission begun in 1943. Its stated aims included preventing enemy ships from using Antarctic harbours, gathering weather data for Allied shipping, and strengthening territorial claims. It established permanently occupied stations and conducted meteorological, geological, biological, and topographical work.

Source:

- British Antarctic Survey, `Our history`
- https://www.bas.ac.uk/about/history/

Design use:

- supports a race where research, weather, strategic access, and sovereignty interests overlap
- supports outposts as real phase gates
- supports Evolution III militarisation without treating it as pure fantasy
- supports weather observation as a practical expedition action

Limit:

Operation Tabarin began in 1943, so it is a wartime and later-campaign anchor. The baseline game can begin earlier. Event text should describe plausible institutions without claiming that every participating country already has a Tabarin-style program in 1936.

## 2. Ships, crews, resupply, and access are central

The British Antarctic Survey ship history states that ice-strengthened ships and experienced crews have been central to Antarctic work, first helping to establish bases and deliver staff, supplies, and mail, while allowing teams to reach otherwise inaccessible locations. Ship officers also performed hydrographic surveys and sea-ice observations.

Source:

- British Antarctic Survey, `History of British Antarctic Survey ships`
- https://www.bas.ac.uk/about/history/history-of-bas-ships/

Design use:

- convoys, fuel, and route capacity are central spendable resources
- direct southern access lowers cost and travel time
- ship condition, sea ice, and route interruption affect Logistics Readiness
- hydrographic and sea-ice work supports survey actions before a landing
- outpost construction requires continuing resupply after the initial payment

Limit:

The event uses HOI4 convoys as an abstraction. It should not imply that every convoy represents an ice-strengthened research ship.

## 3. Early Antarctic radio required relays and failed under harsh conditions

The Australian Antarctic Program describes the 1911 to 1914 Australasian Antarctic Expedition as the first to establish an Antarctic radio link. A relay station on Macquarie Island bridged the distance between Antarctica and Australia. Missing detector parts stopped one receiving station, while extreme wind, damaged masts, atmospheric static, and auroral activity disrupted communications.

Source:

- Australian Antarctic Program, `Pioneering Antarctic communications`
- https://www.antarctica.gov.au/about-antarctica/history/communications/the-wireless-of-wireless-hill/

Design use:

- supports gateway relays and radio infrastructure
- supports redundant communication actions
- supports weather and auroral interference
- supports false or delayed information without using arbitrary fog
- supports Evolution I signal effects through period equipment
- supports outpost integrity and missing-parts incidents

Limit:

The active alien signal remains fictional. Historical radio difficulties guide presentation and gameplay consequences only.

## 4. Gateway ports matter

The Australian Antarctic Program records Hobart as a port of call for many early expeditions. Its modern material describes Hobart as an Antarctic science, research, and logistics hub. Antarctica New Zealand describes Christchurch as an Antarctic gateway and operational hub.

Sources:

- Australian Antarctic Program, `Exploration and expeditions: from sealers to science`
- https://www.antarctica.gov.au/about-antarctica/history/exploration-and-expeditions/
- Australian Antarctic Program, `Nuyina's home port - Hobart`
- https://www.antarctica.gov.au/nuyina/nuyinas-home-port/
- Antarctica New Zealand, `Antarctic-related activities boost New Zealand's economy`
- https://www.antarcticanz.govt.nz/media/news/antarctic-related-activities-boost-new-zealands-economy

Design use:

- supports gateway groups and sponsored access
- supports country distance and route burden
- supports a difference between direct and chartered routes
- supports port, airport, warehouse, and specialist-service presentation

Limit:

Modern gateway-city status is used as a logistics analogy. Event 025 should not grant permanent 1936 bonuses solely because a city is a modern Antarctic hub. Final route checks need the live map, ownership, diplomacy, and period campaign state.

## 5. Large military expeditions can inform the high-chaos evolution

Official United States Navy and National Archives material describes Operation Highjump in 1946 to 1947 as a large naval Antarctic expedition that charted coastlines, established a research base, trained personnel, tested equipment in cold conditions, and studied the feasibility of maintaining Antarctic bases.

Sources:

- Naval History and Heritage Command, `Polar Exploration`
- https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/exploration-and-innovation/polar-exploration0.html
- U.S. National Archives, `Operation Hi-jump: Exploring Antarctica with the U.S. Navy`
- https://unwritten-record.blogs.archives.gov/2017/09/27/operation-hi-jump-exploring-antarctica-with-the-u-s-navy/

Design use:

- supports Evolution III warships, aircraft, training, cold-weather testing, and large logistics
- supports aerial survey and multiple field detachments
- supports a campaign where military scale changes the race without automatically starting a war

Limit:

Operation Highjump is postwar. It should influence Evolution III scale and visual direction, not baseline 1936 wording.

## 6. The Antarctic Treaty is later than the baseline period

The Antarctic Treaty Secretariat states that the treaty was signed on 1 December 1959 and entered into force in 1961.

Source:

- Antarctic Treaty Secretariat, `The Antarctic Treaty`
- https://www.ats.aq/e/antarctictreaty.html

Design use:

- prevents anachronistic baseline references to treaty rules
- supports a late-campaign negotiated demilitarisation or scientific access idea only as a new agreement created by the campaign

Limit:

Event text must not imply that the 1959 treaty governs a 1936 expedition.

## 7. Station sites, aerial photographs, and logistics narrowed real choices

Australian Antarctic Program histories of Mawson station describe the use of aerial photographs, exposed rock, natural harbour access, ice-strengthened shipping, fuel stops, prefabricated buildings, over-snow tractors, aircraft reconnaissance, fast ice, and a short seasonal construction window.

Sources:

- Australian Antarctic Program, `Mawson station, est. 1954: a signal achievement`
- https://www.antarctica.gov.au/magazine/issue-6-autumn-2004/feature/mawson-station-est-1954-a-signal-achievement/
- Australian Antarctic Program, `Founding Mawson: Australia's first Antarctic station`
- https://www.antarctica.gov.au/magazine/issue-22-2012/stations/founding-mawson-australias-first-antarctic-station/

Design use:

- supports site confirmation as a phase gate
- supports aerial reconnaissance
- supports rock, harbour, ice, and weather as site-quality factors
- supports prefabricated outpost construction
- supports an austral-season time pressure
- supports alternate landing and emergency unloading actions

Limit:

The event uses broad sectors and route bands instead of recreating one historical expedition.

## 8. Design synthesis

The research supports this playable loop:

1. identify a plausible gateway
2. commit ships, fuel, supplies, people, and industry
3. cross a difficult route
4. establish a functioning outpost
5. build reliable communications
6. combine aerial, radio, hydrographic, and ground survey
7. protect the route and camp
8. decide how much military force to commit
9. exploit a short recovery window

The research also supports the decision to keep the baseline race grounded. The strange content comes from the craft, signal, survivor, fragmentation, and dependence. The expedition itself should behave like a hard polar operation.

## 9. Presentation implications

Generated art should show:

- period ships and aircraft
- timber or prefabricated huts
- radio masts and valve equipment
- tents, sledges, tracked transport, fuel drums, crates, and weather instruments
- small personnel groups against large landscapes
- rough unloading and improvised construction

Avoid:

- modern research stations
- satellite dishes
- digital screens
- modern snowmobiles
- modern survival suits
- current Antarctic treaty insignia
- clear modern gateway infrastructure in a 1930s scene

## 10. Research boundary

No source was used to claim extraterrestrial life, alien technology, a survivor, or an Antarctic conspiracy. Those elements are fictional event design.
