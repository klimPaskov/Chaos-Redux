# Evolutions

## Evolution rules shared by all five tracks

The event has five true evolutions. Ordinary expedition phases are baseline progression and must not be logged as evolutions.

Each evolution follows the shared event-evolution contract:

- it becomes eligible at its mapped chaos tier
- it uses paced activation, normally through a mean-time-to-happen model around 90 days after eligibility
- it may enter an active race or shape the initial opening when the event fires at a higher tier
- it sets the shared evolution context before logging
- it respects the Event Details enable toggle
- disabling it prevents its incidents, unlocks, and recorded flags
- disabling it never blocks baseline completion
- player-facing evolution text describes visible developments without exposing hidden profiles or future outcomes

Global evolutions use no actor. Country-specific Evolution V milestones use the affected holder as actor.

## Evolution I: The Ship Is Still Active

### Chaos tier

Gathering Storm, 200 to 399 chaos.

### Playable promise

The wreck becomes an active source of information and danger. Participants can use the signal to search faster, protect themselves, or deny the signal to rivals.

### Activation evidence

The evolution may begin through:

- repeated narrow-band transmissions
- magnetic pulses
- short power surges in recovered fragments
- synchronized radio failures
- a signal that changes when expeditions move

The text should not confirm intelligence or intention.

### Global state

Evolution I activates Signal Intensity.

Signal Intensity is a hidden or compact global state shown through the Antarctic board background, site pulses, and tooltips. It changes the risk and value of signal actions.

### New actions

| Action | Benefit | Risk or cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listen through the pulse | Higher survey certainty and progress | Higher exposure and equipment disruption |
| Build shielded receivers | Lower exposure and more reliable data | Support equipment and civilian commitment |
| Study from a distance | Safe fragment analysis | Slower progress |
| Jam the signal | Reduces rival signal gains | Fuel, electronics burden, hostility, and self-interference |
| Broadcast a probe sequence | Can reveal a new sector | May trigger a strong pulse or false response |

### Failure and counterplay

A strong pulse can:

- damage radio equipment
- lower readiness
- injure personnel
- erase or corrupt recent survey data
- reveal the expedition's location to rivals

Shielding, redundant observations, and remote receivers reduce the damage.

### AI behavior

- cautious scientific AI prefers shielding and remote study
- technologically confident AI listens more aggressively
- hostile AI jams when a rival is close to final recovery
- weak logistics AI avoids high-signal actions

### Evolved opening

When Event 025 fires after Evolution I is already eligible, the opening shows active transmissions and starts Signal Intensity above zero. The race still begins in Phase 0.

## Evolution II: Something Survived

### Chaos tier

Rising Chaos, 400 to 599 chaos.

### Playable promise

Evidence suggests that an occupant, machine, organism, or autonomous part left the wreck. Participants decide whether to avoid it, protect against it, capture it, or use its movement against rivals.

### Hidden campaign profile

At activation, the event selects one hidden survivor profile:

- evasive biological survivor
- autonomous repair machine
- wounded or fragmented crew presence
- distributed probe or maintenance swarm

The profile controls incident weights and material traces. Player-facing text may remain ambiguous for the full campaign.

### Visible evidence

- tracks that do not match expedition vehicles
- opened compartments
- missing personnel
- cut or rearranged cables
- fragments moved between survey visits
- damaged outposts with no clear attacker
- radio responses that shift with ground movement

### New expedition stance

Each participant chooses a current survivor stance:

| Stance | Benefit | Cost or risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Avoid contact | Lower exposure and casualty risk | Slower search in affected sectors |
| Guard every movement | Better outpost and team protection | Higher equipment, fuel, and personnel commitment |
| Attempt capture | Potential unique fragment and progress | High exposure and casualty risk |
| Offer a controlled path | May draw the survivor away from the core | Can strengthen or misdirect it |
| Turn it toward a rival | Strong covert disruption | High attribution, hostility, and backlash risk |

### Prepared access rule

A prepared expedition may still reach the recovery core. The survivor cannot create a permanent hard lock.

A participant with escorts, remote systems, high readiness, or a verified safe route can continue the baseline search.

### Personnel loss

Real expedition deaths must use the shared Deaths system with a dedicated Event 025 reason. Losses should remain proportional to expedition scale and incident severity. The event must not treat a small field team as millions of casualties.

### Capture outcome

A successful capture or containment does not create a new unit or country.

It provides:

- a unique fragment category
- a stronger Evolution V analysis route
- increased exposure
- special report events
- Event 036 reaction data when the later spacecraft appears

### Evolved opening

When Event 025 fires after Evolution II is eligible, the opening may include preexisting tracks or a missing survey party. The participant still starts with ordinary mobilization and receives time to prepare.

## Evolution III: Antarctica Becomes Militarised

### Chaos tier

Chaos Tier, 600 to 799 chaos.

### Playable promise

Scientific competition becomes an armed contest over routes, stations, personnel, and access to the wreck.

### Global militarisation state

Militarisation rises from:

- armed escort deployments
- blockades
- captured personnel
- outpost seizures
- repeated attributed sabotage
- aircraft interception
- warships or submarines operating near routes

It can fall through:

- prisoner exchanges
- mutual rescue
- demilitarised corridors
- neutral inspection
- withdrawal of escorts
- settlement after the winner is declared

The board should show broad stages and no fourth meter:

- guarded
- tense
- armed
- contested
- open clash

### New overt actions

| Action | Purpose | Requirements and consequences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Escort the supply route | Protect readiness and convoys | Fuel, convoys, suitable navy or air assets |
| Blockade a rival route | Reduce target readiness | Strong attribution and diplomatic pressure |
| Seize a compromised outpost | Capture data and fragments | Requires target weakness and carries clash risk |
| Recover detained personnel | Restore crew and reduce exposure | May require exchange or force |
| Intercept survey aircraft | Delay target certainty | Air requirement, fuel cost, incident risk |
| Establish a demilitarised corridor | Reduce clashes for both sides | Requires willing rival or neutral sponsor |

### Outpost seizure

An outpost may be seized only when:

- Evolution III is active
- the target is a valid active participant
- the target outpost is compromised or poorly defended
- the attacker has the needed route and military commitment
- the action cooldown is clear

Seizure causes a serious setback, but the target may retake the site, establish a backup outpost, negotiate a return, or convert to a mobile recovery route.

### Limited conflict rule

Antarctic clashes are abstract expedition incidents unless the countries are already at war.

At peace, an exchange of fire creates:

- casualties or equipment loss
- opinion penalties
- world tension according to severity
- an attributed incident record
- retaliation or negotiation choices

It does not automatically declare a global war.

A repeated high-severity incident may open an ultimatum or war-goal route for the affected countries. Declaration remains a separate national choice governed by normal diplomacy and AI logic.

### Condemnation boundary

Ordinary Antarctic militarisation does not use the shared unconventional-warfare Condemnation system. If later alien technology is used for nuclear, chemical, biological, atrocity, or covered-up actions, those owning systems apply condemnation through their normal contracts.

### Evolved opening

When Event 025 fires after Evolution III is eligible, the first AI expeditions may arrive with escorts and the entry report may show governments claiming security zones. Peaceful participants still have protection and negotiation routes.

## Evolution IV: The Wreck Is Breaking Apart

### Chaos tier

Totalen Chaos, 800 to 999 chaos.

### Playable promise

The wreck is physically unstable. The primary recovery race continues, while valuable fragments spread across several sites and disappear over time.

### Wreck Integrity

Evolution IV activates global Wreck Integrity.

Wreck Integrity falls from:

- active energy pulses
- ice movement
- violent recovery
- armed clashes near the site
- repeated failed extraction
- deliberate demolition
- time spent without stabilization

It rises only through rare stabilization actions and has a strict cap below its original condition.

Wreck Integrity appears as a visual hull state and broad label, not a fourth equal-status meter.

### Fragment sites

The evolution opens six fragment roles:

1. command core debris
2. propulsion or energy components
3. sensor and computation array
4. exotic material plates
5. biological or life-support compartment
6. unknown sealed debris

Final localisation should preserve uncertainty and avoid technical claims before analysis.

### Strategic choice

Participants choose between:

- rushing the primary recovery core
- stabilizing the wreck
- collecting smaller fragments
- blocking rivals from a fragment field
- distributing teams across several sites

### Fragment decay

Each fragment site can become:

- available
- contested
- recovered
- buried
- destroyed
- contaminated

Delay can remove a site. Reckless recovery can lower its reward or raise exposure.

### Primary winner continuity

The race still has one primary winner.

If the hull breaks completely, the command core or equivalent primary recovery package becomes the winning objective. The highest fragment count alone does not automatically replace the winner condition.

### Multi-country rewards

Evolution IV should make losing rewards more important. Several countries can return with meaningful fragments even when one country wins the primary technology.

### Evolved opening

When Event 025 fires after Evolution IV is eligible, the wreck begins unstable and fragment sites appear earlier. The participant still completes mobilization, outpost, survey, and final recovery phases.

## Evolution V: The Technology Changes Its Users

### Chaos tier

World Collapse, 1000 or more chaos.

### Playable promise

Prolonged use changes how scientists, soldiers, engineers, and officials make decisions. Continued study can unlock stronger performance, while dependence, accidents, and institutional loss of control increase.

### Activation scope

Evolution V is a global unlock with country-specific incidents.

It affects only countries that possess recovered Event 025 material, Event 025 fragment records, or a compatible Event 036 alien-recovery source.

Country milestones use the affected country as the evolution-log actor.

### Visible transformation

After primary recovery, Exposure Risk changes presentation to Alien Dependence for affected holders.

Dependence rises from:

- integrating alien systems into military production
- continuing unshielded research
- allowing recovered systems to rewrite design standards
- concealing repeated accidents
- using the survivor or active core as an adviser or control system

Dependence falls from:

- containment
- destroying active material
- independent human verification
- transferring dangerous systems
- separating alien components from command networks

### Holder choices

| Route | Immediate effect | Long-term consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contain | Lower risk and preserve the base technology | No strongest integration bonuses |
| Destroy active material | Ends most future incidents | Keeps learned technology but loses fragment upgrades |
| Transfer | Shares or gives control to a valid partner | Creates diplomatic leverage and dependence on recipient behavior |
| Conceal | Keeps research secret and lowers public pressure | Raises accident and institutional-capture risk |
| Continue integration | Stronger custom-equipment and research benefits | Higher dependence, accidents, and strange doctrine events |

### Strange doctrine and behavior incidents

Possible incidents include:

- engineers refusing to remove an alien component
- military planners adopting patterns they cannot explain
- production lines changing tolerances without authorization
- scientists repeating the same design across unrelated fields
- officials prioritizing signals over national orders
- recovered systems resisting shutdown

The text should show observed behavior. It should not declare possession, mind control, or a fixed alien motive unless a later route proves it.

### Institutional outcomes

The holder may end in one of these persistent states:

- Contained Exotic Technology
- Independent Human Adaptation
- Alien Systems Integration
- Alien Systems Reliance
- Autonomous Research Network

These states should use one staged idea lifecycle, not a stack of many permanent ideas.

### Event 016 relationship

A country with Doctor Kruger or an active Directorate may assign its existing scientific institution to analysis.

This route can:

- speed decoding
- reduce some early technical failures
- increase Event 025 Exposure Risk or Alien Dependence when research is reckless
- influence the broad reward field when a valid compatible family exists
- change the country's Event 025 AI posture

Event 025 must not change Event 016 Dependence, Exposure, project history, evolution state, host Mandate, Project Capacity, or Kruger State progression. Existing Event 016 owner logic may read the neutral external-knowledge ledger later.

The recovered technology remains alien-derived in Event 025 text. Kruger may analyze or claim the material, but Event 025 never identifies him as its origin.

### Event 036 relationship

Evolution V reads the shared alien-recovery ledger. A later Event 036 craft can strengthen the holder's route, expose concealed dependence, provide a safer replacement core, or trigger the upgraded aircraft result.

### Evolved opening

When Event 025 fires at World Collapse tier, exposure incidents can begin during the race. The player still has time to use shielding, containment, and withdrawal actions before the technology is recovered.
