# Localisation and narrative direction

## Purpose

This file gives writing direction. It does not provide final localisation lines.

The implementation agent writes finished text after inspecting the live event chain, selected countries, dynamic values, researched references, and available UI space.

## Overall tone

Baseline tone is scientific urgency, polar isolation, national competition, and incomplete information.

The event can use dry understatement, institutional confidence, logistical frustration, and controlled fear. Cheap jokes would weaken the major-event presentation.

Higher evolutions become stranger and more dangerous, but the text should remain concrete. Describe signals, tracks, opened compartments, damaged camps, changes in behavior, missing personnel, broken instruments, and altered procedures.

Avoid vague claims that the world has changed forever. Show what the expeditions and governments are doing.

## Information discipline

### Public at opening

- an object descended over Antarctica
- several observations support a real impact
- the site is uncertain
- more than one government is preparing an expedition

### Hidden at opening

- exact crash sector
- active signal
- survivor state
- fragment layout
- technology family
- later dependence effects

### Public after confirmation

- the winner
- broad recovered field
- major public clashes
- public transfers or containment agreements

### Private

- exact survey certainty
- hidden coordinates
- sabotage authorship before attribution
- private fragment inventory
- internal Event 016 technology key
- Dependence components

## Event name direction

The catalog name remains Alien Technology in Antarctica.

The opening popup and super-event title may use a shorter discovery phrase after quote and title research. Final wording should avoid generic crisis titles and should not state the craft's nature as proven before recovery.

## Entry event viewpoint

The entry event speaks from the participant government's perspective.

It should mention:

- the country-specific route challenge
- the material commitment
- the chance that rivals move first
- uncertainty about the crash site
- public or secret participation stance

It should not list the three visible values as raw mechanics in prose. Tooltips and the board explain those values.

## Entry option direction

### Enter publicly

Speaker:

Government announcing a national expedition.

Tone:

Confident, scientific, strategic, or patriotic according to country context.

Meaning:

Join with visible commitment and lower secrecy.

### Enter secretly

Speaker:

Cabinet or security establishment authorizing a concealed expedition.

Tone:

Controlled and cautious.

Meaning:

Join with higher secrecy cost and different evidence behavior.

### Decline

Speaker:

Government refusing a remote and expensive gamble.

Tone:

Practical, dismissive, or wary according to route.

Meaning:

Become an observer.

Do not write final button lines in the spec.

## Expedition Board labels

Labels should be short and literal.

Required concepts:

- current phase
- Expedition Progress
- Logistics Readiness
- Exposure Risk
- Alien Dependence
- route state
- outpost state
- survey confidence
- selected rival
- current mission
- winner or resolved state

The board should not use internal terms such as participant index, pulse, event target, weight, Kruger technology, or reward arbitration.

## Main value tooltips

Each value tooltip answers:

- what the value represents
- what raised or lowered it recently
- why the current threshold matters
- which current action can change it

The tooltip should not expose every hidden factor.

## Phase writing direction

### Mobilization

Focus on ships, fuel, stores, personnel, gateway negotiations, and the approaching polar season.

Avoid generic cabinet-room scenes. Use docks, rail yards, warehouses, radio schedules, and expedition volunteers.

### Crossing

Focus on sea ice, weather, mechanical strain, route gaps, navigation, and communications.

### Outpost

Focus on shelter, fuel caches, medical capacity, radio masts, survey instruments, and the first winter or storm.

### Survey

Focus on magnetometers, radio bearings, aerial reconnaissance, ground parties, false leads, and fragment evidence.

### Final recovery

Focus on a confirmed sector, limited weather window, rival movement, unstable material, and the commitment needed to reach the core.

### Aftermath

Focus on how the recovered systems change laboratories, production, doctrine, secrecy, and foreign attention.

## Baseline report families

### Departure report

Viewpoint:

Expedition personnel or dock observers.

Visible facts:

Ships, aircraft, stores, route, public interest.

### Outpost report

Viewpoint:

Camp personnel.

Visible facts:

Shelter condition, radio contact, first usable survey work.

### Fragment report

Viewpoint:

Recovery team or laboratory.

Visible facts:

Material behavior, handling difficulty, broad scientific significance.

### Sabotage report

Viewpoint:

Affected expedition.

Visible facts:

What failed and what evidence exists.

Do not declare the perpetrator when evidence is only suspected.

### Rescue report

Viewpoint:

Rescuers and survivors.

Visible facts:

Conditions, losses prevented, practical debt created.

### Winner report

Viewpoint:

International public and winner's researchers.

Visible facts:

Who secured the core and the broad research field.

Hidden facts:

Internal technology key and future dependence routes.

## Evolution I direction

Write the signal through observed instrument behavior.

Useful content:

- repeated transmission intervals
- damaged radio valves
- compasses or magnetic instruments behaving incorrectly
- signal peaks moving across sectors
- personnel hearing or seeing patterns through equipment

Avoid saying that the signal is a warning or invitation. The player should infer intent from its behavior.

## Evolution II direction

Use tracks, opened compartments, missing stores, rearranged equipment, damaged doors, and personnel disappearances.

The first incidents should not name the survivor's species or nature.

Later text changes according to the resolved profile:

- machine
- organism
- injured crew member
- distributed system
- misleading trace with no living survivor

Do not use a generic monster voice.

## Evolution III direction

Describe warships near ice channels, armed aircraft over camps, fortified huts, detained personnel, and blocked supply routes.

The tone is controlled strategic competition. Do not turn every incident into a declaration of total war.

Option directions may include:

- restrained defense
- official denial
- coercive seizure
- negotiated demilitarisation
- bitter compensation

## Evolution IV direction

Describe material failure, deep crevasses, burning or luminous debris, buried systems, and short recovery windows.

The text should make the core-versus-fragments choice clear without reading like a reward list.

## Evolution V direction

Describe changing procedures and judgment through concrete institutional behavior.

Examples of direction:

- engineers refuse familiar methods because the alien system gives faster answers
- commanders adopt planning assumptions derived from the recovered machine
- laboratories are rebuilt around one interface
- officials hide failures to preserve access
- safety teams discover that shutting a system down now disrupts ordinary production

Avoid body-horror as the default. The main threat is institutional and cognitive dependence.

## Country-specific reaction direction

The implementation may vary tone by broad context:

- maritime powers emphasize ships, gateways, and naval reach
- landlocked countries emphasize chartering, secrecy, and foreign dependence
- scientific powers emphasize research leadership
- wartime governments emphasize strategic advantage
- small countries emphasize the cost and prestige of entering
- Kruger State emphasizes compatibility and ownership of impossible knowledge

Do not write hundreds of tag-specific variants. Use scripted localisation where a meaningful route fact changes the text.

## Ideology direction

Ideology may color rhetoric, not determine every action.

- democratic governments may stress public science, oversight, or allied cooperation
- authoritarian governments may stress secrecy, command, and monopoly
- communist governments may stress collective research or state control
- non-aligned governments may stress sovereignty, prestige, or institutional survival

Avoid caricature and copied slogans.

## Humour and irony

Minor dry irony may appear in logistics reports, bureaucratic mistakes, false maps, or rivalry.

Mass casualty, missing personnel, captivity, and dangerous dependence should use serious wording.

Do not use cheap alien jokes, internet references, or modern conspiracy language unless a later event explicitly calls for them.

## Super-event writing direction

### Title

Short, specific, and connected to Antarctica, descent, or discovery.

### Description

Explain the observed event and the emerging international race. Preserve uncertainty about the craft.

### Quote

Verified historical, literary, philosophical, scientific, or polar-exploration source.

### Button

Short reaction with dry expeditionary or scientific tone. Cultural reference requires verification.

The super-event research skill owns candidate selection and attribution.

## Event Details direction

Event Details should describe:

- the impact and race premise
- the need to establish a route and outpost
- the fact that the first expedition to secure the core wins
- public evolution state after it is revealed
- resolved winner when known

It should not list exact rewards, hidden thresholds, secret actions, future evolutions, or internal ledgers.

## Evolution log direction

Each evolution needs:

- distinct working type and stage identity
- public name after reveal
- concise premise
- actor only when the evolution belongs to a specific technology holder

Evolution V may log a global first reveal and later country-specific milestones if the implementation needs separate actor records. Avoid flooding the evolution history with every national Dependence stage.

## Achievement localisation direction

Achievement names can use polar, scientific, and expeditionary allusions.

Descriptions must state the real public objective and important disqualifier. They should not expose internal flags or hidden random logic.

## Words and frames to avoid

Avoid:

- Kruger technology in player-facing Event 025 text
- alien proven before recovery
- generic final crisis language
- empty claims that humanity will never be the same
- contrast templates built from officials denying what witnesses see
- staged warning language
- stock staff-table scenes
- modern scientific jargon that does not fit the period
- raw variable names
- implementation history
- claims that the event was reworked or newly added
- repeated short sentences for artificial weight

## Dynamic localisation requirements

Final text should insert verified dynamic facts when space allows:

- current country
- selected gateway or route
- selected rival
- affected expedition
- broad crash sector
- fragment category
- winner
- broad technology field
- Dependence stage
- public incident evidence state

Dynamic text must fail safely when a target becomes invalid.
