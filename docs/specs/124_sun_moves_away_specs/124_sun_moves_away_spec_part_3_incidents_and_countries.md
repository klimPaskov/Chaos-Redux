# Part 3: Regional incidents, ordinary life and different countries

## A world that continues to function unevenly

The event should show a society trying to sustain familiar work under unfamiliar conditions.
Trains still run when workers can keep junctions usable.
Families adapt their routines when schools, kitchens and clinics remain open.
Factories can maintain protected shifts, although the fuel and workers assigned to them have other possible uses.
The player sees where those arrangements succeed and where they fail.

Predators become one part of that landscape.
Most baseline reports concern cold, visibility, exhaustion, lost access and uncertainty.
Early sightings can be mistaken, while verified disappearances and attacks have concrete receipts when they cause real harm.
Things in the Darkness changes that pattern by establishing actual organized regional predators.

The descriptions in this part are narrative and choice directions.
They are not final event titles, descriptions or option text.
Working incident handles support the specification and asset inventory only.

## Incident selection and attention

The event maintains incidents in affected state or route records.
Eligibility comes from actual local conditions, a valid responsible actor and an episode identity.
A prepared target, an active consequence and a completed consequence are different states.
Every paid-response mission points to one valid incident generation.

Routine consequences continue through the owning systems even when no popup is displayed.
The event selects a small number of important situations for player attention and batches lesser developments into the management panel.
The proposed notification budget is one ordinary incident popup per country per 15 days, with emergency warnings allowed to replace a lower-priority queued notification.
No country has more than three concurrent event missions.

A full mission list cannot silently discard a paid commitment, erase a threatened population or immediately fail a new situation without notice.
The owner queues nonurgent work, applies the current national policy to routine services, and offers the highest-priority actionable incident when space opens.
An urgent threat still produces its warning and a safe default response within existing capacity.
The safe default is conservative protection or orderly service reduction, without spending unavailable resources or promising a rescue it cannot perform.

A state ordinarily waits at least 45 days before receiving another incident from the same family.
An established organized predator center uses its own proposed 30-day attack spacing, with one unresolved attack at a time.
Global and local receipt limits remain stable through capture, country release and save/load.
Moving a target between countries does not restart its warning or create a new attack roll.

## Everyday incident families

| Handle and trigger | Player choice direction | Material result and tradeoff | Resolution and continuity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I01, unreliable heating in an exposed settlement | Supply emergency heating, organize smaller local warmth points, or move a supported group to a valid shelter | Fuel and equipment can preserve service, while local labor has lower reach and occupies productive work | Measure actual supported residents and warmth intervals, carry any physical damage into repair |
| I02, a kitchen or distribution point loses deliveries | Restore the route, request an available owner-funded shipment, or reallocate existing lawful food access | Helps shared famine pressure only when food or delivery exists, with transport and recipient needs fully accounted | Publish actual delivery or failed access, never a free food grant from an acknowledgement |
| I03, workers repeatedly miss or confuse shifts | Fund rest rotations and lighting, reorganize fewer reliable shifts, or protect one essential production site | Improves the relevant deprivation contribution at a real output or service opportunity cost | Benefits follow actual covered work intervals and end when the commitment ends |
| I04, household sleep and school routines collapse | Coordinate daytime-equivalent schedules, support communal care, or use existing local services on a reduced timetable | Stabilizes routines and reduces avoidable exhaustion without claiming that visible lamps cure nutritional illness | The report can remain low-stakes when service is functioning, with no compulsory casualty |
| I05, transport accidents on an unmarked road | Mark and light the route, slow and escort essential traffic, or temporarily close it | Safety competes with throughput and equipment or fuel use | Record observed accident losses separately from predator losses and restore only actual service |
| I06, a rail junction or port handling area fails | Assign construction and maintenance capacity, route around the damage, or reduce the served area | Repairs an identified damaged asset or preserves a smaller reliable delivery chain | Completion verifies usable service, and a late callback cannot repair a recaptured replacement target |
| I07, rumors empty an otherwise usable route | Conduct an accountable search and publish findings, provide a temporary escort, or keep the current allocation | Consumes administrative or security attention, with the chance to resolve a false alarm without killing imaginary victims | A false alarm closes the record, while a verified living threat transfers into its proper incident owner |
| I08, an outlying clinic cannot keep staff and supplies together | Protect its route, move care into a supplied settlement, or serve fewer residents reliably | Changes basic care and shelter capacity, with actual reception pressure if patients move | No medical miracle or universal illness timer is introduced |

These families include small recoveries and competent local action.
A maintained route can generate a report that acknowledges successful delivery without giving extra resources or Chaos reduction for the report itself.
A school or clinic can continue operating under a protected schedule, giving the player a concrete account of the service their earlier decision supports.

## Threat and rescue families

| Handle and trigger | Available responses | Stakes and failure | Proof required |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I09, travelers missing on a valid exposed route | Conduct the bounded rescue mission, secure the route before proceeding, or suspend further travel | A rescue may recover some people while leaving equipment lost, and delay can reduce the reachable group | Identify the real travelers and origin record, then record survivors, observed deaths and unresolved cases without inventing population |
| I10, a convoy or overland consignment threatened by a living center | Protect delivery, reroute within real access, or cancel an uncommitted shipment | A failed attack can destroy the actually dispatched cargo or interrupt delivery | Cargo, sender, recipient, route and dispatch receipts must exist before a loss is applied |
| I11, an isolated military outpost is under repeated pressure | Assign a real security commitment, improve its corridor, or withdraw from the exposed position | Diverted capacity affects other duties, and withdrawal can expose nearby civilian service | The military owner identifies a real eligible commitment, with no fictitious battalion or automatic battle-plan order |
| I12, organized attacks surround a settlement | Contain the verified center, protect a smaller served perimeter, or evacuate through a valid route | Failure can cause one accountable primary loss plus explicitly linked interruption or damage | A live center, exposed residents, incident warning, selected outcome and one consequence receipt |
| I13, a refuge loses a critical delivery | Spend a valid reserve shipment, reduce supported capacity, or begin an orderly transfer | A lit settlement becomes partially functioning when its weakest service fails | Upkeep receipt, actual stocks, recipient capacity and elapsed service determine the outcome |
| I14, an aid partner cannot complete its promise | Renegotiate a smaller deliverable, find a valid alternative route, or release the unused commitment | The sender may lose spent transport capacity, while the recipient's underlying need remains | Bilateral sender and recipient receipts prevent duplicated cargo and repeated diplomatic rewards |

Rescue missions use proposed emergency deadlines of 15 to 30 days because the threatened people or cargo cannot wait for a long industrial project.
The deadline is explicit when the player accepts.
Partial success has a defined physical meaning, such as a smaller rescued cohort or delivered consignment, and pays out only that amount.
A mission succeeds when its actual conditions are satisfied.
It does not require buying the same rescue again through a completion button.

If Return occurs during an incident, darkness-driven preparation and further attacks stop immediately.
A person already displaced, a damaged bridge or a stranded lawful transfer remains real.
The relevant recovery owner either continues the physical operation under ordinary daylight conditions or closes the no-longer-valid threat with exact unspent-resource treatment.

## Rare, condition-bound variants

Rare variants add a different decision or interpretation to an existing incident family.
They use a proposed 5% to 10% share of the already eligible family pool and at most one appearance of each variant per episode.
They never replace a mandatory paid outcome with an unannounced lottery.

| Variant | Required conditions | Distinct decision and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A preserved local heating practice | A cold-prepared rural state with functioning local organization and some usable capacity | Lower the project's transport burden by serving a smaller local population through labor, while still committing that labor and equipment |
| A desert distribution network caught by extreme cold | Deep Freeze active, low cold preparedness and an actual exposed water or transport dependency | Prioritize repairing the lifeline or move residents to a viable supply point, with terrain and real access deciding which is possible |
| An observatory keeps communicating | A valid research or communications site remains serviced through the crisis | Improve diagnosis and coordination of local needs, with honest uncertainty about Return and no secret control over the Sun |
| A mistaken predator report | Baseline sighting without verified creature evidence, with a search actually completed | Recover confidence and reopen a route when the evidence supports it, with no false death debit or free creature reward |
| Former enemies exchange a relief consignment | A valid accepted recipient, an achievable route and an actual bilateral agreement despite strained relations | A small, delivered assistance action can improve the immediate relationship through the existing diplomatic owner without ending a war or changing factions |
| A refuge reaches its design limit | A paid refuge has real reception pressure and a pending group of arrivals | Expand through paid capacity, accept a smaller group or arrange another destination, with every person's final location conserved |

No variant makes one country the universal solver of the crisis.
Historical and regional detail should come from verified context and the actual game state, with no assumption that every state in a climate zone shares the same institutions.

## Country situations

### Cold-prepared industrial country

Such a country begins with more useful maintenance habits and shelter capacity when the actual state data supports them.
Its strongest opening is often to preserve rail, heating and industrial staff, then support exposed neighbors or peripheral regions.
It still faces limited fuel, long routes, food production losses and all-hour night combat.
Deep Freeze reduces its safety margin without deleting the value of its preparation.

### Tropical or desert country with little cold preparation

Cold can disrupt work and transport before ordinary buildings or households can adapt.
The country needs affordable small warmth projects, local service organization and carefully chosen high-value routes.
Importing sophisticated equipment is one route when it exists, while low-capacity labor and salvage remain useful at a smaller scale.
Deep Freeze makes the gap in preparation more serious, with clear warnings about the people and services exposed.

### Small country with scarce industry

The design must remain playable when a country has no spare civilian factory and a small equipment balance.
Projects can accept a smaller served population and use distributed labor with a visible production opportunity cost.
The price ceiling reduces project scale instead of allowing a tiny country to purchase national coverage at the same small price.
Aid still requires a valid sender and delivery path.
A narrow reliable route and a few functioning community services can be a successful strategy.

### Island or disconnected territory

The central question is actual access to the essential goods the territory needs.
A port, available convoy capacity and protected sea delivery can matter more than nearby land infrastructure.
Bad sea access cannot be bypassed by an overland escort button.
The country can preserve a smaller supplied population, request real assistance or evacuate where an accepted destination and route exist.
It is vulnerable when delivery fails, with the famine owner deciding the consequences of its real stocks and need.

### Continental country at war

The military and civilian systems compete for fuel, equipment, transport and protected operating capacity.
Diverting military fuel creates actual opportunity costs.
Night-trained units retain their relative strengths throughout the day, while poorly supplied forces struggle under cold and deprivation.
That relative advantage depends on the all-hour native night-combat behavior required in [part 1](124_sun_moves_away_spec_part_1_core.md#night-rules-at-every-hour), which remains an unproven engine capability.
The event can influence AI priorities and paid commitments through supported strategy interfaces, without promising that ordinary script issues battle plans.

### Occupied territory and civil war

The current controller has immediate service responsibilities within its valid authority.
Ownership, legal custody and humanitarian eligibility determine which transfers or aid actions can occur.
Physical shelters and repaired routes remain in their states when control changes, while operating contracts require a new valid payer.
Old payments cannot fund both sides, and an old popup cannot debit a successor or restore a captured asset under a stale target.
Residents who leave through shared migration are no longer counted as simultaneously protected at their origin.

### Subjects and externally supported countries

The subject can undertake lawful local work with its own resources.
An overlord or partner can offer real aid when the existing diplomatic and humanitarian rules permit it.
The plan does not assume an automatic free shared stockpile.
The route, payer, recipient and delivery conditions remain visible, and a failed shipment does not silently transfer the same cargo to another recipient.

### Actual nocturnal countries and mixed forces

A genuinely nocturnal country can find new military opportunities while the physical cold still affects transport and equipment.
Human-specific systems apply only where their owners accept the population and state relationship.
A human country hosting an eligible creature unit remains a human country for civilian accounting.
Its human divisions do not inherit that unit's temporary creature advantage.
Canonical creature owners retain their own politics, recruitment, costs and cleanup.

## Political consequences without a second political game

Allocation choices can create coherent friction over who receives protection and who bears the cost.
Industrial continuity can create grievances in neglected rural regions.
Concentrated refuge support can strain host settlements and create difficult reception decisions.
Distributed relief can demand prolonged labor and restrict other construction.

Those consequences use the existing stability, service, diplomatic and humanitarian systems where appropriate.
They do not require a new public approval meter or a universal government replacement branch.
The player should see the affected group, the material issue and the available response.
Country identity and ongoing wars remain meaningful throughout the episode.

## Continuity of narrative evidence

Reports refer to actual conditions that can still be inspected when they appear.
When a pending report becomes stale because the route changed hands, the threat ended or Return occurred, it closes or adapts without applying obsolete effects.
The prose never says that thousands died when the protected population floor allowed only a smaller observed loss.
It never reports a delivered shipment that only had a pressure request or an unfulfilled promise.

Presentation can acknowledge successful service without paying a second reward.
Failure can preserve partial achievements and physical improvements without erasing what went wrong.
The event's record should leave the player with a coherent account of the places they protected, the losses they could not prevent and the work that remains after sunrise.
