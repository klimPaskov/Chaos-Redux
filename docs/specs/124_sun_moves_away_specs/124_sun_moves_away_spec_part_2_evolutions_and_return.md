# Part 2: Evolutions, uncertain duration and Return of the Sun

This part is an unresolved design proposal, and its numeric values are proposed tuning.
Its Return transaction depends on the two unproven engine mechanisms named in [part 1](124_sun_moves_away_spec_part_1_core.md): reversible whole-map daytime darkness and normal native night combat at every hour.

## What an evolution changes

Each evolution introduces a new problem with a different response.
Deep Freeze makes heat and transport capacity central to survival.
Things in the Darkness turns scattered dangers into organized regional attacks.
The Endless Night makes continued civilian life depend on supplied settlements that can maintain light, heat, food access and protection over a long period.
The enabled behaviors accumulate, while disabled lower stages are skipped through the ordinary system.

Reaching a threshold creates eligibility.
The ordinary active-event path uses dynamic stochastic pacing before an evolution actually begins.
Eligibility, a settings change, an activation flag, a log entry and an unlock produce no direct Chaos.
Concrete consequences use the [Chaos impact map](124_sun_moves_away_spec_part_6_integrations_and_chaos.md).

## Evolution I: Deep Freeze

### Entry and transformation

Deep Freeze becomes eligible at 400 Chaos when enabled and not already active.
It strengthens the global winter in every state, including deserts and tropical regions.
Countries that previously relied on ordinary heating habits encounter shortages in transport capacity, safe fuel distribution, insulated shelter and cold-weather maintenance.
Prepared cold countries retain useful equipment and experience, although the more severe global conditions still burden them.

The strongest change is a sustained mismatch between essential demand and working service capacity.
Deliveries that were marginally sufficient can fall below the level needed to keep dispersed settlements warm.
Damaged junctions and ports have wider consequences because alternative routes are also cold and under strain.
The state consequences in part 1 move to the Deep Freeze target column.

### Player decisions and outcomes

Heating projects, road servicing, repair and military fuel diversion gain importance.
Countries can concentrate supplies along a smaller number of dependable routes, support dispersed local labor or move people into places with real reception capacity.
Each choice changes who remains exposed.
An industrial priority may preserve national output while allowing vulnerable agricultural settlements to lose more capacity.

Famine receives pressure only where an owner-supported request identifies real affected residents, lost access or production, an actor and a proven cause.
The famine system continues to own reserves, stage warnings, mortality and recovery.
Direct cold-exposure mortality is a separate physical cause and must be accounted for separately from starvation.

As a proposed balance envelope, sustained unprotected exposure can create 50 to 150 direct cold deaths per 100,000 exposed residents over 30 days under Deep Freeze.
Baseline cold exposure uses a lower envelope of 5 to 25 per 100,000 over 30 days.
These are fictional game-balance targets for severe service failure, not predictions of real-world physiology.
They apply only after a supported period of failed warmth and shelter, with an initial warning and roughly 30 days of accumulated untreated exposure for a newly stable population.
Pre-existing hunger or another owner's lethal incident follows that owner's separate warning and timing rules.
Event-owned deaths use the exact observed-loss path and never duplicate famine deaths.

The world begins using the longer Return median when Deep Freeze becomes the highest active evolution.
The transition does not clear history or briefly restore daylight.

## Evolution II: Things in the Darkness

### Regional predators become a real threat

Things in the Darkness becomes eligible at 600 Chaos.
Remote disappearances develop into organized attacks on supply convoys, isolated settlements and military outposts.
The new regional predators are owned by this evolution's incident system, with a real location, active population or presence proof, territory of operation, attack history and containment state.
Their effects are material losses and interrupted services, with accountable victims and repairable damage.

A region can support one active predator center at a time, with subordinate attacks referring to the same center.
For ordinary pacing, a country can receive at most one new center per 30 days and hold at most three unresolved centers at once.
Centers prioritize isolated exposed human settlements with plausible access to a target corridor.
Dense protected interiors and places without a valid target are low or zero priority.
Country splits and control changes preserve the center's state identity and original cadence, so they cannot create a fresh spawn allowance for the same place.

When several eligible regions compete, choose through a complete eligible pool weighted by isolation, real exposure, failed transport and existing attack evidence.
A blocked or cleared center does not immediately recreate itself in the neighboring state.
The cleared region receives a proposed 90-day reprieve from a new center in this episode, while a different already living external actor can still follow its own rules.

### Attacks and counterplay

A center progresses from verified activity to a prepared incursion and then an attack or containment outcome.
It cannot silently inflict large losses without an intelligible warning and an available emergency response.
The exception is a small first encounter whose limited loss establishes the threat and unlocks the larger response.
Each center has at most one active paid-response incident at a time.

Protected travel, a real security commitment, functioning communications and serviced routes reduce the chance of an effective attack.
Closing a route can protect travelers while depriving settlements of supply.
Evacuating a settlement requires a real destination and physical transfer.
Pursuing predators occupies forces and transport capacity that could otherwise support a front.

A failed organized incursion can destroy an identified shipment, damage a selected infrastructure or service level, interrupt delivery, or kill a measured portion of the actually exposed population.
The incident chooses one primary physical loss and any explicitly linked consequences.
It does not charge an entire shipment, all residents and unrelated construction as three independent versions of the same attack.
Severity scales with the target and the elapsed unprotected service interval.
Successful containment stops that center's organized attacks and records the actual means of containment.

### Established nocturnal actors

Actual surviving zombies, Wendigos, vampires and other verified darkness-adapted creatures receive temporary advantages through their owning family or population systems.
An event-fired flag, available unit definition or historical catalog row is insufficient proof of survival.
An extinct actor remains extinct.
The new regional predator centers are a separate evolution feature and never count as resurrected canonical actors.

All-hour night rules already change the usefulness of existing night adaptations.
Additional Sun-owned advantages therefore use a restrained proposed range of 10% to 20% in an appropriate family-specific recovery, movement or incursion-capacity effect, with the higher end reserved for this evolution's verified conditions.
The integration must inspect existing bonuses and avoid stacking a second blanket night-attack increase onto large existing night modifiers.
Mixed armies gain the advantage only for verified eligible units or populations.
If the supported temporary family-specific carrier is unresolved, that integration remains a capability gate.

The highest-active-stage Return median becomes longer when this evolution begins.
Disabling Deep Freeze does not disable this evolution, and its attacks do not quietly install the disabled Deep Freeze cold layer.

## Evolution III: The Endless Night

### Sustained refuges

The Endless Night becomes eligible at 800 Chaos.
The uncertain crisis now demands sustained provision for everyday life.
Ordinary shelter programs remain useful, while selected functioning settlements can become lit refuges with dependable warmth, communications, treatment, food access and guarded approaches.

A refuge requires a valid populated site, working access, sufficient service space and a funded operating plan.
Construction or conversion consumes equipment and construction capacity.
Operation consumes actual fuel, equipment for replacement and repair, and continuing construction or maintenance capacity at the scale accepted by the project.
The player cannot pay only for an illuminated image and receive permanent protection.

A refuge's supported population is limited by its weakest essential service and by actual reception capacity.
Existing residents have needs before newcomers arrive.
Migration records every moved person once and credits only survivors at the destination.
Overcrowding lowers effective protection and can deepen the destination's own shortages.
A refuge may be useful while operating below its designed capacity.

The country may reduce its served area, scale down operating commitments, evacuate through valid routes or close a failing site.
Scaling down preserves delivered supplies and physical improvements.
It removes service capacity for residents who can no longer be reached, with an explicit warning about their next available response.
Abandonment does not delete residents or erase displaced cohorts.

### Longer exposure and the unprotected landscape

Food production and civilian service quality deteriorate when the crisis outlasts reserves, maintenance intervals and ordinary household coping capacity.
The additional agricultural burden is driven by prolonged exposure and proven service failure, then routed through famine.
It does not create an independent event food currency or a fixed date on which everyone starves.

Outside functioning protection, unused roads and isolated settlements become more dangerous.
Existing regional predators can extend an operating corridor when their center has actual reach and an exposed target.
This does not create a second unlimited center-spawning loop.
When Things in the Darkness is disabled, The Endless Night still increases ordinary isolation and existing nocturnal pressure, while its organized-center content remains absent.

The highest-active-stage Return median becomes 810 days as a proposed constant-condition balance anchor.
That value expresses uncertainty over a much longer period without creating a fixed end date.

## Active evolution pacing

| Stage | Required current Chaos | Proposed constant-condition median after eligibility | Dynamic factors |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Deep Freeze | 400+ | 90 eligible days | Higher Chaos above the threshold and a long existing crisis accelerate progression |
| Things in the Darkness | 600+ | 120 eligible days | Higher Chaos, sustained exposed routes and elapsed crisis increase urgency |
| The Endless Night | 800+ | 150 eligible days | Higher Chaos and sustained service exhaustion increase urgency |

Use a proposed factor of 0.75 when Chaos exceeds a stage's threshold by at least 200, and another factor of 0.75 after at least 180 crisis days.
The resulting median has a 30-day floor.
Local threat or service conditions may choose targets and incident pressure, while the initial evolution timing comparison uses the stated common factors so it remains auditable.

The lowest enabled eligible stage that is not active owns the next ordinary evolution opportunity.
A disabled lower stage does not block higher eligible stages.
If a previously disabled lower stage is enabled later, it can receive its own valid future opportunity without rewriting earlier history or reducing the highest active stage.
Falling below the required Chaos suspends that stage's eligibility.
Suspension preserves its eligible-time record and does not reroll the entire episode.

An evolution already active remains active until Return if its future-activation toggle is later disabled.
The interface must explain the difference between future activation and current world state.
Every recorded activation uses the current evolution toggle gate and the shared history owner.
Disabled ordinary content does not gain flags, history entries or effects indirectly.

## Evolved first entry

The first normal entry can select a stronger opening through a complete pool.
Weights are relative, and only eligible enabled stage candidates enter that pool.
Baseline always remains available.

| Current Chaos | Baseline | Deep Freeze | Things in the Darkness | The Endless Night |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Below 400, while ordinary event eligibility is satisfied | 100 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 400 to 599 | 60 | 40 | 0 | 0 |
| 600 to 799 | 20 | 40 | 40 | 0 |
| 800 or above | 5 | 15 | 30 | 50 |

Remove disabled candidates before normalizing.
At 800 Chaos with only The Endless Night enabled, the surviving weights are 5 and 50, so the intended selection probabilities are 1/11 and 10/11.
That selected higher opening installs only its enabled lower behaviors plus the chosen stage.
It does not force disabled evolutions back into the campaign.

The global entry, chosen behavior bundle and actual evolution history commit as one episode.
Each installed evolution records once with a common opening date and its proper stage identity.
Countries receive an opening that describes the actual conditions without a chain of duplicate manifestation windows.
No separate Chaos award accompanies each installed evolution.

## Return probability and uncertainty

Return of the Sun uses a positive event-owned stochastic hazard throughout the active episode.
There is no final expiry timer, hidden maximum duration or forced fallback that changes the world back after a fixed number of days.
The highest active evolution selects the current reference median.

| Highest active behavior | Proposed reference median T |
| --- | ---: |
| Baseline | 240 days |
| Deep Freeze | 360 days |
| Things in the Darkness | 540 days |
| The Endless Night | 810 days |

For a constant-condition interval of delta days, the design probability of Return is `1 - 2^(-delta / T)`.
This gives a 50% chance over one reference median and 75% over two medians when conditions remain unchanged.
It is a desired probability model, not a claim that the native MTTH keyword or an existing helper automatically supplies that distribution.
The probability handoff must verify how the supported consumer implements it.

When an evolution begins, its larger T reduces the future Return hazard.
Elapsed crisis history, service exposure, missions and already recorded outcomes remain unchanged.
The model does not multiply all three delays or reset a guaranteed return deadline, because it has no such deadline.
Every supported state retains a positive Return probability.

Observation programs improve local information, coordination and honest public communication.
They cannot buy an astronomical return, reveal a hidden countdown or guarantee a safe completion date.
The current Holy Realm Sun and Moon power was inspected and does not provide a Return callback, so this specification does not alias it into one.
Any future separately accepted external resolver must request the same idempotent Return transaction after proving its own success.

If a Return and an evolution would resolve in the same authoritative update, Return wins.
The resolved episode invalidates the evolution callback before it can install content or log history.
Saving and loading cannot create additional independent rolls for the same completed interval.

## Immediate Return transaction

Return resolves the authoritative episode once.
It changes the world before country acknowledgements, report windows or player choices.

1. Mark the episode resolved and prevent new Sun attacks, pressure requests, upkeep starts and evolution activations.
2. Restore the ordinary map presentation and ordinary day and night combat behavior immediately.
3. Remove the Sun-owned winter, deprivation and temporary creature contributions in their exact holder scopes, including forced dynamic-modifier refresh where needed.
4. Cancel future Sun-owned jobs and invalidate their episode and generation receipts.
5. Release unused reservations and construction commitments to the lawful payer or a validated successor once.
6. Preserve actual consumed supplies, paid labor, casualties, building damage, famine state and migration cohorts.
7. Publish the resolution and the appropriate global presentation once, then expose remaining recovery work.

The transaction must not wait for a timed idea to expire, an unverified removal callback, the next daily modifier refresh or a popup response.
State or country iteration must cover the entire registered affected set without leaving temporary effects behind in unvisited tails.
Independent winter, pollution, existing creature bonuses and other owners' crises remain intact.

A paid project that no longer has a useful Sun service ends its temporary commitment.
Delivered equipment and fuel are consumed and are not refunded.
An actual unfinished repair or evacuation remains a valid shared-system obligation when its owner accepts continuation.
The UI explains which task ended and which physical work continues.

Established creatures keep their actual surviving units and populations under their own owners.
The Sun removes only its temporary delta.
Regional predator centers cease darkness-driven expansion and organized outward attacks.
Surviving local populations are recorded as withdrawn or contained in a bounded aftermath record, with no hidden Sun bonus or automatic recreation of lost residents.

## Recovery and presentation gates

After Return, the event panel becomes a concise recovery index for actual damaged routes, unfinished lawful transfers and remaining shared relief needs.
The two crisis values are retired from the active national dashboard.
The recovery index links to the authoritative systems and does not continue levying a darkness maintenance tax.
Completed recovery work can close its event receipt while preserving immutable history and achievement proof.

The manifestation super-event uses the committed global start.
The Endless Night presentation fires once only when Evolution III is active and a material global condition has persisted.
The intended conditions are either a paid refuge network serving at least 1% of eligible world population across at least three independently controlled countries, each sheltering at least 10% of its own eligible population for 30 days, or at least 20% of eligible world population living under Civilian Protection below 35 for 30 consecutive days after Evolution III.
Shared state and service receipts maintain these aggregates through their existing changes, with no new whole-world polling scan.
World-population shares use the fixed episode denominator and lawful cohort accounting defined in part 6.
The low-protection condition uses the underlying local essential-service assessment for the affected residents, so a national average cannot label a well-protected locality as unprotected or hide an isolated failing one.
Crossing an evolution flag alone does not satisfy the presentation gate.

Return receives the costly-aftermath super-event when the episode lasted at least 180 days, or when observed Sun-attributed civilian deaths reached 100,000, actual unique Sun-caused displaced people reached 1,000,000, or registered Sun-caused damage reached 10 infrastructure or factory level-equivalents.
These are presentation tuning anchors and exact attributed facts, with reused shared receipts and no duplicate population accounting.
A shorter, lower-damage Return uses global news.
The thresholds do not change the Return probability or delay cleanup for a presentation.
Final researched text and audio remain governed by [part 7](124_sun_moves_away_spec_part_7_presentation.md).

## Manual setup family

The future manual setup family offers Baseline, Deep Freeze, Things in the Darkness and The Endless Night as explicit cumulative behavior bundles.
The chosen bundle is respectively baseline only, baseline plus I, baseline plus I and II, or baseline plus I, II and III.
Manual confirmation can bypass ordinary Chaos, history, date and default evolution-enable gates for this single launch.
It does not change the campaign's saved global settings.
The preview must state the selected bundle before confirmation.

| Intensity | Proposed pressure multiplier | What changes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Low | 0.75 | Lighter environmental and incident pressure, more manageable initial service strain |
| Medium | 1.00 | Reference design pressure |
| High | 1.25 | Stronger losses and service strain at the same behavior type |
| Maximum | 1.50 | Severe pressure requiring active management and sustainable capacity |

Intensity does not set a fixed Return duration, create fake surviving canonical creatures or bypass exact civilian-system eligibility.
The chosen type still determines the Return median.
Heat Wave remains a hard physical conflict and blocks launch until its own active state has validly ended.
Cancellation changes nothing, confirmation uses the previewed values, and duplicate confirmation cannot create a second episode.
Normal achievements are disabled for the manually created episode.

The stable numeric scenario ID remains unallocated until the current workbook, implemented registry, shared identifier registry and other active specification reservations are checked together.
The manual scenario must enter the same owner lifecycle as ordinary play, with a bounded setup-bypass receipt that is consumed and cleared.
