# What other governments can obtain (block 1)

Verbatim part of [089_doctor_wu_spec_part_11_foreign_medicine_and_scientific_competition.md](089_doctor_wu_spec_part_11_foreign_medicine_and_scientific_competition.md), which indexes this specification.

## What other governments can obtain

A country with an epidemic needs medicine that can reach its hospitals. An industrial partner may need the standard that lets its own factories produce that medicine. An army with altered soldiers needs a qualified service that will remain after the first shipment. A government offering Wu asylum may receive one frightened physician, a dated archive, and several unfinished obligations. These are different bargains, and their differences should determine foreign play.

The host can become a dependable medical partner, a selective supplier, a commercial centre, a guarded military patron, or a country whose institutes are increasingly isolated. Foreign countries have their own aims. They can cooperate over a cure and oppose the same institute's military work, employ a former assistant without obtaining Wu, or shelter a qualified copy who disagrees with the original. A fulfilled agreement gives those choices a lasting basis.

The production, coverage, and access policies in Part 4 remain the medical foundation. This part develops the countries and institutions making those agreements, the things they can obtain, and the consequences when commitments fail. Foreign activity begins through actual contact, need, or evidence. It is available during Calm World. The later International Biological Contest changes its reach and pressure without creating the first possibility of diplomacy or espionage.

All new durations, costs, limits, and comparison values below are proposed tuning anchors. Existing owner payments and cancellation rules retain their own authority. Working route, case, and programme labels identify design roles and are not final localisation.

### Knowledge, material, people, appointments, and service

A knowledge agreement names the completed treatment, qualification record, or fixed design being copied. It includes its known limitations and the date of the information supplied. A recipient keeps a legitimate copy after a treaty ends or Wu changes employment. Manufacture and field use still need the appropriate facility, staff, permission, and material. Receiving an incomplete record starts a reconstruction or validation task with its genuine missing work intact.

A material agreement names an actual stock lot, quantity, current holder, location, standard, and permitted destination. It cannot be paid from an item already committed to a treatment course or native operation. The lot keeps its properties through sale, capture, transit, retooling, and a change of government. The physical owner settles departure and arrival once. A promise to manufacture later is recorded as future output, with its due date and production commitment, and is never presented as current stock.

A personnel agreement identifies the person or existing team, qualifications, present appointment, willingness where the arrangement is voluntary, and the work they can perform. An assistant carries the knowledge they actually learned. A copied specialist carries their own completed training and imperfect memory. Neither brings the complete portfolio simply because their employer was Wu.

An appointment gives a person a defined office after arrival and acceptance. Asylum, confinement, a university visit, a medical secondment, and appointment as institute director remain separate states. A captured Wu does not immediately command the captor's hospitals. A visiting Wu does not occupy the resident advisor office in two countries at once.

A service agreement commits qualified people and suitable supplies over time. It names the patients or formations, provider, end date, last safe handover date, and responsibility for interruption. It can survive a political rupture when the parties preserve a limited care arrangement. A former supplier may stop approving new clients while completing an existing course.

These objects may travel together, but the agreement shows them separately. A successful extraction of a scientist can fail to recover the archive. A seized archive can arrive with its medicines destroyed. A purchase can obtain sufficient medicine for one programme without any right to manufacture more. Reports describe those actual results and leave missing objects missing.

## The first foreign approach

Foreign interest starts from a reason that the country can know. Public treatment, a licensed standard, an accepted visiting programme, a shared outbreak report, a captured institute, an existing intelligence finding, or a request from a medical partner can establish contact. A secret institute does not appear in every foreign target list on the day it is founded.

An affected country can seek medical help without knowing that Wu exists. It sends a request through an available diplomatic or humanitarian relationship. The host can answer through an ordinary national medical office and keep Wu's personal involvement confidential. The recipient learns what it received and who supplied it, not every hidden research programme behind the supplier.

A country with no immediate outbreak can request a reserve agreement, training, or a standing emergency arrangement. A military rival can study a publicly demonstrated capability or pursue an authenticated intelligence lead. An asylum offer needs contact with the intended person or an institution able to relay it. Rumours can justify an inquiry, but they cannot establish exact stocks, locations, staff loyalties, or current treatment compatibility.

### Relevant actors and manageable requests

The host's ordinary foreign view concentrates on three current cases. An accepted medical service remains active even when it is no longer one of those three cases. The limit governs political attention and presentation, not the number of hospitals that can receive medicine. Routine contracts operate through their existing production and care owners.

Within a selected case, the player normally sees a practical choice, a counteroffer, a refusal or postponement, and any necessary protective response. Six primary actions is the maximum for that visible state. One to three urgent missions may be shown with it. A list of agreements is a record browser, and selecting a record reveals only its relevant actions.

Requests from the same country for the same clinical need merge. An updated number of patients or a newly unsuitable treatment changes the existing case. A genuinely different emergency can have a separate subject. A hostile government cannot exhaust the host's scarce staff by repeatedly submitting unfunded offers.

For proposed discretionary covert work, each foreign actor can maintain one active Wu operation at a time. A host normally accepts at most two active hostile Wu operations into the concealed operation system. Those are pacing limits for this event's optional operations, not protection from ordinary war or from another owner's valid operation. Ordinary clinical requests do not consume hostile slots, and hostile actors cannot reserve clinical capacity by occupying those slots. Accepted care continues during an investigation or intelligence operation.

Selection and refresh use affected countries, existing partners, known interested actors, and explicit player searches. Changes of host, major public discoveries, completed agreements, and new owner-supplied evidence can refresh that bounded set. No new daily, weekly, or monthly whole-world scan is introduced.

### Access has a purpose

Clinical access permits the agreed treatment work. Training access permits the named curriculum. Research access permits work on specified material under its recorded terms. A military liaison permits contact with the selected command or formation. Inspection access permits the stated review. None supplies the others automatically.

An access grant has a place, holder, purpose, duration, and current permission. A public hospital need not reveal its restricted archive to a visiting surgeon. A sample agreement need not admit foreign troops. A request for military medicine can be accepted without allowing its sponsor to recruit patients or inspect unrelated formations.

Access is also a source of risk. A visitor can observe a real weakness or make contact with dissatisfied staff. The access record creates an opportunity for a separately authorized, uncertain foreign action. It does not automatically award a stolen design, announce the visitor as a spy, or make every public programme unsafe.

A breach narrows or suspends the affected privilege first. Closing a stolen-design archive does not cancel unrelated bedside care. When the host deliberately closes all contact, the resulting loss of training, supply, and trust is shown as its chosen consequence. The game preserves the distinction between a focused response and a general rupture.

## Agreements that can be fulfilled

An agreement fixes what the parties accepted before either side performs the final transfer. The supplier, payer, recipient, carrier, and care provider can be different countries. Each is named when its role matters. A donor's contribution cannot be counted again as the recipient's payment, and a neutral carrier does not acquire production rights through transport alone.

A proposal reserves no foreign resources. Acceptance requires the other party to approve its own contribution and the responsible owners to confirm that the commitments are available. An unaffordable counteroffer remains an offer. It cannot spend another country's equipment or put its factories under an invisible burden.

Accepted obligations distinguish preparations, reserved resources, dispatched objects, delivered objects, and completed service. Preparation that has actually been consumed is not restored by cancelling a later negotiation. Unspent supplies remain with their holder. Reserved capacity is released by the owner that reserved it. A delivered object is not returned by deleting the agreement.

Political approval, manufacturing, shipment, and clinical application are different playable steps because different countries can perform them and different failures affect them. They must not be split merely to hide extra costs. Every gameplay-changing action has at most four distinct spendable cost types, including committed staff or industrial capacity when those are payments. The complete obligation is visible before acceptance, and each later action states which earlier commitments it consumes.

### A clear price and a clear delivery promise

Part 4's payment choices remain available. Equipment payment transfers actual equipment through a valid transfer owner. Industrial payment commits real production or construction in a named programme. Resource terms use a supported trade or access arrangement. Intelligence cooperation and military access require their own explicit, limited concessions. Political Power can pay domestic negotiations, but it is never credited to the foreign seller as an invented international currency.

A sale can use a deposit and a delivery balance in the same principal commodity. The deposit is recorded as actual paid principal. Restitution cannot exceed the principal that the returning party still owes under that agreement, and it cannot materialize equipment the party does not possess. A compensation promise can remain unpaid. It does not become a free refund because a report closes.

The supplier quotes a delivery window from the production and route that can actually be supported. A first relief tranche and a complete programme may have different dates. A blocked route produces a blocked delivery promise, not a longer progress bar that eventually ignores the blockade. The parties can change the destination or split the remaining quantity through a new accepted revision.

A revision affects only uncommitted work. It cannot change the standard of a dispatched lot, take medicine from patients already receiving it, or redirect a native reservation. When a route owner cannot identify the remaining uncommitted portion, the disputed balance waits for reconciliation. An invented estimate cannot authorize a second shipment.

### Partial fulfilment and disagreement

The recipient can accept useful partial delivery while disputing the missing balance. That acceptance does not pretend the whole promise was fulfilled. The supplier may offer replacement stock, local manufacture, a revised timetable, a different qualified provider, or restitution of an owed amount. Each option has a real cost or concession.

Refusal can be reasonable when the proposed standard is unsuitable, the delivery cannot arrive in time, the price would close the recipient's hospitals, or access terms threaten its remaining independence. A poor recipient can offer local facilities, clinical cooperation, or transport participation where it actually has those resources. Poverty does not erase its ability to negotiate.

A breach record names the unmet obligation and available evidence. Deliberate diversion, destruction by an enemy, unavoidable route closure, and an unsupported promise produce different diplomatic consequences. The parties can disagree about blame without the simulation changing who physically holds the missing material.

## F01. Emergency treatment abroad

A request begins with an identified disease or a clinical emergency that the owning medical system can describe. The recipient selects an accessible population or hospital region and states the immediate need. When a current standard exists, the host can send finished medicine, commission production, supply a qualified team, or share the standard for local manufacture. When no current match exists, it can offer interim care and a genuine adaptation project.

The first decision concerns what can arrive before the crisis worsens. A wealthy host may possess enough medicine but lack a safe route. A smaller neighbour may have a qualified clinic and a direct connection. A neutral producer may be able to manufacture locally after receiving a validated standard. The request should make those alternatives visible without turning them into equivalent instant cures.

A dispatch uses Part 4's physical allocation and delivery contract. For a newly designed complete foreign medical-team dispatch, the proposed cost inventory is the selected treatment allocation, one qualified team commitment, one route-appropriate transport commitment, and fuel. Overland and sea versions choose the appropriate transport type, and a multi-leg route must use an owner-approved complete transport arrangement within the same four-type limit. The action does not add another Political Power fee or hidden support-equipment payment. Existing owner deployment actions keep their own complete quote and are not charged this package a second time.

Team size and supplies scale with the programme actually served. The team is unavailable for another simultaneous assignment. An established recipient service can accept medicine without a visiting team when its qualification is sufficient, removing the team commitment from that dispatch. A foreign medical mission never creates an extra domestic research lane.

The relief objective identifies a real first result. For a slow-to-manufacture treatment, it may be the first useful allocation reaching a hospital or a functioning local production partnership. A full national cure is not placed on a deadline shorter than its required research and manufacture. Ordinary new preparation missions use at least 90 days. A shorter emergency mission is reserved for already available resources that can physically meet the shorter deadline, and partial relief is stated as the objective.

Success records the living patients reached and the disease owner's accepted clinical result. It can establish a dependable supplier relationship and make later requests easier to negotiate. It does not award a national recovery for fractional coverage, resurrect casualties, or reduce unrelated contamination. Repeated deliveries earn their actual medical effect without farming diplomatic or Chaos rewards.

A setback leaves a useful next choice. The supplier can redirect undelivered stock, the recipient can secure a nearer site, or a sponsor can finance local production. An incompatible batch enters evaluation or recall, and patients retain whatever genuine benefit already occurred. A route loss does not mean that everyone awaiting medicine has died.

Emergency requests give recipient AI a strong preference for feasible care, including care from a political enemy. Sovereignty demands, known incompatibility, and an impossible route can outweigh that preference. Provider AI protects existing treatment obligations before promising a new programme. A public institute usually favours sharing or a neutral producer when its own transport cannot deliver.

Presentation follows the patients and the arriving or absent service. The important report can show a functioning ward, exhausted local staff receiving help, or surviving supplies being moved to a smaller clinic. Supplier propaganda can take credit, but the result report still identifies the actual provider and recipient.

## F02. Paid cure access and production partnerships

A paid arrangement starts with one selected clinical standard and a declared objective. The buyer chooses between a finished allocation, a production licence, a supported local works, or a continuing supply contract. A seller can refuse to sell the design while offering medicine, and the buyer can decline a recurring dependency in favour of a more expensive independent production arrangement.

The agreement preserves Part 4's distinction between discovery and reach. The buyer does not obtain every future adaptation through one purchase. The seller cannot call an old version current when its own records show a known mismatch. An offer can explicitly sell an older but useful standard at a lower price, provided its limitation is visible and the recipient's medical owner permits its use.

For a simple equipment-priced sale, the default proposed schedule divides the agreed support-equipment principal equally between accepted commissioning and verified delivery. Both transfers retain their exact paid amounts. The illustrative transaction in the economic review uses a principal of 200 support equipment, with 100 paid initially and 100 after the promised object arrives. This is an accounting example, not a mandatory global price. Severity, quantity, scarcity, production burden, route, and relationship determine the actual negotiated price, rounded to suitable five-unit steps.

A manufacture-only licence has a different completion condition. The seller supplies the documented standard and the agreed qualification support. The buyer supplies the production facility and consumes Part 4's normal manufacturing inputs. The licence cannot complete by depositing a free cure allocation. If the qualification service was part of the price and never arrived, the buyer keeps the knowledge already received and retains a service claim.

A local works partnership places investment in an actual site. The agreement states who owns the buildings, who can use the standard, which staff are seconded, and which output is promised. Completed national infrastructure remains at that site after the foreign specialist leaves. The host's proprietary methods travel only in the form legitimately copied or physically removed. A later title change cannot make the same investment portable.

The strongest commercial advantage is reliable supply coupled to useful production knowledge. A seller that repeatedly delivers can negotiate longer arrangements and receive proposals from new partners. A seller that diverts scarce medicine to another client may receive a short-term payment and lose its dependable-supplier standing with the original buyer. Those relationships affect concrete offers and access, not a new global Medical Influence currency.

Industrial, resource, and access alternatives remain distinct. The buyer can finance the seller's manufacturing interval in exchange for designated output, grant a limited inspection visit, or negotiate a real resource arrangement. It cannot promise the same factory interval to three sellers. A resource promise is not fulfilled by a broad trade-opinion modifier, and an intelligence concession must name the access actually granted.

A third-country sponsor can pay for a poor recipient. Its contribution is separate from title to the medicine. The sponsor can demand public acknowledgement or reporting within the agreement, but recipient consent is still required for research access or political concessions. Payment for humanitarian care never implies the transfer of patients as research material.

Contract disputes do not erase clinical facts. Medicine already delivered stays delivered, a legitimate copied standard stays learned, and prior deaths stay recorded. A new settlement can repair the commercial relationship without deleting the original breach. Supplier and buyer AI compare the real alternative cost, the remaining epidemic, and the risk of losing their only provider.

## F03. Visiting scientists and teaching exchanges

A visiting programme names a curriculum and a host institution. It can teach a validated cure, hospital production, a documented maintenance method, nonhuman clinical care, or a compatible research discipline. The visit is not a complete-portfolio research-sharing group, and it does not revive Event 089's old technology-sharing invitations.

The sending country identifies actual personnel and the work they will leave. A hospital cannot keep the full benefit of a senior team at home while that same team serves abroad. Where the personnel are an abstract established institution rather than individual characters, the commitment uses that institution's own staff capacity and reduces its available service accordingly.

A proposed ordinary teaching exchange runs for 120 days. The receiving school commits one civilian factory, 100 support equipment, and a qualified teaching-team assignment. Those three costs create a real teaching service. The visitors' sponsor separately arranges their actual travel through an existing transport contract. Travel and teaching are separate obligations because different parties can perform them, and the agreement discloses both before either accepts.

The taught result is specific. Successful completion can qualify the visiting team to operate one documented clinical programme, or give the receiving institution an independently verified production method. It cannot grant Wu's ability to improvise every cure or skip a research project's uncompleted prerequisites. A specialist who already possesses the qualification can instead contribute to a real comparison or local adaptation, with a result tied to that work.

The host chooses the access setting. A public course allows publication of the taught standard. A restricted clinical course limits the visitor to named services. A reciprocal appointment permits a defined research contribution. A military exchange restricts access to the agreed formations and medical tasks. The setting changes the opportunity for observation and staff contact without making the programme's educational result arbitrary.

If the exchange ends early, completed portions remain in the student's record. They may know the standard but lack independent operating qualification, or be qualified for routine care while still unable to supervise production. Restarting resumes the missing work instead of charging and rewarding the same completed teaching twice. A damaged training record requires recovery or revalidation of the unsupported portion.

A visitor may request asylum, expose misconduct, or receive a recruitment offer. These are separate cases with personal and institutional consequences. The host can investigate an access breach without automatically treating every visitor as an enemy agent. A dismissed visitor takes legitimate learning with them, while actually stolen restricted records retain their origin and evidence.

AI prefers exchanges that solve a real shortage and have a credible host. A country planning to manufacture a cure values production qualification. A country whose hospital workforce is already overstretched avoids sending away its only qualified team. The reports should make those institutional choices concrete through the returning service and its capabilities.

## F04. Specimen and clinical-record agreements

A specimen agreement begins from a source the relevant owner recognizes. The supplier may possess an authenticated clinical case, a registered organism, a recovered archive, a captured stock, or a valid fictional nonhuman lead. The agreement records what exists and which research purposes the supplier permits. A country cannot sell ownership of material that remains in an enemy-controlled laboratory.

Three forms are available. A clinical-record exchange sends useful observations without physical material. A supervised study permits work at the supplier's site. A physical loan or transfer moves a particular object through its custody owner. Each form can satisfy only the prerequisites that the receiving research owner actually accepts.

Clinical data can establish the direction of an adaptation while leaving a required physical lead unresolved. On-site study can qualify a result without moving an independent organism or surrendering the supplier's archive. A physical loan can permit one programme while reserving the supplier's property rights and requiring return of the surviving object. Neither a loan nor a study visit implies unlimited reproduction or offensive permission.

The proposed administrative review lasts 60 days and costs 35 Political Power and 50 support equipment. It verifies the agreement's record and prepares the permitted study, and it can be waived when an existing standing agreement already covers that exact purpose. It does not authenticate a false source merely by finishing the timer. The source owner still supplies the validity finding.

Physical movement then uses the appropriate material owner. The complete transfer can commit the named material, route transport, fuel, and a qualified custody service, at most four types. A stock item already reserved by a native operation is unavailable. Where the current owner prohibits ordinary licensing or lend-lease, the agreement remains an intent until a distinct, explicitly approved custody transaction exists. A generic equipment transfer must not be used to evade that restriction.

The recipient chooses whether to accept narrow access, negotiate broader rights, or pursue another source. A public institute may pay for a supervised clinical lead and reject custody it cannot safely support. A military directorate may accept a costly storage obligation because the material supports several permitted projects. A neutral donor may insist that its contribution remain confined to treatment and publish the agreement if those terms are broken.

The material retains source identity through subsequent research. Independent discovery does not mark the original event as fired, create its country, or award its unrelated rewards. The arrival of a source event later merges compatible capability records without repeating learning or inventing a second copy of the physical specimen.

A failed handover can leave the material at origin, in the carrier's custody, recovered by the recipient, destroyed with an owner-proven loss, or unaccounted for. Missing material is not automatically a released outbreak. Broken seals or inconsistent records produce a custody review and, when supported, a medical incident. All biological content remains abstract fictional game information.

The lasting relationship depends on truthful reporting and observance of purpose. A supplier that receives useful clinical findings may offer later leads. A recipient that hides the result can retain its stolen advantage and acquire a discoverable breach. Scientific credit records the supplier's contribution without transferring command of the recipient's institute.

## F05. Foreign military medicine

A military client requests a service for specified formations or a named field-hospital network. Ordinary casualty care, infection protection, temporary enhancement, permanent alteration, and engineered-lineage control remain different programmes. The recipient cannot purchase the strongest effect by describing every request as emergency medicine.

Ordinary field care uses the completed medical family and existing military medical owners. Selected-formation treatment retains Part 6's actual recipients, funded course, recovery period, and continuing care. Foreign delivery does not restart a course, erase accumulated dependence, or convert an expired benefit into a permanent one. A renamed, split, merged, or transferred formation retains the treatment disposition that its owner can prove.

The client chooses whether to import treatment under its own qualified service, receive an embedded foreign medical team, or establish an independent service through teaching and production. An embedded service can begin earlier when the provider has spare qualified personnel. It also leaves the client exposed to loss of that service. Independent qualification takes longer and gives the client a practical way to reduce dependence without losing its soldiers' care.

A new embedded dispatch uses the same four-type ceiling as other complete medical-team deliveries: compatible care stock, qualified team commitment, route transport, and fuel. The programme's existing enrolment and production costs remain where Part 6 places them. The diplomatic agreement does not add a second payment for the same course. A single host medical team cannot simultaneously support all foreign clients that signed a treaty.

The agreement names operational command and medical authority separately. The recipient commands its army. The provider can report that a formation is no longer fit for the funded treatment or control service and can withdraw its personnel under the agreement. That does not transfer national military command to Wu. Any proposed change in allegiance requires its own existing force-owner process and actual causal conditions.

A request involving engineered zombies, chimeric troops, or other special formations needs the provider's exact family and support requirements. Guardian formations retain their dependence on prepared sites. A foreign hospital agreement does not make them effective mobile expeditionary units. Carrier formations do not release infection during ordinary movement or combat. Any offensive use remains a separately authorized native operation with its own stock, preparation, result, and liability.

The client may ask for control-treatment knowledge as part of the agreement. The host can grant a qualified service handover, supply continued treatment without releasing the full archive, or refuse new enrolment. A partial archive can help a client prepare a successor but cannot substitute for a missing qualification. Ending a commercial licence does not destroy the client's existing treatment records.

The last safe handover date is based on the actual care interval. Part 6's 45-day control emergency remains relevant even when an international negotiation has a longer normal timetable. A successor who needs 90 days cannot be certified after 45 days by accepting diplomatic terms. The client must retain an interim provider, withdraw the formations in time, or face the already defined control failure.

When war begins between the parties, the agreement rechecks physical access and personnel security. The recipient can offer protected return, continued neutral supervision, or custody. A neutral provider may assume an existing service only after qualification and resource commitments are proven. The host's decision to stop supplying new military courses is different from abandoning already dependent people.

The recovery path concerns actual formations. Supported withdrawal, temporary loss of the special benefit, ordinary veteran care, or provider-approved retirement can preserve people. If exact formation transfer cannot preserve strength, equipment, experience, and treatment history, it remains blocked. Deleting the old unit and spawning a fresh full-strength replacement is not an acceptable transfer substitute.

Military AI values a programme according to the formations it can continue to supply. It avoids exporting its last qualified handler team and refuses extreme alteration when no later care is feasible. Aggressive clients can deliberately accept dependence for an urgent advantage, but that decision must leave a real support problem after the operation ends.

## F06. International access and inspection agreements

A demand for international access can come from an affected country, a group of existing partners, or a government with credible evidence of dangerous work. The request names its purpose. Emergency treatment access, production observation, clinical verification, and investigation of misconduct require different permissions and produce different findings.

The host can offer a limited inspection, an independent clinical team, neutral manufacture, a public treatment standard, or a supervised visit to a named site. It can refuse, narrow the proposal, or negotiate reciprocity. A refusal records the actual decision. It does not automatically prove weapon use or reveal hidden experiments that the requesting country has never identified.

A proposed ordinary access conference lasts 90 days and commits 50 Political Power and one civilian factory. Its result is a signed set of access terms, a limited agreement, or an unresolved dispute. Those two costs pay for the political and institutional work. They do not provide inspectors, medicine, transport, or laboratory production for free. The resulting services use their own visible commitments.

Inspection reaches only what was admitted and actually inspected. A clinical team can validate treatment performance in the patients it sees without certifying every secret site. A production observer can verify a named works while remaining unable to identify an unrelated archive. A full investigation needs supported evidence and access to the relevant owner records. The report should state a bounded finding rather than an absolute national verdict.

The shared Condemnation inspection and observer actions remain their own services. When a country takes one of those actions, the shared owner controls its costs, disclosure, decay effects, and record. Wu's access case can lead the player to that existing action. It cannot silently replay it or transform the current aggregate disclosure into an invented exact-case audit.

A public medical institute can give independent institutions standing access to selected treatment work. This improves continuity after Wu leaves and makes inflated claims easier to challenge. A classified institute can preserve a narrow military service and permit independent civilian care. The trade-off lies in real access, secrecy, time, and trust, without requiring a second permanent public value.

A successful agreement can reduce one diplomatic dispute and permit a concrete service that was previously blocked. It does not remove a native embargo, grant general military passage, or force nonparticipants to recognize the arrangement. Each transit or sanction owner still decides the permissions it controls.

When an inspector discovers a relevant fault, the host can correct the affected programme, accept a limited suspension, dispute the finding through an independent review, or conceal it. A correction improves current safety and preserves the discovery history. Concealment creates evidence only through the responsible owner and the actual act, not a generic penalty for choosing a secret mandate.

AI countries favour access that produces useful care or reliable information. A desperate recipient can accept a narrow clinical agreement while continuing to demand broader investigation. A host with legitimate capacity problems can propose local manufacture rather than pay for a conference that cannot meet an immediate medical deadline.

## F07. Asylum, protection, and voluntary departure

An asylum case concerns a specific living Wu, qualified copy, or member of the institute. The foreign government needs a way to contact that person, and the person needs a reason to consider leaving. A request can arise from political restriction, exposure, a threatened laboratory, loss of legal protection, refusal to continue harmful work, or a conflict between qualified copies.

Asylum grants protection and a receiving arrangement. It does not appoint the person to a scientific office, transfer their institute, or supply the receiving country with the whole portfolio. The host may offer refuge without requiring employment. Wu may accept temporary protection while refusing a permanent appointment, and the receiving state must decide whether those terms are acceptable.

The proposed formal protection arrangement costs the sponsor 75 Political Power and commits one civilian factory for 90 days to establish the receiving institution and legal arrangement. It remains a political programme. A prepared institution can use an existing valid arrangement without repurchasing the same protection. Medical care, travel, and any new laboratory still require their own actual support.

A provisional refuge can be accepted once the destination has a proven safe receiving place, a valid route, and the person's agreement. The 90-day programme establishes the standing protection institution. It does not make an endangered person wait 90 days for permission to enter an already prepared refuge. Provisional shelter grants neither a free laboratory nor a completed appointment, and its care and accommodation remain actual receiving obligations.

The person considers the destination's viable institute, the offered mandate, personal safety, existing obligations, institutional rights, and access to legitimate work. A government with a record of confinement or exposed experiment sites can offer asylum, but that record reduces the credibility of its promise. An attractive salary or title cannot turn an unsuitable receiving state into a safe medical institution.

A voluntary departure is agreed separately with the old host when possible. The old country can retain copied knowledge, negotiate a transition period, keep independently qualified institutions, and seek return of borrowed material. The departing scientist can take personal records and permitted objects actually removed. National facilities, staff who choose to remain, patient records held by hospitals, and existing stock do not follow the character automatically.

Active native scientist assignment must be settled through its owning project before ordinary role transfer. The transfer cannot silently remove a scientist from a live project, complete its remaining clock, or refund its native costs. Where the engine offers no supported safe interruption, the voluntary move waits or becomes a proposed owner extension. Physical capture during war remains a separate fact and does not need to pretend an ordinary appointment transfer succeeded.

The new country qualifies the arriving person's office. A fully qualified Wu can provide his established personal work after a viable institute and appointment are ready. A continuity copy retains the qualification actually completed in Part 7. A copy with an older archive can know less than the departed original, and the two can disagree about obligations or political direction.

A protection offer can fail before departure, during transport, or after arrival. Before departure, the offer may be withdrawn and consumed political preparation remains spent. In transit, the actual transport owner records arrival, delay, capture, or loss. After arrival, broken protection can create confinement, exposure, or a second asylum search. None of these outcomes awards another Event 089 arrival demonstration or founding reward.

A former host may later request emergency care from the country that sheltered Wu. That request uses the current clinical relationship and does not erase the earlier expulsion. The asylum state can supply medicine without returning the scientist. A negotiated return is possible only when the person, current holder, and receiving institution satisfy the relevant transfer contract.

Kruger remains one canonical character with his existing eligibility, transfer, and continuity rules. A domestic dispute with Wu can supply a reason for departure, but this case does not relax Kruger's prior-host exclusions, bypass a pending cross-domain review, or activate his international contest early.

## F08. Staff recruitment and institutional competition

Foreign recruitment targets an actual shortage and a real person or team. A country lacking cure-production expertise may seek the teacher who helped qualify an existing works. An army with dependent formations may seek a qualified care director. An independent university may offer a place to a researcher excluded under Part 10's supremacy settlement.

Open recruitment begins with a declared post, receiving institution, and terms. Covert recruitment uses a distinct operation against a known accessible target. Neither selects an imaginary perfect assistant from a generic national pool. A visitor, former colleague, public author, or authenticated staff contact can supply the target, and the available knowledge follows that target's real history.

The proposed recruitment campaign lasts 90 days and spends 100 Political Power, 125 support equipment, and one civilian-factory commitment. A covert version also commits one real operative through the relevant intelligence owner, reaching four cost types. The person being recruited is the target, not an extra free research team or a payment deducted from civilian population.

The old institution can offer recognition, a better post, an independent research guarantee, or protected departure. A noncompulsory employment dispute does not automatically justify confinement. Coercive retention remains a deliberate political action with discoverable consequences, and it can make future voluntary cooperation less credible.

Success obtains the person or agreed team after actual arrival. Their appointment is evaluated separately. A laboratory specialist may bring a valid treatment standard and still need a functioning works to use it. A trained handler may maintain an existing family but lack Wu's authority to redesign it. A replicated specialist has the same finite identity and qualification rules as any other copy.

Partial success can obtain a temporary secondment, a published method, a candidate who lacks the desired qualification, or a useful contact for later talks. The foreign report identifies that narrower result. It cannot describe a declined job offer as a completed theft or grant a full project stage as consolation.

The old host loses only the service actually provided by the departing staff. Completed national knowledge remains available. When the team was the sole qualified provider for a continuing programme, its departure opens a handover problem with the existing deadline. A government can fund a replacement, negotiate a delayed departure, reduce new enrolment, or wind down the programme safely.

A country can build an independent school around recruited expertise. It pays the appropriate teaching and facility costs and earns qualification through completed work. This gives recruitment a durable payoff beyond a small research modifier and gives the old host a reason to invest in independent institutions rather than rely on one indispensable assistant.

AI recruits for demonstrated gaps. It does not pay repeatedly for a method already known and operational at home, except when it genuinely needs another independent team. It values an accepted secondment differently from a permanent appointment and will not abandon its own urgent care to train a rival without an explicit strategic reason.
