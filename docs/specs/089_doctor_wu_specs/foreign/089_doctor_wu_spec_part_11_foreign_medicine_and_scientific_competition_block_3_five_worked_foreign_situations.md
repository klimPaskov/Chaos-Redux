## Five worked foreign situations

### An island client with knowledge but no delivery route

An overseas ally has a severe outbreak and requests two matching civilian allocations. The host has the standard and finished stock, but the route owner cannot establish safe shipment. Accepting the medical request creates an agreement to seek a feasible response. It does not commit nonexistent sea capacity or apply the cure abroad.

The client already has a suitable factory and a qualified clinical team. The host can supply the documented standard, allowing local manufacture through Part 4. The client pays the actual severe-treatment manufacturing burden and then deploys the medicine to its eligible living population. The original host's finished stock stays where it was and can serve another accepted request.

The foreign result is a successful knowledge and production partnership. It is not recorded as a delivered convoy. If the client lacks production qualification, a remote record alone cannot certify it, and the parties must seek a qualified local or reachable teaching service. The first relief objective then concerns what that service can actually achieve.

### Partial commercial delivery

The parties agree to ten comparable allocations for a support-equipment principal of 200, with 100 paid at commissioning. They additionally agree that early closure can settle useful delivered allocations at a principal value of 20 each. Six allocations arrive, and the remaining route becomes unavailable.

The accepted delivered value is 120. If the parties close the order at six allocations, the buyer owes 20 more after its existing 100 payment. If the order continues, the unpaid 80 remains associated with the remaining four allocations and is not credited as fulfilled revenue. The seller retains custody of those four only when the physical owner confirms they remain at its site.

A different outcome with four accepted allocations gives a delivered value of 80. Closing that agreement creates a restitution obligation of 20 against the deposit. The seller must actually possess and transfer that equipment to pay it. An unpaid claim does not create equipment at the buyer. Neither settlement returns consumed manufacture inputs or changes the medical effects of allocations already used.

The quantities are an accounting fixture. A real contract can use a different price or accepted settlement basis, but its stated rule must determine the result consistently.

### A formation whose provider cannot be replaced in time

A client has engineered formations with 45 days remaining before their current control service becomes unsafe. Its relationship with the supplier breaks down. An independent replacement service needs 90 days to qualify. Accepting a new agreement does not close the 45-day problem.

The client can maintain a limited interim service with the old provider, obtain another already qualified provider whose real handover can finish within the interval, or begin the supported withdrawal route. A proposed 15-day handover by an already qualified team can be feasible. A 90-day training course cannot be accelerated into that category by renaming it a handover.

A sanctioning ally may oppose future military contracts while supporting the limited care arrangement. The liability and sanctions owners retain their records, and the transport owner still checks access. If no support reaches the formations, Part 6's actual failure path proceeds. It is not replaced by an arbitrary foreign-relation penalty.

### One receiving district and three kinds of people

A reconstructed district has a verified 5,000-place receiving limit after food, housing, transport, and medical checks. Existing survivor and migrant commitments occupy 3,500 places. A proposed arrival of 1,600 finite new people cannot use the full headline capacity.

At most 1,500 new places remain, subject to the current proof and the movement or settlement owner's partial-admission contract. The other 100 remain in their actual custody with continuing food demand. If partial admission is unsupported, the request must be reduced and accepted again before movement. A medical treaty cannot ignore the occupied places.

An ordinary settled cohort of 1,000 evacuees that loses 20 people on a proven route credits 980 survivors at its destination. Its origin debit is 1,000, including the route deaths. If a named qualified Wu copy is included in those survivors, changing that person's roster does not add another person. An unsettled group uses the proposed finite-custody route and no fabricated origin population debit.

### A stolen portfolio and an operation already reserved

Country A owns 120 items of modified standard A. A native operation has reserved 40, and a separate owner-approved material commitment has reserved 30. Only 50 remain available. A valid separate physical transfer of 20 available items leaves 30 available at A and 20 with the receiving holder.

The production works changes to standard B. The old items keep standard A, including the 40 already in the native operation. A copied design for B does not turn any of them into B. The native operation's completed outcome accounts for its own 40-item reservation according to the inspected owner contract, with no extra Wu debit or scripted refund.

The physical balance after that completed operation is 30 available at A, 30 still committed to the other valid owner, 20 at the receiving holder, and 40 consumed through the native operation. Those amounts total 120. The history of the operation, its evidence, and any supported clinical outcome remain with their owners. Cancellation behaviour remains a separate unverified native question.

## Foreign strategies across a campaign

### The small medical centre

A small country can remain a valuable host by developing standards and teaching independent partners. It does not need to match a major power's production. Its first objective is a defensible domestic reserve and a feasible partnership, followed by a second producer or alternative route that reduces dependence on one patron.

Its difficult choice is exclusivity. A major sponsor can offer real industrial support in return for limited manufacture rights or priority supply. The small host can accept, negotiate a public civilian exception, or release the standard more widely. The different choices affect who funds its work, who can produce independently, and which countries seek access to Wu.

The small host retains its own appointment and clinical decisions unless it explicitly grants more authority. A production contract does not silently make it a puppet. Political pressure can grow through actual dependency and foreign demands, and the later sovereignty parts will decide any route that changes state authority.

### The industrial buyer

A major power can build a distributed clinical network from copied standards and trained teams. Its challenge is qualification and access to new adaptations. It can seek a long-term medical relationship, recruit a specialist, fund a joint project, or attempt theft. Each approach affects its ability to obtain reliable updates and the source's willingness to cooperate.

A major with two active modified production standards must choose which works to retool before adding another. Buying several designs does not expand the country's permitted active standards. Its ordinary cure production remains separate and can continue. A military buyer that monopolizes all qualified care staff may leave its civilian hospitals dependent on foreign help.

### The threatened patient state

A country facing an epidemic chooses among immediate imported care, slower independent manufacture, a sponsor's terms, and help from a hostile power. The best short-term arrangement may carry a long-term dependency. The country can use the time gained by successful relief to train staff and broaden production rather than remain a permanent customer.

Its government may reject a coercive demand even during a crisis. The game should show the cost of that decision through its actual untreated need and diplomatic alternatives. It should not punish refusal with an unrelated automatic outbreak or force surrender through a medical menu.

A request involving an independent nonhuman country identifies the patients or organisms that its owner allows the medical service to treat. Human captives, compatible nonhuman troops, and the independent government remain distinct. A cure cannot dissolve a Rat Nation, delete a zombie army, annex its territory, or turn its government into a patient. Hosting eligibility and an external limited medical agreement are separate questions, each requiring its own owner approval.

### The guarded military patron

A military host can export powerful selected-formation medicine while retaining control of new designs. It gains committed clients and foreign access that can support its strategy. Its limitation is the continuing service it must provide. A wide network of dependent troops can become difficult to maintain during war, staff loss, sanctions, or a dispute with Wu.

Its clients can seek independent qualification or another provider. The patron can permit a controlled handover and retain a narrower partnership, or oppose it and risk recruitment, theft, and exposure. A refusal to share the archive does not give the patron a free ability to command the client's army.

### The country that loses Wu

The former host's position depends on what it built. An independent teaching network can keep routine cures and manufacturing alive. A personally controlled institute may retain equipment and archives without enough qualified people to use them. Foreign partners can help recover the service, and former clients can become providers.

The departure does not reset the portfolio. The country can pursue national reconstruction, new staff, a qualified copy, or an agreement with Wu's new employer. It can also pursue revenge, but an attack that destroys its remaining medical partner may worsen its own situation. The system should make that consequence legible through actual services and obligations.

## Artificial intelligence and scenario expectations

AI first rejects unavailable actions using the same owner proofs and complete payment conditions as the player. It then compares useful outcomes within the actual current candidate set. A hidden clinical match, an unknown site, or an unqualified successor cannot enter its evaluation as certain information.

During an active disease crisis, feasible treatment and preservation of existing care normally outrank prestige and discretionary covert operations. Scarcity, hostile terms, military urgency, institutional dependence, exposed wrongdoing, and the available alternative providers can change that ordering. Ideology affects political preference after validity and does not replace clinical compatibility or transport proof.

The host evaluates the opportunity cost of each commitment. It refuses new work that would abandon an already funded course unless an explicit emergency or coercive route authorizes that sacrifice and shows its consequences. It can renegotiate a promise before failure when the route or capacity changes. It does not keep reissuing the same unaffordable offer.

The following are named design-review scenarios for the later probability auditor. They specify required validity or ordering, not measured campaign probabilities. Complete candidate pools, scenario inputs, timing adapters, and before-and-after source identities are still required before numerical probability claims.

A successful static affordability check does not prove that its staff, production, transport, and appointment schedules work together in a campaign.

## Presentation of foreign medicine

The foreign view uses the existing institute and agreement presentation. Wu Authority remains the main event value where relevant. The selected case shows its subject, present obligation, next useful action, and the critical deadline or shortage. Internal operation generations, ledger joins, source enums, and numerical intelligence components stay out of ordinary player text.

Reports identify the actual people and institutions involved. A medical delivery emphasizes the receiving service and the patients it can now reach. A teaching result shows the returning team using what it learned. A custody report names the missing or recovered person and the institution left to continue without them. A failed contract identifies the unfulfilled promise and surviving options.

Important option families need different voices. A public relief offer can be practical and open. A monopoly counteroffer can use restrained commercial arrogance. A military service agreement can emphasize duty and the cost of dependence. Exposure and civilian harm require serious wording, with any irony aimed at the responsible institution's claims. Captivity, death, and failed care are never treated as cheap jokes.

Secrecy affects what each country sees. The attacker knows its selected objective and the result its agents report. The host sees detected activity and medical consequences it can observe. A third country knows only what diplomacy, witnesses, or its own intelligence establish. The interface must not disclose every secret project because a country has received one shipment.

The proposed report-art families are clinical arrival, local teaching, contested scientific employment, damaged or captured institute, interrupted medical transport, and shared clinical service. Use fictional period-authentic documentary compositions for these invented scenes. Real people and identifiable historical institutions require appropriate sourced references and their own authorized portrait handling. Do not create an extra portrait family merely because a generic minister or surgeon speaks in a report.

The art should show the service or people involved. Generic staff tables, arrows on maps, sealed folders, and conference-room scenes should not dominate the foreign branch. A coalition's public result can be represented by different national teams working in a real clinical setting, with the agreement itself remaining a secondary detail.

Decision icons distinguish care, teaching, employment, investigation, custody, and physical movement. They reuse an appropriate accepted family when authorized, or receive a bounded new requirement where the existing inventory does not communicate the action. They need exact consumer references, native-size review, and asset-owner approval before production. This stage assigns design roles and does not create final artwork, audio, or production prompts.

Existing units, aircraft, ships, and medical teams retain their established model and sound ownership. A foreign agreement does not create a new combat identity. Any later implementation that introduces a genuinely new visible unit must receive its full owner, counter, model, animation, and sound review instead of treating diplomacy as permission to use a placeholder.

## What international medicine leaves for national ascendancy

Foreign medicine can leave the host with trained partners, dependable suppliers, dependent clients, protected personnel, damaged relationships, or copies of its work in countries it no longer controls. Those outcomes already matter in Calm World because they change who can produce, who can provide care, and what survives the loss of one institute.

The next stage must build National Medical Ascendancy from that actual network. It can alter the reach of existing standards, the pressure on hospital appointments, the place of independent institutions, and the cost of removing Wu. It must preserve the countries, people, knowledge, stock, and obligations created here. A national evolution cannot make every foreign agreement vanish or grant the technologies that those agreements never produced.
