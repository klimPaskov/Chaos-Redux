# Doctor Wu: foreign medicine and scientific competition

## What other governments can obtain

A country with an epidemic needs medicine that can reach its hospitals. An industrial partner may need the standard that lets its own factories produce that medicine. An army with altered soldiers needs a qualified service that will remain after the first shipment. A government offering Wu asylum may receive one frightened physician, a dated archive, and several unfinished obligations. These are different bargains, and their differences should determine foreign play.

The host can become a dependable medical partner, a selective supplier, a commercial centre, a guarded military patron, or a country whose institutes are increasingly isolated. Foreign countries have their own aims. They can cooperate over a cure and oppose the same institute's military work, employ a former assistant without obtaining Wu, or shelter a qualified copy who disagrees with the original. A fulfilled agreement gives those choices a lasting basis.

The production, coverage, and access policies in Part 4 remain the medical foundation. This part develops the countries and institutions making those agreements, the things they can obtain, and the consequences when commitments fail. Foreign activity begins through actual contact, need, or evidence. It is available during Calm World. The later International Biological Contest changes its reach and pressure without creating the first possibility of diplomacy or espionage.

All new durations, costs, limits, and comparison values below are proposed tuning anchors. Existing owner payments and cancellation rules retain their own authority. Working route, case, and programme labels identify design roles and are not final localisation.

### Knowledge, material, people, appointments, and service

A knowledge agreement names the completed treatment, qualification record, or fixed design being copied. It includes its known limitations and the date of the information supplied. A recipient keeps a legitimate copy after a treaty ends or Wu changes employment. Manufacture and field use still need the appropriate facility, staff, permission, and material. Receiving an incomplete record starts a reconstruction or validation task with its genuine missing work intact.

A material agreement names an actual stock lot, quantity, current holder, location, standard, and permitted destination. It cannot be paid from an item already committed to a treatment course or native operation. The lot keeps its properties through sale, capture, transit, retooling, and a change of government. The physical owner settles departure and arrival once. A promise to manufacture later is recorded as future output, with its due date and production commitment, and is never presented as current stock.

A personnel agreement identifies the person or existing team, qualifications, present appointment, willingness where the arrangement is voluntary, and the work they can perform. An assistant carries the knowledge they actually learned. A copied specialist carries their own completed training and imperfect memory. Neither brings the complete portfolio simply because their employer was Wu.

An appointment gives a person a defined office after arrival and acceptance. Asylum, confinement, a university visit, a medical secondment, and appointment as institute director remain separate states. A captured Wu does not immediately command the captor's hospitals. A visiting Wu does not occupy the resident advisor office in two countries at once.

A service agreement commits qualified people and suitable supplies over time. It names the patients or formations, provider, end date, last safe handover date, and responsibility for interruption. It can survive a political rupture when the parties preserve a limited care arrangement. A former supplier may stop approving new clients while completing an existing course.

These objects may travel together, but the agreement shows them separately. A successful extraction of a scientist can fail to recover the archive. A seized archive can arrive with its medicines destroyed. A purchase can obtain sufficient medicine for one programme without any right to manufacture more. Reports describe those actual results and leave missing objects missing.

## The first foreign approach

Foreign interest starts from a reason that the country can know. Public treatment, a licensed standard, an accepted visiting programme, a shared outbreak report, a captured institute, an existing intelligence finding, or a request from a medical partner can establish contact. A secret institute does not appear in every foreign target list on the day it is founded.

An affected country can seek medical help without knowing that Wu exists. It sends a request through an available diplomatic or humanitarian relationship. The host can answer through an ordinary national medical office and keep Wu's personal involvement confidential. The recipient learns what it received and who supplied it, not every hidden research programme behind the supplier.

A country with no immediate outbreak can request a reserve agreement, training, or a standing emergency arrangement. A military rival can study a publicly demonstrated capability or pursue an authenticated intelligence lead. An asylum offer needs contact with the intended person or an institution able to relay it. Rumours can justify an inquiry, but they cannot establish exact stocks, locations, staff loyalties, or current treatment compatibility.

### Relevant actors and manageable requests

The host's ordinary foreign view concentrates on three current cases. An accepted medical service remains active even when it is no longer one of those three cases. The limit governs political attention and presentation, not the number of hospitals that can receive medicine. Routine contracts operate through their existing production and care owners.

Within a selected case, the player normally sees a practical choice, a counteroffer, a refusal or postponement, and any necessary protective response. Six primary actions is the maximum for that visible state. One to three urgent missions may be shown with it. A list of agreements is a record browser, and selecting a record reveals only its relevant actions.

Requests from the same country for the same clinical need merge. An updated number of patients or a newly unsuitable treatment changes the existing case. A genuinely different emergency can have a separate subject. A hostile government cannot exhaust the host's scarce staff by repeatedly submitting unfunded offers.

For proposed discretionary covert work, each foreign actor can maintain one active Wu operation at a time. A host normally accepts at most two active hostile Wu operations into the concealed operation system. Those are pacing limits for this event's optional operations, not protection from ordinary war or from another owner's valid operation. Ordinary clinical requests do not consume hostile slots, and hostile actors cannot reserve clinical capacity by occupying those slots. Accepted care continues during an investigation or intelligence operation.

Selection and refresh use affected countries, existing partners, known interested actors, and explicit player searches. Changes of host, major public discoveries, completed agreements, and new owner-supplied evidence can refresh that bounded set. No new daily, weekly, or monthly whole-world scan is introduced.

### Access has a purpose

Clinical access permits the agreed treatment work. Training access permits the named curriculum. Research access permits work on specified material under its recorded terms. A military liaison permits contact with the selected command or formation. Inspection access permits the stated review. None supplies the others automatically.

An access grant has a place, holder, purpose, duration, and current permission. A public hospital need not reveal its restricted archive to a visiting surgeon. A sample agreement need not admit foreign troops. A request for military medicine can be accepted without allowing its sponsor to recruit patients or inspect unrelated formations.

Access is also a source of risk. A visitor can observe a real weakness or make contact with dissatisfied staff. The access record creates an opportunity for a separately authorized, uncertain foreign action. It does not automatically award a stolen design, announce the visitor as a spy, or make every public programme unsafe.

A breach narrows or suspends the affected privilege first. Closing a stolen-design archive does not cancel unrelated bedside care. When the host deliberately closes all contact, the resulting loss of training, supply, and trust is shown as its chosen consequence. The game preserves the distinction between a focused response and a general rupture.

## Agreements that can be fulfilled

An agreement fixes what the parties accepted before either side performs the final transfer. The supplier, payer, recipient, carrier, and care provider can be different countries. Each is named when its role matters. A donor's contribution cannot be counted again as the recipient's payment, and a neutral carrier does not acquire production rights through transport alone.

A proposal reserves no foreign resources. Acceptance requires the other party to approve its own contribution and the responsible owners to confirm that the commitments are available. An unaffordable counteroffer remains an offer. It cannot spend another country's equipment or put its factories under an invisible burden.

Accepted obligations distinguish preparations, reserved resources, dispatched objects, delivered objects, and completed service. Preparation that has actually been consumed is not restored by cancelling a later negotiation. Unspent supplies remain with their holder. Reserved capacity is released by the owner that reserved it. A delivered object is not returned by deleting the agreement.

Political approval, manufacturing, shipment, and clinical application are different playable steps because different countries can perform them and different failures affect them. They must not be split merely to hide extra costs. Every gameplay-changing action has at most four distinct spendable cost types, including committed staff or industrial capacity when those are payments. The complete obligation is visible before acceptance, and each later action states which earlier commitments it consumes.

### A clear price and a clear delivery promise

Part 4's payment choices remain available. Equipment payment transfers actual equipment through a valid transfer owner. Industrial payment commits real production or construction in a named programme. Resource terms use a supported trade or access arrangement. Intelligence cooperation and military access require their own explicit, limited concessions. Political Power can pay domestic negotiations, but it is never credited to the foreign seller as an invented international currency.

A sale can use a deposit and a delivery balance in the same principal commodity. The deposit is recorded as actual paid principal. Restitution cannot exceed the principal that the returning party still owes under that agreement, and it cannot materialize equipment the party does not possess. A compensation promise can remain unpaid. It does not become a free refund because a report closes.

The supplier quotes a delivery window from the production and route that can actually be supported. A first relief tranche and a complete programme may have different dates. A blocked route produces a blocked delivery promise, not a longer progress bar that eventually ignores the blockade. The parties can change the destination or split the remaining quantity through a new accepted revision.

A revision affects only uncommitted work. It cannot change the standard of a dispatched lot, take medicine from patients already receiving it, or redirect a native reservation. When a route owner cannot identify the remaining uncommitted portion, the disputed balance waits for reconciliation. An invented estimate cannot authorize a second shipment.

### Partial fulfilment and disagreement

The recipient can accept useful partial delivery while disputing the missing balance. That acceptance does not pretend the whole promise was fulfilled. The supplier may offer replacement stock, local manufacture, a revised timetable, a different qualified provider, or restitution of an owed amount. Each option has a real cost or concession.

Refusal can be reasonable when the proposed standard is unsuitable, the delivery cannot arrive in time, the price would close the recipient's hospitals, or access terms threaten its remaining independence. A poor recipient can offer local facilities, clinical cooperation, or transport participation where it actually has those resources. Poverty does not erase its ability to negotiate.

A breach record names the unmet obligation and available evidence. Deliberate diversion, destruction by an enemy, unavoidable route closure, and an unsupported promise produce different diplomatic consequences. The parties can disagree about blame without the simulation changing who physically holds the missing material.

## F01. Emergency treatment abroad

A request begins with an identified disease or a clinical emergency that the owning medical system can describe. The recipient selects an accessible population or hospital region and states the immediate need. When a current standard exists, the host can send finished medicine, commission production, supply a qualified team, or share the standard for local manufacture. When no current match exists, it can offer interim care and a genuine adaptation project.

The first decision concerns what can arrive before the crisis worsens. A wealthy host may possess enough medicine but lack a safe route. A smaller neighbour may have a qualified clinic and a direct connection. A neutral producer may be able to manufacture locally after receiving a validated standard. The request should make those alternatives visible without turning them into equivalent instant cures.

A dispatch uses Part 4's physical allocation and delivery contract. For a newly designed complete foreign medical-team dispatch, the proposed cost inventory is the selected treatment allocation, one qualified team commitment, one route-appropriate transport commitment, and fuel. Overland and sea versions choose the appropriate transport type, and a multi-leg route must use an owner-approved complete transport arrangement within the same four-type limit. The action does not add another Political Power fee or hidden support-equipment payment. Existing owner deployment actions keep their own complete quote and are not charged this package a second time.

Team size and supplies scale with the programme actually served. The team is unavailable for another simultaneous assignment. An established recipient service can accept medicine without a visiting team when its qualification is sufficient, removing the team commitment from that dispatch. A foreign medical mission never creates an extra domestic research lane.

The relief objective identifies a real first result. For a slow-to-manufacture treatment, it may be the first useful allocation reaching a hospital or a functioning local production partnership. A full national cure is not placed on a deadline shorter than its required research and manufacture. Ordinary new preparation missions use at least 90 days. A shorter emergency mission is reserved for already available resources that can physically meet the shorter deadline, and partial relief is stated as the objective.

Success records the living patients reached and the disease owner's accepted clinical result. It can establish a dependable supplier relationship and make later requests easier to negotiate. It does not award a national recovery for fractional coverage, resurrect casualties, or reduce unrelated contamination. Repeated deliveries earn their actual medical effect without farming diplomatic or Chaos rewards.

A setback leaves a useful next choice. The supplier can redirect undelivered stock, the recipient can secure a nearer site, or a sponsor can finance local production. An incompatible batch enters evaluation or recall, and patients retain whatever genuine benefit already occurred. A route loss does not mean that everyone awaiting medicine has died.

Emergency requests give recipient AI a strong preference for feasible care, including care from a political enemy. Sovereignty demands, known incompatibility, and an impossible route can outweigh that preference. Provider AI protects existing treatment obligations before promising a new programme. A public institute usually favours sharing or a neutral producer when its own transport cannot deliver.

Presentation follows the patients and the arriving or absent service. The important report can show a functioning ward, exhausted local staff receiving help, or surviving supplies being moved to a smaller clinic. Supplier propaganda can take credit, but the result report still identifies the actual provider and recipient.

## F02. Paid cure access and production partnerships

A paid arrangement starts with one selected clinical standard and a declared objective. The buyer chooses between a finished allocation, a production licence, a supported local works, or a continuing supply contract. A seller can refuse to sell the design while offering medicine, and the buyer can decline a recurring dependency in favour of a more expensive independent production arrangement.

The agreement preserves Part 4's distinction between discovery and reach. The buyer does not obtain every future adaptation through one purchase. The seller cannot call an old version current when its own records show a known mismatch. An offer can explicitly sell an older but useful standard at a lower price, provided its limitation is visible and the recipient's medical owner permits its use.

For a simple equipment-priced sale, the default proposed schedule divides the agreed support-equipment principal equally between accepted commissioning and verified delivery. Both transfers retain their exact paid amounts. The illustrative transaction in the economic review uses a principal of 200 support equipment, with 100 paid initially and 100 after the promised object arrives. This is an accounting example, not a mandatory global price. Severity, quantity, scarcity, production burden, route, and relationship determine the actual negotiated price, rounded to suitable five-unit steps.

A manufacture-only licence has a different completion condition. The seller supplies the documented standard and the agreed qualification support. The buyer supplies the production facility and consumes Part 4's normal manufacturing inputs. The licence cannot complete by depositing a free cure allocation. If the qualification service was part of the price and never arrived, the buyer keeps the knowledge already received and retains a service claim.

A local works partnership places investment in an actual site. The agreement states who owns the buildings, who can use the standard, which staff are seconded, and which output is promised. Completed national infrastructure remains at that site after the foreign specialist leaves. The host's proprietary methods travel only in the form legitimately copied or physically removed. A later title change cannot make the same investment portable.

The strongest commercial advantage is reliable supply coupled to useful production knowledge. A seller that repeatedly delivers can negotiate longer arrangements and receive proposals from new partners. A seller that diverts scarce medicine to another client may receive a short-term payment and lose its dependable-supplier standing with the original buyer. Those relationships affect concrete offers and access, not a new global Medical Influence currency.

Industrial, resource, and access alternatives remain distinct. The buyer can finance the seller's manufacturing interval in exchange for designated output, grant a limited inspection visit, or negotiate a real resource arrangement. It cannot promise the same factory interval to three sellers. A resource promise is not fulfilled by a broad trade-opinion modifier, and an intelligence concession must name the access actually granted.

A third-country sponsor can pay for a poor recipient. Its contribution is separate from title to the medicine. The sponsor can demand public acknowledgement or reporting within the agreement, but recipient consent is still required for research access or political concessions. Payment for humanitarian care never implies the transfer of patients as research material.

Contract disputes do not erase clinical facts. Medicine already delivered stays delivered, a legitimate copied standard stays learned, and prior deaths stay recorded. A new settlement can repair the commercial relationship without deleting the original breach. Supplier and buyer AI compare the real alternative cost, the remaining epidemic, and the risk of losing their only provider.

## F03. Visiting scientists and teaching exchanges

A visiting programme names a curriculum and a host institution. It can teach a validated cure, hospital production, a documented maintenance method, nonhuman clinical care, or a compatible research discipline. The visit is not a complete-portfolio research-sharing group, and it does not revive Event 089's old technology-sharing invitations.

The sending country identifies actual personnel and the work they will leave. A hospital cannot keep the full benefit of a senior team at home while that same team serves abroad. Where the personnel are an abstract established institution rather than individual characters, the commitment uses that institution's own staff capacity and reduces its available service accordingly.

A proposed ordinary teaching exchange runs for 120 days. The receiving school commits one civilian factory, 100 support equipment, and a qualified teaching-team assignment. Those three costs create a real teaching service. The visitors' sponsor separately arranges their actual travel through an existing transport contract. Travel and teaching are separate obligations because different parties can perform them, and the agreement discloses both before either accepts.

The taught result is specific. Successful completion can qualify the visiting team to operate one documented clinical programme, or give the receiving institution an independently verified production method. It cannot grant Wu's ability to improvise every cure or skip a research project's uncompleted prerequisites. A specialist who already possesses the qualification can instead contribute to a real comparison or local adaptation, with a result tied to that work.

The host chooses the access setting. A public course allows publication of the taught standard. A restricted clinical course limits the visitor to named services. A reciprocal appointment permits a defined research contribution. A military exchange restricts access to the agreed formations and medical tasks. The setting changes the opportunity for observation and staff contact without making the programme's educational result arbitrary.

If the exchange ends early, completed portions remain in the student's record. They may know the standard but lack independent operating qualification, or be qualified for routine care while still unable to supervise production. Restarting resumes the missing work instead of charging and rewarding the same completed teaching twice. A damaged training record requires recovery or revalidation of the unsupported portion.

A visitor may request asylum, expose misconduct, or receive a recruitment offer. These are separate cases with personal and institutional consequences. The host can investigate an access breach without automatically treating every visitor as an enemy agent. A dismissed visitor takes legitimate learning with them, while actually stolen restricted records retain their origin and evidence.

AI prefers exchanges that solve a real shortage and have a credible host. A country planning to manufacture a cure values production qualification. A country whose hospital workforce is already overstretched avoids sending away its only qualified team. The reports should make those institutional choices concrete through the returning service and its capabilities.

## F04. Specimen and clinical-record agreements

A specimen agreement begins from a source the relevant owner recognizes. The supplier may possess an authenticated clinical case, a registered organism, a recovered archive, a captured stock, or a valid fictional nonhuman lead. The agreement records what exists and which research purposes the supplier permits. A country cannot sell ownership of material that remains in an enemy-controlled laboratory.

Three forms are available. A clinical-record exchange sends useful observations without physical material. A supervised study permits work at the supplier's site. A physical loan or transfer moves a particular object through its custody owner. Each form can satisfy only the prerequisites that the receiving research owner actually accepts.

Clinical data can establish the direction of an adaptation while leaving a required physical lead unresolved. On-site study can qualify a result without moving an independent organism or surrendering the supplier's archive. A physical loan can permit one programme while reserving the supplier's property rights and requiring return of the surviving object. Neither a loan nor a study visit implies unlimited reproduction or offensive permission.

The proposed administrative review lasts 60 days and costs 35 Political Power and 50 support equipment. It verifies the agreement's record and prepares the permitted study, and it can be waived when an existing standing agreement already covers that exact purpose. It does not authenticate a false source merely by finishing the timer. The source owner still supplies the validity finding.

Physical movement then uses the appropriate material owner. The complete transfer can commit the named material, route transport, fuel, and a qualified custody service, at most four types. A stock item already reserved by a native operation is unavailable. Where the current owner prohibits ordinary licensing or lend-lease, the agreement remains an intent until a distinct, explicitly approved custody transaction exists. A generic equipment transfer must not be used to evade that restriction.

The recipient chooses whether to accept narrow access, negotiate broader rights, or pursue another source. A public institute may pay for a supervised clinical lead and reject custody it cannot safely support. A military directorate may accept a costly storage obligation because the material supports several permitted projects. A neutral donor may insist that its contribution remain confined to treatment and publish the agreement if those terms are broken.

The material retains source identity through subsequent research. Independent discovery does not mark the original event as fired, create its country, or award its unrelated rewards. The arrival of a source event later merges compatible capability records without repeating learning or inventing a second copy of the physical specimen.

A failed handover can leave the material at origin, in the carrier's custody, recovered by the recipient, destroyed with an owner-proven loss, or unaccounted for. Missing material is not automatically a released outbreak. Broken seals or inconsistent records produce a custody review and, when supported, a medical incident. All biological content remains abstract fictional game information.

The lasting relationship depends on truthful reporting and observance of purpose. A supplier that receives useful clinical findings may offer later leads. A recipient that hides the result can retain its stolen advantage and acquire a discoverable breach. Scientific credit records the supplier's contribution without transferring command of the recipient's institute.

## F05. Foreign military medicine

A military client requests a service for specified formations or a named field-hospital network. Ordinary casualty care, infection protection, temporary enhancement, permanent alteration, and engineered-lineage control remain different programmes. The recipient cannot purchase the strongest effect by describing every request as emergency medicine.

Ordinary field care uses the completed medical family and existing military medical owners. Selected-formation treatment retains Part 6's actual recipients, funded course, recovery period, and continuing care. Foreign delivery does not restart a course, erase accumulated dependence, or convert an expired benefit into a permanent one. A renamed, split, merged, or transferred formation retains the treatment disposition that its owner can prove.

The client chooses whether to import treatment under its own qualified service, receive an embedded foreign medical team, or establish an independent service through teaching and production. An embedded service can begin earlier when the provider has spare qualified personnel. It also leaves the client exposed to loss of that service. Independent qualification takes longer and gives the client a practical way to reduce dependence without losing its soldiers' care.

A new embedded dispatch uses the same four-type ceiling as other complete medical-team deliveries: compatible care stock, qualified team commitment, route transport, and fuel. The programme's existing enrolment and production costs remain where Part 6 places them. The diplomatic agreement does not add a second payment for the same course. A single host medical team cannot simultaneously support all foreign clients that signed a treaty.

The agreement names operational command and medical authority separately. The recipient commands its army. The provider can report that a formation is no longer fit for the funded treatment or control service and can withdraw its personnel under the agreement. That does not transfer national military command to Wu. Any proposed change in allegiance requires its own existing force-owner process and actual causal conditions.

A request involving engineered zombies, chimeric troops, or other special formations needs the provider's exact family and support requirements. Guardian formations retain their dependence on prepared sites. A foreign hospital agreement does not make them effective mobile expeditionary units. Carrier formations do not release infection during ordinary movement or combat. Any offensive use remains a separately authorized native operation with its own stock, preparation, result, and liability.

The client may ask for control-treatment knowledge as part of the agreement. The host can grant a qualified service handover, supply continued treatment without releasing the full archive, or refuse new enrolment. A partial archive can help a client prepare a successor but cannot substitute for a missing qualification. Ending a commercial licence does not destroy the client's existing treatment records.

The last safe handover date is based on the actual care interval. Part 6's 45-day control emergency remains relevant even when an international negotiation has a longer normal timetable. A successor who needs 90 days cannot be certified after 45 days by accepting diplomatic terms. The client must retain an interim provider, withdraw the formations in time, or face the already defined control failure.

When war begins between the parties, the agreement rechecks physical access and personnel security. The recipient can offer protected return, continued neutral supervision, or custody. A neutral provider may assume an existing service only after qualification and resource commitments are proven. The host's decision to stop supplying new military courses is different from abandoning already dependent people.

The recovery path concerns actual formations. Supported withdrawal, temporary loss of the special benefit, ordinary veteran care, or provider-approved retirement can preserve people. If exact formation transfer cannot preserve strength, equipment, experience, and treatment history, it remains blocked. Deleting the old unit and spawning a fresh full-strength replacement is not an acceptable transfer substitute.

Military AI values a programme according to the formations it can continue to supply. It avoids exporting its last qualified handler team and refuses extreme alteration when no later care is feasible. Aggressive clients can deliberately accept dependence for an urgent advantage, but that decision must leave a real support problem after the operation ends.

## F06. International access and inspection agreements

A demand for international access can come from an affected country, a group of existing partners, or a government with credible evidence of dangerous work. The request names its purpose. Emergency treatment access, production observation, clinical verification, and investigation of misconduct require different permissions and produce different findings.

The host can offer a limited inspection, an independent clinical team, neutral manufacture, a public treatment standard, or a supervised visit to a named site. It can refuse, narrow the proposal, or negotiate reciprocity. A refusal records the actual decision. It does not automatically prove weapon use or reveal hidden experiments that the requesting country has never identified.

A proposed ordinary access conference lasts 90 days and commits 50 Political Power and one civilian factory. Its result is a signed set of access terms, a limited agreement, or an unresolved dispute. Those two costs pay for the political and institutional work. They do not provide inspectors, medicine, transport, or laboratory production for free. The resulting services use their own visible commitments.

Inspection reaches only what was admitted and actually inspected. A clinical team can validate treatment performance in the patients it sees without certifying every secret site. A production observer can verify a named works while remaining unable to identify an unrelated archive. A full investigation needs supported evidence and access to the relevant owner records. The report should state a bounded finding rather than an absolute national verdict.

The shared Condemnation inspection and observer actions remain their own services. When a country takes one of those actions, the shared owner controls its costs, disclosure, decay effects, and record. Wu's access case can lead the player to that existing action. It cannot silently replay it or transform the current aggregate disclosure into an invented exact-case audit.

A public medical institute can give independent institutions standing access to selected treatment work. This improves continuity after Wu leaves and makes inflated claims easier to challenge. A classified institute can preserve a narrow military service and permit independent civilian care. The trade-off lies in real access, secrecy, time, and trust, without requiring a second permanent public value.

A successful agreement can reduce one diplomatic dispute and permit a concrete service that was previously blocked. It does not remove a native embargo, grant general military passage, or force nonparticipants to recognize the arrangement. Each transit or sanction owner still decides the permissions it controls.

When an inspector discovers a relevant fault, the host can correct the affected programme, accept a limited suspension, dispute the finding through an independent review, or conceal it. A correction improves current safety and preserves the discovery history. Concealment creates evidence only through the responsible owner and the actual act, not a generic penalty for choosing a secret mandate.

AI countries favour access that produces useful care or reliable information. A desperate recipient can accept a narrow clinical agreement while continuing to demand broader investigation. A host with legitimate capacity problems can propose local manufacture rather than pay for a conference that cannot meet an immediate medical deadline.

## F07. Asylum, protection, and voluntary departure

An asylum case concerns a specific living Wu, qualified copy, or member of the institute. The foreign government needs a way to contact that person, and the person needs a reason to consider leaving. A request can arise from political restriction, exposure, a threatened laboratory, loss of legal protection, refusal to continue harmful work, or a conflict between qualified copies.

Asylum grants protection and a receiving arrangement. It does not appoint the person to a scientific office, transfer their institute, or supply the receiving country with the whole portfolio. The host may offer refuge without requiring employment. Wu may accept temporary protection while refusing a permanent appointment, and the receiving state must decide whether those terms are acceptable.

The proposed formal protection arrangement costs the sponsor 75 Political Power and commits one civilian factory for 90 days to establish the receiving institution and legal arrangement. It remains a political programme. A prepared institution can use an existing valid arrangement without repurchasing the same protection. Medical care, travel, and any new laboratory still require their own actual support.

A provisional refuge can be accepted once the destination has a proven safe receiving place, a valid route, and the person's agreement. The 90-day programme establishes the standing protection institution. It does not make an endangered person wait 90 days for permission to enter an already prepared refuge. Provisional shelter grants neither a free laboratory nor a completed appointment, and its care and accommodation remain actual receiving obligations.

The person considers the destination's viable institute, the offered mandate, personal safety, existing obligations, institutional rights, and access to legitimate work. A government with a record of confinement or exposed experiment sites can offer asylum, but that record reduces the credibility of its promise. An attractive salary or title cannot turn an unsuitable receiving state into a safe medical institution.

A voluntary departure is agreed separately with the old host when possible. The old country can retain copied knowledge, negotiate a transition period, keep independently qualified institutions, and seek return of borrowed material. The departing scientist can take personal records and permitted objects actually removed. National facilities, staff who choose to remain, patient records held by hospitals, and existing stock do not follow the character automatically.

Active native scientist assignment must be settled through its owning project before ordinary role transfer. The transfer cannot silently remove a scientist from a live project, complete its remaining clock, or refund its native costs. Where the engine offers no supported safe interruption, the voluntary move waits or becomes a proposed owner extension. Physical capture during war remains a separate fact and does not need to pretend an ordinary appointment transfer succeeded.

The new country qualifies the arriving person's office. A fully qualified Wu can provide his established personal work after a viable institute and appointment are ready. A continuity copy retains the qualification actually completed in Part 7. A copy with an older archive can know less than the departed original, and the two can disagree about obligations or political direction.

A protection offer can fail before departure, during transport, or after arrival. Before departure, the offer may be withdrawn and consumed political preparation remains spent. In transit, the actual transport owner records arrival, delay, capture, or loss. After arrival, broken protection can create confinement, exposure, or a second asylum search. None of these outcomes awards another Event 089 arrival demonstration or founding reward.

A former host may later request emergency care from the country that sheltered Wu. That request uses the current clinical relationship and does not erase the earlier expulsion. The asylum state can supply medicine without returning the scientist. A negotiated return is possible only when the person, current holder, and receiving institution satisfy the relevant transfer contract.

Kruger remains one canonical character with his existing eligibility, transfer, and continuity rules. A domestic dispute with Wu can supply a reason for departure, but this case does not relax Kruger's prior-host exclusions, bypass a pending cross-domain review, or activate his international contest early.

## F08. Staff recruitment and institutional competition

Foreign recruitment targets an actual shortage and a real person or team. A country lacking cure-production expertise may seek the teacher who helped qualify an existing works. An army with dependent formations may seek a qualified care director. An independent university may offer a place to a researcher excluded under Part 10's supremacy settlement.

Open recruitment begins with a declared post, receiving institution, and terms. Covert recruitment uses a distinct operation against a known accessible target. Neither selects an imaginary perfect assistant from a generic national pool. A visitor, former colleague, public author, or authenticated staff contact can supply the target, and the available knowledge follows that target's real history.

The proposed recruitment campaign lasts 90 days and spends 100 Political Power, 125 support equipment, and one civilian-factory commitment. A covert version also commits one real operative through the relevant intelligence owner, reaching four cost types. The person being recruited is the target, not an extra free research team or a payment deducted from civilian population.

The old institution can offer recognition, a better post, an independent research guarantee, or protected departure. A noncompulsory employment dispute does not automatically justify confinement. Coercive retention remains a deliberate political action with discoverable consequences, and it can make future voluntary cooperation less credible.

Success obtains the person or agreed team after actual arrival. Their appointment is evaluated separately. A laboratory specialist may bring a valid treatment standard and still need a functioning works to use it. A trained handler may maintain an existing family but lack Wu's authority to redesign it. A replicated specialist has the same finite identity and qualification rules as any other copy.

Partial success can obtain a temporary secondment, a published method, a candidate who lacks the desired qualification, or a useful contact for later talks. The foreign report identifies that narrower result. It cannot describe a declined job offer as a completed theft or grant a full project stage as consolation.

The old host loses only the service actually provided by the departing staff. Completed national knowledge remains available. When the team was the sole qualified provider for a continuing programme, its departure opens a handover problem with the existing deadline. A government can fund a replacement, negotiate a delayed departure, reduce new enrolment, or wind down the programme safely.

A country can build an independent school around recruited expertise. It pays the appropriate teaching and facility costs and earns qualification through completed work. This gives recruitment a durable payoff beyond a small research modifier and gives the old host a reason to invest in independent institutions rather than rely on one indispensable assistant.

AI recruits for demonstrated gaps. It does not pay repeatedly for a method already known and operational at home, except when it genuinely needs another independent team. It values an accepted secondment differently from a permanent appointment and will not abandon its own urgent care to train a rival without an explicit strategic reason.

## F09. Treatment theft, design theft, and stolen material

The actor must choose its objective. Copying a treatment record, stealing a fixed design, seizing finished medicine, and taking a dangerous physical object are different operations. A single successful theft does not obtain all four. Each objective has its own target evidence, access requirement, outcome, and later use.

A treatment-record theft seeks a documented standard. The complete record gives the recipient that specific knowledge with its known date and limitations. It still needs qualification, manufacture, and delivery. A partial record identifies exactly which stages or supporting records are absent. The receiving institute can complete a bounded validation task instead of receiving either a perfect cure or meaningless generic research points.

A design theft seeks one fixed profile or a specified part of a portfolio. The copied record retains its source and production qualification status. It does not alter the thief's existing stock, satisfy a native payload requirement, or raise the country's active modified-standard ceiling. The thief can preserve more designs than it currently manufactures, under Part 9's normal rules.

A proposed archive operation lasts 90 days and costs 75 Political Power, 125 support equipment, and one operative commitment. It requires a known archive or an authenticated route to a person holding the record. Possession of an intelligence agency alone does not identify the exact object. Success, detection, and attribution are separate findings under the eventual operation owner.

A physical-stock seizure follows the material owner's actual transfer or capture contract. It reduces the former holder's available quantity and credits only the amount actually recovered. Medicine committed to a clinical programme or native operation cannot also be stolen as free national reserve stock. If a native transaction does not expose an exact recoverable remainder, that objective is unavailable until an owner-approved interface exists.

Theft of a living cohort preserves its people, identities, training, medical needs, and first-settlement history. It does not create new manpower at the receiving laboratory. The operation cannot use a generic equipment grant to represent people. Coercive custody changes who controls them without certifying employment, loyalty, or medical safety.

The host can narrow access, move uncommitted material through a real relocation, strengthen independent archives, investigate staff contact, or publish a clinical standard to remove its scarcity. Publication can make future theft of that exact public information pointless while leaving military designs secret. It does not erase an already completed offence or reverse the recipient's acquired knowledge.

A false, stale, or damaged record is useful only to the degree its content supports. The simulation can know that an archive was compromised while the actor initially sees an unresolved evaluation. This uncertainty belongs to the document's condition. It must not secretly rewrite a valid standard after the theft simply to impose a punishment.

The receiving country can disclose that it obtained useful medical knowledge improperly and still use a qualified treatment to save people. Medical success and responsibility for the theft remain separate. A later settlement can license continued manufacture or compensate a real loss. It cannot make the original source disappear from the record.

Counterfeit medicine is a possible downstream case when a third party claims to supply a stolen standard without meeting its qualification or production requirements. The player can compare authenticated records, quarantine suspect stock within the game system, obtain an independent evaluation, or recall affected lots. The event provides no real manufacturing, concealment, or dissemination methods.

## F10. Abduction and contested custody

Abduction targets one identified living person at a location or opportunity supported by current intelligence. An operation aimed at one Wu cannot silently substitute another copy after the target moves. A staff member with a similar title is not a valid replacement target, and a missing character does not establish that the operation succeeded.

This is an abstract intelligence operation with no real-world tactical procedure. The proposed preparation lasts 120 days and commits 100 Political Power, 200 support equipment, and one operative. A suitable receiving custody institution is a validity requirement. Any actual movement and custody mutation must be supplied by a verified character or population owner and remain distinct from an ordinary voluntary appointment.

The actor chooses a stated purpose within the fictional political case: remove the scientist from a threatening programme, force access to his work, exchange custody, or secure him before an opposing country does. These motives influence diplomatic reactions and the target's later willingness. They do not change the physical proof needed for capture.

Full success produces actual custody of the selected person and ends their incompatible active appointment. It does not create willingness to cooperate, duplicate their advisor role, transfer the institute, or grant their entire knowledge as a country technology package. The captive can refuse, bargain for patient protection, provide only routine medical work, or seek contact with another institution.

Partial results are specific. The operation may fail to move the target but obtain an authenticated location, expose its own sponsor, interrupt a visit, or cause an injury that the medical owner records. A partial outcome cannot invent a captured assistant who never existed. A disrupted operation can end without any object changing hands.

The former host responds according to evidence and continuing need. It may negotiate release, seek neutral verification of the captive's condition, protect the remaining institute, or begin a separate rescue operation. Those choices coexist with care for patients left behind. The state can appoint a qualified successor without declaring the captive dead.

A captive's existing continuity network remains external to the operation. Capturing one qualified Wu leaves other actual qualified copies and independent institutions intact. A stored archive without a living qualified recipient does not instantly replace the captive. The medical network's ability to continue follows the resources and people that exist.

Custody can pass through a prisoner exchange, negotiated release, rescue, occupation, or the captor's collapse. Each change retains the same person and known history. The new holder receives the person in their current state, including injury, refusal, or incomplete qualification. A custody cycle cannot repeatedly grant recruitment rewards.

AI requires a meaningful objective and credible access before attempting abduction. During its own severe outbreak it normally prefers a feasible treatment agreement over a slow operation that would leave the target unwilling and without a laboratory. A threatened military power may still choose coercion, accepting the cost, uncertainty, and resulting loss of medical cooperation.

## F11. Assassination, injury, and continuity

An assassination operation targets an exact fictional person and requires a known location or authenticated opportunity, a hostile motive, and an available intelligence route. It is not an automatic response to another country having better medicine. A peaceful medical institution may still attract a hostile actor, but the operation needs a specific political or strategic cause.

The proposed abstract operation lasts 150 days and spends 150 Political Power, 250 support equipment, and one operative commitment. Those values describe a game decision, not a method of violence. The final route must use an owner-approved identity and result contract. A percentage roll alone is insufficient authority to delete a character or add deaths.

The actor can obtain failure, exposed failure, a verified injury, a verified death, or an unresolved outcome. Detection and public attribution are evaluated separately from the target's physical result. A body not recovered and a person absent from the roster should remain unresolved when the owner cannot confirm death.

Confirmed death ends that person's service and enters the shared Deaths system once through its responsible owner. The associated clinical patients do not all die automatically. Their risk comes from interrupted care, missing adaptation ability, or failed control under the existing medical and formation systems. Independent physicians continue the work for which they are qualified.

Wu continuity does not make a living target invulnerable. A copy can die, and its death remains a death even when another qualified copy later takes office. An already existing qualified successor can preserve the institution. An unqualified body must complete its remaining preparation, and an archive alone cannot create a replacement person for free.

A successful attack on one Wu does not identify the original, prove every other copy dead, or reveal the complete continuity archive. Where several copies are known, the operation result names its selected target and the institution that lost that person's service. The government can exploit public confusion, but the underlying identity records remain exact.

An injured Wu may enter the earlier critical-care or recovery programme. His appointment can become temporarily unavailable while his institution continues routine work. The recovery route uses actual medical qualification, supplies, and time. A foreign hospital can provide care without acquiring permanent ownership of the patient or his personal methods.

An exposed operation changes future relationships. It can close voluntary visits, encourage neutral protection, strengthen a domestic opponent's case, or bring independent medical institutions together. Liability for an attack, associated civilian harm, cover-up, or misuse of a protected agreement belongs to the appropriate evidence owner. The event must not invent a biological-use entry for an ordinary assassination.

A later peaceful settlement cannot remove a confirmed death or restore a spent continuity entitlement. It can support remaining patients, compensate an actual loss, and permit the institute to recover. The actor's AI should account for the possibility that killing one physician strengthens hostile institutions while leaving their knowledge intact.

## F12. Exposure, testimony, and disputed evidence

Exposure begins with evidence of a specific act or condition. A legitimate treatment programme, the presence of a guarded laboratory, and a country owning an advanced design are not proof that it used a weapon or harmed patients. A witness, inspected site, authenticated record, captured institution, or verified operation can supply the basis for a public case.

The exposing country chooses what it is prepared to claim. It can publish a supported clinical failure, reveal a broken access agreement, present evidence of coercive experimentation, identify a confirmed attack, or ask for independent inspection. Each claim has a subject, responsible party where established, and a known evidence boundary.

The proposed independent review lasts 90 days and commits 60 Political Power, 100 support equipment, and one civilian factory. It pays for a sustained inquiry and protection of its records. The timer does not turn weak evidence into certainty. The outcome depends on what the investigation actually obtains, what access remains valid, and whether the relevant owner can authenticate the finding.

A clean finding can support a correction, an inspection agreement, a suspension of one programme, or a shared Condemnation disclosure. An incomplete finding can justify further observation without establishing a public accusation. A demonstrably false claim damages the claimant's credibility and can produce diplomatic redress. The underlying act does not change because one party won the argument.

The host can permit a bounded review, provide a genuine correction, contest the interpretation, restrict access, or conceal records. A response that destroys evidence creates its own cover-up history where the owner supports it. It does not delete the original act, the victims, or records already held elsewhere. Publication by several countries refers to the same underlying incident and does not duplicate deaths or repeatedly award the full original liability.

Clinical failure and deliberate misconduct need different wording. A treatment with a disclosed limitation can fail in an unsupported case without becoming proof of an atrocity. Deliberately concealing that limitation from patients or governments is a separate act. A withheld cure can create diplomatic anger, but a public claim that the host had spare stock requires actual knowledge of that stock and its other obligations.

Whistleblowers remain people with their own motives, qualifications, and vulnerabilities. The receiving country can offer protection or a temporary post. It does not obtain every secret that the person might have heard. A witness can be credible about one clinic and mistaken about another. Their testimony can lead to an inspection without itself awarding a complete research family.

The medical system preserves patients' access while an inquiry runs whenever a qualified and supplied provider remains available. The government can remove an accused director from new work without closing every hospital. Where the director is the only current specialist, the case exposes a real dependence problem. The player can retain limited supervised care, accelerate an already possible handover, or withdraw the affected programme.

Shared Condemnation receives only source-supported public or hidden contributions. Its present inspection and observer rules are not assumed to provide a generic exact-biological-case disclosure API. A new Wu evidence contribution needs an explicit owner contract that preserves source identity and avoids re-registering an already recorded act. Missing attribution remains missing, and a suspected actor must not be silently substituted as confirmed responsible country.

AI favours evidence it can substantiate and actions that protect its population or challenge a genuine threat. It can use exposure strategically, including against an ally, but it cannot read the opponent's hidden misconduct state without an information source. Presentation follows the affected people and verified result, with uncertainty left visible where the evidence remains incomplete.

## F13. Site attacks, sabotage, and the surviving institute

A site operation selects an actual institute state and a defined objective. Disrupting research, seizing records, interrupting production, freeing a captive, and destroying a dangerous stock are separate objectives. A raid against one works is not an attack on every Wu institution in the country.

The actor needs current knowledge of the target and a valid operation route. A public institution's location can be known without revealing its hidden stocks. A stale intelligence report can identify a former laboratory and no longer prove that Wu is present. The operation preserves its selected site and cannot follow a relocated scientist automatically.

Native combat, bombing, raids, and existing sabotage owners retain their own requirements and payment contracts. This part adds the medical and institutional consequences of an owner-proven result. It does not create an instant destruction button that bypasses range, resources, permissions, or the native operation's preparation.

For a newly required Wu-specific sabotage or rescue interface, the four-cost ceiling and identity contract must be designed with the native owner before it becomes executable. A Political Power-only placeholder, generic country modifier, or arbitrary successful dice roll cannot stand in for the actual site action. The unresolved interface is recorded in the implementation register without reducing the intended branch to a cosmetic report.

The target's defence concerns what it actually controls. It can move uncommitted stock, relocate available staff, prepare a secondary clinic, harden an established site through its normal construction owner, or seek neutral protection. Those preparations cost time and resources. A new protection agreement does not make a hospital immune to ordinary war.

An attack can damage buildings without killing Wu, destroy medicine without destroying the design, capture staff without obtaining their cooperation, or interrupt a project while independent institutions continue routine production. Each outcome reads the actual site inventory and presence. A scientist already evacuated cannot also be killed in the laboratory they left.

Destruction of a physical stock closes that stock's quantity through its owner. It does not delete copies of the design held elsewhere. Destruction of an archive can remove the host's only usable record of a method, while foreign copies remain available. A damaged institute may therefore recover through a former partner, including a country it previously refused to help.

A biological accident following an attack requires the relevant owner to establish the material, location, and valid incident. The attack itself is not a general outbreak trigger. Ordinary sabotage cannot create an unspecified dangerous strain merely because the story needs a worse result. When an actual exposure is registered, treatment, contamination, attribution, and deaths use their existing owners.

An attack on an accepted medical route can breach that exact agreement when the attack source proves the affected state, actor, and current route generation. Control loss alone does not prove who attacked the convoy or hospital. The humanitarian corridor's existing exact attack path is limited to the evidence it accepts. An unavailable ordinary-combat callback must remain an evidence gap rather than a guessed attribution.

After the attack, the surviving institute receives a recovery case. The host chooses which retained services to restore first, where to place remaining staff, whether to seek foreign medicine, and which commitments it can still fulfil. The goal is an actual functioning clinic, recovered archive, qualified replacement service, or repaired works. A fixed timer cannot certify all four at once.

Hostile AI evaluates a site's demonstrated role and the wider consequences. Destroying a provider needed by its own soldiers should be a poor choice unless an explicit route or urgent threat justifies that sacrifice. A rescue objective should prioritize the captive's survival. A record-seizure objective should not default to complete destruction as a successful equivalent.

## F14. Sanctions, restricted supply, and medical exceptions

Sanctions against a Wu host follow publicly supported conduct and the shared participating-country system. Research capability or possession alone does not count as use. A country can restrict its own cooperation for political reasons without fabricating a shared biological-use entry.

The shared Condemnation owner retains its public tiers, hidden evidence, participant selection, costs, fatigue, practical penalties, and source-owned native embargo claims. Wu's foreign cases can establish a reason to request inspection, cease new military cooperation, or participate in an existing sanction action. They do not apply a second copy of the same sanction burden.

A participant can maintain a clinical relationship while opposing military research. It can continue an existing care course, refuse new enhancement contracts, offer local production to an affected population, or request a humanitarian carve-out. The carve-out has the actual effect its owner defines. It must not be presented as a universal native trade exemption or as permission to move prohibited material.

The parties must distinguish an institutional exception from a physical route. Permission for doctors to treat patients does not prove that medicine can cross an embargoed sea route. A valid exceptional shipment needs the actual transport and material owners to admit it. When that cannot be established, local manufacture from already held knowledge, care from another qualified supplier, or a different valid route are meaningful alternatives.

Existing native embargo ownership is preserved. Releasing a Condemnation claim cannot remove the Air Cleanliness Treaty, Great Embargo, or pre-existing external claim on the same relationship. A medical coalition does not cancel those claims by changing the name of its agreement. Broad native embargo behaviour must be described honestly, including the absence of commodity-specific enforcement where the engine does not support it.

Sanctions also have a time history. New lend-lease can be blocked while an already active agreement remains in place. Existing licences and research-sharing memberships cannot be declared revoked through a generic effect that does not exist. Wu's own future permissions can be withdrawn within their actual contract, but a foreign country retains knowledge it already learned.

A client dependent on control treatment presents a difficult case. The sanctioning government can stop expanding the programme, maintain limited supervised care, seek a neutral handover, or accept the consequences of abandoning the service. The original last safe date remains. Labelling the shipment humanitarian cannot disguise additional offensive stock, and cancelling the contract cannot make dependent people cease to exist.

The sanctioned host can comply with a supported inspection, close a relevant harmful programme, compensate a real victim, seek another producer, or continue defiance. Compliance can improve its current position through the shared owner's rules. It cannot erase prior confirmed use, dead patients, hidden evidence, or the highest tier already recorded.

A commercial dispute alone does not trigger the full global sanctions system. Failure to deliver medicine, licensing disagreements, and recruitment conflicts create bilateral or coalition consequences first unless they also establish a qualifying public act. This keeps sanctions consequential and prevents every unpaid invoice from becoming an atrocity case.

Participant AI runs through the shared sanction owner. Wu's planning logic can prioritize a case for that owner or recommend a political response, but it cannot add a second automatic executor that creates the same pair again. Human and AI clinical decisions still use the same actual access restrictions and cannot gain a hidden exception through a dialogue option.

## F15. Medical coalitions and shared production

A medical coalition grows from countries that have already contributed to a real programme or can make a credible first commitment. It brings together knowledge holders, producers, transport providers, and affected recipients. It is not an automatic military faction, a world government, or a new source of free research capacity.

The proposed founding conference takes 90 days and costs its sponsor 50 Political Power and one civilian-factory commitment. At least two existing countries must accept useful roles before the coalition becomes active. A list of signatures without any qualified provider or feasible service remains a failed conference. The founding action does not pay the members' later production or transport bills.

The charter can follow one of three working forms. An open medical compact publishes selected clinical standards and encourages distributed qualification. A licensed production pool keeps manufacturing within named qualified members and reserves agreed output. An emergency service league concentrates on temporary deployments and neutral access during crises. These forms change rights and priorities while retaining the same underlying medicine and personnel.

Members contribute actual objects or work. A producer can reserve specified output from a real works. A transport member can commit a route it can operate. A teaching member can qualify another team. A recipient can supply clinical access and the facilities needed for care. A contribution of knowledge does not also count as delivered medicine, and a pledged factory is not available to two simultaneous coalition orders.

The coalition holds no duplicate stock balance. Medicine remains with the producing, carrying, or receiving owner until a real transfer changes its holder. The coalition's record describes claims and promised output. It cannot spend a pooled number that is unsupported by those physical records. Lost or recalled lots reduce the relevant promise and leave a new allocation problem.

Allocation follows a charter rule that the members can see. The open compact prioritizes clinically urgent feasible care, with a protected reserve for ongoing courses. The production pool honours its agreed output shares while allowing explicit emergency redistribution. The service league prioritizes functioning routes and teams during its declared emergency. A government can dispute a proposed allocation or refuse an extraordinary diversion, and that refusal has a specific consequence.

Leadership belongs to a country or appointed institution with an accepted mandate. Wu can direct medical work without automatically leading the coalition politically. A foreign qualified copy can hold a clinical office while the original serves another member. Their disagreements become actual project or service cases, not an automatic civil war between all member countries.

A change of coordinator preserves standing commitments and physical holders. The new coordinator cannot reclaim every member's stock or replay the founding benefit. A member leaving keeps its legitimate knowledge and national infrastructure. Its outstanding deliveries and care obligations require completion, renegotiation, or recorded breach. Departure removes future privileges only where the agreement owns them.

A coalition can suspend a member from new military-linked research while maintaining its civilian medical service. Exposure of misconduct does not make the entire coalition guilty of the same act. Members that knowingly support concealment or misuse can acquire their own evidence-backed responsibility. A neutral service member can separate its role and continue care under reviewed terms.

Conflict between members does not automatically end every clinical programme. The coalition can use neutral coordination for existing courses, stop new shared production, or dissolve into bilateral arrangements. Routes that are no longer physically valid close under their owners. A charter cannot create a general ceasefire or permit armies to march through medical passage.

The coalition's strongest payoff is a network of independently qualified providers that can continue without Wu's presence at every site. It can reduce a regional medical crisis through actual manufacture and service, preserve specialist knowledge after one institute is lost, and give smaller countries bargaining power against a monopoly. Those gains come from completed contributions, not from a national spirit that declares everyone protected.

A later national or international evolution can change the coalition's scale, secrecy, or political pressure. The coalition already functions as baseline diplomacy. Its existence never completes a research family, advances an evolution, or supplies a world-end condition by itself.

## F16. Evacuation, safe passage, and people left behind

A threatened institute can evacuate people, move records, relocate uncommitted material, maintain treatment with staff who remain, or negotiate continued operation under a new controller. These are separate commitments because their objects have different owners and may take different routes. The player chooses a viable combination from the institute's actual situation.

The evacuation case begins with a current site and the people or objects present there. Wu's personal movement uses his character and custody owner. Existing settled civilian staff, patients, and dependants use the movement owner's population and cohort rules. A whole institution cannot be moved by transferring only its director's character token.

A medical evacuation offers ordinary protection, controlled reception, onward transit, or a defined temporary hospital stay. The destination must have current food-safety and reception proof, access to the relevant care, and a feasible route. A hospital building alone does not prove spare capacity. Untracked food conditions cannot be treated as stable by default.

The existing humanitarian corridor is an exact adjacent-front arrangement. It uses its actual origin, front state, counterpart, cohort, accepted route generation, and safety proofs. Its acceptance does not move people. The movement and reserve owners execute the later transfer. Part 11 does not reinterpret that contract as a universal overseas passage system.

A multi-leg or overseas evacuation needs its own supported route representation. It must name the real departure and receiving sites, participating carriers, accepted transit purposes, and the population or custody transaction used at each stage. Until those owners can provide the required facts, the larger route remains an implementation gap. A direct instantaneous population grant is not its fallback.

Pre-settlement finite cohorts require special care. They already exist and have food demand, custody, and identity, but they have not yet been credited to civilian population or ordinary military manpower. Their movement cannot debit a fabricated origin population. The proposed D03 owner extension must carry those people and their food-demand record between custodians, then permit their one first settlement only at a proven destination.

Already settled people use ordinary movement accounting. The origin debit includes those who die on the route, and the destination receives only survivors. A named character or specialist included in that cohort must not be counted again when their roster or appointment changes. The transfer record must explicitly state whether the person is already included in the population transaction.

Safe passage has a purpose and an end. It can protect an identified medical movement without granting general military access or asylum to every passenger. The receiving country may agree to clinical care while refusing a permanent scientific appointment. A patient can recover and return through a valid route without becoming the property of the hospital that treated them.

A 90-day ordinary preparation objective can ask the player to secure a receiving clinic, obtain the counterpart's agreement, and move the first identified group. Under immediate occupation pressure, a shorter window can instead ask for the people and records that can actually leave in time. The deadline should arise from the real threat and route, not demand an impossible complete evacuation.

When a route closes, people remain at the location their owner records. They can be trapped, returned under an accepted arrangement, redirected to another proven destination, or captured. A cancelled mission does not move them back by assumption. Food demand and care continue, and route losses are registered only from an owner-proven event.

Staff who remain can preserve a limited hospital service under occupation or a negotiated transfer. They keep their qualifications and the patients they can actually support. The original host can retain their contribution in its institutional history without continuing to count their service as available at another site. Leaving them behind may be a negotiated clinical choice or an abandonment, depending on consent and the actual support promised.

A receiving request can ask the food and reception owners to publish current facts for the exact candidate state. That is a factual initialization or refresh under those owners, not a permission to set unknown conditions to safe. A healthy country remains a possible destination once its actual capacity and safety are established. Missing evidence blocks the current admission, not the entire research family.

## F17. Capture, custodianship, and the new employer

Occupation of a laboratory state creates access to the objects actually left there. It does not prove that Wu is present, that the archive is complete, or that all staff will cooperate. The captor receives a custody case naming the known site, remaining services, and unresolved inventory.

The captor can preserve clinical operations, protect and release staff, seek a voluntary appointment, evaluate records, take control of supported material, or close the site. Each choice acts on the current object. Capturing a building is not the same as capturing a prepared native operation or the research portfolio of every institute in the former country.

The proposed site-evaluation programme lasts 90 days and commits one civilian factory, 100 support equipment, and a qualified evaluation-team assignment. It establishes which retained services and records can be used. It does not manufacture absent knowledge or convert unknown material into a safe standard. Routine protective action and urgent care do not wait for the completion of this administrative evaluation when a qualified local service already exists.

A complete clinical archive can support continued routine treatment. A partial archive may require revalidation, a surviving local specialist, or foreign help. A fixed design record retains its original source and production requirements. Seized material requires a compatible custody and use pathway before it can be spent. Existing restrictions on licensing, transfer, or dangerous stock remain binding.

Wu can accept employment, negotiate conditions, refuse, seek neutral protection, or remain in custody. The captor's offer must provide a viable institute and an explicit mandate. Coerced work does not give the same willingness or independent cooperation as a voluntary appointment. It can create discoverable misconduct and a later departure motive, with evidence owned by the relevant system.

An existing qualified copy can continue elsewhere while the captured person bargains. Their relationship is determined by their actual institutions and prior decisions. The captor cannot claim all copies as national property, and the original host cannot erase the captured person's identity to avoid acknowledging the loss.

Control of the site changes operating authority where the owner permits it. Completed national rights, proprietary rights, personal records, physical custody, and legal claims can remain different. Part 10's settled institutional history is retained even when the site changes hands. The former state may continue to claim a stolen archive without receiving a duplicate usable copy.

A laboratory can be released or returned through diplomacy. Return moves surviving objects that actually exist and restores permission where agreed. It does not regenerate destroyed medicine, replace dead staff, or reset a native project clock. A recipient taking back a damaged institution must decide which service it can restore first.

The captor's strongest practical choice may be preserving an independent local clinic. It gains usable care and a reason for qualified staff to stay, while abandoning the attempt to obtain every secret. A different government may seize everything and then face a site it cannot operate. Both paths should have concrete results without using a generic loyalty modifier as a substitute for staff, supplies, and qualification.

## Commitments that survive departure

A change of Wu's host affects personal direction and exceptional work. It does not automatically end routine manufacturing, invalidate copied treatment standards, recall all foreign doctors, or close a coalition. Each institution retains its actual knowledge, property, staff, and outstanding commitments.

A continuing service names an accountable provider. If the departing person's unique skill was required, the institution must secure a qualified replacement, retain limited access to that person, reduce new work, or wind down the affected service. The deadline follows the earliest medical or control obligation. A longer political negotiation cannot postpone a shorter safe-care deadline.

An unfinished research project remains unfinished at its actual owner. Its completed knowledge and physical progress can survive departure without an automatic completion reward. The new host can begin related work from records legitimately carried by the scientist, but it does not receive the former host's factory interval, samples left behind, or native project receipt.

A country supporting several Wus keeps one country-wide accounting of Wu research lanes and modified production standards. Qualified copies can improve resilience and provide separate services, but they do not each create another free domestic board. Part 9's proposed two-standard ceiling applies across all its Wu branches and joint institutions. Foreign countries can qualify their own production through actual commitments, which is a real strategic consequence of diffusion.

Kruger's international contest remains distinct. Its existing access, operation, evolution, and history rules are not a general Wu API. A cross-border rivalry can supply context to an already valid Kruger operation or a separate valid Wu case. It cannot initialize Kruger's Evolution II, move his canonical character, or award his project stages merely because Wu signed a treaty.

D01's historical Event 089 sharing memberships and Kruger posture records retain their old meaning. New medical licences, clinical coalitions, and visiting programmes are not migrated old invitations. D02's proposed Scientific Research cluster uses reserved ID `21`, shared with Event 054 under the [central identifier ledger](../../shared_identifier_registry.md). The identifier allocation does not itself approve cluster design or membership, or register runtime/catalog content. D03 remains an unapproved shared finite-cohort extension. D04 leaves biological use with native equipment and operation owners throughout every foreign route.

## Changes of government and broken agreements

A treaty is tied to its actual parties and institutional generation. Annexation, re-release, civil war, a new government, and a change of institute director are different changes. A new officeholder can inherit an agreement when its political and physical owners remain valid. A country recreated after annexation cannot answer an old popup as though it were the original contracting institution.

A delayed response must match the agreement, its original subject, and its current response phase. A counteroffer to buy an old standard cannot accept a newer military design selected elsewhere in the interface. A ransom discussion cannot move a different captive. Closing an old case cannot clear the staff or material reservation belonging to a new case.

A government change does not erase completed clinical results or historical responsibility. The new government can repudiate future commitments, honour them, renegotiate them, or expose its predecessor. Each choice affects future action and present obligations. Existing medicine remains at its actual location, and independent institutions retain knowledge unless an actual owner-defined loss removes access to the record.

Coalition members can recognize a successor for a specific care agreement without deciding the entire civil war. Recognition of a hospital custodian does not grant cores, territory, faction membership, or ownership of the state's people. Military or diplomatic recognition outside that purpose remains with its proper system.

A breached agreement can remain useful history after its live actions close. The record distinguishes what was promised, what was paid, what arrived, who supplied care, and what remains disputed. It does not need to stay visible as an active mission forever. When no further playable obligation exists, it moves into the agreement history and stops consuming the current-case presentation budget.

### Neutral intermediaries

A neutral country can carry medicine, host a teaching course, coordinate a clinical service, or mediate a release. Its role is explicit, and its resources are real. A carrier that merely handles a shipment gains neither the recipient's treatment entitlement nor the manufacturer's design licence.

An intermediary cannot cleanse the history of a fixed design or change the identity of a stock item. A prohibited object does not become an ordinary support-equipment shipment by passing through a neutral country. A genuine clinical standard can be published openly, but a physical transfer must still satisfy the material and access owners.

The intermediary can also fail. It may lose a route, withdraw consent, suffer occupation, discover a false declaration, or refuse to continue a broken agreement. The shipment then keeps its actual location and disposition. Medicine already delivered is not pulled back into the intermediary's national stock, and an uncompleted journey is not credited as relief.

A mediator's practical value is the agreement it can make feasible. It can provide an accepted place for care, a compatible route, a qualified institution, or a trusted record. It should not receive a permanent generic diplomacy bonus merely for appearing in every transaction.

## Dynamic commitments and pacing

The numerical anchors represent one bounded case, one existing institution, or one declared service. Actual quotations respond to the work. Clinical quantity scales through Part 4's eligible living coverage. Military care scales through the selected formation's supported strength and course. Production and research use their existing family burdens. A country does not receive a cheap national treatment because it selected one diplomatic case.

Administrative review scales with the number of materially different institutions or rights being settled. The first agreement can use the listed anchor. A broader agreement must show its larger commitment and permit a smaller version. Existing valid terms remove already completed review work. Repeatedly reopening the same agreement cannot farm favourable Authority changes or force the other country to repay a full founding cost.

Transport timing uses the actual route and readiness provided by its owner. A missing route is a missing route, not an arbitrarily large duration. A prepared local producer can remove the shipping dependency while keeping manufacture and clinical deployment costs. An evacuation under immediate pressure can select a smaller achievable group without pretending that those left behind were moved.

Covert preparation depends on the available opportunity, intelligence access, target changes, and the actor's committed capability. The proposed 90-, 120-, and 150-day anchors define ordinary preparation bands. An obsolete target closes or revises the operation through its owner. It cannot complete solely because the original timer expired.

Pacing has room for recovery. Ordinary failed negotiations may be reopened after a material change or a proposed 90-day diplomatic interval, with the earlier refusal preserved. A repeated proposal without a changed offer or changed need merges into the old case. Covert attempts cannot be spammed against an unchanged target immediately after failure, and a new attempt requires both a renewed opportunity and its full new commitment.

Short emergency windows arise from care or control obligations already in progress. They do not replace the normal preparation time of an unbuilt institution. The player must see the mismatch when a proposed provider cannot arrive in time. A useful alternative can preserve routine care or withdraw a formation, but the interface cannot mark the impossible full objective as feasible.

## Five worked foreign situations

### An island client with knowledge but no delivery route

An overseas ally has a severe outbreak and requests two matching civilian allocations. The host has the standard and finished stock, but the route owner cannot establish safe shipment. Accepting the medical request creates an agreement to seek a feasible response. It does not commit nonexistent sea capacity or apply the cure abroad.

The client already has a suitable factory and a qualified clinical team. The host can supply the documented standard, allowing local manufacture through Part 4. The client pays the actual severe-treatment manufacturing burden and then deploys the medicine to its eligible living population. The original host's finished stock stays where it was and can serve another accepted request.

The foreign result is a successful knowledge and production partnership. It is not recorded as a delivered convoy. If the client lacks production qualification, a remote record alone cannot certify it, and the parties must seek a qualified local or reachable teaching service. The first relief objective then concerns what that service can actually achieve.

### Partial commercial delivery

The parties agree to ten comparable allocations for a support-equipment principal of 200, with 100 paid at commissioning. They additionally agree that early closure can settle useful delivered allocations at a principal value of 20 each. Six allocations arrive, and the remaining route becomes unavailable.

The accepted delivered value is 120. If the parties close the order at six allocations, the buyer owes 20 more after its existing 100 payment. If the order continues, the unpaid 80 remains associated with the remaining four allocations and is not credited as fulfilled revenue. The seller retains custody of those four only when the physical owner confirms they remain at its site.

A different outcome with four accepted allocations gives a delivered value of 80. Closing that agreement creates a restitution obligation of 20 against the deposit. The seller must actually possess and transfer that equipment to pay it. An unpaid claim does not create equipment at the buyer. Neither settlement returns consumed manufacture inputs or changes the medical effects of allocations already used.

The quantities are an accounting fixture. A real contract can use a different price or accepted settlement basis, but its stated rule must determine the result consistently.

### A formation whose provider cannot be replaced in time

A client has engineered formations with 45 days remaining before their current control service becomes unsafe. Its relationship with the supplier breaks down. An independent replacement service needs 90 days to qualify. Accepting a new agreement does not close the 45-day problem.

The client can maintain a limited interim service with the old provider, obtain another already qualified provider whose real handover can finish within the interval, or begin the supported withdrawal route. A proposed 15-day handover by an already qualified team can be feasible. A 90-day training course cannot be accelerated into that category by renaming it a handover.

A sanctioning ally may oppose future military contracts while supporting the limited care arrangement. The liability and sanctions owners retain their records, and the transport owner still checks access. If no support reaches the formations, Part 6's actual failure path proceeds. It is not replaced by an arbitrary foreign-relation penalty.

### One receiving district and three kinds of people

A reconstructed district has a verified 5,000-place receiving limit after food, housing, transport, and medical checks. Existing survivor and migrant commitments occupy 3,500 places. A proposed arrival of 1,600 finite new people cannot use the full headline capacity.

At most 1,500 new places remain, subject to the current proof and the movement or settlement owner's partial-admission contract. The other 100 remain in their actual custody with continuing food demand. If partial admission is unsupported, the request must be reduced and accepted again before movement. A medical treaty cannot ignore the occupied places.

An ordinary settled cohort of 1,000 evacuees that loses 20 people on a proven route credits 980 survivors at its destination. Its origin debit is 1,000, including the route deaths. If a named qualified Wu copy is included in those survivors, changing that person's roster does not add another person. An unsettled group uses the proposed finite-custody route and no fabricated origin population debit.

### A stolen portfolio and an operation already reserved

Country A owns 120 items of modified standard A. A native operation has reserved 40, and a separate owner-approved material commitment has reserved 30. Only 50 remain available. A valid separate physical transfer of 20 available items leaves 30 available at A and 20 with the receiving holder.

The production works changes to standard B. The old items keep standard A, including the 40 already in the native operation. A copied design for B does not turn any of them into B. The native operation's completed outcome accounts for its own 40-item reservation according to the inspected owner contract, with no extra Wu debit or scripted refund.

The physical balance after that completed operation is 30 available at A, 30 still committed to the other valid owner, 20 at the receiving holder, and 40 consumed through the native operation. Those amounts total 120. The history of the operation, its evidence, and any supported clinical outcome remain with their owners. Cancellation behaviour remains a separate unverified native question.

## Foreign strategies across a campaign

### The small medical centre

A small country can remain a valuable host by developing standards and teaching independent partners. It does not need to match a major power's production. Its first objective is a defensible domestic reserve and a feasible partnership, followed by a second producer or alternative route that reduces dependence on one patron.

Its difficult choice is exclusivity. A major sponsor can offer real industrial support in return for limited manufacture rights or priority supply. The small host can accept, negotiate a public civilian exception, or release the standard more widely. The different choices affect who funds its work, who can produce independently, and which countries seek access to Wu.

The small host retains its own appointment and clinical decisions unless it explicitly grants more authority. A production contract does not silently make it a puppet. Political pressure can grow through actual dependency and foreign demands, and the later sovereignty parts will decide any route that changes state authority.

### The industrial buyer

A major power can build a distributed clinical network from copied standards and trained teams. Its challenge is qualification and access to new adaptations. It can seek a long-term medical relationship, recruit a specialist, fund a joint project, or attempt theft. Each approach affects its ability to obtain reliable updates and the source's willingness to cooperate.

A major with two active modified production standards must choose which works to retool before adding another. Buying several designs does not expand the country's permitted active standards. Its ordinary cure production remains separate and can continue. A military buyer that monopolizes all qualified care staff may leave its civilian hospitals dependent on foreign help.

### The threatened patient state

A country facing an epidemic chooses among immediate imported care, slower independent manufacture, a sponsor's terms, and help from a hostile power. The best short-term arrangement may carry a long-term dependency. The country can use the time gained by successful relief to train staff and broaden production rather than remain a permanent customer.

Its government may reject a coercive demand even during a crisis. The game should show the cost of that decision through its actual untreated need and diplomatic alternatives. It should not punish refusal with an unrelated automatic outbreak or force surrender through a medical menu.

A request involving an independent nonhuman country identifies the patients or organisms that its owner allows the medical service to treat. Human captives, compatible nonhuman troops, and the independent government remain distinct. A cure cannot dissolve a Rat Nation, delete a zombie army, annex its territory, or turn its government into a patient. Hosting eligibility and an external limited medical agreement are separate questions, each requiring its own owner approval.

### The guarded military patron

A military host can export powerful selected-formation medicine while retaining control of new designs. It gains committed clients and foreign access that can support its strategy. Its limitation is the continuing service it must provide. A wide network of dependent troops can become difficult to maintain during war, staff loss, sanctions, or a dispute with Wu.

Its clients can seek independent qualification or another provider. The patron can permit a controlled handover and retain a narrower partnership, or oppose it and risk recruitment, theft, and exposure. A refusal to share the archive does not give the patron a free ability to command the client's army.

### The country that loses Wu

The former host's position depends on what it built. An independent teaching network can keep routine cures and manufacturing alive. A personally controlled institute may retain equipment and archives without enough qualified people to use them. Foreign partners can help recover the service, and former clients can become providers.

The departure does not reset the portfolio. The country can pursue national reconstruction, new staff, a qualified copy, or an agreement with Wu's new employer. It can also pursue revenge, but an attack that destroys its remaining medical partner may worsen its own situation. The system should make that consequence legible through actual services and obligations.

## Artificial intelligence and scenario expectations

AI first rejects unavailable actions using the same owner proofs and complete payment conditions as the player. It then compares useful outcomes within the actual current candidate set. A hidden clinical match, an unknown site, or an unqualified successor cannot enter its evaluation as certain information.

During an active disease crisis, feasible treatment and preservation of existing care normally outrank prestige and discretionary covert operations. Scarcity, hostile terms, military urgency, institutional dependence, exposed wrongdoing, and the available alternative providers can change that ordering. Ideology affects political preference after validity and does not replace clinical compatibility or transport proof.

The host evaluates the opportunity cost of each commitment. It refuses new work that would abandon an already funded course unless an explicit emergency or coercive route authorizes that sacrifice and shows its consequences. It can renegotiate a promise before failure when the route or capacity changes. It does not keep reissuing the same unaffordable offer.

## Presentation of foreign medicine

The foreign view uses the existing institute and agreement presentation. Wu Authority remains the main event value where relevant. The selected case shows its subject, present obligation, next useful action, and the critical deadline or shortage. Internal operation generations, ledger joins, source enums, and numerical intelligence components stay out of ordinary player text.

Reports identify the actual people and institutions involved. A medical delivery emphasizes the receiving service and the patients it can now reach. A teaching result shows the returning team using what it learned. A custody report names the missing or recovered person and the institution left to continue without them. A failed contract identifies the unfulfilled promise and surviving options.

Important option families need different voices. A public relief offer can be practical and open. A monopoly counteroffer can use restrained commercial arrogance. A military service agreement can emphasize duty and the cost of dependence. Exposure and civilian harm require serious wording, with any irony aimed at the responsible institution's claims. Captivity, death, and failed care are never treated as cheap jokes.

Secrecy affects what each country sees. The attacker knows its selected objective and the result its agents report. The host sees detected activity and medical consequences it can observe. A third country knows only what diplomacy, witnesses, or its own intelligence establish. The interface must not disclose every secret project because a country has received one shipment.

The proposed report-art families are clinical arrival, local teaching, contested scientific employment, damaged or captured institute, interrupted medical transport, and shared clinical service. Use fictional period-authentic documentary compositions for these invented scenes. Real people and identifiable historical institutions require appropriate sourced references and their own authorized portrait handling. Do not create an extra portrait family merely because a generic minister or surgeon speaks in a report.

The art should show the service or people involved. Generic staff tables, arrows on maps, sealed folders, and conference-room scenes should not dominate the foreign branch. A coalition's public result can be represented by different national teams working in a real clinical setting, with the agreement itself remaining a secondary detail.

Decision icons distinguish care, teaching, employment, investigation, custody, and physical movement. They reuse an appropriate accepted family when authorized, or receive a bounded new requirement where the existing inventory does not communicate the action. They need exact consumer references, native-size review, and asset-owner approval before production. This stage assigns design roles and does not create final artwork, audio, or production prompts.

Existing units, aircraft, ships, and medical teams retain their established model and sound ownership. A foreign agreement does not create a new combat identity. Any later implementation that introduces a genuinely new visible unit must receive its full owner, counter, model, animation, and sound review instead of treating diplomacy as permission to use a placeholder.

## What international medicine leaves for national ascendancy

Foreign medicine can leave the host with trained partners, dependable suppliers, dependent clients, protected personnel, damaged relationships, or copies of its work in countries it no longer controls. Those outcomes already matter in Calm World because they change who can produce, who can provide care, and what survives the loss of one institute.

The next stage must build National Medical Ascendancy from that actual network. It can alter the reach of existing standards, the pressure on hospital appointments, the place of independent institutions, and the cost of removing Wu. It must preserve the countries, people, knowledge, stock, and obligations created here. A national evolution cannot make every foreign agreement vanish or grant the technologies that those agreements never produced.
