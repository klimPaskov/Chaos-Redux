# Doctor Wu: the institute and public authority

## An institution that can survive its founder

The Wu Institute consists of a medical service, a research organization, trained staff, a body of recorded knowledge, and actual sites. These components can grow at different rates. A country may distribute emergency medicine widely while keeping unusual research in one secure laboratory. Another may develop a powerful experimental centre while leaving most civilian hospitals dependent on a small number of specialists.

The institute's lasting value comes from teaching, production, and access to patients. Wu's personal value comes from adapting to unfamiliar biological problems and proposing work beyond the competence of ordinary laboratories. Losing him removes that exceptional capacity. It does not make every trained physician forget a completed treatment.

The national institution is represented through one changing institutional effect rather than one national spirit for each project. An acute institute crisis can have a separate temporary effect. A reduced legacy replaces the full institution when Wu is gone. These states should not accumulate as three versions of the same medical bonus.

## Wu Authority

Wu Authority is the event's principal persistent public value. The proposed scale runs from 0 to 100. It measures the control the government has actually conceded over medical resources, staffing, security, sensitive material, and exceptional decisions. It does not measure how intelligent Wu is, how medically effective his treatment is, or how many technologies the country has unlocked.

| Authority | Stage | Institutional meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–19 | Supervised Consultant | Wu leads approved work while ordinary institutions retain budgets, appointments, and oversight. |
| 20–39 | Institute Director | Wu controls an established medical organization and substantial approved resources. |
| 40–59 | Strategic Authority | Wu has privileged access to military and classified institutions, with government limits still enforceable. |
| 60–79 | Sovereign Laboratory | Wu controls important staff and security arrangements, making intervention expensive and politically contested. |
| 80–100 | Biological Power Centre | The state depends on an organization capable of resisting the withdrawal of its privileges. |

Crossing a threshold changes the stage description and relevant decision context. It does not grant a technology, create a private army, activate an evolution, or conjure a facility. A country at 85 Authority with one small clinic has made a politically unhealthy arrangement, but it has not somehow given Wu armies and a transnational production network.

Material power is recorded through the actual institutions and projects that exist. Secure sites, exclusive treatment production, dedicated security, loyal specialists, and researched continuity have their own factual states. These are shown when relevant to a decision. They are not another permanent collection of public meters.

### How Authority is gained

Authority changes should be attributable to a remembered choice or a specific loss of control. A successful cure is not automatically a permanent concession. A government can thank Wu, publish the result, and keep the same chain of authority.

The proposed concessions are deliberately uneven. Temporary priority over emergency resources gives 10 Authority because the state is allowing Wu to direct several services. Exclusive appointment rights or sole custody of an exceptional archive give 5 to 10. Establishing independent institute security or giving Wu sole control of a dependent military programme gives 15 to 20. Making temporary powers permanent has its own change rather than silently refreshing a temporary modifier.

The cabinet can also lose authority through a documented failure to enforce a decision. An ignored closure order matters when Wu possesses the resources to continue operating. A lost laboratory that was already outside practical supervision may create an authority crisis. Mere bad luck in a normal treatment project does not automatically advance his political standing.

A proposal always identifies the particular control being granted. The player should not pay political power for a vaguely stronger institute and later discover that the payment authorized prisoners, private guards, and secret experiments together.

### How Authority is reduced

Oversight works by removing a specific dependency or restoring a specific public function. Publishing a completed treatment removes exclusivity. Training independent staff reduces the need for Wu's personal approval. Dividing production among accountable institutions reduces the consequences of losing one director. Closing an unauthorized programme removes its resources and protects affected patients.

An audit can reveal a problem and support an enforcement decision. Repeating audits of the same compliant site does not endlessly lower Authority. Once the government has restored a particular control, the same action has no further political benefit until a new violation or concession changes that control again.

Authority reductions normally come in steps of 5 or 10. Removing an established independent security arrangement can produce a larger change, but it requires a real settlement, disarmament, or confrontation. A cheap administrative decision cannot erase an army's dependence on institute medicine.

## Permission and autonomy are different

Every advanced research family remains accessible under supervision. A low-Authority host can approve an unusual project, provide the required resources, and prohibit unapproved extensions. The approval authorizes that particular work, including its disclosed risks. It does not require granting Wu a higher stage of institutional independence.

High Authority can reduce the political friction of approving work and can make it easier for Wu to retain resources between projects. It must not become a hidden technology prerequisite. The safest route can be slower and more administratively demanding without losing access to an entire family of research.

Autonomous misconduct belongs to the appropriate evolved behaviour and the choices that enable it. A classified baseline programme may be secret from foreign countries while remaining honestly supervised by its own government. An explicit player-approved atrocity is still an atrocity even without an evolution. The difference is between what the state authorized and what Wu does beyond that authorization.

When the government changes its policy, already treated patients and dependent formations need a transition plan. An order to stop research can take effect immediately. An order to stop all continuing care cannot be treated as a harmless administrative cleanup when people rely on that care.

## Research direction

The institute has one qualitative research direction. Public Medicine prioritizes treatment access and independent clinical knowledge. National Protection prioritizes reserves, detection, hospitals, and protection of vulnerable transport corridors. Military Enhancement prioritizes selected military medical programmes. Controlled Biology prioritizes contained work with unusual living systems. Unrestricted Experimentation signals that the government is willing to consider the most dangerous proposals.

Direction determines which proposals rise to the top of the available list, which staff apply to work there, and which foreign partners are interested. It is not a second currency and does not gain experience through repeated decisions. The player can see the current direction in a short label beside the mandate.

Changing direction requires a 30-day reorganization and a proposed 50 political power. Work already under way can finish under its existing permission, pause with its progress retained, or be closed through the appropriate settlement. The player selects one of those treatments for an affected project rather than losing an entire portfolio during a direction change.

Personnel tension depends on the size of the shift. A hospital network moving toward national protection can retain most staff. A public institute ordered into coercive experimentation loses willing partners and needs replacement personnel before starting the restricted work. Wu may contest a reversal when he holds substantial authority, but a direction change alone does not produce a random coup.

## Sites and reach

The founding site uses an existing suitable civilian or military location in the host. It supplies the basic medical service and one research lane. It does not add a free ordinary factory, special-project building, resource deposit, or recruitable population.

Expansion separates three useful functions. A clinical centre increases the number of patients and states the institute can support. A research centre increases the complexity or concurrency of approved projects. A production centre manufactures treatment from completed knowledge. Giving one site all three functions is efficient, but it makes capture, blockade, sabotage, and dependence on Wu more consequential.

The player chooses the location for a major expansion from accessible states. The choice shows its medical relevance, distance from current outbreaks, vulnerability to the front, and connection to production or transport. The game does not assume that a capital is always safe or that a remote site is automatically supplied.

A small country can create one capable consolidated institute. A major power can distribute functions between sites. Neither arrangement is intrinsically superior. Consolidation reduces administrative costs and evacuation complexity. Distribution protects copied knowledge and provides alternative treatment routes, while requiring more staff and transport.

### Clinical network expansion

A clinical expansion trains local teams and equips hospitals in a selected region. The proposed first expansion takes 90 days, commits one civilian factory, and consumes 100 support equipment. Political approval is represented by a 25 political power cost. These are three spendable cost types.

The result expands supported coverage and strengthens ordinary emergency care in the selected area. It does not grant a universal cure or duplicate a hospital building supplied by another owner system. Where the owning system already has medical-capacity improvements, Wu contributes through that system's accepted medical result.

A public mandate completes this work more readily because independent hospitals are part of its structure. Military and classified hosts can purchase the same result. They must choose whether local staff receive enough knowledge to operate independently or remain dependent on visiting institute teams.

A frontline loss during construction stops further expenditure and preserves only the work that can plausibly be evacuated. Capturing a half-built extension does not grant the occupier Wu's personal medical ability. Restoring the site later uses the recorded unfinished work rather than charging for a completely new project by default.

### Research expansion

The initial research lane can handle one primary project at a time. A second lane requires an established founding staff, one completed major medical project, and an advanced research expansion. The proposed expansion takes 180 days, commits two civilian factories, consumes 250 support equipment, and costs 50 political power.

Two primary research projects are the ordinary concurrency ceiling in this stage of the design. Wu's uniqueness still matters because both teams require his direction at important stages. Trained staff handle routine work between those interventions. The country cannot buy a dozen nominal Wus by constructing additional buildings.

Emergency care continues while research is under way. A new universal cure project still needs a primary lane, so the host may have to pause an elective project. Pausing releases the future industrial commitment and retains established knowledge. It does not refund consumables already used or instantly convert incomplete work into a finished capability.

A third visible mission position is reserved for an actual emergency response, such as evacuating a threatened institute or delivering already completed treatment before a route closes. Ordinary industrial production is a standing commitment shown in the selected treatment's status rather than a separate timed mission for each shipment.

### Treatment production expansion

Production relies on actual industrial capacity assigned to a completed treatment standard. A research expansion does not grant free medicine production. A completed cure can be licensed to another country with industry, allowing a small host to remain the scientific origin while the partner supplies the bulk of the treatment.

Production sites record which treatment they are making and which country controls the output. A site can change its standard through a finite conversion process. Switching production does not refund completed output, erase old stocks, or make obsolete stocks compatible with a new variant without review.

The detailed production and delivery choices belong to the treatment-access specification. Their institutional consequence is straightforward: concentrating all production under Wu increases dependence, while accountable production partners preserve access after he leaves.

## Staff and knowledge custody

The institute begins with a small professional staff that can follow Wu's instructions and sustain the founding service. Research expansions require additional trained personnel. Specialist availability is a prerequisite or an explicit project commitment, not an unexplained reward of recruitable manpower.

Staff can be appointed through public institutions, military services, foreign cooperation, or a later compatible scientist event. The staffing route changes loyalty and outside scrutiny. Public staff are more likely to preserve clinical knowledge and report misconduct. Military staff prioritize their chain of command and assigned formations. Personal appointees are easier for Wu to transfer into private work when the political situation allows it.

A recorded treatment standard has three levels of institutional independence. A working method known only to Wu needs his presence for any substantial adaptation. A documented method can be manufactured and delivered by trained teams. A distributed standard can be produced by approved outside institutions that received the complete instructions. These are knowledge states for a particular result, not a general medical technology score.

The host can copy a finished treatment without acquiring Wu's ability to solve every future disease. Foreign theft can obtain a specific design or incomplete record. It cannot turn an ordinary operative into another universal physician.

Dangerous material has separate custody. A treatment record does not imply ownership of a viable specimen, and possessing a specimen does not imply understanding the finished treatment. This distinction supports both public destruction policies and strategic conflicts over archives without inventing a second sample economy that replaces the disease owner.

## Oversight decisions

### Independent review of an approved programme

The government orders a bounded review of one active or recently completed exceptional project. The proposed cost is 25 political power and 60 days of review. The project continues only within its approved terms during that period. An urgent treatment can receive a parallel review rather than being stopped solely to create a dramatic choice.

A compliant result confirms the current permission and establishes a review receipt. It can expose a particular weakness in documentation or control, but it does not repeatedly generate Authority reductions. A substantiated violation opens a specific enforcement decision. A false or inconclusive accusation leaves the player with the option to expand the review, publish its limits, or close the inquiry without pretending that innocence or guilt has been proved.

The information shown concerns the actual programme under review. Missing consumables, contradictory records, unauthorized staff access, or an undeclared transfer can be meaningful evidence. Generic suspicion is not enough to reveal every hidden activity in the institute.

The AI orders review when a project is dangerous, evidence has appeared, or the current authority settlement promises independent oversight. During an active emergency it prefers reviews that preserve functioning treatment rather than interrupting care without an alternative.

### Distribute a completed treatment

The state trains independent production or clinical teams using a finished treatment. The proposed programme takes 90 days, commits one civilian factory, and consumes 100 support equipment. It reduces dependence associated with that particular treatment and can lower Authority by 5 when Wu previously held exclusive control.

This action does not make the treatment universally public. The host can distribute it domestically while maintaining an external licence policy. A national network remains costly to maintain, and outside theft becomes easier once several institutions hold complete records.

The same standard cannot be distributed repeatedly for additional Authority reduction. A genuinely different adapted treatment or newly dependent region can create new work, but the reward follows the dependency actually removed.

### Revoke emergency powers

When the qualifying emergency is contained, the cabinet can restore the previous institutional settlement. A 30-day transition preserves treatment distribution and returns appointments, transport priority, and spending authority to their former owners. Temporary powers expire through this visible process rather than silently remaining permanent because the crisis flag disappeared.

The government can instead ratify a specific power that proved useful. That creates a new lasting concession and its Authority change. A new emergency may justify extending the existing arrangement, but routine protection does not count as an endless emergency.

If Wu lacks the means to resist, revocation succeeds. If an applicable evolved political confrontation exists and he has a real power base, the decision leads to that confrontation's negotiation or enforcement path. The basic oversight action itself does not invent an evolved crisis while the relevant evolution is disabled.

### Destroy a retained archive

The government can dispose of a particular dangerous archive while preserving treatment records and continued clinical care. Destruction removes that material as a prerequisite for future work. It lowers the associated accident exposure and can reduce Authority when exclusive custody was a meaningful source of leverage.

Disposal is an in-world administrative outcome with a visible completion record. The specification does not describe real biological destruction methods. If the applicable autonomous-research behaviour is active, evidence can later reveal an undeclared copy. That possibility must have an actual event cause and cannot silently invalidate every destruction decision.

An AI public institute prefers disposal when the material no longer serves a defensible research need. A classified host is more willing to retain it, while a country losing control of its last secure site weighs disposal more heavily than the nominal value of future research.

## Supply interruption

A disrupted institute degrades by function. Existing hospital instructions and distributed treatment remain available. New research slows or pauses. Production stops when committed industrial inputs are unavailable. Personal adaptation requires Wu and a working team. A missing transport route prevents delivery to that target rather than erasing medicine already present elsewhere.

The category identifies the immediate cause of interruption: lost site, missing treatment input, unavailable personnel, occupied production region, or blocked delivery route. It should not display a single unexplained inactive state.

The player can narrow the programme, replace the missing input through a valid partner, evacuate staff, or preserve work until the situation improves. A lost resource does not automatically select the most dangerous possible failure. Risk rises when the player keeps an unsafe project operating after its support requirements fail.

## Capture, evacuation, and closure

Wu's physical location and the main archive are known to the host unless an applicable secrecy branch establishes otherwise. When the main site becomes threatened, the host can evacuate him, secure treatment records, move an approved part of the archive, or destroy dangerous material. These choices may compete for the same transport and time.

The event follows actual territorial change. A captured research centre can yield staff, records, equipment, or incomplete work. The occupier receives only what was present and not successfully removed. Owning the state does not automatically secure Wu if he was evacuated or working elsewhere.

Closure has three distinct outcomes. Research suspension halts new experimental work while maintaining care. Institutional dissolution distributes or archives the surviving medical knowledge and removes the unique national organization. Expulsion removes Wu from his appointment and starts a relocation opportunity. The host chooses among them according to what it is trying to end.

Already dependent patients and formations remain a responsibility. A closure settlement can transfer continuing care to independent staff or a foreign partner. Abandoning them produces the relevant medical and political consequences. Those consequences are recorded by the owning population, unit, and evidence systems rather than erased when the institute's national effect is removed.

## Ownership after Wu leaves

The former host retains its buildings, equipment, distributed treatment records, trained domestic staff, and completed local infrastructure. It can continue documented treatment and some well-established work. Its unfinished universal adaptations lose Wu's exceptional problem-solving contribution and may require a successor or a new agreement to finish.

The new host receives Wu and whatever personal knowledge, records, or material actually travelled with him. It does not receive the former host's factories, treatment stockpile, deployed units, foreign contracts, or every national project. The new appointment therefore creates a powerful opportunity without duplicating an entire country's investment.

Independent hospitals abroad retain legitimately shared methods. Patients do not become untreated merely because the legal host changed. A licence agreement may be disputed or revoked according to its terms, but already distributed knowledge cannot be physically recalled through a diplomatic button.

The distinction between person, institution, and result should be visible whenever control changes. It is central to both the humanitarian route and the political danger of giving one person exclusive control of medicine.
