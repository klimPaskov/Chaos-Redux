## The doctor and the opportunity

Doctor Wu arrives with a result that the country's physicians can examine. Patients expected to die are recovering, the immediate chain of infection has been interrupted, and the treatment remains effective when ordinary staff repeat the parts he has disclosed. His education, nationality, previous employment, and age remain unconfirmed. Documents may disagree about his past, but resolving those discrepancies never becomes a prerequisite for using the institute.

The player gains someone who can solve biological emergencies that would otherwise dominate a campaign. Keeping that ability available becomes a continuing strategic choice. Laboratories compete with other construction, emergency production competes with equipment manufacture, and distributing knowledge reduces the leverage of both the host and the doctor. During a quiet period, Wu remains valuable through preventive medicine, treatment reserves, field hospitals, and research that the government can actually direct.

Wu initially speaks and behaves like a competent physician. He explains what he can achieve, what he requires, and what remains uncertain about delivery. His evasiveness concerns authority, his methods, and the use of results. It should not make his first medical success look fraudulent. A supervised humanitarian career is a complete and powerful way to play the event. Dangerous work grows from permissions, material, political pressure, and choices made after that first success.

## Selecting a host

The arrival draws from the deduplicated union of valid player-controlled countries and valid current major powers. The proposed default is an equal draw between distinct eligible countries. Being both a player and a major does not provide a second entry. The event therefore often reaches an important country without making a human recipient inevitable.

Eligibility requires a functioning ordinary civilian population and at least one accessible location where a small medical institution can operate. A poor player country qualifies when it has those foundations. It does not need a large industrial base, an intelligence agency, a special-project facility, or existing biological technology. Wu brings the expertise for a small institute, rather than a free industrial complex capable of every later experiment.

An actual nonhuman country, a pure outbreak identity, or a terminal actor that cannot support ordinary hospitals is ineligible. A human country using unusual soldiers is not excluded merely because those soldiers are strange. Puppets and countries at war remain eligible when the basic medical and territorial conditions hold. A country with no viable controlled civilian location waits for a later opportunity to host Wu through diplomacy or relocation.

A suitable arrival location is chosen from the selected country's current territory. An accessible outbreak state has priority when a real outbreak is present. Otherwise, the location should plausibly contain a civilian hospital, military hospital, university clinic, or emergency treatment station. A highly exposed frontline location is a poor default when a safer location can receive the patients. The arrival does not conjure a protected enclave in enemy territory.

The appearance is one world incident. The original arrival country remains the historical first contact even if Wu later serves elsewhere. Current residence, legal appointment, physical location, and access to his personal work can subsequently change without producing a second Doctor Wu opening.

## The first proof

### A country with an active outbreak

Wu treats a real clinical group associated with an active, medically compatible outbreak. The disease owner identifies a living population that treatment can still help. The initial intervention stabilizes that group and provides a valid medical lead for the wider cure programme. It does not silently resolve every infected state, disband hostile formations, or convert an unfinished global treatment into a completed cure.

The government sees three immediate results. The treated patients are improving, ordinary physicians have usable emergency instructions, and Wu has identified a route toward a full treatment. The arrival report also names the remaining practical problem. It might be obtaining access to another infected region, expanding treatment production, or reaching isolated hospitals. This gives the first decision category an urgent purpose without making the miracle an instant national reset.

The intervention itself is reliable. Randomly killing the demonstration patients would undermine the user's premise that Wu proves his ability. Later projects can fail through incomplete information, mutation, sabotage, overextension, or deliberate misuse. His first proof establishes the capacity that makes those later decisions worth considering.

### A country without an active outbreak

The opening uses a contained hospital case. Several patients suffer from a severe biological illness or an unusual complication, and Wu succeeds where local medicine has failed. The case can be described in clinical rather than agent-specific terms. It creates a demonstration receipt and an initial medical research lead, but it does not register a spreading national epidemic.

The contained case supplies no usable offensive specimen by default. Establishing a dangerous archive requires a later explicit decision and a compatible source. The government cannot obtain both a fabricated emergency and a reward for ending that same emergency. Demonstration patients do not create a new death total or an artificial outbreak-reversal Chaos credit.

A civilian hospital, convalescent military ward, or remote public clinic changes the viewpoint of the report. The underlying reward is equivalent. This prevents countries at peace from receiving a weaker Wu while allowing wartime arrivals to feel relevant to the campaign.

### A mixed crisis

If the host faces famine, displacement, contamination, or a supernatural disaster, Wu can demonstrate success on a biological complication that the crisis owner recognizes. He might stabilize an infection among displaced civilians or preserve patients while relief arrives. The first report keeps the remaining nonmedical crisis visible. Successful treatment does not manufacture food, reopen a blocked route, remove radioactive contamination, or invalidate the supernatural actor responsible for another effect.

## The appointment decision

The host receives four substantially different responses. All appointment routes grant the same unique doctor and access to the baseline research framework. The mandate changes institutional priorities, public visibility, authority, and costs. It does not permanently remove a research family.

The appointment grants a working founding institute without an up-front affordability trap. The first expansion, standing industrial commitments, and advanced experiments require resources. A country unable to finance a major laboratory can retain Wu as an exceptional physician and seek outside support.

The opening should explain the defensive benefit, the initial authority settlement, the first research priority, and the public or classified nature of the appointment. It should not list the entire eventual technology portfolio. The first follow-up makes the initial opportunity usable before introducing foreign bidding, multiple project families, or several simultaneous crises.

### Establish a public medical institute

The country gives Wu a public appointment through civilian hospitals and research institutions. His methods are examined by other physicians, treatment distribution remains a government responsibility, and the initial archive follows a destroy-or-review policy for dangerous material.

This mandate provides the strongest starting broad medical protection and the cheapest route to distributing completed treatments. It begins at 10 Authority on the proposed 0 to 100 scale. Independent review is established, staff appointments are shared, and the government retains the right to inspect facilities. Wu can recommend projects and manage approved work, but exceptional research requires explicit approval.

The host's first priority is either the active cure or a preventive medical project chosen from the current situation. A country with no outbreak can prepare emergency treatment reserves, train hospital staff, or improve recovery of infected personnel. Foreign governments can learn that a useful institute exists, while incomplete methods and patient records remain protected.

Public medicine is not a weak introductory route that must be abandoned to reach interesting content. It supports advanced tissue replacement, safe replication, compatible nonhuman medicine, and other approved research through supervised prerequisites. Crossing into military or coercive work requires a visible political choice. That choice does not retroactively make the original public appointment fraudulent.

The principal vulnerability is institutional openness. Staff and manufacturing partners create more opportunities for imitation and foreign pressure. Shared knowledge also makes the national health service more resilient when Wu leaves. The player exchanges secrecy and personal exclusivity for a stronger independent medical system.

### Place him under military medical command

Wu is appointed through the armed forces and existing civilian care continues under a military emergency partnership. The route begins at 30 Authority. Military hospitals, evacuation services, and protected research sites cooperate with him, but ordinary soldiers are not automatically enrolled in extreme experiments.

This mandate gives balanced outbreak protection and the fastest initial preparation for battlefield medicine. The first nonemergency proposal should concern casualty care, infected personnel, or treatment supply for selected formations. The route can later reach enhancement, controlled fictional organisms, and military replication through the same baseline prerequisites as other mandates.

Military command has a genuine opportunity cost. Committing medical staff and industrial capacity to a selected army group leaves less surge capacity for civilian deployment. A severe civilian outbreak can force the player to choose between maintaining a planned offensive's medical support and reallocating treatment production. The basic public emergency service does not disappear merely because the army owns the institute.

Command relationships remain legible. The armed forces control deployment, the institute certifies medical support, and the cabinet approves exceptional programmes. Giving Wu independent authority over formations is a later concession. Military appointment alone does not transfer the army's loyalty to him.

### Give him a classified biological directorate

Wu receives a secret appointment, a restricted archive, and a protected founding laboratory. This mandate begins at 45 Authority. The government permits research into strategically dangerous fictional applications, subject to the specific permissions attached to later projects.

The directorate receives an advantage in preparing classified proposals and protecting rare material from casual foreign access. Its public treatment network begins less developed than the public route, although Wu still provides major outbreak defence and can develop every compatible cure. The first proposal can preserve a real dangerous specimen or organize a classified research facility when valid material exists. Without that material, the institute must first perform useful medical work or obtain legitimate access through another event.

Classified appointment is not consent to every later atrocity. Coercive trials, secret manipulation of treatment recipients, deliberate release, and independent armed security remain distinct decisions or evolved misconduct. The player sees the risk category and the institutional concession associated with an approval. Wu cannot acquire an unrestricted programme simply because the option used the word classified.

Secrecy makes it harder to share finished treatment and to distribute professional knowledge. It also increases the cost of exposure. A hidden archive discovered after an accident becomes evidence of retained material and failed oversight. Exposure concerns what actually occurred, not an automatic accusation of weapon use merely because the government created a laboratory.

### Restrict or expel him

The country keeps the emergency instructions that ordinary staff already observed, but receives no permanent Wu institution, universal adaptation capability, or advanced project access. The copied benefit is a single host-local medical legacy rather than a second version of the doctor.

The proposed legacy lasts 180 days and modestly assists the demonstrated illness or generic emergency care when the case was contained. It cannot be upgraded through Wu decisions after he departs. A later appointment replaces its overlapping benefit rather than stacking two full protections. This makes refusal survivable without making it as rewarding as permanent recruitment.

Wu is referred to an untried eligible country. The rejecting government cannot choose the recipient as a free diplomatic gift unless a later foreign-service agreement explicitly provides that choice. The doctor does not automatically take the host's public hospital network, industrial investment, or state-owned archive with him.

After a new host accepts, the rejecting country receives one consequence report based on actual public knowledge. A public appointment abroad can name the new host. A secret appointment produces an intelligence lead rather than omniscient confirmation. The report changes the country's information and possible diplomatic options, not the already resolved opening reward.

## Rejection, referral, and underground practice

The proposed referral interval is 10 days. Each country can be offered the opening once during this referral circuit. A change in human control or major status does not reset its refusal history. Countries newly becoming eligible can enter a subsequent discovery opportunity rather than causing the circuit to spin indefinitely.

After the original candidate pool is exhausted, Wu continues working through a small underground medical network. He is unavailable as a national institution, but he has not been killed or removed from the setting. The first suitable later outbreak, a credible request for treatment, or an intelligence discovery can expose his location. A new offer requires a real changed circumstance, such as an emergency, a public guarantee, a new sponsor, or the passage of a meaningful period.

Discovery creates an approach, not ownership. An intelligence service that finds him can offer protection, open negotiations, or warn its government. It cannot instantly transfer his medical capacity to the discovering country. A host that expelled him must offer a credible new settlement. Repeatedly hiring and dismissing him cannot recreate founding resources or manufacture fresh demonstration rewards.

An underground Wu still needs people and facilities. His network may provide a clinical lead or emergency intervention, but it cannot operate a fully industrialized global cure service without a supporting country. The system preserves future play without giving the world an invisible, costless second institute.

## The founding settlement

Within the first month after appointment, one follow-up establishes how the initial staff work with Wu. It appears after the player has seen the institute's current emergency or first preventive proposal. The follow-up is a baseline institutional decision and does not consume an evolution.

The public route's tension concerns clinical publication and staff training. The military route's tension concerns who can redirect treatment supplies. The classified route's tension concerns who controls the archive and which records remain inspectable. The same three institutional responses can resolve those tensions without creating separate miniature event chains for every host country.

A distributed settlement pays a modest political cost, trains independent staff, and lowers Authority by 5. It slightly delays the first expansion because local institutions are learning the methods themselves. An executive settlement gives Wu a single clear reporting line and raises Authority by 5, with faster preparation for the first approved project. An emergency settlement allows temporary priority access to resources for a current crisis, raises Authority by 10, and schedules a visible review when that crisis is contained.

The emergency response is unavailable when there is no qualifying emergency. It cannot serve as a cheap peacetime capacity buff. Revoking emergency powers does not remove completed treatment knowledge, while making them permanent requires another explicit institutional concession. The player should be able to remember the decision that gave Wu control over a resource later used in a confrontation.

The settlement report presents specific staff concerns and the practical consequences of the choice. Professional disagreement can carry dry humour. Patients, displaced people, and experimental victims remain human subjects with consequences rather than scenery for a joke about an eccentric scientist.

## Immediate protection

The founding institution begins functioning as soon as an appointment is accepted. It provides detection, emergency coordination, and treatment organization before the first advanced research project completes. A severe outbreak should become more manageable at once, even when eliminating it still requires work.

The proposed starting medical package uses three separate owner-recognized effects: reduced domestic outbreak escalation, reduced biological spread pressure from treated territory, and reduced prospective biological civilian losses in supported populations. The public route is strongest across all three. Military command is somewhat weaker for broad civilian coverage and stronger at supporting military medicine. The classified route trades some reach for protected research and archive control.

These are game-design effects that compatible disease systems must expose. They are not assertions that vanilla HOI4 provides universal disease modifiers. Exact starting coefficients and overlap rules are consolidated in the cure specification so an institution, a cure, and emergency aid do not accidentally apply the same protection three times.

Wu does not grant generic immunity to every accident. Ordinary stockpile protection continues to respect the existing stockpile system. A laboratory running an extreme project has its own failure conditions, and treatment does not make a deliberate hostile release harmless. The defensive package improves the chance of containing an incident and surviving disease, while the dangerous project remains a real political decision.

## A host that cannot afford expansion

A small state retains the basic institution and the ability to pursue a costly but possible cure. It can narrow treatment to the most threatened living population, seek manufacturing partners, request samples, or share the treatment in exchange for foreign production. It can also offer Wu a temporary protected appointment abroad while negotiating access to the resulting medicine.

The small host should not receive artificial free factories or unlimited supplies to imitate a major power. Its advantage is unique knowledge. A cure that several industrial countries need gives it bargaining power even before it can supply those countries itself. Conversely, accepting foreign industrial support creates a dependency that the host can see and negotiate.

Weak industry is therefore an obstacle to scale, not a reason to exclude a player from the event. Severe territorial collapse can remove the last viable site and force an evacuation choice. It does not retroactively invalidate the country as the legitimate first host.

## Opening AI priorities

Every host AI prioritizes an active biological emergency over elective research. A democratic country at peace normally prefers the public institute, while a militarized country in a difficult war gives greater weight to military medicine. Authoritarian governments can prefer classification when they possess secure facilities and the resources to sustain them. A centralized government can still choose universal public care rather than automatically treating secrecy as its ideological default.

A weak host gives extra weight to cooperative production and controlled public medicine. It should not select a secret programme solely because the projected reward is powerful when failure would destroy its only viable laboratory. Refusal remains possible when Wu's proposed position is politically unacceptable or the country cannot protect him, but an ongoing deadly outbreak makes a workable appointment overwhelmingly more attractive.

Opening AI does not predict future evolution rolls or secret project results. It evaluates visible threats, industry, territorial stability, existing scientist relationships, and current authority settlements. Named probability scenarios and normalized weights are a later audit requirement. The priorities here establish intended behaviour rather than claiming tested probabilities.

## What the player sees after appointment

The Doctor Wu decision category shows his portrait, mandate, Authority stage, current research direction, and one urgent problem. A healthy country sees a preventive opportunity rather than an empty disease browser. An infected country sees the current treatment objective and the principal barrier to wider relief.

The first actionable set contains the active cure or first medical proposal, one institute-management action, and one relevant diplomatic or oversight action. Navigation into a selected subject does not expose every future project simultaneously. The institution's full research potential becomes apparent through purposeful proposals as prerequisites are met.

Arrival art should show the result of Wu's intervention through a credible mid-century medical setting. His portrait is a distinct fictional identity with no invented national insignia or caricature. The institution icon, small category icon, and category picture have separate compositions for their actual consumers. These are authored asset requirements, not produced or approved assets.

The opening ends with a clear playable position: the host has accepted a particular relationship with an exceptional physician, gained immediate medical protection, and received a meaningful next decision. Refusal ends with a real loss of access and a living doctor who can matter elsewhere.
