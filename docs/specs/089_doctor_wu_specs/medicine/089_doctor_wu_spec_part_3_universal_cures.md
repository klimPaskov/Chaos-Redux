# Doctor Wu: universal cures and outbreak response

## The medical promise

Doctor Wu can develop a treatment for every valid registered biological disease. The promise includes conventional illnesses, fictional biological agents, the zombie infection, the Black Plague, temporal diseases, and compatible diseases added by later events. A disease can be difficult to reach, expensive to understand, or costly to treat at scale. It cannot be assigned a permanent medical immunity to Wu solely to preserve its status as a dramatic threat.

The promise concerns biological treatment. A cure does not undo a war, restore the dead, abolish a government, feed a starving population, or dissolve an independent nonhuman society. Disease owners retain those consequences and their other mechanics. Medicine changes the part of the crisis that depends on living infection or another explicitly treatable biological condition.

The universal system also preserves ordinary defensive institutions. Countries without Wu can still use their existing treatments and countermeasures. Wu supplies the exceptional route when an illness has defeated those methods, and his institute provides unusually strong preparation and deployment. Appointing him does not delete ordinary hospitals or make every other scientist incapable of medicine.

## A shared medical description

A disease enters Wu's system through a medical description supplied by its owner. The description identifies the illness, the current treatment-relevant version, the living populations or formations that can benefit, and the result that counts as meaningful treatment. It also distinguishes outbreak pressure from any army, country, supernatural effect, or historical record that treatment cannot remove.

The minimum description contains a stable illness identity, a treatment-version identity, a valid clinical or specimen lead, a current active footprint, and a compatible treatment result. It identifies who owns prospective losses, spread, recovery, and any permitted countermeasure effect. These are proposed interoperability requirements for the game systems, not a claim that every required interface already exists in the repository.

A registered disease cannot advertise universal medical compatibility and then omit every possible treatment result. That would contradict the event's promise. A mixed crisis can register only its biological component, leaving the rest outside the cure. A purely nonbiological crisis remains with its owner and can still create separate biological complications that qualify.

Future disease content should therefore enter the existing browser and cure sequence through its description rather than requiring a newly hardcoded Wu decision for every agent. The presentation can use a disease-specific icon and concise explanation when those assets exist. It must not invent clinical information or suggest a completed cure merely because an event name appears in a catalogue.

## What starts a cure project

A cure project requires a valid lead. The host can obtain one from patients it can access, a legitimate foreign medical exchange, a captured archive recognized by the disease owner, or compatible material supplied by another event. A claim about a distant outbreak is enough to request access, but it is not automatically equivalent to possessing a usable specimen or complete clinical record.

The opening intervention supplies a lead for the demonstrated disease. A healthy opening supplies the contained medical case described in the arrival specification. It does not provide samples for every later project family.

A foreign outbreak can appear as a request for cooperation before the host has access to material. The player sees the barrier and the available political route. A denied request leaves alternatives such as another affected partner, a neutral medical mission, or a later captured site. The institute does not solve a completely inaccessible illness through an invented sample.

Once a valid lead and a viable institute are available, the project has an eventual treatment route. Its design must not require an endlessly rerolled unique success, a permanently missing random item, or a Chaos threshold. External political and logistical barriers can remain difficult, but the disease itself is within Wu's ability.

## The cure sequence

### Establish the clinical problem

The institute confirms which registered illness and treatment version are involved, which living groups are eligible for care, and what the immediate threat is. The host can begin emergency support while this work continues. That support uses the founding institution's protective effects rather than claiming to be the final cure.

The government sees a meaningful clinical objective: stabilize an infected population, restore a treatable military formation, prevent further living infection, or suppress a biological support network. The objective names the relevant state or target group. It does not promise to destroy an independent political actor as a side effect of treatment.

This stage distinguishes a confirmed match from an uncertain lead. An intelligence report that turns out to concern a nonbiological phenomenon produces an information result and closes the medical lead. It does not consume the cost of a full cure or create an arbitrary failure epidemic.

### Develop a workable treatment

Wu and the assigned research team develop a treatment for the confirmed version. The proposed research bands are 45 days for a familiar supported illness, 90 days for a severe or unfamiliar illness, and 150 days for an exceptional fictional condition. These are initial game-balance anchors, not biological claims or validated engine timings.

The proposed starting resource packages use the same three cost types as the wider research portfolio. A familiar illness commits one civilian factory and consumes 50 support equipment and 25 political power. A severe illness commits two factories and consumes 150 support equipment and 50 political power. An exceptional illness commits three factories and consumes 300 support equipment and 75 political power. Research duration follows the cure bands above, rather than the elective-project durations in Part 5.

Each project consumes a primary research lane. Industrial commitment and support-equipment consumption scale with the severity band and available medical infrastructure. The normal project uses three spendable cost types: political power for mobilization, committed civilian industry, and support equipment. Clinical access and a working team are prerequisites rather than additional unlisted currencies.

A larger outbreak does not necessarily make the underlying discovery proportionally slower. It creates more urgent need and a greater production burden. A rare inaccessible treatment version can make research harder even when few people are infected. Keeping research difficulty separate from delivery scale gives both a small host and a major industrial partner useful roles.

A public institute receives its preparation advantage through independent staff and clinical cooperation. A military institute benefits when the cases concern supported formations. A classified directorate benefits from secure material already in its possession. These advantages reduce suitable costs or delay. They do not improve every disease equally and do not change whether the illness is curable.

### Establish a reliable standard

A workable treatment becomes a reliable production standard after the institute has enough evidence to identify its valid use and limitations. The government can fund the normal completion process or authorize an emergency provisional deployment when the current crisis justifies the risk.

Provisional deployment can save time during a deteriorating outbreak, but the visible description states the uncertainty. It may provide incomplete protection or require follow-up care. It is not presented as a finished universal cure while secretly behaving like one of several unrelated random outcomes.

A reliable standard records the version it treats, the populations it can help, the production requirement, and the continuing care or protection it provides. Its medical effect is real. A later political monopoly, manufacturing shortage, or hostile border can stop people receiving it without making the discovery itself fraudulent.

### Manufacture and reach the intended population

Completion opens the production and delivery choices described in Part 4. Research does not fill every depot, create a convoy, or apply treatment to every allied state. The host chooses how much output to prepare and who receives the first allocation.

Treatment can be held in reserve, delivered domestically, sent to a partner, or used for a specific military medical programme. Stocks and delivery commitments follow their current location and owner. A finished standard can be copied, while a physical stock cannot be spent twice.

The disease owner applies the result to the actual living population or supported formation reached by treatment. Cure knowledge remains available after the first deployment. Subsequent production pays normal costs and does not recreate the discovery reward.

## Progress after setbacks

Verified clinical knowledge survives a delay. A failed provisional deployment may reveal an incomplete match, inadequate delivery, or an unrecognized change in the illness. The institute keeps the parts of its work that remain valid and receives a concrete next step.

The player can fund a slower conservative completion, seek another valid case, redirect a project lane, or request additional clinical cooperation. The conservative route has a defined completion path once its requirements are met. It cannot keep failing indefinitely through an unchanged random roll.

Different problems consume different resources. A transport failure wastes or delays a shipment but does not erase the treatment design. A corrupted record requires a documented copy or reconstruction effort. A new treatment-relevant version requires adaptation. Losing Wu can remove the institute's ability to complete an exceptional adaptation even when ordinary production continues.

A persistent project should therefore show what has been learned and what remains to be done. A percentage bar can summarize work, but the current barrier must explain the next decision. A project at high completion cannot become an opaque permanent waiting state because the player cannot infer which hidden flag is missing.

## Mutation and treatment versions

A disease owner can introduce a new treatment-relevant version when its actual event changes the illness. The institute evaluates whether the current treatment still matches, works partially, or requires a revised standard. The change is based on the owner's stated medical consequence rather than the word evolution alone.

Wu can adapt the treatment through ordinary baseline research. The proposed adaptation duration begins at 45 days when a valid new case is available and the previous standard remains useful. An exceptional change can require more work, but completed knowledge reduces duplicated effort wherever it genuinely applies.

An older stock remains an identifiable older stock. It may still treat unaffected cases or provide the partial benefit specified by the owner. It can sometimes be converted to the new standard through an additional production commitment. It cannot become fully compatible merely because the player selected a new research direction.

The player receives notice when a planned deployment no longer matches its target. Existing care can continue where it remains useful. A treatment revision should not secretly turn every previously protected population into an unannounced epidemic on the same day.

The disease can remain dangerous without making medicine futile. After a valid adaptation, there must be a practical opportunity to manufacture and use it. A scripted chain that repeatedly invalidates the treatment before any possible deployment would violate the cure promise and require amendment in the disease owner's design.

## Coverage and simultaneous illnesses

Coverage concerns the eligible living population that actually receives support. A country cannot select a lightly populated state and receive the same national benefit as treating every infected person in a continental empire. The disease owner supplies the affected population or a defensible coverage measure, and the deployment uses that measure consistently.

The proposed presentation uses coverage bands rather than demanding patient-level administration: local clinical group, affected state, national priority network, and international programme. Each band still has real material and route requirements. The underlying calculation must distinguish treated from untreated people rather than applying a national full-cure flag after one shipment.

Two illnesses affecting the same state remain separate medical problems. General hospital support can help both, while a disease-specific treatment addresses only its registered match. Shared logistical support is not charged twice when the same functioning hospital serves both programmes. Specific treatment output remains separate when different standards are required.

The institute can triage simultaneous emergencies. It can continue passive care for all supported populations while assigning its limited research lanes to the most urgent unfinished cures. A less urgent project can be paused without losing verified work. The category shows the selected emergency and the next competing priority rather than filling the screen with a separate compulsory mission for every illness.

## Starting medical strength and overlap

The founding institute's proposed game-balance factors are substantial. They apply only where a compatible owner exposes the relevant biological outcome and the host has functioning medical support.

| Mandate | Domestic escalation risk | Biological spread pressure from supported areas | Prospective biological civilian losses in supported populations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public institute | ×0.50 | ×0.60 | ×0.50 |
| Military medical command | ×0.60 | ×0.65 | ×0.60 |
| Classified directorate | ×0.65 | ×0.70 | ×0.60 |

These factors are proposals for gameplay, not measured medical effectiveness. The public route has the strongest broad coverage. Military medicine adds its own formation-oriented benefits through the relevant military programmes. Classification supports secure research and custody rather than pretending to be the best public health service as well.

The same Wu protection channel does not multiply against itself. For a given target and outcome, the strongest applicable Wu measure replaces weaker overlapping Wu measures. A disease-specific cure can remove eligible active infection from a fully treated group. It should not also report that same removal as a second reduction from the institute's passive protection.

The remaining untreated group can still receive ordinary passive support. Other providers combine their effects according to their owner's rules. This preserves shared system ownership and avoids inventing a hidden stack of three Wu modifiers that produces near-perfect protection before a cure is developed.

Completed treatment can remove active biological disease in an eligible living group. Protection against future exposure, duration of that protection, and any maintenance requirement remain explicit parts of the disease's treatment result. The cure does not imply permanent immunity to every future version or unrelated disease.

## The zombie infection

### Living patients

A living person infected before complete death and reanimation is a valid medical target when the zombie owner exposes that state. Wu can stabilize patients, prevent their progression, and establish a treatment for the current infection. Treated survivors remain members of the living population.

The owner records the reduction in active infection and any legitimate return to ordinary civilian or military activity. Wu does not separately add replacement manpower for every treated person if the owner already restores that person through its recovery mechanism. The same survivor cannot be counted as both a recovered civilian and a newly created soldier.

### Outbreak territory

Treatment can reduce new infections and the biological recruitment that sustains a horde. A protected port, reception centre, or populated corridor can become strategically important because the infection can no longer use it as an easy route into living communities.

When full biological containment is achieved, the zombie country and its existing armies remain on the map. They may become isolated, lose recruitment opportunities, or suffer owner-defined sustainment pressure. The military situation can improve dramatically while still requiring combat, containment, or another outcome defined by the zombie event.

A hospital action never resurrects dead zombie battalions as their original human soldiers. Work involving reanimated material belongs to a separate baseline research programme with different permissions and consequences. A cure for living infection is not a free unlock for obedient engineered units.

### League cooperation and new versions

The Anti-Zombie League can request treatment, production licences, and medical teams. A league member with industry can manufacture an approved standard while Wu continues adapting it. Sharing a treatment does not automatically make every league member capable of all Wu research.

A zombie mutation that changes the medical match triggers an adaptation request. The old treatment's remaining value is stated clearly. The player can prioritize the new cure while keeping existing protection where it still works. The horde's response to the institute belongs to its own event and the foreign-competition design, rather than a universal random attack attached to every completed cure.

## The Black Plague

### Treating human infection

Wu can cure eligible infected humans, reduce biological plague pressure in treated states, and protect vulnerable ports, crowded cities, refugee reception points, and occupied routes. The treatment addresses living patients and medically compatible disease effects supplied by the Black Plague owner.

Successful treatment can break the link between a plague network and a region's continuing human infection. This makes quarantine, relief, military containment, and negotiation more effective. It does not erase the historical suffering or the evidence left by deliberate use of disease.

### Rat Nations and the Rat King

An organized Rat Nation is an independent actor. Human treatment can weaken the plague network that supports its recruitment, influence, or expansion where the owner exposes such a connection. The actor retains its territory, government, and existing armed forces until its own mechanics change them.

A Rat King's human servant or captive can be a medical patient. Treating that person does not necessarily remove political loyalty, coercion, or supernatural control. Those effects need their own owner-approved resolution. The cure should identify which limitation remains rather than claiming that all forms of service to the Rat King are symptoms of disease.

Wu can research compatible protective ecology or unusual anti-rat projects when genuine source material and permissions exist. Those are distinct research projects. Completing the ordinary plague cure does not automatically grant a new organism, biological weapon, or military unit.

A compromised institute can create a treatment mismatch or an archive emergency. The player receives relevant evidence and recovery choices. The Rat King does not receive automatic omniscience about every hidden laboratory merely because the first human patient was cured.

## Other registered biological crises

Temporal illness can require knowledge or material supplied by the Time Traveler owner, while the actual cure remains part of Wu's baseline progression. Future knowledge can shorten a particular project, confirm a treatment lead, or warn about a later limitation. It does not grant unrelated technology or make the Time Traveler event fire retroactively.

Famine and migration can expose disease in vulnerable populations. Wu supplies medical care, screening, and treatment that reduce the biological burden. Food production, reception capacity, movement, employment, transport, and population ledgers remain with their systems. A treated displaced person still needs a place to live and food to eat.

A mixed plague can register a biological component such as treatable infection. Hail, darkness, divine punishment, chemical injury sources, radioactive contamination, and political fanaticism are not made curable by classifying all harm as disease. Wu can support an injured population without claiming to have removed the cause of its injuries.

A nonhuman or alien formation can receive compatible medicine when its provider exposes a treatable biological condition. The provider decides which survivors, units, and supply requirements are affected. A medical project does not grant ownership of the formation or bypass the rules that allowed it to exist.

## Failure categories and recovery

A treatment may initially match only one part of an outbreak. The recovery is to identify and treat the remaining valid version, while retaining the successful work. A provisional treatment may require follow-up care. The recovery is an explicit clinical programme, not a hidden permanent debuff that the player could never anticipate.

A lost shipment creates a delivery shortage. The recovery is another route, a new allocation, or local manufacture. An institute accident creates the relevant owner-recognized incident and an immediate containment choice. It does not automatically invalidate unrelated cures stored safely elsewhere.

A deliberate unapproved change to a treatment is a political and medical incident with evidence, a compatible treatment correction, and a responsible actor. Its occurrence depends on the relevant approved or evolved behaviour. Routine public medicine should not randomly acquire sinister hidden effects simply to ensure that the doctor is always dangerous.

Deaths that genuinely occur enter the shared Deaths system once. Recovery changes future risk and current living conditions. Neither a later cure nor a public apology subtracts those deaths from history.

## Genuine containment and Chaos

A cure earns a containment consequence when it materially reverses a real active crisis. Developing a treatment in advance, copying a treatment, producing another batch, or rehearsing an emergency procedure does not repeatedly reduce Chaos.

The owner identifies the outbreak episode being contained. Local relief, national containment, and international containment can be milestones within that same episode rather than three unrelated incidents that repeatedly reward the same recovery. Later rewards account for what has already been credited.

Wu's own opening demonstration is excluded from outbreak-reversal rewards. Deliberately creating a disease and then treating it cannot be used as a reliable net-negative Chaos loop. Medical relief still helps the affected population, while responsibility, deaths, evidence, and the original escalation remain recorded.

Treatment can reduce a biological footprint contributing to Air Cleanliness pressure when the owner reports that footprint has actually fallen. It does not directly clear nuclear or chemical contamination. The resulting shared-system change and a Wu narrative milestone cannot both claim the same underlying reduction as separate physical cleanups.

## The completed cure record

A completed cure remains a useful, readable result. The record names the illness and valid treatment version, its clinical limits, where production is available, who has received the standard, and whether current stock can reach the selected target. It distinguishes research complete, medicine available, and patients reached.

The player can return to an older cure when a new outbreak occurs. The game checks its current match and the remaining supply route. This creates a long-lived medical portfolio without turning the decision category into an indiscriminate list of every disease ever registered.

Wu's universal ability is therefore visible in play through repeated successful adaptation and meaningful recovery. Industrial, political, and territorial decisions determine how far those successes reach.
