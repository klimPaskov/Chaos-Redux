# Doctor Wu: treatment production, delivery, and access

## A discovery that other countries need

A completed treatment creates a political resource because knowledge, industrial output, and medical access are different things. The host may possess the only reliable standard while lacking the factories to supply everyone. An ally may have spare industry but no clinical lead. A neutral country may control the only usable route into a threatened region.

The player decides who receives the standard, who manufactures the treatment, and which population receives the first allocation. These choices remain useful during peace, when reserves and production agreements can be prepared before the next outbreak. They become difficult during a crisis, when time and transport can matter more than the nominal cost of medicine.

The institution's mandate influences the default policy. Public medicine favours broad access, military command favours national and allied needs, and classification favours restricted knowledge. The government can override those preferences through an explicit decision. Wu's control over distribution depends on the authority settlement rather than being assumed for every mandate.

## Treatment allocations

A manufactured treatment is represented by an actual medical allocation tied to a valid standard. It records its owner, location, treatment version, intended coverage, and any need for continued support. Where a disease owner already supplies an appropriate medical-stock system, that system remains authoritative. A future Wu-specific representation must provide equivalent physical accounting rather than a second reward beside the same medicine.

For initial balance, one civilian treatment allocation is proposed to support up to one million eligible living people in a selected clinical programme. This is an abstract game-scale coverage unit, not a drug dose, biological preparation quantity, or real-world clinical claim. A small target can consume a fraction of the available coverage or retain the unused allocation for another compatible target. Treating a small case does not destroy an entire national stock by rounding every action to one full unit.

The eligible population is determined by the programme. Treating active infection uses the living cases the disease owner identifies. Preventive support uses the exposed living population that the owner allows the treatment to protect. A programme cannot pay for a small group of infected patients and then claim permanent protection for the whole country's healthy population.

Military treatment uses the formation provider's appropriate coverage and sustainment requirements. The civilian allocation scale does not grant millions of enhanced soldiers or convert ordinary medicine into free manpower. Replacement personnel and fictional enhancement belong to their own research and unit systems.

## Manufacture

A country can manufacture a standard that it has legitimately received and can support. Wu's continued presence is not required for routine production of a documented standard. New exceptional adaptations still require his personal ability or a later qualified successor.

The proposed basic production contract commits one civilian factory for 30 days and consumes 50 support equipment per civilian allocation. A severe treatment doubles the industrial time and support-equipment requirement. An exceptional fictional treatment quadruples them. These are proposed balancing anchors that establish the difference between discovery and global reach. They require later comparison with the owning disease systems and the mod's equipment economy.

Production can be parallelized through additional committed industry. Parallel output scales with the resources actually assigned, rather than giving a fixed national cure to every country that can spare one factory. The interface presents the chosen target, projected coverage, industrial commitment, and expected completion. It does not require a separate decision row for every allocation.

A founding public institute can reduce conversion and organizational delay for familiar treatment standards. It does not make the physical requirement disappear. A military institute can give priority to a selected field programme, and a classified institute can protect the standard's records. These advantages should remain different rather than all becoming a generic production-speed bonus.

Production is normally a two-cost action: industrial commitment and support equipment. Changing diplomatic access can add its own political decision, while actual delivery may require transport capacity. These costs are presented at their relevant steps instead of hiding five simultaneous payments behind one button.

### Standing reserves

During a healthy period, the host can produce a bounded reserve for selected supported diseases. The player chooses the treatment and coverage target. Production stops when that target is met or when the host cancels the commitment. It does not consume factories indefinitely after the requested reserve is complete.

A reserve has a known medical match. It can become partly unsuitable after a treatment-relevant change in the disease. The institute then offers evaluation, conversion where compatible, limited continued use, or disposal. Stocks do not decay through an arbitrary invisible timer simply to create repetitive maintenance decisions.

A country may keep a small set of current reserve standards. Older records remain in the medical portfolio, while the visible reserve page focuses on current threats and selected stocks. The player can replace a stock standard without deleting the underlying discovery.

### Conversion and cancellation

Changing an active production line preserves completed medicine. Unfinished work retains a recoverable portion only when the production process and remaining standard are compatible in gameplay terms. The exact result is shown before confirmation. The player cannot finish 90 percent of a cheap treatment and convert it into 90 percent of an unrelated exceptional programme for free.

Cancellation releases future industrial commitment. Spent consumables are not refunded as though they had never been used. Unspent material remains available, and completed output remains in its stock location. Captured output belongs to its current physical holder, subject to whether that holder knows how to use it safely within the game's treatment rules.

## Selecting patients and regions

The default priority is a living population facing severe active disease that the current standard can treat. The player can instead prioritize a strategic port, a refugee reception region, an occupied front, a dense city, a military hospital network, or a threatened ally. A selected target shows why it qualifies and which biological result the treatment can achieve.

A target browser ranks relevant regions rather than listing the whole world without context. The host sees its most urgent domestic candidates first. A diplomatic view shows affected countries with accessible requests or offers. Exact state selection appears after the player chooses a country and programme.

The cost follows the selected coverage. A state with several million eligible people can receive a partial programme while additional allocations are produced. Partial treatment has a proportional, owner-recognized effect on the living group reached. It does not trigger a full national recovery flag.

Prioritizing one region can leave another waiting. The consequence should appear through the untreated region's existing disease progression and political reactions, not an additional arbitrary punishment attached solely to the selection. The institute's normal protective service continues where it has support.

## Domestic delivery

A local programme needs an accessible route between the stock or production site and the intended medical region. Existing hospitals, transport, and reception facilities determine how quickly the allocation can be used. A disconnected pocket does not receive medicine merely because it shares the same national flag.

The host can move output from a reserve, produce closer to the target, or establish a temporary medical deployment. A temporary deployment uses support equipment and available medical personnel, with transport commitments appropriate to the route. The gameplay contract identifies whether those inputs are consumed, committed, or simply required.

Delivery progress is separate from research progress. A completed treatment waiting at a secure factory remains a completed treatment. A delivery interrupted by occupation retains a clear location and disposition: still at origin, successfully rerouted, captured, lost, or partly received. The player can act on the surviving portion rather than losing all information about the shipment.

A domestic programme automatically records its clinical result when the required treatment reaches eligible patients. There is no second confirmation that spends the same stock again. The player receives a result report only for a meaningful first deployment, major recovery, important setback, or political consequence.

## Cross-border delivery

The recipient must accept the medical arrangement, and a valid route must exist. An overland neighbour, a neutral transit country, and an overseas ally create different transport requirements. The delivery does not grant general military access unless that concession was explicitly part of the agreement.

A blocked route can be negotiated, rerouted, replaced by local licensed production, or held until access returns. A country at war with the host can still receive an offer of humanitarian treatment. Accepting it is a political choice for that recipient, not an automatic faction change or surrender.

The transaction commits the selected allocation and any required transport capacity. The same stock is unavailable to a second recipient while in transit. If the route fails before departure, unspent stock remains with its owner. If actual transport losses occur, the outcome follows the applicable logistics owner and only the surviving output can be recovered.

The receiving country gains the medical benefit that arrived and any knowledge explicitly shared. Receiving one shipment does not grant production rights. Receiving a production licence does not create the factories or support equipment required to use it. Receiving both still does not grant universal cure research.

## Access policies

### Release the treatment freely

The host publishes the complete validated standard and makes it available to countries with compatible medical institutions. Affected countries can use their own industry to manufacture it. Countries without enough capacity can request finished treatment from the host or another producer.

The immediate reward is broad medical access and diplomatic recognition. The global clinical benefit develops as countries actually produce and deliver the treatment. Publication itself does not instantly remove worldwide disease or earn the full international containment consequence.

The host loses exclusive control over the published standard. It can still lead future adaptations and maintain superior clinical organization. A later policy reversal cannot cause foreign laboratories to forget what was publicly released. A new, undistributed version can remain politically contested without rewriting the history of the earlier release.

Free release can reduce personal dependence on Wu when it removes an actual monopoly. It also makes the host attractive to foreign physicians and humanitarian partners. The AI prefers this policy during a major international outbreak when cooperation is possible and secrecy offers no urgent defensive need.

The presentation should make the decision feel consequential through patients and countries that can now act. It should not substitute a generic prestige reward for the practical change in production access.

### License selected partners

A licence grants a named partner the right and knowledge to manufacture a specified standard. It can include an update-sharing agreement, a requirement to supply an agreed relief programme, or a restriction on onward transfer. Each term appears explicitly before either government accepts.

A licence does not guarantee obedience. A partner can later violate its terms, creating evidence and diplomatic consequences. The host cannot remotely destroy all existing foreign output because the partnership deteriorated. It can withhold a new version, end future support, expose the breach, or negotiate a revised agreement.

The partnership can be temporary or continue while the current treatment remains relevant. The visible relationship uses ordinary diplomatic and contract states rather than adding a new permanent medical-influence meter. The host can build a practical network through shared production without forcing every participant into the same military faction.

An industrially weak host can exchange a licence for guaranteed allocations delivered back to its own population. The partner then commits its own industry and material. This is a genuine division of labour, not a duplicated factory bonus granted to both countries for the same agreement.

### Sell access

The host chooses a concrete offer that the recipient can evaluate. Suitable payments include an agreed support-equipment transfer, a finite industrial contribution to a joint relief programme, a permitted resource agreement, intelligence cooperation, or a specified political concession. The offer uses owner-supported transactions rather than an invented unaccounted currency.

An emergency sale normally presents one principal payment and one optional concession. It does not demand every possible resource at once. The recipient can accept, counteroffer with a different valid contribution, seek another producer, or refuse. A refusal leaves the disease active without adding an unrelated punishment solely for rejecting the price.

Payment and treatment access resolve together according to the agreement. An accepted payment cannot disappear while the host silently changes the promised standard. A failed delivery produces a visible contract dispute or a replacement obligation, depending on which party controlled the failure. The agreement states whether the sale covers knowledge, finished allocations, or both.

The host can exploit desperation, but the narrative records that choice honestly. Harsh terms during mass suffering can damage relations, encourage theft, and increase demands for open access. A profitable medical contract is not automatically a biological atrocity. Actual coercion, deliberate harm, and prohibited experimentation create their own evidence through the appropriate systems.

### Reserve the treatment for the host

The country keeps its production knowledge and prioritizes domestic patients or selected military needs. This policy can be defensible when domestic capacity is insufficient or the host faces an existential threat. It becomes more politically costly when large stocks remain unused while nearby populations suffer.

Foreign reaction depends on real need and knowledge of the monopoly. A secret cure does not generate a worldwide outrage event before anyone knows it exists. Public refusal during a documented epidemic can create diplomatic pressure and incentives for intelligence operations. The international-contest evolution later expands those behaviours without owning basic cure diplomacy.

The monopoly increases Wu's importance only when he actually controls the relevant standard or production network. A government that keeps the treatment domestic but trains independent public teams has chosen national exclusivity without giving the same personal leverage to Wu.

The host can later release or license the standard. Changing policy does not repeat the discovery reward. It changes who can respond to the continuing crisis and can earn containment credit only when that broader access produces a real additional recovery.

### Retain dangerous material

The host can preserve a compatible dangerous archive after completing the cure. This decision keeps relevant future fictional research available and retains the associated custody and accident responsibility. It is separate from choosing who receives medicine.

A public institute may share a cure and still retain a small reviewed archive. A classified host may keep the cure secret while deciding to destroy material it cannot safely support. These combinations prevent access policy from becoming a disguised single good-or-evil route selector.

The later advanced-research specification owns any fictional weapon or organism application. This medical-access choice supplies custody, political permission, and risk direction. It does not specify real agent properties, laboratory procedures, production methods, or dissemination techniques.

Destroying an archive preserves validated treatment records. A later need for genuine source material can require obtaining a new valid lead rather than pretending the archive survived. The player should understand that safety can close a particular immediate opportunity while leaving the broad research family reachable through another compatible source.

## A humanitarian production agreement

A cooperative agreement can be initiated by Wu's host or an affected country. The parties select a standard, the population to be served, the manufacturing contribution, and the delivery route. A neutral sponsor can supply industry or transport without gaining ownership of Wu.

The agreement is particularly useful when a small host has completed the cure. The host supplies documented knowledge and medical direction. The partner provides actual production. The recipient provides access and ordinary local care. Each contribution has a visible owner, preventing one country's scarce resources from being counted as available in three places.

If the partnership breaks down, completed knowledge remains with those who legitimately received it and completed output remains at its actual location. Unfinished commitments are cancelled or renegotiated. Patients already treated retain their clinical result.

Successful cooperation can improve diplomatic relations and reduce the biological crisis. It should not grant recurring political power simply because several routine production cycles have elapsed. The important rewards belong to the first meaningful agreement, fulfilment of its actual relief objective, and any new institutional settlement it creates.

## Foreign requests and response load

The host should receive a manageable number of requests. Active outbreaks, high civilian danger, and a workable contribution make a request urgent. Repeated demands from the same country for the same unmet need merge into the existing request rather than creating a new popup every few days.

The host can establish a standing policy for routine requests, such as supplying allies from a selected reserve limit or accepting neutral production partnerships under published terms. Exceptional requests still require a decision. A request for unique source material, a major concession, or access to Wu himself cannot be accepted by an unrelated low-level aid rule.

A foreign request never has a deadline shorter than the earliest feasible response unless the objective is explicitly partial relief. When a full cure cannot be researched and delivered in time, the mission can ask for emergency support, a first clinical deployment, or a negotiated treatment corridor. It must not present an impossible full-cure timer and punish the player for failing to compress several months of work into days.

The recipient's AI evaluates the actual offer. During an active outbreak, it places high value on clinically useful treatment. It still considers sovereignty, wartime access, and whether the promised output can arrive. It does not pay for an obsolete version that its own medical information already identifies as unsuitable.

## Care in occupied and displaced populations

The host can treat civilians in territory it controls when the relevant population and disease systems allow access. Treatment does not change state ownership, give cores, or erase occupation policy. It can reduce the biological harm experienced by the population and may create a separate political response where the owner supports one.

Medical teams serving displaced people use the existing reception and route systems. A disease-free reception corridor can help a migration programme function, but Wu cannot generate reception capacity or remove the food requirement. The population remains in its actual location until the movement owner records a transfer.

Consent and custody remain part of the institution's mandate. Providing public medical care does not authorize experimental use of refugees or occupied civilians. A restricted project involving those groups requires its own severe political decision and evidence consequences, or an applicable unauthorized-research branch with discoverable misconduct.

## Blockade, capture, and medical neutrality

A blockade can make local licensed manufacture more valuable than importing finished treatment. A neutral agreement can allow a limited medical corridor when the participants accept it. The corridor's access is bounded to its purpose and does not silently become unrestricted military passage.

Captured medicine can still help compatible living patients when the captor can identify and use the standard. Capturing a treatment stock does not grant all future updates or ownership of Wu. Capturing incomplete or unlabelled material can create an evaluation requirement rather than an instant cure or automatic outbreak.

A hospital or medical convoy is a concrete setting for diplomatic conflict. The game should distinguish an accidental transport loss, an unlawful deliberate attack, an exposed disguised operation, and a negotiated seizure through the systems that own those actions. Wu's event records its medical and institutional consequences without replacing attribution or evidence rules.

## Three complete delivery situations

### A small neutral host and a continental epidemic

The neutral host develops a validated cure but can only produce a limited domestic reserve. It releases the standard under a production agreement with two industrial partners. One partner supplies the host's population, while the other manufactures locally for its own outbreak. The neutral host gains medical influence through the fulfilled agreement and retains Wu, while neither partner gains universal research.

If a partner later refuses to share the next delivery, the existing treatment knowledge remains useful. The neutral host can seek another producer or publish the standard fully. The dispute therefore changes diplomacy and access rather than resetting the entire cure sequence.

### A military institute with a blocked overseas ally

The host has completed the treatment and manufactured stock, but the sea route is unavailable. It can preserve the stock, redirect it to domestic need, negotiate a valid route, or send the standard so the ally can manufacture locally. The finished cure remains finished throughout the dispute.

An emergency mission can ask for a working production agreement rather than an impossible shipment. Once the ally produces and applies the treatment, the clinical result belongs to the actual population reached. The host does not receive credit for nonexistent convoy deliveries.

### A monopoly after a shared outbreak

The host protects its own population and refuses to share while neighbouring countries remain infected. The institute's clinical success is real, but the monopoly creates foreign pressure and a demand for independent domestic production. The cabinet can maintain exclusivity, license limited partners, or publish the standard.

A later release cannot erase the earlier refusal, deaths, or deliberate wrongdoing. It can still save living people and materially reduce the continuing crisis. The event preserves both facts rather than treating a final humanitarian choice as a retroactive cleansing of the entire route.

## The access decision remains meaningful

Research determines whether treatment is possible. Production determines how much support exists. Delivery determines who receives it. Policy determines who may make or request more. Keeping these decisions separate gives Wu a durable role through healthy years, regional emergencies, and global disasters without making the player administer individual patients or repeat the same transaction by hand.
