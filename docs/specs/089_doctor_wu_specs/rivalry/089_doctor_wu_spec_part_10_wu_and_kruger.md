# Doctor Wu: Wu and Kruger
## Two scientists in one country

Wu arrives at a hospital with a treatment that works. Kruger arrives with plans for the machinery, power, transport, and research establishment that could make such treatments available on a national scale. A government that employs both gains unusual opportunities and two men with strong reasons to dispute who should direct them. Their first disagreement should concern an actual laboratory, patient programme, captured specimen, production order, or group of researchers. Unrelated work can continue for months without a rivalry incident.

The country must decide who can approve a project, who can stop it, who receives its results, and who remains responsible when either scientist leaves. A hospital cannot accept two incompatible instructions about its patients. A workshop cannot promise the same production time to both institutes. A soldier's dependence on a treatment gives the medical service an obligation to that person, not ownership of that soldier.

This part develops six working relationship labels: **Ordered division of work**, **Competitive grants**, **Joint institute**, **Wu supremacy**, **Kruger supremacy**, and **Laboratory conflict**. They describe the country's current arrangement and its consequences. They are ordinary institutional progression. Their selection does not advance either scientist's evolution track.

New costs, durations, capacities, and outcome changes in this part are proposals. They have not been approved as balance settings or tested in the game. Existing project costs and rights retain the ownership defined in Parts 1–9 and their underlying systems. The labels used here describe design roles and must not be copied as finished localisation.

## The first shared claim

The domestic rivalry becomes relevant when a working Wu institute and Kruger's actual current host occupy the same country. A qualified continuity Wu can represent the institute. A stored Wu archive, an unqualified copy, an inherited technology, or a dormant Kruger character does not provide the second active scientist.

The first contact identifies one concrete overlap. Preference goes to a resource already needed by both programmes: a hospital supporting a biomedical project, a growth site supporting different clone programmes, a nonhuman patient with two proposed uses, a machine requiring a living interface, or a research team already promised to another laboratory. When both scientists are present without such an overlap, the country receives an opportunity to settle their working relationship without a fabricated accident or confrontation.

The initial contact should normally be folded into the next relevant institute report. It should not stack a second arrival spectacle on top of Wu's medical proof or Kruger's appointment. A proposed fifteen-day contact interval allows the first mandate to settle, but a real dispute over an active service can require an earlier report. This interval is scheduling for an ordinary report, not an evolution MTTH.

The government initially has three useful responses. It can divide responsibilities, fund a bounded comparison, or commission a joint undertaking. The two supremacy arrangements require a later explicit transfer of authority or an already established pattern of dominance. Open laboratory conflict requires an actual hostile act or a failed attempt to resolve competing commands. The opening does not pretend that all six states already exist.

A classified appointment changes who knows about the relationship. Ministers with oversight can negotiate without publicly revealing either programme. A foreign observer receives only information obtained through an existing source. The player must not learn the location of an undiscovered underground Wu institute simply because Kruger is employed nearby.

The first settlement records the two actual institutional representatives and the host country. Later changes preserve that history. A new Wu copy can accept a position, challenge an existing arrangement, or continue a licensed service. It cannot reset the relationship into a fresh appointment reward.

## What can be divided

The government settles distinct rights for each affected undertaking. These rights can belong to different parties and should remain intelligible through the undertaking's current status.

| Subject | What the settlement can assign | What that assignment cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scientific direction | The investigator responsible for a defined question and its next approved work | Ownership of every related project or another scientist's personal knowledge |
| Facility access | Use of a named room, workshop, laboratory, reserve, or hospital under existing capacity limits | Another building, another native special-project facility, or permission to displace its current occupants without a handover |
| Staff service | A real team's employer, assignment, term, and right to return | A second team created from the same roster or a scientist copied into another active assignment |
| Specimen custody | Physical custody and the permitted use of identified material | Proof that a country possesses material merely because it possesses a report |
| Scientific credit | Attribution for demonstrated contributions, independent replication, and publication | A new technology grant or ownership of patients and troops |
| Production | Authority to commission a specified design through its actual owner | Retroactive conversion of old stock or an extra modified production standard |
| Medical responsibility | The service that must continue treatment, follow-up, withdrawal, and recovery | Political loyalty, a right to recruit patients, or a cure for a non-biological condition |
| Military employment | The government's permission to assign qualified forces and accept a defined operation | A transfer of an army through a laboratory decision or authority to release a weapon |

These distinctions create the meaningful disagreements. Kruger may retain credit for a control device while Wu refuses to certify its use on a particular formation. Wu may have discovered a treatment whose public manufacturing rights already belong to a university. A military ministry may own a workshop and still need both scientists' cooperation before the workshop can support an advanced service.

A settlement cannot erase earlier public trust, executive reserve, patent-pool, civic compact, or industrial-charter decisions. It must identify the holder of those rights. Newly negotiated access can be narrower than ownership. A new institution may obtain permission to maintain an existing service without acquiring permission to alter it or distribute the complete design.

The existing Kruger facility and custody reports have their own once-only history. A Wu agreement does not replay them. Their unresolved reports remain owned by their original sequence, and their resolved consequences remain where the owner placed them. A domestic joint institute must not be presented as the already existing foreign joint-laboratory framework.

## One country, real commitments

The rivalry does not create a research budget currency. It allocates actual factories, support equipment, staff time, physical stock, and project access. The country still pays each underlying project through its own system. A grant is permission and a funded commitment to specified work, not a token that can later be exchanged for an arbitrary technology.

Before accepting a second promise, the government sees the capacity left after current commitments. Wu's ordinary research lanes retain their established limit. Kruger's Project Capacity retains its own calculation. A shared building or workshop must have a single record of its actual use, even when two programmes benefit from its output.

A team can contribute to several institutions over a career. During a particular service interval, it cannot provide its full specialist benefit to two projects. An existing team may be seconded after its earlier assignment ends or under a handover accepted by that assignment's owner. Merely changing its institutional label cannot make its old contribution remain active.

Care already promised to living recipients has priority over a new grant. Before diverting resources, the government must fund a replacement service, reduce a programme's future intake, or complete an orderly withdrawal. This protects existing patients while leaving hard choices about new production and future treatment access.

The initial common administrative actions use the following proposed anchors. Committed civilian factories count as a spendable cost type. Named staff and sites are requirements only where they remain in their already funded ordinary roles. A new paid staff reservation must replace another cost type when needed to remain within the four-type action budget.

| Action role | Base duration | Direct payment | Temporary commitment | Concrete result |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Settle the first division of responsibility | 60 days | 50 Political Power, 100 Support Equipment | 1 civilian factory | Named responsibilities, protected staff assignments, and an overlap-review procedure |
| Open a comparative grant round | 30 days | 50 Political Power, 150 Support Equipment | 1 civilian factory | Two distinct accepted proposals and a comparison objective, with project bills still unpaid until individually commissioned |
| Establish a domestic joint institute | 120 days | 75 Political Power, 250 Support Equipment | 2 civilian factories | A staffed joint administration at existing valid sites, with one agreed first undertaking |
| Renegotiate a disputed right | 60 days | 35 Political Power, 100 Support Equipment | 1 civilian factory | A specific access or ownership amendment accepted by the affected parties |

| Protect a disrupted medical service | 30 days | 25 Command Power, 100 Support Equipment, 1,000 fuel | 1 civilian factory | A temporary protected access and transport arrangement for a named existing service |

These actions do not add research progress, production stock, or free capacity. Their costs purchase the described administrative, inspection, or logistical work. An audit consumes time and resources even when it finds no misconduct. A protected service still needs its ordinary treatment stock and qualified personnel through its existing owner. The protection order cannot count as a second purchase of that medicine.

Duration changes must follow the work. A previously documented, undisputed agreement can reduce a new sixty-day renegotiation to thirty days. Disputed public rights, damaged records, or several separately controlled sites can add thirty days, up to 120 for that renegotiation. A case needing a physical relocation uses the relocation owner and its own bill. The administrative timetable cannot turn an unavailable route into a functioning one.

Material scaling follows the number of locations actually inspected or serviced, using whole site allocations and rounded values. It does not follow world Chaos. A small country with one contested hospital can settle that case without paying for a national network. A large state receives no full-network inspection from the single-site cost.

## How the relationship changes

One arrangement governs current domestic cooperation. A country may also have unresolved cases from an older arrangement, because contracts, care obligations, and investigations can outlast the policy that created them. Historical cases do not keep an obsolete route's benefits active.

A proposed replacement begins with a named transition and becomes active only when its handover succeeds. During that interval, earlier projects continue under their existing authorizations unless their own owner suspends them. A late callback from an older transition cannot install its former settlement over a newer one.

The government can always stop issuing new rivalry grants without spending Political Power. Closing a joint institution or moving people is a separate action because it has real consequences. A free stop cannot erase the previous costs, remaining care, or the need to maintain dangerous material safely.

| Current arrangement | Natural next choices | What must be settled first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordered division | A defined competition or joint undertaking | Staff secondment, shared access, and protection of existing work |
| Competitive grants | Another materially different comparison, a joint institute, or restored separation | Current awards, incomplete projects, disputed findings, and production rights |
| Joint institute | Continued cooperation, division, or one scientist's supremacy | Shared services, equipment custody, publication rights, and institutional survival |
| Wu supremacy | Continued biological control, restored division, or a negotiated joint undertaking | Kruger's exclusion terms and the care consequences of replacing his services |
| Kruger supremacy | Continued subordinate medicine, restored autonomy, or a negotiated joint undertaking | Wu's service obligations, archives, surviving staff, and the scope of Kruger's control |
| Laboratory conflict | Emergency cooperation, an investigated settlement, separation, or owner-supported coercion | Actual damage, missing people, disputed evidence, and current command safety |

Repeated switching is costly because real work must be settled. It does not repeatedly award first-settlement Authority, Mandate, or reputation gains. A later change can produce new political consequences when it genuinely transfers a right or exposes misconduct. The same signed paper, renamed laboratory, or previously adjudicated claim cannot generate them again.

## Ordered division of work

### Two established fields

The government gives Wu responsibility for medicine, biological care, replication medicine, and the qualification of living recipients. Kruger directs the wider engineering and scientific programme. Each keeps a recognized establishment, and neither can recruit an assigned specialist from the other without an agreed secondment.

This arrangement is the most dependable choice for a government that wants both scientists without combining their political power. It works especially well when the host can maintain separate sites, has public research institutions worth protecting, or cannot afford failures in an already stretched medical service.

The division governs responsibility and access. It does not remove a research family from either scientist's underlying portfolio. Kruger's existing Biomedical or Cloning knowledge remains learned knowledge. Wu can still study biological interfaces for machines after obtaining the appropriate technical contribution. Their overlapping work needs a bounded agreement that names which institution is responsible for each component.

The initial settlement classifies existing work before assigning new proposals. An already paid Kruger biomedical project remains his project's responsibility through its valid completion or cancellation. Wu receives clinical oversight only where that oversight has actually been authorized. A functioning Wu ward does not become a general Kruger research site because it contains advanced equipment.

A proposed overlap review takes thirty days, uses 25 Political Power and 75 Support Equipment, and commits one civilian factory. It covers one defined undertaking at one pair of existing sites. This review establishes access and responsibility, not another project clock. Routine work inside an already agreed field needs no repeated review.

### Shared work without staff poaching

A secondment specifies the team, its existing qualification, the receiving assignment, and the date or completion condition for return. The providing institute loses that team's service benefit during the secondment. The receiving institute gains only the benefit the team is qualified to supply. A general engineer cannot replace Wu's unique medical judgment, and a replicated physician cannot supply Kruger's missing operational machine knowledge.

The government can decline a secondment and fund independent recruitment through the existing staff path. This costs more time and preserves autonomy. It can instead delay one programme until the team is available, or agree to an emergency loan with a defined return obligation.

The arrangement prevents ordinary poaching by making unauthorized recruitment a visible breach when discovered. A researcher who freely leaves employment is not automatically a saboteur. The dispute concerns a broken service commitment and the resulting interruption. Investigators must distinguish voluntary departure, state reassignment, abduction, death, and an unresolved absence.

Neither institute may claim a specimen solely because it employs the first scientist who examined it. Physical custody follows the existing owner and the specimen's admitted purpose. The government may permit both teams to study already shareable records while leaving the material in one secure site. Access to a document does not duplicate a specimen or satisfy a project requiring live material.

### The cost of caution

The thirty-day review delays genuinely new cross-field work. Separate staff training and separate maintenance also cost real capacity. The arrangement gives up some speed in exchange for fewer ambiguous orders and a smaller set of shared dependencies.

Its safety benefit comes from the work it changes. A correctly assigned team cannot be simultaneously poached through an ordinary grant. A documented secondment cannot count as a missing-person incident without a new cause. A project whose two providers have accepted the same control handover does not receive an additional rivalry penalty for conflicting instructions. Disease accidents, native project failures, outside attacks, and the project's own control weaknesses remain possible.

The player can shorten a specific overlap review to fifteen days during a recognized emergency by accepting provisional shared access. That choice leaves an unresolved access review due after the emergency and removes the protection against an ownership dispute for that undertaking. It does not raise every biological accident chance or grant permanent emergency powers.

### The ward and the workshop

A useful early case places a Kruger machine installation in the same hospital that Wu needs for a cure programme. The equipment exists, the ward is occupied, and both schedules are valid. The government must preserve the ward, move the proposed installation to a prepared alternative, or temporarily divide the available service time.

Preserving the ward lets treatment continue and leaves the machine installation pending. Moving the installation requires its real owner to support the move and preserves the old physical investment. Dividing time reduces the actual capacity available to each programme. The country does not obtain two full-capacity hospitals from one site.

A successful settlement leaves a reusable agreement for that ward and installation. It does not eliminate future disagreements involving another site or a different purpose. A failed relocation can leave the machine unavailable while the hospital remains functional. A failed medical handover can leave a treatment service in recovery while the workshop continues. Results must follow the affected component.

### Supervision and survival

The government can require public clinical findings while keeping Kruger's unrelated military work classified. Wu receives credit for the treatment, the engineer receives credit for the apparatus, and the hospital retains the rights already established for its records. Publication rights are assigned to the actual result, not to an entire national research portfolio.

If Kruger departs, the country retains learned operational packages and the institutions that already own their methods. His active personal contribution ends under his owner contract. Wu can continue the clinical service when the remaining engineering team is qualified to maintain it. An unfinished machine dependent on Kruger's personal work remains unfinished.

If Wu departs, the hospital can maintain established treatment under its preserved protocols and qualified staff. It cannot claim his ability to improvise a new cure. A qualified continuity Wu may take the medical appointment through Part 7's succession process, without recreating the workshop, staff, or funding.

The clean long-term payoff is two durable establishments with a known boundary. The route remains useful throughout the campaign because new cross-field work can be admitted without surrendering all future decisions to either man.

## Competitive grants

### A contest over a real problem

The country funds two distinct approaches to a shared need. The contest might compare a hospital service with a mechanical recovery installation, two different qualified support arrangements for clone soldiers, or separate ways to keep a named medical network functioning after a founder leaves.

The government states the problem before accepting the bids. Useful objectives concern actual delivery: keep an identified service supplied for ninety days, qualify a replacement team, maintain a control arrangement through a real redeployment, or support a stated number of receiving places through their ordinary observation period. Possessing a technology or a large stockpile is insufficient on its own.

The two bids must lead to meaningfully different work. Two names for the same native special project cannot create two parallel copies of it. When both scientists claim the same project, the country chooses one lead investigator and can commission an independent assessment of its result. It cannot duplicate the underlying project clock or its completion reward.

### How competition produces earlier results

Accepted bids can run in parallel through their existing owners when the country can pay and staff both. The first useful result may therefore arrive earlier than it would under sequential development. A completed provisional result can receive a fifteen-day commissioning review instead of waiting for the competing project to finish.

This early commission does not establish the final winner. It authorizes a bounded use already supported by the completed capability and its paid service. It cannot grant unfinished research, skip maturation or training, waive a cure's deployment costs, or remove a native project iteration.

The speed advantage has a practical cost. The country pays for competing approaches, may have to maintain incompatible equipment, and reserves more staff and industrial time. A country unable to fund both bids cannot start the competitive round and quietly provide one side with free resources. It must narrow the problem, fund a single approach, or choose another relationship.

No new universal research-speed modifier is attached to competition. Kruger's existing research anchor, qualified specialist benefits, and native project modifiers keep their own scope. The contest changes which work starts together and when completed work can be commissioned.

### Funding without a private currency

The comparative-round action opens the administrative frame. Each accepted research or service order is then funded separately through its actual owner, with its ordinary costs visible before selection. The round itself does not pay those bills.

Funding commitments distinguish work already paid from future support. A losing bidder keeps completed knowledge and the stock it actually produced. The government can decline its next order, require safe storage, or purchase continuing service if the alternative remains useful. It does not claw back spent industrial time as a refund.

A result does not receive a second award in money, equipment, or manpower merely because it won. The useful award is the next concrete procurement or service assignment, paid normally and assigned to the successful method. Scientific credit and the right to lead that next undertaking follow the accepted finding.

Repeated contests need a different problem, a materially revised method, or a changed campaign need. The same completed treatment cannot win the same ninety-day trial repeatedly for additional Authority or prestige. An alternative supplier can still compete for a later contract without pretending that the treatment has just been discovered.

### Evaluation and disputed credit

The comparison uses the objective announced at the start. For a continuity service, compare whether each programme maintained qualified coverage, its real resource use, and its recorded interruptions. For a recovery installation, compare the supported places and completed care. For controlled formations, compare the defined command and care task without forcing the player to conduct a war solely to finish the trial.

One approach can win on prompt delivery and lose on long-term independence. The government's final choice may accept the quicker service, fund the more independent service, or keep both for distinct roles. The report must state what each actually achieved. It must not combine their best observed results into an imaginary third method.

An independent audit can find that the comparison is incomplete. A destroyed site, lost route, or different patient group may make the results incomparable. The country can extend the same objective once by a proposed sixty days when a recoverable external interruption justifies it, or settle the completed portion and close the round. An extension preserves the original costs, results, and dates.

Scientist credit follows demonstrated contributions. Copying a published method into a functioning independent service earns replication credit, not original discovery credit. A deliberately false claim can be exposed, but a disagreement about interpretation does not establish fraud by itself.

### Pressure inside the laboratories

Competition creates specific opportunities for misconduct. A promised specialist may be offered a second position, a team may overstate readiness to win early commissioning, or a scientist may use access granted for the comparison to seek a broader claim over a specimen. These are cases with actors, affected work, and evidence.

A premature commission can leave a service on provisional qualification with a real recovery obligation. It cannot manufacture an outbreak simply because the government favored one scientist. An actual release, injury, or control failure requires the corresponding source owner's valid consequence.

Discovered interference can lead to an audit, the loss of future grant priority, a protected secondment, or a new division of work. Open sabotage moves the relationship into Laboratory conflict only when an actual hostile action or sufficiently proven pattern supports that state.

Hidden or unauthorized work remains subject to the permissions and autonomy rules of the existing Wu chain. A competitive grant does not silently authorize every forbidden project. It can expose a breach already made possible by accumulated authority and lost oversight, and it can create the opportunity for the government to investigate that breach.

### A grant round under strain

A wartime government funds Wu's trained recovery service and Kruger's proposed mechanical support for the same army. Wu completes his method first and receives a limited service assignment. Kruger's project later produces a working device, but it still needs the medical qualification and staff described in Part 8.

The government may integrate the two through a Joint institute undertaking, keep them separate for different formations, or decline further mechanical procurement. Kruger's completed project remains completed. Wu's treated soldiers remain the same people with the same care record. Neither outcome grants a free formation or reclassifies a legacy clone batch as a finite Wu cohort.

The route's long-term payoff is a country with alternatives and tested performance. Its characteristic failure is a research establishment that spends more effort winning the next contract than maintaining the people and services already entrusted to it. That failure must emerge from choices and interrupted commitments, not a permanent random punishment for selecting competition.

## Joint institute

### A shared institution with separate expertise

The government establishes a durable institution in which Wu and Kruger direct different parts of a common programme. Wu certifies the living system and its care. Kruger certifies the machinery and the technical conditions required to operate it. The state decides where the result may be used and how it will be funded.

The joint institute removes the need to renegotiate ordinary access for every undertaking already covered by its charter. It can share a qualified service team, an agreed archive, and prepared maintenance arrangements across successive work. It gains breadth and continuity through those actual arrangements, not by merging both scientists into a single bonus.

The full institutional route is not a prerequisite for every joint project. Ordered division, competition, and either supremacy arrangement may authorize a bounded joint undertaking with explicit access and the same technical prerequisites. The Joint institute route makes cooperation the continuing structure of the national programme. A government can otherwise obtain a specific biological-machine project without surrendering all institutional independence.

### The founding choice

The 120-day founding action requires a working Wu institute, an active Kruger establishment, and a first undertaking both can actually support. The first undertaking may still need research. Its proposal must identify the relevant completed knowledge, missing prerequisites, physical sites, and intended division of work.

The foundation does not create a new special-project facility or overwrite either primary-facility target. Existing rooms may be assigned only within their actual capacity. If two native facilities must occupy different states under their owner rules, calling them one institute does not make them colocated. The institution can be geographically distributed, with real transport and access between its sites.

Three founding arrangements change who can continue the work later. A **public institutional charter** leaves usable protocols and accepted results with qualified national institutions. A **shared proprietary partnership** leaves more of the methods under the two scientists' direct control. A **military service charter** keeps operational use with the armed forces and separates classified technical knowledge from clinical records needed for continuing care.

These are working charter labels inside the joint route, not three new permanent meters. They do not each create another national spirit. Their effects appear through permissions, custody, independent training, and the service consequences of a founder's departure.

The public charter takes the time needed to prepare independent operators. The proprietary partnership can begin with the existing expert teams, but cannot promise independent operation before that training occurs. The military charter can protect a deployment without granting either scientist command of the country's ordinary army.

### One undertaking, distinct components

Each undertaking names a clinical lead, a technical lead, a service owner, and a recipient or installation. Those positions need not belong to four separate people. They do need clear responsibility. An engineer who designed a machine may remain its technical lead while a hospital becomes responsible for routine operation.

The two research owners keep their own projects, costs, clocks, and completion conditions. A shared final result is commissioned only after its required components are complete and their combined service has passed the appropriate qualification. The commissioning record links those results. It does not grant either component a second time.

A project can reach partial success. The mechanical component may be complete while the biological component still needs work. The living component may be ready while its intended device is unavailable. Both results remain in their proper histories. The government can store the completed component, fund the missing work, or use a compatible standalone result already supported by its owner.

It cannot issue a combined output by averaging two incomplete projects. It cannot count an equipment illustration as a manufactured machine, or a successful cure as proof that the complete living interface is safe for military service.

### Six recurring kinds of joint work

The joint institution supports six application families from the original brief. These applications use the preceding portfolio. They are not six extra research trees.

| Joint application | Wu contribution | Kruger contribution | Result that must actually exist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Biological interfaces for advanced machines | Qualified living service, compatibility, care, and recall | Operational machine knowledge, real equipment, power, and maintenance | A commissioned Part 8 machine service tied to an actual installation |
| Alien medicine | Family-specific diagnosis and treatment for an admitted living case | Authenticated technical or contact knowledge relevant to that case | A treatment result accepted by the nonhuman or disease owner |
| Synthetic organs and clinical machinery | M07–M09 material, compatibility, and recovery work | Appropriate Biomedical or engineering capability | Real clinical material and a qualified service, without new people |
| Stabilized clone soldiers | Generation care, training, identity, and optional enhancement | The correct shared clone programme and its actual technical support | The same people or legacy units with accepted care, not a new manpower grant |
| Controlled nonhuman infantry | Compatible medicine and qualified control service | The existing unit family's control architecture and technical establishment | A supported existing family whose command and political owner remain defined |
| Experimental-technology injuries | Treatment of a proven biological condition | Identification and correction of the responsible technical failure | Treated survivors and a separately repaired technical system |

Alien medicine cannot call a contact or landing event simply to supply a patient. A captured machine containing no living biological component does not become a cure target. Authenticated knowledge can help the institute understand a case, but the source owner decides whether the case is biological and whether the treatment result is compatible.

Synthetic-organ work follows the distinction between material and people. A successful clinical production order supplies its actual material. It does not credit population or manpower. A replicated specialist who operates the installation remains the person already counted through Part 7.

Clone stabilization must name which economy it serves. The accepted legacy clone package continues using its existing equipment and manpower rules. Wu's proposed finite replacement cohorts continue using first-settlement accounting. A joint treatment can help recipients from either programme when compatible, but it cannot convert one economy into the other by changing a laboratory's name.

Nonhuman work preserves the original family's separate control method. An existing xenobiological programme cannot gain all four mutually exclusive control architectures through a joint charter. A new physician can maintain a compatible clinical service without replacing the architecture that governs its forces.

Experimental-technology injuries require two resolutions. Treating an injured operator does not stabilize a temporal anchor, repair a damaged device, or erase the source incident. Repairing a device does not establish that its injured operators recovered. Each owner records its own actual result.

### The three Part 8 machines

The **Service Tender**, **Observation Works**, and **Recovery Cradle** remain the three defined biological-machinery services. Their research, manufacture, commissioning, and care costs are unchanged. The joint institute decides access and responsibility for them.

A Service Tender can support the documented expansion of service places only while the actual machine, staff, maintenance, and compatible living units are available. It does not create the additional service organisms or remove their husbandry needs. Both institutes must see the same remaining capacity.

An Observation Works handles the observations actually returned by the reconnaissance service. It cannot see an enemy laboratory merely because Wu suspects Kruger or because the country has a rivalry case open. Its faster interpretation remains limited to the qualified work and non-stacking rule already specified in Part 8.

A Recovery Cradle provides the established exceptional care places. It does not create patients, restore recorded dead people, or reserve the same place for a new citizen and a wounded specialist. A joint board cannot count one place in both institutes' available totals.

The government can transfer routine operation to an independent team after the corresponding qualification. This preserves a useful result after a founder leaves. It cannot transfer an undeveloped improvisational ability or an unrecorded personal method by decree.

### Shared success and concentrated authority

A successful joint institution can gradually control the country's most important treatments, unusual troops, and technical infrastructure. That concentration must come from actual assignments. A founding signature alone does not establish institutional capture.

The player can prevent excessive concentration by placing procurement with the state, treatment records with public hospitals, technical maintenance with independently trained engineers, and military employment with ordinary command. These arrangements require the real independent training and access work. The same researcher cannot be counted as independent from the institute while still depending on its undisclosed instructions for every task.

The proprietary charter gives the scientists a stronger bargaining position when several services genuinely depend on their continued cooperation. They may jointly seek protected budgets, exclusive access, or exemption from an inspection. Accepting such demands changes the actual rights concerned and can raise the existing Authority and Mandate values through the proposed integration rules later in this part.

Refusal does not automatically destroy the institute. It creates a negotiation over the affected service. The government can fund an independent replacement, narrow the programme, retain the present contract, or face a withdrawal that must still be distinguished from abandonment of patients.

### Failure and separation

A joint institution can fail through incompatible priorities without either scientist becoming a saboteur. A military ministry may require deployment while Wu insists on further care. Kruger may need a machine for another project while the hospital cannot safely release it. The immediate case concerns that assignment.

The government can separate the undertaking into independently supported components, fund additional real capacity, or suspend its future expansion. If a component has no independent use, it remains incomplete or safely stored. There is no automatic refund of the failed cooperation's spent resources.

Dissolution uses a proposed 120-day settlement with 50 Political Power, 200 Support Equipment, 1,500 fuel, and two committed civilian factories. The objective is to assign every active shared service to a qualified continuing operator or a completed withdrawal, reconcile actual stock and equipment custody, and settle the remaining publication rights. The action finishes early only when all required dispositions are complete and their native owners accept them.

A partial settlement can separate the ordinary laboratories while leaving one indispensable care service under temporary joint administration. The unresolved service keeps its existing obligations and deadline. It cannot keep unrelated joint-route advantages alive.

If one founder disappears during dissolution, the surviving staff preserve what they can actually operate. An unfinished joint project is not completed by the other founder's promotion. A qualified Wu successor can fill Wu's role after the normal succession process. Kruger continues to use his single canonical identity and his own continuity contract.

The long-term payoff is an institution capable of work neither scientist could commission alone at the same level. Its lasting value depends on what it teaches its staff, what it physically builds, and which responsibilities survive the partnership.

## Wu supremacy

### Control of medicine and living research

The government makes Wu the principal authority over its medical and biological establishment. Kruger loses general access to living subjects, specimens, medical facilities, and biological service assignments covered by that decision. He retains his wider scientific role, completed knowledge, existing non-biological projects, and the research benefit attached to his valid appointment.

This route differs from Ordered division because Wu can refuse Kruger's new biological requests and choose which of his technical contributions the medical establishment will accept. The government has concentrated approval power in Wu's hands. A public board may still limit that authority, but Kruger no longer has equal standing in the covered field.

The route requires a functioning Wu institute and a real jurisdiction to transfer. A country cannot establish supremacy by giving Wu control of an empty list of laboratories. Existing public rights, military obligations, foreign agreements, and privately retained methods must be identified before the new arrangement takes effect.

The proposed installation takes ninety days and costs 75 Political Power and 250 Support Equipment while committing two civilian factories. It establishes the new jurisdiction and the handover of agreed access. Moving stock, changing a formation's medical provider, or replacing a dangerous control service remains a separate owner-supported operation with its existing costs.

### The biological-access settlement

Kruger receives a list of the sites, samples, and new assignments he can no longer use freely. Access can continue for a narrowly defined technical service, an already agreed emergency obligation, or a project the government explicitly exempts. These exemptions name the actual work. They cannot become a permanent general bypass of Wu's authority.

Already completed knowledge remains with its holder. Restricting a hospital does not remove a technology. A currently paid project needs a valid completion, suspension, cancellation, or replacement arrangement from its own owner. The rivalry route cannot simply clear Kruger's project array and claim the resources for Wu.

The new biological-access restriction needs an explicit Event 016 integration extension for affected future projects and use permissions. The current source does not establish a generic Wu veto. The design requires that boundary without treating it as an implemented helper or silently replacing it with a national research penalty.

A project that requires a living component cannot be reclassified as ordinary engineering to evade the restriction. Conversely, a non-biological machine project cannot be stopped merely because Wu dislikes its investigator. The scope is determined by the work and its owner-defined dependencies.

### Kruger's four responses

Kruger can accept a **technical consultant position** inside the affected work. He remains responsible for defined machinery, with access sufficient to maintain it and no wider claim to patients or specimens. His project credit and permanent knowledge remain intact. This is the least disruptive settlement, but it limits his ability to begin a new biological application without further approval.

He can demand a **protected independent branch**. The state then chooses whether to retain a specific existing technical site and staff under their earlier rights. Wu's authority remains over clinical admission and living research, while the protected branch keeps a named technical function. This can sustain useful cooperation without restoring equal biological authority.

He can **challenge Wu's conduct**. The challenge needs an actual disputed result, denied right, treatment monopoly, interrupted service, or evidence of misconduct. It leads to an audit or a case-specific government hearing. A successful challenge can reverse the disputed decision, restore access for the affected purpose, or move the whole relationship back toward division. An inconclusive finding leaves the uncertainty visible and does not manufacture wrongdoing.

He can **seek departure**. His owner determines whether he is transferable and which recipients are valid. He cannot leave an active special-project assignment through an instant rivalry button. An existing cross-domain review must finish or cancel through its owner. Former-host exclusions and other recipient restrictions remain intact. A country does not erase its prior rejection or departure history merely to invite him back after a convenient policy change.

A failed or unavailable departure can leave Kruger as an excluded researcher with a grievance. It does not automatically kill him, create another Kruger, or transfer a laboratory state. The government can reopen negotiations, maintain the restricted arrangement, or face a concrete interference case if he acts against it.

### Wu's choices after winning control

Wu can use supremacy to unify patient care and remove conflicting orders. The state may give him one authority to prioritize treatment reserves during an outbreak while keeping production records and qualification standards in public institutions. This provides an effective medical administration with a clear way to limit its personal dependence.

He can instead retain exclusive control of cure distribution, specialist appointments, and service archives. These rights create leverage only where countries, hospitals, or formations actually depend on them. The programme does not gain political power merely from the number of pages in its archive.

A recurring case can concern a hospital whose independent staff can manufacture an established cure. Wu asks the government to stop their production in favor of his preferred standard. The country may keep their qualified service, fund a new qualification, or transfer future production after safe notice. Existing medicine retains its identity and actual usability. A production order cannot relabel the old treatment or abandon its recipients.

Another case can concern an engineered formation supported by Kruger's equipment. Wu may deny a new use of living subjects, but the current soldiers still need their equipment and care. The state can keep the technical maintenance contract, pay for a compatible replacement, or withdraw the formation. It cannot confiscate the machines and claim that the clinical service is therefore complete.

### Control without automatic loyalty

Supremacy gives Wu administrative power. It does not make enhanced soldiers, clone citizens, nonhuman allies, or hospital staff personally loyal to him. Political allegiance still follows the relevant character, formation, or actor system.

Wu can acquire a stronger personal following through actual patronage and dependence already supported by his event. That following may influence a later refusal or conflict. It cannot substitute for the people, facilities, command relationships, and chronology required by a later sovereignty outcome.

The state can counter personal dependence by completing independent service archives, training replacement staff, and preserving public production rights. These measures reduce the actual consequences of Wu's absence. They do not make ordinary doctors capable of his unrecorded discoveries.

### Failure and recovery

The characteristic failure is an efficient medical monopoly that becomes difficult to supervise. Wu may use control of clinical approval to exclude useful critics, delay a competing service, or demand broader powers during an emergency. Each case should show the denied work and the people affected, not a generic increase in hostility.

The government can restore a defined right through the ordinary renegotiation action, fund independent verification, or return to Ordered division after settling the active obligations. It may retain Wu as the principal physician while removing his authority over sample custody or military procurement.

Open retaliation by either institute moves the disputed undertaking into Laboratory conflict. The whole country does not instantly lose its medical protection. Services that remain staffed, supplied, and legally accessible continue. Any wider collapse needs its own causal loss of control or facilities.

The route's durable payoff is a strong, unified medical establishment. Whether that establishment belongs to the public, the military, or Wu personally depends on the actual rights the player has assigned.

## Kruger supremacy

### A medical institute inside the wider Directorate

Kruger absorbs or restricts the Wu laboratories as part of a broader national scientific programme. He sets procurement priorities, controls access to the covered technical sites, and decides which proposed joint work receives his establishment's resources. Wu becomes a subordinate medical director unless he obtains a protected exception.

The route can be attractive when the country's main problem is inadequate machinery, power, transport, or production capacity. Kruger's establishment can bring those real capabilities to Wu's work. The price is a medical programme whose future priorities are increasingly decided outside the clinical service.

It requires an operating Kruger establishment and an actual Wu laboratory or network to place under its authority. Kruger's possession of a research-speed bonus alone does not establish control of the national health system.

The proposed installation takes 120 days, costs 75 Political Power and 200 Support Equipment, and commits two civilian factories. It settles the institutional change and the covered procurement rights. It does not pay for new equipment, complete either scientist's research, or move patients without their normal service and reception arrangements.

### What Kruger gains

Kruger receives the authority to rank new covered proposals, direct the assigned technical staff, and require inspection of the institutional records that the settlement actually transfers. A government can grant him access to specified archives while retaining medical records needed by independent hospitals. It can also keep a public clinical service outside the absorbed research branch.

His authority does not give him Wu's unique ability. An existing Wu method can be maintained by qualified staff with the right records. A new disease or unresolved biological failure still needs an available Wu or another genuinely supported treatment route. A machine-controlled Kruger is not automatically a physician for every disease.

Absorption does not turn every Wu project into an Event 016 family stage. It does not increase Kruger's Project Capacity by adding Wu's lane count. The two systems continue to account for their actual work, and shared physical commitments remain single commitments.

The government retains the ability to approve compatible biological research under explicit supervision. A subordinate medical director may be denied a particular request because its resources are unavailable or its purpose is restricted. That is a current political decision, not a permanent technology gate tied to the supremacy route.

### Wu's five responses

Wu can accept the **subordinate medical post**. He keeps responsibility for diagnosis, treatment, and the medical validity of results, while Kruger controls broader procurement and technical priorities. Existing patients keep their care. Wu's continuing role must remain visible, because the country still depends on him for work that has not become independently reproducible.

He can negotiate a **protected clinical service**. This preserves named hospitals, treatment reserves, and necessary clinical records outside the absorbed experimental branch. The country sacrifices some unified procurement in exchange for medical continuity and an institution that can challenge unsafe assignments.

He can **seek another host** through the existing Wu relocation or foreign-approach route. This part determines what must be settled domestically: current patients, qualified successors, personal archives, actual stock, and the government's claim to institutional methods. The foreign operation itself remains later work and cannot be replaced by an instant random transfer.

He can **conceal a surviving domestic institute**. This requires an actual site, an available qualified team, retained methods or material, and resources not already assigned elsewhere. The concealed branch uses the same country-wide research and modified-standard limits. Its existence cannot create a third standard, another free staff cohort, or a second supply stockpile. Its discovery depends on evidence, not automatic knowledge granted by Kruger's appointment.

He can pursue **continuity preparation**. The route still requires Part 7's dated archive, production, maturation, first settlement, orientation, and individual qualification. Political exclusion supplies a motive. It supplies none of those prerequisites for free.

A Wu who lacks the necessary resources may be unable to complete concealment or continuity. The resulting case can end in continued subordinate service, a negotiated release, or an unresolved dispute. The game must not conjure a hidden laboratory or a mature copy merely to keep the conflict running.

### The procurement dispute

A recurring case places a cure-production order beside a major Kruger technical commitment. The host has enough factories for one new expansion, but current treatment is already supplied. The government can prioritize the cure expansion, prioritize the technical project, or delay both until a smaller independent procurement is ready.

Choosing the technical project does not consume the current treatment reserve a second time. It delays additional production and leaves the existing reserve's real exhaustion date unchanged. Choosing medicine does not destroy Kruger's completed project knowledge. It changes which new paid commitment the country starts.

A more severe version begins after current care has become dependent on a shared device. Kruger wants to redirect that device. The government must first arrange a valid replacement, withdraw the affected service, or maintain the original assignment. Its decision changes the real availability of the device, not merely a displayed priority label.

This is where the subordinate route can become coercive. Wu may refuse to certify a transfer that would leave recipients without care. Kruger may interpret the refusal as an attempt to preserve influence. The government's response should be judged against the actual service facts and the rights already granted.

### Archives and independent medicine

Absorbing a laboratory gives control of the archives physically transferred and the uses lawfully or coercively obtained through the settlement. It does not reveal every private record Wu has ever made. A hidden archive needs an established basis, and an unknown archive must remain unknown until discovered.

Publicly distributed treatment knowledge remains distributed. Kruger cannot revoke a country's permanent learned knowledge by ordering the institute closed. He can restrict future access to his own equipment or services where he holds that authority. Those are different consequences.

The government can establish independent maintenance for a known Wu method through the preceding portfolio. Doing so may weaken Wu's personal leverage and Kruger's ability to redirect the health service. That independent institution does not become another Wu and cannot generate new unique cures without the required expertise.

### Failure and recovery

The characteristic failure is a research establishment that keeps producing impressive technical results while its medical service loses the staff, access, or time needed to use them. A completed device can remain idle because its living component cannot be qualified. A hospital can remain supplied with machinery while lacking the actual treatment it needs.

The player can restore Wu's clinical autonomy for a defined undertaking, fund an independent service, or negotiate a return to Joint institute or Ordered division. A new appointment cannot reset his Authority history, restore lost people, or replay the arrival demonstration.

If a qualified Wu copy appears, the state must determine that individual's post. It can appoint the copy to the protected service, accept a lawful division of responsibilities, or refuse another appointment while preserving the person's existence and ordinary care. Kruger cannot treat the copy as an interchangeable spare physician without qualification and consent to the role.

The route's durable payoff is a medical programme backed by a powerful technical establishment. Its survival depends on whether the government preserves clinical responsibility inside that establishment or allows procurement authority to become unrestricted control over people.

## Laboratory conflict

### A dispute that has become harmful

Laboratory conflict begins when the institutes act against each other's actual work. Possible evidence includes an unauthorized transfer of staff, an unexplained loss of access, a corrupted project record, a misidentified consignment, a damaged installation, or a formation receiving contradictory instructions. These incidents are game-level consequences. Their description must not provide real sabotage or biological methods.

The relationship becomes openly hostile only when the government has a sufficiently grounded reason to identify interference. A single failed experiment does not prove sabotage. A missing staff member does not prove murder. A changed sample label does not establish that a biological release occurred.

The first conflict report names the affected service or project and the immediate problem the player can act on. A ward may be unable to admit patients, a maintenance crew may have been denied entry, or an established formation may have lost its authorized medical provider. The report should show those consequences through the people involved and preserve uncertainty about responsibility when the evidence is incomplete.

### One active case with a real subject

The ordinary rivalry surface should concentrate on one principal conflict case. Other actual incidents retain their owner state and can be summarized as dependencies or handled by their existing categories. The rivalry does not hide an active epidemic simply because its case slot is occupied.

Each case has an affected undertaking, original parties, known facts, unresolved questions, a response objective, and a deadline where the situation requires one. The government can choose investigation, continuity of service, a negotiated separation, or a properly authorized coercive response. It cannot solve every case by buying a reduction in a generic rivalry score.

The proposed normal investigation period is ninety days. A dispute over an unavailable critical control service uses the actual remaining care or control deadline instead. The case cannot reset that deadline to ninety days, and changing the visible relationship cannot restart it.

A successful investigation establishes what happened, who was responsible when the evidence supports that conclusion, and which rights or services need repair. A partial result may restore access without identifying the culprit. An inconclusive result can leave the institutes separated while preserving the unresolved evidence. None of these results should be presented as the same generic success.

### Four forms of immediate harm

**Staff interference** removes a real person or team from an assignment. The institute loses only the service that depended on that assignment. Investigation can locate the person, establish voluntary departure, confirm a valid reassignment, discover coercion, or remain unresolved. A confirmed death is recorded once through its owning system. Recovery of a living person never refunds an earlier fabricated death because no such death should have been recorded.

**Material and archive interference** concerns an actual consignment, physical specimen, or accessible record. A shipment with disputed identity is unavailable for the use that requires reliable identity until the owner resolves it. The game does not assign it the currently preferred strain. A legible archive can preserve knowledge while its missing physical material remains missing.

**Facility interference** blocks or damages a named installation. A country can restore authorized access, carry out the real repair, transfer the service to a prepared site, or close the affected operation. A restored access permit does not repair physical damage. Repairs do not recreate equipment removed from the national stockpile.

**Command interference** gives an existing formation incompatible medical, technical, or military instructions. The safe immediate response is to stop new exceptional assignments and preserve its current care while the command relationship is settled. If its source owner supports a control failure, that owner applies the actual consequence. The rivalry does not create an outbreak or transfer the formation by itself.

### Who may be responsible

Wu, Kruger, a subordinate, a ministry, a commercial partner, or a foreign actor may be responsible when the case has a valid basis. A scientist may also be falsely accused. The case should not default to the defeated scientist whenever the player selects a supremacy route.

Known unauthorized access can justify restricting that access during an investigation. It does not automatically establish guilt for every concurrent failure. A foreign operation already owned by Event 016 keeps its original actor, host, result, and pending response. A domestic investigator cannot rewrite it as Wu's sabotage simply because Wu benefits from the outcome.

Evidence of actual wrongdoing can produce distinct sanctions: removal from a future grant, loss of a specific access right, restitution of physical property when recoverable, public disclosure, a negotiated departure, or an owner-supported custody action. The result must identify what changed and who still owes care or maintenance.

An accusation can be politically convenient without being true. A government that knowingly suppresses valid evidence keeps that conduct in the appropriate history. It cannot erase recorded deaths, incident provenance, or already established responsibility by publishing a preferred explanation.

### Government complicity

The player may protect one scientist's conduct, refuse an investigation, or accept a narrow result while excluding further scrutiny. Such choices can preserve immediate output and deepen dependence on the favored institute. They do not produce a guaranteed technical success or repair the affected programme.

A government that rewards interference can encourage further contested claims when the same staff, material, or authority is again at stake. This is a change in the institutions' incentives. It should be reflected through specific future eligibility and existing causal values, not an invisible perpetual chance to damage unrelated laboratories.

The player can also refuse complicity while retaining the useful scientist. Removing a private claim over one archive need not expel its author. Protecting witnesses need not close every military project. The consequences should remain proportional to the rights and work involved.

### Restoring a working relationship

A negotiated separation uses the existing sixty-day rights action for a small case or the joint institute's 120-day dissolution when a network must be divided. Each active shared service receives a continuing operator or a completed withdrawal. The resulting relationship may be Ordered division, one of the supremacy routes, or separate establishments without current shared work.

The settlement does not remove evidence. A scientist who accepts a new position can still be responsible for an earlier act. A country that later publishes the findings receives the appropriate response from the evidence owner without creating a second incident or another death count.

A restored joint undertaking must address the actual failure. It may require independent custody of a shared record, a replacement team, separate control of clinical admission and procurement, or a protected service contract. Repeating the original charter without changing the disputed dependency does not resolve the case.

### Coercion and the Kruger boundary

The country may protect public hospitals, enforce an already valid access agreement, or stop issuing new national grants through the rivalry system. Those actions do not arrest the canonical Kruger, dismantle his Directorate, create KRG, or take over the host.

When a response requires Kruger's confinement, forced exile, Directorate shutdown, territorial concession, or sovereign settlement, it must use the Event 016 owner's valid route and chronology. The ordinary sovereignty board opens after his fourth evolution. A domestic rivalry case cannot invoke that board early or call its private outcome helper as a free shortcut.

If the owner does not expose a valid early custody route, the case remains a problem of access, protection, negotiated separation, or an explicitly unresolved extension. It must not silently manufacture an early arrest mechanic under another title. The design still includes the requested conflict and its meaningful non-custodial responses.

When the genuine sovereignty board is active, rivalry history can supply factual inputs through an approved bridge. The board remains responsible for its own costs, deadline, deterministic strength comparison, territory plan, character transaction, and final outcome. A rivalry agreement does not guarantee that the stronger scientist obeys.

Kruger's failed territorial separation remains his owner's noncountry laboratory crisis when no valid geography exists. The rivalry cannot solve that problem by seizing arbitrary states or treating a care network as proof of national conquest.

## Cooperation during an outbreak

### An emergency with two indispensable services

A major epidemic can make a continuing feud unaffordable. The government can require a bounded common response when both institutes hold something the response actually needs. Wu may provide the only currently valid treatment. Kruger may provide the machinery, logistics, protective infrastructure, or qualified technical staff needed to deliver it.

This emergency arrangement can occur inside any of the six relationships. It is an overlay for a named crisis, not a seventh permanent relationship and not a reconciliation that erases the conflict.

The proposal first identifies the disease owner, affected states or recipients, the relevant treatment and technical capabilities, actual available stock, and the services each institute can supply. A country with no proven case can prepare cooperation, but cannot claim the emergency's treatment results or force an unrelated scientific concession.

### The common response order

The proposed emergency coordination action takes fifteen days, costs 25 Command Power and 100 Support Equipment, and commits one civilian factory. It appoints the response leads and secures the agreed access. It grants no cure, payload, patients, or treatment reserve.

A thirty-day protected-service operation can run when access or transport is obstructed. Its four-type cost is given in the common action table. The medical deployment itself is then paid and resolved through its existing owner. These are distinct jobs with distinct objectives. The player must not be forced to buy an unnecessary security operation when access already exists.

Wu controls clinical qualification within the response. Kruger controls the technical work that actually belongs to his establishment. Ordinary military command decides how forces protect the sites and routes. Neither scientist can use the emergency appointment to order an unrelated biological attack.

Compulsion depends on real government authority over the required service. A public hospital under effective state control can be assigned an emergency duty. A private installation already beyond that control may refuse, leaving negotiation, replacement, or an existing owner-supported coercive route. Clicking an emergency button does not prove that the state has regained access.

### The objectives that matter

The response mission names a concrete service goal. Examples include delivering an already produced treatment to the selected states, preserving a critical hospital through the next care interval, qualifying a replacement maintenance crew, or completing an accepted owner-specific containment response.

Its deadline follows the actual crisis. A proposed ninety-day coordination objective suits a sustained response whose disease owner permits that period. An existing forty-five-day control-archive emergency retains its remaining time. The response cannot restart the clock when the two scientists finally agree to meet.

Success requires accepted results from the owning systems. Shipping a treatment without a valid recipient does not prove deployment. Having reserve stock without route access does not prove delivery. Repairing machinery without restoring qualified care does not prove patient recovery.

Partial success can preserve the principal hospital while leaving another state untreated, restore one control service while withdrawing a second formation, or deliver a treatment whose source disease still requires sustained recovery. The report should distinguish these outcomes and retain the unresolved obligations.

### Emergency powers with an end

Emergency access expires when the defined task and its immediate handover are complete, or when it becomes invalid. Extending it requires another justified service need and a new explicit decision. The first emergency agreement cannot become permanent supremacy through a hidden flag.

The government can negotiate a lasting settlement afterward. It may reward cooperation with a joint institution, restore separate responsibilities, or hold an investigation into interference that continued during the response. The useful reward is a new institutional arrangement or a properly funded service, not automatic research or free units.

A scientist who supplies essential care can receive recognition for the result. That recognition does not erase evidence that the same institute caused the incident. Cause, response, recovery, and responsibility remain separate historical facts.

### Disease and political limits

Wu can treat living populations and compatible biological conditions. The emergency cannot cure a Rat Nation out of existence, restore dead zombie soldiers, remove a supernatural entity, reverse recorded famine deaths, or repair a temporal system through medicine alone.

If survivors need to move, migration owns the physical transfer and its surviving people. Famine owns the food-safety and food-demand facts. Reconstruction supplies only verified receiving places. New people produced through Part 7 compete for those same places and retain their first-settlement accounting.

An unresolved food-safety projection blocks admission even when both scientists sign the response order. Pre-settlement living cohorts still require their real custody and food-demand record. They must not be disguised as evacuees from an invented origin to fit an existing transfer interface.

The emergency's end leaves the country with the services, survivors, damage, and evidence that actually resulted. It does not return the entire research establishment to a clean starting state.

## Credit, staff, and material after a settlement

### Credit can be shared without sharing everything

Every significant joint or disputed result should retain the actual contribution of each party. Original discovery, technical construction, clinical qualification, independent replication, manufacture, and field service are different contributions. The country can recognize several contributors without granting several copies of the reward.

A publication dispute concerns a particular result. The host can publish the clinical findings needed for treatment while withholding an unrelated military design when its existing rights permit that separation. It can acknowledge a technical contribution without transferring a proprietary machine archive. It cannot label a published method secret again to remove other countries' permanent knowledge.

Recognition can improve the scientist's case for a later appointment or procurement. It cannot substitute for the new work's prerequisites or payment. A fraudulent claim creates consequences when proven. A scientifically useful failure can still preserve knowledge without being presented as a completed capability.

### People do not transfer with a title

A staff settlement distinguishes employed civilians, military personnel, replicated specialists, and independent advisers. Changing the name of their institute does not recreate them or automatically change their service allegiance. A new appointment changes the role the person performs, with the original identity and care history retained.

An ordinary staff cohort can be reassigned through its existing employment or service contract. A named specialist requires a valid vacancy and a qualification matching the new work. Their former specialist benefit ends when the new assignment takes effect. One person cannot keep an active foreign post and provide the same full service at home without a supported arrangement that actually divides the work.

Replicated specialists retain imperfect memories and their own qualification. A copy of an engineer who once worked with Kruger does not possess every current Kruger project. A copy of Wu whose archive predates a new treatment cannot certify that treatment from memory alone. The person needs access and training appropriate to the missing work.

A custody dispute involving a person cannot be settled as an equipment refund. People already admitted through civilian settlement remain part of that population. People already assigned to military manpower or a specific finite formation do not receive another first settlement when transferred between institutes.

### Material must remain identifiable

The two scientists can retain different designs. Only actual compatible material can supply a production order or operation. A custody agreement must distinguish a retained design, an actual specimen, a manufactured item, an assigned service unit, and stock already reserved by another owner.

Part 9's proposed active-standard limit remains country-wide. A public Wu branch, a classified branch, and a continuity branch cannot each claim a separate two-standard allowance. A Kruger-led joint institution operating a Wu-modified standard uses that same allowance. Kruger's unrelated legacy production remains in its own system.

A standard change affects future authorized manufacture. It leaves older material and pending operations unchanged. The familiar example remains valid: 120 items of standard A, followed by a native reservation of 40 A, leave 80 A available. A later production run of 30 B creates 30 B. The pending operation remains an A operation.

A rivalry settlement cannot use those 40 reserved items to pay a second obligation. It cannot instruct the native callback to read the country's new preferred standard. A disputed reservation remains with its operation owner until that owner settles it.

A material transfer requires an actual supported transfer path. A design-sharing agreement cannot override the ordinary biological equipment restrictions on licensing and lend-lease. A capture report cannot claim that all national stock was stored in one seized laboratory without a source that proves that location.

## Independent institutions and several Wus

### The institute after its founders

The country should be able to finish a campaign with useful institutions that survive a scientist's departure. This requires actual independent qualification, accessible methods, continuing equipment, and funded service. It cannot be represented by a generic flag awarded at the end of a rivalry conversation.

An independent hospital can continue a known cure. An independent technical team can maintain a known device. A joint service can survive when both functions have valid successors. Its ability to create new work remains limited by the expertise and material still available.

Kruger's learned operational packages remain permanent under their owner. His personal advisor and scientist roles still move or end only through the valid character lifecycle. The country may lose his personal research benefit while retaining knowledge it already learned. The rivalry cannot use that distinction to cancel his valid appointment while leaving an invisible duplicate of the same benefit.

Wu's retained protocols and distributed treatments follow Parts 2–5. A surviving archive may support known care without providing a qualified Wu capable of new improvisation. A completed continuity person can provide the latter only after the established individual qualification.

### A domestic representative, several real people

When several Wus exist, the country designates the actual representative authorized to negotiate on behalf of each institution. The broad domestic settlement still has one current national arrangement. It may contain defined exceptions for a public hospital or a distinct service, but those exceptions cannot create additional national capacity or production allowances.

A continuity Wu can disagree with the original or another copy. The disagreement should concern a real contract, patient group, archive version, or research direction. It should not be a recurring identity argument detached from gameplay.

Kruger can work with one qualified Wu on a named undertaking and disagree with another Wu over another institution. A successful project does not automatically settle every disagreement. The government can appoint one medical director, retain a separate independent clinic, or require the copies to work under a common public contract.

The appointment changes which individual supplies the service. It does not copy that person's role benefits across the institute. A government that wants a second independent service must provide its own staff, site, stock, and qualification while preserving the existing national limits.

### The original returns

If the original Wu returns after a qualified successor has been appointed, both people remain. The original's identity does not erase the successor's service history, contracts, or legal status. The government can restore the original to a vacant or newly funded role, retain the successor, or divide their work under a revised agreement.

The returning original cannot reclaim physical stock already consumed or reclassify a successor's completed design as his own unfinished research. Shared pre-copy knowledge retains its ancestry. Later work belongs to the actual participants and institutions that produced it.

Kruger's continuity remains governed by his canonical character and owner rules. Wu continuity is not permission to create another `KRG_warren_kruger`, bypass a failed Kruger assassination result, or replace a transformed Kruger with an invented ordinary human.

### Departure and a foreign offer

A departing scientist can leave methods, staff, machinery, and unresolved obligations behind. The departure settlement identifies which of those actually move, which remain, and which cannot be transferred through the available owner interface.

An offer from abroad is not an accomplished transfer. The domestic case can create the motive, disclose a contact, or preserve a pending claim for the later foreign-operation part. It does not produce a destination country, a successful extraction, a second national appointment, or a free overseas institute.

Kruger's current transfer contract excludes an active scientist assignment and a pending cross-domain review. It also applies recipient history restrictions. A departure must use those current checks. Wu's transfer and qualified-copy pathways retain their own rules and do not widen Kruger's eligible pool.

A successful transfer can end the personal domestic rivalry while leaving a material or institutional dispute at home. The old country keeps its actual public rights, learned knowledge, recorded losses, and unfinished service obligations. A newly arrived foreign scientist does not inherit those national rights solely through his name.

## Concrete political consequences

The relationship is communicated through current cases and the existing Authority and Directorate systems. No permanent numeric rivalry bar is added. Hidden bookkeeping may identify a case, party, agreement, or completed obligation, but it must not become another general pressure currency.

Wu Authority changes when a result actually changes Wu's control. The following proposed anchors apply once to the meaningful transfer, not every time the route label is selected.

| Completed change | Proposed Wu Authority change | Boundary |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| State institutions obtain independent approval and maintenance rights previously held solely by Wu | −10 | Requires the real qualification and rights transfer, not an announced audit |
| Wu receives general biological commissioning and access authority from a previously shared arrangement | +10 | Requires completed supremacy access settlement |
| Wu loses that authority to Kruger's wider Directorate | −10 | Medical care and permanent knowledge remain intact |
| Wu obtains a new exceptional power over a previously independent service during an emergency | +5 | A distinct accepted concession with an identified service |
| The state successfully ends that exceptional personal power through a qualified handover | −5 | The same handover cannot repeatedly reduce Authority |
| An independent review exonerates Wu without changing any right | 0 | Findings can change future choices without inventing a control transfer |

The existing Authority bounds remain in force. A clamped change is not stored as a future entitlement. Reversing the same still-owned concession removes only the Authority amount actually applied for that concession, so a ceiling cannot create a reduction exploit. A genuinely different rights settlement can have its own consequence, but alternating empty appointments cannot farm reductions, recognition, or emergency concessions.

Kruger's Mandate, Dependence, Exposure, Independent Capacity, and Grievance must keep their owner's meanings. In particular, the name Independent Capacity does not establish that it is a simple measure of public institutional independence. His current containment calculation uses that value as part of Kruger's ability to resist, while separately represented national replication can strengthen the government's case.

This part therefore proposes **case-specific factual inputs** to Event 016: an actual access restriction, a completed independent replication, an imposed loss of a role, a protected technical service, an exposed wrongdoing, or a shared undertaking whose dependence has changed. The Kruger owner must define and accept the corresponding causal update. No new direct numerical Kruger delta is asserted here without that contract.

That integration gap is explicit. It does not prevent designing the six routes, their costs, or their outcomes. It prevents an implementation from guessing at hidden meter semantics or calling a legacy Event 089 technology-sharing effect to imitate a new rivalry consequence.

## Pacing and visible management

A stable arrangement should be quiet while its agreements are being kept. Ordinary research in unrelated fields does not generate rivalry pressure. A newly completed project can provide evidence or create a shared need without automatically opening a dispute.

A proposed sixty-day interval separates nonurgent relationship reports for the same host. A new failure threatening actual patients or control can interrupt that interval. The interface should not display every possible route as a permanent tray of buttons.

One principal rivalry case and one comparative or joint objective can be active alongside an emergency obligation when needed. An emergency can suspend new ordinary cases. Existing disease, migration, or Kruger missions remain in their own categories and are referenced truthfully, not copied into new independently resolving missions.

The relationship uses the existing institute presentation and qualitative state. It does not add a standalone spirit for every arrangement or allow six simultaneous route modifiers. ## AI choices and review scenarios

AI uses the same resource, source, care, and authority conditions as the player. It must first reject impossible actions, then compare the useful valid alternatives. It cannot compensate for missing equipment, an occupied scientist, or an unsupported transfer with a high choice weight.

Ordered division should be favored when institutions already hold independent rights, the country cannot afford two parallel bids, or continuity of current care matters more than rapid expansion. Competition should be favored when two distinct approaches are actually available, the host can fund both, and the announced objective can distinguish their results. A Joint institute should be favored when complementary capabilities already support a useful undertaking and their continuing services can be maintained.

Wu supremacy should be favored by a government that needs unified medical control and accepts the corresponding concentration of power. Kruger supremacy should be favored when technical capacity is the main constraint and a protected medical service can remain viable. These are contextual preferences, not ideology-locked outcomes.

The AI should not deliberately enter Laboratory conflict to obtain a supposed reward. It responds to the actual dispute and any source-supported hostile conduct. During a serious outbreak, it prioritizes continuing treatment and valid protection over new competitive work, while keeping existing evidence and rights in view.

## Three connected campaign examples

### A public hospital and a useful machine

A player-controlled country employs both scientists while the world remains in Calm World. Its university has rights to an established treatment, and Wu operates a hospital that can maintain it. Kruger has the relevant operational machine knowledge but no spare installation ready for that hospital.

The country selects Ordered division. The hospital keeps its current patients and public method. The state later authorizes a bounded joint undertaking to manufacture and commission the appropriate Part 8 service. The machine's owner receives its actual payment, and the medical service keeps its own qualification and care obligations.

After the undertaking succeeds, the country can retain the limited contract or establish a Joint institute for repeated cooperation. A full joint charter is not required merely to make the first machine possible. Independent engineering staff are then qualified through actual work, allowing the established service to survive Kruger's departure if his owner later permits that departure.

The result is a stronger hospital with a real machine and trained operators. It is not a newly created hospital, a repeated technology reward, or another copy of Kruger's research anchor. The public university's rights remain visible in a later supremacy negotiation.

### A contest, two Wus, and unchanged stock

A larger country funds two distinct approaches to a clinical support problem. One qualified Wu directs a medical service while another qualified copy works at a separately funded branch. Kruger supplies the technical alternative. The two Wus share the same national research-lane and modified-standard allowance.

The first useful result receives provisional commissioning. Its actual stock is standard A. A native operation later reserves forty of 120 A items. During the final comparison, the government prefers another qualified design and changes future manufacture to B through the paid retooling process.

The relationship subsequently changes to Kruger supremacy. That political change leaves the eighty unreserved A items, forty reserved A items, and thirty newly produced B items distinct. It does not create another allowance for the subordinate Wu branch. The original operation cannot consume a newly preferred profile by reading the changed national appointment.

One Wu accepts a protected medical post. The other seeks an independent role. Their existing population records and care remain intact. The country must settle their appointments and actual service capacity, not choose which person is real and delete the other.

### A feud during a genuine outbreak

Laboratory conflict has interrupted a shared control service. Forty-five days remain before its established safe continuation expires, and the ordinary transfer of service responsibility would take ninety days. At the same time, a disease owner reports a real epidemic affecting several states.

The government can protect access to the existing service, employ a genuinely qualified interim custodian, or withdraw the affected formation safely before its actual deadline. It cannot shorten the handover by inventing qualifications, and the outbreak does not restart the forty-five-day clock.

For the epidemic, the state commissions a bounded common response. Wu provides the actual treatment and Kruger supplies the technical service his establishment can support. Their coordination consumes its own administrative resources, while treatment manufacture, delivery, and accepted recovery remain with the medical and disease owners.

A reconstruction district can receive 5,000 people under current shelter, food, medical, and transport proof. The state assigns 1,000 places to survivors, 2,500 to migrants, and 1,500 to finite new citizens. Another 500 newly produced people remain unsettled in actual custody, with continuing food and care needs. The joint institute cannot count those people as evacuated from a fictional origin or add another 500 places through its charter.

The immediate response may succeed while the sabotage investigation remains unresolved. Survivors remain alive, actual deaths remain recorded, and neither scientist receives an automatic moral or political pardon. The government can negotiate a durable settlement after the emergency using the rights and results that now exist.

## Completion of an agreement

A successful agreement changes the specified rights and closes its own temporary commitments. It leaves unrelated work alone. Direct preparation for the new administrative actions in this part is spent at selection and is not refunded on an interrupted agreement. Native temporary factory commitments end through their own action lifecycle. Existing projects and operations retain their own distinct refund rules.

An objective cannot complete from historical work done before it began unless the objective explicitly recognizes an already earned right. A ninety-day comparative service trial requires the announced service interval after its commission. A handover requires an accepted current operator. A settlement requires the actual outstanding obligations to be assigned or withdrawn.

An old timer cannot release a factory commitment belonging to a later agreement, overwrite a newer national relationship, or replay a completed credit decision. Identity must include the host, undertaking, parties, and specific agreement instance. Missing or mismatched identity blocks the new result and preserves already recorded history.

Finite people, native operations, and provider-owned formations retain their original transaction owners. A successful domestic settlement can request a supported change, but it cannot act as a generic transfer, recovery, or cancellation API. Any required new bridge must state what it accepts and returns, with its acceptance and implementation evidence recorded separately.

The main unresolved bridges for this part are cross-owner biological access, shared physical capacity and staff assignment, case-specific Kruger causal updates, exact care-preserving handovers, identity-preserving formation changes, and a domestic joint-service contract distinct from the foreign joint-laboratory framework. The design requires these capabilities. It does not substitute a global bonus, duplicated project, early sovereignty call, or free resource grant for them.

All compatible Wu research remains ordinary baseline work, reachable through real prerequisites and explicit permission in Calm World. This includes bounded joint work without adopting the full Joint institute relationship. Existing non-biological or terminal Kruger projects keep their own access rules. Wu's medical route cannot be used to bypass those unrelated rules.

The four Wu evolutions remain National Medical Ascendancy at 200, International Biological Contest at 400, Forbidden and Autonomous Biology at 600, and Sovereign Biology at 800. Their detailed parts still need contextual MTTH, active-chain and evolved-opening forms, and zero activation Chaos. A grant award, institutional merger, supremacy settlement, or laboratory dispute is not an extra evolution.

## What the domestic settlement sends abroad

A domestic agreement leaves concrete interests that foreign governments can pursue: a qualified scientist seeking a post, a hospital needing continued machinery, a published method available for independent manufacture, a proprietary archive, an unpaid care obligation, or evidence of interference. The next part can build foreign competition around those actual people, services, records, and rights.

The country's existing relationship determines what can be offered and who can consent. A foreign invitation cannot move an active Kruger, a cure contract cannot transfer every Wu archive, and a stolen record cannot create the material it describes. Foreign pressure should exploit or repair the domestic dependencies already established here, with its own real access, costs, outcomes, and responsibility.
