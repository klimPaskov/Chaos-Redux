# Event 32 research notes

## Purpose

These references support the design of launch-site survivability, dispersed command, maintenance pressure, false warning incidents, two-person control, and backup command paths. They are design references. They do not require Event 32 to reproduce a modern real-world arsenal or doctrine.

## Hardened and dispersed launch sites

United States Air Force material on Minuteman systems describes dispersed missiles in hardened silos, underground launch control centers, hardened communications, and backup airborne command. This supports four Event 32 mechanics:

- launch sites should be distributed when a country has enough territory
- site hardening should reduce strike and capture risk
- one destroyed command center should not automatically remove every missile
- alternate command paths should improve survivability while increasing the complexity of control

Sources:

- Air Force Nuclear Weapons Center, `Minuteman III (LGM-30G)`, https://www.afnwc.af.mil/Weapon-Systems/Minuteman-III-LGM-30G.aspx
- Offutt Air Force Base, `ALCS celebrates 50 years`, https://www.offutt.af.mil/News/Article/1206767/alcs-celebrates-50-years/
- United States Air Force, `Whiteman home to Cold War reminder`, https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/125199/whiteman-home-to-cold-war-reminder/

## Command authentication and crew control

Air Force historical material describes two-person launch crews, authentication codes, hardware safeguards, recurring training, and continuous monitoring. This supports the Command Control value and the Secure Launch Authority decision family.

## Maintenance and readiness

Air Force maintenance reporting describes launch control systems as dependent on routine maintenance, repair, security, and continuous operating support. This supports a readiness value that falls after launches, site damage, captured infrastructure, and maintenance neglect.

Sources:

- Air Force Global Strike Command, `Maintaining Malmstrom's launch control centers`, https://www.afgsc.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2015641/maintaining-malmstroms-launch-control-centers/
- Air Force Global Strike Command, `Minot's Wing Operations Center serves as nucleus for security, maintenance missions`, https://www.afgsc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/629454/minots-wing-operations-center-serves-as-nucleus-for-security-maintenance-missio/

## False warnings and redundant verification

A 1985 United States Government Accountability Office report describes false warning events, incompatible computers, hardware failure, maintenance problems, and redundant systems in tactical warning and attack assessment. This supports the Automatic Retaliation track's verification windows, false signals, independent confirmation paths, and emergency network isolation.

Source:

- United States Government Accountability Office, `Tactical Warning and Attack Assessment System`, https://www.gao.gov/products/127994

## Design conclusions

The research supports the following Event 32 rules:

1. Site survivability and command survivability are related but separate.
2. Redundancy protects against decapitation while increasing the number of places that can be compromised.
3. Maintenance and training should change readiness and failure risk.
4. Automatic retaliation must include verification, communication failure, and false-warning risks.
5. Strong command safeguards can reduce unauthorized launch risk without eliminating capture, bribery, sabotage, or forged-signal incidents.
6. The player should be able to choose central control, delegated survivability, or automation after the relevant evolution.
7. Every backup route should carry a tradeoff. Survivability without added command risk would make one posture universally correct.
