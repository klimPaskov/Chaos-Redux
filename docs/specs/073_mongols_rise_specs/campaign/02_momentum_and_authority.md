# Momentum and Authority

## Two different pressures

Momentum describes the army's ability to turn success into the next campaign. Authority describes whether the empire's leaders and territories accept the central settlement. Both are visible on a zero-to-one-hundred scale. The player can understand one without mistaking it for the other.

Supply, khan loyalty, and tribute obligations are shown as concrete conditions and records. They do not become additional public meters. A khan is loyal, bargaining, withholding, or in revolt for stated reasons. A route is connected, contested, or cut. A tribute payment is due, paid, deferred, or in arrears.

## Momentum gains

Momentum rises from meaningful first-time campaign achievements. Examples are taking and holding the current strategic objective, completing a regional settlement, defeating a substantial opponent, relieving an encircled imperial force, or reopening a blocked supply route that mattered to the active campaign.

A victory's importance is measured against the empire and its opponent when the campaign began. A small frontier success helps early Mongolia more than an empire that already holds several regional capitals. A scripted claim or an unopposed diplomatic renaming is not a battlefield victory.

Gains happen once for the relevant achievement. Recapturing a farmable objective, transferring a state between friendly countries, or repeatedly releasing and reconquering a khanate cannot restore Momentum indefinitely.

## Momentum decay

The opening includes a short grace period so a human can organize the army and choose a target. During war, a long period without qualifying progress gradually lowers Momentum. A serious defeat lowers it sooner. Active supply disruption and a poor equipment reserve make a stalled campaign more damaging.

Peace alone does not force a collapse. In peace, Momentum settles toward a modest readiness level. Planned consolidation can protect that readiness while roads, equipment reserves, and obligations are repaired. It does not keep the army permanently at its highest offensive strength.

The player sees the recent cause of a change and the present direction. They should not need to reverse-engineer a hidden monthly roll to understand why the horde has slowed.

## How Momentum affects the army

At high Momentum, mounted formations exploit breakthroughs faster, recover more effectively between battles, and draw stronger benefits from offensive command. At low Momentum, those temporary advantages weaken. The army keeps its permanent defensive disadvantages.

Momentum does not secretly give every cavalry division unlimited movement supply. The campaign's logistics condition can support a country-level or unit-category benefit that has been verified in the engine. Its description must match that actual scope. A general campaign bonus must never claim to apply only while a particular division is advancing unless that condition truly exists.

## Authority gains and costs

Authority grows when the center honors settlements, appoints acceptable regional rulers, resolves succession, and demonstrates that it can protect the empire. Successful campaigns support it, but conquest without a workable settlement can leave Authority unchanged or even reduce it.

Authority is damaged by unpaid promises, repeated demands beyond a tributary's means, loss of imperial centers, arbitrary revocation of khanate rights, and a contested succession. A Council government is especially sensitive to violating negotiated rights. A personal Great Khan suffers more when defeats undermine the claim that only his command holds the empire together.

Spending political power on a repeated button is not a substitute for repairing the underlying dispute. Political power may pay for a conference, appointment, or administrative effort. The recovery comes from its actual outcome.

## The relationship between the meters

High Momentum and low Authority describe an army winning faster than the state can govern. The player can press onward and risk rebellious rear areas, or settle obligations before the next war.

Low Momentum and high Authority describe an empire able to survive a military pause. It can replenish equipment, shorten the frontier, hold a kurultai, and try again.

Low values in both create a dangerous crisis. A collapse still needs a concrete trigger and a response period. Meter thresholds provide warning and change willingness. They are not a substitute for a visible political event.

## Relief and recovery

A relief campaign, a successful negotiated settlement, or an orderly withdrawal can halt a downward cycle. The empire is allowed to survive a defeat. Recovery costs territory, obligations, time, or resources. It does not restore all losses through an emergency spawn.

The political branches alter the way recovery works. The Heir calls on personal loyalty and remaining tumen obligations. The New Great Khan relies on institutions and reserve planning. The Council renegotiates its commitments. All three can recover, and each gives up something different.
