# Events, news, and super-events

## Narrative approach

The event's world is a twentieth-century restoration built around an older imperial identity. The army is deliberately extraordinary. Its ability to threaten modern states is a gameplay premise, not a historical claim about cavalry superiority over tanks.

Political texts should make promises and conflicts concrete. A khan disputes a territory, an obligation, or a succession. A tributary reports a shortfall. A commander asks for equipment or authority. Avoid interchangeable declarations about destiny that say nothing about the player's actual decision.

The Mongolian people and conquered populations remain human societies with different interests. Imperial propaganda can claim universal obedience. The surrounding narrative must not treat that claim as an objective description of every population.

All labels below are planning identifiers. Final titles, descriptions, buttons, quotes, and cultural remarks require the appropriate writing and source checks.

## News milestones

News can announce the restoration, recognition of the Great Khan, restoration of the Yassa, Karakorum's completed center, the first revived khanate, the Beijing settlement, a Golden Horde settlement, a meaningful European foothold, universal proclamation, and imperial collapse.

Each milestone has one recorded occurrence for its relevant campaign identity. A recaptured city does not replay the same world news. A minor border reversal does not announce the collapse of a continental empire. News wording names actual actors and the settlement that happened.

## Super-event SE-A: the restored imperial center

**Purpose.** Establish that the restoration has become a functioning imperial project beyond its opening muster.

**Trigger.** The restoration is accepted, the Great Khan's governing settlement exists, and the empire completes its first substantial regional settlement or the developed Karakorum center. It fires once. A barely surviving opening army alone is not enough.

**Tone and image.** Formal and martial, with a recognizable mounted imperial command and the twentieth-century setting still visible. The image must not imply that a researched real person attended an invented event unless presented clearly as alternate history.

**Text research.** Find a verified quotation or a documented source passage suitable for the relationship between command, service, and empire. Establish exact wording, attribution, translation, and rights. A phrase that sounds Mongolian or medieval is not a source.

**Audio direction.** Controlled ceremonial and mounted-military character. Research a usable recording and its license. Cultural instruments or throat-singing should not be selected merely as a stereotype. Final length follows the owning skill's normal one-to-two-minute range and stays within 120 seconds without an approved exception.

## Super-event SE-B: universal proclamation

**Purpose.** Mark a late continental political achievement.

**Trigger.** Evolution III is active, the universal route is completed, and its distinct regional, institutional, and Authority requirements are satisfied. Crossing Chaos 600 alone does not fire it.

**Tone and image.** An expansive imperial assembly or command scene with clear regional participation and an authoritative focal point. Avoid a modern infographic or an unverified historical map presented as current territory.

**Text research.** Research universal rule and imperial authority through a specific credible source. Final wording must describe the alternate campaign's actual proclamation and distinguish propaganda from narration.

**Audio direction.** Broader ceremonial scale than SE-A without requiring an unlicensed commercial soundtrack. Recording rights, composition rights, attribution, and redistribution conditions all need review.

## Super-event SE-C: the continental empire breaks

**Purpose.** Mark the failure of a previously substantial empire and the survival of its successors.

**Trigger.** An empire with several completed regional settlements and established imperial institutions fragments into multiple meaningful successors or loses its central actor after a major crisis. A small early restoration that fails in its first war uses ordinary events and news instead.

**Tone and image.** Political separation and a broken command structure, with readable regional banners or an abandoned imperial setting. Avoid graphic spectacle as a substitute for the actual political outcome.

**Text research.** Find a verified source relevant to division, succession, or the limits of command. Do not invent a famous quotation or convert a working achievement title into a cultural remark.

**Audio direction.** A restrained aftermath treatment from a rights-cleared source. It should allow the player to recognize the outcome without falsely presenting a modern copyrighted film cue as reusable.

## Release gate

Unresearched final text, uncertain quotation attribution, unclear recording rights, missing final images, or missing runtime wiring are blockers. Planning directions are not shipping localisation. The development package may show clearly labeled placeholders during review, but the event cannot be marked complete while those placeholders remain.
