# Karakorum and the Silk Roads

## A useful imperial center

Karakorum begins as a practical restoration project. The first stage secures the site and establishes command facilities. The second develops workshops and transport. The third creates a functioning imperial administrative center. A final permanent capital move remains a political choice.

Each stage has an actual cost and physical result. A command stage can support appointments and campaign coordination. Workshops improve the country's ability to maintain support equipment through real facilities or a narrowly defined economic effect. Transport work connects the site to a valid existing network.

The same building level is never added beyond the installed game's limit. A fully developed site receives a relevant alternative improvement or counts as already meeting that part of the project. It does not generate a building in an unrelated state as compensation.

## The capital choice

The map binding must distinguish Karakorum's actual site from Ulaanbaatar and from a broad Mongolian state that may contain both. A state-level capital effect cannot be advertised as an exact provincial relocation unless the engine and map setup support it.

The desired experience is a real permanent move to Karakorum with valid government continuity. If this requires an additional map or victory-point change, that work is a separate approved implementation decision. Until then, the command-center stages remain a valid design, but the capital-move requirement is explicitly blocked instead of silently substituted.

The player can keep the current practical capital while preserving Karakorum's symbolic and military role. The New Great Khan route makes this especially natural. The Heir has stronger political reasons to move. The Council considers how the choice affects regional access and representation.

## Construction under pressure

Work slows or pauses when the site is lost, the economy cannot support the project, or the required route is cut. An occupied site cannot continue receiving safe construction as if nothing happened. The player can protect it, evacuate the government where necessary, or defer the final stage.

A major restoration milestone receives visual and news treatment once. Repeated loss and recovery does not replay the full ceremony or its rewards.

## A network of route sections

The Silk Roads are a finite network of reviewed geographical sections. The first section links Mongolia to the current campaign. Later sections can connect China, Central Asia, Persia, the western steppe, and a southern frontier according to actual geography.

Each section identifies its endpoints, required territorial or access conditions, relevant transport infrastructure, and parties responsible for it. A chain works only when all required sections work. Friendly ownership alone is not proof of a usable railway or sufficient supply.

## State logistics and negotiated commerce

The player can develop direct logistics infrastructure or use agreements with khanates and tributaries. Direct infrastructure offers greater control at a higher cost. Agreements can be cheaper and more flexible but depend on the partner's rights and stability.

A functioning network can improve construction, transport efficiency, access to usable resources, and the economy that supports the army. Exact game effects must reflect verified mechanics. The design does not invent a tradable stockpile for a resource that the game models through production and trade access.

## Broken routes

A cut section suspends benefits that require it. Local sections that still function can retain their local benefits. A break in Central Asia should not erase an unrelated functioning Mongolian workshop, but it can end the continental network bonus and prevent a western khanate from delivering support.

The player sees the cause and available remedies: recapture an anchor, repair infrastructure, negotiate transit, or build a reviewed alternative. A recovery mission concerns the actual broken section. It is not a generic button that restores the whole network regardless of control.

## The economic capstone

A late connected-empire settlement requires several functioning sections and dependable regional institutions. It provides the third and final ongoing event-owned national spirit if that slot is not already occupied by its earlier stage. It does not add a separate spirit for each route section.

A stable network supports a lasting empire and creates a useful reason to consolidate between wars. It does not remove the defensive weakness of the cavalry or make a fragmented empire economically whole.
