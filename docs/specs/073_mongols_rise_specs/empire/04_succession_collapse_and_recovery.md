# Succession, collapse, and recovery

## A vacancy in the office

A succession begins after a valid vacancy in the Great Khan's office. The vacancy can follow a supported character death or removal event, a political abdication, or the failure of an existing leadership arrangement. It is not an arbitrary recurring execution of the ruler to keep the system busy.

Real historical characters require consistent identity and lifecycle treatment. The design must not present an unsupported death date or fictitious genealogy as fact. A caretaker institution can keep the country functioning until the succession is resolved.

## The kurultai

The kurultai presents the actual claimants or the eligible institutional nomination process. Support depends on prior promises, military achievements, honored obligations, political route, and the relative position of regional khans. It does not create a dozen invisible loyalty variables for the player to manage individually.

A prepared succession can resolve quickly. A disputed succession has a visible response period during which the center can confirm an heir, negotiate a compromise, recognize a council choice, or challenge a claimant. The army remains on the map. The country does not lose every order or active war merely because a political meeting opened.

A human khanate or claimant receives the choices that belong to it. AI support is recalculated from the actual arrangement and cannot be faked through one universal chance of loyalty.

## Peaceful transfer

A peaceful transfer preserves the empire's earned institutions, legitimate technology, surviving troops, and active agreements that remain valid. It updates office-specific command effects and the role of personal cavalry. The new ruler does not receive the opening muster or recreate every khanate.

A successor may owe concessions made during the kurultai. Those concessions become real obligations. They are not forgotten as soon as the event closes.

## Strain before collapse

Three visible conditions can place the empire under strain: a serious unresolved succession, a military defeat combined with weak central authority, or the loss of essential imperial territory and routes. Momentum and Authority influence severity, but a low meter alone does not instantly fragment the map.

The first warning identifies the problem. The player can honor an overdue promise, shorten an exposed frontier, organize a relief campaign, recognize a successor, or negotiate regional autonomy. A response must address the cause. An unrelated political-power purchase cannot cancel the crisis.

## Defiance

An unresolved crisis can lead a khan to withhold troops, suspend tribute, dispute a succession, or request revised terms. Withholding is a political action, not the deletion of that country's army. A loyal but disconnected khanate is marked unable to deliver rather than automatically rebellious.

Several simultaneous disputes can form a rival league or rival imperial claim. The rival has actual members and territories. A human country decides whether to join. The center can accept a looser settlement or attempt coercion under the game's legal war rules.

## Fragmentation

Fragmentation resolves the actual territorial and diplomatic situation. Existing khanates can become independent or join a rival compact. Directly governed regions require a viable successor arrangement before release. No state is transferred from an unrelated third party simply to make a cleaner-looking map.

Each successor keeps a viable capital, real surviving forces, appropriate inherited research, its assigned economy, and regional goals. Armies already belonging to a khanate remain its armies. Splitting the central army requires explicit allocation without copying units or crediting the same equipment twice.

Former tributaries review their agreements. An old central recipient cannot keep collecting after it no longer exists or controls the relationship. Shared route benefits end where their conditions fail.

## Loss of Mongolia

Loss of the homeland or imperial capital is a severe emergency. A surviving government with valid territory can evacuate or establish a temporary seat through the existing country rules. The event does not create a landless government and then spawn troops in an enemy capital.

A government that cannot preserve a viable territorial basis may cease to be the central imperial actor. Surviving khanates continue as countries. Their existence is not conditional on Mongolia remaining alive forever.

## Recovery choices

A surviving Mongolia can build a smaller stable state, negotiate a new league, or fight a selective reunification campaign. Each former khanate has its own willingness and interests. Recognition of a new compact can restore selected common institutions without regranting the old army.

A military reunification follows current ownership and actual wars. It does not restore pre-collapse borders through one universal annexation effect. A negotiated reunification can leave wide autonomy in return for renewed routes and obligations.

## Meaningful outcomes

The campaign can end with a stable regional empire, a continental empire, an organized confederation, a smaller recovered Mongolia, several independent khanates, or a rival imperial center. These outcomes arise from the same campaign systems.

Even a continental collapse leaves a world with countries that can continue playing.
