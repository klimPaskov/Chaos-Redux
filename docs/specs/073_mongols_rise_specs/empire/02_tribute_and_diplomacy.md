# Tribute and imperial diplomacy

## What tribute means

A tribute agreement is a limited contract with an actual country. It identifies the payer, recipient, terms, duration or review point, and consequences of default. Independence, subject status, access, military participation, and nonaggression are separate terms.

A country can accept tribute to avoid an immediate invasion, but the empire is bound by the stated bargain. Attacking a compliant tributary without the required rupture damages Authority and its credibility with other partners. Tribute does not promise protection from every unrelated war unless the agreement includes that protection.

## Choosing an obligation

The normal agreement selects one main contribution and at most one supporting term. Equipment is the most direct contribution. A manpower contribution requires a verified transfer that debits an actual available donor reserve. A military contingent requires actual owned formations or a fully accounted muster. Transit requires real access. Resource terms use a supported trade or subject arrangement rather than an invented resource stockpile.

The payer's economy, usable equipment reserve, existing war commitments, and relationship determine the offer. The empire cannot demand more than the country can plausibly provide and then treat unavoidable failure as a profitable excuse for repeated invasion rewards.

For a human target, the offered terms are explicit. The player can inspect the payment, interval, review conditions, and military consequences. For AI, acceptance reflects the cost of payment against the cost and likelihood of resistance.

## Delivery

Recurring payment runs only for active agreements. Equipment is removed from the donor before the same amount is credited to the receiver, within one guarded transaction. A failed or invalid transfer pays neither side a duplicate amount. The agreement stores what was actually delivered.

A country that loses its capital, is annexed, or becomes unable to operate does not continue making fictional payments. A change of owner or diplomatic relationship triggers review. The empire cannot collect an old agreement from both a defeated country and its successor.

## Arrears and revision

A missed payment first produces a stated shortfall. The payer can request deferral or reduced terms. The center can accept a temporary concession, ask for another available contribution, or insist on the original bargain.

A temporary inability caused by war is different from deliberate refusal. The center's response affects Authority and the willingness of other partners to cooperate. Repeated impossible demands should make the empire less trusted, not provide a reliable conquest accelerator.

A broken agreement can open an enforcement campaign. The player sees the actual relationship change and legal war route. Enforcement does not bypass faction protection or automatically annex the country.

## Khanate obligations

Khanates use the same accountable delivery principles but also have political rights and regional responsibilities. A joint campaign obligation identifies an objective and a contribution. The center cannot change the objective after receiving troops without reopening the bargain where the agreement requires it.

Council governments negotiate obligations more openly. Personal governments rely more on service expectations. Administrative governments favor predictable schedules and written responsibilities. The differences affect play without requiring three separate payment engines.

## Counterplay

A threatened neighbor can seek support through existing diplomacy, strengthen its defended approaches, reject the demand, or accept a limited arrangement while preparing for a later review. There is no new global containment currency.

A coalition opposing the empire uses existing war and faction relationships. Event content can explain a common threat and offer appropriate diplomatic actions, but it cannot promise alliances that the underlying game does not allow.

## Ending an agreement

An agreement can expire, be replaced, end through a negotiated release, or break during war or collapse. Its benefits and obligations end together. Territory is not automatically returned unless the actual agreement says so and a legal transfer is possible.

Former tributaries remain countries with their own interests. Some may accept a new relationship with a successor khanate. Others may resist. Their choice is based on current power and terms, not an old global flag that binds them forever.
