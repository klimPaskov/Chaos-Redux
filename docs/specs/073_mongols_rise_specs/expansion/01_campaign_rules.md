# Campaign and settlement rules

## A campaign is a limited commitment

Each campaign begins with a selected geographical objective and the country or countries that currently control it. Its offer explains the expected alliance exposure, route into the region, principal military obstacle, and available settlements. A historical name does not override current ownership.

The player can prepare more than one direction, but active preparations and operations share a small campaign capacity that grows with command institutions and functioning supply. This limits interface burden and prevents every remote war goal from appearing at once. It does not prevent a human from managing ordinary wars outside the scripted campaign system.

## Demands before invasion

Where submission is possible, the target receives a demand with a response interval. It can accept a limited tribute agreement, offer another settlement, or refuse. The selected terms are displayed before commitment. Strong allies, existing wars, distance, and the target's ability to resist affect its willingness.

A refusal opens the relevant legal conquest route. It does not guarantee an automatic declaration against an overlord, a faction, or a country with which the empire cannot legally fight. A political or diplomatic block remains visible and receives a valid next step.

A demand can fail. The empire may withdraw it at a political cost, continue preparing, or choose another front. It cannot demand tribute from a remote state repeatedly until a random acceptance appears.

## Objectives and occupation

Military control of an objective opens the occupation phase. Political ownership, postwar rights, or an accepted agreement determine whether a permanent settlement can complete. Temporary occupation does not authorize the empire to give away another country's unceded states.

A campaign can include a narrow military objective and a broader optional ambition. Securing the narrow objective allows a useful outcome even when a total conquest would be costly. The player's options should distinguish a frontier settlement from full regional domination.

## Three settlement forms

Direct rule keeps the relevant territory under the empire's government. It offers control and access to resources while requiring occupation, administration, and transport. Cores follow a separately justified integration rule. Medieval conquest is not sufficient reason to make all of Eurasia a Mongolian core.

A khanate places an actual regional country under an agreed imperial relationship. It receives a viable capital, territory, institutions, a sustainable military basis, and its regional content. Its forces and tribute are obligations from a real actor, not duplicate copies of the empire's grant.

A tributary keeps its country and pays the limited obligations it accepted. Access, nonaggression, subject status, and military participation are separate terms. Tributary status does not silently imply all of them.

## Held achievements

A first-time objective is recorded only after it meets the campaign's hold and settlement conditions. Losing an objective before completion cancels or pauses its progress. Regaining it resumes the same objective, not a new reward transaction.

A major regional settlement can provide Momentum, Authority, a spoils allocation, and access to a follow-up path. The actual economic and military value is distributed once across those choices. The reward system must not also pay an equivalent generic special grant for the same conquest.

## Reachability

Land campaigns require a usable approach. A route through a friendly country requires actual access and workable supply. A maritime campaign requires a real transport plan and the game's naval conditions. Universal claims do not remove these requirements.

The state puzzle shows the territory that the selected settlement actually requires. It distinguishes owned, occupied, allied-access, khanate-held, and missing pieces when those distinctions matter. Its final qualification matches the formation decision and AI.

## After completion

Completing a region opens administration, further campaign options, and a possible dispute based on the chosen settlement. It does not end regional content. The empire may need to rebuild industry, secure a route, revise a tribute obligation, settle a succession, or respond to a neighboring power's resistance.

The regional files describe different strategic problems and outcomes. They are not seven copies of a generic annexation reward.
