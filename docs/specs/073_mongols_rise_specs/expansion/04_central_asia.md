# Central Asian khanates

## Strategic purpose

Central Asia links Mongolia to the western and southern campaigns. Its value is a chain of viable approaches, regional partners, and transport agreements. It also creates disputes over routes and the authority of regional khans.

The root requires a real approach through the current frontier or a valid access agreement. The campaign can begin before China is fully settled. It must not require traversing hostile territory without supply merely because a historical map connects two labels.

## First fork

A northern approach emphasizes a connected land corridor and larger steppe operations. A southern oasis approach emphasizes transport nodes, local settlements, and a possible route toward Persia or India. The exact required states follow the installed map, not an invented line drawn for a diagram.

The player can seek a narrow transit compact or a larger territorial settlement. A compact may be cheaper and faster but can be revoked or disrupted. A territorial campaign gives more control and a greater administrative burden.

## The Chagatai settlement

A major success opens a Chagatai-style khanate or direct regional government. The khanate's main role is maintaining the central corridor and mediating local interests. Its mounted army can secure approaches and support a regional campaign. It is not a free duplicate of Mongolia's opening horde.

The khan receives a defined territory and obligations. A route agreement, equipment payment, and troop contingent are alternatives or negotiated combinations within the obligation budget. The region cannot promise a railway it does not control.

## Political choices

The center can give the khan wide responsibility for route administration or retain direct oversight of key anchors. Wide responsibility reduces the center's burden and strengthens the khan's political position. Direct oversight gives the center more control and creates more occasions for friction.

A dispute over transit rights is settled through an actual adjustment of access, territory, or obligations. Paying political power alone does not make a permanently hostile route safe.

## Capstone and follow-up

The capstone is a settled, functioning link between the Mongolian core and a western or southern frontier. It opens the Persia route, the Rus route, or a southern campaign according to geography. The player chooses which next campaign receives the region's support.

After completion, continuing content includes route repair, mediation of a regional border dispute, a khan's request for a larger role, and participation in succession. A successful khan can become a major supporter of the center or a strong rival after a failed continental war.

## Opposition and recovery

The main danger is overextension along a narrow corridor. Enemies can cut the connection, force the mounted army to fight for defended nodes, or separate Mongolia from a western khanate. Desert benefits do not make supply irrelevant.

Recovery can involve a shorter route, a negotiated transit agreement, or giving the khan more autonomy in exchange for restoring the connection. The player can preserve a useful regional partner without reconquering every lost province.

AI favors a connected limited success before attempting a distant war. It does not choose a route solely because it has the largest theoretical territorial reward.
