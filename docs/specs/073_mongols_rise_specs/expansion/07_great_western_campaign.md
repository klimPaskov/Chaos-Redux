# The Great Western Campaign

## Opening the route

The western route opens after the empire or a dependable khanate has a usable approach to Eastern Europe. The first objective is chosen from current geography and diplomacy. Entering Europe is an achievement of the campaign, not an instant claim on every European state.

This route is deliberately harder than the early frontier wars. Dense settlements, fortified approaches, industrial armies, and a widening enemy coalition make pure momentum less reliable. The mounted army remains dangerous where it can exploit space and vulnerable when forced into prepared fronts.

## Three operational directions

An eastern lowland direction emphasizes mobile warfare and transport centers. A Balkan direction offers a series of smaller political settlements but more difficult terrain and fragmented diplomacy. A Central European direction offers major industry and the greatest risk of a sustained conventional war.

The player can pursue one direction while preparing another. The campaign capacity and available supply determine how much can be supported at once. Evolution III broadens that capacity. It does not make three distant armies free to supply.

## Submission or occupation

A vulnerable country can accept limited tribute or a negotiated imperial association. The terms reflect the empire's actual ability to reach and punish refusal. A remote country protected by a strong coalition is not expected to submit because a universal title exists.

Direct occupation offers usable industry and strategic positions at a heavy administrative cost. A regional partner can hold a limited frontier and share obligations. The route does not automatically create a new khanate for every European country. Existing countries and their institutions remain the default basis of a settlement.

## The military problem

The focus path supports operational preparation, transport repair, and coordination with the Golden Horde or another western partner. It offers tools to sustain a campaign, not a removal of urban and fortified penalties.

An enemy that holds the main cities and attacks exposed routes should be able to stop the advance. A Mongolian player can then negotiate, change the main effort, withdraw to a shorter frontier, or use another approach. The route must support a limited outcome instead of requiring total conquest for every useful reward.

## Capstone and universal rule

The regional capstone requires a meaningful European settlement and a functioning route back to the empire. Its news milestone records the achievement once. Its rewards support the current campaign rather than granting a continent-wide core package.

Under Evolution III, this success can contribute to the universal claim. It is one distinct regional achievement among several required achievements. Taking several nearby states in one war cannot masquerade as several continental campaigns.

## Continuing play

After the capstone, the empire faces the cost of enforcement. Submitted rulers may seek better terms, khans may argue over settlement shares, and an external coalition may exploit a succession. The player can consolidate a stable frontier or continue toward wider domination.

A successful opponent can break the empire's offensive confidence and make western khanates reconsider their dependence on Mongolia. A retreat from Europe may therefore begin a political crisis without immediately destroying the entire state.

AI should prioritize a coherent limited campaign with supply and allied support. It becomes more ambitious after successful settlements and more cautious after a stalled front. The player-facing result should show both the speed of the advance and the specific means by which it can be stopped.
