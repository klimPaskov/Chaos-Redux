# Mongol union and the Siberian approaches

## Uniting the Mongol territories

The earliest union route concerns adjacent territories that the project can substantiate as part of its Mongol restoration objective. The required state set must be bound to the installed map and reviewed before implementation. A vague claim to all related peoples cannot justify unlimited cores.

The root offers a limited unification demand. A neighboring government can negotiate territory where the game's diplomacy permits a valid agreement, accept a limited association, or refuse. The player sees the actual controller and its protecting powers. Mengjiang or another Mongol-associated state is not treated as empty land simply because its own political situation is inconvenient.

The military branch favors a short frontier campaign and a first supply corridor. The diplomatic branch offers a narrower settlement with fewer immediate gains. Both can support the next campaign. Neither requires first defeating every major power in East Asia.

The commitment point is the chosen status of newly acquired territory. Direct integration is restricted to a defensible homeland set and includes an administrative requirement. A regional association leaves more autonomy and provides a smaller mounted obligation. An existing human-controlled country must receive the relevant political choice.

The payoff is a more secure Mongolian base, a practical recruitment relationship, and a route to northern China or the Siberian frontier. Its capstone establishes the union's political settlement. Continuing play concerns border rights and the treatment of local institutions.

AI should take this route early when the frontier is legally reachable and the protecting alliance is manageable. It should not declare a hopeless war against a major faction merely because the first target is geographically small.

## The Siberian approaches

The Siberian route opens when the empire has a direct frontier or a valid northern approach. Its first objective is a useful frontier corridor and a defensible transport anchor. Full northern conquest is optional.

The root asks whether the empire wants a limited border settlement or a deeper campaign toward major rail and resource regions. The limited route can deliver a viable northern frontier without becoming a total war for the whole Soviet or Russian state. The deeper route creates a larger military commitment with serious winter, distance, and supply problems.

The first fork is operational. Following existing transport routes offers better supply and predictable resistance. A wider mounted approach offers opportunities for encirclement but makes recovery harder when a link is cut. Terrain bonuses reduce selected difficulties without removing the need for transport.

The regional settlement can retain a narrow corridor directly or establish a viable frontier administration. A new country is created only if an appropriate existing identity cannot be reused and a complete country package is possible. The design does not require a generic Siberian khanate solely to fill another subject slot.

The payoff concerns transport access, resource development, and a northern military approach. The capstone is a connected northern frontier with a settled administration. It can lead west toward the Rus route or return the army to a Chinese campaign.

Opposition should make cities, rail junctions, and winter concentration important. AI avoids scattering a huge opening army across low-supply territory. It prefers a limited objective when it cannot sustain a deeper front.

After a northern defeat, the empire can shorten its frontier and preserve Mongolia. A limited settlement should not be scripted to require annexing the entire opposing country before any success is recognized.
