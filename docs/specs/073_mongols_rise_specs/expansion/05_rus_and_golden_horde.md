# The Rus and the Golden Horde

## Strategic purpose

The western steppe route creates a major mounted theater and the possibility of a Golden Horde-style subject. It connects military opportunity with a difficult question: should the center govern a large western frontier directly, or entrust it to a powerful regional khan?

The root requires an actual western approach from the northern or Central Asian routes. Current states and governments determine the target. A Soviet collapse, civil war, or earlier country-creation event can change which countries exist, so this route must reuse current identities where appropriate.

## Limited domination or a larger campaign

The initial fork distinguishes a limited frontier and tribute settlement from a wider campaign for the region's political and transport centers. A narrow settlement may be enough to open an imperial route or establish a viable subject. A larger campaign can generate greater military power and a much greater occupation burden.

The mounted army benefits from room to maneuver but still faces defended cities, rivers, armor, and air attack. The route should reward using mobility to isolate major positions. It must not turn every city in the region into easy terrain.

## Restoring the Golden Horde

A Golden Horde settlement needs a coherent territorial foundation and a valid capital. Its identity is a revived political form in this alternate campaign, not proof that every modern population in the region accepts the same medieval allegiance.

The player chooses between a campaigning khan responsible for the western frontier and a tribute-centered governor with more limited military independence. The campaigning khan can support future European operations and becomes a stronger succession participant. The governor provides more predictable obligations but less offensive reach.

A human-controlled regional country receives the proposed relationship and retains meaningful choices. The center cannot silently convert its player into a background tribute source.

## Direct rule alternative

Direct administration offers immediate central control and a closer hold on transport anchors. It requires the center to manage a long frontier and reconcile local institutions with imperial demands. Its benefits arrive through actual industry, resources, and controlled routes, not through unconditional cores over an entire continent.

A later autonomy settlement can ease the burden. Changing from direct rule to a khanate transfers only territory and resources the center can legally assign. It does not replay regional conquest rewards.

## Capstone and follow-up

The capstone requires a regional settlement, a functioning connection east, and a usable western frontier. It opens the Great Western Campaign and the region's role in imperial succession.

A successful Golden Horde can lead a joint campaign, request a larger share of the settlement, or bargain for recognition of its preferred successor. Its continuing focuses and decisions should make it a viable human campaign as well as an AI partner.

## Failure and successors

If the center loses authority, a western khan may withhold forces, demand revised terms, or recognize a rival Great Khan. Independence leaves the country with its actual army, economy, territory, and regional ambitions. It does not erase the country or return its land automatically to pre-event owners.

AI should use this settlement when distance and administrative strain make a strong regional partner useful. It must account for the danger of empowering a khan whose army becomes stronger than the center's surviving force.
