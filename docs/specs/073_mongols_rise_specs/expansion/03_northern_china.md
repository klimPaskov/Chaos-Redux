# Northern China

## Strategic purpose

This route gives the empire access to an industrial and population base capable of sustaining a larger army. It also introduces a difficult occupation problem and opponents who can stop cavalry in cities, forts, and prepared fronts.

The root begins from a valid Mongolian approach into northern China. The target follows current control. A divided China, unified China, foreign occupation, or a puppet arrangement changes the diplomatic and military preview. The script must not assume one fixed Chinese tag owns every objective.

## Opening choices

The player can pursue a narrow frontier corridor, prepare a campaign for the Beijing region, or seek submission from a vulnerable regional government. The corridor supplies an immediate military route. Beijing is a larger political and industrial commitment. Submission preserves more existing administration and provides more limited control.

The early military anchors improve preparation for defended approaches and supply acquisition. They do not remove the army's urban weakness. The player still needs to isolate a city, secure its approaches, or commit enough support to take it.

## The central fork

After the first major settlement, the player chooses direct imperial administration or a Yuan-style regional government. Direct rule gives more central control over military production and future campaigning. It increases occupation and political obligations.

A Yuan-style settlement reuses a suitable existing country where possible. It adapts local institutions, supplies equipment under an agreement, and has its own regional interests. It does not automatically overwrite an existing human country's national focus tree or annex all Chinese countries into a new empty tag.

The country package defines the rights of the regional government and the land actually assigned to it. A title associated with China is not a blanket transfer of every Chinese state.

## Industry or immediate spoils

The player can recover stores for the next campaign or restore functioning industrial administration. Stores offer a stronger immediate push. Restoration provides a more dependable source of equipment and a better basis for recruitment and tribute.

The value responds to actual industry, war damage, existing capture rewards, and the territory the settlement controls. Repeatedly capturing Beijing does not produce repeated industrial grants or news milestones.

## Capstone and follow-up

The regional capstone requires the principal objective, a viable transport link to Mongolia, and a completed governing settlement. Its payoff is a sustained production and recruitment relationship, a political milestone, and access to deeper Chinese and Korean routes.

The player can then consolidate the north, expand along the Chinese coast, negotiate with remaining Chinese governments, or support the regional government's own campaign. The route leaves the empire free to turn west before conquering the entire region.

## Local resistance and diplomacy

A heavy extraction policy, broken promises, or arbitrary seizure of authority can produce withholding, revolt, or a new coalition. These reactions reflect the chosen settlement. They are not an endless random unrest tax that no decision can address.

Other Chinese governments can resist, cooperate against a rival, or seek an agreement. Foreign occupiers are handled through their real ownership and diplomatic position. Tribute to the empire cannot be accepted on behalf of a country by a third party that lacks the right to bind it.

## AI and acceptance

AI prioritizes usable industry and connected approaches over broad empty occupation. It settles an initial gain before expanding deeper when Authority or reserves are weak. It uses a regional government when direct administration has become inefficient.

The route succeeds as design when China makes the horde stronger and more politically complicated at the same time. Its industrial reward must be worth the fight without making the occupation burden meaningless.
