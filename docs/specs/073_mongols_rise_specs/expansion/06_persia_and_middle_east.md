# Persia and the Middle East

## Strategic purpose

The Persia route opens a southern imperial direction with industry, resources, difficult terrain, and an Ilkhanate-style governing option. It is a distinct regional campaign, not a universal claim over all territory loosely described as the Middle East.

The root requires a viable approach from Central Asia or another genuinely connected frontier. Mountain approaches and defended cities are major obstacles for the cavalry army. The player needs a limited initial objective and a transport plan before attempting a broad regional war.

## First campaign choices

A northern corridor campaign secures access and an initial settlement. A campaign toward a major Persian political center seeks a wider governing settlement. A tribute approach preserves the existing country's independence under limited obligations.

The target is the actual current government. Event 071 or another formable system may already have changed Persia's identity. This campaign must negotiate with or fight that existing country. It must not create a second Persia or overwrite an active human restoration campaign.

## The Ilkhanate choice

After a valid regional settlement, the player can retain direct administration or establish an Ilkhanate-style partner. The Ilkhanate uses regional institutions and provides a bridge toward further Middle Eastern routes. Its obligations can emphasize equipment, resources through a verified trade arrangement, or access and transport support.

Its strength comes from usable local institutions and infrastructure. The center can preserve those institutions for a more dependable long-term partner or demand a larger immediate military allocation at the cost of cooperation.

Historical inspiration is limited to a regional imperial structure and cultural exchange documented in the research notes. Religious policy is not reduced to a generic loyalty bonus. Any later detailed religious or personal narrative needs its own sourced treatment.

## Middle Eastern follow-up

A completed Persian settlement can open a route toward Mesopotamia, a Caucasian approach, or another geographically valid neighboring objective. The player selects one current commitment. These campaigns consider mountain and desert logistics, current ownership, and protecting alliances.

A port can support future operations only when it is actually owned or accessible and supplied. Gaining one port does not create a free navy or remove naval invasion requirements.

## Capstone

The capstone is a functioning Persian settlement connected to the broader empire. It offers a dependable regional contribution and access to new campaigns. It can also become a western end of a restored trade route without requiring every Middle Eastern country to be conquered.

The player can develop the partner's institutions, renegotiate a tribute arrangement, or ask for support in a new campaign. An Ilkhanate can disagree with another khanate over routes or settlement shares, creating a specific mediation problem for the center.

## Failure and AI

Overextension across mountains and a narrow approach can cut the empire off from its new partner. The regional ruler may remain loyal while unable to deliver support. The interface distinguishes inability from refusal.

A weak center can retain the Ilkhanate through a looser compact or lose it as a meaningful successor. AI should seek a viable corridor and governing settlement before widening the war. It does not treat historical association as a guarantee that cavalry can win every battle in the region.
