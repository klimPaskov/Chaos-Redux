# Shared army, economy, and crisis routes

## The tumen command route

The route begins with standard formations, reception sites, and a commander arrangement. It forks between broad mounted readiness and a concentrated elite spearhead. Broad readiness spreads affordable support across more standard tumens. The spearhead develops a smaller force with stronger artillery, signals, and reconnaissance.

The player later brings those branches together through an operational command settlement. Its purpose is to coordinate standard, raider, and elite formations without adding noncavalry line battalions. The capstone gives a defined campaign capability and opens reserve reorganization after a defeat. It does not grant every commander every trait.

An optional guard branch follows the political settlement. A personal ruler develops a limited personal guard. An institutional ruler develops a central service guard. A council develops a pledged common reserve. These are variants of a limited formation role and do not stack as three free guard armies.

## Weapons that keep the cavalry relevant

Support progression develops field artillery, anti-air, anti-tank support, and communications when the necessary technology is available. The player can select the immediate threat: enemy armor, air attack, or entrenched infantry. The path gives access and allocation benefits, not automatic immunity to that threat.

Air support is a parallel branch. Its early purpose is reconnaissance and liaison. Its later payoff is a sustainable air force appropriate to the country's industry. A small empire can stop after a useful early capability. A large empire can invest further without replacing the cavalry campaign.

## Karakorum route

The first project establishes an imperial command center and restores usable facilities. The next fork favors a practical military center or a broader civil capital. The military path offers command and workshops sooner. The civil path adds administration and a stronger basis for political legitimacy.

Both converge on a functioning imperial center. A late capital decision requires valid territorial control and a verified map location. The player can retain the current capital while keeping Karakorum's command role. The capital move remains a meaningful option and is not silently replaced by a cosmetic rename.

After the center is finished, continuing content concerns ceremonies tied to actual achievements, emergency continuity after occupation, and the protection of its transport links. Repeating a ceremony does not repeatedly grant Authority or Chaos reductions.

## The Silk Roads route

The first route section connects Mongolia to the chosen regional campaign. The player can first build toward Chinese industry or toward Central Asia. These branches later connect to western and southern routes when the frontier permits them.

The economic fork favors concentrated state logistics or a looser network of protected trade agreements. State logistics is more direct and expensive. Agreements use tributaries and khanates, depend on rights being honored, and are vulnerable to diplomatic rupture.

The capstone requires several functioning connected sections and a settled regional partner or administration. It creates an economic payoff that is suspended by actual route failure. Afterward the player can restore a damaged section, negotiate a detour, or reduce dependency on a hostile member.

## Succession preparation route

This branch becomes useful before the first vacancy. It records a succession principle, identifies an eligible candidate or nomination method, and resolves the role of major commanders and khans. The political route determines the alternatives.

A prepared succession does not require the player to predict a random death date. It provides standing arrangements. Their benefits are tested when the office becomes vacant through a valid character or political event.

## Crisis and recovery route

Visible strain opens limited crisis anchors. The player can shorten the frontier, honor an overdue settlement, evacuate the government, or call a kurultai. The chosen action addresses the actual cause of the crisis.

After fragmentation, a surviving Mongolian state can consolidate a smaller country, negotiate a restored league, or pursue selective reunification. It keeps earned technology and surviving institutions where appropriate. It does not reclaim the opening military grant.

The recovery capstone is a functioning settlement after the crisis. It may be a smaller personal empire, a reformed central state, or a new compact with former khanates. Reaching it is a meaningful campaign outcome, not a requirement to reconquer the entire previous empire.

## Universal rule route

The route opens under Evolution III after achievements in several distinct regions. It requires a functioning center, a credible succession order, dependable obligations, and sufficient Authority. A player can prepare the claim while fighting but cannot satisfy it through one city and a stack of duplicate tribute agreements.

The final fork chooses broader coerced submission or recognition through a federated imperial order. Coerced submission supports faster campaigns and higher expectations. Recognition relies more on agreements and is harder to extend to hostile powers. Both preserve cavalry warfare and the possibility of collapse.
