# Political route: Council of Khans

## Route purpose

The Council route turns a growing empire into a negotiated association of regional rulers under a Great Khan. Its attraction is a better way to govern distance and use specialized khanates. Its cost is that the center must bargain with rulers who have their own armies, regional aims, and rights.

The route is available before several khanates exist. Its early council represents the existing military and territorial leadership. It gains regional members as actual khanates are established. The opening does not invent four independent countries to populate the council.

## Root: a common campaign compact

The first compact states what the center promises and what its members provide. Early obligations concern a limited mounted contingent, access to a route, and participation in one agreed campaign. The player can see whether an obligation is currently fulfilled.

The compact allows early conquest while avoiding a full constitutional delay. A first victory gives the council something real to distribute.

## Fork: military league or regional compact

A military league concentrates on joint campaigns. Members promise forces for agreed objectives and receive a stated share of the settlement. It supports offensive coordination but creates disputes when the center changes objectives after the troops have arrived.

A regional compact concentrates on territory and route rights. Members receive more reliable autonomy and provide equipment or infrastructure access. It is less able to summon an immediate large army, but it is better suited to a broad empire with difficult supply lines.

These choices change obligations. They do not create separate public meters for every council member.

## Commitment: the council's rights

The binding settlement gives khans defined rights and establishes how a Great Khan is recognized. The political route locks at this point. A routine wartime council in another route does not qualify.

The center can reserve authority over common war and succession or allow a wider regional veto. Stronger central powers help rapid campaigning but make infringements of autonomy more serious. Wider regional rights improve legitimacy and make opportunistic wars harder to coordinate.

## Regional participation

A khanate chooses or receives a concrete obligation suited to its position. The Golden Horde can support a western campaign. The Chagatai settlement can maintain a Central Asian corridor. The Ilkhanate can provide industry and route access toward the Middle East. Yuan-style administration can supply equipment and help settle occupied Chinese territory.

No region pays every possible kind of tribute simultaneously. The council distributes a limited common effort. The human khanate receives proposals through its own events and can refuse obligations that exceed its agreement.

## Mediation and competing ambitions

Disputes can arise over a contested border, access to a railway, the division of captured stores, or the recognition of a successor. The center can mediate, grant compensation, accept an altered frontier, or enforce the compact.

A compromise costs something measurable. Compensation uses actual equipment or a real obligation. It is not an invisible gift that raises loyalty. Enforcement can preserve central authority after a victory and risk secession during a weak period.

## Succession

The kurultai is built into the political order. Existing members nominate or endorse eligible candidates. Their positions reflect honored agreements, military achievements, and regional interests. A candidate with sufficient support can succeed peacefully even when Momentum is modest.

A tied or disputed election creates a caretaker period and another round of bargaining. The game does not immediately start a global civil war because two members prefer different candidates. Persistent refusal after a clear settlement can lead to a rival league or independence.

## Capstone: an imperial compact that holds

The capstone requires at least one actual regional khanate, honored obligations, a connected shared route, and an accepted succession procedure. It improves the reliability of joint campaigns and regional government.

The compact does not make khanates permanently obedient. Its strength depends on keeping the agreement. A universal claim that ignores regional rights can undermine the very institutions that made a continental empire possible.

## Continuing play and failure

After the capstone, the player can convene a council for a major campaign, revise an obligation, mediate a dispute, or recognize a successor. A lost war can be survived by reducing common demands and preserving the compact.

Failure usually produces negotiated independence or a rival league before complete collapse. The center may retain Mongolia and its direct territories while former members remain meaningful powers. Reunification must be negotiated or fought for through the recovery branch.

## AI and experience tests

AI should prefer the route when several distant regions are already involved or direct occupation is inefficient. It must avoid making obligations that its members cannot fulfill. A player should feel that autonomy solves a real administrative problem and creates a real political cost.
