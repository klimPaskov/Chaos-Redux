# Political route: The New Great Khan

## Route purpose

This route develops a modern imperial command state while keeping mounted combat as its defining military form. Its main advantage is the ability to sustain operations through better equipment allocation, communications, air liaison, and administration. Its weakness is the cost of those institutions and the tension between central planners and commanders who expect freedom after victory.

The route does not replace cavalry with armor. It makes the cavalry empire more organized and gives it a better chance to recover from a failed campaign.

## Root: command beyond personal orders

The first anchors establish a dispatch office and a reliable record of military obligations. The immediate result is a clearer reception and reserve system. The player can move waiting muster formations through prepared locations and see which campaign is consuming replacement equipment.

This work begins alongside the first conquest. It is not an industrial prerequisite for fighting.

## Fork: field initiative or central allocation

Field initiative gives commanders more discretion to exploit a victory. Follow-up campaigns can use a recent success more readily, but a commander may press beyond the center's transport plans.

Central allocation directs spare equipment and transport toward a selected main effort. It supports a more reliable large campaign and makes simultaneous distant offensives more demanding. Switching the selected main effort has a visible transition cost and cannot distribute the same reserve to every front.

Both choices keep ordinary tactical control in the player's hands. They change resource allocation and political expectations, not the basic ability to issue army orders.

## Commitment: the imperial service state

The binding settlement establishes offices that survive a ruler. It defines a designated transition process, written regional obligations, and professional appointment criteria. The political spirit now reflects administrative continuity.

The succession branch chooses between a nominated executive with an advisory kurultai and a confirmed appointment that requires regional assent. One is faster during war. The other is more dependable when several strong khanates exist. Neither grants a second political route's full benefits.

## Logistics and weapons

The military branch improves artillery supply, anti-air and anti-tank support, signals, workshops, and field repair. Aircraft support develops from reconnaissance and coordination toward a sustainable air arm. Every equipment reward uses technology the country can actually operate.

The player can prioritize broad support for standard tumens or a smaller support-heavy striking force. The first preserves numbers and economy. The second helps break difficult conventional armies at greater supply and equipment cost.

These reforms reduce avoidable losses and improve operational endurance. Urban and fortified attacks remain difficult. Low entrenchment and reduced static defense remain part of the mounted host.

## Governing conquered industry

A successful Chinese or Persian campaign gives the route its main economic decision: restore usable regional industry through local institutions, or extract a larger immediate military allocation. Restoration supports sustained production and future tribute. Extraction supports the next attack while damaging longer-term cooperation.

The choice concerns actual facilities and negotiated resources. The route does not convert every factory under temporary military control into a permanent free factory in Mongolia.

## Karakorum as a command capital

Karakorum develops workshops, communications, and transport connections before a capital decision. The route may keep an existing practical capital while using Karakorum as the imperial command center. Moving the permanent capital is available once the site can support the government and its actual map location has been validated.

This distinction allows the symbolic project to matter without forcing a bad geographic move as the price of finishing the tree.

## Capstone: a state that can sustain the army

The capstone requires a functioning transport corridor, a trained administrative settlement, a dependable succession process, and a successful regional settlement. Its payoff is a more reliable reserve and obligation system, not a general removal of the horde's weaknesses.

At high Evolution, this system can coordinate several large campaigns if the player has built the transport and reserves. It cannot make a distant disconnected front free of supply constraints.

## Continuing play and failure

After the capstone, the player chooses main efforts, reviews regional obligations, repairs damaged corridors, and determines how much autonomy a productive khanate receives. A lost campaign opens orderly withdrawal and reserve reconstruction options.

The route can still fail when its plans demand more than the empire can supply. A large administrative state without victories or dependable agreements loses Authority. Commanders may ignore allocations, regions may withhold equipment, and an institutional succession can be contested by a successful military rival.

## AI and experience tests

AI should favor this route when it has access to industry, support technology, and long transport commitments. It must still attack early. It invests in administration because a current campaign needs it, not because the tree contains an arbitrary industrial checklist.
