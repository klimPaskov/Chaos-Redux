# The imperial focus tree

## Structure and pace

The dedicated Mongolian tree begins with the two short restoration anchors. From there, the player can work on the political settlement, mounted army, imperial center, or a currently reachable campaign. The early tree presents a manageable number of directions. Distant regional branches appear progressively as the empire reaches the relevant frontier.

This is a path-level design. Anchor names identify functions and working themes. They are not approved final focus titles, localisation strings, identifiers, coordinates, or a fixed focus count. Each anchor can become one or more focuses only when its gameplay purpose and pacing justify the split.

Ordinary development should generally use normal focus pacing. A short emergency opening and urgent follow-up after a recent victory are deliberate exceptions. A shortened conquest focus consumes the opportunity created by that victory. The same success cannot shorten every remaining war focus permanently.

## Main families

| Family | Early purpose | Deeper consequence | Continuing play |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imperial settlement | Decide the Great Khan's authority | Different law, subject, and succession arrangements | Appointments, mediation, succession and reform |
| Tumen army | Organize mounted operations | Specialized cavalry and command obligations | Rebuilding, reserve allocation and campaign doctrine |
| Karakorum | Establish the imperial center | Workshops, command facilities and capital choice | Route maintenance and political ceremony |
| Silk Roads | Connect the first campaign | Continental logistics and negotiated commerce | Repair, alternative routes and access disputes |
| Regional campaigns | Reach a nearby objective | Direct administration, tribute or a khanate | Further campaigns and regional settlement problems |
| Imperial crisis | Prepare for reverses | Succession, contested authority and recovery | Smaller empire, reunited empire or successors |
| Universal claim | Make several regional successes coherent | Wider demands and a continental political order | Enforcement, negotiated submission and containment |

## Political exclusivity

The three political settlements become mutually exclusive when their binding constitution or governing compact is completed. Early common work can be shared. The player can inspect the consequences of each route before commitment.

An emergency council used during a succession is not a free switch into the Council of Khans route. A permanent constitutional change requires its own late reform settlement, forfeits incompatible obligations, and cannot collect the opening rewards of a second route. A reversal is a consequential political event, not an undo button.

## Regional independence

Regional campaign branches are not mutually exclusive. Their geographical prerequisites determine availability. A Persia route can follow Central Asia before northern China is fully conquered. A Siberian opening can precede western expansion. The southern routes can begin after a viable frontier exists, without demanding completion of Europe.

Settlement alternatives within a region can be mutually exclusive for the same territory. Once a region becomes a khanate, its direct-rule branch changes into supervision, integration, or renegotiation content. It does not continue granting direct ownership rewards that the empire no longer qualifies for.

## Bypasses and lost targets

A focus whose actual objective is already achieved should bypass only when its intended payoff has also been accounted for. Ownership of one city is not automatic completion of an entire region's administration. Bypass must not grant a second army, a second khanate, or duplicate industry.

When another power annexes the intended target, the campaign follows the current owner through a new diplomatic preview. When the empire loses a required corridor, the relevant route pauses and points to a recovery objective. When no legal target remains, an alternative settlement or a clearly complete branch replaces a dead end.

## Idea budget

The tree uses at most three concurrent event-owned national spirits. The first is the staged military identity of the mounted host. The second is the imperial political settlement. The third is the connected imperial economy once the Silk Roads justify it.

Focuses amend these identities, create actual facilities, grant a researched capability, change agreements, or open decisions. They do not produce a new permanent spirit for every small reward. Temporary crisis effects replace or modify the relevant stage where possible. Hidden dynamic modifiers still count toward the effect budget and must not reproduce the same benefit under a different name.

## Layout and navigation

The opening sits near the center with immediate political and military branches visible. Major regional directions occupy distinct readable bands. Prerequisite lines must not cross unrelated political branches. Filters or shortcuts help the player find a current frontier without exposing a giant wall of every distant ambition.

A compact event-owned inlay shows Momentum, Authority, current campaign, and relevant blocking status. It occupies reserved space and does not cover focus icons, connectors, tooltips, or branch controls. Detailed tribute and khanate actions remain in their appropriate decision context.

The developer must produce a real focus-tree inspection and rendered layout before the tree is considered visually ready. A textual route diagram only establishes structure. It cannot prove that the in-game focus layout is readable.
