# Cavalry and the tumen system

## The normal field army

Every normal imperial line battalion is mounted cavalry. Support companies can provide artillery, engineering, signals, logistics, reconnaissance, anti-air, or anti-tank capability where the game supports them. Support transport is allowed. An armored or mechanized line battalion is not a cavalry loophole.

The defining rule applies to armies created or developed by this restoration. Existing noncavalry formations must enter a safe conversion process. Personnel and equipment are conserved. No formation is deleted in combat, converted into a stronger free unit, or used to duplicate stockpiles. Foreign expeditionary forces retain their owner's identity and cannot be used to claim the cavalry-only achievements or receive cavalry-specific grants.

The normal recruitment, event grants, focus rewards, template progression, military bonuses, AI plans, and campaign qualification all preserve the cavalry army. A universal prohibition in the human division designer is not assumed. Human-built conventional formations receive no cavalry-specific benefits and cannot satisfy mounted-army qualifications. Verify every restriction actually advertised by the interface. Do not claim that the engine forbids a template when it only changes recruitment options or incentives.

## Tumen identity

Tumen names provide organization, command identity, and formation families. A game division need not contain exactly ten thousand men. Historical inspiration for decimal organization is documented in the research notes. Actual division size follows the game's equipment and manpower accounting.

One custom mounted line unit supports the normal army. Distinct template families use different line counts, support combinations, training, and eligible command effects. The design does not require seven redundant copies of the same subunit solely to obtain seven names.

| Family | Military purpose | Defining tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Standard tumen | General-purpose mounted attack and exploitation | Affordable and flexible, vulnerable when pinned |
| Raider cavalry | Reconnaissance, pursuit, long approaches | Smaller formations, less sustained combat strength |
| Elite tumen | Main breakthrough formation | More training and support demand |
| Heavy cavalry | Mounted troops with concentrated support fire | Higher equipment and supply cost, not armored horses |
| Horse-archer tradition | Mobile skirmishing doctrine and mounted reconnaissance | Historical identity expressed by tactics and modifiers, no claim that bows defeat tanks |
| Imperial guard | Reliable central reserve and limited elite spearhead | Limited recruitment and political obligations |
| Great Khan's personal cavalry | Small prestige formation tied to the ruler's office | Valuable command role and succession exposure, never the whole army |

## Progression

The opening army is mostly standard tumens with a smaller raider element. Elite and support-heavy formations develop through experience and supply. The guard requires the imperial settlement. Personal cavalry requires a secure Great Khan and a limited recruitment commitment.

Each upgrade changes what the formation can do. A radio and logistics improvement helps coordination and endurance. Better anti-tank support helps against armor without granting armor to the cavalry. A horse-archer identity improves scouting and movement doctrine rather than introducing a separate medieval ammunition economy.

Opening technology enables equipment required by the actual initial templates. Later support upgrades need research or a compatible technology transfer. A focus cannot provide a unit whose essential equipment is still unavailable.

## Offensive advantage

The horde concentrates on attack and breakthrough while keeping enough speed to exploit a gap. Reconnaissance and recovery help it sustain a sequence of battles. Strong Momentum adds temporary advantage on top of its permanent mounted doctrine.

Bonuses are scoped to the intended cavalry category wherever the engine supports that scope. A generic country army bonus must not accidentally create excellent armored divisions that defeat the point of the restriction. The design keeps a clear distinction between permanent mounted characteristics, Momentum, commander traits, and political-route benefits so the same benefit is not counted several times.

## Persistent weakness

The army has weak entrenchment and reduced defensive staying power. Urban areas and forts remain costly. Desert and cold-country benefits address selected movement and attrition problems, not immunity to weather or starvation. Hills are an opportunity for mobile fighting, not a promise that every mountainous region is easy terrain.

Supply cuts remove the ability to keep attacking effectively. A retreat can save formations, but it sacrifices Momentum and equipment. A formation destroyed in an encirclement remains destroyed. Recruiting replacements requires the ordinary reserve process.

## Supporting forces

Aircraft can support reconnaissance, air defense, and close support. They consume production and fuel. Trains and trucks support logistics. Ports and convoys become important when a later campaign needs maritime supply, but the main campaign does not depend on a free opening navy.

The New Great Khan route develops these supporting systems most deeply. The other routes retain access to enough support to remain playable. They are not forced to fight aircraft with symbolism.

## Visual and audio identity

The army uses a dedicated mounted model with readable period equipment, proper horse and rider contact, appropriate firing and movement animation, and sourced sound. Family differences are primarily template and interface identities. Additional model variants are only added when they improve recognition enough to justify their full production and validation cost.
