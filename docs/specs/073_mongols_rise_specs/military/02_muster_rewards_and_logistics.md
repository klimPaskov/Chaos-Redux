# Muster, conquest rewards, and logistics

## Four opening scales

Baseline provides a large army capable of an early regional offensive. Evolution I substantially enlarges it and improves reserves. Evolution II supports war against larger conventional armies through concentration and encirclement. Evolution III supports several fronts when geography and logistics permit them.

Each tier has a cumulative entitlement consisting of deployed formations, formations ready to muster, actual equipment needed to fill those formations, a separate reinforcement stockpile, available manpower, Army Experience, and appropriate command support. The amount responds to start date, the actor's viable reception territory, nearby opposition, and active Evolution. A small reception area changes deployment staging, not the total entitlement already granted.

Balance anchors are recorded separately in the planning documents. They are starting points for testing, not claims that an untested number can sustain itself in Mongolia's actual supply network.

## Forming the army

An opening formation arrives with its own required personnel and equipment. Its cost is accounted for separately from the spare stockpile. This prevents the apparent reserve from disappearing because the same rifles were counted once for the new units and again as reinforcement equipment.

A waiting formation has an entitlement record but no second physical unit. When it deploys, that record is consumed. When a deployed formation is destroyed, its consumed record does not become available again. An Evolution adds the difference between cumulative tier entitlements, never the difference between the target army size and the army that currently survives.

## Spoils and recruitment

A conquered objective can provide a limited occupation recovery package after the settlement is valid. Captured equipment must be distinguished from equipment already obtained through the game's normal capture and capitulation systems. A special settlement reward represents additional secured stores or negotiated delivery, not the same captured depot paid twice.

Recruitment from a defeated region requires an actual available population or manpower basis. Forced occupation does not instantly turn the whole region into a Mongolian core. Recruitment can be greater under a negotiated administration or a functioning khanate, but the donor and recipient ledgers must agree.

Additional cavalry can come from mustering actual reserves, reforming a negotiated auxiliary contingent, or receiving a khanate obligation. Every formation has a personnel and equipment source. War cannot become a machine that creates an unbounded army from repeatedly defeating empty tags.

## Choosing a settlement reward

A substantial victory offers a limited allocation choice. The center can prioritize ready equipment, regional administration, mounted recruits, or a transport restoration project. The same victory does not automatically pay the full value of every option.

Early Mongolia benefits from equipment and personnel. A larger empire often benefits more from administration or connected transport. The choice scales with the actual value of the captured region and with its condition after the war. A devastated province cannot provide the same usable stores as an intact industrial center.

## Supply support

Opening support establishes a usable reception network and a temporary campaign logistics advantage. It does not create supply hubs in every state. The player should be able to assemble and launch an offensive without immediately losing the entire army to congestion, while still needing to secure transport as the front moves outward.

A finite set of campaign anchors directs supply projects. Each project repairs or builds valid infrastructure, rail connections, or facilities subject to actual building limits and ownership. A focus that targets a lost anchor offers a retargeting or recovery path. It does not build inside the enemy country by accident.

Mounted supply benefits decline when the army outruns its network. The interface reports the broken link or missing reception site. It does not hide the problem behind a general positive national spirit.

## The cost of stopping

After a long failed offensive, replacement demands rise while victory income falls. The army may need to shrink to a sustainable force, convert exhausted elite formations back into standard tumens, or accept regional autonomy in exchange for support. These are choices with consequences.

A planned consolidation period can stabilize the reserve and prepare a new offensive. Its value comes from restoring actual resources and political agreements. It cannot replenish destroyed soldiers through a timer alone.

## Accountability

The opening muster, active-tier additions, conquest allocations, tribute transfers, and ordinary production remain separate records. A player should be able to identify which source supplied a formation. This distinction is also necessary for fair achievements, multiplayer behavior, save continuity, and diagnosing army duplication.
