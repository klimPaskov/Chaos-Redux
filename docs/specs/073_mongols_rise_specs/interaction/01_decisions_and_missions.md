# Decisions and missions

## Interaction pattern

The decision category changes with the current phase. It normally shows three to five primary actions and never more than six. One to three missions can be active. A target selector narrows the relevant action instead of multiplying one visible decision for every country and every state.

The focus tree opens strategic possibilities. Decisions handle current commitments, agreements, and recovery. Missions track a concrete objective that the player can accomplish through normal play. Routine payment processing is not a mission that the player must click every month.

## Opening phase

The main actions select a reachable campaign, inspect or deploy a prepared muster reserve, and handle the sovereignty confrontation when applicable. The current opening mission concerns a first valid campaign or a required reception problem.

The player does not need to spend political power to learn what the army's defensive penalty does. Military identity, entitlement, and current blockages are information, not paid actions.

## Campaign phase

The primary actions prepare the selected objective, send a valid demand, commit available reserves, and choose a settlement once it becomes valid. A supply recovery action replaces a lower-priority action when a critical route is cut.

A campaign mission can require holding a strategic objective, securing an approach, or completing a territorial settlement. It completes automatically when its stated conditions are met. The same predicate drives the mission text, completion, map status, and AI decision.

## Imperial government phase

Actions concern a selected khanate or tributary: negotiate an obligation, review arrears, appoint or confirm an eligible officeholder, mediate a dispute, or revise a settlement. Only contextually relevant actions appear.

An appointment is not a universal repeatable Authority purchase. A mediation must name the dispute and the actual concession. A review must state whether it changes future payments, forgives existing arrears, or breaks the agreement.

## Succession phase

The category prioritizes the kurultai, an eligible candidate or nomination process, a concrete concession, and the response to a defiant member. Ordinary campaign preparation is reduced while the political crisis occupies the category. Existing wars continue under normal game control.

The succession mission tracks a resolved political settlement. Its deadline allows negotiation and reacts to the severity of the current crisis. It is not a hidden timer that creates a civil war without warning.

## Recovery phase

A fragmented or defeated empire sees actions appropriate to its surviving country: secure the current seat, rebuild an accounted reserve, renegotiate a compact, or select a reunification objective. The original opening decision is unavailable.

A recovery mission can require a functioning capital, a restored route, a settled succession, or a valid regional agreement. Success leaves a smaller but playable empire where appropriate.

## Costs and feedback

The decision set uses no more than four spendable cost types. The proposed set is political power, command power, infantry equipment, and support equipment. Obligations, automatic transfers, construction results, and ordinary production are accounted for separately and are not hidden fifth click costs.

A decision displays no more than three inline cost values. The complete tooltip shows every actual debit and condition. Command power costs stay at or below sixty. Affordability includes the exact boundary value. The visible state, availability, click check, final debit, and AI all use the same rule.

A failed target recheck consumes nothing. A repeated click cannot pay the same grant twice. The player is told what changed when a target becomes invalid.

## Mission pacing

Ordinary local objectives use a generous short-campaign window. Regional objectives receive a longer window. Continental or major construction commitments can run for several months. Exact starter ranges and dynamic factors are in the tuning plan.

The mission window responds to distance, target strength, active war conditions, transport access, Evolution, and the type of objective. A timer does not accelerate solely because the designer wants more dramatic pacing.

## Acceptance

The same ordinary campaign must remain understandable with the custom inlay unavailable through a supported fallback. Information can move to standard tooltips and category presentation. The implementation must not drop required state qualification or hide invalid costs in that fallback.
