# AI and multiplayer behavior

## Strategic order

AI Mongolia first establishes a valid army and sovereignty arrangement, then selects a reachable campaign with useful rewards. It normally values nearby territory and usable Chinese industry before a distant prestige war. Central Asia becomes attractive when it offers a practical western route. It uses khanates when direct government becomes inefficient.

AI becomes more ambitious after successful settlements, strong reserves, and a connected route. It becomes more selective after a stalemate, an equipment shortage, or a succession dispute. Evolution increases its available strength and campaign capacity. It does not remove these checks.

## Campaign selection

The target score considers reachability, protecting alliances, relative usable army strength, supply access, terrain, likely settlement value, current commitments, and political strain. A target is filtered for legality before being scored. An invalid target cannot become available through a high score.

A small country inside a strong faction may be a worse opening target than a larger isolated government. A wealthy distant region may be a worse objective than a nearby transport corridor. The AI should explain these differences through observable choices in the test scenarios.

A score is not an acceptance probability. Probabilities require a complete valid option pool, normalization, and a tested script. The planning scenario matrix specifies desired ranking and exclusions without pretending the probabilities have already been measured.

## Army management

AI uses the mounted templates, support priorities, and limited formation families. It deploys prepared reserves through viable reception sites and does not create a supply disaster by concentrating the whole entitlement in one province.

During operations it favors breakthrough and exploitation opportunities while accounting for supply. It should avoid deliberately feeding cavalry into fortified cities when a practical encirclement or alternative objective exists. Perfect tactical behavior is not promised. The implementation must demonstrate that its actual AI plan supports the intended strategy.

## Politics and subjects

AI prepares succession before a large empire depends on one ruler. It honors obligations that remain feasible and renegotiates agreements that no longer fit the situation. It can choose coercion when strong and accept autonomy after a costly defeat.

A khanate AI maintains its own viability before providing an impossible military contribution. It distinguishes a disrupted route from disloyalty. A tributary AI weighs the real payment against the military danger and its alliance support.

## Collapse response

At the warning stage, AI chooses an action that addresses the cause of strain. It can shorten a frontier, settle an overdue promise, convene a kurultai, or relieve a key route. It does not spend all resources on unrelated focus rewards while the capital is about to fall.

After fragmentation it pursues survival, a viable regional objective, or a new agreement. Successor countries do not remain attached to the dead center's AI plan.

## Multiplayer ownership

The event actor is resolved once through the existing event engine's shared or independent context. There can be only one physical Mongolian opening grant for the same accepted restoration. Several clients receiving a notification do not each receive or trigger the army.

Human overlords, targets, tributaries, and khanates receive the choices that belong to them. Mongolia cannot select their responses on their behalf. A joint campaign, political concession, or subject transition rechecks the actual country and relationship at commitment.

Temporary disconnects, save reloads, observer notifications, and repeated event delivery must not duplicate entitlement, payment, a country release, or Chaos changes. Shared logging happens for the correct actor and occurrence.

## Information and fairness

Players can inspect their own obligations and public imperial conditions. They do not gain private enemy information that the normal game would hide merely because a target selector exists. A feasibility preview can show known diplomacy and territorial qualification without revealing exact hidden stockpiles or plans.

The same costs, target conditions, and military rules apply to AI and human actors. Difficulty scaling belongs to the existing difficulty system and must not be disguised as an unexplained AI-only exception.
