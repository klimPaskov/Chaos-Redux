# Event 15 Utopia Manifesto, Part 2: The Commonwealth Ledger

All names in this specification are working labels, not final localisation.

## Mechanic purpose

The Commonwealth Ledger turns the manifesto into a living state-building system. It gives the player a compact view of whether the country is meeting material need, building practical abundance, preserving consent, and honoring its promise that people can choose useful work.

The mechanic must change what the player can do. Its values gate focus groups, decisions, integration methods, external cases, final identities, route events, and AI behavior. It must not exist as four numbers that drift beside the focus tree.

## Player-facing presentation

The mechanic should appear through a scripted GUI opened from the main Utopia decision category. A compact category header should always show the current values and their trend. The full window should have three operational tabs and one overview.

### Overview

The overview presents:

- Need
- Plenty
- Concord
- Choice versus Assignment
- the current interpretive route
- the current national problem
- the next public threshold
- active crises and active external cases

Every value should have a concise breakdown. The player should be able to see which public actions changed it and which current conditions are pushing it up or down.

### Callings

The Callings tab presents the six labor families, current shortage pressure, current training commitment, and the selected method for filling each shortage.

The tab should not simulate individual workers. It should present national priorities and route-specific methods.

### Common Stores and Settlements

This tab presents reserve capacity, housing strain, transport coverage, district projects, and active public works. It connects Plenty to real map and production changes.

### Necessary Ground and Associates

This tab presents the selected foreign target, the asserted need, available peaceful offers, escalation status, local acceptance, stewardship deadlines, and associated municipalities.

The human player should inspect one target at a time. AI countries use equivalent scripted actions without depending on the visible selector.

## Core values

### Need

Need measures the gap between what the country requires and what it can provide through its current territory, trade, infrastructure, and institutions.

Need can rise from:

- food or resource shortage represented by trade dependence and missing strategic inputs
- insufficient housing during migration or population pressure
- blockade and lost ports
- damaged railways, supply hubs, or infrastructure
- shortage of a critical calling
- refugee pressure from compatible events
- prolonged war and mobilization
- occupation burden
- loss of the capital region or an island project site
- excessive concentration of population or industry
- a declared public project that lacks the required material base

Need can fall from:

- domestic substitution
- trade agreements
- leased access
- new infrastructure
- restored supply networks
- common-store reserves
- balanced callings
- voluntary migration agreements
- successful associate partnerships
- demobilization or peace
- giving up an obsolete external claim

Need is not automatically bad. Moderate Need creates direction and unlocks legitimate projects. Very low Need supports generosity and stable local autonomy. Very high Need creates emergency options, Concord pressure, and susceptibility to coercive arguments.

A country should not be able to manufacture Need cheaply for conquest. The calculation should discount self-inflicted shortages, deliberate trade cancellation, and artificial production sabotage. Repeated attempts to create a case through obvious manipulation should generate domestic scandal or foreign distrust.

### Plenty

Plenty measures practical provision. It combines reserve stocks, housing, local industry, transport, health, education, and the ability to absorb disruption.

Plenty can rise from:

- common stores and reserve depots
- infrastructure, railways, ports, and supply hubs
- housing and Garden City districts
- balanced production and callings
- public kitchens, hospitals, schools, and technical institutes represented through focuses and decisions
- stable trade access
- reduced elite consumption or redirected luxury capacity
- successful harvest, reconstruction, or industrial missions
- associate municipalities contributing surplus voluntarily
- peace and low occupation burden

Plenty can fall from:

- reserve drawdowns
- war damage
- blockade
- uncontrolled migration without housing
- assignment policies that exhaust workers
- excessive military procurement
- maintaining distant territories
- failed public works
- corruption or black-market diversion
- penal labor systems that appear productive but damage efficiency and trust over time

High Plenty unlocks aid, shorter workday reforms, generous integration, and the strongest voluntary final identities. Low Plenty creates rationing, emergency assignments, delayed projects, and pressure to seek external resources.

### Concord

Concord measures consent, public trust, willingness to cooperate, and acceptance of the manifesto's institutions.

Concord can rise from:

- open votes and local charters
- transparent value breakdowns
- voluntary calling programs
- fair rationing
- successful provision
- religious and political latitude
- peaceful settlement agreements
- public education and debate
- protecting dissent and lawful exit
- ending obsolete claims
- integrating territory through local consent

Concord can fall from:

- shortages after grand promises
- repeated quotas
- arbitrary relocation
- forced integration
- penal labor
- censorship and loyalty tests
- using mercenaries to spare citizens while exporting casualties
- unjustified territorial cases
- elite exemptions
- local resistance
- contradictory route choices
- failed projects and concealed losses

Concord should affect stability and compliance indirectly through the mechanic, but it must also unlock or block actions. A low-Concord commonwealth cannot credibly invite partners, hold a fair plebiscite, or proclaim a voluntary regional order. A high-Concord coercive regime should be possible only temporarily. The route's methods eventually push the value toward its real social consequences.

### Choice versus Assignment

Choice versus Assignment is a balance of power. It measures who determines occupations, relocations, service, and public duties.

Choice rises from:

- open calling recruitment
- worker and household councils
- education that enables voluntary transfers
- right of exit
- local self-government
- shorter workday and public lectures
- mixed cooperative and private arrangements on the hidden humanist route
- successful voluntary response to shortages

Assignment rises from:

- quotas
- compulsory service
- directed relocation
- planned occupational households
- labor registration
- penal work
- military labor battalions
- emergency assignment during war or famine
- technocratic staffing rules

Neither end is a simple good or bad meter.

High Choice:

- strengthens Concord
- improves education, innovation, and local initiative
- makes sudden shortage correction slower
- can create uneven staffing when the state fails to train or incentivize workers

High Assignment:

- fills shortages quickly
- improves standardization and emergency mobilization
- strengthens the technocratic and coercive routes
- damages Concord when used without crisis justification
- can reduce innovation, increase evasion, and create hidden inefficiency

The middle position supports guaranteed placement. The state identifies shortages, pays for training and relocation, and offers secure work without immediate compulsion.

## Calling families

### Provisioning and Agriculture

Purpose:

- food security
- rural transport
- agricultural training
- reserve production
- emergency cultivation

Shortage effects:

- rising Need
- reserve depletion
- higher import dependence
- greater migration pressure

Surplus uses:

- aid
- reserve growth
- lower food costs
- integration support for new districts

### Workshops and Arsenal

Purpose:

- tools
- consumer production
- military equipment
- repair capacity
- local industrial workshops

Shortage effects:

- weak construction and repair
- equipment dependence
- stalled public works

Overcommitment effects:

- reduced consumer provision
- higher Assignment pressure during mobilization
- war-oriented route drift

### Civic Works and Transport

Purpose:

- infrastructure
- railways
- ports
- supply hubs
- housing construction
- water and power systems

Shortage effects:

- Plenty loss
- project delays
- weak integration
- higher cost for external stewardship

### Learning and Care

Purpose:

- schools
- technical education
- medicine
- public lectures
- administrative training
- research

Shortage effects:

- slow calling conversion
- weak innovation
- poor public health
- higher failure risk for planned settlements

### Maritime and Settlement

Purpose:

- ports
- convoys
- navigation
- island construction
- migration logistics
- foreign leases

Landlocked adaptation:

- rail corridors
- river transport
- waterworks
- capital-ring logistics
- survey and settlement administration

Shortage effects:

- blocked external case options
- higher convoy or train requirements
- slow refugee and settlement projects

### Defense and Watches

Purpose:

- militia
- border guards
- civil defense
- engineers
- local fortification
- emergency response

Shortage effects:

- vulnerability
- inability to protect stewardship zones
- pressure to use mercenaries or compulsory service

Overcommitment effects:

- lower Plenty
- reduced Choice
- risk that the manifesto becomes a military constitution

## Methods for filling shortages

### Open Call

The state publishes the shortage, funds training, and relies on volunteers.

Typical costs:

- time
- education capacity
- consumer support during training
- local recruitment infrastructure

Typical benefits:

- Choice
- Concord
- long-term skill quality

Typical risks:

- slow response
- failure when the calling is unpopular
- regional imbalance

### Guaranteed Placement

The state guarantees wages, housing, training, and relocation support for people who enter a needed calling.

Typical costs:

- civilian construction
- trains or convoys
- support equipment or trucks where appropriate
- temporary consumer burden

Typical benefits:

- reliable shortage correction
- moderate Choice
- migration and integration support

Typical risks:

- expensive administration
- overbuilding
- patronage or favoritism

### Assignment Quota

The state assigns a quota to households, districts, councils, or occupational bodies.

Typical costs:

- Concord
- stability risk
- enforcement burden
- possible production disruption

Typical benefits:

- rapid shortage correction
- standardization
- strong emergency response

Typical risks:

- evasion
- low innovation
- radicalization
- route corruption

### Emergency Levy

A temporary assignment available during severe Need, war, famine, or infrastructure collapse.

The levy must have a clear end date and cleanup. Extending it repeatedly converts emergency assignment into permanent route movement.

## Common stores and reserves

### Reserve bands

The reserve system should use readable bands rather than raw stockpile trivia.

- Empty stores
- Emergency stores
- Seasonal reserve
- One-year security
- Two-year security
- Surplus commonwealth

The actual calculation can use equipment, convoys, trains, fuel, civilian capacity, and state-level infrastructure proxies. The interface should present a coherent public concept.

### Reserve decisions

The player can:

- build or expand common stores in named core regions
- stock food and essential tools
- rotate old reserves into civilian use
- release emergency stores
- send surplus abroad
- use surplus to stabilize an associate municipality
- protect reserves from bombing and blockade
- investigate diversion and black markets

Releasing reserves should lower Need immediately and lower Plenty later if replenishment fails. Aid should improve Concord and foreign relations but should not be free when the recipient has little surplus.

### Two-year reserve ambition

A late economic objective can ask the player to maintain a high reserve band for a meaningful period while keeping Choice and Concord above route-appropriate thresholds. It should grant a durable institutional payoff, not only a one-time modifier.

## Workday and learning

The six-hour workday inspiration becomes a staged reform.

### Early reduction

The player reduces the workday in selected sectors after Plenty is adequate.

Effects direction:

- lower immediate output
- higher Learning and Care capacity
- greater Choice and Concord
- improved long-term productivity

### Universal short day

A late voluntary or council route can establish the shorter day across the economy.

Requirements direction:

- stable reserves
- sufficient housing
- balanced callings
- low emergency Need
- no active total mobilization

Failure or reversal should be possible during crisis. Repeatedly suspending the reform weakens Concord.

## Property and common ownership

Property treatment varies by route.

### Household commons

The democratic route protects personal property and small enterprise while moving land, utilities, and major infrastructure into public or cooperative trusts.

### Common table

The council route socializes major production and expands worker ownership. Local councils determine use rights within national reserve targets.

### Measured tenure

The technocratic route assigns use rights through surveys, production targets, and planned districts. It can allow private management when output and provision targets are met.

### State allotment

The coercive route treats land and major property as revocable grants tied to service and loyalty.

### Practical mixed order

The hidden humanist route uses public land trusts, cooperatives, regulated private enterprise, and social guarantees without declaring one ownership form universally correct.

The system should avoid blanket confiscation events that give instant factories. Property reform should create transition costs, legal conflict, local negotiation, and route-specific benefits.

## Settlements and Garden City network

### District projects

The player develops a network of planned districts. Each project should target a real state or region and answer a concrete problem.

District roles include:

- capital relief
- industrial housing
- agricultural market town
- rail junction settlement
- port and shipyard town
- research and education town
- refugee settlement
- associate municipality reconstruction
- Inland Island ring district

A district should require housing, transport, industry, and a calling plan. Completing only factories should not count as a complete settlement.

### Network effects

Several functioning districts can create a Garden Commonwealth network.

Benefits direction:

- local jobs
- lower housing pressure
- stronger Plenty
- faster calling conversion
- internal migration capacity
- distributed industry and defense

Risks direction:

- construction burden
- standardized monotony under Assignment
- local inequality if one district receives most investment
- transport dependence

### Land value and public return

Public works should return part of their value to common stores or local services. This can be represented through staged ideas, construction decisions, and district completion effects. It should not become an unexplained national income bonus.

## Doctrine of Necessary Ground

### Purpose

The doctrine replaces ordinary opportunistic expansion with case-based territorial policy. A case must name the deficit, the target area, and the proposed remedy.

### Valid case families

#### Port access

Used when the country lacks reliable sea access and maritime trade is a real strategic or provisioning need.

Possible remedies:

- commercial access treaty
- leased harbor
- free-port partnership
- joint port administration
- protectorate or territorial demand on coercive routes

#### Defensive corridor

Used when the capital, island project, or associate network is exposed and a specific corridor is needed for defense or supply.

Possible remedies:

- demilitarized agreement
- transit rights
- mutual defense zone
- leased fortification strip
- coercive occupation under severe threat

#### Essential resource

Used when domestic substitution and trade cannot supply a critical resource.

Possible remedies:

- long-term purchase
- investment concession
- joint extraction
- associate municipality
- coercive seizure on radical routes

#### Settlement and housing

Used during genuine population, refugee, or housing pressure.

Possible remedies:

- migration agreement
- planned new district with host consent
- joint municipality
- voluntary population exchange with safeguards
- coercive colony on the Closed Island route

#### Island or capital refuge

Used for the national island project.

Possible remedies:

- lease
- purchase
- shared sovereignty
- transfer after guarantee or debt settlement
- conquest only on coercive or desperate routes

#### Reconstruction zone

Used when a neighboring devastated area cannot recover and both sides can benefit from a stewardship compact.

Possible remedies:

- aid mission
- temporary stewardship
- associated municipality
- later return, autonomy, or integration vote

### Case preparation

A case requires:

- a visible deficit
- a target that can plausibly solve it
- domestic alternatives evaluated first
- route-compatible diplomatic capacity
- transport and housing plan
- a maximum active-case cap

Preparing a case costs administrative attention, survey work, intelligence exposure, and route-specific resources. It should not cost only political power.

### Escalation ladder

1. Domestic substitution.
2. Purchase or trade guarantee.
3. Lease or transit agreement.
4. Migration or settlement treaty.
5. Joint administration or associate charter.
6. Ultimatum.
7. War.

The player can skip steps only when route, war state, repeated rejection, severe Need, or high chaos justifies it. Skipping peaceful steps reduces Concord and increases foreign opposition.

### Case integrity

A case has an integrity score or equivalent hidden calculation based on:

- real deficit severity
- whether domestic remedies were tried
- target relevance
- proportionality
- peaceful offers made
- local consent
- route conduct
- whether the state gained unrelated land

High integrity supports recognition and local acceptance. Low integrity creates contradiction events, foreign condemnation, resistance, and route corruption.

### Expiration and renunciation

When Need falls or the target no longer solves the deficit, the player receives a choice to:

- renounce the case
- convert it to a commercial or defense agreement
- continue it and accept political cost

Peaceful routes should find continued unjustified claims increasingly difficult. Coercive routes can preserve them but move toward Closed Island logic.

## Occupation and integration rules

### Temporary Stewardship

A stewardship begins with a public objective and a deadline.

The player must:

- restore transport
- provide food and housing
- establish local administration
- reduce resistance
- choose return, autonomy, association, or integration

Failure creates local resistance, Concord loss, and foreign pressure. The territory cannot remain in indefinite benevolent occupation without consequence.

### Commonwealth Partnership

A partnership retains local institutions and grants broad autonomy. The partner contributes defined surplus or access and receives reserve support, defense, and reconstruction.

The route favors peaceful commonwealths and league building.

### Necessary Settlement

A settlement project moves people only when housing, transport, and local governance exist. It should track settler capacity and host acceptance. It must not instantly remove or replace the local population.

### Open Municipality

An open municipality uses elected local government, public guarantees, and a later status vote. It is the strongest route to consensual integration.

### Assigned Colony

The coercive route can establish an assigned colony with compulsory labor, relocation, and production quotas.

Benefits direction:

- rapid construction
- resource extraction
- military access

Costs direction:

- resistance
- Concord loss
- condemnation
- deaths-system integration where applicable
- long-term garrison and supply burden
- risk of revolt or foreign intervention

### Integration ladder

1. Survey and emergency provision.
2. Local charter or imposed administration.
3. Reconstruction and calling plan.
4. Consent vote, negotiated association, or coercive declaration.
5. Associate status.
6. Long integration mission.
7. Core or permanent autonomous status.

Large or culturally distinct areas should normally remain associates or require long integration. Instant core grants are reserved for small, closely connected territories with strong local consent and sustained administration.

## Contradiction and failure

The mechanic should expose failure through the four values and event conduct rather than adding a separate contradiction meter.

### Promise without provision

Condition direction:

- grand reforms
- low Plenty
- reserve failure
- high public expectation

Consequences:

- Concord loss
- ration conflict
- route radicalization
- emergency assignment pressure

### Choice without training

Condition direction:

- very high Choice
- repeated calling shortages
- weak education capacity

Consequences:

- stalled projects
- regional labor imbalance
- pressure for guaranteed placement or temporary assignment

### Measure without consent

Condition direction:

- high Assignment
- high Plenty
- falling Concord

Consequences:

- technocratic hardening
- hidden inefficiency
- evasion
- possible transition to Closed Island

### Need without restraint

Condition direction:

- active territorial case
- low integrity
- unrelated annexation
- skipped peaceful steps

Consequences:

- claim scandal
- foreign coalition
- resistance
- loss of peaceful formable eligibility

### Island without outlets

Condition direction:

- extreme self-sufficiency
- closed trade
- high mobilization
- low Choice

Consequences:

- shortages
- militarized society
- radical isolation route

### Failed literal experiment

A country can fail without losing all content. Severe low Plenty and Concord can trigger a constitutional crisis.

Possible resolutions:

- restore voluntary institutions
- empower councils
- invite planners
- impose emergency order
- unlock the hidden humanist reinterpretation if the player preserved debate and education

The event should transform failure into a route decision rather than deleting the tree or ending the campaign.

## Cleanup and terminal states

The Ledger remains active through ordinary formation and late play. It closes or converts only when:

- the country rejects the manifesto at the opening
- the country is annexed
- a terminal world state disables ordinary country systems
- another approved transformation explicitly supersedes the mechanic

Temporary external cases, stewardship missions, selected targets, emergency levies, and local project flags must clean up when targets disappear, wars end, states change hands, or the country changes route.
