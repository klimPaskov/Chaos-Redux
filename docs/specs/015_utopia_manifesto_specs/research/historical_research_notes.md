# Historical and Conceptual Research Notes

## Research purpose

The event should not treat *Utopia* as a vague synonym for a pleasant society. Thomas More's book supplies a specific and contradictory political model. The strongest event design comes from preserving that contradiction. The country can pursue common welfare, social training, religious latitude, public provision, and chosen work. It can also use compulsion, colonial settlement, slavery, exclusion, and mercenary violence in the name of a perfect order.

This note translates the source material into design principles. It does not provide final localisation.

## Thomas More's design material

### A book that is also an argument

*Utopia* was published in 1516. Its title plays on the Greek sense of "no place" and "good place." Modern scholarship regularly treats the book as satire, social criticism, or a deliberately unstable political thought experiment rather than a simple constitution to copy. That ambiguity is important for Event 15. The player should be able to read the manifesto literally, selectively, critically, or opportunistically.

Design consequence:

- The opening event should establish that the text has several plausible interpretations.
- The focus tree should divide after an interpretive congress, not after an arbitrary ideology picker.
- A hidden humanist route should become available when the player treats the text as a critique of existing institutions rather than a sacred blueprint.
- The coercive route should emerge from real source tensions instead of appearing as an unrelated evil branch.

### Agriculture, trades, and preference

In More's account, every citizen learns agriculture. Each person also learns a trade. A person whose talent or inclination points elsewhere can move into another household or occupational family and learn another craft. The text also says that public need can override personal preference. Utopians work six hours a day and spend substantial time on learning and public lectures.

Design consequence:

- Every accepted country establishes universal basic agricultural and civil-defense training.
- The player manages calling families rather than assigning individual workers.
- The central labor choice is not private employment versus state employment. It is voluntary calling versus planned assignment.
- Open calling raises Choice and Concord, but it fills shortages slowly.
- Guaranteed placement combines training, relocation support, and public need.
- Assignment quotas fill shortages quickly, but they push the balance toward Assignment and can damage Concord.
- A shorter workday path should trade immediate output for research, health, education, and long-term productivity.
- A country that promises free choice while repeatedly using quotas should accumulate visible political contradiction through Concord loss and route events.

### Common stores and public provision

Households receive goods from public stores according to need rather than by ordinary purchase. The society also uses common dining halls and maintains large reserves. More's Utopians are described as keeping more grain than they expect to use, partly to protect against scarcity.

Design consequence:

- Plenty is not a generic prosperity statistic. It measures the state's ability to provide food, housing, tools, transport, and reserve stocks.
- The player should build common stores, rail links, local workshops, housing, and reserve capacity.
- Plenty should be damaged by blockade, war damage, occupation burden, refugees without housing, and overexpansion.
- High Plenty should support aid, migration agreements, reconstruction, and voluntary integration.
- Low Plenty should make territorial demands tempting while making any new territory harder to integrate.

### Gold without honor

More's Utopians deliberately treat gold and silver as objects without moral prestige. The book assigns them degrading uses, including chamber pots and slave chains, so citizens learn not to admire them.

Design consequence:

- One route can deliberately strip prestige from luxury reserves and elite consumption.
- The mechanic should not create free resources from a joke. It should convert elite consumption, treasury prestige, or ceremonial stock into reserve purchases, public works, foreign trade credit, or emergency imports.
- Overuse should create diplomatic or elite backlash where appropriate.
- Achievement direction can reward building an abundant society without accumulating conventional financial prestige, but the final title requires localisation writing and asset work.

### Necessary land and colonial contradiction

When Utopia has too many inhabitants, settlers establish colonies in nearby underused territory. The book presents this as a natural right when existing inhabitants possess more land than they cultivate. Those who cooperate can join the Utopian settlement. Those who refuse can be displaced by force.

This is the most important source for the event's territorial doctrine. It gives the player a reason to claim land only when need can be demonstrated. It also exposes how easily a doctrine of need becomes an excuse for conquest.

Design consequence:

- Ordinary claim generation is replaced by the Doctrine of Necessary Ground.
- A case must be built around a real deficit such as port access, strategic depth, essential resources, refugee settlement, or a capital-island project.
- The peaceful ladder begins with domestic substitution, purchase, lease, migration treaty, joint administration, and associated autonomy.
- Coercive routes can escalate to ultimatum or war earlier.
- A claim can expire when the need disappears.
- Acquiring territory that does not resolve the stated need should reduce Concord and increase foreign distrust.
- Local people must have routes to chartered autonomy, plebiscite, partnership, or resistance.
- The player should be able to reproduce the book's colonial logic, but the game must expose its cost rather than treat the claim as morally self-validating.

### Slavery and compulsory order

More's Utopia uses several categories of enslaved labor. These include criminals, foreign condemned people, and poor outsiders who enter service. The system relies on chains, hard labor, and severe punishment.

Design consequence:

- The coercive route can create penal labor and assigned-colony mechanics.
- These mechanics must be powerful enough to tempt the player and harmful enough to define the route.
- Costs should include resistance, condemnation, Concord loss, deaths where the implementation connects to the shared deaths system, and diplomatic isolation.
- The humane routes need explicit alternatives such as rehabilitation, public service with time limits, paid reconstruction corps, and autonomous local charters.
- The design must not hide the historical contradiction behind euphemism in the specification. Final player-facing text should remain in-world and avoid implementation commentary.

### Mercenary warfare

The Utopians prefer not to risk their own citizens when they can hire the Zapoletes, a foreign mercenary people whom the book describes with open contempt. The arrangement allows Utopia to preserve its own population by externalizing death.

Design consequence:

- The military branch can unlock an auxiliary-contract system.
- It should provide quick expeditionary strength without turning into free unit generation.
- Costs should include money or equipment, convoy capacity, foreign leverage, reliability risk, and Concord loss when casualties are being exported.
- A humanist route should expose and reject the system.
- A coercive route can deepen it and face betrayal, scandal, or dependency.

### Religion and limits of tolerance

The island permits several forms of worship and restricts violent religious coercion. The tolerance is incomplete. People who reject certain moral and metaphysical premises can be excluded from public trust and office.

Design consequence:

- A pluralist route can support freedom of worship and public debate.
- A technocratic or coercive route can convert tolerance into licensed belief and loyalty tests.
- The hidden humanist route should protect dissent more consistently than the literalist routes.
- The game should not reduce the issue to a simple stability bonus.

### The made island

The legendary founder Utopus conquers the land and orders a channel cut, turning it into an island. The island is therefore a political and engineering project as much as a natural refuge.

Design consequence:

- Island countries can perfect an existing island network.
- Coastal countries can seek a leased island, defensible harbor district, or nearby island capital.
- Landlocked countries can create an Inland Island through a fortified, self-provisioning capital ring, rail control, green belts, and waterworks.
- A high-chaos route can attempt an extreme separation project only where geography supports it.
- The plan should never promise map geometry the engine cannot change.

## Cooperative and worker-governance parallels

The International Cooperative Alliance defines cooperatives through voluntary membership, democratic member control, member economic participation, autonomy, education, cooperation among cooperatives, and concern for community. The International Labour Organization describes worker cooperatives as enterprises governed democratically by their member-worker-owners.

These principles help distinguish the Consent of Households and Common Table routes from simple nationalization.

Design consequence:

- Voluntary membership and local votes strengthen Choice and Concord.
- Democratic control should appear through council composition, route decisions, and integration votes.
- Education and training should reduce the cost of changing callings.
- Cooperation among small states should support a league built around reserves, transport, reconstruction, and mutual recognition.
- Autonomy rules should prevent the cooperative league from becoming an ordinary annexation machine.

## Garden City parallels

Ebenezer Howard's Garden City idea links planned settlements, community land stewardship, local jobs, public services, and a town-country balance. The model also imagines a network of settlements rather than one endlessly expanding city.

Design consequence:

- The housing and public works branch should create several linked settlement districts.
- Industry should be geographically grounded in named capital, rail, port, and low-industry regions.
- Land value created by public works should feed common stores or local services.
- Population and industry concentration should create pressure to found another district instead of granting abstract national bonuses.
- The final city network should support Plenty and Choice differently depending on route.

## New Harmony as a caution

Robert Owen's New Harmony experiment offers a useful historical warning. The community attracted members quickly and collapsed within a short period. National Park Service material highlights governance conflict, weak self-government, and criticism that individuality and initiative were suppressed. The community still left an educational and scientific legacy.

Design consequence:

- Acceptance should not guarantee success.
- A country can have strong ideals and fail through bad governance, shortages, faction conflict, or compulsory uniformity.
- Early focus choices must establish procedures for dispute, exit, local autonomy, and allocation.
- Failure should transform the route rather than simply remove the mechanic.
- The hidden humanist path can preserve educational and institutional gains after a literal blueprint fails.

## Design synthesis

The event should let the manifesto operate in five ways:

1. **A constitutional inspiration** for democratic welfare and voluntary cooperation.
2. **A program of common ownership** managed through worker and civic councils.
3. **A planning manual** for a technocratic state that solves shortages through measurement and assignment.
4. **A justification for coercion** in which need, unity, and public order excuse dispossession and forced labor.
5. **A satire and diagnostic tool** that helps a country reform without declaring the book infallible.

Each interpretation must be mechanically coherent. The player should be able to build a prosperous and consensual society, an efficient but paternal state, an expansionist island regime, or a practical mixed commonwealth. None should be a cosmetic ideology switch.
