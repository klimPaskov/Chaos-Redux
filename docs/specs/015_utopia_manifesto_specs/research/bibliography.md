# Research Bibliography

## Primary source

### Thomas More, *Utopia*

- Project Gutenberg HTML edition: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2130/2130-h/2130-h.htm
- Used for agriculture and trades, chosen occupations, six-hour work, common stores, reserves, treatment of gold, colonial settlement, slavery, mercenary warfare, religious tolerance, and the founding island story.
- Public domain source.

## Publication, title, and interpretation

### Folger Shakespeare Library, two ways of looking at *Utopia*

- https://www.folger.edu/blogs/collation/two-ways-of-looking-at-the-same-book/
- Used for publication context and the book's satirical and critical function.

### Library of Congress, Mapping the Land of Utopia

- https://blogs.loc.gov/maps/2022/08/mapping-the-land-of-utopia/
- Used for the title's no-place and good-place pun and the imagined geography.

### George M. Logan, discussion of war and slavery in More's *Utopia*

- Cambridge Core PDF: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/1DACD4A7E353564E9B8E088A1D53E298/S0020859000002091a.pdf/war_and_slavery_in_mores_utopia.pdf

### *Utopia within Utopia, 1516*

- Cambridge Core: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/historical-journal/article/utopia-within-utopia-1516/E8898E020F5796554D3412A29A32A07E

## Research confidence

The primary mechanical inspirations are directly grounded in the public domain text of *Utopia*. The modern institutional parallels are used as design aids, not as claims that More anticipated modern cooperative law or Garden City planning. New Harmony is used as a cautionary comparison, not as a direct continuation of More's program.
