# Research Evidence and Design Implications

## Method

Research was used to sharpen physical causes, response choices, period flavour, and implementation limits. The event remains alternate-history game design. Modern institutions and statistics are not presented as 1930s or 1940s in-game facts.

Sources were checked on 1 September 2026. Preference was given to public institutions, military safety guidance, international organizations, infrastructure operators, museums, and primary public-domain texts.

# Heat, health, cities, and nighttime exposure

## World Health Organization, Heat and health

Source: [WHO Heat and health](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/climate-change-heat-and-health)

Relevant findings:

- Extended high daytime and nighttime temperatures create cumulative physiological stress.
- Heat affects health services, infrastructure, workers, food, water, and transport.
- Effective response depends on warning, public communication, health planning, and local protection.

Design implications:

- Local exposure must accumulate over time.
- Hot nights reduce recovery and weaken night-shift mitigation.
- Health, water, labor, and infrastructure should interact instead of appearing as separate random penalties.

## World Health Organization, Heatwaves

Source: [WHO Heatwaves](https://www.who.int/health-topics/heatwaves)

Relevant findings:

- Heatwaves can last several days and create large health consequences whose losses are not always immediately visible.

Design implications:

- Baseline reports should show strain before broad mortality.
- Evolution I mortality needs exposure confirmation and visible lethal conditions.

## World Health Organization, Tips for keeping cool

Source: [WHO heat protection guidance](https://www.who.int/initiatives/initiative-against-extreme-heat-and-health-risks-in-workplaces-and-major-events/tips-for-keeping-cool)

Relevant findings:

- Urban heat islands can intensify city exposure.
- Non-urban livelihoods can also be severely disrupted.

Design implications:

- Dense cities receive nighttime and service pressure.
- Rural states remain important through agriculture, water, and labor.

# Military heat operations

## United States Army, Heat Illness Prevention

Source: [Army Heat Illness Prevention](https://safety.army.mil/MEDIA/Risk-Management-Magazine/ArtMID/7428/ArticleID/8240/Heat-Illness-Prevention)

Relevant findings:

- Gradual acclimatization over roughly one to two weeks is a central protection.
- Work-rest schedules, hydration discipline, monitoring, and planning reduce risk.

Design implications:

- Divisions need hidden acclimatization and exposure memory.
- Army heat protocols should change work-rest, hydration, and rotation behavior.
- Fresh units arriving in hot areas should suffer more than acclimatized units.

## United States Army, Military efforts preventing severe heat illness

Source: [Army heat prevention measures](https://www.army.mil/article/286206/military_efforts_preventing_severe_heat_illness_cases)

Relevant findings:

- Work-rest rules and controlled water intake are operational measures.

Design implications:

- Army protection should consume organization, time, transport, or command resources.
- More water is not a limitless solution, so the event should avoid a simple water-purchase button.

## Imperial War Museums and Australian War Memorial, Siege of Tobruk

Sources:

- [Imperial War Museums, Siege of Tobruk](https://www.iwm.org.uk/history/second-world-war/north-africa-campaign/siege-of-tobruk)
- [Australian War Memorial, Siege of Tobruk](https://www.awm.gov.au/tobruk)

Relevant findings:

- Troops faced stifling heat while food and water supplies declined.
- Heat combined with siege, bombardment, insects, illness, and logistics.

Design implications:

- Military heat should scale strongly with supply and local damage.
- Water, sanitation, and combat pressure should compound.
- Reports can show ordinary desert-war routines without presenting modern doctrine as period dialogue.

# Agriculture and food systems

## Food and Agriculture Organization, Extreme heat and agrifood systems

Sources:

- [FAO and WMO extreme heat report summary](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/extreme-heat-is-pushing-agrifood-systems-to-the-brink-worldwide/en)
- [FAO digital report on extreme heat](https://digital-media.fao.org/archive/EXTREME-HEAT-IMPACTS-ON-AGRIFOOD-SYSTEMS-2A6XC5L6RULY.html)
- [FAO, The heat is on](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/story/the-heat-is-on/en)

Relevant findings:

- Crops, livestock, aquatic systems, and agricultural workers have different thermal limits.
- Heat can damage reproduction and yield.
- Livestock drink more, eat less, and can die under persistent stress.
- Heat increases post-harvest spoilage when storage and power fail.

Design implications:

- Agricultural pressure must distinguish crop, livestock, water, labor, and transport channels.
- Harvest failure needs sustained exposure and can outlast the temperature peak.
- Famine receives a proven heat incident after these systems fail.

# Migration and habitability

## World Bank, Groundswell

Source: [World Bank Groundswell report summary](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2021/09/13/climate-change-could-force-216-million-people-to-migrate-within-their-own-countries-by-2050)

Relevant findings:

- Water scarcity, declining crop productivity, lost livelihoods, and loss of livability can drive internal movement.
- Movement patterns depend on origin pressure and safer destination conditions.

Design implications:

- Migration requests should follow water, food, and habitability failure.
- Safer mountain or northern regions need reception and food checks.
- Event 51 publishes hazard projections and lets Migration own cohorts and routes.

# Railways, industry, and utilities

## Network Rail, hot weather and rail buckling

Sources:

- [Buckled rail and summer heat](https://www.networkrail.co.uk/rail-travel/delays-explained/buckled-rail-and-summer-heat/)
- [How Network Rail prevents tracks from getting too hot](https://www.networkrail.co.uk/stories/how-we-prevent-tracks-from-getting-too-hot/)

Relevant findings:

- Rails can become much hotter than surrounding air.
- Steel expansion can cause buckling.
- Operators use inspection, restrictions, rail stressing, and reflective treatment to reduce risk.

Design implications:

- Rail consequences should progress from monitoring to restrictions to damage.
- Preventive maintenance should preserve the corridor while reducing short-term throughput.
- A rail failure should target a real critical corridor rather than a random state.

## United States Environmental Protection Agency, community water resilience

Source: [EPA Community-Based Water Resiliency Guide](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P1011DSI.txt)

Relevant findings:

- Water service depends on power, infrastructure, emergency planning, and public-private coordination.
- Service interruption can create large public and economic consequences.

Design implications:

- Water, power, industry, hospitals, and transport should form a cascade.
- Pump failure should persist into recovery instead of clearing with global temperature.

## International Labour Organization, Working on a warmer planet

Source: [ILO report on heat stress and productivity](https://www.ilo.org/publications/major-publications/working-warmer-planet-effect-heat-stress-productivity-and-decent-work)

Relevant findings:

- Excessive heat reduces work capacity and can force slower work or lost working hours.

Design implications:

- Factory, construction, agricultural, and rail work should lose output before taking direct damage.
- Night work, rest, and controlled shutdown become meaningful policy choices.

# Wildfire and compounding hazards

## National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, Wildfire climate connection

Sources:

- [NOAA Wildfire climate connection](https://www.noaa.gov/noaa-wildfire/wildfire-climate-connection)
- [NOAA drought and wildfire management](https://www.noaa.gov/wildfire-management)
- [NOAA interconnected heat hazards](https://cpo.noaa.gov/extreme-heat-and-interconnected-hazards-new-research-on-compounding-and-cascading-impacts/)

Relevant findings:

- Heat, drought, dry air, wind, and dry vegetation can combine to raise wildfire risk.
- Heat often compounds with other hazards rather than acting alone.

Design implications:

- Event 51 should own persistent heat and request actual wildfire through Event 013.
- Air Cleanliness receives smoke and ash from the disaster owner once.
- Wildfire eligibility needs terrain, dryness, exposure, and owner validation.

# Period heat reference

## NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information, July 1936

Source: [NOAA on the July 1936 heat](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/news/bold-stripes-bright-stars-warm-july-climate)

Relevant findings:

- The July 1936 heat occurred during the Dust Bowl and remains a useful period anchor for extreme heat, drought, and agricultural stress.

Design implications:

- Period-authentic art and reports can draw from Dust Bowl-era conditions.
- The event should still remain global and alternate-history, not a recreation of one United States episode.

# Research restraint

The following were deliberately avoided:

- importing modern policy structures directly into 1930s or 1940s flavour
- using exact modern mortality projections as game tuning
- treating one modern rail network as universal technology
- turning climate migration projections into direct in-game population formulas
- selecting a final quote or recording without the live repository, rights file, and dedicated research handoff
