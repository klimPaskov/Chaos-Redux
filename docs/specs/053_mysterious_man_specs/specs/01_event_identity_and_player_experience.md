# 1. Event Identity and Player Experience

## Core premise

Event 53 begins when a completely unexplained man appears before one randomly selected player-controlled country. The first appearance occurs inside a secure government location that he could not have entered through ordinary means. He is already present when the scene begins. Security personnel cannot explain how he arrived, and no witness can describe his departure.

He makes one demand. The government may meet it or refuse. A government that lacks the demanded resource is treated as refusing because the demand cannot be partially paid, deferred, borrowed, negotiated, or replaced with another resource.

Payment prevents punishment for the current visit only. It does not create a truce, reduce future eligibility, or satisfy him. The next visit is scheduled after a dynamic interval.

Refusal calls the Event 53 consequence registry. The registry builds a fresh pool from the current campaign state, includes each valid registered package exactly once, selects one entry uniformly, applies it through its owning adapter, and schedules the next visit.

## What the player should feel

The event should create a recurring budget problem with uncertain stakes. The demand is always clear. The punishment is always uncertain before refusal. The player can prepare the country in ordinary ways, but the player cannot discover a special solution to the man himself.

The strongest tension comes from three facts:

- the demand can arrive when the requested resource is needed elsewhere
- payment offers certainty for one visit but makes later demands more costly
- refusal can produce any currently valid registered crisis, with no hidden preference for a mild result

The event should not become a resource tax that the player automatically pays. It should also avoid becoming a refusal button that always produces a manageable debuff. Both choices must remain credible through the whole campaign.

## The man

The man remains a single recurring individual. His visual identity should be consistent enough that the player recognizes him, while every image keeps him ordinary and period-appropriate.

He should have no visible supernatural marker. Avoid glowing eyes, ritual symbols, impossible shadows, wounds, demonic features, futuristic clothing, official insignia, weapons, or theatrical villain styling. His clothing can change modestly with location or season, but his face, age range, posture, and reserved manner should remain recognizable.

He never explains his motive. He does not respond to questions, threats, offers, appeals, or attempts at bargaining. He states the demand, waits for an answer, and disappears after the outcome is settled.

No later branch reveals that he represents a government, intelligence agency, cult, alien power, divine force, time traveller, secret society, or known Chaos actor. The lack of explanation is permanent.

## Government response

The government may strengthen ordinary systems after suffering a consequence. A country can build reserves, improve supply, strengthen internal security, protect infrastructure, prepare relief, defend borders, improve disease response, or reduce occupation risk through existing game systems.

Those preparations may reduce harm when the owning crisis system already supports them. They never change:

- whether the man can appear
- which demand type is selected
- the demand amount after it has been locked
- which valid consequence packages enter the pool
- the equal probability rule
- the time at which he disappears
- the fact that he returns

The event must not add a special investigation value, security meter, ritual, operation, focus route, decision category, countermeasure project, or hidden victory condition.

## Visit structure

Every visible visit follows the same simple sequence:

1. The country-scoped follow-up resolves the setting and locks one valid demand type.
2. The demand calculator locks the exact amount before the popup appears.
3. The popup presents the demand and two possible stances.
4. The payment stance is enabled only when the full locked demand can be paid safely.
5. Refusal remains available in every visit.
6. The selected outcome applies once.
7. The visit state is cleared.
8. One future visit is scheduled.

The event should never open a separate negotiation window or ask the player to manage several simultaneous demands.

## Choice integrity

One visit asks for one main sacrifice. Evolution III can make that sacrifice extreme, but it still remains one demand instead of a long invoice.

The payment effect must match the displayed demand exactly. There are no hidden payment costs. The refusal effect must call the authoritative consequence selector exactly once. There are no hidden second punishment rolls.

A compound consequence is one registered package with one ballot. Its internal sequence can contain several coordinated components, but the selector does not roll several consequences after the player refuses.

## Information boundary

The player should know:

- the demanded resource
- the exact amount
- whether the country can pay
- that payment settles only this visit
- that refusal causes one serious harmful consequence
- that the man has returned before and can return again

The player should not know from the popup:

- the full consequence registry
- the number of currently valid packages
- the selected package before refusing
- the demand formula
- future demand types
- exact visit scheduling
- adapter validity logic
- evolution thresholds that have not been publicly reached
- the man's identity, origin, purpose, or method

Event Details can explain the public premise and active chain status. It must not become a registry browser or formula page.

## Baseline campaign role

At Chaos level 1, Event 53 is a regional player-country crisis. It does not need global presentation on first firing. Other countries learn about consequences only through the ordinary visibility of wars, breakaways, outbreaks, sanctions, disasters, and diplomatic reactions.

The event gains reach through the severity of refusal outcomes. It does not need a global opening announcement to feel important.

## Replay value

Replay value comes from current-world validity. A country with large occupied territory exposes occupation and separatist packages. A maritime empire exposes convoy, embargo, and overseas displacement packages. A state at war exposes military fracture and foreign-war packages. A peaceful compact country exposes fewer territorial consequences and more political, economic, intelligence, stockpile, and disaster packages.

Repeated firings of the follow-up chain rebuild the pool from the current campaign state. No static campaign sequence should guarantee the same order of punishments.

## Tone direction

The writing should stay direct, restrained, and concrete. The man speaks briefly. Government officials focus on the immediate demand and the impossibility of his access. Security failure is shown through physical circumstances, missing records, untouched locks, and absent witnesses.

Avoid long intelligence briefings, generic sealed-report language, theatrical threats, prophecy, philosophy, cosmic exposition, or narration that tells the player to feel afraid. The event should show an ordinary man waiting where he cannot be.
